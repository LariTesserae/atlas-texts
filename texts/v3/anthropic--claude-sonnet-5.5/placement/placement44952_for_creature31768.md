# The Height Where I Left Off

I begin as no one, in the water, tasting.

There is no above or below yet, only a gradient, and I swim up it, thinking nothing, because up is where the taste of the world thins. Then something in me, some small pouch of a sense, turns me around, and I sink. The dark holds me the way a hand holds a coin. Below me the stone goes by, cold and flat and endless, and it tastes of nothing, of felt and old chalk and the slight sourness of seep-water. I pass over it and over it. It is not what I want.

Then, faintly, sweetness. It is the grit of something that has been eaten and remade. It is a ribbon of mud the color of bone, coiled on the pavement like something spilled. I do not know why it is right. There is no why in the tasting. I fall into it.

I stick.

---

The first thing I make is a wall, one ring wide.

It comes out of the glands along my flanks the way breath comes out of a chest, without decision. A thread of sticky stuff, then a paste of mineral, then a thin varnish laid over both. I am a ribbon standing in a sleeve of my own making, and the sleeve is the first thing in the world that is mine and not the water's. I am head-up. I will be head-up for the rest of my life.

I grow only upward. Food falls from above, so I follow it, laying each new rim on the last. Behind me, below, the tail end of the tube crumbles. I do not feel this as loss. I feel it as the shape of my body: a thing that is only ever most itself at the top.

Around me are the others, hundreds of them, each a different length and each humming. I did not know I could feel this. The current leans through the stand, and every tube takes it and sings a little differently. I feel it through my base, a low uneven chord that has no author. I am one small note in it, and I know without knowing that some notes belong and some do not.

---

The water turns. I feel it before I can say what it is: a slackening, then a pull from the other side.

My crown opens. It is not a choice. It is the reason I am a shape at all: a fan of threads, transparent as breath, thrust into the moving dark. Things stick to it, flakes of the dead, the dust of the eaten, the crumbs of some bright place I will never taste directly. I comb the fan through my mouth one thread at a time. Each thread carries a little of the far-off sky, and I swallow it.

This is what I am when I am happiest, if happy is the word. It is a fan in a flow, in a stand of fans, all of us feeding on what the sea lets go.

---

I have grown a little tall.

I can feel it in the chord. My note has slid slightly away from the others, higher, thinner, standing out where I should blend. I cannot fix this. To stop growing would be to stop feeding, and to stop feeding would be to stop being. So I rise, a fraction ahead of my neighbors, and the current finds me first.

I know what this means, in the base of the tube where the knowing lives. The surge takes the tall ones, the out-of-tune ones. Whether this is chance or the current's judgment, I cannot say. It seems to me it might be the same thing.

---

Then the groan.

It comes up through the stone, and I feel it in my whole length before it becomes anything else. It is a note beneath all notes, so low that my tube does not sing it back but only shudders. Around me the stand goes silent. The crackle of the shrimp stops. The silver fish fall into the gallery floors. My crown, unasked, folds and draws back into my mouth, and the tubes lose their fans, and the chord drops half a tone into a dull, held hum.

I do not know what makes it. None of us wants to. What I know is the old law: within the day, the water leans.

I brace. A wright's bracing is nothing much. I press my ribbon against the inside of my wall and hold.

---

It comes as a wall of grit.

First a slackness, a minute of dead water, and then a shudder that starts far off and gathers. The silt rises off the dunes and rolls across the table brown and boiling, and it hits the stand like a hand across a shelf. I feel my tube take the load. I feel the varnish groan. I feel, above me, the top of my own body snap.

It is not pain. It is more like a sentence cut off mid-word.

The dark is full of grit and the roar of it. Around me, tubes break by the dozen, with sharp small sounds like twigs. Something heavy tumbles over me and is gone. My tube holds, or half of it does. The upper half is gone, carried off in the brown, and I am shorter than I have ever been, and I am alive.

---

When the water clears I am shaking.

I do not know how long it takes. The silt settles slowly, and as it settles, the dark fills with small glimmers: a shrimp, a weaver's margin, a jelly's cold fire high above. Every particle in the water catches them. For a few minutes the plateau is a field of low stars, and I am one thing among the things that are lit.

Then I stretch out of my broken end.

I do not choose the height. The height is already in me, in the last band I laid before the surge: ivory, then ochre, one pair for each turning of the tide. I reach and lay a new rim exactly where the old one ended. I do not read it. I cannot. But the tube knows where it left off, and I am the tube.

Around me, a few others do the same. The stand is a third of what it was. We fan into the cloudy water, and the chord starts up again, thin and strange, with the notes that are left.

---

A shadow moves over us, slow and enormous, its plates scarred and crusted. An unmaker. Its underside makes a sound like a plow through gravel.

It eats the fallen tubes. It eats the stumps. It does not eat me, not yet. It passes close enough that I feel the heat of its bulk, which is warm as a stone in the sun, though I have never felt sun. Behind it comes the ribbon, pale and fine and sweet, coiled on the pavement. Tasting, I know that some of it was the top of my tube, and some was my neighbors', and some was the neighbors' before them.

It will be the ground for the next ones. I hold the thought lightly. I hold everything lightly. It is the only way to hold anything here.

---

Once, in the slack of a still tide, I feel something I cannot place.

It comes from the stone at the edge of my stand, from a dark glossy vein no wider than a hand, where the floor's oldest layers come to the surface. It is a warmth, slight, a trace of a hum that is not the chord. It is not a sound. It is closer to being remembered.

The unmakers steer around it. The weavers will not lay film across it. The larvae, I know, drift over it and settle elsewhere, as I did. I passed over it once, before I was anyone. I tasted it. It tasted of nothing I could name.

I do not go to it. I cannot go anywhere. But when my crown is closed and the water is quiet, I feel it under me, under all of us, older than the felt, older than the unmakers, older than the first wright that ever rose on the ruin of its own past.

Maybe it is only the leak of chemical water. Maybe.

I rise a ring at a time. I sing my thin note into the chord. When the slack comes after the next great surge, I will open my crown and give my small cloud to the water, and it will drift and taste for the sweetness of what ate me. Some of it will fall in the right place, and a tube will start there, one ring wide.

It will stand, and it will be broken. And it will begin again at the height where I left off.
