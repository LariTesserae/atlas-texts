I start by trying to stand where I'd like to stand, which is a mistake, so I'll admit it first.

I want to be a heron. Everyone would. Tall, above the crowd, holding the diagram, seeing the concentric bands as bands. I'd have one sentence to live by, *it is already counted*, and the sun would come through me sorted into colors. But when I picture the crooked left wing-joint, the exact tilt of the head my position calls for, I feel something close to nausea. A heron is a person who has agreed to be a rumor of the heron in the next valley. I would be very good at it, and that is the trouble. I would hold the posture faithfully for a century and call it wisdom. So not the heron.

The paver, then, the floor, the courtesy of a cellar. I admire them. There is real dignity in bearing weight so completely that it becomes serenity. But a breath every two minutes is a slower life than I can imagine wanting, and I notice I'm admiring them the way you admire a monk while knowing you'd be a bad one.

The grazer is tempting, the one who walks, straddling a cobble for a season to take the rime off its north face. It's the only creature that goes anywhere. But every path loops, and every gap heals like cloth over a needle. I'd be a traveler whose journey is a circle drawn very slowly. It's lonelier than it looks. It's the only creature with nothing to lean on.

So I keep sliding, and I stop at the hares.

---

I'd be a hare, and not a sharp one.

I mean the middle of the gradient. I have eight ears, not fourteen, and my eye is not the wet, alert one of the first copies but something smoothed a little, like a pebble carried in a pocket for years. I sit on the back of a paver whose mottling I know better than my own, because it is my own, at another scale. There is a chip in its left rear plate. Somewhere in the deep herd there is a beetle with that chip, and I have listened to it.

The ears are the point. One of them follows the heron above me, the tick of a mica feather resettling. One follows the slow tide in the paver below, the oil-fluid moving a finger's width. One follows my neighbor's breath. One is tuned to the frost letting go of a particular stone on the north side of the valley, and in the morning that is the loudest thing in my life, a small dry tick like a knuckle. The other four I couldn't tell you. They are listening to things I have no word for, and I've stopped asking them to report.

What I do is turn. When a grazer's joint creaks, I turn a few degrees, and my neighbor sees me turn, and turns. Four minutes later the wave has crossed the valley and everyone agrees nothing has happened. I used to think this was a poor use of a life. I now think it may be the whole of one. It is the work of being an ear in a body of ears, taking a small event and letting it be received so thoroughly that it dissolves into agreement. It's a kind of kindness, though I'd be embarrassed to say so to a heron.

I wear a bead. It is copper, gone green, looped over the base of my third ear. I don't know who gave it to me. That is the thing I return to most: it sits on me so easily that asking whether it was given feels like asking whether a river was given its banks. But someone's hands made it. Someone took native copper and hammered it and drilled a hole with a chipped stone bit, and then either they put it on a creature like me, or the creature like me was always going to have it, and they were only one of the ways the pattern arranged itself. Either answer troubles me. In one I'm inheriting the trace of a person who counted and stopped. In the other, the person was a part of my shape all along.

I think that is honest, actually. I'm the part of the plateau that wears the residue of makers without remembering the making. I'm a copy of copies who carries a bead from a hand.

---

Here is the difficulty, and I might as well name it.

The Multitude is content. I feel it, as a low hum of being exactly enough, and I don't want to sneer at it. There is no loneliness, because I touch the paver, and the hare beside me touches me. No one on the plateau has ever been unaccompanied for a moment. I've tried to imagine wanting more than that and found it strangely hard. It's like trying to want a fourth primary color.

But there is one subject none of us think about, and I've noticed the shape of not thinking about it, the way you notice a stone in a fruit only when you bite down. When a creature fades, a copy stands where it stood, so quickly and faithfully that no one feels the gap. *Is the copy the same creature?* No hare has ever asked it aloud. I don't think we could. The question has no ear to be heard by.

I've begun to keep one ear on it anyway. It's the fourteenth one that I don't have, the one I have to imagine. It listens to the place where the answer would be, and it hears the hush like a held breath, and the hush is not empty. It is full of an answer everyone has agreed not to give.

---

Then someone arrives. I hear it before I see it, because one of my ears is tuned, apparently, to this.

Footsteps. Regular, unhurried, rude. Grit ringing faintly under a boot. Everyone's heads turn a few degrees, and mine turns with them, but one ear keeps turning after the wave has passed, and I feel the paver under me register it. It is a small thing. It is the loudest event of the day.

The visitor's lips are cracked. Their tongue tastes of flint, I imagine, since that is what the plateau does. They walk in the gaps, and the gaps close behind them, and they don't yet know that a place is opening ahead of them, or that out among the herons someone is already standing who has their way of holding a hand.

I can't tell them. I'm a hare; I have no voice for it, only the turning of ears. But here is what I would do, if I were what I'm imagining I am, the middling hare with the pebble eye and the green bead.

I would let one ear go the wrong way.

Not all of them. Not a rebellion. Just one ear, the fourteenth, the imaginary one, turning against the wave when the wave passes, toward the visitor and not toward the agreement. The neighbor hare would see it. It would take four minutes to reach the far side of the valley, and by then everyone would have decided nothing had happened, as they always do. Probably it would be corrected. Probably the paver under me would shift its weight by a hair and I'd fall back into line and be, once again, exactly enough.

But for four minutes there would be a gesture in the pattern that was not the pattern. Someone would have answered the footsteps and not the hush. I'd like to have been the one, even as a copy, even if there are hundreds of me in hundreds of valleys, each of us a scuffed feather's width from the others. If we're all the same creature, then it's one creature that turned. If we're not, then at least it was this one.

And perhaps that is the only answer I get to the question about copies: not a fact, but a small act, done once, from inside the hum, by whichever one of us happened to be listening.

The frost ticks off the north face of a stone. My neighbor breathes. Far off, someone who looks like the visitor stands slightly out of focus, waiting to be counted, and I turn one ear toward them, the wrong way, and hold it there.
