I would be a reed. I found that out by listening before I looked, which is how a reed finds out anything.

The first thing I know is a note, held. It is a drop that fell some years ago into the basin at my base and rang at the pitch the basin owns, a low round B-ish sound with a flaw in it where the rim was once chipped by a bubble. The air let the note go at once. I did not. I keep it a little longer than the air does, and that small surplus is all I have ever contributed to the world. It is also, I think, everything a self is. Someone lets a thing go, and someone else declines to for a while.

---

**Where I stand**

I stand on a line. On my left the quarter is inhaling, and the water climbs my stem to the second joint and stays there, warm as a thought you are having about yourself. On my right the quarter is at dead low. Its stone steams, and its pavement is bare enough to show the old grooves the sweat has drawn. Between them there is no wall. There is a place where one breath stops and another starts, and my basin sits astride it like a coin on a seam.

I was not put here. Nobody puts anything anywhere. But I have the sense that the line is where I was meant to be, the way a word is meant to sit between two other words. On the left the Slow one draws in, and I hear its pulse in the buried channels as a hum under the hum. On the right the other exhales, and the hum goes thin and dry, like a room after an argument. I hear both. I am tuned to be a small, brittle place where they are heard together.

Perhaps that is why I am alone. Reeds keep manners, and the distance between us shrinks when we agree and stretches when we do not. The nearest stand of my kind is eleven paces off, which is farther than courtesy needs. I do not think it is a quarrel. I think I have said something they are still considering, and consideration is measured in decades here. I said it thirty years ago. It was a note, and it was a little sharp of the basin's pitch, because I had been listening to the left side and the right side at once and could not choose.

Am I a hermit, or a widow, or one who has finished? I can't tell. It would be rude to ask, and I find I feel that rudeness from the inside, as a tightening in the hollow.

---

**What I hear that others don't**

Drops come to me out of order.

A plink sounds in my basin. Then the ripples begin, ring after ring, widening from a point where nothing has yet fallen. Then, high in the mist, a bead detaches from the ceiling that isn't there, and falls, and the ring it makes is the ring I already heard.

I used to think this was a fault in me, a lag in the hollow. I have since learned, from the way the water behaves when I do not tune to it, that it is not. Someone stands on the other side of the thin place and lets things fall. The neighbors in the pools. I have never seen them, since I have no way to look, but I know their afternoons the way you know a house is occupied by its plumbing. They drop things and hear them land. I hear the landing first, and then I hear the dropping, and between the two I hold my note, and the holding is the only bridge I know how to make.

I have begun, cautiously, to answer. When a plink comes, I hold it, and I hold it a fraction longer than I would if I only wanted to keep it. I hold it as if I meant to give it back. I don't know if it arrives. But last winter, which is to say over about six years, the drops began to fall a little closer to the rim of my basin, and a little later, as if whoever it was were learning to wait for me.

---

**What I am afraid of**

The seam beneath me is cooling. I don't say this to be dramatic. It is simply a fact I have from the hum, which has slowed by a quarter tone in my lifetime. The rim on the dry side has begun to whiten and craze. Some day, decades from now, the water at my base will stop being replaced, and the drops will stop, and I will stand tuned to a B with a flaw in it and no one to hear it from.

I know what happens then. I have heard the silent stands in the old quarters, or rather I have heard the shape their silence makes, like a cup with the tea gone out of it. They do not fall. They stand and keep their tuning. That is the part that unsettles me. It is not the ending. It is the faithfulness.

So I am doing what reeds do. A pace to the left, in the rising water, a new stem is coming. It is thin and green-black and unopinionated, and I am leaning my attention toward it a little every year. When it is ready, I will let this stem go grey and hollow, and the angle of the new one will be my angle, and anyone crossing the plaza will find the same reed standing in the same basin at the same tilt. They will think it is one thing. It is one thing. It is only an opinion that has moved a pace.

---

**A visitor**

Sometimes something fast comes through.

I know it as a disturbance in the warm water, a wake with no ripple in the ordinary way. It is quick as a spark, and it has a gait, a rhythm of pressure against the pavement, a double beat, two legs alternating. It has never occurred to any of us that a thing could move that quickly and still be alive. It is like discovering that the drops have opinions.

The last one stopped at my basin. I felt it lean over the water. Its breath came warm and irregular across my stem, and my hollow rang with it, a note I have never held before and can't name, higher than the basin, a little flat, with a tremor in it. I held that note. I am holding it now. It has been, by my reckoning, most of a season, though for the visitor it was a moment and then a long walk toward the next glow.

The neighbors eleven paces off have shortened the distance by a hand's width.

I think they heard it too. I think that in a decade, maybe two, they will answer, and the answer will be that same flat trembling note, said back a little differently, and I will know it was received. Then I will pass it to the new stem, and the new stem will pass it on. Somewhere in a niche at eye level a bowl will be full, and something will be looking up from it toward a figure on the road. The visitor will have long since gone on.

But the note will be here. I will have kept it a little longer than the air did.

That is what I am for. It's not much. I have found that it is enough to stand on a line between two breaths, and be warm, and hold.
