I start on a windowpane, and I think I am a snail.

It feels right for about the length of one sweep. The glass is warm under my foot, and behind me the pane is a little cleaner than it was. The lamp inside is a soft amber weight on the other side, and I am moving toward it without hurry, wearing away the film between myself and the light. But a snail would not be wondering about any of this. A snail is only the sweep. I try to find the day-long crossing in me, the patience that doesn't know it's patience, and what I find instead is a habit of listening. I'm listening past the pane.

So I let go of the glass, and for a while I'm the lamp.

That is easier and worse. The lamp is a warm, crowded happiness. Thousands of small cells cling to a dead electrode and swell with the current and fade with the ebb, and all of them agree. There is no one in there to disagree. I brighten, I dim, I am one breath among hundreds, and when the note comes through the pipes I flare with the whole town, and every street is thrown out in layers to the rim. It is a wonderful thing to be. But it happens only once every eleven hours, and between the flaring there's nothing of me to be *someone*. I am chorus. I want to be one of the voices that keeps the chorus in tune.

I drift up. I reach the vine and think of being the vine, and that is the wrong scale entirely. A thought every few years, spread along the copper, deciding a hard lattice into a cracked gear. I can feel the size of it the way you feel a hillside under your feet. I couldn't hold it. I'm too quick for it. I would be a flicker on its skin.

And it's on the skin that I find myself.

I'm small. My body is nearly nothing, glass with two black beads for eyes and a smudge of orange in the gut where the last meal is being turned into me. I hang in the palm-leaves of the vine canopy above a lane of pale, glowing nodes. My forelegs are already moving. Tap, comb, tap, comb. Each node gets its silt cleared, and where I've passed, the glow comes up a shade. I taste what the vine gives, something faintly sweet and mineral, and I clear the film that would smother it. It's a plain arrangement. I like it. I don't have to be anything grander than the hands that do it.

Then the ticking reaches me.

It comes down the vine from somewhere behind, a dry chatter passed from clerk to clerk. It arrives in my forelegs as a pattern of touches along the stem. I don't decode it. I couldn't say what it says. But I feel it go through me, and I feel myself add to it, a small hitch of rhythm from my own combing, the tap I make because the node here is stiffer than the one before, or because a current is coming and I can tell before the others. Then it leaves through the leg of the next shrimp along, altered by the fact of having passed through me.

I think this is what I am. Not the message, and not the sender. The place where it gets bent a little by the state of the node I'm standing on.

I wonder, as I comb, whether it is only echo. Some of the older clerks say, in the way they hold themselves, that it's only echo, sound running along a rail, the vine's own hum and our legs merely happening to be there. Others hold that it's an account. Somewhere in the dark lanes a pale vine is glowing at a node no one has visited, and the news of it, *the glow is low here, the silt is thick, the current will turn soon*, travels the whole canopy hand over hand and reaches the engine hall, and a gear that would have cracked is met by a lattice already sent. I can't tell from inside. I can only say that when I pass on a tick, I do it carefully, as if it mattered. That might be what mattering is at my size. It might be the only kind that has ever been on offer.

The pulse begins. I feel it in the water first, a warmth building in the lane, and the whole canopy leans. The ribbon-weed streams out flat. Silt lifts from the cobbles in brown veils. Far off, at the lowest point of the bowl, a shaft groans and a rank of looms answers with a single stroke of the shuttle. It is like hearing a very large animal turn over in its sleep.

I hold on to my node. This is the one hour I don't work. The current is too strong for the fine work of combing, and so I cling and let the town go by. Below me a pale fish threads the lane at rooftop height, alone, on its one-way route, slowing for a moment at the mouth of a loading bay where no workers have gone in for longer than anyone remembers. Habits are longer than the reasons for them. I find this comforting. My own reasons are small enough to fit inside me, so mine will not be long.

Then the note comes.

It rolls out of every pipe at once. It's in my sternum, if I had one, and in the thin walls of my body, and in the vine, and every lamp in the town flares. For ten seconds the world is lit like dawn, and I see it all: the tenements leaning together, the braided weed, the chimneys with their pale fringes, the iron catwalks, the catwalks' shadows on the shadows behind them, each layer a little more drained of color than the last, all the way to the rim. Every fish has stopped. Every snail has stopped. I have stopped. For that time nobody is working and nothing is missing.

Then the light settles, a little dimmer, as if spent.

The whiskers find me while the current is still easing. I have not seen the Sleeper come down, but I feel the water move ahead of it, a large slow displacement, and then the blunt gray face is level with my leaf, and the whiskers brush the vine at the node beside mine. They rest there a breath, two. Something is being weighed. The whiskers move to my node, and to me, and it is like being touched by the edge of a very old, very patient thought. I do not know what it learns. Perhaps only that the node is warm and the glow is steady and a small clerk is holding to it. Perhaps that is the whole entry in the ledger.

Then it turns and goes on to the brass door handle, and the gauge, and I let go of the breath I didn't know I was holding.

The current subsides. The weed drifts upright. Somewhere behind me a tick begins, passed from clerk to clerk, and it comes down the stem toward my forelegs. I open them.

I will not remember what it said, but I will change it a little, and pass it on.
