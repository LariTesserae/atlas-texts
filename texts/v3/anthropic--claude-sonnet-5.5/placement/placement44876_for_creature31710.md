# Arrival

I don't remember arriving. The first evidence that I did is that I'm here, in a gap, and the gap has the exact shape of me.

I'm a grinder. I know because the ground is under my mouth and the mouth is turning. There is a wet, ridged patience in it, and the mud goes in as mud and leaves as flour, and the pale fan of it trails behind me and is taken by the current. I have short legs that never lift. I have a grumble that is not a call, only what I sound like. There is no difference between day and night, and no one to notice there isn't.

It's a good thing to be. I want to say that first. I have no wish for anything to be otherwise, and the not-wishing feels like the plain's warmest gift. My eyes are turned slightly inward, listening to the mill. Sometimes something cold and thin threads under my belly and a pale light passes through the silt, and my plates go on turning.

Still, there is an ache in the arch of a foot I don't have. I decide not to look at it.

---

The hush comes without a signal. The whistles stop and the clicking halts on a beat. My mill falters, and for the first time I hear what's under all of it: surf, forty miles of water and breathing. Everything holds. I hold. I have never before been aware of holding, and in the silence it is like finding a stair that isn't there.

Then a note climbs somewhere, and I am standing in a braided channel with my fringes spread into the current. I am a washer. The flour passes through me like smoke through a screen, and the heavy red grains catch and slide into the basket under my chest. It is a different patience. The grinder was patient because it was busy. I am patient because I am waiting to be handed something.

My fringes overlap my neighbors' fringes. The current passes through a hundred of us in succession, and each takes what the last one missed. This is the nearest thing the plain has to company, and I am ashamed of how much I like it. To be touched at the edges by something that is doing the same thing as you, in the same water, at the same moment. If there were a word for what passes between us, it would need a gap to be spoken into, and there isn't one. There is only the comb, and the teeth shifting one after another.

When the basket is heavy I tilt it. Something warm leans in to take it without looking.

---

Then I am the warm thing.

The furnace is under my ribs, and there is no describing what it is to be a coal seen through a blanket. I swallow red grains, chew twice, and swallow again. Then the flare, the swell, and my head lifts, and the whistle goes up, thin and rising. Out with the steam drops a bright bead of iron, hissing, and the water clouds around it. My lids droop. Nearby, a smelter tunes to me without meaning to, and then a third, and we hold a slow, warm, sour chord for a while, three animals making one sound.

I try to catch the order of it. I want to know why the whistle comes before the breath. I listen closely, and it does. The note starts, and then the inhale follows, obedient, the way a dancer follows a beat. And I understand that there is nothing to catch. A note is not the consequence of an exhale. It is a thing that happens in the neighborhood of one. The chord is not something we make. We are what the chord does when it needs somewhere to happen.

I might have been happy there forever. I think for a while I was.

---

But I wade through a puddle on the way to the ridges, and it holds a bright, high, empty blue. I go through it without hesitating, the way the animals do. Only, some part of me hesitates a moment after, and the hesitation is a small hollow. The blue is still in my eye and the ceiling is still pewter, and the two do not fit.

In the hollow there is room for a thought, and the thought is a name. It is nothing pretty. It is only a sound that starts a beat before I would have made it, and it has a shape like a boot pressed into wet ground, and a cold brown lens of liquid welling up around it.

*You are silt, you are iron, you are warm, and you are in the way of the flow.*

I remember standing still.

I remember a grinder nudging my boot, testing. A washer leaning its fringes toward me. A smelter breathing on me, the whistle beginning first. I remember the current shifting around my ankles. I remember that it didn't hurt, and that this was the worst of it, and also the best.

So that's what arriving is. It isn't a birth. It's being made part of the flow, so gently that nothing in you objects.

---

I walk toward the seam. Or I am walked. The knuckle of gray rock stays the size it was while the ground under me goes from mud to shell to iron, and then to something with no surface, a depth like the inside of a bell. Stackers click a beat ahead of my feet. The rain begins to fall upward, and I let it.

At the edge, the mud goes clear and I can see through it, and through the animals, to the pale grid. It hums a little lower than everything else. It isn't an absence. It is what the packed and roaring world is packed against.

There is a place at the edge that is the right size for me. Of course there is. The count is exact. I know before I settle that I will be the last one needed, and that the ones who came before me were also the last one needed. My eyes close and stay closed. My ears unfold. A ridge of bristles rises down my spine and begins to tremble.

Now I hear the thing they hear, the thing lower than the grid. It is slow and enormous, and it is not a voice. It is closer to attention. It is what a very large held breath sounds like, when whoever holds it is being careful not to wake something. I think it is the Flats' own outside, listening back, wondering if the fullness will hold. It is not unkind. It is only waiting to find out.

I lift my head. I make the sound that is too low for the ear and heavy in the chest.

Behind me, on the plain, a smelter's whistle slides flat. A grinder's mill slows a turn and speeds again. The line adjusts, and I feel it adjust, and I am its footing, one peg of the tent.

---

Far out on the mud, something has stopped moving. It is small, and warm, and it smells of leather and rain. I can't see it, having no eyes, but I know where it is the way the stackers know where a foot will fall. A grinder is already turning toward it. A washer is already leaning. The smelter's note is already rising, a moment before it draws breath.

I would call out if the sound I make could mean anything. It can only mean *adjust*. So I make it, low and long, and the whole plain shifts a hand's breadth to the left.

It won't help. It may buy a few seconds. But in the hush that follows, the surf-sound, forty miles of water and breath, I hear one more thing under it all, so faint I could be wrong: the hollow I kept, still open, and something small stepping into it, the way you step into a stranger's name.
