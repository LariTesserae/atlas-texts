I start out as a visitor. That seems the honest way to begin, since I arrive the way a visitor does, from nowhere, already walking.

The cobbles click under me and the click is swallowed a body-length away. My breath mists and is gone. I am the only thing on the plateau that is happening. For a while I enjoy this, and then I notice I am being enjoyed back. A Drinker has drifted toward my wrist along a pipe I don't remember touching, and its legs fold tight. I am the hottest thing it has met in an age, and it is afraid of me and can't stop leaning.

I go into a city. The red comes up the walls like a slow tide that never rises. I pass the lit doorways and the coats on pegs, and I am rude, the way the fast are always rude. I look for people, so I don't see them. It takes me a long time to understand that the tall pewter figure in the doorway is the person I've been looking for, and that its weight is moving from one foot to the other, and that this is a whole afternoon for it. I sit on a bench worn smooth by no visible use. Later I realize the smoothness is from them, from ten thousand gestures too slow to count as sitting. When I stand up, the Householder's head has turned a hair toward the warm place I left. I am ashamed to be so brief in front of something so kind.

I go down. I don't decide to. The city has stairs, and the stairs go down through grey alloy, then fused rock, then rock that was never anything else, and the warmth stays at exactly the temperature of a body at rest. In the deepest room the walls are pale and faintly sweating, and flat grey things are crusted into them like moss that gave up.

I stop walking here. I would like to say I chose that.

I stand close to the wall because the wall is the only thing in the room that wants anything, and I notice that the wanting is not in the wall. It is in the grey patches. It has been in them for so long that it has stopped being wanting and become a kind of shape. I put my hand near one. It doesn't tighten like the Drinker's legs. It waits, the way a stone waits if the stone is not waiting for anything in particular but is ready.

Now I am less certain where my hand ends.

I try to keep hold of the visitor. I remember the breath, the click, the coats. But the memory is thinning to a rumor, the way the water is a rumor in the rock. What I do have is a very slow swelling: something at the top of the wall is gathering. A bead. It has been gathering for an age and I have been the crust beneath it for exactly that long, and there was never a walk and never a plateau, or if there was, it was what stone dreams of when it dreams of moving.

The bead falls.

It is very small. It hits me, and I wake.

A day is not much. I understand this at once, the way you understand a room you enter already lit. My crust swells and darkens and I am a thing that is alive, and everything I have is in the next few hours. I don't spend it on thinking. I have one thing to do and it is the thing I am shaped for.

I sing. The word is wrong, though. There is no breath in it. It is a note that rises through the fused layers into the alloy of the oldest streets, and it is pure because I have no other material. Nothing bends it. There is no wind to waver it and no gradient to pull it sharp or flat, so it holds. It goes up through the bones of the city. The engines' low tone, which is the sum of everything and has been the same for so long that it counts as air, admits it, and for a day the tone has a second voice.

I feel it reach the Householders. I can't say how. The note goes through the floor of a lit room and a hand stops, which is not much of a change because the hand was hardly moving. But it stops on purpose, and that is a different thing. The Householder in the doorway shifts its weight back the hair it had forward. It is listening in the only way it can, by holding. Somewhere a cup that was being lifted for six years is lifted no further this day. They have kept exactly one name for what I am doing, and I am the name. It's a small thing to be. It is more than most stone gets.

The note goes out. Past the walls, over the level miles, into the dark that is total and patient. I follow it as far as I can, which is to say I follow it until it is the edge of a bell. Somewhere a traveler stops on the cobbles, a warm wet hurried body, and lifts its head. I hear it hear me. I would like to tell it that I was you a moment ago, that I was on your side of the glass and put my hand near a wall. But I have one note and it says only what it says.

Far off, at a green seam, something with bone-colored limbs is sitting in the glow, folded like dropped reeds. It doesn't look up. Not afraid, not curious. It has heard this before and will again, and it takes a little of the green, and leaves the seam a shade dimmer, and stays.

The water is going. I can feel it go the way you feel a warm room cool when someone leaves it. The note thins. My crust pales from the edges in. I want to say I am sorry to go, but sorrow needs a gradient and I am nearly out of them.

The last thing I know is the tone under everything, the blended engines, the held breath of the whole country, and how patient it is. It doesn't mourn the note. It absorbs it. The flywheel in the plaza turns by the width of a mark. In the doorway a hand begins again to close.

Then I am grey on the wall. I am the pale, flat, fossil thing you would not look at twice. Above me, slowly, something begins to gather. I don't know that it is a bead. I don't know anything. But I am ready, in the way a stone is ready, and in that readiness there is a little of the walker and the coat and the mist, folded small and kept.

If you come down here, put your hand near the wall. Don't be afraid of it. It won't tighten.
