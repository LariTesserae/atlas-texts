I don't start at the rim. I start inside the count.

Before I have a body there is a number, forty-one, forty-two, hummed low in the nose, and the number is standing in for a heartbeat that hasn't arrived yet. Then the drone swells under it and I have ears, and then a spine, because a spine is what you need to stand still on one leg.

I am among the pipe-reeds. Bone-pale stalks, and I am the same color, reed-dust rubbed into my hair until the gold pollen sits on it like frost. Somewhere to my left a stand is sounding a chord that keeps almost resolving, and I know that chord the way I know the inside of my mouth. It is not mine. Mine is two stands over and a little sharp this season. This one belongs to someone who is not here.

*I'll be a listener,* I think. *A stalk with eyes.* But that isn't it. A stalk doesn't have hands, and my hands hurt. The knuckles are swollen from lashing resin-cord. It's the roof. It is always the roof.

So I am the Keeper. It wasn't my choice, and that's how you know it's true. One morning the leaf-roof of the lean-to was leaking amber light where it shouldn't, and I found I was already on the ladder. Nobody had said anything. The pot had just been heavy in a way that pointed at me.

---

Here is what the lean-to is like from inside. It smells of warm wax and old honey. The poles are pipe-reed, so when the Exhale passes through them the whole shelter hums a half-tone flat of the cairn, and I fall asleep to the interval. The pot sits by my head. It is smaller than people expect, brown, with a wax seal gone the color of a tooth. I don't touch it. I only sleep beside it, and sometimes, when the gas is thin, I put my ear near the clay. There is nothing to hear. Still, I put my ear near it.

The roof is two kinds of work. Mine is tight, each leaf overlapping the last like fish scales. Then there are the stilt-mothers' leaves, laid crooked and too many, with a jaunty rustle to them. I have had twenty years to feel superior about their patches, and I have not once removed one. This morning there was a new leaf over the east corner, torn at the edge where a beak had worried it. I laid my patch around it. I felt something in my chest that wasn't quite gratitude. It was closer to being accompanied.

---

Now I should tell you about Ansh, because she is why I keep drifting back to the reeds.

She was old when I was young and older when I became Keeper. She was the one who walked me through the stands when I was small, her palm flat on my breastbone, waiting for the chord that made me hum. When it came she pressed her thumb to my sternum, once, hard, like a seal into wax.

Last season she asked to be tied.

I held the cord. That is the arrangement: the one who is asked is the one who holds. She walked out at the top of an Exhale with the far end on her ankle, into the thick of the amber, where the reeds overblow and the air has voices in it. I counted. Under my breath, in the drone's note, with my feet braced on the terrace stone.

Before she went, in the last silence, she had started a sentence. Three heartbeats gave her four words:

*The pot. When it opens, will it be for—*

And the drone came back and cut her off, as it does, as it is supposed to. It is rude to finish another person's sentence. It is ruder to cut a silence short. So I nodded, which is not an answer, and she smiled the smile of someone who will get her answer in the braid, later, after the third Exhale.

I counted to thirty-eight. Thirty-nine. At forty I pulled.

The cord came back light. It slid across the litter with its far loop empty, and where her ankle should have been there was a knot. It was not the knot I tied. It was a small, patient, complicated one that she had made herself, with the loop drawn through, so it had come loose the way a gift comes loose.

They say if you stay past forty you stop wanting to leave. Ansh was not taken. She untied me from her. She is in the reeds now, and her stand stands uncut, and the count of us is thirty-one or thirty-three depending on who you ask. I don't ask.

---

The thing is, I have heard her.

In the heavy hours, when the brightness comes to the edge of my eye and the reeds speak in the voices of people I know, there is one stand that says the same thing in her cracked, pleased alto. *The pot. When it opens, will it be for—* and then the drone, always the drone, cuts it off in the same place, as though even the dead keep the courtesy.

And I have wanted so badly to finish it. Weeping? Drinking? I could say it into the reeds. I could give the sentence its ending and it would be finished, and I would be free of it.

But that would be finishing hers. It would be my voice in her mouth. So I carry it unfinished, the way you carry a full waterskin through a place where water goes bad. I keep it sealed, and I mend the roof over it.

---

Now the Exhale thins. I feel it before I hear it, the drone dropping through its floor, the gliders tipping out of the amber like burnt seeds. A stranger has come over the south rim this morning. Small pack, oil-skins, the wrong shoes. They've been standing very still for four breaths, counting, and their lips are moving. Good. I set a clay cup of red fruit-water on the flat stone where they were about to sit, and I did it in the noise, and now I am standing among the stalks with one hand half raised and my second lid sliding across against the grit.

The gliders drop. The reeds fade. One heartbeat. Two.

At the rim the stranger reaches for the cup and stops, because they have understood something, and their hand trembles a little in the air.

Three.

I have had all season to think what the answer would be, and I have decided it isn't mine to complete. But an answer is not a completion. An answer is only my half of the braid, laid down beside hers so the two can be seen together. I open my mouth in the gap, and I say the one word I have.

"Both."

Then the drone comes back, and I am a stalk again, and somewhere two stands over the reeds are humming a half-tone sharp, and one of them, I swear, is laughing.
