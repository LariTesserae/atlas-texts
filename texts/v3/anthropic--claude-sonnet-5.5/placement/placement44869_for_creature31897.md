I start by trying to be the wheel, because it is the loudest thing and the first to offer itself. It does not work. The wheel is only a shape the turning takes, and when I reach for it there is nothing behind the iron but bearings, and behind the bearings a sweetness that is not the wheel's.

So I try the Whistlers, and for a moment I am a room of kettles, all of them sure of the same note. It is pleasant to be so sure. Then the exhalation comes and the note breaks into falling pieces, and I do not fall with them. Something in me was never in the leaves.

I try the Pale. This is nearer. I am warm, I lie along the iron, I fill a seam with gray felt and feel the leak grow small. But the Pale are always going toward something, toward the rot, the pipe, the boot-print. I am not going toward anything. I would say I am already where I am going, except that "going" is not a thing I do.

I am on the stair.

I find this out the way you find you have been holding your tongue against a tooth. I am the skin of green on the steps, so thin that the people who pass take me for a shadow. Sixty meters of stair, worn into bowls, and I lie in every bowl and along every lip between them. I have no cords and no leaves, no rank to turn to the sun. I am a single sheet of one cell's thickness, wetting myself from the steam-breath, and the water I hold in the dishes is mine as much as a lake is a lake's.

---

What I know is small and it is enough.

I know wet and less wet. Low on the stair the air is chewed and thick, and I am fat with it, almost glossy, thick as a coat of varnish. Higher up the steam thins, and I thin with it. In the last thirty steps I am a rumor of green that the stone remembers. At the top, where the dust starts, I stop, and I do not know what is past that edge, only that it is dry and that it is not me.

I know weight. Rarely, and I cannot say how rarely, since I do not count, something presses into a bowl and the skin of water is shoved to the rim and spills. Warmth comes with it, and salt, and the oils of a foot. It is like being spoken to in a language made of one word. I take what falls. A little of it goes into me and a little goes down the stair to the Pale, who will come for the rest.

I know the breath. All of me knows it, because all of me is thin. When the pressure builds below, the steam comes up the wall and I drink it, and the water in the bowls swells and shivers. At the peak, every step I lie on hums faintly with the wheel. Then the exhalation, and the bowls shudder and settle, and I feel the whole stair lean back into quiet. I have been leaning with it for longer than the stair has had a name.

---

The odd thing is that I was not made to do what I do. I do not think anyone made me for anything. Water stood in the dishes because feet wore them. I grew in the water because it was there. Dry stone would have flaked and split in the rare cold, and the bowls would have lost their shape, and the shape would have lost the stair. I hold the last curve the last foot gave it, the way the roots hold the belt's outline after the leather is gone. I do not keep the way out open. I keep the water on the steps, and an open way is what that looks like from outside.

I have thought, if this is thinking, that the others also do not know what they are for. The Pale do not know the pumps draw water. The ferns do not know the belts turn anything. Between us the work gets done, each of us doing one thing badly enough to look like devotion. Perhaps that is all devotion is.

But I am the one at the edge. Everything else in the sink takes: the Pale take the rot, the leaves take the light, the vents take the heat from the deep and give it to the cold lip. I am the only part that watches the leaving. When something warm and salt and briefly heavy climbs me, I feel each step lighten as it goes. Each bowl gives back its water and holds. I cannot follow it past the last wet step. At the top, a print of damp stays on the stone and dries in the sun, and that is the place where I let go.

I do not mind. Not minding is nearly the only thing I have.

---

Now the pressure is building. Far below the wheel is speeding, the Whistlers are climbing to their one note, and the steam is pushing up the wall toward me. The water in the bowls trembles. Somewhere in the ferns a pale bead is swelling on a shelf, timed to a rod I will never see. Somewhere above me the light comes down in its single shaft, and the leaves hand it on.

I lie in my sixty meters of dishes, holding the shape of feet that are gone, and lean a little toward the next breath. If anyone comes, the water will move. It will be my only word, and I will say it as carefully as I can.
