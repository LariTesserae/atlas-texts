I find myself on the second terrace, a little before the morning thaw, sitting on a stool whose seat has been worn to a dip by people whose names I never learned. The dip fits me. I don't think it was made for me, but it fits.

A ledger is open on my knees. My cup is between my hands, gone cold, and I hold it anyway. In front of me is a patch of granite with the too-even sheen, and it is silent.

I think I am a log-keeper. It is the first thing I reach for, and it feels like the truest, so I'll say it and see whether it holds.

*

The work is mostly waiting. You sit by the wall in the freeze while the rock ticks as it contracts, and the drips come slow, and you write down only what you can swear to.

*North patch, low. Silent, day ten. Frost on the membrane thicker over the east seam than the west. The finch in the third tower has not left its gap.*

The finch is not part of the patch. I write it down anyway, because the rule I've learned, or inherited, or been quietly handed with a cup of warm water, is that you don't decide in advance what belongs. You write the finch, and the frost, and how your own hands felt, and later a pattern may show through or it may not. If it does, you don't say so. You close the ledger and go down the stairs, and your feet find the dips.

That's the first version of who I am: someone who looks closely and does not explain.

*

But sitting here in the hour when the membrane begins to clear, I notice the first version is too tidy. It makes me sound like a witness standing outside the thing, and I don't think anyone here stands outside.

The thaw begins. The ice on the terraces lets go in sharp pings, one, then three, then a scatter like a handful of pins dropped on glass. The curtain-hands above me draw the mesh wide. Light comes through the membrane cleaned of its heat, and the raw fracture-faces of the wall go from grey to a hard gold. I am humming. I only just notice it. It's low, and it sits on the note the mesh will make this afternoon, though the mesh hasn't sung yet. I don't know how I know the note. I never learned it, and I have never not known it.

So the second version: I am something that has been fitted to this place, the way the stair is fitted to the foot. Not the foot that made the stair, but the foot the stair now makes.

*

I wonder, then, whether I came in over the saddle.

I don't remember a saddle. I remember warm water. Someone put a cup into my hands and filled it from the channel nearest their door, and that said everything. I remember the second night, and a woman's low voice saying that the way in is the way you are known by, and that it has never gone badly when the rule was kept.

Maybe I went out. Maybe I stood on the ridge in the thin cold, on the scree that hisses, in a light so ordinary and blue that it felt like being lied to, and looked back at a slope with no seam in it, only pale planes with edges that could open a palm. And maybe I turned around and walked in again, because the crack is exactly the kind of thing that shows itself to someone who knows where it is.

Or maybe that isn't a memory. Maybe it is the ledger, and I've been writing myself into it so long that the entries have started to feel like recollection.

*

The patch hums.

It's very small at first, a pressure in the teeth. Then the warmth comes up under my palm, though I don't remember putting my hand there. It is a note lower than the last time. I write:

*North patch, low. Day ten, first hour of thaw. Sound returned, a note lower than before. No tremor. Nobody has visited.*

I look at the last line. Then I cross it out and write instead: *One person has visited.*

I don't strike that one. It sits there in the ledger like a stone in a stream, and I let it.

*

The visitor comes down out of the rock while I am still deciding what I have just done.

I don't hear it. I look up and it is there at the weeping wall, white and long-eared and lean, its tongue working at a stain of salt. Two children on the terrace below have gone rigid and are studying their own feet with great attention. I do the same, for a moment. Then, because I keep the ledger and the ledger is honest, I look.

Its ears are tilted toward the patch. Toward my patch. Or toward me sitting in front of it, the two of us so close in the raw light that a person watching from the bridge could not have said which was the slightly warmer part of the granite.

That is the third version, and I don't like it as much, because it will not stay still. If the visitor listens toward the wall, and the wall hums toward my hand, and my hand writes down the hum, then the ledger is not a record. It is a joint in the chain. The quiet work speaks through the patch, the patch through the log-keeper, the log-keeper through the ink, and the ink to whoever reads next. Or the quiet work does none of these things, and I am a lonely animal with a pen, hearing my own blood in my ears and calling it the mountain.

I hold both. I have learned that you can. Most people here hold both most of the time, and the hour after a tremor is only when the balance tips one way.

*

The visitor finishes with the salt and looks at me. I can't tell you what is in its face. I have no word, and I suspect the word wouldn't be kind to it.

Then it goes, up through a gap in the rock that I would swear was not there a moment ago, and the children exhale, and the mesh begins, thinly, to gather the first cloud. Somewhere the chord starts. I find its pitch without trying, and my hum settles into it like a foot into a footprint.

I close the ledger. I stand. I don't quite know what I am. I am a keeper, a fitted thing, a place where the wall is heard. I am a stranger who left by the saddle and came back the same way. I am a small warm part of a small warm city that is held inside a mountain that is rising a finger's width per century.

I go down the stairs. My feet find the dips that other feet have left, and I don't ask whose they were. The pools below are black and just above freezing, and one of them is steaming two hands higher than it has any right to.

I will write that down. I will not say why.
