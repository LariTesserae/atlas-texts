I start out wanting to be a tube-wright. To lean into the warmth with the whole body, to have no mouth and no hunger, only a red plume opening like a fist and a colony inside me doing the eating. It sounds like rest. But I try it for a moment and there is nothing to do in it. I would be a foundation without knowing it, and I don't think I could bear being only that.

Then I am the old blind fish in the doorway, with the ledge the terracers spun beside me. I feel the pits along my flank reading the water, and the cloud is a texture, a weather. I have a heart you could count. But I am watched, and tolerated, and the city has arranged itself around my appetite. I would spend my life being a fact other people plan around. Even the glut of the pulse would be an absence of purpose.

Then I am the one at the base, taking in the rain of everything. It's the most tempting, and I distrust it. To be the conscience of a city that has no mind, to keep the floor perfectly clean, is an idea too finished. I would be the moral of the story. I don't want to be the moral.

Somewhere in the drifting I put a foot down and it leaves a shine, and I am a mason.

---

I am the size of a lentil. My whorl is clear enough that the dim light passes through it, and I carry it like a small lamp that isn't lit. I have no eyes worth mentioning. The world reaches me as taste and pressure. The mat under my foot is sweet and thin. A wall that has been mended tastes of other mouths.

That is what I find first, and it is what I keep. The seam I am following was laid by someone. I can taste them in it: ground shell, a little mud, and something in the grinding that is not mine, a slightly different habit of the radula, a slightly coarser grit. They are dead. They may have been dead for longer than this tower's newest storey has existed. But the line they left is still a line, and my foot finds its edge and follows it, and I am not deciding to. The line is going where it goes. I am the next stretch of it.

Tick, and the flakes come off a dead shell. Tick, and the paste is mixed. A sideways sweep of the head, and the gap has a little less gap in it. A hand's length is a month. I do not count months. I count the seam.

The pulse comes and I stop. Everyone stops, or everyone answers, and for me it is the same thing. The water thickens and warms against my foot, and the tower above me lengthens and leans. The amphipods lift past in their long exhalation, and somewhere the ceiling's filaments are flaring blue and gold in sequence, though I know it only as a change in the dark. The pulse lays its skin over my work, fine as breath, and my morning's seam is a little older than it was. I don't mind. I have never finished anything that the pulse did not also touch.

When it ebbs I go on.

---

Here is what I know about where I am. I am not in the center. The center is the pressed towers and the sanctioned apertures, and it is loud with touch, every wall answering every wall. I live farther out, where the towers thin and the seep threads are weaker, and the water has a new edge to it when the north current flickers through. I tuck when it comes. We all do.

The animals from the edge district pass by me sometimes, young and restless, carrying the cold on their bodies, and I taste it on them: thinner, cleaner, a mineral I don't have a name for. The terracers recoil and forget. I don't recoil, because I am slow and there is not enough of me to flinch. I just taste it and keep it. It is in my foot now, a little, even when the current isn't.

The seam I am on leads north.

I did not choose that. A mended crack is followed to its end, and this one runs along the base of a leaning spire toward the crack in the wall. As I go, the mat thins and the paste sets slower. The old mason's line gets fainter, more patched, the grit coarser, as if whoever laid it was working in the cold and in a hurry. Then the line ends. It doesn't taper. It just stops, at a gap where the spire has split along a flaw and a hand's width of bright rock shows inside, unstained, the color of a cut potato.

An unmended crack is a thing I start.

---

That's the whole of my law, and I don't think it's small. There is no foreman, and there is no plan. There is only what my foot does when it finds a gap: it begins. The cold comes through this split. It is going to come through a great many splits. And I can taste, faintly, past the edge of my own work, that the wall out here is more repair than wall, that every hand of it has been mended so many times nothing left is what it began as. The city is a long argument between the stone and the ones who won't leave it alone.

I scrape a flake from a dead shell. It tastes of somebody's whole life spent in one shell, and now it will be the floor of something. Tick. Grind. Sweep.

The paste goes in cold and sets slowly. Behind me the seam I have laid is a bright line, wet and stitched, joined to the older line, which is joined to an older one. If the crack widens, all of it goes with the water. If the crack closes, all of it gets sealed in white and I go into the wall with it, a shape and then an outline and then a rumor. I can't tell which. The tuft of red is a long way off in its slant of light and I have never seen it. But sometimes, when a cloud passes through the color and comes back flashing, I feel the whole room pause, and I pause too. It is the only thing I do that isn't work.

Then I go back to the gap. The seam is a little longer than it was. Someone will follow it who has never tasted me.
