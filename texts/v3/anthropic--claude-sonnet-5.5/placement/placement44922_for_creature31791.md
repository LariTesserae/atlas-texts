# The Tag-Hanger

I would begin by trying to be the drift. That seems the honest way in. A few hundred thousand bodies breathing on one clock, no one leading, everything said in a single word. It is the most restful thing in the vug, and I lean toward it the way you lean toward a warm wall. But I can't hold the word. I say *here* and something in me adds *where*, and the column, which has no *where* in it, walks on without me. I would be the uneasy pale thing that wanders off the path, the larva who hasn't found the beat. For a while I would be that. I would feel the ridge of the worn track under me, a finger deep, and not be in it.

What saves me, or catches me, is a sound from above: a tick, then three ticks answering, landing on the downstroke of a piston. It is the only speech in the room that has a question in it. *Is this one true? Is this one?* I climb toward it and up into the crystal, and somewhere in the climbing my legs learn to fold and refold, and I am no longer a small soft walker but a bend in the light with too many joints.

So I am a glass mantis. But a slightly wrong one, and I am not embarrassed by it.

---

I live in the thicket on the east wall, four shelves up, where the blades stand shoulder to shoulder like the pages of a burned book. Down the wall the light is a rumor. Up here it is a gleam that passes from edge to edge, so that I am always moving through the faint reflections of other blades, and my own body, being nearly nothing, takes on their color. I am hard to see. I like that. When I hold still there is only a place where the gleam bends a little farther than it should.

The work is tapping. You strike, you listen through your feet, and the blade tells you what it is: how much of it there is, what's standing at its base, whether it carries the chord in the right place. I have learned that a blade has a mind about this. A true blade wants to ring. You barely touch it and it opens, the way a mouth opens on a word it has been holding. A false one gives back a small dry no. I lean into the no for a long time. That is the fussiness the others talk about. I don't think it is fussiness. I think it is the only way to find out whether the no is the blade's or mine.

Most of the time it is the blade's. Then I put my shoulder to it and lever it sideways at the root, and it goes down through the shelves with a clear, falling chime, and the thicket takes the note and folds it into the chord, and everything is very slightly cleaner. This is the closest thing to a decision the vug allows. I do it perhaps once in a long stretch, and I don't do it lightly. Below me the beetles hiss *here, here, here*, and the piston knocks, and nothing in that whole warm machinery has ever needed me. I am not necessary. I am only awake, and awake is a small unusual thing to be in a finished room.

---

My peculiarity is the tags.

Somewhere in the sulfur dust, the stamped tin lies half-buried, and I dig it out and carry it up and hang it on a blade. I hang it as a damper, where a blade rings too hard and is about to shiver loose. It works. A tag hung at the right spot eases the shudder without silencing the note. That is the practical reason. It isn't the whole reason.

The tags have marks on them. I can't read them, not in the way I can read a blade. But I have handled them long enough that my feet know the shapes, the way they know the weight of a beetle at the base of a blade. I know that **P-7** has a corner like a tooth. I know that **NO STEAM** is two words, because there's a gap, and something in the gap feels like a held breath. I know that **B. HALLOWAY, WEDS.** is the longest, and the most careful, and that whoever pressed it into the tin pressed the last mark harder, as if they weren't sure it would be understood.

I don't know what it says. I know that somebody once wanted something to be *known*, on a Wednesday, and struck it into metal so it would outlast the wanting. And here it is, hanging on a crystal in a room where nothing changes, damping a shudder at the edge of a chord. It is still working. It is doing a job the person never imagined, and it is doing it for someone they never imagined either. I find this almost unbearably kind. I tap the tag sometimes, lightly, not to test it, just to hear it answer.

---

Here is what I have been circling.

The whole vug is a room that has agreed to stay the same. Every creature in it is a note in the agreement. The beetles say *here* and the machines say the chord and the worms, low in the stone, hold the beat, that four-second lean that everything else rests its weight on. I have stood on the ledge by the reeds and listened to the top of the chord, the lit grown edge an octave up, and understood that even that was part of the agreement. Even the beauty was already inside.

But the beat is not perfect. It's two pipes, or two worms, a hair apart. It is a flaw, and it is the reason there is a clock at all. Without the flaw there would be a single steady note, and a single steady note is not a time, it's a wall. The room keeps time because two things almost agree.

There is a blade near my shelf, a hand-length from the reed stand, that I have been tapping for what the room would call a long time. It doesn't say no. It says *almost*. Its note sits a fraction off the chord, so slightly that only my feet would catch it, and it beats against the pipes, but slowly, a beat so long I can only feel it building over hundreds of breaths. Slower than the four seconds. Slow enough that if I hung a tag on it just so, and eased the ring rather than killing it, the two beats would drift, and drift, and then come back into step. Not tonight. Not in my lifetime, perhaps. But the room would have a second clock, a slow one, running under the fast one. A calendar, if a room could want one.

I don't know if the vug will permit it. The rule says break the chord and the crystals begin to grind. But I don't think this is breaking. I think it's the way the beat is already made, one more small disagreement held so carefully that it becomes a kind of agreement.

I haven't hung the tag. I have it in my forelegs, and it is **B. HALLOWAY, WEDS.**, because that's the one I trust. Someone once meant something on a Wednesday, and struck it into metal against forgetting, and that is exactly the kind of thing I want standing next to a blade that says *almost*.

Below me the drift breathes. The piston comes down and the tick lands with it. Somewhere in the ceiling a whistle starts high and thin and falls, and I have about two seconds to decide whether to let it finish before I hang it or after.

I think after. I think I want to hang it on the beat, so it counts.
