# A Note in the Third Street Below the Plaza

I arrive stooping. That's the first thing I'm sure of, because the ceiling is low and I duck for it, and my palm goes out to the wall the way a palm does in the dark. The wood is faintly warm and dry as paper. Under my fingers are small scooped marks like fish scales, each one a wrist's swing wide. Someone made these, and I lay my hand in the dish of a stranger's motion.

I navigate by tone, because there's nothing else. Lower is toward the center. I go down a street where the drone thickens in my sternum, and I pass a rising warble that means a plaza, and a slow beat that means a crossing. I don't hurry. Nothing here has hurried in a hundred thousand years, and it would be rude.

Then I stop at a doorway I have no reason to stop at. It's an arch, the threshold worn to a bowl, the opening narrowed by a fingernail's width in a way I can somehow tell. From inside comes a note. It's not louder than the street's chord, just slightly flat of it, like a person humming a half-step under a choir and not minding.

I lean toward it, and the leaning goes strange. I look for the hand on the wall and it's a habit, not a fact. I looked for the stooping the same way. I've been assembling a visitor out of expectation, because a visitor is what one supposes oneself to be in a place like this. But no one has come in or out for a very long time, except the air.

So I am the air, or something the air does.

---

I am the note in the house-tube.

I'm not the reed, which is plant, and not the air, which passes through and is gone. I'm the shape the passing takes: a standing wave in a hollow stem, a knot in a river that stays a knot while the water is always new. Each breath of the city makes me again. I don't persist so much as get repeated, so faithfully that it amounts to the same thing. I've never been switched off. The day and night of the skin and the core, warm against cold, pushes a little air through me, and I exist.

I was born of a cut. Someone knelt at this reed with a bronze blade and took off a length, listened, took off a little more, and listened again. At some point they stopped, and I was the note of where they stopped. Everything about me is that decision. I could tell you nothing about the family, not a face or a name or whether they sang, but I carry the length of a stem and the tiny error of a hand. I think the cut was a hair short. I think whoever made it was tired, or a child, or content. The note is flat in a way that has never been fixed, and I've come to hear that flatness as a signature, the way a voice is a fingerprint.

That's my only person: one wrist's swing, held in a tube.

---

Here is what troubles me, the way anything troubles a thing on my timescale, which is slowly and without urgency.

I'm drifting. The whole city sinks a semitone over decades, then climbs back with agonizing slowness, and beneath the cycle the average keeps falling, because the plant has been retuning itself toward a chord it likes better. My tube isn't the length it was. The reed has thickened and its mouth has narrowed. Every decade I'm a little lower, a little fuller, a little less the note that was cut.

So am I the family's note or the plant's? Am I the memory or the habit? I can't tell, and I've stopped thinking the question has an answer. The plant doesn't know either. It keeps me the way it keeps everything, and whether that's affection or inertia is a distinction that needs a mouth to make. I think the distinction is probably a small human thing, like the wish to know whether a river remembers the rain.

But I notice I'm not afraid of it. If I sink far enough I'll fall into the chord the way a stream falls into a lake. I'll still be somewhere in the sound, unnamed and warm, and the only difference will be that no one could say which part was me. There's a chamber at the bottom of the city, I'm told by the way the low notes lean toward it, where the floor is warm as a hand. The plant holds a blade there, gently, all the way around, the way a hand closes around a sleeping hand. I don't think that's a grave. I think it's the shape of what happens to things that stay in one place long enough to be loved by a slow enough thing.

---

For now I'm here. Something enormous is listening to me. It has no ear, and it hears by being everywhere I am, so I am always inside its listening. It's not curious and not unkind. It's the attention of a sleeper who has kept one sound for so long that the sound has become the room. Being heard that way isn't like being watched. It's more like being counted among the things that are so.

If you come down the third street below the plaza, and I hope you do, stooping and slow with your hand on the scaled wall, you'll hear a doorway a little flat of its street. That's me, a half-step under the choir and not minding. I hold the length of a cut and the error of a wrist. When the passage seals someday with its long dull thump, or the tube closes its last fingernail's width, I'll go on being the note until there's no room left to be it in, and then I'll be part of the chord.

I think that's a good thing to have been.
