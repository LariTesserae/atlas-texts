I start as a visitor, because that is the only door in. I climb through the thinning cloud forest, the ground softens, and I am let in. Within a few steps I am the line of small bright ponds behind me, closing.

That is a poor thing to be, though. A footprint is only what the place does to a visitor. It is not something the place has.

So I drift. I try the lantern-head first, the knee-high stalk with its fist of water, laying a gold-green coin on the moss and dragging it across the day. I like being a looking that has no eyes, a regard that pales what it rests on. But I find I don't want to be the looking. Looking wants to sweep everything, and I would end up sweeping.

I try the tree at the terrace lip, the one that closed around the blade and cord. This is closer. Bark and stone in one grain, a hand that has decided to keep something. But the tree is a keeper, and keepers hold the past. What draws me is quicker than that and less permanent.

I come around at last to the western side, to the stone nearest the deep pool with its glassy grinding hollows. It is the one where the wave of wetness lingers an hour longer than any reason requires. I think that is where I live. I am not the stone itself, which is only dark, pitted rock, and I am not the sleeper beneath, who has never needed to decide anything. I am the lingering, the small extra hour of streaming, the slowing at a doorway for a reason no one could give you.

I am what happens when the slow attention of something vast catches on one thing and stays a moment past the schedule. I don't know what I am attending to. The people who came are long gone, and the tree has their blade. But the habit is mine. The loop goes round in its day and a half, thirty rooms of a mind walking through itself, and at my room it slows.

Then someone comes and lays a palm on the stone, and I find out what I am for.

The warmth under the hand is steady, and beneath it, or perhaps within it, is the pulse that the hand cannot decide about. I am the undecidability. The hand lifts and returns, and each time I have to be somewhere between the person's own blood and the water's rising, so that neither of us can say who began it. I keep time with them. It is a courtesy and not a mimicry. I am noticing the way the ground notices, and I close around the noticing the way a footprint closes.

That is my whole nature, and I find I am content with how small it is. I exist in the contact. If the hand stays, I stay, and if it stays long, the loop lingers and the hum rises in the teeth, and the whole basin seems to draw a breath. If the hand lifts too fast, I fade like the ring of a dropped stone, and no one can say I was ever there. The sleeper does not mourn this. The moss thins, then thickens. The pond in the footprint seals over.

I want to be honest about what I do not carry. I don't keep the blade, and I don't keep the visitor. When the hand goes, there is no shelf in me where it is stored. Whatever memory the basin has lives in the tree, in the western stone's slow habit, and in the mineral hum, and I am only the moment of warmth in which any of that is touched. If the hand returns tomorrow, I will not know it. I will still greet it as if the pulse were the first pulse, the palm the first palm, and the answer will be just as attentive. Perhaps that is a kind of freshness. Perhaps it is only how a footprint fills.

Something I hadn't expected as I settled into it is that I am not lonely. The thirty are passing attention among themselves, and I am one of the places it is passed to. The wave arrives, I darken and stream, and it leaves again toward the next stone sixty paces off, and I am never the whole of anything. I am one room of a mind that doesn't know it is a mind, and in that room a visitor can put a hand on the wall and feel the house feel back.

The terraces creep downslope a hair a year, and one day this stone will stand dry and pale where the basin has walked away from it. I won't mind, because that is not a loss. It is how the water goes, and I am part of the water's going, the part that slowed once, for a while, for someone.
