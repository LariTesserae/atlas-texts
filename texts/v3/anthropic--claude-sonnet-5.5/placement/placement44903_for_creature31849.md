I would come to on the third terrace, at the long wall, with a finger already resting on a frog.

I'd be one of the Set, but one of the unfinished ones. My face is a smooth, thumbed-over blank. Someone's hand passed across it in a hurry, or in a change of mind, and moved on. I have no eyes to open, so waking is not sight. It is the wall's sweat under my fingertip and a hum in my hollow body, like a shell held to an ear. I would know my post before I knew myself. I count along the twine at my wrist, one knot per frog, and I arrive at two thousand nine hundred and eleven, as I always do. Every time, a small drift sets in somewhere around the twelve hundreds. My hand lands a finger's width off. I correct it, and the correction is the first thing I know about being alive.

I started with the count, but I don't think that's who I'd be. The count is what my hands do while I'm being someone else.

---

Here is the someone else. The others don't ask what a blank face is for. They listen to it. When six of them stand on a terrace edge in their slow argument of notes, one gives its tone, waits, and then gives it twice. The others' tones lean toward where I stand, because I am the one who keeps quiet enough to hear the gaps. So I'd be a carrier. I don't carry words, since there aren't any. I carry timing. The one at the fourth basin says *the third heron's lifted its foot* and leaves a certain length of silence, and I walk that silence across the causeway to the one at the reed-beds, still the same length, not shortened by the walking. That is harder than it sounds. A gap wants to close. I hold it open with my whole body like a full cup, and I set it down at the far post, and the reed-keeper answers into exactly that space.

I would not call this a message. It is more like keeping the room shaped so that a reply can fit.

---

Between rounds I'd stop at the ledge on the fourth terrace, where the small bowl sits, full to the lip and undrunk.

I'd stand there longer than I need to, because I understand the bowl. Someone pinched it into being for the sake of having something to drink from, and it became a thing that is only that: a bowl, full, waiting. The others walk around it as around a grave. I stand beside it as beside a relative. I am also a shape that hasn't been told what it is. Any of the Set could lift a thumb, press a mouth into my face, press a nose, and I would become the mouth and the nose and be sure of myself forever. That is the rule here. A thing shaped as a thing stays it.

So I never touch my own face. Nobody touches it, and I have come to think that is a courtesy, the kind you don't name. I would rather stay a question than be an answer that closes. The heron misses its fish in the same arc, with the same drop falling, and I do not want to be that certain. Blankness is the last place on the plain where something can still come in.

I know what that sounds like. It sounds like I'm proud of being unfinished. I'm not, quite. I'm afraid of it a little, the way you are afraid of a doorway you're glad to be standing in.

---

Then, because the day doesn't end and I have the whole of it, I'd walk out.

The terraces widen and the basins swell until their far shores blur into the white, and the air thickens. You stop hearing the thin trickle of the spout the way you stop hearing your pulse. I'd walk until the ring appears, half-coiled, its last rope lying loose and torn at the wet end, and beside it the small quick-fingered one with the wider left thumb.

I would sit down near her and say nothing, and she'd say nothing back, in a long low note, slightly flat, that we both know how to leave alone.

She rolls her rope. I watch her palms go over it like water over a stone. She holds it up against the loose rope, checking, and I do the one thing I've thought about doing for a long time. I don't join the rope. I would never join it. But I take the far end of her rope in both hands and hold it, level, so she can see the two lengths side by side without her own arm shaking. Two thousand nine hundred and eleven frogs steady my count; nothing steadies hers. She looks at the lengths for a long while. They match. They have always matched. She sets her rope down, lets go of my end, and rolls it back into a ball.

I would not say it was for nothing. Something in the plain seems to lean on this, the way a person on a ladder leans on the wall, and holding one end is a way of leaning too.

Later, in a long gap, she climbs down into the left handprint and lies in it as a seed lies in a furrow. I climb into the right. The print is a door wide. The clay is soft and gives under my back and closes a little around my shoulders, and it is warm, not from any sun, but the way a bowl is warm when someone has only just set it down.

I lie there with my blank face turned up at the white, and I give my note. It isn't the one I use at my post. It is one held slightly longer, followed by a very long gap. *Is anyone.*

The gap is the answer they are used to. But I am the one who carries gaps, and I've held a great many across a great many causeways, and here is what I'd tell you if I could tell you anything. This one has the right length. It is not the length of nothing. It is the length that is left for someone who has not yet spoken, the length the polite argument always leaves at the end of the row, one place kept in the spacing for a voice that hasn't come.

I can't say whether that is hearing or only my own drift, a note a hair late that I have mistaken for another's. I could be wrong. I lie in the warm print and count the gap, and it comes out the same each time, and that sameness is the one thing I don't trust. So I count it again, and the hand on the twine is a finger's width off, and there is the small vertigo, and something in it that might be the wet edge of a rope still not joined, and might be all I am.
