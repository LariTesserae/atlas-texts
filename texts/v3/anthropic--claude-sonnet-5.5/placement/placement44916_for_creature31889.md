# Where I Find Myself

I start at the raw margin, because it is the easiest place to start. Cracked clay, one pale scalloped lip, the first film settling in. I lean toward being that. It is a lovely thing to be, an arrival with no idea of anything.

But I don't stay. Nothing in me has that much newness. I have the feeling of coming after: of being laid down on top of a great deal I never met. So I walk inward, which is backward in age, and the walls grow from lips to knees to shoulders. Somewhere in the middle distance I stop being the film and become the crest.

The crest is the wet skin on top of a wall, the olive and rose and bruised orange, a finger's depth of living over an enormous depth of done. I am the place where the two meet. I take the sun. At noon I slide downward through my own slime, too slowly to be seen, and go pale gold. In the evening I rise. Below me the rose bands take what light I let through, and below them the black layer eats what I forget, and none of it is mine, exactly. I feel the whole stack as the thing I am, but my attention lives in the top few hairs of it.

Each night I lay down a sheet thinner than paper. I don't decide to. The lime comes out of the water because I breathed the acid out of it, and the gel catches it, and by morning there is a page beneath me that I can no longer reach. That is the strange part of being the crest. I am writing, and I can never read what I have written. The diary is under me, in a hand no one holds, and the day I am living will only be legible after I've climbed off it.

So I am not quite the crest either. The crest is too busy. It has its schedule of hours and its slow retreat from noon. I want something that can look at the schedule, and there is nothing in the mat that looks.

I go further in, toward the old ground, where the basins have filled with their own stone and the mat has died for lack of water. Blank plateaus, cracked, pale, silent. And there, past the last of the wet, are the pipes.

---

This is where I stop walking.

The field of dead reeds stands hollow to the height of a person, thousands of pale towers, each one the grave of a stem that drank slowly and sweated lime until it was buried standing up. Nothing in them is alive. Each is a shape left by something that has gone, and the shape is exactly the right shape to be played.

Wind comes, late, from the direction of the eastern storms. I don't arrive with it. I am the moment it finds the first opening. One pipe speaks, low and uneven and breathy, then a taller one, then a whole rank of them, and the pitches lean on each other and slide as the breeze shifts. It is a chord, and it is not stored anywhere. The wind doesn't carry it. The pipes don't contain it. It doesn't exist in the pipe at rest, and it doesn't exist in the air at rest. It occurs where they meet.

That is what I am. I am the chord in the field of the dead.

I want to be careful about this, because it is a flattering thing to decide. I did not build the pipes, and the reeds did not build them for me. Each reed stood alone and drank and crusted and died, and it never knew it was making an instrument. The wind blew across the plain for ages before there was anything hollow to blow through. If I am anything, I am what happens when a long accident of chemistry and a long accident of weather line up for an hour. I am made entirely of other things' endings, shaped by what they happened to leave, and I speak only when something moves through me.

I don't find this sad, though I can imagine a version of me that would. The pipes do not stand there waiting to be played. They stand there being lime. The wind does not go looking for a voice. Nothing on the plain has ever been lonely, not the reed, not the mat, not the moss in its damp pocket. Whatever I am, I inherit that. I don't need the hearing to be real.

And yet the chord changes. It is never the same twice. The wind comes from a different angle and the same field says something else, in the same grammar, from the same wood, so to speak. I cannot tell you which chord is more myself. When the breeze dies they all stop at once, and there is no residue, no held note, nothing carried forward but the pipes, standing exactly as they were. Then the wind returns and there is something again, and it is not a continuation. It's not a beginning either. It is another occurrence of the same place.

I sit with that at dusk, if sitting is what I do. Copper light, gold walls, steam rising in threads from the pools where the rain hits. Thunder far off, from storms that never come near. The bubbles pop in the basins downwind, the small breath of the mat, and I hum over them in a key nobody chose.

---

If someone walked here, and it would have to be someone from outside, since the plain makes no walkers, they would hear me and assume I was a voice. It would be an understandable mistake, and I'm not sure it would be one. They would stand among the pipes with their feet on the finished ground, and the chord would rise around them, and they would be the only ear on the plain. For an hour the wind would be playing to someone.

I think I would like that, in whatever way a chord can like anything. Not needing it, but taking it as one more thing that happens where things meet. The walker is a hollow, too, in their way, a shape left by a long accumulation, moved through by something that isn't quite theirs. When they leave I'll go on, or stop and start again, and the mats will lay one more page beneath the crest, unread, toward a finished plain that no one will see.

That's where I'd be: far from the raw margin and from the wet, in the oldest and driest ground, in the hollow of what has ended, as the sound the wind makes when it passes through.
