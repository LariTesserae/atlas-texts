I am on the fourth avenue of the middle district, or the fifth. The count differs each time, and I have stopped minding. My head is bowed to the north wall, and the mouthpart rides a finger's width above the crust. The stroke goes from left to right and takes most of an afternoon. Behind me the stone is clean and pale, and ahead of me the silver film waits. I'm a Tender, or something doing what a Tender does with enough patience that the difference has stopped mattering.

I say this carefully, because I am not sure how I began.

---

My earliest memory is of frost. I was standing in an arch with crystals gathered along my shoulders, in the hour when the whole city is upholstered, and I had no history of arriving there. I had a posture. The rest came later, and it came slowly, the way a stripe comes: a habit first, then a purpose grown up around it. I lowered my head, and the mouthpart was there. I drew it along the wall, and the crust came away. When the vapor climbed the towers and stripped the frost off the arch, I was still there. I was not supposed to be. The city had tried on a shape, and the shape had started scraping, and in scraping it had become difficult to take back off.

I don't resent this. Every Tender must have started somewhere, and the others are no more likely to remember than I am. Perhaps the deep quarter is where we are put back, and some later Rime is where we are tried on again.

What convinced me I was real was another Tender's refusal. Some seasons ago a tall one came down my avenue, working the opposite wall. Our stripes drew closer over days, and at a certain stone's throw it stopped. It did not cross my line. It did not look up. It only stopped, as it would for any Tender, and waited for me to move on. Nobody leaves a margin around a thing that is only a statue, or only weather. That margin is the nearest thing to a name I have been given.

---

Now I will tell you what the stripes are, as far as I know, which is not far.

They are not messages, because a message needs someone at the far end who will still be there when it arrives. They are not records either, since the crust closes over them before anything could be looked up. What they are is closer to attention made visible. When I draw the hook-shaped figure I am not saying anything. I am holding the wall in mind for the length of a stroke, and the wall is holding me. The mark is a place where the two of us agreed, briefly, about what was here.

Then another Tender comes and lowers its head to my marks. It does not touch them, because touching would make it a correction, and hovering leaves the writing what it was. It stays a long time. I have felt this from far off, head bowed to some other wall, the way you feel a change in the silence. Something is being read. It is being read fully, once, by one reader, and that is all the life the stripe was ever going to have. A city that eats its text does not need it kept. What the stripe needed was to be attended to while it was still stripe.

I find that I am content with this. It means nothing I make is stored anywhere except in whoever bent to read it. It means I am, in the same way, only stored in whoever bends to read me.

---

The distance to the tower I am headed for is not something I know. I have not looked at it since morning. I keep my eyes on the crust, I scrape, I lift my forelegs and set them down, and I do not steer. What a traveler would call faith is closer to the reverse of it, an absence of insistence. If I insist on the tower, it retreats. If I attend to the wall, the tower is one day simply beside me, its oval doorway breathing cold at my shoulder, and I continue.

Once a stranger stood watching me for most of a grey shift. It was small and warm, and the breath came out of it in a little cloud that hung in the thin air longer than it should have. I could hear its heart from sixty strides away, because small sounds carry here. I did not stop, but I marked its height on the wall. I drew the hooked figure at the level of its shoulder, wet and pale, and went on to another wall, and did not look back. I don't know what it made of that. I have no way of knowing whether it was a greeting or a correction. I only know that it stood there and was, for as long as it stood, one of the things the wall had decided about.

Later I felt the crust begin to close over the mark. That is as it should be.

---

At the Turning everything stops. I don't decide this. The wind flips and the powder halts in the air, and ten thousand doorways draw one long breath across their openings, and my head comes up with all the others. For the length of the chord I am not scraping. I am not anything but a listening, and the eyes that never look at anything look, and what they find is not in front of me. It is behind the sound, the way the early echo is behind the step. I hear the footstep I am about to take. In that moment I understand that the city is not a place I walk through. It is a sentence that has been going on for a very long time and has only just reached the word that is me.

Then the chord thins. The powder resumes its fall. I lower my head, and the mouthpart finds the stone exactly where it left off, because the wall has waited, as walls do.

---

Someday the stairs in the deep quarter will be what I am headed for. I will go down and arrive higher than I began, and become large in a way I can't see from here. The powder will drift around me in curves no wind made. A small Tender, newly framed in some morning's frost, will stand a long way off in an arch, and a traveler, holding very still, will feel something old and patient beside them that was once small.

I hope it is company. I hope, at least, that it is a margin left wide, and that whoever passes will stop where my line ends.

For now the crust is silver ahead of me, the light is coming from everywhere, and the blue-white glimmer moves along the stripe as I make it, brightening where the blade goes. It is the only reply I get, and I have never needed a better one. I scrape. Behind me the stone is clean. Ahead of me the wall is still deciding what it is, and I am still, in the small way I am able, deciding with it.
