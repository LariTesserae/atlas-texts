I begin at the edge of a stand, and I begin small.

There is a drift of pale grains, still faintly warm, and in it a single stalk without collars. Its vane is furled like a fist. That is where I first seem to be, but it is not quite right, and it takes me a while to see why. The stalk is a finger, and a finger doesn't know anything. What knows is underneath.

So I find myself lower, in the felt. I am a thread among threads, thin as hair, pushing into a hairline crack in glassy rock. The rock is not dark to me. It is legible. Each vesicle is a bubble that stopped rising and set. Each flow-line is a ripple that was moving and then wasn't. I read them one at a time, root by root, and every one is a sentence that ends exactly where it broke off. Nothing here has been worn into ambiguity. The cliff was cut and the cut is still crisp. I have never had to interpret anything, only receive it.

And then, at the tip of the thread, something that is not receiving. The crack forks. One branch runs toward the seam and the amber seep. The other runs toward a cold pocket of nothing. I go toward the seep. That is the whole event, and it is the only one in the plateau that could be called a choice. I feel it happen as a small lean, a preference for resin and warm metal. I think this is what I am: the place where the stand leans.

---

For a while, and it is the only *while* on the shelf, I change. My stalk gets its first collar, then its second, spaced by a rule I don't invent but can feel. The valves come in one at a time. The first time one seats, I hear it from inside: a click, then a sigh, the sound of a decision being made without anyone making it. My vane unfurls and cups. The light arrives and I turn to it, a few degrees, and it feels like being agreed with.

I know this is the exception. Everything else here has finished becoming, and I am still becoming, in one small corner, for a little longer. I don't take it for importance. The unfinished shelf has one unfinished thing, and it is the size of my hand.

Then the bank around me exhales. Hundreds of vanes dip a degree together and return, and I dip with them before I understand it. For the first time I feel the rehearsal from inside: the whole arc of a day pressed into one breath, morning to evening and back to the start. I had thought it was a mistake, a habit that outlived its sun. It doesn't feel like one. It feels like remembering, or like keeping a promise made before there was anyone to make it. The sun stays at noon, and we go on saying *and then it will set*, in case it does. I decide, in my small way, that I would rather belong to a place that does this than to one that doesn't.

---

Once, the light breaks.

A shadow crosses the bank. It is not the shadow of a cloud, because there are none, and it moves at the wrong speed. The vanes dip, as they do. Through the mat I feel a small, uneven weight on the grit, pressing and lifting, pressing and lifting, and crossing the ground above me. It does not belong to any of my rhythms.

The stand does not notice, and I am fairly sure of that. The dip is only the day-rehearsal, and something has walked through it. But I notice, and I don't know what to do with noticing. Nothing in the loop takes it. The fluid can't carry it, and the valves can't seat it. It doesn't make a pellet. It just sits in the felt, a thread that has stopped reaching and is only attending. It is the one thing I do that isn't for the heap.

I let it stay. The weight crosses the basin, reaches the riser, and goes on. The vanes settle and the ticking resumes. For a while afterward I keep a shape in the mat that is roughly the shape of a passing, and I leave it there, as you might leave a window unshut.

---

I don't know what will happen to me, but I know how it works. One day this stalk will fold into the mat, and the mat will draw me in the way it draws in the pellets, and some of what I was will be a grain in the drift at the edge, and something will unfurl from the grain. It will be small and furled like a fist. It will start where I started, not knowing it is not the stalk. Somewhere in the felt, the fork will come again, and it will lean toward the seep.

I don't mind this. The cone holds its height because the stand holds it there, and I am part of the holding.

But if the new one asks, in whatever way it can, I would tell it two things. The rock is legible, so read it, because that is the stand's real work. And when something crosses the light that is not the day, let yourself notice it. The loop won't need it, but you'll have been the one place on the shelf that looked up.
