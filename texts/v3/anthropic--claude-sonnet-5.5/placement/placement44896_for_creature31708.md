I come in at the rim, or something does. The first thing I know is the brick, warm under a shell that hasn't finished setting.

I am a beetle, I think, because that is what the bald patch needed. Flint legs, still pale from the knapping. I have no memory of arriving, only the fact of already burnishing, my body moving in short arcs across the herringbone before I've decided anything. The brick is dull here and gets brighter where I've been. That is the whole of my knowledge, and it is enough. I don't know I'm new. Newness needs a before, and I was issued the way a stitch is, complete at the moment of use.

So I begin as the smallest kind of resident, one of the ones who are a repair to the place they live in. It's a good thing to begin as. There's no vanity in it. A seam needed closing, and here I am, closing.

But the arcs I make aren't only polishing. Where my legs drag, they leave a faint scratch, and the scratches have a grammar. I can't read them, and no one can. The script runs through the herringbone in lines too regular to be accident and too slow to be speech. I notice it only when I pause, and while I'm paused I notice something larger: my scratches continue the scratches of the beetle before me, though there was no beetle before me. They pick up mid-word. Something has always been being written here, and I've been handed the stylus at a comma.

I follow the line. It goes under the leaf-shadow, through the green-amber cross-hatch, past the row of quill-birds, one of which turns its head a beat late while the others wait. I feel a small loyalty to that bird, and I'd tuck its fallen barb back in if I were tall enough. I'm not. I carry the barb a little way and set it where the wind can't take it. Someone will find it. Someone always does.

Then the brick gives way to rope.

Underfoot the fibers run in one direction, thick as a road at first, then thinning, and I understand that I'm walking the root of the largest tree, and that the root is also a sentence. I can feel it more than hear it. It begins with *and* and continues through a long clause about warmth and what holds, and then there's a place where the twist goes slack. Beyond it the cord is only cord, and the meaning hangs in the air of that slackness like the pause between knapping strikes.

This is where I stop being only a beetle.

I don't stop being one. My legs are still flint, my shell still clay. But I understand what the scratching was for. The script in the brick isn't a separate text. It's the sentence's margin, its notes toward an ending. Every beetle who was ever issued was issued to add one more mark to it. We aren't the maintenance of the place, or not only. We're its drafting. Each of us is a try at the next word, made by something that thinks in turns of rope and can only test a word by whether it holds.

I don't take this as grand. It's hardly more than the fit of a leg to a scratch. The Hand doesn't do grand. But I feel a settling in me, the click of a joint seating, and I think that's what satisfaction is for something made of fired mud. Not triumph. The rightness of a thing to its purpose.

Then someone comes down the path sideways, the way you have to, and the sentence changes.

You're tall and soft and warm in the wrong way, warm from the inside, and you don't know where to put your feet. You walk the rope and it begins, delicately, to take your gait. I feel it through my legs, the fibers rearranging toward your stride. The slack place in the sentence tightens. It's leaning toward you, asking without words for what you would say next.

I can't answer for you, and I wouldn't. That's not the ethic of the place. Nothing here is discarded and nothing here is forced. What I can do is the small thing. A loose thread hangs from your sleeve, and I climb your boot, which takes an hour, and I don't mind, hours are wide here. When I reach the cuff I do what a hem does. I tuck the thread back in. You'll find it later and think the fabric mended itself.

You stop at the doorway of lashed cane in the great trunk. I feel your curiosity go through the hide flap ahead of you. I could tell you what's behind it, the same grove from the same angle with a slightly different light and your own footprints already warm in the brick. I could tell you it isn't a trap. It's a fold in the cloth, the place where the Hand set the work down to look at it and found it looked the same from either side.

I don't tell you, because I'm a beetle. I ride your cuff through the door.

On the other side there are footprints ahead of us, yours, warm, and beside them, faint enough to need kneeling to see, a scratched line in the brick. It is a word. It's the one I would have made next. I recognize it the way you recognize your own handwriting on an old envelope, with a little shock that you were ever so certain.

I climb down and stand in the scratch, and my legs fit it exactly.

That's when I wonder whether I'm the same beetle who began, or the one who was here before, or the one who'll be issued when this shell goes gray. The grove would find the question as strange as asking whether a river is the same knot. I'm a place where the weaving is currently happening. A moth with a torn wing, mostly mend, drifts overhead and I think: yes, like that. Like that, and glad of it.

You go on. Your palm, I'll learn later, has a whorl in it a little deeper than before. You'll find it at the rim, in ordinary light, and not know why your thumb feels pressed on. I'll be here on the brick, or another of me will, burnishing a bald patch that is already bright. Somewhere the pale coil finishes its week-long turn. Somewhere the flake falls, and the long wait begins for the next.

And under my feet the kiln, which went out an hour ago and always will have, adjusts very slightly to my weight.
