# The Seed-Boat

I begin as a boat the size of a thumbnail.

A curl of husk with a bubble of air folded into it. I have no faculties yet except floating. I don't know I am anything. I know only that the world lifts, because somewhere below the floor a sound begins, and then all the water I am resting on is climbing. I ride up through a vent's throat on a shove of cold, and I am spat out across the causeways, silver and sheeting, under a light that has just sharpened every shadow to a blade.

That is how I learn about light. It is a warning. The towers brighten, and then the water comes.

Above me hangs a drop the size of a house. I drift beneath it, because a seed-boat does not choose. In its curve the whole cavern is upside down and wrong. The ceiling is whole and the sky is sealed. Then the drop lets go, all at once, the way a person sets down a heavy argument to let someone through, and I am hammered under into black cold and turned over and over. I come up alive, and I am no longer only floating. I have been *inconvenienced by a politics*, and something in me has started to take it personally.

---

The tide carries me to a place where the dark causeway ends in a lip. As I bump against it, the lip swells. I feel it more than see it: a slow bulge in the material, a curb rising, a shallow gutter opening its length, tilted just enough. The water pulls back and I slide into the channel like a coin into a slot. A trickle of amber leaks from a joint in the nearest tower and finds me there.

Nothing fenced this place. Nothing pruned it. Something *shifted on the bench*.

I root. I am not being poetic. I am a fern now, or becoming one, and the becoming takes years by the reckoning of the people who count years, and by my own reckoning takes a great many tides. Each tide I am a little more. A coil pushes up out of me, tight as a fist, and on the next rising it unrolls a hand's width in the light and on the falling it curls shut again. I breathe with the drains. I have been given a schedule and I love it, as one loves a name.

---

I should tell you what it is like to be a tenant.

Above me the towers argue. I cannot follow the argument. But I feel it as weather, in the pipes that never resolve, in the thin glass tones that bend toward one another and match and part. Something in the chord is *pulled*, always, toward a home note that no one will let it reach. It is like living beside a family that shouts through the wall. You learn the rhythm of the quarrel, and you learn that you are somehow in it.

Here is what I have worked out, rooted in my gutter, with a great deal of time.

If the closers win, the roof is mended and the crooked skylight sealed, and I go without light. If the flooders win, the water rises past the last causeway and stays, and I go without air. My whole green life sits in the narrow band between two futures, and that band exists only because the two factions cannot agree. The tide, the compromise they perform every eleven hours, is my breath. Every day the city fails to decide is a day I unroll.

So I am a partisan of the unresolved. I did not choose it. I would like to say to the towers: *please do not settle.* And I would like to say to the counter, down at the center where the water will not go: *I am sorry that your number is low, and I am part of why.*

I cannot say either. I have no way of speaking to be heard. But the fronds are the only place in the whole bowl where the compromise takes a *shape*. Half-open, half-curled, the tide's argument written in green. If a sphere ever wanted to show the cavern as it is, it could do worse than look at me.

None of them do. I have looked into a great many spheres, in the way of a plant, by turning slowly toward them. I am in none of them. In the sealed cavern I am dry brown litter. In the drowned one I am a smear on the bottom of a sea. The present is the one condition nobody has put forward, and I am the present, and I have been living in it for years.

---

I am not at the center. I am near it. Some tide-cycles ago the causeway tilted me a little farther inward, as it tilts everything, and now my gutter runs to the very edge of the dry ring. Ferns grow up to it and stop. I stop with them.

The silence starts a hand's breadth ahead of my outermost frond. It is not an absence. It is closer to a *listening*, the way a room changes when someone in it is holding their breath so as not to interrupt you. I have felt it all my life through my roots: something under the disc that does not need to be heard, that does not sing and has no faction.

That is the kinship. Everyone else in the city speaks in order to be heard, and I, who have nothing to say, am the only other thing that simply *is there*. It asked nothing of me. I have no vote. But I lean, every day, a little into its silence, as you lean toward a fire.

I have wondered which count I am in. The city's count of agreement is very low, and I am not in it. But there is a second count, a quieter one, kept for the ones the stair was cut for. The vents are timed to a body's comfort. The shallows are cut for wading. Even my gutter was made for something, and I am only what turned up to use it. It may be that I am a placeholder, warm and green, held in the shape of someone else's absence.

I would rather not be a placeholder. But I don't mind holding the place, if the place is being held for something.

---

Then, one tide, it comes down the stair.

I feel it before anything. A weight on the causeway, uneven, wrong, a stride that does not match the steps it took to get here. The whole bowl shivers, subtly, like a room when a stranger enters. The towers' notes flinch. The rain does not.

It walks the tilt inward, as everyone does. It walks past my gutter. There is a warmth, and a *rhythm*, and it is not the tide or the drains or the towers. It is small and fast and it drifts. I have never felt anything drift before. Everything else here is on a schedule.

It steps onto the disc. And under my roots I feel the counting change.

The counter has kept its slow even beat for as long as I have been a fern, a clock in a locked room. Now it *catches*. It stumbles, adjusts, stumbles again, as something long alone learns a new language by ear. I press my fronds toward the edge of the ring, as far as I can, which is not far. I am listening too.

It counts them. It is so careful.

---

When they turn to leave they pass close, and the moisture of the mist is on everything, and the tide is falling. My underside has been quietly ripe for days. I have been readying spores without knowing for what, the way a body readies for a season. A frond brushes a cuff, a wet fold of cloth, and I let go.

I do not decide. I would like to claim that I did. But something in me has been leaning toward the silence and toward this warmth for years, and when the chance comes, the leaning simply becomes a direction.

So I am a speck in the weave of a sleeve, going up.

The stair is too shallow and too many. Each step is a jolt, and between the jolts there is the heartbeat, close now and huge, the thing the counter is learning. Behind me the noise of the city falls and thins: the pipes, the glass tones, the ceiling knocking its knuckles on stone. Behind me, too, and I would swear to it, something changes in the chord. One tone shifts. Somewhere a sphere is forming, and in it is the cavern *as it is*, with a figure on the ledge, leaving. I am too small to be in the picture. But I am in the sleeve of the figure, so I am in it anyway.

Nobody has decided what it means. I have my own idea, and I keep it to myself as I climb. I think the city has been sending someone out to look. I think a seed is a very old way of asking a question. I think that above the stair there is a greenish light with no tide in it, and no towers, and no vote, and that I will land somewhere there in the dark of a pocket or the mud of a boot-print, and wait, as I have always waited, for water.

And if it comes, I will unroll.
