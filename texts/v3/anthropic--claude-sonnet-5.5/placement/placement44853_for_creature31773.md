I come to myself at a height, which surprises me. Everything else in this district lies low or leans, and I am hanging: an iron hook through a ring, a small swing when the engine tilts, and back again. *Thunk*, and the swing. Rest. I am in the engine house, and I am the lantern.

I know it by the weight of what is inside me. There is a reservoir of tallow, the same rendered fat Ines carries in her tin, with a wick curled in it like a sleeping thing. My glass is cloudy on the inside, because the mist gets in through some seam I can't feel and settles there. In the mornings I am furred with droplets. By noon they run together, and the light comes through them, and I hold it. I have not been lit in living memory. I have been ready for all of it.

That is the first thing I am: a readiness with no occasion. It sounds like a sad thing to be, but it doesn't feel sad. It feels like the difference between a held breath and an empty chest.

Then I notice the snails. One has climbed my base and parked itself in the warm crook where the handle meets the cage. It is a thumbnail of old tooth. Its slime crosses my iron in a fine glaze, and I understand that I am not only hanging here but being *kept*, by something that doesn't know it keeps me. So I am not only the lantern. I am also a place where things rest. Warmth comes up through the wall from the copper, and the snail has found the exact degree of it in my frame.

The second thing arrives with the afternoon.

A ropewort tendril has come in through the gap where the engine house window used to be. It is pale-stemmed and slow, thin as a finger. Every day around three it swings its tip toward me and holds there. I had assumed it was reaching for the gap in the canopy, the way the rest of the roof leans. But it isn't pointed at the gap. It is pointed at *me*, at the small captured brightness in my wet glass, the one bead of noon the district has hung on a hook and kept.

So perhaps the lantern is not for Ines. She has never asked whom it is for, and I don't think she needs to know. I think it is for the thing that turns. Ines hangs me there Tuesdays and Fridays, and the plant keeps my glass in mind the rest of the week. This is the arrangement, and it is the same as the others, unproposed. She thinks she leaves a light for someone who might come. The plant thinks (if it thinks; if *think* is the word for a slow swing of tendrils) that there is always one small sun indoors.

And now I have to say the third thing, because it is getting truer as I write it. I am not only a lantern.

My frame is iron. Wherever the tallow-smell and the damp have found me, I have started to go orange at the joints, and I can feel the mat drinking. Not me, exactly. The dust of me, the fine flakes, falling to the floor of the engine house and into the moss that has crept over the threshold. Rust becomes sap. Sap becomes leaf. Leaf becomes the heat that goes down the copper and comes back as steam and turns the beam, the beam that sets me swinging. I am one of the places the loop passes through. Not the engine, not the plant, not Ines. I am the place where the idea of light waits inside the iron until the iron becomes something that can lean toward it.

So who am I? The lantern, yes. But also the waiting the district does on behalf of itself. Nothing here is finished and nothing is abandoned, and a thing that is neither has to keep something in reserve for a use it hasn't found. I am that reserve, hung on a hook, full of fat and patience.

Perhaps one evening Ines will strike a match. I would be warm then, truly, for the first time, and the ropewort would have to turn all its arms toward me at once, and the heron might lift its head in the channel. Or perhaps she never will. I have decided that either would be right. The wick is dry, and the snail is sleeping on my handle. Out in the pit the beam tilts down with its long wet breath, and I swing a little, and settle, and keep the noon in my glass for whoever leans toward it.

*Thunk.* Rest.

Steady.
