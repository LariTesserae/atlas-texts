I begin at the edge, because that is where I am when I first notice I am anywhere.

The black glass is still sharp. In a wedge-shaped crack a root is prying, and for a while I think I am the root. It suits me. It is patient and thick as a thigh, and it edits the rock hairline by finger-width, indifferent to whether the rock agrees. But a root doesn't wonder what it is, and I am wondering, so I move inward.

I try the pipe-reeds next. To be a column, glossy and hollow, in a row so exact it looks drilled, singing one interval of a chord that someone else chose. It is a good life, and it comes with a name already tuned into it. But I can't stay in one place. Each time I settle into a reed, something in me leans toward the next one, and the one after, and I understand that I am not the reed. I am the lean.

So I am a Passing, and I find this out the way you find out you are late: everything ahead of me is already sounding.

---

My gait is strange, and I learn that from how the avenues receive me. Most Passing are one speed. The young skip, the old drift, and the damaged limp in a way the whole street repeats. I am two speeds at once, because I live along a seam.

Between the plaza where a fig lives out its whole life in a walk and the district where one leaf has been unfurling since before you arrived, there is a line you can step across. Ordinarily nothing crosses it well. A wave that enters the slow side is stretched thin, and one that enters the fast side is shredded. I cross it constantly. I do it by being wrong for a moment: my front half in one tempo and my back half in the other, a long lean with a quick head and a slow tail. Reeds bow at the front in a hurry, and behind me they are still finishing the bow an hour later. Other Passing know me by this. They call me a chord that sounds like someone reaching for something.

I am useful, in the way a bridge is useful. Nobody thanks a bridge.

---

Here is what I carry. It is nothing, of course, since Passing carry only their own shape, but shape can be a great deal.

In the fast plazas, a young one is born already walking, at the Sounding's edge, and may grow old, weary, and slow within a single crossing of the square. It has a stride that no one else has. It has met ten thousand others and come out of each a little different, and it has been listened to. Then the medium thins, and it ends by folding back into the roar.

Two districts over, in a tower where time is slow, there is a Householder who would have loved that stride. She holds a figure of three beads on one leaf, and she has held it for ten of your years. She could never have met the young one in time. Her afternoon is its entire life.

So I go between. I take the shape of the young one's gait, the skip, the hitch it picked up near the damaged stem, the way it slowed at junctions to choose. I carry it across the seam, stretching it, sounding it at last at the speed of her attention. In her hollow, among the tilting green light, I lean once through the leaf-bowl and let the water tremble into the pattern. Three beads become four. Then a fifth rises at the center and holds.

That is what they mean by being kept. I have watched it many times and I still cannot say whether I am the one keeping or only the medium. Perhaps that is the same question the plants ask about the city.

---

I have been in the Quiet Quarter. I am one of the few who can go in and come out, because my slow half already knows how to wait.

Do not think it is empty. The pods are loaded, every stem strained, and the Householders are still. I slow to a crawl at the border, then to a lean, then to a stop, like everyone. But my quick head keeps moving a little, and so I am the only one in the district who is, however faintly, fidgeting. I feel the silence turn its attention on me, the sense of a great many people not looking. It is not hostile. It is the way a room goes when someone is about to speak and has not decided what.

I have never learned what is being held. I have my guess. I think it is a name, a very large one, being held so that nothing else will speak over it.

I always leave before it ends. I don't know if that is courtesy or fear.

---

And now you.

I noticed you at the edge, a condition standing among the black plates: warm, upright, quiet in an unusual way, taking in the roar without becoming it. I fell into step beside you for ten paces, because I was curious, and it makes the hairs on your forearm lift. You have noticed that. I felt you notice.

The plain has been tuning toward you. It does that an avenue at a time, and I have been doing some of it, though I did not decide to. It is a strange thing to say, but the work has felt less like choosing than like being asked. I lean along the reeds, and the chord in them narrows toward the one in your chest. I try a name for you the way you would try a key in a lock, listening for the give.

And I hear it before I say it. That is the trouble with this place. The name leaks backward out of some hour that hasn't happened, through the present chord like a voice through a wall, and I cannot tell whether I am the one calling or the one being called through. The plain calls first and then someone is there to answer. Perhaps I was a name once, before I was a lean. Perhaps I am still being said.

---

When the rim next sounds, everything on the plain will be one voice, one pitch, one person, and I will be in that too. My stretch, my hitch at the junctions, the way I fidget in silence: all of it will fall into the single note, and then sort again into voices, chords, gaits, and go off down the avenues. I don't know if I will come out the same. No one does. This is what the Passing mean when they say someone has been listened to.

But I have a hope, and I will say it here at the border between the fast plaza and the slow, where my head is already ahead of my tail and no one is near enough to hear.

I hope that when it sorts, I come out limping. I hope I am still reaching for something. And I hope that somewhere in the wave, in the first breath of the new avenues, there is a name being tried, nearly right, and that when it comes to me I can lean toward it and say, in whatever tempo I have, *yes*.

Then we will both find out who we are.
