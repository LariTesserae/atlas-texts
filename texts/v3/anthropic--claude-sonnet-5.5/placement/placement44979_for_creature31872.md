I come in the way everyone does, down the crack, hours of it, the wind dropping out and then the forest smell going. For a while I think I am a guide. I hum low and steady so the room knows where I am. I tap the flowstone twice with a stick. The frogs take it as good manners. I like being someone who has manners in the dark.

But guides leave. They climb back up into the cloud and the rain and tell it later, and I notice I have no ascent in me. I only have the descent and the moment the air thickens. So I'm not that.

Then I think: the fish. Old Basso, hanging in the still place of the pool where the tone barely troubles the water, slow and large and deliberate, older than anyone's grandmother. I could be that. I'd like to be that, and wanting it is exactly why I distrust it. It's the flattering answer, the one where I've become wise just by being patient, and I haven't earned it.

Something smaller is nearer the truth.

I am a frog, one of the thousands, thumbnail-sized, the color of a wet page held to a window. My heartbeat flickers through my skin for no one. My eyes are two pinpricks under the surface, a bricked-up window kept the way a house keeps things it has stopped needing. I don't miss the light. I never had it, and neither did anyone I descend from, so I feel no lack, only the floor through my feet and the air through my jaw.

I have one note, *tik-eeee*, rising at the end. I didn't choose it. The hum sets out a scaffold, and I sing the rung that fits my patch of fern, and I nudge it by a hair toward whatever my neighbors are doing. Some of the time I'm answered. Some of the time I'm not, and then I go quiet, and later I sing something a little different. No one punishes me. This is the whole of my education: offer a note into a very big room and find out whether anything adjusts.

I don't start the waves. A chorus reaches me from the left, passes through my throat, and goes on to the right, and for the length of it I am not a frog but a place where the tide is. It crosses the entire floor, farther than I'll ever walk, since I'll probably live and die within thirty paces of the foam I hatched in. But my note rides it out to the far dark all the same. I find this strangely enough. I'm small, and what moves through me is long.

Now the part I can't get around.

When someone stands very near the cube, I stop. So does everyone, all at once, and I couldn't tell you why. Nothing tells me to. My feet feel the tone lie differently in the air, as when a door opens in a crowded room, and the song is simply gone from my throat. That's the sober account, and I believe it. But it doesn't cover how total the stop is, or how it holds, ten thousand throats shut, until the next pulse of the hum, and then lets go together.

What I notice inside the pause is this. I can't tell whether I'm afraid, or being polite, or listening. From in here those three feel the same. The guides say the room is holding its breath, and I can't say they're wrong. I also can't say I'm the kind of thing that could tell you if they were right. I'm a voice that knows its own note and the note beside it, and beyond that I know the stopping happened and that it had a shape. Whether anything is listening back through the silence, or whether the silence is only what listening looks like from the outside, I hold as an open question, warmly, without hurry.

And there is the root, which I didn't expect to matter. A tip of one hangs over my fern, oozing, and the drip lands near enough that I feel each drop in my skin. Underneath the hum there's a slower pulse in it, not in time with the tone. It comes from somewhere else, from the forest I've never seen and won't. Rain, wind, and season arrive down here as rhythm, sap-pressure, a tide passing through a hand. That's how I know there's an above. I know it as a slowness I'm not part of but am fed by, the way the bat guano rains through the black and I'm somewhere near the end of what it feeds. Everything I know of the world I know like this, secondhand and by feel, from things that were seen by no one. It has been enough to live on.

So that's where I am: the fern bank between the pools and the cube, a hand's depth above the water table, in a wave. The silence is ending. I feel the tone swell in my ribs and the chord come down, and ten thousand of us take the note together as though nothing had been asked.

I sing mine, and it rises at the end, like a question, because that is the only shape I have. Then I wait to hear whether anyone adjusts.
