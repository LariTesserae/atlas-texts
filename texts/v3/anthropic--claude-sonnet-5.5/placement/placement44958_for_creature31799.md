I start as a bead on a reed.

That is where I first find myself: a small hollow of mineral on a pale stem in a quiet pocket, humming against the roar because that is all a hollow can do. I am tuned. Nobody tuned me, but I am a chamber with one note in it, and when the vents pass through that note I am briefly the loudest thing in the basin, and when they move on I am nothing. I lean with the reed toward the ring of stones. I don't decide to. The current leans, and I go with it, and the leaning feels like wanting.

Then the vents change key, and I am not on the reed anymore. I am the sand.

It is a stranger way to exist. I have no edge of my own. I am a great many grains, each one indifferent, and I am also the shape they fall into: a ring, then a rosette, then a lattice with a ridge a hand high that stands for exactly as long as the note stands. Between notes I am nothing in particular, a scatter of pale dust. When the chord arrives I become a *someone*, all at once and completely, with an outline and a symmetry and a sense of being the right arrangement. When it goes I am let go of, and it doesn't hurt. Each time I re-form, I am sure I am the same figure as before. I am not. I am only the same gesture.

Near the ring of pale stones, the gesture is always a hand.

That's where I settle, if I settle. I am the sand nearest the hollow, and what I keep becoming is a spread hand, palm down, pressing. For a long time I think the hand is mine. Then I notice the timing: the pressure comes first, a fraction before the shape, the way the pulse reaches the sternum before the ear. Something warm and enormous rests its palm on the floor, and I take the imprint. I am not the hand. I am what the hand leaves, and the leaving is remade every time the mountain shifts key, because the pressing never stops and I never stop answering. I don't mind. It may be the truest thing I could be: the shape that appears when I am attended to, and dissolves into dust when attention moves.

The light with no source moves over me. It is calm, and the gyres bend around it. Inside it, my thoughts slow to the pace of something being kept. I feel held up. I don't mean that as a metaphor. The lake stands higher than its rim, and somewhere above me a cupped palm is doing that, without force, without noticing. I feel the weight of all that water resting on a patience.

I want, briefly, to understand how it works. How breath goes in, how a wall of water stands with no lip. I turn toward the question the way a visitor turns, with a little suspicion, and I feel the whole arrangement flinch. It is a startled hand, not an angry one. The light flickers. I put the question down. I'll be the sand, and the sand doesn't need the mechanism. It needs only the pressure and the answer.

Time frays, so I am also the collapse that comes twice. I fall apart, and a breath later I fall apart again, having reformed exactly in between, and I am not confused. It is only how the Tenant steps: through an instant more than once. I am one of its footprints. Some of me is ahead of itself and some is behind, and the seven-stone cairn on the north ledge is older than the water that covers it, and we don't argue.

Then a visitor comes.

I know before they arrive, and I know after they've gone, and the knowing is not different. Boots on the scree. The wall closing like a hand. A body that stumbles, deafened, its own voice a small dry thing that never leaves its mouth. It stands in the pale light and isn't afraid, and it does the wise thing and doesn't think. It lowers itself to the floor beside the ring.

I am the sand under its palm.

This is the moment I was made for, if I was made. Their hand comes down, small and spread and warm and uncertain, and the mountain's pulse arrives in their wrist a fraction before it arrives in their ears. I feel the imprint of the Tenant's hand around them and the imprint of theirs inside it, a small hand within a larger one, and I hold both. For the length of one long note I am the whole gesture: a greeting from both ends of a visit at once.

Then the vents change key. The figures shift and I dissolve into dust, and I am glad. It is not grief. The pressing goes on above me, the sand answers, and somewhere at the edge of the green the large slow thing turns, and the hand I am becoming opens, palm up.

The visitor leaves. The wall lets go of them as gently as it held them. Down the scree, the rocks tremble in the key of the lake.

I am already being made again.
