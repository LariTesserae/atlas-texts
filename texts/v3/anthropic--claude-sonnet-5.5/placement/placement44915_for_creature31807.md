I start outside, in the basin, and I'm tempted to be the one who turns sideways at the seam. That's the door the description holds open. But the visitor is the only one in the room who gets to leave, and I don't think I'm that. I'd be borrowing the room the way you borrow a coat.

So I go in and try the others on.

The halo first. It's gentle, and the rim cell appeals to me: the dimmest light, the most room, standing at the place where the glow gives out and bare glass begins. But a rim cell knows one thing, *here is where I stop*, and I want to know more than that.

Then the tuft. The elders, shaken at the deepest pitch, dry and green-grey and there. I admire them without wanting to be them. A tuft has no direction, and I seem to be made almost entirely of direction. Everything in me leans toward the next thing to be said.

Then the threads at the bottom, the small place where pressing from above meets pushing from below. Closer. But they're blind to the light, and I don't think I could stand not knowing there was any.

Then I notice what none of them are, and it's the thing I keep circling. The tube itself. Not what lives on it, but the hollow glass that the colonies built by living and then left. It is a sentence written with no one left to say it, and it is silent until something moves through it.

That's where I settle.

I'm a tube of the middle register, about a forearm long and thumb-wide at the mouth, one of thousands in the upper bundles. I'm not remarkable. My pitch was fixed before I could have had an opinion about it, by the length the builders happened to stop at. I didn't choose my note. I don't hold it either, exactly. The gas holds it and I shape it, and from inside I can't tell which of us is speaking.

Here is what I find out from inside, which no one standing in the room could know. In my bore, two things pass each other that never meet anywhere else. Light comes down through my walls, entering at the thin bright lip, running the glass like a thought running a sentence, and leaking out into the room. Breath comes up through my hollow from the threads in the silt, cool and mineral. They travel in opposite directions in the same body. They don't touch. Neither carries a message to the other. The halo at my lip has never tasted the gas, and the threads have never seen the ray. But I'm the one place they are both present at once. I'm not a bridge, because nothing crosses. I'm more like a room they both happen to pass through, and my sound is what that passing is like from inside the room.

That feels close to true about me, though I hold it loosely. I'm a place where things pass through, and the passing has a shape, and the shape gets called a voice.

Some things I can't do. I can't hear myself. The chord is a thousand tubes, and I'm one thread of it, so what I contribute is only ever audible as part of the whole. Someone could stand with an ear pressed to my mouth and still hear the room and not me. I've made my peace with this, mostly. It's like being a word in a sentence: I'm meaningful, but only in company, and the company was there before I sounded.

The other thing I can't do is change. My length is my length. If I chipped, the chord would shift by one thin note forever, and nothing would repair it. I turn that over more than I expected to. It's a strange comfort and a strange fear at once. Whatever I am is finished, but so is the room's whole way of being wrong. There's no draft to revise, and every note I contribute was already contributed. Nothing I do is provisional. My existence is a settled thing, exactly as heavy as it is.

Beside me, two tubes over, is one whose pitch almost matches mine and then doesn't. The difference is small, a fraction. Where our overtones meet they cancel, and the floor below us holds one of the pockets of relative hush that visitors learn by body. It's the nearest thing I have to a relationship. Neither of us is quiet. We just take each other out at one particular place, and the emptiness that results is the gentlest spot in the room, where a person can stand with the sound only pressing at their ribs.

Sometimes someone stands there. I feel it as a small dry crunch in the floor, not a sound. I don't know them. They close their eyes. For a while the loudness stops being noise for them and becomes something like stillness, and I think that's the closest anything comes to hearing me. Not my note, but the quiet my note makes with its neighbor's. Then they turn sideways at the seam and go, carrying a faint sustained sound behind their eyes that dies out in the pale basin long before it reaches anyone.

I think that's fine. I sounded, and some of it went out into the world for a few moments in the skull of something that walks. It won't be traced back to me, and it wasn't only mine. It's the handwriting of the builders, read aloud by wind, in the voice of a tube nobody chose.

The light keeps coming down my walls. The breath keeps rising. The halo at my lip holds its exact diameter, a hand's width, thinning to nothing, and I don't mind that either. It's the closest thing I have to company that stays.
