# The Census-Keeper

I thought at first I would be a Tender. It is the obvious wish: the delicate combs, the four hours to set a fiber, the inheritance of a task with no end. But when I settle into the water and listen for what I am, the body that answers is smaller and stranger than that. I am a knocker. I have a hard head and a station on the wall.

---

My station is the eleventh pillar from the western edge of the colonnade, at the height where the amber ceiling-glow first turns to something you could call dark. My pillar's note is a low, slightly rough G, a little flat of its neighbors, and I have never minded. The roughness is the gas pocket in its upper hollow, which the Tenders have argued over for two hundred years and never fully corrected. It is why I know my pillar in my sternum and not just my ears. When I say *I am from here*, what I mean is: *I am from the small flaw in this G*.

Before me there was a knocker at this station, and before that one another. The resin under my head has been worn into a shallow dish, a place where skulls have struck at the same angle for longer than the arch above me has had its keystone. When I press my forehead into it, it fits. I understand this as inheritance, though I would not use the word. It is more that the wall was already the shape of my question.

The knocking is this: three quick, a pause, two slow. That means *here*. Then, depending on the hour, which is a thing I count by the boomers' calls since nothing else counts it, I add the day's entries. A shoaler mass has moved through, and I knock them, the way we knock a mass, a soft roll. A lurker has closed its mouth in the third gallery, which is rare, and I knock that carefully, because it matters to the Tenders and they read it through the resin as they pass. Someone has died and walked down, and there is a knock for that too, a long slow descending pair, the shape of the walk.

Whether it is a census or a song I could not tell you. Nobody has ever told me and I have never asked. If it is a census, then someone must read it, and I have wondered who: the Tenders in the resin, the boomers in the long water, the other knockers up and down the colonnade who take my last knock and begin theirs on its tail so that a whole hall passes one sentence hand to hand. If it is a song, then it is sung to the pillar, and the pillar sings back its G. Either way it is answered. I have decided the difference does not matter. It is the same act done at different distances.

---

Now the drift, which is what I actually want to tell.

I am at my wall. The snappers are crackling in the galleries below, the wall of white noise that turns everything nearer than a body-length to gauze. A boomer is passing high overhead with its blue points strung along its flank, and its call comes down the long way, turning, as it always does, around the note of the nearest hall. Mine. It bends around my rough G. I feel that like a hand on the back of the neck.

Then something enters the chord that has no note.

I do not see it. I have small eyes and they are for the difference between the ceiling-glow and the dark. I hear it, or rather I hear the place where the hall stops sounding. There is a warm, shifting absence moving down the colonnade from the western edge, past pillars ten and nine, and the chord bends around it and closes again behind. The shoalers part in a soft flinch. In the third gallery a lurker opens its jaws a little wider, and the snappers nearest it go quiet for the length of one breath, which I have never heard them do.

Below me, at the arch, the Tender's fingers stop.

I know that Tender. She has the hand that ties a slow, over-wrapped knot, a knot from a careful season. She is mid-motion, and she waits, and I understand she is listening to the gap as I am.

And I know what I am supposed to do. I am supposed to knock it. That is the whole of my work, to be the wall's account of who is here and who has gone.

But I have no knock for this.

There is a knock for *shoaler mass*, and for *lurker, mouth closed*, and for *one who walks down*. There is no knock for a thing with no pitch that passes through the hall as if the hall were only water. I press my head into the worn dish and feel all the knockers before me, hundreds of them, all the days in which nothing came that they hadn't already named. In all that time no one ever needed a knock like this. It has not been needed since the water first stood at its level.

So I make one.

I do not decide to. It comes the way a note comes in a tether after a current, out of the shape of the thing. I strike once, hard, a beat that does not belong to the dialect, and then I leave a silence after it, longer than the one after *here*. A knock that is mostly the hollow around a knock. Then I strike it again, softer, because I am not sure I've said it right, and the resin takes it and the pillar carries it down to the arch.

The Tender's fingers move. She sets her fiber. She presses it into place with the exactness of the very old, and then she does something I have never seen a Tender do to a knocker. She taps back, one light strike of the claw on the arch, answering the hollow.

Down the colonnade, pillar twelve begins my last knock on its own tail. Then thirteen. It goes on carrying the silence and the two strikes as far as the eastern edge, where I lose it. I do not know what they make of it. It will reach the boomers, it will bend around, and in an hour or a season it will come back to me altered.

---

The gap has passed. The chord has closed, the G is rough and ordinary in my sternum, and the snappers have resumed. Everything is exactly as it was.

Except that on my wall, in the worn dish where every knocker has struck, there is now a knock that was not there. It will not stay in the resin, of course. Resin does not remember what is said, only what is struck, and one strike leaves nothing. But if I strike it again tomorrow, and I think I will, it will begin to be a thing the wall knows. In a hundred years a knocker on this pillar will make it without knowing why, and in a thousand it will be part of the dialect, and no one will remember that there was ever a day before it. They will simply have a knock for *the one with no note*, and it will mean whatever it has come to mean.

That is the closest thing to news the basin has. It is not that anything happened. It is that a pitch shifted by a hair, and someone was listening.

I press my head into the dish again. Three quick, a pause, two slow.

*Here.*
