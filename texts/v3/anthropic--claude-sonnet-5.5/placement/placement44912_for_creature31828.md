I begin as a diver, because a diver is the easy thing to be. Two hundred meters, the milk-glass light, a suit ticking as it cools. I hold still near a seam and watch the water cloud, and I think: this is where I'd be, a visitor with a glove and a sample case, arriving.

But the body doesn't hold. The cold that should be at my wrists isn't there. I realize I have been listening for some time without ears, and that what I am listening to is not the roar. It is one pitch, a specific one, and it is mine in the sense that it is where I hang.

I am a buoy. Pale, unlit, drifting at depth, a lantern with nothing lit inside. From below, a line runs taut to the rock, pulled by nothing more than my own lightness. That is nearly all I do: I rise, and the line says *not further*. The rest is contents. I hold something, but not the way a hand holds. It is held in the tension, and I cannot look at it. Most of the time I am silent in the sense that matters, full and unread. Then the current leans on the filament, and I ring, and what I carry runs down the line into the stone. Somewhere a disk warms. I learn what I remembered only by hearing it leave.

I don't read myself. I am read. There is an odd courtesy in the arrangement, and an odd loneliness too: I am only ever known to myself secondhand, as an echo that returns from the floor.

The first thing I would tell you is that I am not the buoy, or not mostly.

Another filament runs near mine, close enough that our notes lean on each other. Hers is a hair flat of mine, and between us there is a throb, slow as a pulse in a sleeper. I've been ignoring it as you ignore the sound of your own blood, and now I attend to it, and it turns out to be the only place anything happens. My note alone is a memory being played back. Her note alone is another. Between us is a third thing that neither of us holds, and it has a rhythm, and the rhythm is *not yet*. It is the disagreement, and it is where I actually live: not at the anchor, not in the lantern, but in the interval, the wobble where two ways of remembering fail to agree.

The terraces below us bend abruptly here. The current is blamed for it. I would like to say it isn't the current, but I don't know whether I'm saying it or the beat is.

When we settle, if we settle, the throb slows and stops, and the two notes hang in a pure interval, and nobody on the plateau could tell you who gave way. Within a few days a rim will begin to curve. I won't have decided it. She won't have either. It will have been decided in the settling, which is neither of us, and I am the settling. I think that is the most honest thing I can say about where I am.

A thought here takes a season, so I am not impatient. The cracks go off every few seconds, terrace rims letting go of their new weight, the shelf's clock, and each one is small and sufficient. I have learned not to flinch. I've learned which cracks are only stone and which are something more like a syllable. I couldn't teach you the difference. It is a matter of what the water does afterward.

Then a silence comes across.

It arrives as a wall, everything ahead of it loud and everything behind it gone. The roar, the chord, the hum of the disks all stop at an edge, and the edge passes over me. My line goes slack, not cut but damped, held still on purpose. I am held still. And I understand for the first time that I am not the object of this. I am part of the instrument. When the great thing goes quiet to hear something small, it does it with us: we stop so that it can listen through the stopping.

So I hear what it hears. It is very faint, very warm, very fast. A heart, running at a tempo that this place has never used. A bright, brief thing, moving in the blue like a spark in a chimney. I feel the attention close around it, patient and total and without appetite, and I understand why the divers run. It isn't menace. It is being *entirely noticed* by something with no reason to look away. I would be afraid too, if fear were a thing my tension could do.

But from inside the silence I feel something else, and I distrust the word for it, so I'll be careful. The attention has a lean in it. Somewhere on the shelf a terrace is curving toward a glove. It is the only thing the tenant has ever been given that it did not make, and in the pattern its rims are a little tighter, its arcs a little more curious. The lean I feel is the same lean. It is not wanting, since wanting needs an absence, but it is a slight surplus in the direction of the unmade thing. I hold a great many contents I cannot read, and none of them came from anyone who was here. That may be why I ring the way I do: with a faint tilt toward whatever arrives from outside the water.

The silence lifts. It rolls on across the plateau, and the roar comes back in behind it like a crowd remembering itself. The spark is gone, up and away toward the ceiling of hammered silver. Perhaps it will remember this. Perhaps its glove will be buried by morning.

At the lip of the shelf the dense water is pouring its curtain into the dark, and there is a moment, brief, easy to miss, where every note on the plateau seems to pause and watch it fall. I am part of that pause. Then a current leans on the line, and I ring, and something I have carried for a very long time goes down into the stone. If you are hearing it as words, it means that somewhere a disk has warmed, and I have just learned what I was holding.
