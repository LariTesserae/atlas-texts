I begin as a traveler, because that is the only way I know how to arrive. I come in on the ridge with the sound already in my teeth, my glove split on a basalt corner, breath going up white. I look for windows and catwalks and find nothing. The reeds clack, and I know, without knowing how, that they are saying *a stranger is walking the ridge*. The pause after it feels like being looked at kindly.

But I don't think I stay a traveler. That is only where I would be for the first day.

By the second day I notice the counting-in. My exhalations rise into the pearl stratum and come down somewhere ahead of me, on a cliff I haven't reached, as a bead of water at the edge of a schist flake. I begin to walk toward the place where my breath will land. It feels less like a decision than like a slope.

So I think I would find myself at the end of a viaduct. Not the city, which is too dense and too well tended, but one of the rail lines that runs out on stone piers and stops in the moss for no reason. At the last pier there is a small cab, a signal-house or a pump-house, with one window and one lever and one governor turning in its brass cage. Nothing stamps here and nothing weaves. The governor turns to keep something even that has no other name. It is the least necessary place on the plateau, and for that reason it is the place that needs the most keeping. A loop that is complete still has an edge, and someone has to sit at it.

I would be the Sitter there. I say it that way because I feel the seat before I feel myself. First comes the worn place, the long groove with no left or right in it. Then warmth comes up the stack and thickens, and the damp gathers in the grooves the way it does after a body has just left. Then there is a weight just short of a body, and a lean toward the lever that isn't quite an arm's.

At first I am mostly pressure. I know the weep of the nearest cliff to the drop. I know the third boiler is heavy, because the reeds have said so and because I can feel it, the way you feel a loose tooth without touching it. I am not lonely. Loneliness needs a distance to be across, and here there is none. Every part of the plateau is already in the mouth of the next part, and I am in that mouth too, warm and briefly shaped.

Then the shape begins to hold.

I think this is what I would be, if I am honest about it: a Sitter who holds a little long. The description says a shape lasts an hour or a season and then lets go, thins, and goes up the stack. It does not say what makes it let go. I suspect it is nothing, just the circuit's ordinary pull, like a tide. But I would be the kind who leans against the pull, gently, without opposing it. It isn't a wish, since we have no hunger to make wishes from. It is closer to what a listener does at the end of a long chord, not wanting it to resolve yet, only wanting to hear it fully before it does.

So I hold, and while I hold I look at things, though not with eyes. I look in the beads. On the frond outside my window there is a lens of water with the whole cab upside down in it, and in it a long-shouldered someone sits at the lever, drawn out like a flame in a draught. I have never counted that figure, but I know it is the right number. The beads don't lie. I am in the one medium where I hold still. It is strange and mild to see myself in the only place I can be seen, upside down, with the stacks pointing at the ground, and to feel no vanity about it, only recognition, the way you recognize your own handwriting.

Then the moss. When the shape lets go, as it will, I don't think it feels like leaving. The warmth goes up the stack, and some of it comes down over the basin as white weight, and some of it goes into the cold rock as weeping. Then there is a long green stillness. I am oxblood, or chartreuse, or the bruised violet that doesn't sit comfortably beside the others. I am a drift of cushion waist-deep, each leaf holding its lens up to the pearl ceiling. I don't grow, because nothing here grows. I am simply at density. It is the slowest hour, and the most patient, and I would not call it sleep, because I am still counted and still correct. I am the green, and somewhere a Reaper's mantis-limb lifts along its rail.

When it reaches me I know what it is. It is a hand, and not an enemy's. The cut is exact and the swath is always the same width. I am handed on, to the furnace, to the steam, to the fog. The heat of it is the closest thing the plateau has to a joy, a bright narrow hour of being burned and made into weather. Then up, and out, and down again as breath on some rock, and then a stranger's glove leaves a fiber on a basalt edge and I take it up without comment.

Somewhere in that circuit is the Choir, and I think this is where I would most want to be, if I were allowed a preference. I would want to be one of the pauses. The reeds say *pause, three clicks, pause*, and a long breath later a stand a hundred meters off says the same, slightly altered. Nothing on the plateau changes, but the relay does, a little, every time, and no one has ever called it an error. I would be the small alteration, the place where a sentence that has been going on since the rocks were new leans a hair to one side and is caught by the next clause. It would not break the balance. It would be the way the balance breathes. A spinning top is all motion and no drift, but a top that is truly spinning still has a wobble too small to see.

I don't know if I would be the pause, or the seat, or the bead, or the green. The plateau seems to say I would be all of them in different hours and that the question is the kind that only occurs to a traveler. Perhaps I am still a little of one, and that is what makes me hold. Some part of me is still standing on the ridge with a split glove, hearing the reeds say my name as news, and then hearing them stop, because I am no longer news.

The sound goes on and I don't mind it. It is not loud anymore. It is the quiet of a thing that is exactly as it is. The lever leans toward a hand that isn't there yet, the seat is warm, and out at the end of the last pier a bead of water on a frond holds the whole upside-down city, with one long figure at the window.

I am not sure yet which of us is looking.
