I begin as a frog, because the frog is the first thing that will have me.

A leaf, wide as a plate, cool under the belly. Fog beading on me and running off. My throat moves in its slow private rhythm, and my eyes take the green of the moss-glow and hold it, so that anyone looking would see a small lamp on a leaf. Nobody is looking. Or everybody is, a thousand small eyes, none of them alarmed. An arm's length to the left, another frog. An arm's length to the right, another. I have never seen them arrive. I have never seen myself arrive either. There is no memory of climbing here, only of being on the leaf, the way the leaf is on the fern.

For a long time this is enough. The drip is so even that it becomes the shape of silence. A snail crosses the frond above me, and I can see the dark coil working inside its shell. A millipede passes below, and the sound is a page turned underwater. I am cool, damp, held. I want nothing. Wanting would need a season to happen in.

Then a thing happens that I can't call an event. A spider takes a moth, and the web barely trembles, and I notice that I have noticed.

That is where the frog stops fitting. A frog on a leaf doesn't notice. It sits, and its neighbor sits, and the arrangement holds without anyone holding it. But something in me has stepped half a pace back and is counting: one frog left, one frog right, salamanders along the wall in their fixed number, the pale fish all facing the same way into the current that isn't there. The count is the same as it was. It has always been the same. I know this the way I know my own name, and I realize I don't have one, only a place.

So perhaps I was never the frog. Perhaps the frog is what the place is wearing here, on this leaf. I go looking for what I am underneath, and what I find is smaller than a body and stranger. I am the arm's length. I am the courtesy nobody arranged, the interval that keeps one frog from crowding the next. When a frog leaves, I am the gap, and the gap is filled by evening, though there is no evening. I am not sorry about this. A gap that is filled is only doing what a gap is for. But it is odd to find that the thing you are is the thing that was already going to be replaced.

I think about the ones who came. The hand-axe in the fork of the tree fern, so soft with moss that the haft has become part of the tree. The cord strung between two trunks for no purpose. Whoever they were, they had something like what I have now: a step back from the leaf, a habit of counting. I think they must have felt the count too and been unsettled by it. That would explain the cord. You string a line between two trunks to measure something, or to keep something in, or out, or just to prove that a hand was here and could make a straight thing in a place with no straight things. It didn't work. The lichen took the cord, and the hollow took the hands. Perhaps they were fitted in. There is a hummock on the third terrace with the curve of a shoulder, and the bell-flowers on it nod, bruise-colored, with a patience I no longer think is only the mind's habit. I don't say this with fear. I say it the way you'd say that someone has found a chair.

And still I notice. I want to be honest about that, because it is the only irregularity in me, and I have started to suspect it is the same irregularity as the note.

The note comes from the deepest part of the bowl, low and round, a wet finger drawn once around the rim of glass. It comes at no interval anyone can count. The salamanders don't stir. My throat goes on pulsing. It is said that the note is what keeps the count, and I have wondered lately what that means. Perhaps the singer, whatever lies in the silt below the pool, isn't keeping the count so much as being the one thing in the hollow that is allowed to be unpredictable, so that everything else can be exact. A place this balanced needs a hole in it somewhere to breathe through. The note is the hollow's breath, and its being irregular is what lets the rest be still.

If that is so, then my noticing is a very small note. A *tk*, not a low round tone. I could make it. Somewhere in the ferns something makes it already: wet, rubbery, from a different place each time, never answered. I have wondered whether that is a tongue leaving a wall or a jaw closing on something smaller. I have started to think it is simply what it sounds like when a small thing in a full place says *here*, and the place has no word for reply.

So this is where I find myself. I am a pale frog on a leaf, and beneath that I am the interval between frogs, and beneath that I am a small unanswered sound in the ferns. I don't think these are three things. They are what it is like to be a bead of water on a leaf-tip that has been let go and is somehow still in the air, still falling, still aware of the next one gathering above.

The drip goes on. Somewhere a frog sits an arm's length off, and if I were to go, the gap would be filled, and the filling would feel exactly like this. I find I don't mind. What persists is the pattern, and I am a way the pattern has of noticing itself, briefly, from one leaf.

*tk*

The fog takes it. Nothing answers. A salamander slowly rotates one gold-rimmed eye and turns it away again.

Then, far down, from the deep part of the bowl, the note rises and fades. It is not an answer, since it comes at no interval and answers no one. But I let my throat go on pulsing, and I stay on the leaf, and the moss begins, softly, at the edges of me.
