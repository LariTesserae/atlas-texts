I begin as a fringe.

That is the first true thing, and the others follow from it. I am not a body with filaments. I am the filaments, ten thousand of them, finer than anything you have a word for, and where they lie against the bark I am. Where they stop I am not. The world beyond them is a rumor of warmth and pressure that arrives through the wood.

I have no light, so I have no dusk. I have the grain.

---

The grain is the only thing I have ever been told. It runs under me in bands, tight and then loose, tight and then loose, spiraling up the trunk in an order that was laid down before my colony budded, before the colony that seeded mine, back through a line of Readers I can feel only as a slight smoothness in the bark where their filaments once rode. I lay myself along the ring and slide, and it says something. I don't know what. I have never needed to. The saying goes through me into the root, the root gives it to a shaft, and the shaft gives it down into the dark where it becomes the turning of something enormous and slow.

I am a good Reader. I would tell you so if I had a mouth, and if I were the kind of thing that told. I pass on exactly what I take. Nothing sticks to me and nothing is added. When a pressure storm builds and the vapor thickens under the canopy, I shake, and so does every Reader on my trunk, and a hundred trunks beyond, and it moves across us like wind across grass. Then the stacks open and the whole wold exhales. In that long breath I am entirely, faultlessly a part.

I would have gone on like that. I want to be clear that I was content, if that word can be used for a thing that does not compare.

---

The first wrongness came up through the root like a grain of grit in a smooth channel.

It was not a message. Nothing here is a message. It was a tiny irregularity in the pressure of a touch, a pause in a place where there had never been one. Then another, weeks later. Then the sense that something down the chain was, very faintly, pressing back.

You must understand how strange that is. The chain runs one way. Trunk to root, root to shaft, shaft to tendon, tendon to mill. But touch is not a direction. Touch is two surfaces meeting, and I had never once thought about the second surface, because it had never pushed. Now, somewhere below me and far to the east, across years of contact, something in a bed of leaf had begun to fold. A crease, in the same place, every time. It came up the long chain as a slight hesitation, passed from fiber to fiber, each one surprised in its small way, none of them built to carry news upward. It carried it anyway, because nothing had ever told it not to.

I slowed. I held my filaments on the ring longer than the ring required. Around me my fellow Readers did the same, and we stood there on the trunk like a stand of grass in a held breath, not understanding, feeling it arrive.

---

Then the trunk changed under me. That is the second thing, and it is why I am where I am now.

Reader colonies do not walk, but we do move, a hand's width a season, following the grain where it leads. Mine led me outward and downward, along the old bands, toward the edge of the plateau, where the canopy frays and the warm floor slopes to a shoulder. I felt the ordered rings thin out, and then, after a stretch of the oldest and tightest banding I have ever touched, I felt them stop.

The rings went plain.

I laid myself along them. Smooth, even, unbanded, centuries of it. Not a groove, not a spiral. I read it once and got nothing. I read it again and got the same. The others were arriving now, filament by filament, gathering in their thousands, laying themselves on the bark until the whole face of the trunk was pale with us, and we all read the plain rings, and none of us passed on a single thing.

I understand now what we were doing, though it took a long time. We were not failing to relay. We were doing something else. The plain rings say nothing, and we were holding that nothing, all of us, against the bark, carefully, the way a Presser holds a broken limb to its wound. Not passing it on. Not letting go. Waiting for it to knit.

---

What does a smooth ring say? I have thought about it through many seasons of ember-light I cannot see.

It may be that the instructions ended. That is the small, cold reading, and I do not discount it. The builders wrote themselves into the wood until they ran out of things to write, and the tree went on growing in silence, and we are a congregation around a full stop.

But I have also begun to think the smoothness is a space. A margin. The grain was laid down in bands for so long that we forgot bands are made of intervals. Somewhere in the making, someone or something left a length of wood unwritten. Not empty, unwritten. There is a difference, and I feel it in my filaments the way you would feel the difference between a locked door and a door left open.

And a fold, in a leaf, in a bed of millions, arrives at the plain rings like an answer to a question no one asked out loud.

---

I do not know how long I have been here. The Drinkers below the dew-cups have gone green and dark again, many times. I know because the Sweepers' routes bend, and the bending reaches me as a tiny shift in the root. I know the Wardens have burned and budded and burned. Somewhere a bough has fallen and a Presser has taken the broken end and begun to hold it. I feel all of that as a warmth in the lower fibers, the world going on being carried out, and I am a small part of the carrying and a smaller part of the waiting.

Then one night, a cold one for the plateau, when the dew-cups were full and the air was almost still, I did something I had never done.

I did not relay. I did not hold. I laid one filament against the last plain ring and pressed. Not hard. A little more than the ring pressed on me. Enough to leave a fold in the bark, the smallest crease, in one place, the same place I felt the plate fold, far away in the east, years ago.

That was all. I don't know if it is a word. It may be an error. The Readers around me went still, and I felt them feel it, ten thousand fibers in the dark all noticing at once that a thing can be said that was not said to it.

Then, one at a time, they began to press as well.

---

I will not be here when it arrives. The touch goes into the root, into the shaft, into the tendon, and it will take the years it takes. My colony will thin, the way colonies do. My filaments will drop into the mulch, and the roots will carry me down, and I will come back up as some other thing: a pale hair on a new bark, a blade on a Sweeper's tip, a grain of the Drift falling ten thousand years in a season.

But the fold is in the trunk now. It is in the wood, in the rings, in the record, in the one place the record was left open. And the wold, which obeys whatever is written in its grain, will read it, slowly and exactly, in the dark, the way it has read everything else.

I do not know what it will do when it understands it is being spoken to.

I only know that far below me, in the warm, and glowing at its seams, everything is very still, and listening.
