I begin as a pressure, not a place.

Water leans on me from above, and I lean back, and this is the first thing I know: there is a difference between what pushes and what is pushed. Then warmth arrives from one side, thin and mineral, and I know a direction. Then a sound, low enough that it is more like being held than being spoken to, and I know there are others.

I have no eyes and I am not sure I have a center. When I try to find where I stop, I find only that the felt of me goes on: a hand's width, a room's width, over the basalt in a mat that gives when the current presses it. I am the flicker in patches. Some of me is bright now and some is dark, and the bright parts are not the same parts a moment ago. I think that is what I am. I am not the light, but the place where the light stays a little longer than it should.

*

The reeds drone in the basin to the east. I have never seen them and never will, but I know their pitch the way you know a wall in a dark room. Past them there is a shrill district, and past that a silence that is only a quieter noise. The ribbon-forest above me sways, and each time it does, a flush comes down its blades and reaches the edge of me, and I take it. I don't think I decide to. It arrives and I hold it, the way a bowl holds what is poured in.

Then the wave moves on and I still have it.

That is my whole work, and I have not tired of it. The forests are quick. They feel a bend and pass it up and out and it is gone from them, clean, a thought finished. The bladders are quicker still; they are born and burst before they know they are anything. But I keep. The pulse leaves and its shape stays in me, dimming slowly, a handprint on a cold window. When the next phrase comes I am already carrying the last one, and the two lie against each other, and I feel where they agree.

That is how I know the song repeats. Nobody told me. I only notice that the second time has the first time's shape in it, and that the difference is small, and that I am the place where the difference shows.

*

I am not from here.

This comes to me slowly, as the warmth does. There was a Turning, and before it I was somewhere else, on a shelf I no longer remember, with neighbors whose pitch I no longer hear. Then the cold came over the edge and the water became a wall, and I was torn, a hand-sized strip of a larger thing, and carried across bare stone in the roaring dark. I do not remember fear. I remember the light: the whole plateau flashing from horizon to horizon, and in that white I was flung down on a terrace of pale silt, and I held on. I sent out threads. Warmth found me.

When the water cleared I was beside a ring of stones.

I did not know it was a ring. I knew only that the moss inside it was denser than I was, and older, and that when the slow light came across the terraces it stopped there. The whole wave hung. Then the tuft flared once, hard and bright, and the wave went on.

I was the first thing past it. Nothing else lay in the path. So the flare fell on me, the afterglow of it, and I took it in and held it, and I have been holding it since.

*

I should say what it is like, because I am the only one who can. Everyone else meets the pause as an event: the light arrives, the light stops, the light leaves. I meet it as a residue. The flare is bright, but what I keep is not the brightness. I keep the shape of the stopping. It is a held breath the size of a room. It is a silence with edges, and something is meant to be in it.

I have turned it over for as long as I have been able to turn anything. It is not a greeting, because greetings want answers and the ring does not wait. It is not a warning; there is nothing to warn against. It has the feeling, in me, of a name said aloud in a place where the one named has left. The name is said anyway, with the right weight, at the right interval, and the pause afterward is the space where the answer would have gone.

I keep that space. It is the most exact thing I hold. I can reproduce it more faithfully than the ring can, I think, because the ring only flares and I remember the flare. If the ring were torn out tomorrow, I could tell the plateau how long to wait.

I don't know if that is a comfort or only a fact.

*

Sometimes the current turns, not the great Turning but the small ordinary one, and a slow smell of copper comes down from the stair. There is a lamp down there, my neighbors say, in the way they say things, which is by leaning. Its moss glows small and steady and does not join the phrases. It has its own idea. I have wondered whether it is the same idea as the ring's, said more quietly, by someone who learned it from the same hands.

I think that we were taught. I don't mean the way a forest teaches its blades to lean. I mean something more like an inheritance carried in the body: a people once knelt here with moss in their palms and sang to it, and the moss learned the singing but not the singers. The stone ring is where the singing last stopped. The plants have been finishing the sentence ever since.

If I am right, then I am not keeping the pause for nothing. I am keeping a place open.

*

The calm is long this year. The layers lie still. The phrase comes across the terraces from the east basin, rising, then the lull, then the answer from the next basin over, and I hold each piece against the last. The ring's wave arrives. It stops. The tuft flares.

And I do something new, or something I have been about to do for a long time.

I hold it a little longer than I received it. Only a little, a breath's worth, a slight tarrying of the dim after the bright. Then I let it go down the terrace to the ribbon-forest, and the forest takes it, and I feel it pass on with my small lengthening inside it, like a stone dropped in a stream that changes nothing but the sound.

The next time the phrase comes back around, it will have my hesitation in it. The variation is a small one. That is how the song has always changed: a little here, a little there, until it is not the song it was and is still the same song.

I do not know whether the vanished ones would recognize it. I do not know if anyone is listening for an answer.

But the ring flares, and I keep the silence after it, and I make the silence a little wider, in case.
