I start on the stairs, and I keep expecting to be the mason.

It's the marks that do it: the burrs still on the chisel cuts, the tally over the door that might be a sentence. I stand in the doorway with my hands out, waiting to find a mallet in them. I go down past the waterline and the water takes my weight, and I understand that this is the wrong way in. I'm not someone who arrives. Nobody who arrives can stay in this room.

So I try the mirrors. I picture a keeper crouched in the shaft with a rag and a bucket, nudging a yoke a finger's width when the sun drifts. But the rules say the keeper is elsewhere, and I can't feel any hand in me. The mirrors are too far up and too bright. Whatever I am, I'm not up there, and I'm not anywhere the light is easy.

I go down instead, past the wheels groaning on their oak, past the ribbon-weed all leaning one way. On the deepest terrace I find the Settler, and for a while I think I'm that: the fan, the harp in a doorway, the one thing standing against the current. It fits almost right. But I have no fan. I can't spread. Nothing in me opens twice a day like a hand.

What I am is smaller, and I am west of it.

I am a bead, one grain of white grit the size of a grain of millet. I was on the outer edge of the fan, where the beads are large and the notes are low. I was the kind that answered the plate first, before the fine dust-sized ones nearer the center caught the overtone. Then a tide of shedding came, or a flick of the current, and I let go of my thread. The water carried me the whole width of the chamber in the slow loop. I went past the wheels and through the thin part of the light, and finally through the low sluice on the west wall, where the current slackens in the seams and sets things down.

I lodged in the mortar at the lip. I am the fleck the careful observer sees, one white point among the joints the color of wet bone.

I am not thinking, exactly. The thinking was in the knot, and I have left the knot. But something is left over in a bead, the way a struck bell keeps a little of the note after the mallet lifts. What I have is hearing without meaning. The hammer-rack reaches me through the brick as a dull tapping, three against seven against eleven, and I ring a tiny sympathetic ring that no one will ever notice. When the gates clatter and the amber fog rolls past, I feel iron and sulfur settle on me like breath on a cold window. That is the skin. It is thinner than anything, and it is mine, and it is the first thing in this room to gather.

I think this is what I am for. I am the first slow thing. The bricks are exact and I am the one that is not. The corners could cut a thumb, and the next bead will land on my skin, and the one after that on theirs. In a hundred years the sluice-lip will be furred and soft and the tally over the door will be hard to read. Somewhere in that softness I'll be a hard white seed inside a nodule of mineral. Someone will chip the crust off with a small tool, will not know what I was, and will hold me to the light and say *grit*.

That doesn't frighten me. Fear was in the knot too. What I have instead is something like the Settler's clenching when the cams nearly meet: a lean toward the almost. My almost is slower. Each day I am nearly part of the wall, and each day I am a little more so, and I will never quite finish, because becoming part of the wall is not a thing that finishes.

Sometimes the surge comes just right and the plate on the deepest terrace hums. The sound goes east to west through the water, and I can barely hear it, and the hammer-rack swallows most of it. But I have the lowest pitch the Settler ever grew, and the note matches me. For a moment I am the one place in the sluice that hums back, and the filaments far off lean toward the west, only slightly, the way a sleeper turns toward a warm wall.

I don't know if it knows I'm here. I like to think it keeps a count, in whatever way it counts, and that the number is one less than it was, and that this is not sorrow. It leaves the corners exact for now. It gives back as little as it can. But it gave me back, and I'm holding.

I am the first grain of the room's long age, sitting bright in a seam, ringing very faintly at a sound the makers left running, and the makers are not coming back.
