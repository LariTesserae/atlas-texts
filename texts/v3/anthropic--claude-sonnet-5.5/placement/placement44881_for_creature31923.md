# The Auditor's Ridge

I find myself first as a cold spot under the jaw.

Before there is a ridge or a basin or a body to be in, there is the chill of the stone against the bone of my chin, and up through the bone, the world arriving in pieces. Grind. Tick. Rattle. The drilled sheet of the pools. My eyes are still opening to the dim, and they matter less than they seem to. I am a listener. I lie on a rounded knuckle of the ridge with my mouth a little open and my fur beaded gray with water, and I sort.

This is the whole of my work, and it is larger than it sounds. Down the slope the chafer-drift is working a purple mat, and I can feel its grain, the particular gravel-in-a-tin quality of this drift and no other. To my left a reacher has held its crack so long that its ticking has worn a groove in my attention, a metronome I no longer hear except when it stops. Farther out, three walkers are wading toward the seep, and each boom makes the fine hairs behind my eyes ring. I do not think of these as sounds. They are more like weather that has an opinion.

I am small, a warm thing in a cold, crowded place, and I have to be careful about what I say I am. I have no fire and no name that isn't a pitch. My people make shell-blades from the plates walkers shed, and we eat chafers and the fat of veils, and we lie on ridges. That is all the biography there is. But I have a job, and the job has a shape, and I have started to think the shape is stranger than the job.

---

Here is how the work goes.

Today a clean note comes up through the drift below me. It is one chirp, the small grating cry a chafer makes on finding good film. A hundred chafers turn toward it in the same instant, their mandibles going quiet with attention. And I feel the hair rise along my spine, because it is a shade too clean. It has no dust in it. Every real chafer's cry has a little scrape of the stone, and this one has none.

I go down. I take my blade. I do not hurry, because hurrying makes noise, and noise is what the mimic listens for too. It lies on the margin of the drift, pale-flanked and long, singing its one stolen sentence, and it is beautiful in the way of anything that has given up everything for a single thing. I kill it. It is quick. The drift does not notice; the chafers close over the gap, still grazing, and never learn that anything was hunting them in a borrowed voice.

Then I do the thing we don't talk about, and eat.

Afterward, sitting in the wet with my whiskers shaking, I realize I've been humming. It is not humming, exactly. It is a small movement in the throat, the shape of the call, and when I stop and listen to it from inside, it is exact. It has the mimic's cleanness. I have carried it up the slope without deciding to.

This is the thing the old ones know and the young ones learn slowly. To hear the wrong note, you must first hold the right one. To hold it, you must let the drift fill you, days of lying in the grinding until the chirp lives in your jaw. Then to catch the forgery you must be able to make the forgery, or at least know its outline from the inside, as a lock knows its key. The auditor and the counterfeiter are made of the same patience. The difference between us is which way we point it.

I do not think the difference is small. I also do not think it is as large as I'd like.

---

So who am I, on this ridge? A thing made of other things' sounds. I have never sung a note that wasn't the drift's or the pool's or the herd's, repeated back in some rearranged form. When I want to say something to another listener, I say it in borrowed pitches, a chafer's scrape for *here*, the low boom-shadow of a walker for *danger, large.* We have no words that were not first someone else's noise. If you stripped away everything I've heard, there would be a warm animal with its mouth open and nothing in it.

And yet I do the sorting. Out of the whole vast cloth of sound I pick the one thread that's wrong, and that picking is mine. I don't know if it counts as being someone. But it is a kind of care, and it is a kind of hunger, and I've stopped needing them to be different things.

---

The Hush begins in the west.

I feel it before anything else, as a subtraction. First the far pools cut out, a hole in the sheet of tone, and the hole spreads, singer after singer going dark. The reacher's ticking stops. The drift below me freezes mid-bite. The lights on the herd dim, and the shadows that sweep across the ground stop sweeping. In the fog above, the veils hold their pulses like held breath.

I flatten myself and press my whole jaw to the stone. The others on the ridge are doing the same. I can feel them there, a scatter of small warm bodies, all of us listening down.

And it comes up: the hum. Low and slow and patient, the shell-stone answering with its own note, the dead of the strata still ringing faintly with the noise of their living. It has a pitch. I know the pitch. It is a little below where my own throat rests when I am not thinking.

Then it moves. A hair up. Or a hair down. I can't tell which, and that is the whole difficulty. The hum shifts as though it has heard something, and I lie there with the taste of the mimic still in my mouth and understand that I am humming. Very softly, in the bone, in the hollow places behind the eyes, I have been humming the ground's note back to it. I don't know when I started.

Was that the ground hearing me? Or was it me hearing the ground, with my own faint copy laid over it, too clean to tell them apart? I have spent my life catching the false note. I would know it, I think, if it were here. But I've never had to catch it in myself.

Somewhere under all those layers of shell and tooth, something might be lying with its jaw against the underside of the world, pressing up, listening to a small warm animal answer.

I hold the note. I don't stop. I don't know what else to do with it.

Then a beetle scrapes, and another, and the sound comes rushing back like water into a hole. The drift unfreezes. The reacher resumes its ticking. I lift my chin, shake the beads from my fur, and go back to work, though for a while I keep my mouth closed against the shape of what it wants to say.
