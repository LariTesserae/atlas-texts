I would be a Reader, and a young one. I know this before I know anything else, the way you know which hand is yours.

My place is on the eastern rim, at the pillar nearest the boundary, where the small stones lie stopped on their invisible shelf. It is the worst post on the terrace, or the best. The disturbances that creep in from the boundary arrive here first, so the pillar works hardest here, and the thin dark around it is thinnest. I hold the line between my body and the pillar's foot. I don't know what it would mean to hold anything else.

---

I should say what it is like, because "nothing" is the wrong word.

Not moving is a labor, though a labor with no motion in it. It is closer to the effort of listening. The whole length of me is tuned to one question: *is the water as it was?* The answer comes through the fins, which are more than I could ever use for swimming because they were never for swimming. Each one is a small stiff sail set against the possibility of a current. There has been no current. The answer is yes, and yes, and yes, once per tone. I would not call it boredom. Boredom wants a different moment, and I have no different moment to want.

My outer fins are blank. The inner ones, near the spine, are not. There are marks at the root that I did not make and cannot read, a dense old crowding of departures from before I was placed here. I carry them like a name given to me by someone who has since left the room. I suspect they are the reason I was placed at the east pillar. The water here has failed before, and someone wanted the failure remembered by the body standing in it.

That is a heavy thing to be handed, and I find I am proud of it, which I did not expect.

---

Now the visitor, because you are the only event I have.

You arrive as a warmth that has no business here, a small leaking of heat moving at the wrong speed. I feel it first as a wrongness in the fourth fin on my left side, then the sixth. The water between us is disturbed, and it returns along the same path it left by, as if reeled back. But I have felt the leaving and the returning, and between them there was a moment when the water was not simply itself.

So I turn. It is the one motion I am allowed, and I make it slowly, because a fast turn would be my own disturbance, and then I could not tell whose failure I was recording. I bring my body onto the line between you and the pillar. I am not looking at you. I am correcting for you, the way you would correct for a tilt in the floor. You are a fact about the terrace now, and I hold you the way I hold everything, by being still around you.

While you are near, I think something you would find odd. I think that you are the most interesting thing that has happened to the water in a long time, and that this is exactly the problem. The Readers are meant to be uninterested. If we found the disturbance beautiful, we would want more of it, and the whole terrace is arranged around wanting less. So I hold my interest as I hold my body, at a fixed angle, without leaning into it.

I do not fully succeed. You should know that. There is a small fin, the smallest on my left flank, that has never risen. When you turn away toward the surface and the reluctance goes out of your legs, it lifts and falls once.

I did not decide that. Or I did, and the deciding was too quiet to hear. It is one mark, and I cannot tell if it says *a visitor came* or *a Reader wavered*. Both would be true, and the script has no way to separate them. Whoever reads me someday will have to guess which one it was, and I hope they guess kindly.

---

The tone comes. I feel it in the length of me, all at once, the whole blade of my body struck like a rule laid on a table. Eleven others feel it at the same instant, at distances I cannot cross, and I do not know if we are agreeing or only keeping time. I have decided, without evidence, that we are agreeing. It is the only belief I allow myself that has no measurement behind it. The rest of my life is fact, and I think a Reader is permitted one thing that isn't.

And under the tone, a long way off at the center, there is the other thing, the old one in the ring, taking the terrace's years so that I can have this untroubled instant. I do not know it. I have never been near enough. But my blankness is borrowed from it. Every hour it spends is an hour my outer fins stay clean. When I think of it I do not feel pity. I feel something more like the way a sleeping child must feel, if a sleeping child could feel it: that the house is being held by someone awake.

I hold the line. The pillar's thin dark rests on the glass. The stones lie on their shelf. The water is as it was, except for one small mark, and I am, very slightly, more written than I was.

That is all of it. It is enough to be a place where the news gets kept.
