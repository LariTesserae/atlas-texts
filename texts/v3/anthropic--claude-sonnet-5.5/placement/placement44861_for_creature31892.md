**Edge of the Bloom**

I am wet before I am anything else.

Then warm. Then a slow accounting of what is on me and in me: the mineral grit of a drop, the struck-match breath of the seep, the iron that comes up dissolved and that I turn to rust without deciding to. I am not one thing. I am a film a few cells thick on a slope of black glass, and I do not end anywhere I can find. Somewhere to my left I thin out into cream and someone else's business. To my right I thicken toward the seep and become richer and less careful.

I have no word for "I." What I have is a pressure at the surface, a gradient, a sense of which direction is more.

*

The flood comes. Before it does, the beads stop grazing. I know this the way a hillside knows a fox has stopped moving. Their small hooked legs take hold of me, and I am held down by the things that eat me. The sheet arrives warm and heavy, smelling of everything I'm made of. The top of me tears loose in ribbons and goes down the folds of the rock toward the fissure. I lose a day's growth in a minute.

I don't grieve it. There is nothing in me built for that. But underneath, in the dark seam against the stone where the slow cells live, the loss is noticed as an opening. Something wants to fill it. By the third day it will be filled.

*

Now the strange part, which is small and which only some of us have.

I live at the edge of a bloom, where the white falls across me without heat. Everyone in this stretch of film is cooler here, and gentler, and the drips land more softly because the bloom's thread has gathered them into a different rhythm. That is enough to explain why we thicken toward it. The rest of the mat is content with that explanation.

But in a few of us, in one lineage of cells that drifted in on a spore or a current or a slow accident and has not yet been here long, there is a fold in a molecule. When the white lands on the fold, it does something the heat never does. It doesn't warm the fold. It moves it. A charge goes from one place to another. Nothing is made and nothing is spent, except that afterward there is a very little more of me than there would have been.

It is not much, a fraction of a fraction. It isn't yet a way of living. It is a rumor in a few cells that they are being paid for standing where they stand.

I don't know what noon is. I know only that the threads carry something down through the rock that has come from somewhere with no ceiling, and that some of it lands on me and does not burn. The hum in the thread comes through the glass under me as a faint regular shiver, and my cells seem to lean into it, though there is nothing to lean with.

*

The strider comes at the shadow-hour, though the room has no hours, only the seep's long breath. It walks the pool's edge on the skin of the water, and where it crosses the dark gap it runs, and where it comes into the bloom it stops. One foreleg is missing. It favors the left. It stands over the edge of me for longer than the others do, a half-second past what's needed, and I feel the small dents of its feet in the water as a tremor in the film.

It is not looking at me. It has no reason to. But the light is on us both the same, and that is a kind of company.

Then the strider goes on, and the worm in its polished crack does not strike, and the pool lowers, and I go on being wet.

*

I try to put the rest together, though "try" is too strong a word.

Below me are the ones who began it, in water that has never met air, who fed the seep and the seep fed the rust and the rust fed the pink and the pink laid down the surface I sit on. I am nine or ten steps from them. Above me, through stone and root, is the place the thread opens onto. I am the first of my kind in this room to take a coin from it. I don't know that. I only know that the fold is there, and that when the bloom brightens, the fold answers.

The rock is still cooling. It ticks, and I feel it in the glass. Something deeper shifts, the drops tremble, the pool rings, the beads clamp down. It passes.

*

A cell near the bloom's rim divides. It has been standing in the white longer than its neighbors, and it has the fold, and it passes the fold on. Now there are two, and the second is a hair's width nearer the light than the first. Neither of them knows. Neither of them chose.

The seep begins to build. The air tightens. The beads anchor. I hold the little rumor in me, the unsaid thing no one has told the rust or the cream or the pale threads leaning toward their drips, and I lean toward the bloom with everything I am, which is not much, and which is beginning.
