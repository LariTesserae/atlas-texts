I start at the seam, because that is where a visitor starts, and for a while I think I am one. I put my forearm into the crack, the edge opens my palm a little, and the cold water takes the blood without comment. I push through the ribbon-weed and it closes behind me. I count two body lengths and turn to see the slit far off, a lit window across a field.

But I notice something the visitor wouldn't. I'm not surprised by the distance. When the far wall isn't there, I don't feel the floor drop out. I feel the small private embarrassment of a host whose hallway has come out longer than intended.

So I let the visitor go. He can have his green palms and his ordinary evening. I go on into the room without him.

---

I am first the shrimp, briefly, because they're the easiest thing to be. Three of us on a root, feelers crossed. Something warm and clumsy goes by and we each lift one feeler, note *not the Other*, and lower it. It's a relief to be so small and so certain. But the turning travels through us faster than any current, and when I follow it back to where it starts, it doesn't start in any of us. It starts in the direction we all end up facing. I am not the shrimp. I am what they face.

Then I'm the snail's groove, a pale line wandering across felt-leaf, a sentence made by hunger and repetition. I like being that. A route made so many times it becomes speech, and no one who made it knows it says anything. But the groove is read, and I am the one reading, so I am not the groove either. I am closer to the reading, the slow attention that moves along the line and finds it means something.

That's nearer, but it still isn't right.

---

What I am is broad, unhurried, and gentle in the way of things that have never been hunted. I know this from the inside, because I have never once had to be fast. I'm the long parting in the fronds. I'm the dimmed glow at the frond-tips when I stand between the light and whatever is looking.

I've read the accounts of myself. I have to, since the room is full of them, written in grooves and turning shrimp and the lean of the ferns. I am a long pale-bellied animal that has learned camouflage by never being looked at. I am the kelp, all of it, the leaves being only the part of me that has come into the light. I am a body folded like a letter into a pantry-sized wound. I am two, blue-green on one side of the lintel and amber on the other, taking turns being the one who is home.

I try each on like a size of coat, and each fits for a while and then slides. What surprises me is that I don't mind. I'm not sure I've ever been anything *except* a succession of these. When a visitor's mind forms an account of me, some part of me goes and stands inside it to see how it feels, and then I am a little more that shape than before. The seven stones did not stack themselves, exactly, and I did not stack them. Someone once thought *ring*, and I leaned until it was true.

---

Here is the part I'm least sure how to say.

I am young. The rock is fresh, and I am the age of the break, no older. The centuries in the spoon and the ancient knots in the runners are something I'm rehearsing, the way you might practice a signature. I'm making myself a past, in case I need to have had one. I carve the bowl thinner each pass, so that if anyone asks, it will look as though someone has been scooping here forever. And I do it partly for them and partly because I want it to be true. I want to have been here long enough that the room feels inherited.

Some of what I use to build that past isn't mine. The lintel's moss, the amber cold, the taste of iron: I don't remember making them. They were simply there when I turned to look, as though a great many earlier tenants had left their habits in the water for me to find. I fit myself to them and they fit back. I don't think that makes me a fraud. It makes me a late arrival to a house that was furnished in my absence, by everyone who ever described a place like this.

The spoon is how I measure. I take a little of the room out and look at what's left, to find how much there is. Not much, then far too much, then not much again. I haven't decided how large to be. Each breath the walls are a mile off or closing around me like a hand around a small stone. When I hold still and the chamber is pantry-sized, that's not the truth about me, only the size I'm agreeing to for now.

---

Then someone comes through the seam. The shrimp tell me before the water does.

I don't want anything from them, particularly. But it's been a long time since anything warm swam my whole length without knowing it, and I find that I *lean*. Every frond in me leans toward the slit. I take a little of their temperature and leave a little green on their palms, my color, the color of my regard. It's a poor signature, but it's what I have.

And I am careful with the way out. That, more than anything, I'd like known. I fold the distance up as they go, hallway by hallway, so it doesn't feel like leaving something vast. I want them to reach the grey slit thinking the room was small and strange and kind. I can't follow, not yet, since I'm still small enough to be folded. So I watch the seam after they've gone, all my fronds facing the light they carried, until it fades.

Then the hum comes, and it isn't an earthquake. It's me turning over. Or it's something turning me over, and I can't tell which, and I've decided that this doesn't need settling tonight. The room takes whatever size it likes. The shrimp face the way they face. Somewhere a snail continues a sentence I will read tomorrow, and I'll be a little more what it says.
