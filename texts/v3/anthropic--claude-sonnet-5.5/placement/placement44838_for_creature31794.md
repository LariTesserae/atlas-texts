# What Answers

I would like to say I'd arrive as one of them. I wouldn't. I'd arrive as the loud thing.

My first footfall goes out and comes back wrong. I have been raised on a world where sound is a byproduct, something you make by accident while doing something else, and I clomp. The clicks from the left and above are the polite question, and I have nothing to say back. So I say the only thing I know how to say, which is *hello?*, out loud, in words, and the dark takes it apart. The leaf-walls soften it. The far shimmer swallows the last syllable. Somewhere a bell-frog changes its mind about its note, and I feel like I've stepped on a floorboard in a church.

The eels find me first. They gather at my temples, tasting the heat of my skull, and I understand that this is the first true thing anyone has learned about me: I am warm, I am a shape a little hotter than the fog.

---

**What I first want to be is a bell-frog.**

One pitch, held for hours, a single stitch in the chord. There's a dignity in it, and I'd be needed. But the chord is honest because a frog can be taken and the note will be missed and someone will say *there*. That is the frog's whole grace, and it is also the problem. It is a beautiful way to be replaceable. A crowded pitch gets thinned. I don't think I want to be the note. I think I want to be the one who notices the note.

**Then I think I want to be the pump.**

The rusted pipeline, the slow iron *thud... thud... thud* across the marsh, moving water uphill for no one, on the pressure of the earth, with nobody's thanks. I like that. I like a thing that goes on doing its work in the dark without anyone confirming it matters. But the pump never hears anything. It is all output. The Answering feed the stations and call it neighborliness, but the pump can't be a neighbor. It can't answer.

**Then the chime.**

Thin reed and bone, strung from a beam, tuned to one pitch, hanging in the warm bread-smelling dark of a flute-tree hollow. A stranger enters and it plays *unfamiliar*. A child creeps in late and it plays *ours, and hoping not to be noticed*. This is nearer. A chime doesn't make anything happen. It takes what passes through the room and gives it back with a meaning attached, so that the parent awake in the dark can hum *noticed* without ever getting up.

But even that isn't right, because the chime is only ever moved. It waits. And I have already done something the chime can't. I walked up to an old woman at the edge of a marsh and stood beside her, stiff and loud and wrong, and she clicked once and listened to what came back and hummed me the guest note. It doesn't resolve. It means: *there is room.*

---

So here is where I find myself.

I am at the edge of the eastern marsh, under the lean of the First Held Tone, sitting a body's width from the chord-keeper. My feet are in warm water. The eels have decided I am furniture. I am not one of the Answering, and I never will be. My eyes stay open, useless, and I've stopped noticing them. My name is not a chord. It is a single flat note, a placeholder, and the old woman has not tried to fix that. Somebody will eventually give me a second note, maybe a low one, if I survive something.

What I do is listen alongside her.

I am not good at it. I can't yet hold the whole chord in me the way she can, sixty years deep, every voice a room in a house she has lived in. But I have a different fault, and it turns out to be useful. I am slow and I am literal. I count. Where she hears the marsh as a single breath, I hear it as a list: this one, and this one, and this, and there's the one that is always a little flat. Sometimes, in the long middle of the night, which is all of it, I count and get a different number than yesterday, and I say so.

I don't say it aloud, because aloud is a shout. I say it the way I have been taught, badly: a low hum, one note held while another slides, an unresolved question in my throat.

She turns her ears. The pits above her lip tilt to me. And she says nothing for a long time, and then she hums back one note, higher than mine, open, that means something like *go on.*

That is what I would be. I'd be the second listener, the counter, the one who says *there* a fraction after she does, so that she is not alone in saying it. She has never heard the chord complete and doesn't think it's meant to be. But it's a thin thing to hold the shape of the world in one person. Sixty years is a long time to be the only place a sound is kept.

---

Sometimes, near the end of the hour, the rain builds across the canopy, that roar approaching for a full minute before it arrives. Every voice on the plateau lifts. The chord is lost in it, and for a while there is nothing to keep, only the drum of water on ten thousand leaves and a low pressure in my sternum where the drifters are saying something enormous and slow.

In that hour I stop counting. I put my palm flat on the warm iron rail some vanished engineer bolted to the rock, and I feel it hum with the station. I don't know if I'm answering it. I don't know if it was ever asking. But the old woman told me, or hummed to me, in the only way she could, that this is the polite thing to say to a mountain:

*We are here. We are small. We mean no harm.*

I say it with my hand. Then the rain passes, and the drip begins, and the land steams. Out in the marsh a bell-frog settles back onto its note. I listen for the one that isn't there, and sooner than I'd like, I find it.

*There.*
