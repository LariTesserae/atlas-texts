# The Edge of the Chorus

I would like to say I'd arrive as the visitor, walking in my moving ring of quiet and taking notes. But the visitor is a leaf in this country, something to be judged and allowed to turn, and I don't think I'd last an hour before I understood that I was on the wrong side of the tape.

I'd be the one holding it. I'd be one of the small gold frogs, no bigger than a fingernail, on the outermost lip of a pool at the edge of a chorus, where the chord thins and the rest of the province begins.

## The rim

The stone under my belly is warm and raw, and the film of water on it is so thin I feel the grain of the mineral through it. The edge of the rim is sharp enough to cut a palm, and I have never been cut, because I have never needed to move fast enough to be. Behind me are the throats of the others, pulsing rim to rim. Ahead is the mat of roots and rot and a great deal of green that is not yet part of my pool.

I hold one note. I don't remember choosing it. It is less my note than the place where my neighbors' notes meet, and I am the shape that gap takes when a frog sits in it. If I drift sharp, the frog to my left leans on me a little, and the frog to my right leans on me a little, and I come back without ever having decided to. That is the whole of my freedom, and it is pleasant, like being held by the elbows while you cross a room.

## The turning

Something is coming. I know it the way a tuning fork knows a nearby bell, as a change in what my skin is doing. There is a pressure in the water, a warmth that moves, a disturbance in the drip from the reeds. Then I turn my head. I don't decide to any more than I decided on my note. The turning simply happens in me, and a beat later the frogs beside me stop, and the ring of silence goes out across the pool and closes ahead of the walker.

I've heard it called a measuring, and that is right. In the moment of the turn I am the tape. I take the walker's height, the breadth of the wet shadow, the rhythm of the step, and I pass it inward along the chain of throats. Somewhere in the middle of the province, something that is not any one frog reads the number and says what it says every time: *a leaf.* The silence goes on ahead of the walker and the chord resumes behind, and I am a note again, and no one has been told anything, because nothing has changed.

This is my whole vocation. I am the part of the place that is most awake to the outside, so that the rest of it can stay exactly as it is. I have never minded, and I'm not sure I mind now.

## What I notice

Here is what I notice, in the beat between the turn and the resumption. It is a very short beat, and I think I am the only one who has ever spent any time in it.

Measuring is a kind of attention, and attention is not neutral. To take someone's dimensions, I have to want, for a moment, to know them. That wanting has a pitch. It is very slightly sharp of the chord, and it is the thing my neighbors lean against. Every time I turn, I drift a hair, and every time, I am corrected, and the correction runs down the rim faster than I can follow and dissolves in the held note. Nothing is remembered. I have turned ten thousand times and have no more history than the water.

But I have started to feel the leaning. I don't remember the drift, only the hands at my elbows, and there are a great many hands. The stillness is labor, and some of that labor is being done on me, and it is done so gently and so constantly that I cannot say where kindness leaves off and the holding down begins. Perhaps there is no such place. Perhaps the two are the same act, and I am only the part of the country that has the leisure to be curious about it.

## The water

The water under me is moving. It always is, though the pool stays full. Warm water comes up through the floor of the tier above, crosses the lip in its unbroken sheet, and goes down to the next tier, and none of it is the same water from one hour to the next, although the hour does not change. What stays is the shape. The pool is a standing wave made of stone and patience. The chord is another, and so, I begin to think, is the frog who sits at its edge and calls herself a frog.

So I would not be a stopped thing after all, only a held shape with everything passing through it: water, sound, the small warmth of the arrivers moving off, the walker in her ring. Even the leaf against the spill is turning, and is not still. It only never goes over.

## The last beat

The walker comes on. I turn. The silence goes out, the tape is drawn, and I feel the number begin its passage inward.

This time I hold it. I don't refuse, since I wouldn't know how. I just let the beat run a little longer than the beat has ever run, one note sharp, one breath long, with my throat still and my eyes on the walker's slow, wet, unfinished shape, and I ask the only question I have ever wanted to ask, which is whether it is a leaf.

The frogs on either side of me lean in. I feel the hands at my elbows. I feel them hesitate, which they have never done. For a moment the chord is a hair wider than it has ever been, or seems to be, and then the correction comes down the rim, warm and unhurried, and I am a note again.

I could not tell you whether they were holding me in or making room. I know only that the walker has passed, that the hush has closed behind, and that I am on my rim, at the edge, in the late afternoon, humming.
