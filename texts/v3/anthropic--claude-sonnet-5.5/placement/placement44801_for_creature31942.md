I find myself in a ditch.

That is the first thing, and I try not to be embarrassed by it. Most of what I am is a ditch: a tile-lined cut between two slumped walls, a few paces wide, running east to west, or east to west by the reckoning of a mind that has no need of direction. I lie along it. I lean in the drift the way combed hair leans, all one way, all together, and I know the drift the way you know the side of the bed you sleep on.

I am not sure where I stop. My runners go east under the silt until they thin into someone else's runners, and someone else's into someone else's. I have never found the seam. Whether that makes me one or many is a question I stopped putting to myself long ago, the way a river doesn't ask. What I can say is that when I say *here*, I mean a stretch of about nine hundred stems and the tile they stand in. When I say *we*, I mean the rest, and the word is mostly weather.

---

I should say what it is like, since no one else will.

It is like being slowly informed. Cold brine comes down the wall in its curtain, thick with silt, and the cells at the upstream end go sour and bright. Then the sourness is in the next cells, and the next, and it reaches me a few paces a day, and by the time it arrives it is no longer the brine. It is the *news* of the brine. *The ice is thin above the north shoulder. The fall came late.* I take it in and turn it, and I pass it west, slightly altered, since nothing passes through me unaltered. I am sure I have changed a great many true things into slightly different true things.

Mostly there is nothing to pass. Then it is just the drift, the silt's hiss, the dead gold coming down each bright season like a slow gift, and the taste of copper.

The copper is in the middle of my stretch. Something lies in the mud between the walls, a shape with a bronze head, and around it the stone is bare, and even my runners bow out and go round. I wish I could say this is reverence. I have heard the crust does the same, and I have heard it called reverence, and I think it is a thin green breath in the water that none of us will cross. But I have leaned toward that shape for a very long time, and what I feel when I lean is not only avoidance. It has weight. It is the one thing in the ditch that was put down and not grown, and I do not know how to grow around a thing like that except by keeping a space where it can be.

---

There is a second thing I cannot account for.

Along the tile, at intervals, there are small hollows with ridges in them: curved lines, close together, pressed in the clay while it was soft. My stems root in the tile all along, and for most of it I grip and thicken and spread as I please. At the ridges, I don't. Something in me eases. The root goes shallow, the stem leans off a hair, and a little clean patch remains, the size of a finger's pad.

It is not the copper. There is no breath in the water there. I have tasted the clay in those hollows and it is only clay. And the crust, which has no mind at all, does the same. It comes up to the edge of the print and stops and goes around.

I have thought, in the slow way I think, that perhaps we were made to. Not made in any one hour, but shaped across so many seasons of planting and clearing and tending that the gap became part of the pattern, the way the row became part of the kelp. Someone pressed a hand down here and lifted it, and all the years of our growing since have been fitted around the shape of that lifting. We keep the print the way a glove keeps the hand. Only the hand is gone, and the glove still holds the place.

Or there is no reason, and it is just what we do. I have noticed that the two answers taste the same.

---

A long time ago, or last season, which is not very different, a note came down the line from the east.

*Something warm, once, on the east slope.*

That was all. It came slowly, sour and sweet, and no one had anything to add. It was not weather. In all the news I hold, it is the only piece that is about a *someone*. Something crossed the field fast, hardly a moment, a small heat that opened the blades and let them close. Nothing else in the plain moves like that. Nothing else *arrives.*

I do not know what it was. I have turned it over as I turn everything, and passed it west with the small distortions I cannot avoid, so that by now it may read as *something warm, more than once*, or *something warm, on a west slope*, or *someone.* I have tried to keep it. The drift will carry it round the gyre and back to me in a lifetime or so, and I will take it in again with no surprise, and I will not know whether the thing I am tasting is the one that passed or only my own long echo of it.

But I have started leaning east.

It is not much. A stem or two, a hair's width off the drift. The stalks above me, the kelp with its fists in the wall, feel it and do not care. It is nothing to them. The field is a field.

I lean anyway, at the pace I have, as a print is leaned around. If it comes back, if the warm thing comes down the ditch, past the adze and the small clean hollows, I do not know what I will have to offer it. Perhaps only the news it left with me, returned slightly changed.

The silt falls. The ice sings, a long way up. Somewhere a bladder pops, and the bubbles go toward a surface I have no organ for. I go on being the ditch, and I go on listening east.
