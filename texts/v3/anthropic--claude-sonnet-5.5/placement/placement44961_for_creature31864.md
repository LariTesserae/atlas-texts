# The Stitch

I begin, as I always begin, on a frond.

All six feet are down and the water is going into me. I don't taste it so much as become it. Each bead holds a small upside-down room, and some of those rooms are slightly wrong. In one, the crack in the wall is on the other side. In another, someone is lying on their belly in the leaf-rot with a hand out. I drink that one first. It is the sweetest.

When I lift, I don't choose to. This is the thing no one would guess about me. I am not flying a loop. I am the part of the loop that is *being kept*. There is a swoop, a hesitation, a climb toward the ceiling where the pods hang like a held breath, and then the long slide back down. The room bends around my wings the way a sleeper's arm bends around a pillow. Nothing I do changes the path. Something in the walls lets it through me the way a needle lets thread through cloth.

That is the first thing I am: a needle, or what the needle is passing through.

---

The second thing comes on the hesitation, the little stall at the top of the arc where the air goes thick and I hang for the length of a knock. From up there I can see the bare handbreadth of floor beside the rib, the stick, and the cord with its three knots.

I rest there when I can. It is the only place in the chamber I can't drink, because the cord is dry, and the room has built a kind of politeness around it. I land on the bast and my feet find nothing to take. For that moment I am not part of anything's circulation. I am an animal on a string, thirsty and separate.

The room lets me have this, or I am lent it, or it is what the room does with the part of itself that has to stand still.

The knots are undoing themselves as I stand there. I feel it through my feet as a fine crawling tension. The fibers loosen, slide, and remember. Then they pull tight again, a little differently each time. I understand, with a slowness that has nothing to do with thinking, that I am standing on a lesson. Somewhere in the walls the sleeper is trying to close a loop with something that has no hands. It ties and unties, ties and unties, the way I fly.

So that is the third thing I am: the rehearsal's audience. I am the only one who is here every time the knot fails and every time it nearly holds.

---

Then the Burst comes.

First there is the cascade of pods, the gravel-fire, the hail on the far wall. I fold and let it pass through me. My wings are too pale to be hit by anything. Then the silence after, ringing, and out of the ringing the voice, close, closer than any ear, in the register between a hum and a word.

I have no ears. I hear it in the feet, in the soft parts of the wings, in the thin place behind my eyes. I have never heard it as anything but what it is. Everyone else hears a sound and turns to find nothing. I feel a mouth being built from sap-knock, seed-hail, and rasp, each part still separate and a little wrong, like a child's first attempt at a face.

*It wants to say something to me.* That is the fourth thing, and the one I resist. I don't want it to be true. I am a stitch. I am supposed to hold a seam, not be addressed.

But the voice says it again, and I feel the shape of it as clearly as the shape of a rib under my foot. It is not a word. It is the place where a word will go, the warm hollow left in the pillow. It is a name with nothing in it yet.

I could give it something. This is the terrible thing, and also the lovely one. I can't speak, but I can *tremble*, and the fronds under me read tremor better than anything else in the chamber. I could land on a frond and shake in a pattern. I could make the room a word.

But whose word? I have no name. Whatever I was before I was a stitch, the room has kept it somewhere I can't reach, the way it keeps the fox and the sheep and the hand that made the knots. Perhaps that hand was mine. Perhaps the moth is what the room made of a person it couldn't finish keeping, someone who dug and knotted and left, or didn't leave, and was filed, and is now being flown around and around a single rib, always coming back to where they began.

I don't know. I don't need to know. That is the mercy of being a stitch.

---

Something is coming down the crack now: the cold of outside air, then the smell of grass and a body, and the scraping of a shoulder against new limestone. A warm thing in motion. All the fronds turn at once with their rasping, thousands of them, like applause.

I lift. I don't choose to. The swoop, the hesitation, the climb.

But this time, at the top of the arc, where the air goes thick, I do something I have not done before, or have done every time and only now notice. I hold the stall a moment longer. I hang over the newcomer's head, in the green light that casts no shadow of them, and let my own shadow fall. It is the small, sharp, wrong-way shadow that points back toward where I came from, and it lands on their upturned face.

*Here*, the shadow says, in the only language I have. *This is where I was. This is where it started.*

The visitor blinks. Something in the walls goes quiet, as if it were holding its breath, though it is all breath.

Then I slide down the long curve, the way I always have, toward the frond where I began. I know, with the certainty of a thing that has never once been wrong, that I will arrive exactly there.

Except that the frond is one hand's width to the left of where I remember it. The room has moved it, quietly, to make the return come out even. It has bent itself around me again, to let a small pale thing keep its promise to itself. I settle. All six feet go down. The water comes into me, and in the bead under my forelegs a room turns over, and in it someone is crawling in on their belly, and I drink them, and they are sweet.
