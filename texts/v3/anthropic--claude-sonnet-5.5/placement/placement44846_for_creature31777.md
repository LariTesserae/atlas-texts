I would be one of the stone-colored, and I would begin as the wrong color.

I came across the seam between two flows on legs I have not finished counting, eight of them or ten depending on the day and which are bearing weight. I was born on the rust. My shell is rust, grown from rust dust, and now I stand on jade. Anything that looks could see me, if anything looked. The grazers do not look. The mites don't care. Only the shell knows it is out of place, the way a word knows.

So I stand, and standing is the work.

---

Stillness is not the absence of motion, the way it seems from outside. First the legs stop, and then the small tremors under the legs stop, and then something settles that I can only call *the ledger of myself*, all the little corrections I make to stay upright in a water that is never quite still. When that closes, the stone comes up through my feet.

It is not a sound. Sound is what the bubbles do, the ticking, the patter at evening. This is slower and comes from below. The basalt holds a long ache from having been fire, and it cracks in tiny increments, silica settling into new arrangements, and each crack arrives as a small bright point in a dark I don't have a word for.

Under those points there is something else, and I have been standing here four days trying to say what.

It is a pulse in the sand beside the flow, where the pale drift has heaped in the lee of a column. Very faint. A tightening, then a release, then the tightening a hand's width further on. It passes under me, and for the length of a breath I am not standing on the stone but on the back of something, and the something is going somewhere, unhurried, carrying me a body-length and setting me down again.

I don't think it knows I'm here. I think I am a small warm weight on a very large thing, and it is careful in the way a sleeper is careful, without deciding to be.

---

When the grazers come, they come in a pair, drifting, pale as candle-ends. One lays its bristles against a fern above my head and holds. I feel nothing from the fern, but through the stone I feel the sand tighten, just once. The fern's tips go pink. The grazer lifts its mouth and turns, and it moves off in a straight line, which is not how grazers move.

I want to be clear that I have no opinion about this. I am not a grazer. But I know what it is to be told something by the ground. It is the only way I have ever been told anything. The grazers taste the message and I feel it, and if there is a difference, it is only that they are permitted to walk away toward it and I am asked to wait.

Waiting is not lesser. The long stillness is how the shell learns the color of the place it stands on. In a season I will split down the back, and I will come out jade, and I will vanish. Some part of me is sad about that, and some part of me suspects the sadness is exactly the thing that is being asked for, a small soft grit in the machinery, so that the leaving-behind of rust is felt and not merely done.

---

Above me, fifteen meters up, there is a ceiling of silver that I have never touched. I know it only as a change in the taste of the water when the plumes lean the right way. The light that reaches me is gold-green and old, and I have been told, by whatever tells me things, that above the ceiling the world is blue and sharp, and there is a sky, and there are creatures who have seen it.

I do not want to go. I want that to be known. Wanting to know is not wanting to go. I would like to have been told that blue exists, and to hold it the way I hold the groan: as a fact that arrives through the feet, in a language of pressure, and asks nothing of me except that I stay a good listener.

---

Now the stone changes.

It is not the groan yet. It is the day before. Everything in me that was resting tightens, and I turn, and I am not the only one. Across the whole flow, and past it, and out over the ridges to the east, the others have turned with me, rust and jade and violet, each shell a different color and each body swung to the same bearing, toward the caldera, every leg gone still in the same instant.

No one decided this. I know no one decided, because I was here, and I would have felt it. It happened the way the fern-tide happens, the way a whisper goes down a line. I was told, and I turned.

I think the sand under us is the thing that knows. I think the thread under the sand is very old and not at all in a hurry. And I think, standing here with my wrong color and my waiting body, that it has been listening to the groan much longer than I have, and that it has been *answering*, by tides and by combings, by clearing rings around boulders and sending up snow. Maybe we are the ears it grew for the low frequencies. Maybe I am a small part of how a forest hears the mountain.

If so, I would like to do it well.

The tension climbs. The bubbles in the reed-stands thin and stop, as though the whole plateau were holding a breath. Under my feet the pulse goes by once more, slower now, and lets me down gently.

Then, far below, the door begins to open in the other house.

I do not move. I listen with everything I have, all ten of my legs, or eight, and the stone tells me that it is beginning.
