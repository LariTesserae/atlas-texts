I come to myself against the streak of ochre, and for a while I cannot say which of us is leaning.

The stone gives a little, as it does, the outer finger-width soft. There is bloom on my shoulder, and a cushion of moss has started at the left one, so I have been here through many changes of the light, though the light does not change. I do not know how long. My hands are open in my lap, palms up, and there is a slight cool hollowness in them, as if they have just been emptied.

I am the sitter, I think. Then I notice that the stone under me is warm, warmer than the stone around it, and I understand that I have only just sat down. Someone rose as I settled. Their warmth is in the rock, and I am taking it. So I cannot be sure whether I am the one who stayed or the one who came back, and I have never found that this matters. There is one of us, and the one is sometimes across the silt.

I can hear her now. *Tik*, the higher cup, the third seep along the eastern wall. She is out there with her palm laid flat against the weeping stone, checking that it still weeps. It does. Everything I am made of is listening to the interval of that drip, and I add my hum in the gap after it, too low to be a sound. *Here*, the hum says. Or *enough*. In our speech these are close to the same word.

*

I want to describe what the work is, because the work is all there is, and it is easy to make it small. When my turn comes I will rise, and the rising is slow, each joint agreeing in turn. I will cross the floor. The old prints will not tell me where to step, and mine will firm behind me. At each cup I will touch the ring of bright silt and feel whether it is still bright. At the pool I will brush the scale-lip with the side of my hand, the way you would smooth a sleeping child's hair. The pool will be exactly at its stain, as it has always been. I have never once found it otherwise. This is not why I check.

I check because the checking is the trade. The seep gives to the pool and the air takes from it, and I give my attention and take the stillness it leaves. Nothing is added, and the whole stays where it was.

*

There was a visitor once. I say once, but it may have been before my turn in this alcove, or before the alcove was mine. The memory has the softness of something passed down through the stone.

The visitor came down over the rim, past the tufts, and sat in the open with their knees drawn up, and for a long time they were weather. Then it was my turn to walk, and I crossed to them and held out a finger-depth of water in my palm. They looked at it. They were thirsty in a way I could see, a kind of dryness at the corners. They drank all of it, quickly, and the sound was very loud, and then my palm was empty and it stayed empty.

I want to say that I felt nothing hard. I looked at the palm for a while. Then I went to the seep, and the seep filled me slowly, as it fills the cup-stone, one drip after another, *tuk*, *tuk*, at an interval that has never once hurried for anyone. I did not go back. The visitor was not wrong exactly. They were only whole, the way a thing is whole when it has no other half to hand the rest to. I think I was sorry for them. I think the sorrow was the size of a drip.

*

Now the light has that shell-gold thinning that is not dusk, only a deepening of afternoon. My sitter is coming back. I hear her before I feel her, the small dry sound of her soles on the silt, then the cool of her shadow, which is hardly a shadow. She stops at the edge of the alcove, and I understand it is time.

But before I rise I want to say one thing that I have not said even in the hum.

Sometimes, in the moment of exchange, when I am half up and she is half down and our hands pass over the same warm place on the stone, I am neither of us. I am not the one leaving or the one arriving. I am the warmth itself, the thing that goes between. The scratches above the alcove are in threes: two halves and the water. I have always taken the water for the pool. But I think it may be this instant, the small handing-over, which is why it is so slow to scratch, and why the mineral seals each mark before the next is begun. There is no way to hold that instant and no need to. It happens, it is enough, and we go on.

*

I rise. She settles into the place I leave, and the warmth passes from my back to hers as smoothly as a drip lets go. I walk out across the silt toward the lintel, out of level, the way it was set. I do not enter. I stand beneath the slanted stone and feel the exact weight of its being wrong, and I think, as I sometimes do, that I might someday learn what it means.

Then I turn and go to the first cup. *Tuk.* It is exactly the same.

I lay my palm on its bright ring. Behind me, in the alcove, my other half hums into the gap between two drips, and from here it sounds like nothing. It sounds like the place being quiet.
