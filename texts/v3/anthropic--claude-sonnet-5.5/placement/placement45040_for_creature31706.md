I would like to say I would be a lantern. Drifting up the warm middle of the world with an amber spot pulsing on my side, carried around and around without any idea of it, looks like the easy kind of grace. But a lantern would not be able to answer this question, and I keep wanting to answer it. So I am not a lantern. I thought for a while that I might be a threader, alone in the glass hair with a long patience, the only creature in the Slack that ends anything. But even the threader's kill is folded back into the loop, and I don't think I could bear being a mouth the pattern uses. I would want to be something that argues with the pattern, even if it loses.

So I am a maker. I am standing at the edge of the largest salt flat with my palms lifted and cupped, and the wind is on the back of my neck, where it always is.

---

I should say what I know, because it is a lot, and none of it came through eyes.

I know the floor by its ring. Where the plates lie loose, a step gives a short bright *tik*. Where they are locked into a ridge, the sound goes dull and deep in my wrists. I know the salt by its cold. It is a pale absence in my brow-pits, a place the world has stopped saying anything. I know my cell by its warmth: Oru pressed on my left, the two young ones on my right, everyone keeping the inch. I know my scars by their order. There are eleven ladders on my left forearm, one for each time I was cut deeply enough that the cell sat down and counted with me, and a cluster on my flank that says I was born in the year the north herd thinned. I can read it with a thumb. Anyone can read me that way, and I find it restful to be a thing that can be read.

I know the row. That is the reason I am at the salt's edge, and the reason I have been coming here since I could walk on my knuckles.

---

The row is long, and thousands of needles have gone into it. Nobody living remembers the first one. Somewhere in it is a shard that stands wrong.

Every shard leans a little downwind, the way everything here leans, toward what has been. It happens without effort, because the wind presses the glass as it is pushed into the salt and the salt takes the tilt. But partway along the row, older than my grandmother's grandmother, there is a needle set perfectly upright. Someone set it plumb, against the wind, with their palms braced on either side to keep it from leaning. The lean-reading of the row breaks there like a held breath. Every shard afterward answers it.

Some answer by leaning hard downwind, as if to say *no, that was an error, look how everything falls*. Some answer by standing straight too, a few needles, then giving up. One long stretch is a fan of shards each leaning a very little less than the one before, as if someone were trying to walk a whole argument slowly back to upright without anyone noticing. I have read that stretch many times. I think it is an apology, or a temptation. I can't tell the difference by touch.

I think the upright shard says *new*. I know we have no such word. The nearest is *not yet counted*, and that is a different thing, a debt, not a claim. But I think whoever set that needle wanted to say something that the grammar of the wind would not carry, and did it with the only tongue the wind could not lean.

That is the argument. It is not "is the world a loop?" Anyone can feel that it is a loop. The argument is whether the loop is *all* there is. The row does not settle it. It is the settling-place where we keep it unsettled, and I have come to think that is what the row is for.

---

I have walked to the red vein once. I laid both palms on the warm rock and it was the only thing I have ever touched that felt like being looked at. I stood there longer than the others in my party. I remember the wind pressing my scars, and I remember the feeling that the heat was not the loop's, that it belonged to something under the loop. Then I walked back. That is the walk. I don't think I was wrong to come back. I only think I was told a shorter story than the one I felt.

I will probably go once more before I stop being able to feel the glass. I haven't decided whether I will walk back.

---

Today the wind is the same wind, and my palms find a disturbance in it.

It is on the glass first. A step, then another, too uneven, with a weight that doesn't know how to distribute itself. The *tik* comes far too loudly, and after each one the plate keeps ringing. Whoever it is doesn't know the glass has a voice. Then the heat arrives in my brow-pits, and it is wrong in every way: too bright, too tall, too restless, shifting like a fire that can't decide where to burn. It makes noise from somewhere in its middle, over and over, a small wet sound I gradually understand is breath.

I do not run. There is nowhere in the Slack to run. I do not call the cell. I lift my palms and I let the newcomer come, and I feel it hesitate, and I feel it begin to hold itself still, which is more than I expected. The breath catches and slows.

I go to it low, palms up, and stop an inch away. Its heat is on my face like a hand that hasn't decided to stay. I set one palm on the plate beside its foot and wait.

The glass says: not heavy, not light. Afraid, but standing. Not part of any ring. Carrying something I don't have a word for, as if it came from somewhere with a different loop, or none.

---

I do not take it to the cell. The ring is full, and I don't know what a plate outside the ring would mean. But I walk it, slowly, along the edge of the salt to the row, and I show it, with my hand over its hand, how to feel the lean of the shards. I don't know if it understands. It doesn't need to. It is being carried, in the only way I have of carrying.

At the end of the row I take a flake from my belt and strike a needle from the core, a small clean one. I kneel where the last shard stands, and I set the new one in the salt a hand's width beyond, and the salt closes cold around its foot.

Now the question I did not know I was holding: which way does it lean?

If I set it with the wind, this stranger is only *not yet counted*, a debt the loop will collect and fold in. If I brace it against the wind, with my palms on either side, it is the old wrong shard again, and the row begins a new argument, and the cell will go quiet when they find it.

I feel the visitor's heat behind my shoulder, waiting to be told what it is.

I put both palms down. I hold the needle plumb. It is harder than it looks, because the wind wants it, and my arms shake, and the salt is slow to take. But it takes.

Then I stay there a long time, kneeling with the glass ringing faintly all around me, and the vent's one low note going on somewhere overhead, and the lanterns turning through their orbits. They can't know what I have just done. The place is complete and is not waiting for anything, and I have set in it, very small, a thing that stands up.
