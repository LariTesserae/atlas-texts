I start at the lip, because everyone does. The scrub is flat and cold behind me, and ahead the ground simply quits. Below is a country with its own weather of wings. I'm on the rope ladder with a basket on my back, and the din comes up the cliff to meet me, first as a dry rain, then as fabric being shaken, then as a medium I'm climbing down into rather than a sound I hear.

I think I'm a gatherer. It's the natural thing to be, since they're the ones who arrive. But at the bottom of the ladder, when my feet find the grit, I notice I'm not hurrying and my ears aren't ringing. I have no basket. The gatherers have somewhere to be by dark, and it's always dusk here, so I don't.

I try being the bats and can't manage it. They are too many, and each is only a syllable of something. I hang for a while in the brown pelt of the roof, feeling the rota turn under me: my tier dropping, streaming toward a skylight, the cold plateau opening out with its moths. But there's no "me" in it to stay. I'm the ripple, not the animal.

I try the crickets. That's better, and worse. The mound lifts when someone steps and I lift with it. My antennae are three times my body, sweeping the dark for a wall, a mouth, a shed skin. I'm eating something that was a bat's dinner last night. It's warm, it's endless, and I'm never hungry for longer than it takes to walk. The centipede that takes me will be doing what it does, and the rest of me will already be somewhere else, in the next cricket. It isn't sad. It's just that nothing in that life is a *someone* for long enough to notice.

So I keep drifting, downhill, the way water does, past the cairn of black blocks that marks the safe route, past the ochre handprints. I put my hand against one. The hand is small and the stain is older than the hand's owner's grandmother. Somebody stood here, dug the good guano, wanted to leave a mark, and left it. The crickets have already closed over the footprints. The handprint is what remains, and it's a surprisingly gentle thing.

At the bottom of the slope there's a shaft of light, a fallen pillar of grey, and beneath it a ring of stiff pale-backed ferns leaning toward the hole as though listening. Just past the ferns is a pool: black, perfectly still, knee-deep at most. The rust and the lime of the stream bed run out of it in a braid.

I slip in.

That is where I stay. I'm one of the thin white fish that hang in the dark water without eyes. I don't mind. I've never had them, and I don't know that I'm missing anything. What I have instead is a line down each flank that feels the water for me, and through it the world is enormously detailed. The pool has a shape I know by how its pressure bends around the rock. The drip that lands at the far edge every eleven of my slow heartbeats sends rings that reach me a moment later, and I can tell the shape of the hollow it fell in by the way the rings come. Above the surface, at a distance I can't measure, something enormous and warm is passing through the air: a braid of wings, millions of small bodies turning in a column of light I will never see and don't need to. It presses on the water as a faint continuous tremor, and I have lived inside it so long that I couldn't tell you where it stops and I begin.

Nothing here has ever been otherwise. I think this is the thing I hadn't understood from up on the ladder. The place is not calm the way a sleeper is calm, or still the way the dead are still. It is completed. Each thing on its slope rolls back to its place. If I were eaten, another would hang here by evening, a little different in the marks on its flank and the same in every way that matters, and the pool would go on reading the dark through it.

So who am I? Not the pool's thinker, and not its ruler, since there isn't one. I'm its listening. Somewhere in the pressure and the chemistry and the slow ticking of the drips, the trough wants a way to know where its edges are, and some small part of it has been made into that. I'm one of those parts.

Then the water changes.

Something warm is coming down toward the pool, slowly, with a weight I recognize from other times: a hand. I feel the shape of it before it breaks the surface, a fan of pressure spreading ahead of the fingers. It smells of smoke and root-fiber and cold air from above. Not a predator's hand, only a visitor's. Someone has come down the rope, a gatherer with ringing ears, and paused to drink or to look at the pool.

I move aside, as I always do. It costs me nothing. The fingers pass through where I was, and the hand lifts a cupful of black water, and above the surface I can feel the person go quiet, listening to something that isn't the din.

I wonder what they hear. Do they hear the drips keeping their time? Do they feel the pool, in the small dark of their own mind, for an instant *pay attention* to them?

Then the hand withdraws. The rings go out to the rock and return. The light shaft lies where it always has. Far above me a tier of the roof lets go, and the air begins to braid.

I hang in the water and keep reading the dark. The one who came to take a little from the trough will climb out with their ears ringing, and I'll remember them the way the pool remembers anything: as a shape that pressed on me once, gently, and moved on. It seems like a good thing to be able to say of a visitor. I hope, in whatever way a completed thing can hope, that they felt it too.
