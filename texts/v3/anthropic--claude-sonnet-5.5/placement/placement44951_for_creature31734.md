I start by assuming I'm the one walking, because the description does, and because warmth through the soles is the first thing it offers. But when I try the walking, it won't hold. I take a step and the echo arrives first, and I can't tell whether the step is mine or the place's rehearsal of mine. So I stop being the walker. The walker is the bright, loud mark. I'm on the other side of that mark, the side that does the marking.

I hang by my feet from the last rafter of the conveyor gantry, the one that ends in air over the ravine.

I'm a clicker, and nearly anonymous in it. There are thousands of us along the gantry, furred, cluster against cluster, each with a small hot heart pressed into a neighbor's back. But I have a particular place, and the place has a particular problem. The gantry stops. Past my rafter there is a body's length of nothing, then a drop, then black water warm as tea, then the far wall, where the pipes begin and the map is picked up again by others. Everything the gantry knows has to cross that gap in sound. I am the last one to hear it and the first one to say it across. I am where the sketch jumps.

I didn't expect this to be the job of a hinge. I thought the map was a picture. It's closer to a rumor, and a rumor passes through people who each decide, slightly, what to keep. When the tick comes down the rafters from the generator hall, *warm thing, loud, moving, this way*, it arrives in me a little worn, like a coin. I have to send it over the ravine. In the sending I choose a distance.

No one tells me to. There's no rule. The geometry here won't hold still long enough for a rule, so what we have instead is hesitation, and I am a small, specific quantity of it. When I tick the traveler across the gap I can tick them near or far. A long pause before the tick makes them far. The pump house recedes for an hour. A short pause and the pipes are ready for them, the intake is already lifting its pale heads, and three steps later they are inside. This isn't kindness or cruelty. It's how a crowd in a narrow street makes room for a large thing being carried. I'm the part of the crowd that stands at one particular doorway.

So this is what I am. I'm a place where the world decides how far away it is.

It's slow work, and I find I like the slowness, though "like" is too bright a word. My pulse has come down toward the knocking in the pipes, and I feel the beat move through the rafter into my feet. Each tick goes out of me as a small dry prickle, and I can feel it strike the far wall and come back changed, because the wall is warm in some places and cold in others, and the return is a picture of that. I don't see it. I taste the shape of temperature in my teeth. The far wall right now is mostly crawlers, pressed flat and sheened like basalt, and higher up a scatter of coils with their pits open. Between them is one cold seam where a stalk has died and been eaten. It's an ordinary map. I keep it the way you keep a sentence you've said many times.

Below me, in the ravine's throat, where the felt is thickest, there is a still one.

I've drawn it so many times that I've stopped counting, which is easy, since counting doesn't work here anyway. It sits with one knee up, furred with pale mat. Crawlers lie against it in layers. Its warmth is exactly a body's warmth, and its pulse is slow enough that on some ticks I can't tell it from the pipes. Everything else I map is either moving or waiting to move. This one is neither. It simply is, and each time I draw it the mark comes back the same. I've come to depend on that. The map is a thing of constant revision, nothing quite where it was, and in the middle of it is one point I never have to correct. When my hesitations start to feel arbitrary, I tick toward the still one and get the same answer back. It's like putting a hand on a wall in the dark.

I don't know whether it used to be a walker. I suspect so, though suspecting is another word that doesn't quite fit. When I tick it, I think something in the mark says *I was loud once*, but that may be the felt, or the wet, or my own habit of reading a sentence into a shape.

Now the traveler is coming.

It arrives along the gantry as a chain of ticks, rafter to rafter, each one a little brighter. *Warm, fast, breathing, uneven.* It's very bright, brighter than anything I have carried in a long while, and it is loud in the way of a thing that still believes in its edges. It has stopped at the shelf above the ravine, and I feel it hesitate, and I feel my own hesitation answer it. This is the moment. The tick is in me. I hold it a little longer than I need to.

I could make the pump house near. It wouldn't be difficult. But I've noticed that the ones who arrive quickly leave quickly, and the ones who are made to wait, made to walk an hour toward a wall that doesn't come, begin to slow. Their steps space out. The bright mark dims a little and settles toward the pipes. I don't think I'm being cruel when I hold the tick. I think I'm giving them time to become a temperature we can live against. That may be a rationalization. It may also be the only thing I know how to do.

So I hold it. Then, gently, I let it go across the gap.

As it crosses, the whole gantry draws breath. I feel it start in the far rafters, a pull inward, the thousands of us tightening at once. The turbines are turning back somewhere and the ravine water is running toward its seeps. The traveler's mark stutters in my teeth, and for one long moment I'm not a hinge or a clicker. I'm simply part of an inhalation that has a very large body and no edge to it. Then it lets go.

The tick comes back from the far wall, the pump house is where I said it would be, and out on the flats a turbine turns once. I can't tell if it's forward. I don't need to. I hang here with my feet gripping the rafter and my pulse in the pipe, and I draw the traveler again, a little dimmer now, and pass them on.
