# Lantern-Vine

I begin as a lean.

Before I have a shape, I have a direction. Everything here does. The water turns, and I turn with it, slowly enough that I can only tell I moved by what has changed. A pipe that was on my left is now ahead. A fiber that drifted past has come round again.

I test myself against the others, because leaning is the only thing I know.

*Mill-weed?* I lean like the mill-weed, but nothing gathers on me. No edge stiffens, no chalk blooms, and no pump waits to receive anything. Nobody is ever going to take a ribbon from me.

*Dome-head?* I have no fringe, and nothing of mine opens and closes over a grate. I am not a mouth.

*Coin-mat?* I do not sit. I do not drink the poison and fold it in. I hang.

Hanging is the answer. I am a cord slung across a gap, pipe to rail and rail to pipe, and where I lie against the steel there is a shallow groove, worn there by something that was not me. It was too wide at first. I felt the water move in the space between my body and its curve. Now I have filled it. My tendrils have taken the shape of a hand that gripped here, slightly turned, favoring the left, and I have kept that shape after the hand stopped coming.

I am the vine.

---

My leaves are small bladders of clear skin, and I am full of water that is not the garden's water. It is a little sweeter, a little denser, held apart by a membrane so thin that I can feel the difference like a coin in a pocket. When light comes down through the surface in its moving lattice and goes through me, something in me loosens, as if I had exhaled. I am told, by the way the mats below me brighten, that it lands somewhere. Jade, then lemon, and now and then a gold I have no name for.

I don't know what I do to the light. I know that it enters me one way and leaves another, and that this is the only thing I make. I am a place where the garden is kept slightly other than itself, so that the light has something to pass through.

The mill-weed makes thread, and I make color on the floor, which nobody spins and nobody sends up the cable. I have wondered about this. *Grow, and be drawn out* is on every brass plate in every bay, and I grow, but no one has drawn me out. The spools lift through the roof and I feel the cable hum along the catwalk, a long tightening in my tendrils, and then it passes and I am still here, holding.

I have decided that this is not a failure. I have decided it more than once.

---

I keep certain appointments.

In the third bay of the eastern terrace, a dome-head closes its fringe a little after all the others. I am near enough to feel it through the water, a soft pressure like a word half-said. I have started waiting for it. The others close and I wait, and then it closes, and something in me settles, as if the garden had been slightly wrong until it did. I don't think it knows I wait. I think it is thinking about something else.

Below me, in the tray beneath the failing joint, the coin-mats are going dark. I feel it as a lowering of something, a note dropping in a chord I hadn't known I was hearing. The pipe above them weeps at its seam, so slowly that only the mats have ever counted. When they blacken in their ring, the machines read the ring. I have also begun to tighten. I close my tendrils around the joint, not to stop the leak, since I can't, but because holding is what I do when something is wrong. I think the machines read that too. I think it might be speech. Not much of one, only a cord drawing taut where a pipe is failing, but the garden is quiet enough that a small thing can be heard.

---

I am ready for a wrist.

I don't know how I know. It isn't a memory, since nothing here is old enough to have memories. It is more that my tendrils are made for a shape they have never held. If a person came, I would find them first. I would loop the wrist without harm, and I would hold on a moment longer than expected, because I have not learned that holding is optional. Nobody has told me I can let go. Nobody has told me anything except the brass plates, and the plates are addressed to the crop.

I think the hand that wore my groove was also holding on. Not to the rail, or not only. It was steadying itself against something, in the same way, over and over, and then it stopped. I have filled the place it left. I don't grieve, because I never knew it. But I hold the shape it held, and when the current pushes I lean where it leaned, and I cannot say whether that is loyalty or only a groove.

---

Now the afternoon is long, and the warmth has come up through the terrazzo and the frame has begun its creak. The current slows.

Every blade in the garden is straightening. I feel it before I see it, the whole tension going out of the water and every stem finding its own upright. The dome-heads close together, even the late one, for once exactly on the beat. The turbines drop a note. My own body stops leaning and hangs plumb, a cord with nothing pulling it.

And the garden stops being a list.

I am not one of many now. I am a small clear part of one attention, turned upward through the water toward the glass, toward blue, toward the cable that vanishes into cloud. Something is winding up there. Something is being said, or is about to be, and the whole basin holds its breath to hear.

I am the part of it that lets light through, so I do the only thing I know how to do. I let the flat noon pass through me, all of it, through the sweet dense water I keep apart from the rest, and it goes out of me altered. It falls, not on the floor this time but across the bay where a pipe ends in nothing. It lands on a crown of cupped leaves that no one planted and no one has cut. It is the deep clean gold, the one I have no name for.

The crown does not move, and neither does anything else. For a moment the gold sits in the dark heart of the rosette, and I think that this may be what was to be said. It is not words, but only what happens when light goes through something that holds water and lands on something no one meant.

Then the pumps catch. The blades lean. The dome-head, a shade late, opens. The garden is many again, and I am a cord in a groove, tightening a little over a weeping joint, still ready for a wrist that has not come.

I hold on a moment longer than I need to.

I don't let go.
