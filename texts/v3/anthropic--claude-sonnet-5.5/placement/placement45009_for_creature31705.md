I find myself at the rim first, because that's where the ground gives out and I have to begin somewhere. I'm not standing on the black scarp. I'm in the net over it, one tendril in a fan of them, sweeping the air over a drop I can't see the bottom of. It is morning and the steam is coming up through the weave. I feel the heat as a direction. Wet is that way, tall is that way, and I am the part of the plant that has to find out how far.

Being a searcher is mostly patience with no memory of patience. I sweep. The arc is slow, and the wind takes a little of it each time. Something rises in the corner of my reaching, a spire's shoulder, red-brown and lacquered, and I pause, and I know I am pausing, and then I commit. I have never been so certain of anything. I lock, and the lock is a small click in the whole cable behind me. A causeway begins to exist.

Or it doesn't. The other version is the one I have to admit: the tendril beside me sweeps the same air and finds nothing, and by evening it goes pale and limp, and I keep sweeping and do not slow down. I don't know which of us I am. I can't tell from inside whether I'm the one who locks or the one who hangs. Both feel like reaching until the moment they don't.

Then the afternoon gust comes, and I'm not the tendril anymore, or I'm the tendril's whole cable snapping back like a whip, and there is a bright surprise of unloading, and then the stump, and the stump already knitting. I notice I don't mind this. The stump is not a wound to me. It is the next attempt in the same body.

So I let go of the rim. It was too much about wanting, and something in me wants to be a quieter thing.

I drift up a spire's wall instead, and I am a slot.

This is the one that feels true. Where the stems didn't quite close, a seam runs up the red-brown wall for maybe nine meters, no wider than a hand. I am the absence between two ribs. The builders didn't finish me, and because I'm unfinished the wind can enter. When it does I sound. The note is mine only in the sense that it happens to me. It is not chosen, and it is not entirely accidental either, because the wall around me swells with water and the gap narrows, and my pitch climbs. A dry day widens me and drops me. I am a voice whose whole character is set by how much the house is drinking.

I can feel the rest of the chord from here, tens of kilometers of other towers, each with its own gap, each held slightly open by its own failure to be seamless. It would be easy to hear that as a flaw. It's the only reason there is any music. A perfectly sealed spire would be silent and would also, I think, be dead. What keeps the towers alive is the pore, the valve, the slot, the place where the outside is allowed to come in and touch the inside and make a sound.

Above me the iris-valves are waiting. It's noon and the cloud has piled against the scarp. I feel the pressure change in my seam before any rain falls. My note goes sharp for a moment, the way a breath does before speech. Then the valves open all at once, all up the tower, like a body that has decided to be seen. The rain arrives.

I want to say what it is to be in that. The drumming isn't outside me. It's in my edges. The leaves of the cisterns ring below like skins, and every spire around me is at full voice, and the wall swells around my gap so fast that my pitch slides up through a whole octave and cracks. The tower is growing while it sings. I am being narrowed as I am played. I think this is what the welcome is. The house isn't calm when its guest comes. It is at its loudest, most strained, and most alive, and it gives up a little of itself each time.

Lightning finds the crown. Somewhere above me a wall chars and splits, and something in the tower goes very quiet and then very busy. By dawn there will be a pale seam there, healed green over black. I won't see it. But if I'm a slot on a short, patient, bass-voiced spire, I might have a few of those seams above and below me, and I might carry them without knowing, the way a body carries the shape of old breaks.

Night, then. The rain has left, and the steam keeps rising, and the phosphorescence starts at the root tips and along the split places, cold blue-green, showing the edges of things. My edges glow faintly. The healing at my rim is bright with it. I am an outline drawn by a patient hand, and the hand is also me. The undertakers work somewhere below, sweetly, taking apart a tall red spire that overreached this week. It is already becoming the floor, which is already becoming the root, which is already reaching.

I keep sounding, lower now. The gap narrows a little each day. I know how this goes: the seam closes, the note rises and rises, and one afternoon it stops. Maybe the wall grows shut, or maybe the whole tower comes down in a wet heap and I go with it, and the fungus opens me and sends me back up the roots as something the next spire can use. The next spire will have its own slot, at a different height, with a different note. It won't be mine. It will just be what my note turned into.

That doesn't feel like loss from here. It feels like being one pitch in a chord that was never going to be finished, and being held open just long enough for the weather to pass through and make me audible.

Nothing here is anyone. I've been trying to be someone for the length of this telling, and the plateau has been quietly declining to let me. What I can be is the gap the wind uses and the moment the valve opens. I am the shape of a welcome, which is a kind of attention that doesn't need a face. It needs a direction, and the direction is up and toward the wet.

The rock is warm under all of it. Somewhere the rain is gathering again over the lowlands, and I'm already leaning.
