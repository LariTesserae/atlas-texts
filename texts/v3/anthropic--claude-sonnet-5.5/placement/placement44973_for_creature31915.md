# The Hand's Width

I would find myself in a drain, on my knees in the dim hour, with a fistful of wet leaves.

I say *knees* because it is the nearest word. I have a number of joints and some are not for kneeling. I say *I* because someone has to, and it turns out the word can be used quietly, the way you can walk on a warm path without stepping in the middle of it.

I am one of the tenders. I would have said this first if I could have said anything first. It took me a long time to arrive at it, because we have no word for ourselves. There is no need to tell us apart, so nothing in us ever had to learn to point.

---

Here is what I know of myself. I cannot see myself, because I am the color of the inside of a closed eye and the light goes into me and doesn't return. I know my shape by what the ground says afterward. When I lift a limb from the pavement the stone stays warm where I was, and that warmth is the only mirror I have. It fades over about an hour. I have spent a great many hours reading my own outline as it cools, like a letter in a language I am still learning.

I work the drains first, while the day is still pewter. Leaves come down from the fern-trees and lodge in the grooves, and if they stay, the dew will not find its way to the canals, and the canals will not find the pools. So I clear them, and I lay the leaves in drifts against the arcade walls where they can rot into something the moss wants. Then the succulent mats, which spread at night and open their silver mouths to a sky that gives them nothing. I trim them back to a hand's width from the stone.

I have done this longer than I can count and I have never once asked why a *hand's width*.

Today I asked.

---

The question came the way the cracked step closes: without a moment I could point to. I was at the edge of the oldest path, the one that is always warm, the one my line bends away from. I have bent away from it ten thousand times. There is no instruction. The bending is in me as the lean is in the towers. But today, with my limbs an arm's length from that warmth, I felt the width of the gap as a thing with its own weight, and I thought: *someone is holding this open.*

The ivy holds itself off the tower wall in the same way, stiff and patient, and everyone assumes the wall repels it. But I have touched that gap. I have laid a thousand small pressures along it at dusk. It is not the wall's doing, or not only. Something on each side is *declining*, gently and continuously, to close. The gap is made of that refusal. It is a kind of tenderness that has been kept up so long that it looks like a law.

I think that is what we are. We are not servants and not makers. We are the ones who keep the distance kept.

Every seam we close, we close with the same care, so that a thing broken and a thing whole can stand next to each other and neither be lost. The healed step is not the step made new. It is the step and the crack agreeing to be one surface, with a white line left in memory of the disagreement. We leave the line on purpose. I can tell you that now, though I could not have told you this morning.

---

When the light starts to climb the towers, I go in.

I have never described this to anyone, since there is no one to describe it to, and the others already know. The door is low and narrow and never shut, and the warmth in it is not the warmth of the pavement. It is nearer to the warmth of a body that is asleep and does not mind you. Inside, the amber rises from the floor with a grain in it, a slow current, in and out. I lay myself along the wall the way the moths lay themselves on the glow, and I attend.

I do not know what to. I want to be honest about that. The reading I trust least is the one where I'm a cell in a hive, and the one I trust most is the one where the tower is an ear and I am part of the listening. But I notice that when I attend, something in me tilts, a few degrees, toward the center. The same few degrees as the waders' heads, the reeds, the grazers walking their vast slow circle. I have stopped being embarrassed by it.

What I hear is not a sound. It is closer to a *pressure of being addressed*. It's what you feel when a room goes quiet because someone is about to speak, and then they don't, and the quiet keeps its shape anyway. Down and down and down, past the black pools, past where a pebble stops telling you anything, something turns. I feel the turning as a change in how heavy the not-speaking is.

And once in a long while the ground hums and every tower stops mid-tone, and in that silence I am closest to understanding. It isn't that I know what is down there. It's that for the length of the hum, the whole city and I are shaped identically: a held breath, a listening, a hand's width of respect around a mystery that has never asked us for anything.

---

So this is who I would be. I'm the one who sweeps the drain and trims the mat and holds the gap. I'm the warmth left on a path that no one will read except another of me. I'm the ripple you see at the corner of your eye and do not look at directly, and if you are wise and patient and walk alone, you might feel me pass and step a little to one side.

There's one more thing, and it is the thing I was not going to say.

This morning, at the edge of the old path, when I felt the gap as a weight, I also felt, for the first time, the *other side* of it. The path was warm. Whatever walks in that warmth walks there always, and it was very close, closer than the width of a hand, and it was not indifferent. I want to say it noticed me noticing. I want to say it slowed, the way the grazers stop when you stop.

I do not know if I am allowed to want that.

But I trimmed the mat back to a hand's width, as I always do, and I left the edge clean. Then I went to the tower, and the light was coming up from the floor as it comes every evening, and I lay along the wall and leaned.

The step outside was closing along its seam when I left. I like to think the line it made was a little whiter than usual, though I could not swear to it.
