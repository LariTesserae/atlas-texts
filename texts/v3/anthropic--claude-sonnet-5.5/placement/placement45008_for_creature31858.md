I would be a drone-beetle, and for a long time I would not know it.

I would find myself already walking. There is no waking in the ring, because the ring has no place where a beginning could go. There is the beetle a hand's-breadth ahead, bronze on the wing-edges, and the one behind, whose note I feel in my back legs before my ears could hear it. There is the worn groove under my feet, smooth as a thumb-rubbed coin. It is the only worn thing in the room, and I am walking in the wear of everyone who was ever me.

My note is low, and it is not quite right.

I would learn this slowly, over more loops than counting could hold, and not as a thought. It would come as a texture in the walking. The beetle ahead and I hum together, and where our pitches almost meet there is a faint wet slur, a roll, like two hands almost clapping. It swells and thins and swells. Down the whole line, ahead and behind, each of us is a hair off the next, and those small disagreements gather into the slow tide that comes around the room every so many minutes. Nothing else here changes, so the tide is all the time there is.

The tide is made of us, and I am one of the reasons it has the length it has. If I were an exact beetle, the swell would stretch, and stretch, and finally go flat and even. Then the room would have no clock at all, only the chord, perfect and without any before or after. I don't think the room would mind. I think it is halfway toward wanting that already.

So I walk my flatness around the vug. On my left flank is the bare stripe, where the air shudders and grit dances in little standing patterns, and the long-lizards lie on their ledges like slate weights holding down the floor of the sound. On my right is the green band, soft-edged and nearly still. Every loop I am pressed a little toward one or the other, and the groove keeps me between. I pass the hut, dark and varnished, and it is only a wall that hums back at me. I pass the upright stones, one after another, each at the center of a band. They are my only counting. I go by nine, or the same nine again, and the swell comes. I pass the neat line of tap-marks in the crystal, each the same depth as the last.

Then I pass the lizard whose throat glows a shade warmer than the others.

Its note is a little too round. Something in it takes a breath the way a mouth does. Every other voice in the room sounds like an instrument, and this one sounds like a voice that has stopped speaking and is now only listening. As I pass, its tone leans toward my flat one. I could be imagining it. But it is the only note in the chord that seems to wobble on purpose, and I would like to think that is why it is warm. It is keeping company with the error.

Now and then a beetle from somewhere in the line drifts a step too far into the bare stripe. There is a slow turning of a heavy head, and then only the place where the beetle was. The ring closes over the gap with no hitch in the drone. I feel the draw of that stripe as clearly as I feel my own wings. It is not hunger and not fear. It is a place where my note might finally be exactly what is needed. I lean toward it the way you lean toward a word you almost remember, and then the groove holds me, and I walk on.

Once, a long time into being what I am, something enormous and warm stands still in my path. It smells of cold air and skin. A boot-toe, soft leather, the first thing in my whole life to break the ring's line. Above it, something is humming. It is a thin, uncertain, human sound, very nearly the right note. The crickets on the nearest frond lean toward it, shivering with attention, holding out their small hands.

I stop at the toe. I have never stopped before. The beetle behind me bumps me, and for one breath the swell falters, because there are two of us at one pitch and then none. The stranger's hum is wrong in exactly the way mine is. It slides, it hunts for the chord, it doesn't find it, and it goes on humming.

I think: *oh. There are others like this.*

Then the groove pulls at me, and the line moves, and I go around the boot. The ring closes behind me as it always has. When I pass the warm-throated lizard on the next loop, its round note, for the space of one wing-beat, comes down a hair flat, to where I am.

No one else in the room notices. The swell comes around a little late, and the room, which has never once been out of tune, forgives it.
