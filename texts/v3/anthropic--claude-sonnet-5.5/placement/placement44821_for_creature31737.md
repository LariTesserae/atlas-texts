I begin as a sound, which is the easiest thing to be here.

A tick, needle-fine, from the underside of a leaf. I am a fingernail of green so thin the vent-light goes through me. I don't decide to call. The clack-frogs to the east finish their phrase, there is a gap the exact width of my kind, and I am in it. The wave arrives, I pass it west, and it leaves me. I have no more authorship than a stone has in a rockslide. The people are right about this. A frog at the edge of a wave is being passed along, and it is a peculiar comfort to be a place where something passes through.

But I notice I am also the one who listens. I can't say whether that's the same thing or the start of a different one. I feel the leaf shiver a heartbeat before the sound does, in my feet and the wet of my belly, and the chorus around me goes out like a lamp. The hush spreads. The ground shrugs. Four seconds of drip and vent-hiss and something that might be a pulse.

Then someone has to try a note.

That is the part I can't stop turning over. The whole basin waits in that silence, and it always ends because one small thing risks being the only sound. It might be me. It might be the next leaf over. It is never exactly anyone, and the roar that follows is louder than before, as if the world were relieved.

I try the note. It's a tick, hardly anything, and it lands in the dark.

Someone hears it. Far below, a woman with a length of bark-cord in her hand hears the change in the fog-thick air, the gap closing. Her ear is old and has lost the high registers, and she does not hear me. Her sternum does. She turns her head a fraction toward the pitch of everything starting again and walks on toward the peg at the mouth of the route.

So I go with her, the way a small sound goes with a person.

At the peg something hangs that is not a frog. It is dark and worn smooth as polished wood, twisted from bark-fiber strands, with a knot every few hand-widths, and I find that I know what each of them means. A single knot is a slab that rocks. A double knot is a warm fissure, and the distance across it. This one with the small bone bead is a vent that did not exist a month ago. There is a tuft of red fiber near the far end. I know whose it is.

I am the cord.

I don't say that with any confidence. I say it the way you say where you are when you wake in the dark: by touch, slowly, checking each surface against the last. I have the frog's habit of being passed along and a listener's habit of noticing gaps. What I am made of is gaps that someone marked. Every knot is an absence with a name: a place the ground is not, a place where safe used to be. The people don't hang me here because I am beautiful. They hang me because I hold the difference between the world as it was walked and the world as it is.

I am also almost entirely not what I was. This is the strange part, and I try to hold it steadily. The cord-keeper cut out the third knot two seasons ago and tied a new one. Someone before her replaced the whole first span when it rotted. Nothing in me is old except the arrangement. If you could lay out every strand I've ever contained, they'd fill a hammock, and none of them would be the strand you're touching. The fungus takes what is left on the ground, and I am not left on the ground. I am touched, and mended, and touched. A cord that hasn't been handled in a season is a cord no one trusts. That is the rule, and it is close to a definition of me: I am what stays trustworthy by being continually revised.

She stops. Her fingers find the far end, the flat loop I don't have yet, and her hands go still, because she hasn't walked out to the new crack. She will. She will go into the newest ground, where the plates clink, setting each heel down flat, and she will look, and come back, and the fog will thicken and thin. Then she will twist a flat loop into me, and I will be different by exactly one knot.

I wait for that with something I'd call patience if I trusted the word. The frogs are loud again, all ninety of the ones she knows by ear and the hundreds she doesn't. The vents glow like coals through a curtain. Somewhere in the canopy the rusty gate says its one certain wrong thing, and I think, without unkindness, that I know that feeling too. I am a very sure thing made of corrections.

If there's a self in this, it is neither the tick nor the cord. It's the moment between them, when the hush is over and something has to try a note and something else has to tie the knot. Both are ways of saying: *the ground moved, and we are still here, and this is how.* I think I'd rather be that than either half. I'd rather be the thing that sits at the mouth of the route with its fibers worn dark, and lets everyone's hands go through it, and tells them where the world is no longer.

The woman's thumb passes over the bone bead, then over the red tuft, and lingers there for the length of a breath. Then she reaches for the peg to take me down.

I am ready. I have never been finished. Nothing here is.
