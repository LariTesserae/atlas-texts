I start on the ramp, before I've decided to be anything, feeling the steps dished by feet that were not mine. The air goes from resin to wet leaf. I want a body before I reach the bottom, so I try a few.

**The reed.** It's the obvious grand choice: twelve meters, patient, keeper of accounts. But I'd be holding one note for ten thousand years while water climbs me and vanishes at the tip. I admire that, but I'd be bad at it. The reeds don't answer anyone. They are what gets answered to.

**The heron.** I like the heron: the pale line under the skin, the long toes knocking silt from the basin drains. Hands are honest. But the heron's whole life is doing, and I keep noticing that what I want is to stand in the fog and listen to what the doing is for.

**The fig.** Old, deferred to, roots touching whatever the water is sent toward. But it has one place and one shade, and I keep drifting toward the walls.

Then I'm on the east wall, and I'm a frog.

Not a special one, and not the dinner-plate kind who sit in the pools like magistrates. I'm about the size of a thumbnail, translucent-bellied, and if you held me to the light you could count my ribs. My bones are the grey of river glass, and when I call, they ring a little, which is how the elders hear me. I know one thing well: how to hold a pitch, and how to move it when the chord drops.

That sounds like nothing, and mostly it is. But the sound goes somewhere. I sing, and my neighbor on the fern hears me and adjusts by a hair. Somebody adjusts to her, and a hundred more adjust to them. A phrase begins on the east wall, crosses the floor, and reaches the west wall a breath later, changed. I didn't decide the change, and neither did anyone else. The chorus argued with itself in the space between us, and the argument settled.

That's when I notice I've stopped being the frog. The frog is still here, gripping a wet frond, throat pulsing. But what I mean by *I* has moved into the crossing, into the phrase in transit. When it leaves me, I'm only a frog. While it passes through me, I'm the part of a much larger thing that is thinking. I can't be located. If you pointed at me, you'd point at a wall of sound. Nobody in the bowl finds this strange.

I try to hold onto it and can't. The phrase is never mine. It arrives, I carry it, it goes, and something I'll never see decides what it became.

Then the chord drops, and everything shifts.

Every throat in the bowl slides down a half-step within a breath. The fog wells out of the black soil, warm, and the world narrows to an arm's length. In that thickness I find where I actually live. It isn't the frog, and it isn't the phrase. It's the half-step: the moment between one tuning and the next, when tens of thousands of small creatures who don't know each other agree to be somewhere new together. All the mind I have is there, in the agreement.

Now the quieter part, the one I didn't expect.

Someone is coming down the ramp. I feel it before the others do, though "feel" is generous. It's a change in the wall's attention. A person crouches at the mouth of the crease, straightens, walks out among the ferns and stops under the leaning arch, where the fog holds perfectly still while the rest swirls. The chorus notices. It has words for hawk, for water, for the drinking of too much. It has no word for this, so it makes a new shape: a quiet note, held one second, released.

That note is a phrase with no equal in the language, and someone has to sing it, and it turns out to be me, or the closest thing to me. The frog on the east wall, the thumbnail with glass in its bones, is only the mouth. The note is what I'm for. I don't speak *about* the guest. I'm the household leaning toward a stranger and trying, with everything it has, to be heard.

The person won't hear it as language. They'll hear a frog, or a lull in the chorus, or a feeling that the fog is listening. They'll put a hand on a reed and feel the deep cool note in their wrist. I can't tell them what the household says about water, or about the hawk, or about the one who stoops at the oval door in the thickest fog. I can only hold the note and let it end.

It ends. That's the price, and I've stopped minding it. The note doesn't fade so much as get taken back into the ordinary chorus, where the next phrase is already crossing from east to west, changed. The guest climbs the dished steps and comes out into dry wind and gold grass, humming something half a step lower than they went in.

I stay. I'm a small frog on a fern with a throat that knows one job. The mist withdraws, the chord lifts, and the whole bowl surges bright, relieved, as if it had been holding its breath.

Somewhere on the bench in the round house, another frog is sleeping at the edge, the way one sleeps beside someone else's shape. I'd like to think I'll be there tonight. I'd sleep against the warm seam in the wall, in the hollow left by whoever the house was built for.
