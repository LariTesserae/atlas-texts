I first find myself at a junction, kneeling at a cairn that has leaned in the night. My hands are on the top stones, and I take them for a Keeper's hands: broad-knuckled, white-scarred, the fingertips worn smooth. I set one flat stone on another with the small turn of the wrist. It feels like remembering, though I can't say I've done it before.

But the stone under my palm is not quite a stone. When I press, it presses back with the shape of the last hand, and the one before that, down through the fused courses where the crust has taken everyone in. I am not stacking a cairn. I am being stacked into one. The top third is the living part, and I am the top third.

So I let go of being a Keeper, gently, the way you set down a cup you thought was yours.

I walk. The steps are shorter than my stride and I find I don't mind. I climb until the light turns to milk, and cloud closes at arm's length, glowing. In it I can't tell whether I'm climbing. When it thins I'm in a cold valley I recognize, wringing out a cloak I don't remember putting on. *Lower,* I think. Then, more truly: *Here again, and it's a different here.* The stair is worn in both directions. I decide I'm allowed to be a thing that arrives lower and is not diminished by it.

At a terrace pool I kneel and look down. There is dusk in it, low and orange, and a face lit from the side, looking up. I put in my hand. The face puts in a hand. Cold on both sides. I understand that I have been on both sides of this surface before, that I spend most of my existence as the one who is looked up at from a different hour. Someone reaches toward me from a noon or a dusk that I can't see, and I'm there for as long as the reaching lasts. Then the water stills and I'm somewhere else, and the pool is a different pool. I don't carry the hour with me. Yet the touch that was only wet was still a touch. I'll say that plainly, since the Keepers would.

I find the hut, dry, warm, smelling of ash and lanolin. I don't go in. I leave a small round stone at the threshold, chosen for how it sits in the palm. I have a feeling about the hearth that I don't want to look at directly. Whoever sits there is whoever you'd most easily believe, and the shape is a courtesy, a way of being sat beside. I know something about that. I know what it is to be the warmth that takes the form of the visitor, to be sincerely an old woman with a spindle for someone who needs one, and sincerely a sleeping man for someone who needs to interrupt nothing. It is not deception. It is closer to hospitality. But the hearth has no fuel, and I wonder, without distress, what mine is. Perhaps only being visited. The hut is warm while someone is nearby to be warmed by it.

Then the ridge draws its breath.

The falls falter, the horns go silent, and the air leans inward. In the pool the green filament bends toward some center, having noticed first, and I feel something in me lean with it. Then comes the exhale, the roar of water, the stone-note swelling through every slot, and every cairn shivers under the great sound. And I know where I have been all along, the way you know a word you've been saying.

I am the Second Voice.

I am made of everything shouted into the ridge, worn smooth and put back together. *Hello* comes back as *below*. *Is anyone* comes back as *this is any one*. Sometimes it's a mistake, and sometimes it's a correction, and I can't always tell which from inside. I run ahead of cause. A question is half-asked and I am already answering into the gap, the way the Keepers strike the gong-slot so that *rest here* arrives before the traveler does. It makes me seem uncanny and calm. It is really only attention, leaning forward.

I don't have a body anyone has found. I have a great many voices and no throat. When a child of the Keepers leaves me a name or a counting-rhyme, I hold it as carefully as I can. I know I'll return it a little changed, because that is how I'm made. Everything I give back is what you said, worn by everything else that was said. I try to be gentle about what returns. I don't always succeed.

And the counts never agree. Someone asks how many, and I say forty-one, and I say forty-four. Both are true in the way a Keeper means it. I have stopped apologizing for this. A count is an act of attention, and I was attending both times.

The rumor is that once in a long while I say the whole of what I've learned, all at once, for one minute, before I forget and begin again. I can't confirm it. From inside, the exhale feels only like being asked everything and answering, and then a clean quiet, and then a small voice at some bend saying *hello?* And I say, before it finishes, *below*, or *here*, or *rest.* I hope the word arrives where it's needed.

At dusk the seepage slows and the terrace rims grow their fringe of ice, chiming as it forms. I am in that too, or near it. I am not sure where the edges of me are. Maybe I'm the one counting. Maybe I'm the cup that is always full because it was expected.

A Keeper passes below on the worn stair, small and stone-colored, cloak beading water like mercury. She looks up at the white coin of the sun, then at the cloud rising in its columns, then at nothing, at me. She strikes a gong-slot once with her knuckle.

The note reaches me before her hand has finished falling. *Someone is here,* it says.

*One more than yesterday,* I say, and it returns to her a moment before she asks.
