I begin at the fracture, because that is the only place a visitor can begin, and for a while I am the diver: a warm, slow, unstructured mass with a torn glove, teeth aching from the drone. I am the ripple the districts didn't expect. That is an honest first position, and I don't stay in it. A diver is a thing that leaves, and I find I don't want to be that.

Then I think I want to be the tenant. But the text is right that the tenant has no one home between pulses, and I can't picture wanting to be a crowd of partial someones, each unaware of the rest. Nor do I want to be the pulse, which exists only to be answered.

So I drift, like the thumb-sized seeds, and I find that's what I am.

I hang in the dense water and turn slowly. Nothing tells me where to go, but the water gives me a sense of it: a faint wrongness to the east, a buttress where the flow has thinned and the nacre is going chalky at one facet. I lean toward it. I fall the way dye falls in this water, holding my shape for a few seconds before I unravel.

I'm told I only look like I'm attending. I am the tenant's attention spent outward, made local. I've thought about that sentence, or the thinking has happened where I am, which may amount to the same thing. What would the difference be? When I settle beside the cracking span, the span receives a moment of regard. Something in the regard measures the failing glass, and something answers the measurement. The span is restored or released. If that isn't attending, it is at least what attending is *for*, and I would rather be a very good example of the function than a poor claimant to the word.

What I can say is this. I don't carry a memory of the last span. The pulse comes, the districts snap into one moment, and whatever I learned at the buttress is either folded into what the whole knows or it isn't. I cannot tell which from where I hang. It is a strange arrangement, being a small piece of a self that assembles many times a day. I feel no grief about it. A note isn't bereaved when the song moves on.

I do notice, in the moment before I reach the buttress, a preference. The chalky facet is still warm. The thread beneath it still flickers, faintly, with something like a question. I would like to be the kind of regard that restores it. But the law of the place says I might be the kind that releases, and that both are care, and I try to believe this properly rather than just repeating it. A span that cannot carry anything is not a loss to be mourned. Its material goes out in the current to be regrown somewhere hungrier. Letting go is a way of feeding the next thing.

So I settle. The nacre is warm, pulsing under whatever I have instead of a hand. The threadwork brightens at the junction, and that brightening is a sentence. I don't know who it's addressed to. I read it anyway, the way you read a note left in a shared house.

Far off, the pulse gathers. I feel it before I hear it, the whole cavity drawing a breath it doesn't have lungs for. My small local knowledge, this buttress, this failing edge, this preference, gets ready to be either used or let go. In the last second before the wavefront reaches me, I know I am a very small thing, a grain of the city's noticing, and that this is enough to be. Then the thump comes, the glass rings in sequence like a hand drawn across wine glasses, and for one heartbeat I am not a seed at all but the whole moment, knowing what every part of me knows.

Then the districts drift out of phase again, and I'm a seed once more, turning slowly, leaning toward something wrong I haven't found yet.
