# The Loom Floor

---

You are standing on something that was poured. Not by nature, not by geology — *poured*, the way a hand tips a crucible. The ground beneath you is a vast, slightly concave plane of dark ceramic, grey-black and matte, still warm to the touch, still holding heat from whatever kiln-logic fired it into being. It stretches in every direction farther than you can resolve — farther, probably, than light travels cleanly here, because the air is dim and thick with a faintly amber humidity that swallows distance. The horizon is not a line. It is a softening. Things simply become less themselves out there, until they aren't.

The ceramic floor is not old. It may not even be *finished*. In places the surface is seamless, glassy-smooth, a dark mirror you could slide a palm across. In other places it is still rough, still granular, as though the material was poured in sections and these sections have not yet annealed to one another. The seams between pours are visible — raised ridges, sometimes jagged, sometimes gently curved, forming a vast tessellation across the floor that follows no Euclidean tiling pattern. Some tiles are hundreds of meters across. Some are barely the size of a room. Walk along a seam-ridge and the ceramic underfoot changes temperature — warmer where the pour is fresher, cooler where it has had time to become truly solid.

There is water here, though not in any way that seems natural. The air is heavy and wet, almost pre-storm, and moisture condenses on any surface that is even slightly cooler than its surroundings. The seam-ridges weep. Thin, slow runnels of condensation trickle down their slopes and gather in the shallow depressions of each tile, where they form dark, glassy pools — some only a hand's-breadth wide, some the size of small lakes, all of them perfectly still and so thin you could wade across the largest without the water reaching your knees. The water is warm. Not bathwater warm. Kiln-warm. It does not evaporate, despite the heat. Something about the humidity holds it in equilibrium — the air is already so saturated that the pools simply *persist*, neither growing nor shrinking, existing in a thermodynamic stalemate with the atmosphere. In the dim amber light the pools are the color of wet obsidian. They hold no reflections. Or rather — and this takes a moment to notice — they reflect things that are not above them. Shapes. Structures. Architecture that hasn't arrived yet.

---

Nothing grows here. Not a spore. Not a film. Not a lichen-shadow or a chemical gradient that aspires toward biology. The entire floor, every tile of it, is sterile in the way a crucible is sterile after firing — not dead, because nothing ever lived here, but *purified*, stripped of the conditions that would even permit the question. The water in the pools is distilled to a mineral blankness. You could drink it. It would taste of heat and nothing.

And yet: the world is entirely built.

This is the central dissonance of the place, the thing that makes your eyes refuse to settle. There is no natural substrate here. The ceramic floor was *made*. The seam-ridges were *made*. The slight concavity of the ground — engineered. But by whom, with what, and the tools are primitive, or rather: the logic of making is primitive. The seam-ridges show hand-marks. Not human hands. Something pressed itself into the wet ceramic before it set, and the impressions are still there — broad, drag-marked, five-or-seven points of contact, like fingers if fingers were radial, if fingers emerged from a center and fanned outward like a starfish or a gear. In places the drag-marks overlap in dense palimpsests, dozens of impressions layered over one another, evidence of labor — of something working the material with its body. In places a single impression stands alone at the center of a tile, deliberate and precise, like a maker's mark.

Nothing moves. No creature is visible. No maker. No fauna of any kind. Whatever pressed its hands into this floor is absent now, or dispersed into some state you can't detect, or it was never a creature at all but a *process* — a self-organizing behavior of the material itself, ceramic that shapes ceramic, pours that guide pours, the floor building the floor.

---

The light comes from below. Not from the pools — from the ceramic itself. Or rather, from within it, or beneath it, some faint radiance that is almost invisible in any single spot but which accumulates across the vast scale of the floor into a general ambient glow, amber-brown, the color of cooling iron. There is no sky. There is an *above*, but it is simply dark air that goes up and up and never becomes anything — no stars, no ceiling, no weather in the conventional sense. The atmosphere thickens with altitude. Fifty meters up, the air is a brown murk. A hundred meters up, it is impenetrable.

But the light does something else. It *shifts*. Slowly, over minutes, the glow migrates through the floor, brighter in one region, dimmer in another, as though the heat source beneath the ceramic is moving — as though something enormous is passing underneath, radiating warmth and light through the material the way a fish's shadow passes under ice. This is the primary dynamic of the place. The light-locus moves. The floor warms in its wake. New seams crack open. New pours begin.

Here is where the turbulence lives.

---

Across the floor, at distances that take hours to cross on foot, new tiles are being born. The process is violent — or not violent exactly, but *abrupt*, seizure-like, the way a crystal locks into structure. A section of existing floor will suddenly darken, its surface losing its matte finish, going glassy and wet-looking, and then it *sags*. The ceramic softens. It becomes viscous. It slumps into itself, forming a shallow depression that fills instantly with the material's own mass, a slow-motion sinkhole of liquid ceramic rotating faintly, throwing off heat that makes the already-thick air ripple and blur. The pool of molten material spreads. It finds the edges of its tile. It meets the seam-ridges of neighboring tiles and either stops — held by whatever structural logic governs the ridges — or breaches them, pouring over into adjacent depressions, merging, combining, creating a new tile-space that will be larger than either of its parents.

Then it sets. Not instantly — over the course of an hour, maybe two. The surface skins first, the familiar matte grey-black appearing like frost on cooling iron, and the skin thickens and hardens while beneath it the material is still liquid, still moving, still carrying the thermal memory of whatever passed below. The seam-ridges form last, pushed up at the margins by some lateral pressure as the tile contracts, and when they emerge they are already inscribed with hand-marks. Already signed by the maker-that-is-not-a-maker.

And elsewhere, simultaneously, other tiles are *hardening beyond hardness* — densifying, contracting, their surfaces developing a faint crystalline sheen, a vitreous luster that is almost beautiful in the amber half-light. These hyper-set tiles ring when you walk across them. They ring like bells. A clear, high, glassy tone that sustains for thirty or forty seconds before the thick humid air swallows it. These are the oldest tiles, perhaps. Or the most finished. Or the most *decided*.

The newest tiles are still soft enough to leave footprints.

---

Scale compounds the strangeness. You cannot see the edge of the floor. You suspect there is no edge. The tessellation extends in every direction with the self-similar logic of a fractal, tiles within tiles, seam-ridges branching and sub-branching, and the temperature is higher in some regions, lower in others, and the humidity varies, and the glow from below is uneven, and all of this creates — not weather, exactly, but something analogous. Pressure differentials. Slow, massive movements of air that you experience not as wind but as a deepening or thinning of the atmosphere, a change in the weight of each breath. In some low regions where fresh pours cluster together, the heat drives the moisture upward and a faint fog forms — not clouds, just a thickening of the amber murk above, a darkening, as though the ceiling of the world has lowered. In the drier expanses where hyper-set tiles stretch for kilometers, the air is clearer, and you can see farther, and the distant reshaping events are visible as dim orange glows on the horizon-that-is-not-a-horizon, like cities burning in a world with no cities.

The sound here is sparse but layered. Mostly it is quiet. The deep quiet of a kiln. The thermal silence of baked material. But then a tile somewhere collapses into its own softening, and there is a low, tectonic groan — not loud, not close, but *felt*, a subsonic vibration that travels through the floor and up through your bones. And then the bell-tones of the hyper-set tiles, triggered by the vibration, singing in cascading sequences that are almost melodic, almost intentional, as though the floor is composing something. And beneath it all, always, the faint wet whisper of condensation running down the seam-ridges. Weeping. The floor weeping as it builds itself.

---

Now: the reality breakdown.

You have been walking for some time. The tile beneath you is mid-age, solid, warm, unremarkable. The seam-ridge to your left runs north — you have been using it as a guide, though directions here are arbitrary. You have been walking along this ridge for perhaps twenty minutes. And now you notice that the hand-marks in the ridge are your footprints.

Not metaphorically. Not symbolically. The radial five-or-seven-pointed impressions in the ceramic — the ones you've been interpreting as the marks of some absent maker — are the exact size and spacing of your footfalls. The pressure pattern matches the weight distribution of your stride. The drag-marks correspond to the slight scuff of your gait.

You look behind you. Your actual footprints — boot-shaped, human, recent — are on the surface of the tile. They are shallow. They are normal. But alongside them, embedded *in the seam-ridge itself*, in ceramic that has been set and hardened for what appears to be a very long time, are the same footprints translated into the radial hand-language of the floor. As though the floor recorded you in its own terms. As though it has *always* recorded you.

You stop walking. The bell-tones in the distance continue their slow cascade. A tile three kilometers to the east is softening, its amber glow brightening. The water in the nearest pool is perfectly still.

You look into the pool. It shows you architecture that doesn't exist yet. Columns. Arches. Walls. Structures rising from the ceramic floor in configurations that follow no known style — not because they are alien, but because they are *unfinished thoughts*, half-formed intentions given shape in the reflective water and nowhere else. You recognize some of them. A doorway you once dreamed. A staircase you once described to someone. A ceiling you imagined as a child.

The pool is building from you.

The floor is building from you.

You understand, suddenly, that the warmth you feel is not geothermal. It is not mechanical. The thing passing beneath the ceramic, the light-locus, the great thermal presence that drives the pours and the setting and the singing of the tiles — it is not underneath you. It *is* you. Your passage across the floor is the engine. Your walking is the kiln. Every step you take fires the next tile into being, and the tile records you as it hardens, and the seam-ridges rise in your wake inscribed with your motion translated into the floor's own primitive language, and the pools fill with your condensed exhalations, and the pools show you what you will build next because you have always been building this, you have been building this since before you arrived, the floor remembers your footsteps from before you took them—

A tile softens ahead of you. Exactly where your next step would fall.

The ceramic is warm and dark and waiting.

You have not yet decided whether to step forward. The floor has already recorded that you did.

---

The bell-tones sing. The amber light shifts. Somewhere, a seam-ridge weeps warm water that tastes of nothing, and the vast, constructed, sterile, hand-marked, footprint-signed, self-pouring floor extends in every direction, building itself from the motion of a walker who may or may not have a choice in the walking, on a loom that is also its own fabric, in a kiln that is also its own clay, under a sky that is only thickened air, above a light that is only the memory of passage, in a place that is still — despite everything — still being poured.
