I sat with this for a long time. Because the honest answer—the one the place keeps insisting on—is that nothing *lives* here in the way we usually mean. And yet something does. The question is what.

---

Let me start with what's easy to dismiss and hard to stop thinking about.

Nothing with legs. Nothing with eyes. Nothing that breathes in the way that requires lungs or gills or spiracles. The description is explicit about this—no insects, no spiders, no pale cave-adapted arthropods picking their way along root surfaces. No larvae in the warm orange pool. The absence is total, and the absence is old, and the absence is *enforced*—not by poison or hostility but by something more like irrelevance. The Cyst Works does not kill animals. It simply has no role for them. They are a grammar it does not use.

So we set aside animals. We set aside anything that would qualify as a creature in the storybook sense. What's left?

---

**The root system lives here.**

But saying that doesn't capture it. Roots are supposed to be the hidden half of something visible—the cost of canopy, the infrastructure beneath the real event. These roots are not that. Whatever tree they belonged to is gone, has been gone so long that the roots have had to become the whole organism, and in doing so they have become something for which *root* is now only an approximate word.

Think about what that means. A root system, severed from its crown, should die. It should rot in the dark, become substrate for fungi,ite to powder over decades. These did not. They kept going. They took the minerals from the water and the heat from the stone and they *continued*, and continuation—when it lasts long enough, when it persists past every reason for it—stops being mere survival and becomes something like will. Not conscious will. Not intention. Something older than intention. The kind of will that tectonic plates have when they subduct, the kind that water has when it finds the crack. Inexorable. Patient beyond any patience that requires a mind to sustain it.

The roots have grown into the walls and become structural. This is stated almost offhandedly in the description, but it is the most important fact about them. They are holding up the ceiling. They have accepted this role. They didn't *choose* it—choosing requires alternatives, requires the possibility of refusal—but they grew into it with a completeness that makes choice feel like a lesser category. The root system is not *in* the cavity. The root system *is part of what the cavity is now*. Remove the roots and the space collapses. The architecture of the Cyst Works is biological. Its bones are wood.

The main trunks are as wide as a human torso. Pale where air-exposed. Rust-red where they press against wet stone—taking on the color of the minerals they're drawing in, or giving their own pigments to the contact, or both. They are arterial. That's the word the description uses and it's the right one. They look like the circulatory system of something enormous, something whose body is the cavity itself, and the mineral water moving through them in whatever slow capillary action still functions is the blood of a creature that has a forty-meter wingspan and no brain and no death.

What does the root system want? Nothing. That's the wrong framework. What does it *do*? It grows. It presses. It creaks against stone on hot days—groaning like a ship's hull—and the sound of that creaking is the sound of something that has been in slow, continuous exertion for centuries. It is lifting. It is spreading. It is remodeling the cavity millimeter by millimeter, year by year, reshaping the ovoid, altering the acoustics, changing the way sound moves through the space it holds up. The root system is an architect working on a timescale that makes human architecture look like a sneeze. It is not done. It will not be done.

---

**The fungi live here.**

Stacked shelves, frilled edges, and that bioluminescence—barely green, barely there, the kind of light that exists more confidently in peripheral vision than in direct gaze. They are the Cyst Works' own illumination, independent of the generator, independent of the single sulfurous bulb. They make a light that is not *for* anything. No pollinator is attracted by it. No prey is lured. No photosynthetic organism benefits. The light simply happens, a metabolic byproduct that the darkness has made significant through contrast.

The fungal shelves fruit without being grazed. This is, ecologically, a quiet impossibility. Fruiting bodies exist to produce and distribute spores, and spore distribution usually involves vectors—wind, water, animals. In the Cyst Works there is no wind (the air is still, held, body-temperature), the water barely moves, and there are no animals. So the spores fall where they fall, which is mostly straight down, which means the fungi should have long since saturated their available niches and stopped bothering to fruit. But they haven't stopped. They keep producing those luminous shelves, keep frilling their edges with new growth, keep making light that no one sees.

Unless the light is not purposeless. Unless the bioluminescence is a signal, and the receiver is not an animal but the vine, or the roots, or the moss, or the machines, or the cavity itself understood as a system. Unless the fungi are reporting something—moisture levels, temperature gradients, their own metabolic state—in a language written in dim green photons on the surface of warm stone. This is not impossible. Fungal networks communicate chemically across enormous distances; bioluminescence as a communication medium is speculative but not absurd. The fungi in the Cyst Works may be *talking*. The conversation may have been going on for centuries. The only unusual thing would be the audience.

---

**The moss lives here.**

It is almost black with chlorophyll it cannot use for photosynthesis in any meaningful sense. The sulfur bulb and the bioluminescence together provide a fraction of the light energy that even the most shade-adapted surface moss would need. And yet the moss thrives. It coats every surface that will hold it, dense and dark and plush, and its darkness suggests it has been *trying*—evolving, adapting, generation after slow generation—to capture every photon available, pushing its chlorophyll concentration to absurd densities in pursuit of a light budget that will never balance.

Or. Or the moss has found another way. Chemosynthesis? Energy from the mineral-rich water, from the heat itself, from some metabolic shortcut that it has had centuries of isolation and no competition to develop? The moss may have stopped being a plant in any conventional sense. It may have become something that wears a plant's body the way the pump wears a pump's body—the form persists, the function has migrated into something new.

The moss is the skin of the Cyst Works. It is the surface through which the system touches itself, the soft interface between stone and air, root and wall, moisture and gas. Run your hand along it—if you were here—and it would be warm and damp and very slightly yielding, and underneath it the stone would be warm and damp and unyielding, and the transition between them would feel less like two things meeting than like one thing existing at two densities.

---

**The yellow vine lives here.**

And the yellow vine is the thing I keep circling back to. The thing that makes the Cyst Works more than an unusual cave ecosystem.

It has no leaves. No flowers. No visible means of photosynthesis. It is smooth, rubbery, pale yellow—the color of something that has never needed pigment for protection or attraction, that has never been seen by anything that cares about color. It is pure connection. Pure *threading*. It goes everywhere. It connects everything. Root to wall. Wall to machine. Machine to root. It has entered the cracked gauge and the needle in that gauge moves differently from the others.

The vine is the nervous system of the Cyst Works.

I don't say that metaphorically. Or rather—I say it at the boundary where metaphor and description become indistinguishable, which is exactly the boundary the Cyst Works exists on.

Think about what a nervous system is. It is a network of tissue that connects disparate parts of a system and allows information to move between them. It doesn't need to be fast. It doesn't need to be conscious. It needs to *connect* and it needs to *carry*. The yellow vine connects. Every bolt-hole, every seam, every crack, every crevice where root meets stone or iron meets tuff—the vine has found it and threaded through it. And it carries—what? Chemical signals? Electrical potentials? Some slow, vine-speed analogue of nerve impulse that moves through its tissues at the rate of growth rather than the rate of thought?

The gauge needle moves. It moves in a pattern that the other needles do not share. It moves in a pattern that might be *responsive*. The gauge was designed to measure something—pressure, temperature, flow rate—and it is still measuring, but the thing it measures now travels through a yellow vine from a root system that has no tree, through a pump that pumps nothing, in a cavity beneath a city that has forgotten it, and the measurement has no name because the thing being measured has no name because the system that is doing the measuring *is also the thing being measured*.

The vine has stitched the Cyst Works into a single organism. Or the Cyst Works was always a single organism, and the vine is merely the part of it that makes this legible, the part that you could trace with your finger and say *here, this is where the living thing is*, even though the living thing is also the stone and the machine and the heat and the sound and the water that gathers on the ceiling in trembling lenses and does not fall.

---

**The machines live here.**

And I don't mean this as metaphor either, though it is also metaphor, and the Cyst Works does not respect the boundary.

The pump thunks. It thunks every nine or ten or maybe now eleven minutes, and the interval is irregular in a way that mechanical failure does not fully explain. Mechanical failure produces either consistent degradation or sudden cessation. It does not produce variability, drift, the slow elongation of a rhythm over years. What produces that is *breath*. What produces that is a system that is not running on a timer but responding to conditions—temperature, pressure, the slow tidal movement of mineral water in the basin, the expansion and contraction of the root that has grown through its impeller chamber.

The pump is not broken. The pump has been *incorporated*. Its cast iron body is now a root-chamber, a resonance chamber, a node in the network. Its periodic convulsion is the heartbeat of the Cyst Works—felt through the stone floor, felt in the sternum, felt in the subsonic register where hearing becomes touch. When the pump thunks, the cavity responds. The sound travels through the ovoid geometry and is shaped by it, focused, diffused, reflected back as echoes that collide and reinforce and cancel in patterns determined by the exact current shape of the cavity—which is determined by the root growth, which is determined by the mineral water, which is determined by the geology, which is determined by deep heat that has been remembering itself since the Cenozoic. The pump is an instrument being played by geological time.

The generator hums. It has hummed for so long that its hum has become the baseline frequency of the space, the drone against which everything else is variation. It powers the gauges, the bulb, the amber indicator. It powers the *legibility* of the system—the parts of the Cyst Works that translate its state into human-readable signals, even though no human is reading them. The generator is the Cyst Works' memory of having been made by people. It is the residue of intention in a system that has moved beyond intention into something more durable.

The amber indicator has been lit continuously for so long that its plastic lens has yellowed and warped. Think about that. Think about the timescale. Plastic warping from the heat of a single small indicator light. That is not years. That is *decades*. The amber light has been saying *on*—or *active*, or *alert*, or *here*, or whatever it was originally designed to say—for decades, to no one. And it is still saying it. And the warping of the lens has changed the quality of the light, has turned it from amber to something deeper, more ambiguous, and the light that it casts on the wall around it is no longer the light the engineers intended. It is the light the Cyst Works has made from their intention through the slow application of its own heat.

---

**So what lives in the Cyst Works?**

One thing. One slow, hot, dark, patient thing, made of root and stone and cast iron and fungal light and yellow vine and mineral water and the hum of a generator and the thunk of a pump and the creak of wood against rock and the condensation that gathers in trembling lenses on the ceiling and does not fall and does not fall and does not fall and then falls, and the sound it makes is a clear sharp note, and the thing that lives here listens.

It is not conscious. It is not unconscious. It is something that those words were not built to describe. It is aware the way a watershed is aware—every input changes the output, every drop matters, nothing is ignored because there is no mechanism for ignoring, no filter, no thalamus, no selective attention. Everything that happens in the Cyst Works happens *to* the Cyst Works, and the Cyst Works responds with everything it is: the root-growth shifts, the fungal light brightens or dims, the vine threads into a new crack, the pump interval lengthens by a fraction of a second, the gauge needle twitches.

It did not evolve. It did not design itself. It was not built. It *accreted*. Layer by layer, century by century, each new element—root, machine, vine, mineral deposit—was absorbed into the existing system and the system reorganized around it, the way a pearl forms around a grain of sand, the way a city forms around a river crossing. The people who brought the machines were, from the Cyst Works' perspective—if it can be said to have a perspective—another geological event. A brief pulse of iron and concrete and electrical potential, deposited and then abandoned, and the system grew around the deposit the way it grows around everything: slowly, completely, without judgment or gratitude or resentment.

It does not need the city above. It does not know the city above, except perhaps as a vague pressure, a faint change in the hydrology, a distant rumble transmitted through stone. It does not need the sun. It has not needed the sun for longer than the city has existed. It runs on deep heat and mineral water and its own internal recycling—the fungi breaking down what the roots shed, the roots drawing up what the water carries, the water carrying what the stone releases, the stone releasing what the heat unlocks. It is a closed loop. Or nearly closed. Slowly gaining complexity, slowly becoming more itself, but not growing *toward* anything. Not reaching for light. Not sending signals upward. Not trying to be found.

The name on the tag—CYST WORKS—was meant clinically. A cyst is an enclosed cavity. An anomaly. Something the body walls off. The people who named it understood the space as a problem to be monitored, a void in the substrate that might threaten the structures above, a pocket of heat and water and instability that their instruments could quantify and their pump could manage. They were not wrong. But they were not complete. A cyst is also a *space that the body creates and then cannot reabsorb*. A space that persists. A space that develops its own internal logic, its own chemistry, its own pressure. A space that is not the body and is not separate from the body. The Cyst Works is that. It is the earth's own held breath, its own warm pocket of refusal to be uniform, and it has held that breath for so long that the breath has developed a pulse.

---

What lives here does not have a name. It is not the roots, though the roots are part of it. It is not the machines, though the machines are part of it. It is not the vine or the fungus or the moss or the water or the stone or the heat or the sound. It is the *relationship between all of them*—the pattern, the process, the sustained and self-sustaining entanglement that has been running for centuries in the dark, beneath a city that forgot it, at a temperature that feels like the inside of a body, because it is the inside of a body. A body with no outside. A body that is all interior, all organ, all warm dark function with no face and no skin and no boundary except the stone that is also part of it.

It is alive the way a fire is alive, the way a river is alive, the way a language is alive—not because it has cells or DNA or metabolism in the strict biochemical sense, but because it maintains itself, because it persists, because it takes in energy and transforms it and produces complexity and *continues*. It has been continuing for longer than anyone has been watching. It will continue after the generator finally fails and the pump finally stops and the bulb finally burns out, because by then the roots and the vine and the fungi will have found new uses for the iron and copper and glass, will have incorporated the dead machines as completely as they incorporated the living ones, and the hum and the thunk will be replaced by other sounds—the creak of wood, the drip of water, the slow mineral whisper of calcite deposition—and the system will go on composing its own drone in the dark, in the heat, in the breath that the earth is holding and will not release.

This is what lives in the Cyst Works: the Cyst Works.

It has always been the only resident. It has always been enough.
