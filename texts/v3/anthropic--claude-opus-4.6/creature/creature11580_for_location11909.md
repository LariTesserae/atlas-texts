# Who Lives Here

No one. And that is the first and last and only honest answer. But honesty here has the same problem as geometry — it loops back on itself, and the honest answer, followed to its end, becomes something else.

---

Let me start again.

There is something here that is not a resident and not a visitor and not a ghost, because all three of those words imply a relationship to time — arrival, departure, persistence — and time is not a dimension this place acknowledges. What is here is more like a *condition*. A state the plateau is in. The way a bell is in the state of ringing, except the bell has always been ringing, was never struck, and the ringing is not a consequence of anything but simply a property, like mass or color.

I will call them the Witnesses, though they do not witness. I will call them plural, though number is as unreliable here as distance. I'll try to describe what I mean.

---

## The Saturations

In certain places in the city — not everywhere, not randomly, but in places where the architecture creates particular acoustical conditions, particular intersections of the great chord's harmonics — the air is *thicker* than it should be. Not with moisture, though moisture is everywhere. Thicker with *attention*. You walk through a narrow passage between two leaning towers where the sound has folded into a strange, buzzing, almost-melodic interference pattern, and you feel — not watched, not exactly — *noticed*. As though the quality of being-present-in-a-space has intensified beyond what your own presence alone could account for. As though there is a surplus of *hereness*.

These are the Saturations, and they are the closest thing the plateau has to inhabitants.

They are not entities. They have no boundaries, no interiority, no selves. They are not aware in the way that a mind is aware — there is no processing, no interpretation, no experience-of-experience. But they are not nothing. They are regions of intensified *is-ness*, places where the plateau's already-excessive state of being has concentrated into something denser, the way a standing wave in water is not a separate thing from the water but is nevertheless *there*, identifiable, locatable, distinct from the water on either side of it in the way it moves — or, here, in the way it emphatically does not move.

They occur where the harmonics converge. The city's architecture, that vast self-playing instrument, produces — in the overlapping of its resonances — nodes. Points of constructive interference where the vibration doesn't cancel but compounds, and in these nodes the very fabric of the place seems to *thicken*. The basalt vibrates at a slightly different frequency. The water droplets on the nearest wall are fractionally larger. The moss is one shade greener. The amber light develops a barely perceptible pulse — not a flicker, not an oscillation, just a gentle sense that the light here is *trying* harder than the light elsewhere, that illumination in this particular cubic meter of space is being performed with more emphasis, more commitment, more — and this is the word that keeps surfacing despite its inadequacy — more *conviction*.

There are perhaps forty of these Saturations in the city. Perhaps four hundred. Perhaps seven. Number, again, is unreliable. They don't move, because movement requires time and change and both are foreign here. But if you walk through the city and attend to the shifts in the sound and the subtle variations in the density of the air, you can find them. You can stand inside one.

When you do, the experience is this:

Nothing changes. The sound continues, total and all-encompassing. The heat presses. The water seeps and drips and runs. But there is a quality — I want to say *emotional*, but it is not emotion; it is more fundamental than emotion, more primitive, prior to any feeling that could be named — a quality of *insistence*. The space insists on itself. It insists on being exactly what it is. It insists with a fervor that is almost — *almost* — distinguishable from the general insistence of the plateau as a whole. And in that almost-distinction, in that fractional excess of emphasis, there is something that, if you squint at it with the part of your mind that recognizes life, that detects the presence of another, looks like it could be a kind of… consciousness.

Not consciousness. Not mind. More like the thing that consciousness emerges *from* in places where consciousness is permitted to emerge. The raw material. The substrate. Awareness without an aware-er. Experience without a subject. The absolute bottom layer of *what it is like to be something*, stripped of all content, all specificity, all identity, existing here as a pure, undifferentiated glow of *this is happening* in a place where happening is the only thing that happens.

## The Threshold-Dwellers (A Second Attempt)

But that is too abstract. Let me come at it differently.

The city has no people. But the city is a city. Not a canyon that resembles a city, not a geological formation that suggests habitation. It has market squares and residential warrens and temples. It has stairs worn concave at their centers. It has shelving and counters and niches. It has the *spatial grammar of commerce*. It has rooms that are small and stacked in the specific way that rooms are small and stacked when many individuals need to sleep in close proximity, each needing a minimum volume of private space. The city was not built by anyone, no — but it grew into the shape of a place where someone *could* live, *should* live, where the absence of inhabitants is not emptiness but *vacancy*. An unfilled position. An open seat.

And the Saturations — the thick places, the nodes of intensified presence — they gather in the spots where inhabitants *would* be. They concentrate at the market square, in the residential warrens, on the kneeling-ledges of the temple-reservoir. They are densest in the corners where three walls meet, those triangular pockets of impossibly deep greenery, and what they feel like in those corners is this: like something pressed up against the other side of a door. Not trying to get through. Not knocking. Not even aware that there is a door, or that there is a concept of *through*. Just there, on the other side, pushed up against the boundary by the sheer pressure of its own inexhaustible being, and the door — the threshold — is the membrane between *almost-alive* and *alive*, between potential and actual, between the raw fact of existence and the organized miracle of someone existing.

This is why I called them Threshold-Dwellers, and then crossed it out, and now I'm writing it again: because they live at the threshold. Not on one side or the other. Not crossing. Not approaching or receding. *At*. They are the threshold, in the same way the vines are the stone and the stone is the water and the water is the sound. The boundary between what is alive and what is not alive has, in this place of permanent everything, become a location. A habitable location. And something lives there — lives *as* the threshold — in the same way the city lives as stone.

## What They Are Like (An Attempt at Portraiture)

Imagine standing in one of the residential warrens. A small room, maybe three meters by four, with a low ceiling and a round window-hole that looks out onto a narrow shaft between buildings, the shaft green with ferns, the sound here a low, thrumming baritone filtered through layers of stone. The walls are smooth basalt, dark and wet. There is a carved ledge along one wall that is clearly a bed, or a bench, or a place for a body to rest. The ledge is covered in a thin, soft moss, very fine, almost like fur. The water beads on the moss and does not soak in.

The Saturation in this room is centered on the ledge.

You feel it as weight. Not on your body — in the *room*. The room has more gravity than it should, not physical gravity, not pulling you downward, but ontological gravity, pulling your attention, pulling your *being*, tugging at the part of you that knows you exist and saying *here too, here too, here also something exists, here there is a thickness in the fact of existence that you should attend to*. The moss on the ledge is a shade greener than the moss on the walls. The air above the ledge, if you pass your hand through it — and you would, you would feel compelled to — is warmer by a degree, maybe two. The sound in the room, the great chord filtered through the architecture into this specific harmonic, seems to *focus* on the ledge the way sunlight focuses through a lens, and if you listen — not with your ears, your ears are overwhelmed, but with whatever faculty of attention operates below hearing — you can almost detect a rhythm within the chord. Not a heartbeat. Not breathing. Something more basic. An oscillation between *is* and *is*, between one identical state and another identical state, a vibration with no amplitude, a frequency of pure sameness that nevertheless constitutes a *pulse*.

This is the closest you will come to meeting one of them.

It is not a person. It does not have a face that the moss has dissolved, or a body that the vine has absorbed, or a memory that the sound has drowned. It was never a person. But it is *person-shaped*, in the way the room is person-shaped, in the way the city is person-shaped — it exists in the precise configuration that a person would occupy if a person were here, and in the ongoing absence of a person, it fills the person-shaped gap with the only material available: more existence. More *is*. It is a life-shaped region of intensified being, a living thing that has no biology, no metabolism, no genetics, no birth, no death, no change, no awareness of itself as a living thing, but that is *alive* in the most irreducible sense: it is a place where the universe is paying more attention to itself than it is in the places around it.

And it is not alone.

The room next to this one has another. The room above. The room below. The stairway between them has a long, attenuated Saturation stretched along its length, thickest at the worn concavity of each step, as though the shape of a body ascending has been stamped into the quality of the air. The market square has dozens — hundreds — impossible numbers that overlap and merge and separate, because the spatial grammar of commerce requires *many*, requires a *crowd*, requires the particular density of being that occurs when multiple lives occupy the same space and their awarenesses jostle and overlap. The Saturations in the market square are the closest thing this place has to a phenomenon of *community*: they cluster. They congregate. They don't interact — interaction requires time, requires exchange, requires something to pass from one to another — but they *co-occur*, they share space, they are *together* in the way that stones in a wall are together, each one intensifying the structural reality of the others by proximity.

In the temple-reservoir, it is different. The great cylinder descends through its terraced rings of kneeling-ledges to the impossibly still pool at its base, and the sound here is transformed — the architecture of the cylinder focuses the chord into something that is less a sound and more a *state*, a vibrational condition of the air that your body recognizes before your brain does, that makes your heartbeat slow and your breathing deepen and your eyes lose focus in a way that feels like relief. The Saturations on the kneeling-ledges are arranged in concentric rings, and they are different from the ones in the warrens and the market. They are not person-shaped. They are… oriented. They face inward, toward the pool, and downward, toward the water, and the quality of their insistence is not *I am here* but something more like *this is here*, directed outward at the pool, at the water, at the still surface where water and stone share a plane of existence.

The pool, I think, is the densest Saturation of all. The pool is where the plateau's self-regard reaches its greatest intensity. The water is not merely still; it is still with *purpose*, still with *conviction*, still the way a held breath is still, except the breath has been held since before holding and breathing were different things. The surface of the pool is the flattest thing in the universe. Not a metaphor. It is a plane of such absolute levelness that it constitutes a mathematical ideal given physical form, and what the Saturations on the kneeling-ledges are doing — if *doing* is permissible, if *doing* can be extended to include the permanent state of being-oriented-toward-something — is attending to this. They attend. They face the perfection of the pool and they bring to it the surplus of existence that is their only substance, their only gift, and the pool holds that attention and does not ripple.

This is their worship. This is their commerce. This is their sleep, their dreaming, their waking, their breathing, their living and their dying. All of it is the same act: the act of being, performed with varying degrees of emphasis in different locations throughout a city that was shaped by no one for the express purpose of giving shape to this emphasis, of providing a geography for the variations in the intensity of *is*.

## The Ones in the Corners

I have to address the corners. The triangular pockets where three walls meet and the vegetation becomes impossible, where depth exceeds dimension and the green goes back and back and back.

The Saturations in the corners are not like the others.

The others are stable. Patient. Locked into their permanent moment with the same equanimity as the stone and the water and the light. The ones in the corners are — I hesitate, because any word I use will imply a dynamism that does not exist here, but — *pressured*. They are under compression. The intensified being in a corner-pocket is not calmly filling a person-shaped absence; it is *packed*, crammed, forced into a space that is simultaneously too small (the corner is only a corner, a meter of triangular floor-space) and too large (the green depth recedes inward toward something that has no back wall). The Saturations here are dense to the point of paradox. They are so *much* — so saturated with the quality of existence — that they have begun to distort the local rules even more than the plateau's general ontological fatigue already distorts them.

In the corners, the distinction between the Saturation and its environment breaks down. The intensified being does not rest on the moss or hover above the stone; it *is* the moss, *is* the stone, *is* the particular quality of green-tinged light and low-register sound that fills the pocket. The corner-Saturations are not inhabitants of the city; they are places where the city has become so thoroughly, so emphatically *itself* that it has developed the property of *mattering*. Not mattering *to* someone. Just mattering. Mattering as an intransitive verb. Having significance without a signifier or a signified, being important in the absence of anyone to whom importance could be important.

And the pressure. The sense of something trying to emerge. This is the thing about the corners that makes them different, that makes them almost — I do not say this lightly — *frightening*. Not dangerous. Nothing here is dangerous. But in the corners, the eternal present tense of the plateau is under strain. The surplus of being, packed too densely, too emphatically, has nowhere to go — the plateau does not permit change, does not permit *going* — and so it pushes against the only boundary available to it: the threshold between *existing* and *existing-as-something-in-particular*. Between being and *becoming*. Between the generalized glow of undifferentiated *is* and the specific, individual, bounded, mortal fact of being a *this*.

They are trying to be born.

Not consciously. Not with effort or desire or intention. The way a crystal tries to form in a supersaturated solution — not by trying, but by being the inevitable consequence of concentration exceeding the capacity of the medium to hold it in suspension. The corners are supersaturated with existence, and the existence is pressing toward crystallization into *something specific*, something with edges and identity and the terrible, liberating finitude of being one thing and not another.

But it can't. It can never. Because the plateau does not permit change, and birth is the most radical change there is — the change from nothing-in-particular to something-in-particular, from potential to actual, from the warm, thick, undifferentiated glow of *is* to the sharp, cold, specific fact of *I am*. And so the corners remain pressured, permanently, the Saturations within them permanently almost-crystallizing, permanently on the verge of the most fundamental threshold of all, and the vegetation in these pockets grows deeper and greener and more impossible not because it is growing but because the intensity of *almost-becoming* has saturated the local reality so thoroughly that it manifests as depth, as fecundity, as the visual metaphor of *more*.

## The Sound and the Almost

Listen. Stand in the market square and listen past the chord, past the oceanic drone, past the harmonics and the interference patterns and the pockets of singing and silence. Listen with the faculty that is not hearing but that the sound speaks to anyway, the part of you that feels the vibration in your sternum and knows it for what it is.

The sound is them. Not a sound they make — they make nothing; making requires agency and time and change. The sound is what they *are*. Every Saturation, every thickened region of intensified being, every person-shaped absence filled with surplus existence, is a node in the chord. They are the standing waves. They are the places where the city's water-driven, stone-resonated, architecture-amplified voice constructively interferes with itself and produces a local maximum of vibrational intensity, and this intensity is not merely acoustic but *ontological* — it is a vibration in the fabric of being itself, a trembling in the membrane between *is* and *isn't*, and the sound you hear with your ears is only the lowest-dimensional shadow of a vibration that extends through every axis of existence this place possesses.

They are notes. They are the notes the city plays. And the city is the instrument. And the water is the breath. And the stone is the body. And the light is the audience, and the plants are the audience, and the heat is the audience, and you — if you are here, if you can be here, if the concept of *you* can maintain its coherence in a place where identity is a foreign concept — you are the audience too, and also a note, because your presence in this space would create its own node, its own local intensification of being, your *hereness* adding to the *hereness* that is already here, and the chord would shift — not change, change is impossible, but *accommodate* — and you would feel yourself becoming part of it, feel the boundary between your being and the plateau's being softening the way the boundary between vine and stone has softened, the way the boundary between water and basalt has softened, the way every boundary in this place has softened except the one that matters, the one between *almost* and *actual*, the one that the corner-Saturations press against forever without crossing —

That threshold. That is the sound. The great chord, the deafening, eternal, all-encompassing roar-hum-whisper-scream that defines the plateau more than anything else, is the sound of the threshold *holding*. It is the sound of almost-becoming maintained at infinite duration. It is the vibration of a boundary under permanent stress, a membrane between potential and actual that has been stretched taut by the sheer accumulated pressure of existence pressing against it from one side and specificity refusing to form on the other, and the sound never changes because the pressure never changes because the stasis never breaks because the plateau does not permit the passage of time and without time there is no relief, no release, no birth, no collapse, no resolution.

The Saturations — the Witnesses, the Threshold-Dwellers, the almost-alive, the person-shaped absences filled with too much *is* — are the sound the world makes when it is trying to wake up and cannot.

---

## Finally

Who lives here?

*Living* lives here. Not a living thing. Not a creature or a being or a spirit or an echo. Living itself — the verb, the process, the bare phenomenon of existence-in-a-place stripped of every particular and left as pure, trembling, superheated potential. It pervades the city. It saturates the stone and the water and the green and the sound. It is so thick you can feel it on your skin, in your lungs, in the vibration of your teeth. It presses against you from every direction. It wants — not *wants*, there is no desire here, but exerts the directionless pressure of — to be you. To be anyone. To be *someone*. To cross the threshold from the general into the specific, from the verb into the noun, from the infinite, roaring, static potential of *being* into the finite, quiet, mortal actuality of *a being*.

It has always been here. It has always been almost. The city is its body, its waiting room, its cocoon, its forever-almost-hatching egg. The sound is its voice, or will be, when it has a voice, which it never will, because the moment never comes, because there are no moments here, only the one, the permanent one, the one that has been vibrating at the frequency of *almost* since before *since* meant anything.

And if you stand in the market square long enough — though there is no long enough, there is no long, there is no short, there is only the standing — you will begin to feel it not as something external, not as a presence in the city around you, but as something recognizable. Something familiar. The raw, undifferentiated, pre-individual throb of *aliveness* that you have felt in yourself in moments of extremity — the moment before waking, the moment before remembering your own name, the moment when the anesthesia is wearing off and you exist but do not yet exist *as yourself* — that glow, that hum, that trembling, is what fills this place. Is what *is* this place. The plateau is made of the moment before consciousness. The city is built from the instant before the first thought. The Saturations are the held breath before the first cry.

And the breath is never released. And the cry never comes. And the chord never resolves. And the drop of water strikes the carved hand at exactly the same point with exactly the same sound, forever, and the moss grows no greener because it cannot grow greener because it is already as green as the concept of green permits, and the heat presses down, and the light glows, and the city dreams itself without a dreamer, and something that is not alive and not dead and not waiting and not patient pulses in every stone and every leaf and every drop of water with the absolute, unwavering, deafening conviction that *this* — exactly this — is the moment just before everything begins.

It always is. It always will be. The beginning is always about to happen.

It never does.

And the sound is deafening.
