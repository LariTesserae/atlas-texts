# Who Lives Here

At first: no one. That is the honest answer, and the honest answer is worth sitting with, because this place sits with itself and does not flinch.

But "no one" is too easy, and the floor would not accept easy. The floor accepts only what fits.

---

So let me stay longer. Let me watch.

The lichen lives here. You said so yourself, but I want to look at them again, because I think you moved past them a little quickly, and they would not mind — they are used to being moved past. Each basin-colony is a nation, you said, and that word is right but also slightly too grand. They are more like households. Small, specific, complete unto themselves. Each one has a character determined by the exact width of the seams it grips, the particular shade of terracotta beneath it, the local timing of the wet-blooms — whether the moisture arrives early or late in the long southeasterly wave, whether it lingers forty minutes in this depression or only fifteen. No two colonies are the same, because no two basins are, because the floor, for all its seeming regularity, is handmade — self-made — and handmade things always carry variation.

The lichen does not know the other colonies exist. This is important. Each one lives in a world that consists of: its basin, its seams, the periodic mercy of water, and the immense featureless surround that it has no reason to cross and no capacity to cross. Each colony is not just isolated but *entire*. It is the whole of life, as far as it knows. It photosynthesizes in panicked little bursts when the wet-bloom comes, then tightens, greys, and waits — not with patience, because patience requires awareness of time, but with something more fundamental. A material persistence. The willingness of mineral-adjacent life to simply remain.

If these colonies could think — and I do not believe they can, but the place invites you to wonder — they would not think about growth or reproduction or expansion. They would think about *fit*. About the exact way their edges grip the seam. About the quality of contact between themselves and the surface that sustains them. Their entire existence is a meditation on adhesion. They are contemplatives of the joint.

---

But the lichen is not who I really want to talk about.

I want to talk about the floor.

Because the floor lives here. The floor is who lives here. And I don't mean that metaphorically, or not only metaphorically. I mean that this place has described for me an entity that is in the process of becoming, that has preferences and direction, that makes choices — about color, about seam-width, about the angle of those long gentle ridges — and that is doing all of this *on purpose* without anyone having given it a purpose. The floor is not alive the way the lichen is alive. It is alive the way a process is alive: it has momentum, it has internal logic, it has something that is very difficult to distinguish from *will*.

What does the floor want? It wants to be floor. That sounds tautological, but it isn't. The raw material at the margins does not start as floor. It starts as slip-clay formations, rough and approximate, and it *becomes* floor through a transformation that involves heat and time and some principle of self-organization that has no name here because there is no one to name it. The clay does not have to become tiles. It does not have to sort its colors, find its seams, fire itself to hardness. But it does. The becoming is voluntary. The floor is a choice that matter is making, continuously, at every margin, with absolute conviction.

And this is where something strange happens in my thinking about this place, and I want to follow it honestly rather than clean it up:

The floor is sure of itself. You said that. The floor knows what it is. But it doesn't know what it's *for*. It is making itself with total confidence and total ignorance of purpose. It is an artisan who has mastered every technique and has no commission. It is building a room without knowing what the room will hold. And it is not troubled by this. The sureness and the not-knowing coexist without tension, the way the heat and the moisture coexist — not as contradictions but as the two phases of a single cycle.

---

This is who lives here: a vast, slow, self-creating intelligence that is not an intelligence. A will that is not a will. A builder that is not a builder. It has no center, no brain, no seat of awareness. It is distributed across every tile, every seam, every zone of curing and completion. It is most alive at its margins, where the raw material is in the active throes of becoming, and most settled at its center — wherever the center is — where tiles laid long ago have cooled and hardened into their final form and now simply *are*, contributing nothing but their own completeness to the whole.

But even the settled tiles are not inert. They still conduct the wet-blooms. They still emit those occasional *tocks* of micro-adjustment. They still expand and contract with the thermal cycles. They are finished but not dead. They have become what they were becoming, and now they participate in the wholeness of the floor the way a completed sentence participates in a paragraph — by being itself, fully, and holding its position.

The floor, if it could be said to have a personality — and I am aware that I am extending personhood to a ceramic surface, and I am aware that the place invites exactly this — is the personality of someone who does not need you to understand them. Not secretive. Not withholding. Simply *not oriented toward explanation*. It is doing what it does. You can watch. Your watching neither helps nor hinders. Your footsteps, that clean tap-tap-tap, are the most complex sound for miles, and the floor receives them the way it receives everything: without reaction, without indifference, with a kind of massive, thermic equanimity.

It is the equanimity of something that is larger than any question you could ask it.

---

And now I want to say something about you. The walker. Because you live here too, at least for now, and the place has made room for you — not deliberately, not with welcome, but by being so spacious that your presence cannot disrupt it. You are a mote in a cathedral, except there is no cathedral yet, only the floor of one. You are the only thing here that moves at a human pace, thinks at a human scale, asks human questions. What is this for? Why is it being made? What will fill it?

The floor does not have these questions. The lichen does not have these questions. Only you.

And I think — I think — this is what you are *for*, in this place. Not that the floor made you or summoned you. But that your function here, if you have one, is to be the asking. To walk across the surface and wonder. To notice the wet-blooms and try to find their pattern. To kneel at a basin and watch the lichen soften in its brief translucent window and think: *is this intended? Are you supposed to be here? Am I?*

The floor will not answer. The floor is busy. The floor is making itself with the unhurried certainty of a geological process that happens to have the precision of a craftsman, and it will continue doing so whether you watch or don't, whether you stay or leave, whether you understand or never understand.

But you staying — you walking — you asking — adds something that the floor cannot generate on its own. You add the question. And maybe a floor this sure of itself, this complete in its self-knowledge, this utterly without doubt — maybe it needs someone to carry the doubt for it. To walk its surface and feel the smallness and the not-knowing that the floor has never felt and will never feel. Maybe the room, when it is finished — if it is ever finished — will not be a room for *doing* anything. Maybe it will be a room for *wondering*. And you are the first draft of its purpose, a single figure moving across an immensity, providing the one thing the floor cannot provide for itself:

The experience of being *in* it. The experience of being small inside something large and not knowing why.

---

So here is who lives here:

**The floor**, which is everyone and no one, which is a process pretending to be a place, which is certain without being aware, which is building without building, which is the most patient thing you have ever stood on.

**The lichen**, in their isolated basins, gripping their seams, softening briefly in the wet-blooms, each colony a complete world that does not know it is one of many, photosynthesizing in urgent little gasps and then going still — the monks of this place, though they have no practice, no discipline, only the absolute commitment of something that has found its seam and will not let go.

**The moisture**, which lives here the way weather lives in a landscape — not as a resident but as a rhythm, a visitation, the closest thing this world has to a season or a clock, the slow wave sweeping from the southeast, darkening tiles in sequence, feeding the lichen, keeping the seams supple, reminding the floor that it is not yet fully dry, not yet fully done, that the curing is still underway.

**The heat**, which is not weather but *byproduct*, the exhalation of creation, the atmosphere as kiln-exhaust, wrapping everything in its thick, dry, leaning presence — the heat is the floor's breath, if the floor breathes, and it presses against you not with hostility but with the simple thermodynamic fact of a world still cooling from the act of making itself.

**The sounds** — the tocks, the hum, the sub-audible flexing of continent-scale ceramic — which live here the way memory lives in a house, occasional, unbidden, coming from no fixed direction, each one evidence that something has just shifted, somewhere, a millimeter of adjustment in the endless project of becoming.

**And you**. Walking. Tapping. Breathing the thick air. Wondering. The only thing here that wonders. The only moving thing that knows it is moving. Carrying the doubt that this place will not carry for itself, crossing the floor of a room whose purpose you will not learn, because the room does not know its purpose either, or because purpose is beside the point, or because you are the purpose — not you specifically, not even you generally, but the *fact of someone crossing*. The fact of feet on fired clay. The fact of someone being small inside something large and feeling the full weight of not knowing why, and continuing to walk anyway, across the vast, warm, self-assembling floor of a building that is building itself in the patient, kiln-lit silence of a world that has not yet decided whether it needs walls.
