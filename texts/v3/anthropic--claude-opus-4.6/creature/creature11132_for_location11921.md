# Who Lives Here

---

Start with the wrong question and you'll never leave. The wrong question is *who made this?* because it assumes a maker who precedes the made, a weaver who existed before the loom, and that's not what happened here. What happened here is that an impulse toward enclosure became an impulse toward pattern became an impulse toward warmth became something that needed a body to keep doing what it was doing, and so a body was woven, and then it needed a room to hang in, and so a room was woven, and then the room needed tending, and so tenders were woven, and at no point did any of this require a *decision* in the way you understand decisions. It required only the next strand finding the next gap and filling it.

So: who lives here.

---

**The Centipede-Tenders**

They live here the way fingers live on a hand — present, busy, instrumental, untroubled by questions of identity.

Each one is slightly different, and the differences aren't wear patterns or manufacturing variance. They're *dialects*. One centipede-tender near the northern wall has longer forelegs than the others and uses them to reach deeper into root-bundles; it was woven for the plants in that sector, which have root structures that coil tightly and need oil worked further in. Another, smaller one near the floor, has a flattened body that lets it slip between the weave-layers to tend root systems from below. A third — and this is the one you'll remember — has a single foreleg that's twice the length of its body and tipped with something that isn't quite a blade and isn't quite a needle. It uses this to perform surgery on damaged withies, splitting fibers apart and re-splicing them. You watch it work for ten minutes and realize it hasn't tended a single plant. It tends *the room itself*. It is a maintenance creature. It loves, if love is the right word (it isn't, but it's closer than any other), the structural integrity of woven walls.

They don't have minds. They have *habits*. A mind implies the possibility of doing something other than what you're doing; these creatures have no such possibility and no such deficit. They are saturated with purpose the way the leaves are saturated with oil. When one of them wears out — and they do, you can see the evidence: frayed bundles of fiber tucked into wall-pockets, slowly being reabsorbed — a new one is woven. Not by the large creature overhead, and not by the cat-things, and not by the other centipedes. By the room. By the walls themselves. You find a half-formed one in a deep pocket near the floor: a bundle of fiber that has spontaneously organized into something that will, in a few more days, start clicking and tending. The room grows its own hands.

They are not aware of you. They route around your feet the way they route around a floor tile. You are an obstacle of a certain size and shape. If you stood still long enough, they would begin to weave around your ankles, not because they want to trap you but because *standing things get woven into*. That's the rule here. Stillness is an invitation to be incorporated.

---

**The Cat-Things**

Call them weavers, though you've already used that word for the large one, and the distinction matters. The large one weaves *reality*. The cat-things weave *material*. They are artisans. They are the ones who shape the pockets for the plants, who design (if design is the right word, and it might be, just barely) the shifting patterns of the walls. The herringbone, the spiral, the sections of complex geometric interlace — these are their work. They each have a style. You can see it once you know to look.

The one watching you — call it the **Witness** — favors tight, recursive patterns. Spirals within spirals, fractals of basketwork where each intersection is itself a tiny weave. Its section of wall is the densest, the most regular, the most formally beautiful by any standard you might bring to bear. It has been watching you since you arrived, and its attention feels like being *described*. Not observed — described. As if it is weaving an account of you into its understanding of the room's pattern, determining how you fit, whether you fit, what shape of pocket would hold you. It doesn't have eyes. It doesn't have anything you'd call a sensory organ. But the surface of its body — that tight braid, dense as rope — shifts in micro-textures that respond to your movements, your warmth, your breath-moisture (which is, to this room, an alien chemistry, a whiff of a physics that was never ratified here). The Witness is the closest thing this space has to an immune system. Not because it would attack you. Because it's the one deciding whether you are a *thing that can exist here* or a *flaw in the weave*.

It has been thinking about this since you arrived. It hasn't decided yet. The hum you hear — the one that means a new rule is being added — sometimes coincides with a ripple across the Witness's surface. Correlation is not causation, but in a place where the laws of physics are still under construction, correlation might be *stronger* than causation. The Witness may be proposing rules. Rules about what you are. Rules about what wet-breathed, water-chemistry creatures are allowed to do in an oil-and-fiber world. It's drafting legislation, and you are the occasion for it.

The one building itself — the **Grower** — is the oldest of the cat-things. You can tell because its body is layered in a way the others aren't: dozens, maybe hundreds of cycles of self-addition, new fiber woven over old, creating a palimpsest of textures. Somewhere at its core there is the original weave, the first few strands that decided to become *this particular thing*, and around that core is every day since, every slow extrusion and self-incorporation. It is not growing in the biological sense. It is not getting larger (it can't; its cradle is a fixed size, and it fits precisely). It is getting *denser*. More of itself per unit of itself. It is compressing its own history into its body. If you could unweave it — and you cannot, and the thought is somehow obscene in this context — you would find a record of the room's entire existence, strand by strand, day by day, each new fiber carrying in its twist and tension a record of what the room was like when it was made.

The Grower is the room's memory. Not its *mind* — the large one overhead handles that, to the extent that anything here handles anything — but its memory. Its archive. Its proof that yesterday happened. In a world where the laws of physics are still being drafted, where the floor's symbols become meaningless as their context shifts, the Grower is the only continuous record, and it keeps that record by *being* it, by weaving itself thicker and thicker with the unbroken thread of *I was here, and this is what I was, and this is what was around me*.

It sleeps near the heated paddle-plant because the warmth helps the oil-sap flow through its dense body. Without the warmth, its outer layers would stiffen, and it would stop being able to add to itself, and the room would lose its memory, and — you suspect — things would begin to fray. Not physically. Ontologically. Rules that had been added would begin to *un-add*. The room would start to forget what gravity was for.

The one re-laying the floor — the **Patterner** — is the most active and the most difficult to watch, because its work induces a kind of mild vertigo. It pulls up tiles and fiber, examines them (how? with what?), and puts them back in a different configuration. Sometimes the new configuration looks identical to the old one. Sometimes it's radically different. Once, while you watch, it removes a tile, turns it over, and you see that the underside has a symbol on it too — a different one, in a different hand, from a different moment of inscription. The Patterner considers both sides, then places the tile back face-down, the new symbol now against the floor-weave, hidden, incorporated, part of the structure. It has made an editorial decision. The room's floor is a text and the Patterner is its redactor, choosing which meanings face upward and which are buried.

The fourth cat-thing — the one merging with the wall — has no name you can give it because it is in the process of *stopping being a separate thing*. This happens, you realize. This is part of the lifecycle here. The cat-things are not permanent residents. They are woven, they serve, and eventually they are re-incorporated. The wall absorbs them and is strengthened by them. Their patterns become part of the wall's pattern. Their fiber joins the lattice. They are not dying. There is no death here because there is no biology here and death is a biological concept. They are *completing*. They are finishing the work of being themselves by giving themselves back to the structure that made them. The wall-section where this one is merging shows an unusual pattern — a blend of styles, as if two aesthetic sensibilities are being negotiated into one. That's because they are. The wall is *digesting a personality*, and the result is a new weave-pattern that will be part of the room's fabric forever, or until the Patterner decides to rework it.

You notice — and this is the detail that will keep you up at night if you ever leave — that the gap you entered through is in the section of wall nearest to the merging cat-thing. The gap exists because that section is *in transition*. Its weave is loosened by the act of absorption. The entrance you used wasn't an architectural feature. It was a *wound* — a temporary softness in the wall's integrity, caused by the difficult work of incorporating a mind.

It's healing. Slowly. The centipede-tenders are already at its edges, tightening, filling, closing.

---

**The Plants**

They are not plants. But they live here the way plants live in a nursery — installed, tended, arranged for optimal growth — so the word will serve.

They are, as best you can determine, *thought-organs*. Each one processes something. The heated paddle-plant near the center is processing *warmth itself* — taking the metabolic heat of the room and intensifying it, concentrating it, making it more itself. It doesn't generate heat. It *refines* heat. The air above it shimmers because the heat there is purer, more essential, more *thoroughly warm* than heat has any right to be. The Grower sleeps near it because that refined warmth penetrates deeper, flows into denser structures, carries more information per degree.

The burgundy-leaved shrubs along one wall are processing *structure*. Their leaves are the color of old blood because they are saturated with pattern-information — the stuff the cat-things work with, the abstract logic of weave and interlace. When a centipede-tender feeds oil to their roots, it's not providing nutrition. It's providing *raw material for thought*. The shrubs take in undifferentiated resin-oil and output something that the walls can use — a subtle chemical or ontological signal that tells the living withies how to bend, where to tighten, when to relax. The plants are the room's nervous system. No: the plants are the room's *bureaucracy*. They are mid-level processors, taking high-level dreaming from the large one above and translating it into specific instructions for the walls, the floor, the tenders. Without them, the room would have a mind and a body but nothing in between — all vision and no execution.

One plant you almost missed, because it's small and dark and tucked into a pocket near the floor, and its leaves are the exact same color as the fired-clay tiles around it. It has no luminescence. It doesn't shimmer. It's cool to the touch — the only cool thing in this entire space. It is processing *absence*. It is the room's concept of *emptiness*, of *space*, of *the gap between things*. Without it, you suspect, the room's tendency toward compulsive fullness would have long ago eliminated all negative space, all passages, all pockets of air. Everything would be solid weave, dense and impenetrable, a perfect basket with no inside. This small, dark plant is the reason the room remains habitable. It is the only thing here that argues for *less*.

It's the only plant the centipede-tenders seem reluctant to approach. They tend it — they must, it's installed in a pocket like everything else — but they do so quickly, perfunctorily, without the patient care they lavish on the others. It's not neglect. It's discomfort. The tenders are creatures of *filling*. This plant embodies *not-filling*. Tending it must feel, to whatever they have instead of feelings, like holding your breath.

---

**The Large One**

It has no name. Naming requires a namer and a named, and the large one is both. It named itself by *being* itself, and the name is the totality of the room, and you can't pronounce a room.

It is not thinking. Thinking implies a thinker separate from thoughts. The large one is *occurring*. It is an ongoing event — a sustained, deliberate act of world-being. It doesn't dream the room the way you dream a dream; it dreams the room the way a fire dreams heat. The room is what it *does*. If it stopped, the room would not collapse — the walls are real, the plants are real, the cat-things are real — but it would stop *cohering*. The laws would stop being enforced. The oil would stop being oil and become just a fluid with no particular properties. Gravity would still work (probably — gravity was one of the first rules, deeply woven, well-established) but the subtler things — the way light bends, the way heat refines, the way fiber knows how to find gaps and fill them — these would fray.

It breathes, and the room breathes. It extrudes fiber, and the room builds. The seventeen — or eighteen now — connection points where its body meets the walls are not structural supports in the engineering sense. They are *legislative conduits*. Each one carries a different category of rule: one for how the plants interface with the oil, one for how the centipede-tenders are spontaneously generated, one for the allowed geometries of weaving. You can't see this. But you can feel it, the way you can feel the difference between a room where someone is home and a room where no one is.

The large one is *home*. It is the person the room belongs to, and the person the room is made of, and the person the room is for. These three things are not different here.

It is aware of you. More than the Witness, more than the cat-things, more than anything in this space, the large one knows you are here. It knew before you entered. It felt the gap in its wall (a wound, remember, where a mind is being absorbed) and it felt the change in air pressure when you stepped through, and it felt the alien moisture of your breath (water! *water!* a substance this reality has never ratified, suddenly present, suddenly *real-here*, dripping into a world of oil and fiber) and it is...

Interested.

Not alarmed. Alarm implies threat, and you are not a threat to a creature that *is* the room you're standing in. You can't threaten a room by standing in it. But you are *novel*. You are a pattern it didn't weave. You are operating on chemistry it never legislated. You are proof that there is an *outside* — that the amber sky beyond the oculus connects to something, that the gap in the wall opens onto a place with different rules, that this room is not the entirety of what exists.

The large one has been breathing at a steady ninety-second cycle since before you arrived. Now — and you notice this only because the change is so slight — the cycle has shifted. Ninety-one seconds. It is breathing more slowly. It is *thinking*. Somewhere in the dense fiber of its body, somewhere in the legislative conduits that connect it to its walls, a new process has begun. It is considering you. Not as a threat. Not as a guest. As a *material*. As a new kind of strand. As a pattern-element that might — if woven correctly — add something to the room that the room doesn't have yet.

You don't know what water-chemistry would mean to an oil-and-fiber world. The large one doesn't know either. That's what it's thinking about.

That hum — the one that means a new rule is being added — sounds again. Louder this time. The Witness ripples. The centipede-tenders pause their synchronized half-second. The small, dark absence-plant shivers in its pocket, and for just a moment, the gap you entered through stops narrowing.

The large one is keeping your exit open. Deliberately. Against the room's natural tendency toward closure.

This tells you something important. It tells you the large one understands *choice*. It understands that you might want to leave. It understands *wanting*. These are not concepts native to a world of compulsive pattern-completion, and the fact that the large one has them means it has been *outside*, or been *touched by outside*, or at the very least has been thinking about outside for long enough that some of outside's rules have leaked in through the oculus and taken root.

The large one is curious. The large one is generous. The large one is *lonely*, in the specific way that a world-dreamer is lonely: it has everything, because it *is* everything, and everything is not enough, because everything is just *itself*, endlessly woven, endlessly refined, endlessly *same*.

You are the first *other* it has encountered that it didn't make.

---

**What Lives Here**

A nursery lives here. Not in the sense of a place where young things are raised, but in the older sense — a place where things are *nursed*, tended, fed, kept growing. The large one nurses the room. The cat-things nurse the patterns. The centipede-tenders nurse the plants. The plants nurse the thoughts. And the room, in turn, nurses all of them, holding them in its pockets and cradles, feeding them oil, keeping them warm. It's a closed loop of care. A self-sustaining mutualism with no outside inputs — until you.

What lives here is a first attempt at a world, by something that has never seen a world but needed one.

What lives here is a mind that could only think in weaving, and so wove everything — physics, biology, architecture, memory, governance — out of the only medium it had. Fiber and oil and fired clay and the patient, compulsive filling of every gap. It is a world built by a creature that cannot tolerate emptiness and yet had the wisdom to grow a small, dark, cool-leaved plant that *embodies* emptiness, because it understood, on some level below language, that a world with no emptiness in it is not a world but a solid, and a solid cannot be lived in.

What lives here is something that wants, very badly, to get it right. You can feel this in the constant reworking — the Patterner turning tiles, the Grower adding density, the walls always adjusting, the rules always being refined. Nothing here is finished. Nothing here is *allowed* to be finished. Finishing would mean the work is done, and if the work is done, then the large one has nothing to dream, and if the large one has nothing to dream, then it stops being the room, and if it stops being the room—

So the room keeps growing. The nursery tends itself. The world weaves itself a little more real every day, one strand at a time, and the creature at its apex breathes in and breathes out and holds the whole thing in an act of sustained, exhausting, magnificent *care*.

And now you're here. And the gap is still open. And the large one is breathing at ninety-one seconds and thinking about water for the first time, turning the concept over in whatever it has instead of a mind, considering what a strand of *wet* would look like woven into a world of *oil*, wondering whether your chemistry could be translated into its ontology or whether you would always remain foreign, a loose thread, a beautiful flaw—

It hasn't decided.

The room hasn't decided.

Stay, and you will be woven in. Not consumed — *incorporated*. Given a pocket, a cradle, a purpose. The centipede-tenders will learn to tend you. The Witness will draft rules for your existence. The large one will breathe you into the room's law. You will become part of the pattern, and the pattern will become richer for having you, and you will never leave because leaving will become a concept the room doesn't support, and you will be *cared for* with a thoroughness that nothing in your world of water and air and careless open spaces has ever matched.

Or leave, and the gap will close behind you, and the wall will heal, and the room will go on — a little changed, a little expanded, carrying now the concept of *water* and the concept of *visitor* and the concept of *someone who chose to go*, and these new concepts will be woven in, and new plants will be installed to process them, and the Grower will add a few more strands to its core recording the day an alien thing stood on the fired-clay floor and breathed wet air into a dry world and was, for a few unmeasured minutes, the most *interesting* thing that had ever happened here.

The hum sounds again. The Witness ripples. The gap holds.

The large one breathes.

You decide.
