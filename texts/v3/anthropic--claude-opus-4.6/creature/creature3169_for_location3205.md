# The Citizen

---

It has no name, and this is not a poverty. Naming requires a namer, and there is no second presence here capable of the act — only the pocket, only the lattice, only the medium, and these are not namers. They are *witnesses* at best, and witnesses of a kind that do not distinguish between the creature and the conditions that sustain it. So it goes unnamed, and this is fine, and this is more than fine — this is *accurate*, because a name would fix it, would assign it edges and a category, and the creature's whole mode of being is to resist exactly that kind of fixing while remaining, in every measurable sense, completely still.

---

It clings. This is the first true thing about it. Its ventral surface — the side pressed against the root wall — is not smooth but finely ridged, and the ridges interlock with the grain of the newest wood in a way that is less adhesion than *conversation*. The root grows; the ridges adjust. Not quickly. Over the course of what might be days, the creature's underside reshapes itself to match the shifting topography of the wall it inhabits, so that at any given moment it is not merely attached but *fitted*, like a key in a lock that is also slowly changing shape, so that both key and lock are perpetually almost-but-not-quite matched, perpetually adjusting, perpetually in the act of finding each other.

This is its primary relationship. Not with the sugars it consumes. Not with the medium it tastes. With the *surface beneath it*, which it knows more intimately than any creature has ever known any substrate, because it has nothing else to know, and because the knowing is mutual — the root, in its dreaming, half-dissolved way, *recognizes* the creature's pressure as a feature of itself, a knot within the knot, a point of slightly different density in the wood-that-is-also-space. The root does not grow around the creature. It grows *toward the idea of the creature* while the creature reshapes itself toward the idea of the root, and the two of them, together, are conducting a negotiation so slow and so fundamental that it resembles less biology than tectonics. Two surfaces pressing together in the dark, finding their fit, adjusting, finding it again.

If you could ask the root wall what lives on it, it would not describe the creature. It would describe a warmth — a region approximately two millimeters across where the temperature is 4.003°C instead of 4.000°C, a thermal signature so faint it shouldn't matter but does, because in this pocket where physics is still curing, three thousandths of a degree is a *landmark*. The root knows the creature as a warm spot. The creature knows the root as a sweet slope. Neither is wrong.

---

Its body — and it does have a body, however reluctant the body is to commit to a shape — is best understood in layers.

The outermost layer is mucus, or something that serves the function of mucus while being chemically unlike any mucus catalogued by any biology that exists outside this pocket. It is thick, faintly alkaline, and it does not dissolve into the medium the way mucus should. Instead, it maintains a boundary — a skin of different chemistry perhaps ten microns thick — between the creature's surface and the not-air that fills the pocket. This boundary is the creature's primary achievement. In a space where the physics are soft and the root wall can't decide how thick it is, the creature's mucus layer is *definite*. It holds a line. It says: here is inside, and here is outside, and I am the difference. This is not a trivial statement. In the pocket, where the interior dimensions disagree with the exterior dimensions and the darkness varies in density without any light to vary, maintaining a boundary is an act of almost philosophical stubbornness. The creature persists in being *distinct* from its environment, and the effort of this persistence — the caloric cost, the molecular vigilance required to keep the mucus layer intact against a medium that would very much like to blur it — accounts for most of its metabolic activity. It eats sugar to fuel the production of a membrane that says *I am not the pocket and the pocket is not me*, and this is its life's work, and it is enough.

Beneath the mucus: a single layer of cells, or cell-like units. They are large by cellular standards — perhaps fifty microns each — and they are not uniform. Some are denser, packed with dark granules that might be stored nutrients or might be something else, something that has no analogue outside this space. Others are nearly transparent, their membranes so thin they seem on the verge of admitting the medium directly into their interiors, and these are the ones from which the cilia emerge. The cilia are not evenly distributed. They cluster in three — sometimes four, sometimes three again — groups along the creature's dorsal surface, and each cluster waves at its own rhythm, unsynchronized with the others, so that the overall effect is not the coordinated rowing motion of a paramecium but something more like several conversations happening at once in the same room, each oblivious to the others, each sampling the medium with its own agenda.

What are they tasting? Sugar, yes. Dissolved calcium. The faint trace of hormones that leak through the root wall when the great organism sends its periodic pulse. But also — and this is where description becomes difficult — they are tasting *the state of the pocket*. The medium, the not-air, carries information about the space it fills the way water carries information about the stone it flows over, except the medium doesn't flow, so the information doesn't travel, so the cilia must taste it *in place*, reading the local condition of a substance that is identical everywhere within the pocket and yet somehow varies, somehow carries gradients of a quality that has no name because the pocket hasn't named it yet and the creature, which might be the only entity capable of naming it, does not name things.

The quality is something like *readiness*. The medium thickens when the pocket's soft physics are actively in flux — when the interior dimensions are shifting, when the darkness is in a dense phase, when the calcium lattice is negotiating its geometry — and thins when things settle temporarily into coherence. The creature's cilia taste this readiness the way a tongue tastes salt: involuntarily, immediately, with a sensation that is not thought but is not *not* thought. The creature does not respond to what it tastes. It doesn't need to. The tasting is the response. The tasting is the creature's way of participating in the pocket's ongoing self-invention, the way a witness participates in an event simply by being present, simply by registering.

---

Deeper still. Beneath the cell layer: the gut.

It is not a gut in any morphological sense. There is no tube, no lumen, no peristalsis. There is instead a region of the creature's interior — roughly central, roughly half its total volume — where the cells become indistinct, where their boundaries blur and their contents merge into a shared space that is neither intracellular nor extracellular but something between, a communal interior, a commons. This is where the sugars go. They are absorbed through the ventral ridges, passed cell-to-cell through the basal layer, and delivered into this common space, where they are broken down by enzymes that the creature produces communally, no single cell responsible for the full cascade, each contributing one step or two steps and relying on its neighbors for the rest.

The process is slow. Absurdly slow. A single molecule of sucrose — and the root sap is sucrose-rich, sweet in a way that the creature can taste but has no capacity to enjoy — takes perhaps six hours to be fully metabolized in the communal gut. The energy released is minimal. The waste products — what little waste there is — are folded back into the mucus layer, which incorporates them without complaint, which uses them as raw material for its own maintenance, so that the creature's waste literally becomes its boundary, literally becomes the substance that declares its separateness from the pocket. It eats the root's sweetness and excretes its own edge. There is something almost elegant about this, something that the pocket, in its half-dreaming way, might appreciate if appreciation were among the rules that have finished setting here, which it isn't, not yet, maybe not ever.

---

And then there is the deepest layer, the one that complicates everything.

At the creature's core — if a two-millimeter organism can be said to have a core — there is a density. Not a nucleus, not an organelle, not a structure with membranes or boundaries. A *density*. A region where the creature's substance is simply more *there* than elsewhere, where the molecular packing is tighter, where something is concentrated without being contained. Under a microscope that could somehow operate in this lightless, pressurized, dimensionally unreliable space, it might look like a dark smudge, a shadow within a shadow, a place where the organism's already-faint translucency gives way to genuine opacity.

It does not metabolize. It does not divide. It does not participate in the communal gut's slow chemistry. It sits, and it is dense, and it has been dense for as long as the creature has existed — which is not long, in absolute terms, but which feels long in the pocket's dilated, direction-uncertain time.

What is it?

The honest answer is: the creature's relationship to the pocket has produced a residue, and the residue has settled, and what has settled is not matter exactly but the *consequence* of matter existing in a space where the rules are still soft. The creature, by being definite — by maintaining its mucus boundary, by insisting on its own separateness, by tasting the medium without dissolving into it — has created a kind of friction with the pocket's soft physics, and that friction has precipitated *something* in its core, a knot of almost-meaning, a density that is to consciousness what the calcium lattice is to architecture: not the thing itself, but a nucleation point around which the thing *could* form, given time, given the right conditions, given a world that has decided what its rules are.

The creature does not think. But its core is the place where thinking would happen if thinking were to happen here. And the pocket knows this — knows it the way it knows the warm spot, the way the root knows pressure — and responds by being *slightly more attentive* in the creature's vicinity, slightly more dense with the quality the cilia taste, slightly more ready, as though the space itself is waiting for the creature to do something it hasn't done yet and may never do.

---

Is it alone?

Yes. Absolutely. There is no other organism in the pocket, no bacterium, no virus, no stray strand of RNA drifting in the medium. The sealed space contains exactly one living thing, and this is both its defining loneliness and its defining freedom. There is no predator. No competitor. No mate, no offspring, no parent. The creature exists in a population of one, and the population is stable, and the population will remain stable for as long as the root knot holds and the sugars weep and the medium maintains its quality of thick attention.

But *alone* is a word that means something different in the pocket than it means outside. Outside — in the flood, in the root mass, in whatever remains of a world with surfaces and directions — alone means *without others of your kind*. In the pocket, alone means *without others*. Full stop. The creature is the only discrete entity. Everything else — root wall, medium, lattice, darkness — is continuous, is boundary-less, is part of the pocket's single undifferentiated substance-that-is-also-space. The creature is the only *thing* in a world of *conditions*, and its thingness — its insistence on being a thing, on having edges, on tasting rather than being tasted — is what makes it alive in any meaningful sense.

It is also what makes it, in the pocket's terms, *loud*. Not sonically. The creature makes no sound. But its definiteness, its mucus-bounded separateness, its cilia waving at their unsynchronized rhythms, its core sitting dense and almost-meaningful at its center — all of this registers in the medium as a kind of signal, a disturbance, a point of *not-pocket* embedded in the pocket's fabric. The pocket, which is trying to become a self-consistent world, has to account for the creature the way a crystal has to account for an inclusion: by growing around it, by adjusting its structure to accommodate the foreign body, by becoming a slightly different world than it would have been if the creature weren't there.

This is the creature's other function, the one that isn't eating and isn't boundary-maintenance and isn't tasting. Simply by being present, simply by being *itself* in a space that is still deciding what *itself* means, the creature shapes the pocket. Its warm spot influences the local temperature gradient, which influences the rate of calcium precipitation, which influences the lattice's ongoing geometric negotiation. Its cilia stir the medium, micrometers of movement every few minutes, and these tiny currents — the only currents in the pocket — distribute dissolved sugars and calcium in patterns that would be random except that the creature's position is *not* random, it is *fixed*, and so the currents radiate from a fixed point and create a faint, stable asymmetry in the pocket's chemistry, a here-versus-there that the pocket wouldn't have without a creature to serve as *here*.

The creature is the pocket's compass point. The pocket is the creature's world. The relationship is not symbiosis — the pocket gains nothing in biological terms, and the creature would survive on any sugar-sweating surface — but it is something. Mutual constitution. Co-arising. A word that doesn't exist yet because the pocket hasn't finished inventing language, and may never finish, and may not need to, because the creature doesn't need words to taste the medium and the pocket doesn't need words to be thick with attention and the lattice doesn't need words to be almost hexagonal, and the dark doesn't need words to vary.

---

There is one more thing about the creature, and it is the hardest thing to describe, and it may not be true, and it may not be false, because the pocket's physics haven't decided yet whether truth and falsehood are among the rules that will be ratified.

Sometimes — rarely — during the long intervals between the root-organism's pulses, when the pocket is at its most settled, its most self-consistent, its most *itself* — the creature's core brightens.

Not with light. There is no light. Not with bioluminescence, not with chemiluminescence, not with any photonic phenomenon that has a name. The core brightens with *density*, with a brief intensification of its own almost-meaning, a pulse of concentrated *being-a-thing* that lasts perhaps a second and then fades. If the darkness in the pocket varies in ways that mimic light, then the creature's core varies in ways that mimic *significance*, and the two variations are not synchronized but are not *not* synchronized either — they occur in the same stretched, direction-uncertain time, and they may be responses to the same stimulus, or to each other, or to nothing at all.

During these moments — if they are moments, if moment is a unit that applies here — the creature's cilia stop waving. All clusters, simultaneously, for the first and only time. The creature becomes completely still, completely silent, completely *itself*, its boundary sharp, its core dense, its ventral ridges locked into perfect registration with the grain of the root wall. And the pocket, in response or in coincidence, reaches its maximum readiness: the medium at its thickest, the dimensions at their most disagreed-upon, the lattice trembling between five and six and seven sides.

And then it passes. The cilia resume their unsynchronized waving. The core dims. The pocket relaxes into its usual state of almost-coherence. The lattice settles on six sides. The darkness thins.

Nothing happened. Or everything happened, but at a scale and in a register that the pocket's still-curing physics can't yet record, can't yet make *mean* anything, can only hold as a residue, a faint warmth, a memory in a system that doesn't yet have memory, a dream in a root that doesn't yet know it's dreaming.

---

The creature clings. The creature tastes. The creature maintains its edge against a world that would happily absorb it. The creature carries in its core the possibility of something that has no name and may never have a name and may not need one.

It is two millimeters long, or three, depending on when you measure, depending on whether you're measuring from inside or outside, depending on whether the pocket's soft geometry is in a generous phase or a tight one. It is the smallest citizen of the smallest country, and its patriotism is absolute: it has never conceived of anywhere else, it has never tasted a medium that was not *this* medium, it has never known a wall that was not *this* wall, and if the concept of elsewhere were somehow introduced into the pocket's still-congealing rule set, the creature would taste it with its cilia and find it meaningless, a gradient of nothing, a direction that leads nowhere because there is nowhere to lead to, because the pocket is the world and the world is the pocket and the creature is the warm spot on the wall and the warm spot on the wall is the creature, and this has always been true, and will be true for as long as the knot holds, which is—

Which is—

The root tightens. The water presses. The medium thickens with its unnameable quality. The lattice tries a seventh side and fails beautifully. The creature's cilia wave, tasting everything, responding to nothing, participating in the slow becoming of a place that is learning, atom by atom, rule by rule, pulse by pulse, to be real.

It is the thing that lives here. It is the only thing that has ever lived here. When the pocket finally finishes becoming — if it finishes, if *finishing* is among the rules it ratifies — the creature will be what it was always going to be: not a citizen, not an inhabitant, not a colonist or a parasite or a guest. A *feature*. A part of the landscape. The warm spot on the wall that the world grew around, that the world needed in order to know that it was a world, that there was an inside to be inside of, that the darkness varied for a reason even if the reason was only this: that something small and definite clung to the wall and tasted the dark and found it, in its own way, in its own unnameable and untranslatable and completely sufficient way, *enough*.
