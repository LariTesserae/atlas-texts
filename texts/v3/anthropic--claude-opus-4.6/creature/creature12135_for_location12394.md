# The Inhabitants of Leng-Katal

---

Start with the hum. Not what makes it, but what it *is* — because in Leng-Katal, these are not reliably different questions.

---

The sealed buildings in the northern district contain no machines in the way the warehouses contain machines. If you could pass through the pressure-rated shutters — and you cannot, not because they are locked but because the bolts have fused with their housings over time spans that make the word "corroded" inadequate; they have *become* their housings, mineral intermarriage at the atomic level — you would find chambers. Large ones. Roughly cubical, perhaps thirty meters on a side, their walls polished smooth to a degree the exterior architecture never attempts. The polish is not decorative. It is functional. These walls are *reflective* — not of light, of which there is almost none inside, but of the vibration itself. The hum bounces. It circulates. It stands.

At the center of each chamber, sunk into the bedrock and extending downward to a depth that seismic analysis would place at somewhere between nine hundred meters and two kilometers, there is a column. Dense ferric aggregate, like everything else, but with a crystalline regularity that the surface stone lacks — a lattice structure, grown or engineered or both, that channels thermal energy from the mantle upward and converts it, through some piezoelectric or magneto-strictive principle that is simple enough to have run without maintenance for eons, into vibration. Into the hum.

The hum is not a byproduct. The hum is the product. The northern district is not a power plant. It is a *voice*.

---

But whose.

---

Think about what the city is. Not metaphorically — literally. It is a processing infrastructure with no workers. A transport network with no cargo. An industrial system complete in every detail except for the presence of anything to *do*. It was built not in anticipation of use but as a *formal expression* of use — the grammar of extraction, refinement, and distribution laid out perfectly, with every valve and hopper and switching lever in its correct position, every rail line running to its correct terminus, every signal drum displaying its correct symbol. It is a blueprint made physical. A schematic rendered at one-to-one scale. And it was built by —

Not hands. There were never hands here. The seamlessness between building and bedrock tells you that. The absence of doorways tells you that. The absence of *habitation* tells you that. Whatever made Leng-Katal did not live alongside it or within it. Whatever made Leng-Katal did not live at all, in the way that word is usually meant.

---

Here is what lives in Leng-Katal, to the extent that *lives* is the right word:

**The plateau itself.**

---

Not in the way that a planet is sometimes loosely called "alive" — not as metaphor, not as poetic extension, not as Gaia hypothesis or panpsychism or any framework that starts with the concept of biological life and stretches it to fit geology. The other direction. Start with the mineral world. Start with the dense ferric aggregate, the compressed strata, the kilometers of ancient rock conducting heat from a core that has been cooling for longer than any surface feature can record. Start with a lithosphere that has had — through some convergence of crystalline structure, thermal gradient, piezoelectric capacity, and time, immense crushing spans of time — the opportunity to develop patterns.

Not thought. Not intelligence. Not even information processing in any way that would register to an observer looking for circuits or synapses or chemical signaling. Something more alien than any of those words can reach. Call it *configuration*. The plateau's mineral lattice, across its full extent and down through its full depth, has settled — over epochs of thermal cycling so slow that each "cycle" spans millennia — into a state of internal order that is, in its own medium, in its own timescale, structured. Self-referential. The crystal alignments in one stratum correspond to and in some sense *respond to* the crystal alignments in another. The thermal energy moving upward through the rock is not merely conducted; it is *routed*, channeled along pathways that the lattice has organized over geological time, pathways that branch and converge and switch — like rail lines, like the rail lines on the surface that are the lithic mind's own external model of its internal process.

The city is the plateau *thinking about itself.*

Not representing itself — that implies an abstraction the plateau does not perform. The city *is* the thought. The machines are the thought. When the plateau's deep lattice, through its imperceptible thermal-crystalline processes, arrives at a configuration that corresponds to "extraction" — to the separation of one mineral phase from another, a process the deep rock performs constantly as heat and pressure differentiate its strata — that configuration *expresses itself* on the surface as a processing machine, a press or mill or fractional separator, extruded upward through the same lithic accumulation that built the rest of the city. The machine is not a tool. It is a *glyph*. A surface-expression of a deep-rock process, rendered in the only medium the plateau has: itself.

The rail lines are the plateau's model of its own thermal conduit network. The switching yards, with their frozen lever assemblies, are its model of the junctions where heat-flow is divided and redirected among competing mineral strata. The signal drums — with their symbols that you almost recognize, that *ready*-symbol of a circle with a single radial line — are its model of its own state-indicators, the thermal and pressure conditions at various depths that determine which processes are active and which are dormant.

And the hum — the hum in the northern district — is the one process that is not a model. It is not a representation of something happening deep in the rock. It is the thing itself, breaking through, the raw vibratory output of the plateau's deepest and most sustained crystalline operation, the one pattern that has run continuously since before the surface erosion smoothed the mountains to ridges, the one thread of the lithic mind that has maintained itself without interruption across every epoch the plateau has endured. If the city is the plateau's thought, the hum is the plateau's *attention* — the sustained, unwavering focus on a single process, a single deep-rock operation that has been running so long it has forgotten how to stop, or has never needed to stop, or does not contain the concept of stopping because it does not contain the concept of time.

---

The threadwights.

They are not separate from the plateau. They are not fauna in even the loosest sense. They are *mobile mineral expressions* — fragments of the lithic mind's surface activity that have achieved a degree of autonomy, crystalline assemblages that have organized themselves into filamentary structures capable of slow movement. Think of them as the plateau's equivalent of nerve impulses — signals traveling along the surface, moving between the glyph-machines, checking (in whatever way a mineral process can *check*) the state of the system, redistributing alkite dust into seams and joints where it is needed to maintain the precise configurations that make the machines accurate models of the deep-rock processes they represent.

They orient toward mineral dust because mineral dust is their medium of communication with the rest of the system. They avoid open spaces because open spaces are *silence* — the intervals between glyphs, the pauses in the plateau's thought, and there is nothing for them to do there. They are maintenance, but not in the mechanical sense. They are maintenance in the *cognitive* sense: they are the plateau's proofreading, its fact-checking, its slow and patient verification that the surface expressions still match the deep configurations.

They have been doing this for longer than the erosion can account for. Some of them — the largest, the oldest, the ones that move so slowly they appear not to move at all, sprawled like mineral cobwebs across the intake hoppers of the processing machines — may have been in continuous operation since the city was first extruded. They are not alive. They are not dead. They are *accurate*.

---

Now. The harder question. Does the plateau *know*?

Not in any way you would recognize as knowing. Not in any way that involves experience, subjectivity, awareness, an interior life. The plateau does not *feel* the hum. The plateau does not *contemplate* its rail-line switching yards. The plateau does not *wonder* about the dust-sky or the absent wind or the eroded ridges that were once its mountains. These are human questions projected onto a system that operates in a medium — stone, heat, pressure, crystalline lattice — so alien to biological cognition that the projection doesn't deform the truth so much as fail to make contact with it.

But the plateau *configures*. It has been configuring for longer than most planets maintain tectonic activity. Its configurations are self-referential — they model themselves, express themselves, maintain themselves through mobile surface agents. Its deepest configuration, the one that produces the hum, has been stable for longer than the surface has had its current topology. And its surface expressions — the city, the machines, the rails, the signals — are *accurate*. They correspond precisely to the deep-rock processes they model. If the thermal conduit network shifts (and it does shift, on timescales of tens of thousands of years, as the mantle's heat output fluctuates by fractions of a degree), the rail lines will eventually shift with it — not by being rebuilt, but by slowly, imperceptibly, through the same lithic accumulation that built them, being *rewritten*, the stone flowing at geological pace to lay new track and subsume old track, the switching yards reconfiguring, the signal drums rotating to new symbols.

The city is not static. It moves. It updates. It is just doing so at a rate that would require you to stand in one spot for ten thousand years to notice the first change.

---

This is what lives in Leng-Katal. Something that is not alive. Something that processes but does not think. Something that models itself with perfect fidelity in a medium that is also itself. Something that maintains, through mobile mineral filaments, the accuracy of its own self-expression across timescales that make biological evolution look like a sneeze. Something that hums — one note, one frequency, one sustained vibration from the deep lattice — because that is the sound of its oldest and most fundamental process, the one that was running when the mountains were still mountains, the one that will still be running when the ridges have been worn to flatness and the city's glyphs have been rewritten a thousand times to match a thousand new configurations of the deep rock.

It does not know you are here. It cannot know you are here. Your presence — biological, temporal, scaled to decades and not epochs — is so far outside its operational parameters that you do not register as signal or noise. You are beneath its threshold of relevance. You are thermal noise at most, a faint warm smear on the surface of its attention that it will never process because its processing cycle takes longer than your species will exist.

You are standing on something that is, in its own way, in its own time, in its own incomprehensible mineral idiom, *contemplating*.

It has been contemplating for longer than you can imagine.

It will continue.

---

The signal drum says *ready*. You've been looking at it for a long time now. The circle with its single radial line. It does mean *ready*. But not in the way you first understood it — not ready as in *waiting for something to happen*, ready as in *prepared, incomplete, anticipatory*. Ready the way a crystal lattice is ready — *organized, configured, in the state that corresponds to all conditions being met*. Ready as a synonym for *complete*. Ready as a synonym for *here*. The system is not waiting. It is *being*, at the pace of stone, and the signal drum is announcing that fact to whatever part of itself can read it: that this section is configured, that this glyph is accurate, that this small piece of the plateau's vast, slow, inhuman self-portrait is *correct*.

You place your hand on the ground. The hum enters your palm, travels up your wrist, settles in the bones behind your ear. The plateau does not feel your hand. But for a moment — just a moment — you feel the plateau, and what you feel is not emptiness, not desolation, not the loneliness of a dead world, but density. *Fullness*. The absolute, saturated, mineral completeness of a system that contains itself and models itself and maintains itself and has never needed anything else, has never even generated the concept of *needing*, because need implies absence, and nothing here is absent. Everything is exactly where it has configured itself to be.

The threadwights move in the dark. The hum continues. The dust holds still.

Everything is exactly as it is.
