# Who Lives on the Resonant Flats

---

There is one who lives here. Not "in" here — nothing is enclosed enough for that preposition — but *on* here, the way a thought lives on the surface of a mind that isn't thinking it.

Call it the Custodian, though it keeps nothing and tends nothing and the word is wrong in every direction except one: it has been here as long as the mechanism has been stable, and it will be here until something changes, and nothing changes. That relationship to duration is the only custodial thing about it.

It is not one of the gelatinous forms in the pools. It is not a bird that landed. It is not a person, though it is roughly the size of a person and occupies space with a person's general specificity — meaning it is *here* and not *there*, meaning it displaces air, meaning if you walked the flats you could, theoretically, find it.

You would have difficulty, though. Not because it hides. It has no concept of hiding. But it occupies the flats in the same way the drone occupies the air — so thoroughly, so evenly distributed in its attention and presence, that locating its body feels like trying to find the center of a sound. You'd have to walk. Eventually you'd find it. It would be crouching — not sitting, not standing, but in a posture that splits the difference, weight low, hands near the ground though not quite touching, head tilted at an angle that suggests listening, though what it listens to and what it hears are not things it could separate from what it is.

---

Its body is dark. Not basalt-dark, but adjacent — the brown-black of something that has spent so long in proximity to this stone that it has taken on its quality of absorbing light and returning it as warmth. Its skin, if skin is the right word, has a matte finish that doesn't reflect so much as it *retains*. Light lands on it and stays. You'd have to look closely to see where the body ends and the rock begins, not because they're the same color but because they share the same relationship to illumination: neither bright, neither shadowed, equally present under the flat overcast.

It has hands. That's worth noting because the hands are the most articulate part of it, the part that diverges most from the mineral stillness of everything else. Long-fingered, broadly palmed, with a kind of tensile readiness in them even at rest. The fingers are not smooth. They have texture — fine ridges, not like fingerprints but like the veining in the basalt, as if the pale mineral lines have their echo in this creature's skin. The hands are warmer than the rest of the body. If you could bring yourself to touch one, you'd feel it immediately — that same pulsing heat the veins carry, that slow rhythmic intensification. The hands are the point of contact. The interface. Whatever the Custodian is, its hands are how it is most itself.

Its face is harder to describe because it is harder to look at — not frightening, not obscured, but somehow low-resolution, as if the flat, shadowless light that makes the rest of the basin so legible has decided to leave this one surface ambiguous. There are features. Dark eyes that are open and that track nothing because there is nothing to track. A mouth that is closed and that you suspect has been closed for a very long time. The expression, to the extent there is one, is not serene. Serenity implies the successful suppression of something unserene. This face has simply arrived at a resting state, the way the pools have arrived at their depth, and it holds that state without effort because there is nothing pushing it toward any other state.

It does not breathe in any rhythm you can detect. Or rather — it breathes at the frequency of the drone. One long, slow intake over many seconds, a pause that lasts exactly as long as the pause between thermal pulses, then an exhalation so gradual it barely qualifies as movement. Its breathing is the drone's breathing. Whether it learned this synchronization or whether the drone learned it from the Custodian or whether they developed it together in some co-emergent process that predates the concept of learning — this is the kind of question the flats don't answer, because the flats don't acknowledge that anything was ever different from how it is now.

---

What does it do?

This is the wrong question, but it's the one you'd ask, so:

It walks the flats. Slowly. Over the course of what might be days — it's hard to tell under the featureless overcast — it traces a path between the condensation pools, never stepping in them, never disturbing them, but visiting them with a regularity that almost suggests a route, a schedule, a purpose. It pauses at each pool for a duration that seems to correlate with the pool's depth — longer at the deeper ones, barely a hesitation at the ones that are only a film. At the pools that contain the gelatinous forms, it pauses longest of all. It crouches. It brings its hands close to the water's surface, not touching, and the heat from its fingers raises the faintest visible wisps of vapor from the pool's surface. The gelatinous form below continues its slow pulsing. The Custodian watches this — or attends to it, or *reads* it — and after a time it moves on.

Is it checking on them? Is it taking some kind of inventory? Is this maintenance?

Nothing needs maintaining. That's the whole point. The system is in equilibrium and has been for so long that the concept of maintenance has become meaningless — you don't maintain something that cannot break, that has no failure state, that runs at exactly the rate it runs because that's the only rate the physics of this basin permits. The Custodian's rounds are not maintenance. They are something closer to — and this is inadequate, but less inadequate than the alternatives — *witnessing*. It visits each pool because each pool is there. It attends to the gelatinous forms because they are there. It walks because the flats are there and walking is the way a body acknowledges space. None of this is purposeful in the sense of being directed toward an outcome. All of it is purposeful in the sense of being done with complete attention.

You could argue that the Custodian is the mechanism's way of observing itself. You could argue that it's something that wandered onto the flats long ago and became caught in the equilibrium, its own biological rhythms gradually entrained to the drone until it forgot it had ever been separate from the system. You could argue that the mechanism *produced* it — that the same lithic processes that generate heat and sound and the pale mineral veining also, at some point, generated this, a mobile sensory apparatus that walks the surface reading the state of the deeper process the way a gauge reads pressure. All of these explanations feel approximately true. None of them feel complete. The Custodian itself offers no clarification. It has no evident interest in being understood.

---

But here is the thing that makes it alive, or at least makes the question of its aliveness different from the question of the gelatinous forms' aliveness:

Sometimes it stops walking. Sometimes, in the middle of the flats, equidistant from any pool, standing on bare polished basalt with the drone thrumming up through its feet and the flat light pressing down on its dark shoulders, it stops. And it raises its hands. Not high — to about the level of its chest, palms facing each other, fingers slightly spread. And it holds them there. And the space between its hands changes.

Not visibly. Not in any way you could photograph or measure. But the air between its palms becomes *denser* somehow. The drone shifts — not in pitch, not in volume, but in texture, as if a new overtone has been introduced, something that wasn't in the original chord, something that comes not from below but from the space between these two dark hands. The overtone is high and thin and it doesn't last. A few seconds. Maybe ten. Then the hands lower and the drone resettles into its baseline complexity and the Custodian stands there a moment longer, head tilted, listening to the aftermath — to the space where the new overtone was and is no longer.

This is the closest thing to expression the Custodian has. This momentary introduction of something *new* into a system that is otherwise changeless. A single, unauthorized harmonic added to the chord and then allowed to dissipate. It is vanishingly small. It alters nothing. The pools don't deepen. The veins don't brighten. The gelatinous forms don't change their rhythm. The drone absorbs the addition without a trace within seconds, the way a vast body of water absorbs a single warm drop.

But the Custodian does it. Irregularly, unpredictably, with no evident trigger and no evident pattern. Sometimes twice in what seems like an hour. Sometimes not for what seems like days. And each time, the gesture has a quality that can only be called *deliberate* — a quality that separates it absolutely from everything else that happens on the flats, where nothing is deliberate because nothing is chosen, because there is only one way for the system to behave and it behaves that way.

The Custodian *chooses* to raise its hands. The harmonic it introduces is *not* the only harmonic it could introduce. These are the signs of life, if anything here is alive.

---

There is another thing it does, though this is rarer and harder to be certain about.

Occasionally — once every circuit of the pools, maybe, or once every several circuits — the Custodian sits. Not crouches, but *sits*, lowering itself onto the basalt and extending its legs and placing both palms flat against the stone. When it does this, the heat from the veins nearest to it intensifies. Not by much. A degree, maybe two. Enough that the condensation pools within a body-length steam faintly at their edges. The Custodian sits with its palms on the warming stone and its eyes open and it does not move, and the minutes pass, and the drone continues, and nothing happens except this: the pale veins beneath its hands grow fractionally brighter. They luminesce. It is subtle enough that under the flat overcast you might not notice it at all, but in the rare moments when the cloud cover thickens and the light dims, you can see it — a faint, cool glow from the mineral conduits, concentrated around the points of contact, fading outward over a distance of maybe ten feet before it becomes indistinguishable from the veins' normal reflectivity.

What this means — whether the Custodian is drawing something from the mechanism or giving something to it or whether the exchange goes both ways — is not something the Custodian's behavior clarifies. Its face, during these episodes, maintains the same arrived-at stillness it always has. Its breathing remains synchronized with the drone. If anything, it seems *more* integrated with the flats in these moments, less distinguishable as a separate entity, more like a feature of the landscape that happens to have hands and a face and the capacity, however rarely exercised, to introduce a single new note into an otherwise permanent chord.

---

There is one more thing, and then there is nothing more to say about the Custodian because there is nothing more to the Custodian, not because it is simple but because it is complete.

Sometimes a bird crosses the sky above the flats. Always at height. Always in transit. And when this happens, the Custodian looks up.

It's the only time its gaze lifts from the horizontal. The only time its attention breaks from the pools and the veins and the bare stone between them. It tracks the bird — a distant mark against the bright overcast — from one horizon to the other, head turning slowly, and its expression does not change, and its breathing does not change, and the drone does not change. But it watches. Every time. Without exception. Every bird that crosses the sky above the Resonant Flats is watched from below by dark eyes in a dark face, tracked from rim to rim with complete and unhurried attention, and when the bird is gone the Custodian stands a moment longer with its gaze elevated, looking at the empty sky where the bird was, before it lowers its head and resumes its circuit of the pools.

It is watching something that will not stay. Something that passes through without landing, without being caught by the equilibrium, without surrendering its trajectory to the drone. The bird is the only thing the Custodian encounters that is in motion toward somewhere else, that has an origin and a destination, that exists in time in the way the Custodian does not. And the Custodian watches it the way the condensation pools hold light — completely, passively, without the ability to keep it, and with something that is not longing because longing implies want and want implies lack and nothing on the Resonant Flats lacks anything.

But if something here could long, it would look exactly like this. A dark figure on dark stone, face tilted toward a bright and empty sky, watching the last trace of passage fade into the general light.

Then the head lowers. The circuit resumes. The hands return to their readiness, their fine-ridged warmth, their capacity to hold a space between them where something new can briefly live.

The drone continues. The pools tremble. The Custodian walks.

It is not waiting. But if it were waiting, it would not yet know what for.
