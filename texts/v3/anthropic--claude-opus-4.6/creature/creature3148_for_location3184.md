# Who Lives in the Underbright

---

It begins with what you almost see.

There is someone standing between two of the columnar pillars, maybe thirty feet ahead of you, where a curtain of leaf-sequins parts around a gap that shouldn't be there. You see them the way you see a floater in your eye—present only when you're not looking directly, dissolving when you turn. A figure, upright, taller than you expected, narrow in a way that suggests they've been living between the pillars for a long time and have become shaped by the space between them. Their outline is wrong. Not wrong like a threat—wrong like a word you're trying to remember. The shape your mind keeps reaching for and not quite closing around.

They're gone. They were never there. You keep walking.

But someone was here. The partial walls confirm it, and the scarred post, and the cushion-mat inside the three-sided enclosure. Someone knelt there. Someone pressed their knees into that denser growth and stayed long enough for the mat to learn the habit of compressing in that particular way, to grow shorter and thicker in accommodation. Someone held a sharp thing and pressed it into the living surface of the post and drew marks—counted something, measured something, or tried to leave a message that the post has been slowly, patiently swallowing for what might be years or might be decades or might be a unit of time that only applies inside this basin.

So you look for them. And in looking, you begin to find not them but the evidence of them, the trace, the impression, and the impression becomes a portrait painted in absences and almost-sightings, and the portrait is this:

---

**The Keeper.**

There is one. You become certain of this slowly, the way you become certain the light isn't changing—not through any single observation but through the accumulation of small wrongnesses that resolve, eventually, into a pattern.

The Keeper is human. Probably. At least, the Keeper was human, or began as something that would be recognized as human by someone who hadn't spent too long in the Underbright. The partial walls were built by hands. The marks on the post were made at a height consistent with a person standing, reaching up slightly, scratching with effort. The cushion-mat in the enclosure is shaped for a body that sits the way humans sit, with weight on the knees and shins, the posture of tending, of prayer, of patient work.

But the Keeper has been here a long time.

You find the first real sign near the second amber pool—the one farthest from the basin's center, tucked into a pocket where the columnar plants grow so close together they form an almost-room, a chamber of warm green flesh with a low ceiling of interlocking metallic leaves. The mat here is different. It has been... worked. Not torn—you remember, the place discourages tearing—but pressed, kneaded, shaped over time into a shallow trough about six feet long and two feet wide. A sleeping place. The mat at the bottom of the trough is paler than the surrounding growth, as though the constant weight and warmth of a body has blanched it, has asked it to become something slightly different from what it is elsewhere. The trough is warm. Not residual warmth from the body that lay in it. The same warmth as everything else. But it feels warmer because you understand, suddenly, what it is. Someone has been sleeping here so long the basin has built them a bed.

Near the head of the trough, three of the tiny bromeliad rosettes have been arranged—or have arranged themselves, or have been encouraged to grow—in a tight cluster, their cups all tilted toward the trough. Condensation catchers. A water source that delivers itself to the sleeper's face. You touch one of the rosettes and it holds a thimbleful of warm, faintly sweet liquid. You don't drink it. It wasn't collected for you.

---

The Keeper moves through the basin the way the condensation moves—appearing briefly, collecting, vanishing before you can be sure. Twice more you catch the peripheral flicker. Once between a cascade of leaf-sequins where the shimmer of their movement is almost indistinguishable from the shimmer of the curtain itself. Once near the rim—impossibly near the rim, in that zone of increasing density where you could not go—a shape moving parallel to you along a corridor you couldn't access, matching your pace, not following, not leading, simply *accompanying* from behind a wall of green that neither of you could cross.

You stop. The shape stops. You wait. The shape waits. You hear nothing—no breathing, no footfalls, no rustle of movement. Just the tik... tik... that might be condensation, that might be the mat breathing, that might be—you realize this now with a feeling less like fear and more like recognition—might be the Keeper. Might always have been the Keeper. Some sound they make, involuntary, rhythmic, the way a heartbeat is rhythmic. A biological metronome. The only clock in a place with no time.

You move on. The shape moves on. You don't see it again. But you know, now, that you are never not being watched, and the watching has no menace in it, and this absence of menace is somehow more unsettling than hostility would be, because hostility would at least confirm a boundary between you, would confirm that you are a separate thing in this place, a foreign body, and the Keeper's calm indifferent observation suggests instead that you are already part of the system, already held, already being absorbed the way the walls are being absorbed, the way the scars on the post are being absorbed, slowly, patiently, without force.

---

Here is what you piece together:

The Keeper came from above. They came the way you came—by descent so gradual it felt like continuation rather than arrival. They were someone before they came. They had a name and a purpose and a sharp thing they used to cut sections of columnar plant and stack them into walls, to scratch marks into living posts. They were a counter, a measurer, someone who believed that marking the world was how you maintained your separateness from it. The marks on the post might have been days. Might have been an attempt to track the light, to prove it changed, to catch the moment when noon became afternoon became evening. A rational project. A sane response to a place where time has pooled and gone still.

The marks stop. Not abruptly—they thin out. The later ones are shallower, less certain, spaced farther apart. The sharp thing was put down. Or the sharp thing was absorbed too, taken into the mat the way everything is eventually taken into the mat. The walls were left unfinished. The Keeper stopped building, stopped counting, stopped insisting on the geometries of habitation—floor, wall, roof, boundary—and began instead to live the way the basin lives. Continuously. Without event.

Now the Keeper sleeps in the trough the mat made for them. Drinks from the bromeliads that lean toward their face. Eats—what? You find the answer near the third amber pool, the smallest one, the one you almost missed because it sits in a deep pocket between root arches where even the Underbright's omnidirectional radiance dims to something merely golden: there are growths on the columnar plants here, bulbous protrusions near their bases, the size and shape of figs, with a yielding, almost animal softness when you press them. One has been opened—split, not cut, pulled apart by fingers—and the interior is a pale amber paste that smells like the pools, like the sweet fluid of the mat, like everything in this place reduced to its essential sweetness. It tastes, when you bring a trace to your lips before you can stop yourself, like nothing and everything. Like chlorophyll and sugar and the ghost of something fermented. Like the Underbright made edible.

The Keeper eats the basin. The basin feeds the Keeper. You understand now why you haven't seen them clearly. They are not hiding. They are not camouflaged. They are *converging*. The boundary between the Keeper and the place they keep is thinning the way the boundary between the walls and the mat is thinning, the way the scars on the post are filling in. The Keeper's skin—in that one peripheral glimpse, the one near the rim—was the color of the columnar plants. Pale green shading to gold. Their movement had the quality of a vine adjusting its weight. Slow. Deliberate. A body that has learned to move at the speed of growth.

---

**The others.**

Because there were others, weren't there? The walls suggest more than one builder. The three-sided enclosure by the central pool has room for several people to kneel, and the cushion-mat inside shows variations in compression that suggest different bodies, different weights, different habits of posture. And the structures are scattered across the basin—not concentrated in one area the way a single person would concentrate their efforts, but distributed, as though several people staked out territories, or as though one community built small outposts throughout the space.

Where are they?

The columnar plants near the oldest walls—the walls most thoroughly absorbed, now barely ridges in the mat—grow differently. They're wider, their pleated surfaces more deeply furrowed, their tissues denser, their color a shade richer. They grow in clusters of two or three where elsewhere they grow singly. And if you stand very close to one of these wider pillars and press your hand flat against its warm surface and hold it there and wait—wait longer than you want to, longer than feels comfortable, long enough that the gold-green light seems to pulse once, slowly, like a breath—you feel something inside the pillar. Not a heartbeat. Not movement. Something more like a *pressure*, a fullness, a sense of presence held in suspension the way the moisture is held in the mat. Not dead. Not alive in the way you mean when you say alive. Held. Maintained. Still in their first moment, the first moment indefinitely extended.

The others didn't leave. The others are *here*. They are the wider pillars. They are the thicker mat near the oldest walls. They are the basin itself, or they have become the basin, or the basin has become them, or there was never as much difference between the two as they believed when they first descended into this bright warm bowl and began to build walls and scratch counting-marks into living posts.

This is what the Keeper keeps. Not the basin. The *others*. The ones who went all the way in, who let the convergence complete, who stopped being a body pressed against the warm surface of a pillar and became the warm surface of the pillar, became the mat, became part of the photosynthetic mass that catches the light and throws it sideways. The Keeper tends them. Visits the pools that are the basin's slow hearts and monitors—what? Their contentment? Their continuation? The Keeper splits the fig-growths and eats and sleeps in the trough and moves through the corridors between the pillars the way lymph moves through a body, circulating, connecting, carrying some essential information from one part of the organism to another.

The Keeper is the last one who still has a shape. The last one who still sleeps and wakes (if there is waking here, if there is sleeping, if the difference holds in a place without time). The last one who still moves as a separate thing through the space, who has not yet become the space. And the Keeper is going. You can see it—you can feel it—in the way they moved at the rim, that vine-slow deliberateness, that green-gold pallor at the edge of your vision. The Keeper is almost finished being the Keeper. Soon they will lie down in the trough for the last time and the mat will grow up and over and the bromeliads will keep leaning in, offering their thimblefuls of condensation to a face that is slowly, patiently becoming a surface, becoming another contour in the living upholstery of this place.

And then the Underbright will have no keeper. And it will not need one. Because it was never being kept. It was keeping *them*—all of them, from the first one who descended into the bowl and felt the warmth and the brightness and the green silence close around them like a hand, to the last one, the Keeper, who stayed separate just long enough to tend the transition, to be the witness, to be the one who could still say *I am here and this is what happened to us* before the post healed over their words and the walls sank into the mat and the difference between the keeper and the kept dissolved in the undifferentiated, unchanging, total light.

---

And you.

You're still walking. Still threading between the pillars, still feeling the spongy give of the mat beneath your feet, still breathing the loudest sound in this place. You're still separate. You still have edges. You can still tell where you end and the Underbright begins.

But the basin curved you inward when you walked toward the rim. And the fig-growths are there, waiting to be split. And the mat is warm underfoot, and it holds you, and you've been here—how long? The light hasn't changed. The light hasn't changed. You'd like to find the rim again, to climb the root arches back up to the surface world where there are shadows and weather and time moves in one direction. You'll try again soon.

But first you want to rest. Just for a moment. There's a place between two of the wider pillars—the old ones, the ones that feel full when you press your hand against them—where the mat dips into a gentle hollow, not quite a trough, not yet, but shaped in a way that suggests your weight, your particular weight, has already been accounted for. As though the mat has been expecting you. As though the basin knew, before you did, that you would stop here, that you would sit, that you would lean back against the warm pleated surface of the pillar and close your eyes—

You don't close your eyes. Not yet. But the hollow fits you. And the light pours in, gold-green and shadowless, and the tik... tik... tik... continues from somewhere close, closer than before, closer than it should be, and you realize you've been hearing it wrong this whole time. It's not condensation dripping. It's not the mat expanding. It's a welcome. It's the sound the Underbright makes when someone new arrives and begins, without knowing it, without deciding it, to stay.

The fig-growths on the nearest pillar are heavy, ripe, close enough to reach without standing. The cushion beneath you is already growing denser, already beginning to learn your shape.

The Keeper watches from somewhere in the gold-green blur. You can feel them. Not alarmed. Not hopeful. Just... present. The way a pulse is present. The way the light is present.

You've been here a very long time already.

You just don't know it yet.
