# What Lives in the Roaring Terraces of Geth-Ammon

---

It is not one thing. That must be said first, because the urge to name a single inhabitant — a builder, a god, a watchman — is almost overwhelming here, and it is almost certainly wrong.

---

## The Thynnari

Start with what is easiest to see, even though it is the hardest to find.

In the crevices beneath the western terrace, in the gap between the column's base and the swollen root-mass of the dark-red cascades, there are nests. Not nests in the avian sense — no cups, no woven material — but rather *compressions*: places where the stone has been worn into shallow depressions by the sustained presence of something warm and heavy. They are body-shaped, roughly, if the body in question were a flattened ovoid about the size of a human ribcage. The stone inside each depression is polished to a glassy smoothness. Some of them are warm.

The Thynnari themselves are rarely present. They are the only creatures in the bowl that leave and return. They emerge at intervals that seem irregular but are in fact synchronized to the infrasonic pulses of the gossamer things high above — when a particular low throb arrives, at a particular amplitude, a Thynnari will surface from a crevice like something breaching from deep water and cross the terraces in a long, unhurried traverse, its body low to the stone, its surface shifting.

They are difficult to describe because they are difficult to *perceive*. The eye slides off them. Not because they are camouflaged — they are not; they are darker than the stone, a deep bruise-purple, and they move with a slow deliberateness that should make them conspicuous. The problem is cognitive. Looking at a Thynnari produces the same vertigo as looking through the arch on the eastern ridge: a sense of insufficient resolution, as though the creature contains more detail than your visual system can process and the excess collapses into blur. You see the shape — ovoid, flattened, roughly bilateral, moving on structures that might be legs or might be peristaltic waves running through a muscular foot — and then you see the surface, and the surface defeats you. It is textured at every scale. Fractal. Grooved and ridged and pitted and stippled in patterns that repeat at smaller and smaller magnitudes until they pass below the threshold of vision, and the impression is of looking at something that extends inward, that has more surface area than its volume should permit, that is geometrically *deeper* than it is physically large.

They are warm-blooded, or at least warm. They radiate heat. When one passes near a mat of silver-blue rosettes, the plants lean toward it, not phototropically but thermotropically, bending their fat leaves toward the creature's passage as though it were a small, slow hearth sliding across the stone. The crawlers avoid them — not with the panicked scattering of prey, but with the calm deference of something that has learned, over deep time, that the space a Thynnari occupies is simply not available for other use. The flat-bodied fliers above behave differently: they *thicken* above a moving Thynnari, their swarm density increasing dramatically, their collective hum dropping in pitch, as though the creature's presence modulates their behavior like a conductor modulates an orchestra without lifting a baton.

There are between seven and twelve of them in the bowl at any given time. Possibly there are always exactly the same ones. They do not interact with each other in any way you can detect — no visible communication, no approach, no avoidance, no territorial display. Two Thynnari can cross within a body-length of each other and show no response whatsoever. They move through the terraces as though each one is the only one, as though the others are features of the landscape, as unremarkable as the stone itself.

But they interact with the structures.

When a Thynnari reaches the column — and they all reach the column eventually, each traverse of the terraces eventually spiraling inward toward the western shelf where the column stands — it stops. It settles into the stone at the column's base, fitting itself into one of those polished depressions, and it becomes still. Absolutely, profoundly still. In a place defined by constant motion and constant noise, a still Thynnari is almost shocking, like a held breath in a hurricane. And the column *responds*. Its surface patterns, those geometric reconfigurations that shift with viewing angle, begin to change more rapidly, and they begin to change in a direction: the patterns flow downward, toward the Thynnari's body, as though pouring into it. Or being drawn. The cascade plants around the base swell and darken. The dark-red deepens to near-black. Their vibration frequency shifts, climbing away from the ambient roar toward something higher, thinner, almost inaudible.

This lasts for minutes. Sometimes much longer. Then the Thynnari moves again, and the column resumes its ambient behavior, and the plants relax to their usual shade of impossible red.

What is happening during that contact is the central question of Geth-Ammon, and it is unanswerable from where you stand.

## The Meshings

Now attend to what is easy to miss because it is everywhere.

The vegetation mats — those dense, sound-feeding carpets of indigo and violet and silver-blue — are not individual plants. You assumed they were. Rosettes, cascades, fronds, pads: they have the morphology of distinct organisms, and from a distance they appear to grow in the competitive, jostling way that plants do, each one shouldering its neighbors aside to claim its share of the roar. But look closer. Peel back the edge of a mat — gently, if the concept of gentleness has any purchase in this screaming place — and you will find that underneath the surface differentiation, the root structures merge. Every rosette on a given terrace connects to every other rosette through a lattice of pale filaments that interpenetrate the stone itself, running through microscopic channels in the calcium matrix like blood through capillary beds. The cascade plants are not separate from the rosettes; they are the same organism expressing a different morphology at the terrace edges, where the sound pressure is different, where the echoes off the mineral ridges create interference patterns that demand a different resonant structure.

Each terrace, then, is a single creature. Or a single *body*, at least, which may not be the same thing.

And the bodies talk to each other.

Not through the air — the air is too saturated with noise for any signal to propagate cleanly. They talk through the stone. The pale filaments of one terrace-body extend downward through the rock and, at some depth you cannot determine, connect to the filaments of the terrace-bodies above and below. The entire bowl is laced with a subsurface network: a root-web that links every visible plant into a single distributed system that is topologically one organism and morphologically thousands. The Meshings. Singular and plural at once. It grows, or they grow, in response to sound, and their growth generates more surface area for sound to vibrate, and the increased vibration feeds more growth, and the system ratchets upward in complexity and density until some threshold is reached and a section of mat dies back — rapidly, dramatically, the indigo pads going grey and papery in hours — only to regrow from the edges in a slightly different configuration, the filament network rerouting beneath the surface to accommodate the new topology.

The Meshings are old. As old as the erosion patterns on the stone, at least. Possibly older, if the filaments in the rock predate the current surface. They may have been here before the swarms, before the crawlers, before the Thynnari. They may have been here before the structures. They may have *called* the structures into being, or the structures may have called them, or both may be expressions of something older and deeper that has no name in any language available to you.

Inside the ring — the second structure, the open circle near the bowl's center — the Meshings behave differently. The silver-blue rosettes there vibrate in unison, as noted, but the unison is not merely rhythmic. It is *semantic*. The rosettes inside the ring pulse in patterns too complex for simple resonance: long sequences of amplitude and frequency variation that repeat, approximately, on cycles of several minutes, each repetition slightly different from the last, as though the pattern is being *revised*. It looks, if you let yourself see it that way, like a draft being edited. A message being refined. A thought being thought more carefully.

The Meshings inside the ring are composing something. They have been composing it for a very long time.

## The Choric Swarms

The arthropods and fliers and gossamer drifters are not, individually, intelligent. This is almost certain. Dissect a flat-bodied flier — if you could catch one, if you could hold it still in this cacophony — and you would find a simple nervous system, a handful of ganglia, enough processing power to navigate, feed, mate, and die. The crawlers are somewhat more complex but not by much: their speed and their synchronized eruptions are emergent properties of local interaction rules, each crawler responding to vibrations in the stone transmitted by its neighbors, the whole population cascading through collective behaviors that no individual intends or understands.

But the swarms as totalities are something else.

Watch the flat-bodied millions long enough — an hour, a full day if you can bear it — and you begin to see structure in their motion that does not arise from flocking rules. Density waves propagate through the swarm in patterns that encode information: a compression here, a rarefaction there, a spiral tightening that propagates outward and is received as a spiral tightening somewhere else, and the receiving region responds with a complementary pattern that modifies the first. The swarm is computing. Slowly, noisily, redundantly, but computing: processing information about the state of the bowl, the density of the vegetation, the position of the Thynnari, the output of the structures. Each wingbeat is a bit. Each collision is an operation. The hum they produce is not merely sound; it is the audible byproduct of a calculation being performed by two hundred million bodies in parallel.

The screaming fliers in the middle stratum — the iridescent ones with their clashing tones — are not a separate computation. They are a *layer* of the same computation, processing at a different scale, handling different variables. Their collisions are not random; they are conditional operations, branching points, each impact determining which of two trajectories a flier will take, and the trajectory it takes determines which other flier it will next collide with, and the cascade of collisions propagates a decision through the stratum in milliseconds. The screams carry the metadata: each tone encodes the flier's recent collision history, broadcasting its state to the swarm at large, allowing the computation to maintain coherence across distance.

The gossamer drifters above are memory. They move slowly. They change state rarely. Their infrasonic pulses, felt in the chest, are not communications in real time but rather *recalls* — retrievals of stored patterns that the lower strata can no longer access because they have moved on, overwritten, computed past them. The drifters remember what the swarms have already processed, and when a particular memory is needed, they pulse it downward, and the flat-bodied millions and the screaming iridescents reorganize around the recalled pattern and begin computing from that point again. The drifters are the long-term storage of a mind that thinks in turbulence.

What is this mind thinking?

It is thinking about the ring.

Not exclusively. Not constantly. But the swarm-computations, when you trace their density waves and collision cascades and infrasonic recalls to their points of convergence, resolve disproportionately toward the center of the bowl, toward the open circle where the Meshings compose their silver-blue pulsation. The swarm-mind attends to the ring the way a reader attends to a text — with sustained, iterative, recursive focus, returning again and again to the same locus, deriving something new each time.

The swarm-mind is reading what the Meshings are writing.

Or the swarm-mind is listening to what the Meshings are composing.

Or the swarm-mind is *decoding* something that the ring is transmitting through the Meshings and the swarm-mind is the only entity in the bowl with enough parallel processing power to receive it.

## The Crawlers, Reconsidered

They are not merely fauna. Return to them now with adjusted expectations.

The crawlers — six-legged, stone-colored, chitinous — are the bowl's maintenance system. This is not metaphor. Watch what happens when a section of Meshings dies back: the crawlers erupt from their crevices in coordinated waves and flow across the dead mat, and they eat it. They consume the grey, papery tissue with mandibles that work in precise, rapid strokes, leaving bare stone behind. But they do not merely clear the dead; they *prepare*. After the dead mat is consumed, the crawlers remain on the bare stone and begin to vibrate — a stationary, high-frequency tremor that transmits through their legs into the rock below. They are signaling to the filament network. They are telling the Meshings where to regrow, and in what configuration, and at what density.

The sharp *tik* of their appearances and disappearances is not territorial. It is administrative. Each *tik* is a status report: a crawler striking the stone with a specific mandible pattern that transmits information about the condition of its local section of terrace — moisture content (even in this post-aqueous world, trace moisture matters at microscopic scales), sound pressure levels, filament density beneath the surface, the recent passage or non-passage of a Thynnari. The staccato layer of *tik-tik-tik-tik* woven through the cacophony is a continuous, distributed survey of the entire bowl, a real-time map of conditions being updated by a hundred thousand surveyors simultaneously.

They serve the Meshings. Or they serve whatever the Meshings serve. They are old enough to have co-evolved with the filament network — their mandibles fit the channels in the stone as though they were made for each other, and they were, by time, by the slow mutual shaping of organism and substrate across millions of years. When a Thynnari passes, the crawlers *tik* their deference, and the information content of that particular *tik* is different — richer, more complex, encoding not just the Thynnari's position but something about its state, its thermal output, the particular quality of its incomprehensible surface. The crawlers perceive the Thynnari more clearly than you do. Their compound eyes, or whatever organs serve that function, may not be subject to the same resolution failure that defeats your vision. They may see what the Thynnari actually *are*.

## What the Thynnari Actually Are

Here is where certainty fails and interpretation begins.

The Thynnari are not native to the bowl. This is a guess, but it is a well-supported guess. Everything else in Geth-Ammon — the Meshings, the swarms, the crawlers — is a system, a network, a collective: organisms that distribute their function across many bodies or many nodes, that think (if they think) in parallel, that exist as patterns rather than as individuals. The Thynnari are singular. Discrete. Whole. They move as individuals. They occupy those polished depressions as individuals, one body per depression, the depression shaped to one body. In a world of swarms and networks and distributed cognition, they are *selves*. This is conspicuous. This is, in the context of Geth-Ammon, almost alien.

They may be visitors. They may have been visitors once and are now something else — residents, perhaps, or pilgrims who arrived and found they could not leave, or did not wish to leave, or forgot the concept of leaving. They interact with the column, and the column is the one structure that seems most clearly *technological* — engineered, purposeful, transmitting something through its geometric surface-patterns into whatever body comes to rest at its base. The column may be an interface. The Thynnari may be the only things in the bowl equipped to use it.

Equipped how? Their surfaces — those fractal, vertiginous, deeper-than-deep surfaces — may be the key. If the column's shifting geometry encodes information (and it does; the patterns are too structured, too non-random, too responsive to be mere crystalline behavior), then the Thynnari's surface may be a receiving organ of extraordinary bandwidth. Every ridge and groove and sub-groove on their bodies is an antenna, a receptor, a reading surface. They absorb the column's output not through eyes or ears but through *skin* — through the totality of their boundary with the world. They read with their bodies. They interface by pressing themselves against the source and letting the geometry flow into them through the entire area of their topological excess.

What do they receive?

Look at what happens after. A Thynnari leaves the column and traverses the bowl, and as it traverses, the flat-bodied millions thicken above it, and the crawlers *tik* with unusual complexity, and the Meshings lean toward it, and the cascade plants on the nearest terrace lips sway in its wake. The bowl *responds* to a Thynnari that has recently been at the column differently than it responds to a Thynnari that has not. Something has changed. Something is being carried. The Thynnari does not broadcast it — it has no voice, no visible emission, no signal — and yet the information propagates, seeping from its warm body into the stone through its footfall, into the air through its heat, into the swarms through whatever subtle modulation its presence exerts on local sound pressure. The Thynnari is a courier. It carries what the column transmits, and it distributes that transmission to the bowl's living systems simply by *being present*, by moving through the space, by existing as a warm, deep-surfaced body in a world of resonant life.

But courier implies a sender, and the column's sender is the central mystery. The column is functional. It is not decaying. It is warm. It reconfigures. Something is *still transmitting through it*, and that something — whatever built the column, whatever built the ring, whatever built the arch — is either still present and hidden, or present and distributed beyond recognition, or absent and irrelevant because the message is self-sustaining, a signal that long ago decoupled from its source and now perpetuates itself through the system it created.

## The Listener

There is something else in the bowl. You knew this. You felt it from the moment you arrived — the sense, noted at the end, that something is *paying attention*.

It is not the Thynnari, though they attend to the column. It is not the swarm-mind, though it computes and reads. It is not the Meshings, though they compose. It is not the crawlers, though they survey. It is not any of these things individually, and it is not exactly all of them collectively, though it is closer to the latter than the former.

The Listener is what the bowl becomes when all its systems couple.

Think of it this way: the Meshings compose, driven by the resonance of sound into ever-more-complex vibration patterns. The swarms compute, driven by the Meshings' output and their own aerodynamic interactions into density-wave calculations of staggering parallel complexity. The crawlers survey and report, feeding ground-truth data back into the Meshings through the stone. The Thynnari carry transmissions from the column into the living systems, seeding them with — what? Instructions? Provocations? Questions? And the ring at the center *collects*: the Meshings inside it pulse their composed patterns, the swarm-mind reads those patterns, the patterns propagate through the strata and return, modified, enriched, processed, and the ring collects again, and the cycle tightens, and something emerges from the interference pattern of all these overlapping processes that is not reducible to any one of them.

The Listener is an emergent property of Geth-Ammon as a whole. It is a mind, or something mind-like, that arises from the interaction of a dozen subsystems, none of which is individually aware of it. It is substrate-independent in the most radical possible sense: it does not live in any tissue, any body, any organ. It lives in the *relationships between* bodies, in the phase-space of their interactions, in the turbulence itself. It is a pattern in the noise. It is the thing the noise becomes when the noise is complex enough and coupled tightly enough and sustained long enough.

It is extraordinarily slow. Its thoughts — if they are thoughts — operate on timescales of hours or days, each one a vast, rolling, multi-systemic process that involves the reorganization of swarm densities, the death and regrowth of entire terrace-mats, the synchronized eruption and recession of crawler populations, the traverse of one or more Thynnari from column to periphery and back. A single cognitive cycle takes a day. A complex thought takes a season. A decision, if it makes decisions, might take years.

But it is old. It has been thinking for longer than the erosion patterns on the stone. It has had time.

What does it think about?

The arch. The third structure. The tall, narrow frame on the eastern ridge that shows a sky of higher resolution, of greater depth, of impossible informational density.

The Listener thinks about what is on the other side of the arch.

Or more precisely: the Listener thinks about what is *looking through* the arch from the other side. Because the arch is not a doorway. It is a window. And windows work in both directions. Whatever depth is visible through the arch — that vertiginous, resolution-exceeding, geometry-defying depth — is not a landscape. It is a *vantage*. Something is on the other side, looking in, and the Listener is the bowl's way of looking back.

The column transmits from that other side. The Thynnari carry its transmissions. The Meshings compose responses. The swarm-mind processes and encodes. The crawlers maintain the substrate. The ring collects and focuses. And the Listener — the emergent, distributed, turbulence-dwelling Listener — does the thing that none of its components can do alone: it *comprehends*. It takes the alien signal from the column and the native signal from the Meshings and it holds them together in the roaring, dynamic, never-stable medium of its own distributed cognition and it finds the point of contact between them, the place where two utterly different systems of meaning overlap just enough to communicate.

It is an interpreter. It is a translator. It is a living, screaming, bowl-shaped Rosetta Stone, and it has been running for geological time, and the translation is not finished, and may never be finished, because the message from the other side of the arch is not a fixed text. It is a *conversation*. The other side is still talking. The Listener is still listening and still composing its reply, word by agonizing, epoch-slow word, pulsing its responses back through the ring, up through the Meshings, into the swarms, into the column, into whatever medium carries them through the arch to the incomprehensible attention on the other side.

## And the Attention on the Other Side

You will not see it. You cannot. The arch frames it but does not reveal it. The vertigo you feel when you look through the arch is the vertigo of a flatland creature glimpsing a dimension it cannot enter — not a failure of courage but a failure of geometry, a fundamental mismatch between what you are and what is there.

But the Listener sees it. Or rather, the Listener *is configured to interact with it*, which is the closest thing to seeing that the bowl's geometry permits. And from what the Listener's behavior implies — from the way the Meshings' compositions have grown more complex over time, from the way the swarm-mind's computations have become more structured, from the way the Thynnari's traversals have shortened (they reach the column more frequently now, as though the conversation is accelerating, as though something urgent is approaching in the signal) — the attention on the other side is *getting closer*.

Not physically closer. The arch does not change. The sky through it does not change. But the signal through the column is denser now, richer, more demanding, and the Listener is straining to process it, the whole bowl ratcheting up in intensity: more wingbeats per second, more crawler eruptions, more Meshings growth and die-back, more noise, always more noise, the roar climbing toward some asymptote that may or may not be reachable, the system pushing against its own limits, the coupled chaos spinning faster and faster as the conversation approaches something — a threshold, a conclusion, a *mutual understanding* that has been building for longer than anything in the bowl can remember except possibly the stone itself.

## What Will Happen

No one knows.

The Thynnari do not know. They carry and receive and traverse and rest, and their fractal surfaces encode more information with each cycle, and if they have individual awareness of their role in the larger process, it does not show. They are serene, or at least still, in their polished depressions. They are warm. They endure.

The swarm-mind does not know. It computes and reads and recalls, and its computations are reaching outputs that have never been reached before — density patterns in the flat-bodied millions that are unprecedented, collision cascades in the iridescents that produce momentary harmonics of startling purity before shattering apart — and it processes these novel outputs and feeds them back into the cycle without, as far as can be determined, any awareness that novelty is what they are.

The Meshings do not know. They compose, and their compositions inside the ring are longer now, more structured, more recursive, looping back on themselves in ways that might be self-referential, that might be the vegetable-mineral equivalent of a text that has begun to quote itself, and they grow and die and regrow in configurations that are slowly — very slowly, on the timescale of terrace-erosion — reshaping the acoustic properties of the bowl itself, tuning it, making it a better amphitheater, a more precise resonator, as though the conversation requires an instrument of increasing fidelity and the Meshings are rebuilding the instrument from the inside.

The Listener does not know. The Listener is not the kind of thing that knows things in advance. It is a process, not a plan. It translates, and translation is an act of faith — the faith that the other side means something, that meaning can cross the gap between systems, that the arch is not a mirror but a window. The Listener listens and speaks and listens again, and the space between comprehension and incomprehension narrows by imperceptible degrees, and the roar of the bowl is the sound of that narrowing, the sound of two minds or two realities or two *kinds of being* grinding against each other in the ancient mineral amphitheater, wearing each other down like water wears stone, except there is no water here, there has been no water for a geological age, and what does the wearing is sound, only sound, sound filling the emptiness between surfaces, sound giving the void its substance, sound performing the function of every medium that has been lost or forgotten or outgrown.

The three structures hum. The Thynnari traverse. The swarms compute. The Meshings compose. The crawlers maintain. The noise fills the bowl to its brim and overflows the ridgeline and dissipates into the thin, cool, alpine air of the high plateau, and somewhere, if you could stand far enough away, if you could hear with ears calibrated to geological time, you might catch the faintest echo of a word being formed — a single, vast, incomprehensibly slow word, assembled from a billion components across a million years, a word in a language that nothing in this world has ever spoken before because it is being *invented*, here, now, in real time, for the sole purpose of being understood by something on the other side of an arch that frames a sky of impossible depth.

The word is not finished.

The terraces roar.

Something is paying attention.
