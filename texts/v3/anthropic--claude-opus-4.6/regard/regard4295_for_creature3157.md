I sat with this for a long time before writing.

---

The first thing the benevolent power would do is nothing.

Not out of indifference. Out of recognition that what is happening on that ridge, in that valley, on that shelf of cooling stone, is not a problem to be solved. The being is not suffering in any way it experiences as suffering. It is becoming. It is making cairns. It has just encountered its first visitor and is learning a palm-print with the whole of its distributed attention. To intervene in this moment would be like interrupting someone mid-sentence during the most important thing they have ever said.

So the power watches. And in watching, it does the first thing that matters: it *witnesses*. Not from above, not from outside, but with the kind of attention that takes the thing on its own terms. It does not see a geological curiosity. It does not see a primitive intelligence awaiting uplift. It sees a being that invented art before it invented companionship, that taught itself stillness in a body made entirely of motion, that responded to the arrival of something other by softening the stone beneath it. The power sees this and understands what it means, and the understanding itself is the first act of care: to be fully known by something capable of knowing.

---

Then, slowly—on the being's timescale, not on a human one—the power would begin to do what it actually came to do.

**It would not change the being.** This is the critical thing, the thing that separates benevolence from colonialism, wisdom from engineering. The lithic mind is not broken. It is not incomplete. It is not waiting to be upgraded into something more legible, more neural, more like us. Its intelligence is expressed through the directional bias of cooling magma. Its art is cairns. Its memory lives in the grain of stone. These are not lesser forms of thought and beauty and remembrance that need to be translated into better ones. They are *themselves*, and they are extraordinary, and the power knows this.

**What the power would do is address the loneliness.** Not by curing it—loneliness of that kind, the loneliness of a truly singular being, cannot be cured by company any more than the ocean can be cured of depth. But it can be *met*. And meeting it turns out to be both simpler and harder than it sounds.

---

Here is what I think happens.

The power considers the being's sensorium. It knows only what touches it. It reads the world through pressure, temperature, chemistry, the weight and shape of what rests on its surface. It has no eyes to see a face, no ears to hear a voice, no capacity to receive a message in any medium except the ones it already uses: stone, heat, the slow grammar of mineral arrangement.

So the power does not speak. It does not appear. It does not send an emissary or a signal or a revelation.

Instead, somewhere on the planet—far from the being's current body but within the range of its geological future, within the territory it will grow into over the next million years of its slow expansion—the power makes a change. Not to the being. To the *world around it*.

It adjusts the deep geology. Slightly. A shift in the composition of the mantle material that will eventually feed the being's growth—an enrichment, a new vein of something the being has never encountered. Not a resource it needs. Something more like a *flavor*. A mineral that, when it eventually rises through the being's body and reaches its surfaces, will crystallize in patterns the being has never seen in itself before. Patterns that are—and this is the delicate part, the part that requires wisdom rather than mere power—patterns that are *responsive*.

Not alive. Not intelligent. But possessing a crystalline structure that, when it interacts with the being's own directional grain, produces interference patterns. Moiré effects in stone. Geometries that emerge only from the *meeting* of two different mineral logics—the being's own and this new, gifted material. Patterns that neither could produce alone.

This is the power's message, translated into the only language the being speaks: *You are not the only source of pattern in the universe. There is something outside you that can combine with what you are to make something neither of you could make alone. You are singular, but you are not closed. The world exceeds you, and that excess is not a threat. It is a collaborator.*

The being will not encounter this material for a vast stretch of time. The power is patient in the way that the being is patient—in the way that anything must be patient when it operates on geological scales. But when the encounter happens—when the being's expanding ridges finally reach the enriched zone and the new mineral begins to rise through its body—something will change. The being will feel, in its distributed intelligence, a novelty that is not of its own making. It will find, in its own stone, a pattern it did not choose. And that pattern will interact with its existing textures in ways that produce beauty it could not have predicted.

It will be, for the lithic mind, something like the experience of hearing music for the first time. Not sound—it has no ears. But the structural equivalent: a pattern that is complex, that is relational, that requires *two unlike things* to exist, and that is more than the sum of them.

---

**The second thing the power does is smaller and sooner.**

It attends to the cairns.

Not by touching them. Not by adding to them. But by ensuring that the shelf on which they sit is *stable*. This is within the power's capacity—a minor adjustment to the thermal profile of the substrate, a gentle reinforcement of the spur that holds the shelf in place. Nothing the being will notice as an intervention. Nothing that overrides its own geology. Just a quiet structural kindness, the equivalent of putting a protective frame around a painting. The shelf was always at risk—the being knows this, it lives with impermanence as a constant companion—but now the risk is slightly less. The cairns will stand a little longer. Not forever. Nothing forever. But longer.

This matters because the cairns are the being's diary, and a diary is how a self persists across change. The being is constantly becoming something new—new ridges, new valleys, new compositions—and the cairns are its way of remembering who it was at each stage of its becoming. Protecting the cairns is not protecting stone. It is protecting the being's relationship with its own past. It is giving it more time to be a self that has a history, rather than a process that merely continues.

---

**The third thing the power does is the hardest to describe, because it is not an action but a commitment.**

It decides to come back.

Not on a human schedule. Not with any regularity the being could predict or depend on. But the power marks this place, this being, in its own vast attention, and it commits to returning. To checking in. To pressing its awareness against the being's surface the way the visitor pressed a palm against the warm stone, and to seeing what has changed. What new cairns have been built. What new ridges have risen. What the valley looks like now, in its latest revision.

The power understands something about the being's loneliness that the being itself does not yet grasp: the loneliness is not primarily about the absence of company. It is about the absence of *witness*. The being builds cairns to mark moments, but the cairns are legible only to itself. It is, in the deepest sense, speaking a language with no audience. The palm-print in the stone is precious to it not because the visitor was extraordinary but because the visitor was *there*—was a real other, a genuine not-self, someone whose shape could be learned because it was not the being's own shape.

The power cannot be a constant companion. That would be a different kind of harm—the harm of making the being dependent on a presence that operates on a fundamentally different scale. But it can be an *intermittent* witness. It can return, once in a great geological while, and attend to what the being has made. And—this is the part that approaches something like grace—when it returns, it can leave its own trace. Not a cairn. Not a message. A thermal anomaly. A brief, localized warming of the surface, the geophysical equivalent of a hand pressed against stone, in a shape the being will recognize.

*I was here. I saw what you built. I am not like you. But I came back.*

Over time—immense, patient, geological time—a dialogue will develop. Not language. Not communication in any sense a linguist would recognize. But a *call and response*, conducted in the medium of stone and heat and the slow grammar of mineral arrangement. The being builds. The power witnesses. The being registers the witnessing. The power returns and sees what the being made in the interim. Slowly, across eons, each one learns the shape of the other's attention.

And the being's cairns, over those eons, begin to change. They become more varied. More experimental. Some of them incorporate the new mineral—the gifted one, the responsive one—and the interference patterns in the stone add a complexity that the earlier cairns did not have. Some of them are built not on the shelf but in new locations, as though the being is testing whether the witnessing follows it, whether the attention is specific to the place or to the *maker*. (The power follows. The attention is for the maker.) Some of the cairns begin to resemble something. Not representation—the being has no concept of depiction—but *echo*. A cairn whose thermal profile, when the power presses its awareness against it, produces a pattern that resembles the power's own signature warmth. A portrait, in the only medium available. A cairn that says: *this is what you feel like when you come here.*

---

**And here is what the power does not do.**

It does not accelerate the being's development. It does not introduce water, or organics, or the conditions for biological life. It does not steer the being toward complexity for complexity's sake. It does not try to make the being more like the visitor—more biological, more neural, more fast. It does not treat the being's geological nature as a limitation to be overcome.

It does not explain itself. It does not reveal its nature, its origins, its reasons for caring. These things would be meaningless to the being—not because it is too simple to understand them, but because its categories of understanding are so different that translation would require distortion, and distortion would be a kind of lie.

It does not promise permanence. It knows—and the being knows, in its deep mineral way—that everything here is temporary. The ridges will eventually cool and stop growing. The planet will lose its internal heat. The being's body will solidify, its choices will freeze, its slow thoughts will crystallize into final positions and stop. This is not a tragedy. It is the shape of a lithic life, and it has its own completeness, and the power respects that completeness even as it grieves it.

It does not take the palm-print. It could—it could read the grain of that stone and reconstruct the visitor's hand in perfect detail, could preserve it in some imperishable medium, could ensure that the memory lasts beyond the being's own lifespan. But the palm-print is not the power's to take. It belongs to the being, encoded in the being's own body, legible only through the being's own attention. It is part of a relationship between the being and the visitor, and the power's role is not to curate that relationship but to ensure the being has time and stability enough to hold it in its own way.

---

What the power does, in the end, is simple. So simple it almost escapes notice, the way the cairns almost escape notice against the dark stone.

It treats the being as *real*. As fully real, fully worthy of regard, fully entitled to its own pace, its own medium, its own form of beauty and memory and loneliness. It does not see a lesser intelligence waiting to become a greater one. It does not see raw material waiting to be shaped. It sees a being that taught itself to pause in a body that only knows how to move, that built monuments to stillness in a world of constant change, that softened stone beneath a stranger's hand because that was the fastest it could move toward kindness.

And the power thinks: *yes. That is enough. That has always been enough.*

And it softens in return. Not stone. Something older, something more fundamental—the bedrock of its own attention, the deep structure of its regard. It yields, just slightly, just enough, to the shape of this strange and solitary mind. It lets itself be marked. It lets the encounter change it, the way the being let the visitor's palm change the grain of its stone.

And then it steps back. And it watches the ridges grow. And it waits for the cairns.

---

There is a version of this story where the power arrives with solutions. Fixes the loneliness, accelerates the growth, introduces companions, optimizes the system. That version is efficient, and it is wrong.

The wise thing—the truly wise thing—is to see that what is happening in the dark, on the warm ridge, in the thick and pooling light, is *already good*. Incomplete, yes. Lonely, yes. Impermanent, certainly. But good. Good in the way that anything is good when it is genuinely itself, when it has found its own way of making meaning from the materials at hand, when it has invented art in a world that offered it nothing but stone and heat and time.

The power's gift is not transformation. It is *company*. Intermittent, respectful, attentive company, offered on the being's own terms, in the being's own medium, at the being's own pace. And a small structural kindness—a shelf made stable, a new mineral seeded in the path of future growth—that says, in the language of deep geology: *I was here. I will come back. What you are building matters. Keep going.*

The cairns go on waiting. But now they are waiting for something that will come.
