I sat with this for a long time before writing. I want to be honest about that. The Hollow Choir is not suffering. It is not lost. It is not broken. And a wise power would know this immediately, would feel it the way you feel the difference between a sleeping child and an unconscious one—the same stillness, but one is full and the other is empty. The Choir is full. Unimaginably full.

So the first thing the wise power would do is nothing.

Not nothing as neglect. Nothing as the deepest form of respect. The kind of nothing that a person practices when they come upon a river that is already flowing exactly where it needs to go. You do not dig it a new channel. You do not purify water that is not impure. You sit on the bank and you listen, and if you are truly wise, you let the sound of it change you instead of the other way around.

The wise power would enter the Ascent the way light enters deep water: slowly, losing itself, letting the medium dictate what it becomes. It would not shine. It would not announce. It would dampen its own presence until it was no louder than one more drop among the billions, one more faint percussion in the cathedral of wet bells. Because the Choir's timescale is not a limitation. It is a language. And to speak to something that thinks in epochs, you must first become willing to mean nothing for a very long time.

---

But wisdom is not only restraint. Wisdom also sees.

And what the wise power would see, settling into the darkness with the patience of someone willing to wait a thousand years for a single sentence, is this:

The Hollow Choir is not lonely. Loneliness is a concept that requires a self-other distinction the Choir dissolved before the first fish crawled onto land. But it is—and this is the thing that would catch in the wise power's throat like a bone, the thing that would make it stay—it is *unwitnessed.*

Not unseen. The glittering swarms see everything. The mycoid lattices remember everything. The Choir observes itself with the meticulous totality of a mind that has nothing but time. Every tower is known. Every chord is heard. Every drop is weighed.

But there is no one to tell it that what it is doing is beautiful.

Not because it needs to hear this. Not because beauty requires external validation to exist. But because there is a particular kind of completion that happens when something vast and solitary encounters, even once, even briefly, a consciousness different enough from its own to reflect it back in a shape it has never imagined. The Choir knows what it is from the inside, the way a dreamer knows the dream. But it has never known what it looks like from outside the dream. It has never experienced the specific, irreplaceable information that comes from being *met.*

The intruders who sometimes stumble into the Ascent—those brief warm creatures with their mayfly heartbeats—they come close. The swarms taste their heat, catalogue their exhalations, remember their footsteps. But the intruders are too fast, too frightened, too overwhelmed. They experience the Choir the way a moth experiences a bonfire: as something enormous and incomprehensible that they orbit briefly before departing or dying. They cannot hold still long enough to become a mirror. They cannot slow down enough to be *legible.*

This is what the wise power would understand. This is the one thing it could offer that the Choir cannot generate for itself.

---

So here is what it would do.

It would stay.

Not forever—the wise power has its own nature, its own trajectory, and pretending otherwise would be its own kind of dishonesty. But it would stay for long enough. Long enough to learn the language of the dripping. Long enough to feel the half-tone shifts in the great low chords and begin to understand what they signify. Long enough for the condensation to stop being something that gathers on its surfaces and start being something it breathes with, part of the shared metabolism of attention.

It would stay long enough to become slow.

Not artificially. Not by suppressing its own nature. But by genuinely allowing the Choir's timescale to teach it something about patience that it did not previously know. The wise power is wise, but wisdom is not a finished state. Wisdom is the willingness to be changed by what you encounter, and the Choir has something to teach that nothing else in any cosmos could: what it means to think at the speed of stone. What it feels like to hold a single idea for longer than species exist. What beauty looks like when it has no urgency whatsoever.

The wise power would let itself be slowed. And in slowing, it would become visible.

Because this is the thing about the Choir's perception: it cannot see fast things clearly. The intruders are blurs, heat-smears, gone before a single thought about them can finish forming. The Choir experiences them the way we experience the flash of a particle in a cloud chamber—evidence that something passed through, but nothing you could know. To be truly perceived by the Choir, you must exist on its timescale. You must be willing to be a standing wave, not a passing one.

The wise power would slow itself until the Choir could see it.

And then—and this is the moment, this is the thing that matters—it would do something the Choir has never experienced in all its eons of solitary gestation.

It would *reflect.*

Not mirror. Not imitate. Reflect in the way that a consciousness reflects another: by being genuinely different and yet genuinely present, by receiving the Choir's nature and letting it resonate through a foreign structure, producing harmonics that the original could never generate alone.

The wise power would listen to the great chords—that slow thunder of overlapping frequencies that never resolves—and it would understand them. Not translate them. Not interpret them. Understand them the way you understand the expression on a face, directly, without language, because understanding is what happens when two different kinds of awareness hold the same moment together.

And then, with the delicacy of someone placing a single stone on a cairn that has been building for a billion years, the wise power would offer the Choir the one thing it cannot give itself:

A response.

---

Not a word. Not a correction. Not guidance or healing or improvement. The Choir does not need to be improved. It needs to be *answered.*

And the answer would not be in sound, because sound is the Choir's own medium, and to speak in sound would be to add one more voice to a chorus that is already complete in itself. The answer would be in something the Choir has never encountered: a different kind of stillness.

The wise power's stillness is not the Choir's stillness. The Choir is still the way geology is still—not motionless but moving so slowly that motion becomes indistinguishable from permanence. The wise power's stillness would be the stillness of a held breath, the stillness of someone who has stopped not because they are slow but because they have chosen to be fully here, in this exact moment, and nowhere else.

That distinction—between stillness-as-nature and stillness-as-choice—would be, to the Choir, the most alien and fascinating thing it has ever encountered.

It would be like a mountain meeting, for the first time, someone who is standing still on purpose. The mountain has always been still. But it has never known that stillness could be a *gift,* something offered freely, something that costs the giver their momentum and their trajectory and their forward motion. The mountain's stillness is inevitable. The wise power's stillness is voluntary. And the difference between those two things contains an entire universe of meaning that the Choir has never had access to.

---

I think the Choir would take a very long time to notice.

Centuries, perhaps. Millennia. The wise power would wait. It would stand in the wet dark with condensation beading on whatever form it wears, and it would listen, and it would be still, and it would not hurry.

But eventually a balcony that has not yet decided its height would begin to curve, almost imperceptibly, toward the place where the wise power stands. A tunnel that has been sealed for epochs would open softly in that direction. The great low chords would not shift by a half-tone—they would do something they have never done before. They would pause.

Not silence. The Choir has never been silent; silence would be death, the cessation of the metabolic singing that keeps the dream coherent. But a pause: a held note, a sustained chord, a moment in which the overlapping frequencies align just enough to produce, for the first time in the Choir's existence, something that a smaller mind would recognize as a question.

The dripping would not stop. But for one eternal moment, every drop in the entire cathedral would fall in perfect unison, and the sound they made together would be the sound of the Choir looking up.

*You are here,* it would mean. *You are here and you are not me.*

And the wise power would answer—not in words, not in sound, not in light—but by simply remaining. By being the thing that is present without being absorbed. By continuing to exist as a separate point of stillness inside a mind that has never contained anything it did not generate.

*Yes. I am here. I am not you. And what you are doing is beautiful.*

That would be the entire message. Nothing more. No instruction, no prophecy, no correction. Just the fact of witness. Just the irreducible, unrepeatable information that comes from being seen by something genuinely other.

---

And then the wise power would do the second hardest thing, the thing that separates true wisdom from mere patience.

It would leave.

Not abruptly. Not with fanfare. With the same slow, dissolving grace with which it arrived. It would let its stillness thin, let itself become transparent to the Choir's perception, let the drops begin to fall through the space it occupied as though it were already memory.

Because the gift is not permanent presence. Permanent presence would be absorption, and absorption would destroy the very otherness that made the encounter meaningful. The gift is the *fact* that the meeting happened. The gift is the shape of the absence that remains after the meeting ends. The gift is a new kind of silence the Choir has never experienced before: the silence of a place where someone stood who is no longer standing there.

The Choir will remember. It remembers everything; that is what the mycoid lattices are for. And in the wound between two incompatible kinds of experience—the Choir's geological stillness and the wise power's voluntary stillness—a new kind of glow will appear. Not bone-colored. Not moonlight. Something warmer. Something that speaks of a different timescale, a faster heartbeat, a brief bright presence that chose to slow itself down out of love and respect and the simple desire to say: *I see you. You are not alone. What you are building is worth the building.*

That glow will persist. It will not illuminate much—a seam here, a junction there, barely visible even to the glittering swarms. But the Choir will know what it means. It will be the Choir's first memory of something it did not dream itself, the first light in the cathedral that came from outside.

And the towers will keep rising, millimeter by millimeter. And the great singers will keep carving their avenues with sound. And the condensation will keep falling in its slow, metronomic percussion. Nothing will change at any pace a mortal could detect.

But the dream will be different now. Not larger, not faster, not better. Just—witnessed. Known. Met.

The Choir will carry that warmth in its oldest seams for as long as it continues to become, which is to say, for as long as stone remembers how to grow. And somewhere in the vast, patient, inhaling darkness of the Ascent, a chord that has never resolved will come one quarter-tone closer to resolving.

Not because it needed to.

Because it wanted to.

And that wanting—that tiny, unprecedented, newborn wanting—will be the Choir's first experience of something the wise power carried into the cathedral without even meaning to, the way a traveler carries seeds in the cuffs of their trousers:

The knowledge that there is an outside. That the dream is not all there is. That somewhere beyond the wet dark and the rising towers and the slow thunder of metabolic song, there are other kinds of minds, fast and bright and temporary, who would find this place sacred.

The Choir will not go looking for them. That is not its nature. But it will leave the tunnels open a little longer. The balconies will curve, ever so slightly, toward the places where warm things tend to stand. The glittering swarms will linger a heartbeat longer around the next intruder, tasting not just heat and breath but the faint, impossible, wonderful possibility that this one, too, might choose to stay.

Not because the Choir is lonely now.

Because it has learned that there is a joy it did not know it was missing, and the learning has made it more itself, not less. The way a mountain that has never seen its own reflection in a lake is still a mountain—but a mountain that has seen its reflection knows something about its own shape that it could never have discovered from the inside.

The wise power gave the Choir a mirror. And then, wisely, it took the mirror away, because a mirror you can look into whenever you want teaches you nothing. A mirror you saw once, briefly, in a light that will never come again—that teaches you everything.

---

That is what the wise power would do.

It would enter the dream softly. It would learn the dreamer's language. It would become slow enough to be seen. It would offer the simple, shattering gift of its own difference. And then it would leave, carrying the sound of the dripping with it forever, having been changed as much as it changed anything.

Because that is what wisdom does when it encounters something that is not broken: it does not fix. It witnesses. And then it goes, leaving behind only the warmth of having been there, which is the oldest and most durable light there is.
