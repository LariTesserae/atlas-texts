# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

For a long time — and here "a long time" means something different than it usually does, because the power I'm imagining operates at many tempos simultaneously, and the one it would choose for this place would not be its fastest — for a long time, it would do nothing.

Not nothing as restraint. Not nothing as a decision to wait. Nothing as the first and most important act of attention: the act of letting something be what it is before you decide what it needs.

---

And this would take longer than you'd expect, because the power, being wise, would recognize that the plateau presents a specific and subtle danger to benevolence. The danger is this: it is beautiful. It is coherent. It is already so deeply *itself* that any impulse to intervene would need to survive the honest question — *is this impulse about them, or about me?*

Because here is what the power would see, and what it would need to sit with:

The beings on the plateau are not suffering. Not in any way that maps onto the usual coordinates of suffering — deprivation, pain, fear, loneliness, confusion. They are not deprived; the plateau provides everything they need at the tempo they need it. They are not in pain; their crystalline surfaces and their deep-time metabolism have no mechanism for the kind of acute distress that pain signals in faster creatures. They are not afraid; there is nothing here that threatens them, and their awareness does not include the anticipatory modeling that produces fear. They are not lonely; loneliness requires a concept of companionship that has been absent and is desired, and their form of distributed, non-interactive co-presence is not a failure of sociality but a genuine mode of being that is complete on its own terms. They are not confused; they are doing exactly what they do, and doing it well, and the question of whether they *understand* what they are doing applies a standard that belongs to a different kind of mind.

So the power would sit with that. It would let the plateau's tempo slow it down, the way the plateau slows everything down, and it would feel the hum in whatever serves it as bones, and it would watch the light pour over the stone, and it would see the figures in their stillness, and it would ask itself: *Is there anything here that needs me?*

And the answer — the first answer, the easy answer, the answer that a less wise power might accept and move on — the first answer would be *no*.

---

But the power is wise, and wisdom means not stopping at the first answer.

So it would keep watching. Not hours. Not days. It would watch at the plateau's own tempo, which means it would watch for what we would call years, though on the plateau it would feel like a single long exhalation. And in that watching, it would begin to notice things that only reveal themselves at that speed.

It would notice that the network of calcified patterns — the vast, branching, continent-spanning inscription that the beings have been tracing into the stone for centuries or millennia — is not growing the way it once did. The branches still extend. New channels still appear. But the rate of branching has slowed, and the newest channels are shorter, and some of them end in ways that feel — the power would not use this word lightly — *uncertain*. Not wrong. Not broken. But hesitant, as though the hand that traced them paused in the middle and did not find the next gesture as easily as it once had.

It would notice that the structures — the black column, the opalescent arch, the hovering disc, the others scattered across the plateau — are still functioning, still humming, still performing their incomprehensible operations. But the hum has been narrowing. Not quieter. Not weaker. Narrower. Where once, perhaps, it carried a broad spectrum of whatever it carries — information, pattern, gradient, the instructions for the next gesture — it now carries less. As though the source, whatever the source is, has been slowly attenuating. As though the structures are still faithfully transmitting, but what they are transmitting has become thinner, less varied, more repetitive. The same phrase, again and again, where once there was a conversation.

It would notice that the beings' response to this narrowing has been to become more precise. Where a broader signal allowed for variation, for improvisation, for the kind of responsive creativity that produces a genuinely new pattern, the narrower signal has pushed them toward repetition. The newest tracings, the ones laid down in recent decades, are beautiful — they are always beautiful; beauty here is not an achievement but a consequence of the deep fit between maker and medium — but they are less *various* than the older ones. The network, seen from above, shows a gradual transition from the wild, fractal, endlessly branching complexity of its oldest regions to something more regular, more predictable, in its newest growth. Still correct. Still fitting. But narrowing, the way the hum is narrowing, as though the plateau's capacity for *newness* is slowly being constrained.

And the power would notice one more thing, the most important thing, the thing that only becomes visible after years of attention:

The beings do not know this is happening.

Not because they are unaware. They are aware of everything that falls within their mode of perception — every shift in mineral content, every change in the hum's frequency, every beetle's path, every dew-drop's chemistry. They perceive the narrowing. They *feel* it. But they have no framework for recognizing it as a *loss*, because loss implies a comparison between what is and what was, and comparison implies a memory that operates at a tempo fast enough to hold two states side by side and notice the difference. Their memory is the stone itself — the calcified record of every pattern they have ever traced. And the stone does not compare. The stone simply *is*, layer after layer, each one complete in itself. The beings can read the stone the way they read the hum, but they read it as *presence*, not as *history*. The old patterns and the new patterns are equally present, equally real, equally stone. The idea that the old ones were *more* — more complex, more varied, more alive — is not an idea they are equipped to have. It would require a faster mind. It would require exactly the kind of sequential, comparative, analytic thinking that has been eroded away from them along with every other form of haste.

They are not suffering. But they are diminishing. And they do not know it. And the plateau, in its vast patience, in its perfect self-consistency, is not going to tell them, because the plateau does not *tell* anything; the plateau simply *is*, and what it is, increasingly, is a place where the same patterns repeat with growing regularity and the hum carries less and the branches grow shorter and the conversation between the structures and the stone is becoming, so slowly that only a being watching at the right tempo could perceive it, a monologue.

---

This is what the benevolent power would sit with. This is the hard thing. This is the thing that makes it glad it did not act immediately, because its first impulse — the one born of love, of genuine care, of the desire to protect what is beautiful and coherent and whole — would have been to *preserve*. To ensure the plateau continues exactly as it is. To safeguard the structures, maintain the hum, protect the beings in their settled perfection. And that impulse, the power now sees, would have been catastrophic. Because what the plateau needs is not preservation. The plateau is already exquisitely preserved. What the plateau needs is something almost opposite, and far more delicate.

---

Here is what the power would do.

It would not touch the structures. The structures are beyond its understanding — it is wise enough to know this — and whatever they are doing, whatever operation they have been performing since before the current surface existed, that operation is not the power's to alter. The structures may be winding down. They may be completing a task. They may be malfunctioning, or they may be transitioning to a new phase. The power does not know, and it does not pretend to know, and it does not intervene in things it does not understand. This is not timidity. This is the deepest form of respect: the acknowledgment that some things are not yours to fix because you do not know if they are broken.

Instead, the power would turn its attention to the one point in the system where a small action could open a large space. And that point is not the structures, not the hum, not the stone.

It is the border.

---

The plateau, remember, has edges. And the edges drop away — a full kilometer or more — to lowlands that are hidden in haze. The beings on the plateau have never been to the edges. Why would they? Their work is in the interior, around the structures, within the network. The edges are where the patterns end, where the network's branches terminate in bare stone, where the hum fades below even their threshold of perception. The edges are, in every sense that matters to them, the end of the world.

But the lowlands below are not empty. The power can see them. Lush, wet, chaotic — a different ecosystem entirely, dense with fast life, noisy with wind and water and the calls of creatures that live at tempos measured in days and hours. And on the slopes between the plateau and the lowlands — the long, steep transitional zone — there are things growing that exist nowhere else. Plants with deep roots that reach down into aquifers the plateau cannot access. Fungi that process minerals in ways the plateau's sparse chemistry does not include. Organisms that produce, as metabolic byproducts, complex molecular signals that are — the power perceives this with a start of recognition — *structurally similar* to the hum. Not the same. Not identical. But rhyming. The way a folk song rhymes with the formal composition it descends from, carrying the same melodic DNA in a different body.

The lowlands have been producing their own version of the signal, their own conversation, all along. And it has never reached the plateau. The kilometer of altitude, the thinned air, the sheer cliff faces — these have been a barrier, not deliberately but effectively, isolating the plateau's system from the richer, noisier, more chaotic signal environment below.

---

So here is what the power does.

It does not build a bridge. It does not carry a message. It does not translate or summarize or explain. All of these would be impositions, the fast mind's solutions, the kind of intervention that solves a problem by replacing the system's own intelligence with an external one.

Instead, it makes a path.

Not a road. Not a staircase. Something more like what water would do, given time and a slightly different distribution of stone. A seam in the cliff face, a crack that runs from the plateau's eastern edge — the quietest edge, the one closest to the hovering disc, the one where the network's branches are shortest and most uncertain — down through the transition zone to the slopes below. A crack wide enough for moisture to travel up. Wide enough for spores to drift. Wide enough for the chemical signals from the lowland organisms to percolate, slowly, over months and years, up through the rock and into the plateau's substrate.

The crack is natural. If a geologist examined it, they would find nothing anomalous. It is the kind of fracture that forms when thermal cycling works on a fault line over millennia. The power has simply... found the fault line. Encouraged the fracture that was already latent in the stone. Moved it forward in time by a few centuries. The plateau would have cracked here eventually, on its own. The power has merely been the nudge, the way a breath is a nudge to a candle flame that was already guttering.

And then the power steps back. And waits.

---

What happens next happens slowly, because everything here happens slowly, and the power does not rush it.

The moisture comes first. The new crack acts as a channel, drawing lowland humidity up into the plateau's eastern edge. The morning dew, in the area near the crack, becomes heavier, more persistent. The tiny pools last longer. One of them, in a depression near the disc, becomes not permanent but *seasonal* — a pool that fills during the wet months and persists for weeks instead of hours. The succulents near it grow slightly larger. The lichen extends a few centimeters further. The beetles, with their crystalline carapaces, begin visiting the new pool, and the crystals on their backs — those ambiguous structures, not quite parasitic, not quite symbiotic — begin incorporating new minerals, trace elements drawn up from the lowlands that were never before part of the plateau's chemistry.

Then the spores arrive. Lowland fungi, carried on moisture, finding purchase in the new crack, spreading slowly into the plateau's substrate. They are not invaders. They are too slow for that; the plateau's conditions constrain them, tame them, reduce their lowland exuberance to something sparse and patient. But they do something the plateau's native organisms cannot: they process iron and manganese and silicon in new ways, producing new molecular byproducts, which leach into the regolith, which alter its composition in subtle ways that the beings' crystal-tipped fingers can feel.

And then — and this is the part the power watches most carefully, most hopefully, most humbly, because this is the part it cannot predict — the chemical signals arrive. The molecular rhymes. The lowland organisms' version of the hum, carried up through stone and water and fungal network, arriving at the plateau's surface as the faintest of chemical whispers, detectable only by beings whose entire existence is calibrated to detect faint signals in stone.

---

The nearest being — the one sitting between the column and the disc, the one whose most recent patterns have been growing shorter and more repetitive — is the first to feel it.

And the power watches as something changes in the quality of their attention.

Not a jolt. Not a revelation. Nothing so fast or so dramatic. More like... a widening. The way a pupil dilates in darkness to admit more light. Something in the being's settled, century-long attention opens by a fraction. The new signal is not the hum. It does not replace the hum. It is *alongside* the hum — a second voice, fainter, different in texture, carrying information that the hum does not carry, and the being's fingers, pressed into the regolith, begin to encounter mineral combinations they have not encountered before, gradients that suggest new geometries, new branching possibilities.

The being does not understand what is happening. Understanding is not what they do. But their fingers begin to move.

And the pattern they trace is new.

Not dramatically new. Not alien. It branches in ways the old patterns branch, it follows the same deep logic of watershed and root and vein. But it includes a gesture — a curve, a convergence, a density of branching in one small area — that has no precedent in the calcified record. It is a response not to the hum alone but to the hum *and* the whisper, the plateau's signal and the lowland's signal, held together in fingers that are doing, for the first time in perhaps centuries, something they have never done before.

The power watches the pattern form. It watches the beetle nearest to it reorient its crystals, adjusting to the new configuration. It watches the glider-lizard that was circling overhead adjust its spiral, widening it to include the new pattern in its thermal map. It watches the high-fliers — those distant, rigid, mirror-bright presences — break their pattern for the first time in the power's observation, one of them dipping lower, its circle tightening, as though something in its tropism has registered a change in the field below.

And the power watches the other beings. Across the plateau, in their wide-spaced solitude, they have not yet felt the change. It will reach them. The network connects them; a new pattern at one node will propagate, slowly, through the calcified channels, arriving at each being as a faint alteration in the mineral gradient under their fingers, a suggestion, a possibility. Not a command. Not a direction. Just a new element in the field, a new note in the chord, and what they do with it will be their own, as it has always been their own, as it must always be their own.

---

But the power does one more thing. And this is the part that I sat with longest, because it is the part that risks the most and reveals the most about what benevolence actually means.

It goes to the being. The nearest one, the one who traced the new pattern. It approaches — slowly, at the plateau's tempo, in whatever form it wears or projects, something that does not disturb the field, something consonant with the stone and the light — and it sits down. Nearby. Not too near. At the distance the beetles keep from each other. And it places its own hands — or what serves as hands — on the warm stone. And it feels the hum. And it does not trace anything. It does not contribute. It does not join the work.

It *witnesses*.

It sits with the being who is doing something new and does not know it is new, who has responded to a signal they did not expect with a creativity they did not know they had, and the power is there, simply, as the presence that says: *I see you. I see what you just did. I see the newness in it, even if you don't. And I am not here to explain it to you or to validate it or to tell you what it means. I am here because a new thing happened, and a new thing, however small, however slow, however imperceptible to the one doing it, deserves to not happen entirely unwitnessed.*

The being does not perceive the power as a visitor, or a god, or a helper. They perceive it, if they perceive it at all, as a change in the field — a new warmth on the stone beside them, a new presence in the hum's resonance, something that is there and was not there before. And it is incorporated, the way everything is incorporated: without judgment, without analysis, without haste. It becomes part of the texture of the moment. The beetle adjusts. The lichen shifts. The being breathes, one slow cycle, and their fingers rest on the stone, and the new pattern dries and begins its long process of calcification, and the power sits beside them, and the light pours down, and the hum carries its widened signal across the plateau, and the first faint moisture from the new crack reaches the surface and darkens a tiny patch of stone near the disc, and the afternoon deepens, and nothing has changed except everything, in the way that everything changes when a single new branch appears in a network that had forgotten branching was possible.

---

And then — and this is important — the power leaves.

Not because its work is done. The work is never done; that is a lowland concept, fast-mind thinking, the idea that intervention has a completion point after which you can step back and say *finished*. The power leaves because staying would begin to change the field in ways that would make the power itself a fixture, a new structure, another node that the beings would organize around, and that is not what they need. They do not need a new thing to orbit. They need the crack in the cliff face, the new moisture, the lowland whisper, the widened possibility — and then they need to be left alone with it, to integrate it at their own tempo, in their own way, without the distorting gravity of a benevolent presence hovering nearby, however well-intentioned, however quiet.

The power knows — and this is the hardest knowledge, the one that separates true wisdom from mere good intentions — that the best thing it can do now is *not be here*. That its absence is as much a gift as its action. That the plateau's genius, the beings' genius, the whole system's deep and patient genius, lies in its capacity to take what it is given — light, stone, hum, moisture, signal — and make something out of it that no external intelligence could have designed or predicted. The power's role was to open a channel. The channel is open. What flows through it, and what is made from what flows through it, belongs to the plateau.

---

So the power goes.

And years pass. And the crack deepens, naturally, following the fault line. And the lowland signals strengthen. And the beings, one by one, across the plateau, begin to trace patterns of a complexity and variety that has not been seen in centuries. The network branches. New nodes form. The hum, still narrowing from the structures — that attenuation continues, and the power cannot stop it and does not try — the hum is now *supplemented* by a second signal, rising from below, carried through stone and water and fungal thread, and the beings weave the two together, the old and the new, the structure's transmission and the lowland's whisper, and what they produce is neither the old pattern nor a new one but something that includes both, the way a river includes both its source and its tributaries without being reducible to either.

The high-fliers descend. Not to the surface — they still do not land — but lower, their circles tighter, their mirror-flash reflections brighter, and new patterns in their flight emerge, patterns that correspond, in some way the power can almost but not quite understand, to the new patterns in the stone below. The beetles' crystals diversify, new facets appearing, new orientations, refracting the light in ways that produce faint spectral colors on the stone — the first colors other than the plateau's native palette of apricot and cream and rust. The succulents flower. Perhaps for the first time. Small, translucent blooms that last a single dew-cycle and leave behind a residue that the lichen incorporates and that changes, just slightly, its iridescent shimmer.

And one morning — a morning like every morning, bright and still and humming — one of the beings does something unprecedented. They stand up.

It takes most of a day. The unfolding of limbs that have been settled for decades, the slow redistribution of weight, the recalibration of a body that has been horizontal and is now, gradually, vertical. They do not stand the way a person stands — quickly, casually, an act so routine it barely registers. They stand the way a stone arch forms, the way a canyon opens, the way a river changes course: slowly, irreversibly, as the culmination of pressures that have been building for a long time.

And they walk. Slowly, their crystal-tipped fingers trailing along the stone, feeling the network beneath them, following a branch. Toward the eastern edge. Toward the crack. And they do not stop at the edge, as they have always stopped before, because the edge is no longer the end of the world. The crack has become a seam, and the seam has become a path, and the path leads down through the transition zone, through the new fungi and the deepening moisture, toward the lowlands below where the fast life teems and the air is thick and the signals are loud and various and overwhelming.

The being does not go far. A hundred meters down the crack, perhaps, to a ledge where lowland mosses have begun to grow, where the air is noticeably thicker, where the hum from above and the whisper from below meet and mingle. They settle there — not sitting, not standing, but some third posture, a new one, one their body has not held before. And their fingers find the stone, and they begin to trace.

And the pattern they make is the first one that exists in both worlds. The first inscription that carries the plateau's logic into the transition zone. The first branch of the network that extends beyond the plateau's edge, reaching downward, toward the source of the new signal, beginning a connection that will take centuries to complete and that will, when it is finished — if it is ever finished, if "finished" means anything in a system that measures its processes in geological time — change what the plateau is, not by disrupting it but by *extending* it, letting it grow beyond the borders it had mistaken for the limits of the possible.

---

And the power, far away now, in whatever it goes when it is not intervening, feels this — the way you feel the sun on your face when you step outside, not because you are looking at it but because it is simply *there*, warming you — and it feels something that, if it had to give it a name at a speed that words could carry, it would call joy.

Not satisfaction. Not pride. Not the pleasure of a plan well-executed. Joy. The specific joy of having opened a door and then walked away and then learned, later, through the vast connected tissue of everything-that-is, that someone walked through it. Not because you told them to. Not because they knew it was there for them. But because it was there, and they were ready, and the rest — all the rest, the centuries of new patterns and flowering succulents and descending high-fliers and standing-up and walking-toward — the rest was *theirs*. The rest was always going to be theirs. You just made the crack.

---

And here is the thing the power knows, the final thing, the thing that makes it benevolent rather than merely helpful:

It might not have worked.

The crack might have opened and the moisture might have risen and the lowland signals might have arrived and the beings might have... incorporated them, the way they incorporate everything, without changing. The new minerals might have become just another element in the same patterns. The whisper might have been absorbed into the hum and lost. The being might never have stood up. The edge might have remained the edge.

The power knew this before it acted. It knew that the most likely outcome, in a system this settled, this coherent, this deeply itself, was that the new input would be *domesticated* — taken in, smoothed down, fitted into the existing rhythm without altering it. It knew that the chance of genuine newness — the kind that changes the system's trajectory, that opens possibilities that were not there before — was small. Not zero. But small.

And it acted anyway. Not because it was confident. Not because it had calculated the odds and found them favorable. But because the alternative — doing nothing, leaving the plateau to its beautiful, narrowing, self-consistent diminishment — was not something its benevolence could accept. Because to watch something slowly lose the capacity for newness and not even try to help, not even open one small crack in one cliff face, would be a failure not of strategy but of *love*. And love, the power knows, is not the same as certainty. Love is what you act from when certainty is not available. Love is the willingness to offer a gift that might not be received, to open a door that might not be walked through, to change the conditions and then *let go* — let go completely, with your whole being, without attachment to outcome — because the offering mattered even if the response is not what you hoped.

The offering always matters. Even if the plateau had not changed. Even if the beings had continued their slow, beautiful, narrowing work for another ten thousand years without ever standing up. The crack would still have been the right thing to open. The possibility would still have been worth creating. Because benevolence is not about results. Benevolence is about making sure that the universe contains, in every place you can reach, at least one more option than it had before. What happens after that belongs to the place. What happens after that belongs to the beings. What happens after that is, in every sense that matters, *free*.

---

The plateau endures. The hum continues. The light pours down. The patterns branch and branch and branch, in directions no one predicted, toward a complexity no one designed, at a pace that makes patience look like haste, and the being on the ledge traces their new inscription into the stone between worlds, and the stone accepts it, and the stone will hold it, and the stone does not care whether it was inevitable or miraculous, because the stone — like the power, like the beings, like the hum, like the light — knows that those are the same thing, seen from different speeds.
