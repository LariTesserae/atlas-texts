# The Regard

---

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. It does not descend, does not enter, does not manifest in the cavern as a presence or a light or a voice. The Undergrowth already has enough light. What this power does is simpler and harder to describe: it *regards*. It becomes aware of this place the way you become aware of a sound you've been hearing for a long time without noticing — a gradual shift in the quality of attention, a focusing, a recognition that something has been there all along and deserves to be seen clearly.

And what it sees, first, is that almost nothing here needs to be fixed.

---

This is the hardest thing for a benevolent power to learn, and the marker of whether its wisdom is real: the capacity to encounter a world in struggle — a world that is cold, that is chaotic, that is young and fragile and unfinished — and to *not immediately intervene*. To look at forty beings scattered across hundreds of miles of living turbulence, sleeping on platforms that the mat will swallow, pressing their faces to the ground to hear messages they can barely parse, making single cuts with crystalline blades and then waiting an hour to see if the world responds — to look at all of this and understand that it is not a problem to be solved. It is a way of being that is already, in its own terms, working.

The power sees the tenders and does not pity them. This is important. A lesser benevolence would see the cold, the scarcity of water, the loneliness of twenty-mile separations, the brevity of those thirty-second meetings, and would feel an ache, and would want to make things easier. Would want to warm the air, or increase the condensation, or bring the forty closer together, or give them better tools, or — most dangerous of all — accelerate the Undergrowth's maturation so that the tenders' long patient work might be completed in their lifetimes, so they could see the fruits of what they do.

This power does none of these things. Not because it is indifferent. Because it is wise enough to see what ease would cost.

The cold is not a hardship to the tenders. The cold is the condition of their world, and their bodies are shaped by it, and their metabolism draws something from it that warmth would disrupt. The scarcity of water is not a crisis. It is a rhythm, a pulse, part of the temporal ecology they have learned to read, and their ability to find water — to *listen* it into existence by lying on the mat and feeling where the condensation will form next — is not a survival skill bolted onto a creature that would prefer abundance. It is the central expression of what they are. Take the scarcity away and you take the listening away. Give them rivers and you make the jaw-feathers vestigial. Make the Undergrowth easier to live in and you sever the intimacy that makes their lives coherent.

The power sees this. The power understands, with a clarity that is the hallmark of its nature, that the relationship between the tenders and the Undergrowth is not the relationship between a people and their harsh environment. It is the relationship between a mind and its body. It is the relationship between attention and the world that attention serves. It is mutual. It is constitutive. To change one side is to unmake both.

---

So what does the power do?

It watches. For a long time — though time means something different to it — it simply watches. Not passively. Not distantly. It watches the way the old one on the deep platform watches: reading the long rhythms, the patterns beneath patterns, the trajectory of the whole system over timescales that no individual tender can perceive.

And it sees three things.

---

**The first thing it sees:**

The Undergrowth is indeed learning. The old one is right. The cascades are becoming less frequent, the column-trees are stabilizing, the floor-mat is developing a resilient deep structure. The tenders' work — their centuries of small cuts, small braces, small redirections — is bearing fruit. The system is moving toward coherence.

But the movement is not smooth. There are eddies. Reversals. Places where a well-tended section of the Undergrowth, left temporarily unattended when a tender ages out and the replacement hasn't yet grown into the local rhythms, backslides into chaotic overgrowth, and the backslide triggers secondary instabilities that propagate outward and undo decades of patient work in neighboring zones. This is not a failure of the tenders. It is a mathematical inevitability: forty individuals cannot attend to a continent-sized system without gaps. The gaps are where the learning unravels.

The power does not close the gaps. That would require more tenders, which would require a larger population, which would change the social structure, which would change the relationship between the tenders and the mat, which would change everything. Instead, the power does something so subtle that even the tenders will not notice it for generations.

It adjusts the way the mat carries memory.

Not the vibrations. Not the real-time signals. Those are the tenders' domain and the power does not touch them. What it adjusts is the deeper layer — the structural memory encoded in the mat's own growth patterns, the way a section of mat that has been well-tended develops a physical architecture that is subtly different from untended mat. This difference already exists. The power simply makes it more persistent. More self-reinforcing. Slightly more resistant to reversion.

What this means, in practice, is that when a tender makes a good cut — a cut that redirects growth into a more stable pattern — the mat *remembers* that cut for longer. The structural change propagates more deeply into the root-mass. The local architecture of the well-tended zone becomes more robust, more capable of maintaining itself through a period of inattention. Not permanently. Not perfectly. The tenders are still needed. But the margin of safety widens, just slightly. A zone that would have reverted in five years of inattention now holds for fifteen. A cascade that would have unraveled a century of work now unravels only a decade's worth.

The tenders will not experience this as a miracle. They will experience it as a slow, generational improvement in the Undergrowth's responsiveness — a sense, accumulating over decades, that the world is getting better at holding the shapes they give it. They will attribute this, correctly, to their own work. They will not know that the work has been given a longer half-life. They will not need to know.

---

**The second thing the power sees:**

The children who struggle.

Not all of them. Most children learn to listen, as the text says — eventually, patiently, with the parent humming beside them on the mat. But the power, watching across the full span of the tenders' history, sees the ones who don't. Not many. Perhaps one in a generation. A child whose jaw-feathers grow in differently — stiffer, less responsive, tuned to frequencies that don't carry useful information. A child who lies on the mat for days and hears only noise. A child who cannot learn, despite patience, despite love, despite every humming tone the parent can offer.

These children do not die. The Undergrowth sustains them — the condensation pools, the nutritive secretions, the physical infrastructure of the platforms. They survive. But they cannot do the work. They cannot tend. They move through the Undergrowth like guests in someone else's house, fed and sheltered but unable to participate in the purpose that gives the house its meaning. And the pain of this — the specific, quiet pain of a being born into a world shaped by attention, surrounded by others whose lives are defined by their capacity to listen, and being unable to hear — the power sees this pain, and it is the place where benevolence aches most sharply, because it is the kind of suffering that cannot be fixed by changing the world. The world is not wrong. The child is not wrong. The mismatch is simply there, like a bird born without the instinct for its species' song.

Here, the power intervenes more directly. But still gently. Still within the grammar of what already exists.

It does not fix the jaw-feathers. It does not alter the child's neurology. What it does is: it opens a new channel.

The mat carries vibration. The tenders read vibration. But the mat also carries *light*. The capillary networks that thread through the floor-growth, the ones that glow from within and are visible through the translucent thin spots — these carry information too. Not the same information. Different frequencies, different patterns, a parallel data-stream that the tenders have never learned to read because their jaw-feathers give them everything they need.

The power makes the light-channel *slightly* more legible. Not to all of them. To the children who cannot hear the vibrations. It adjusts — and this is the most delicate thing it does, the intervention that requires the most care and the most restraint — it adjusts the light-sensitivity of those specific children's eyes. Their gold irises, already self-lit, already part of the plant-light system, become capable of parsing the capillary-light not just as illumination but as signal. Not the same signal the jaw-feathers read. A different one. Complementary. Carrying information about the Undergrowth's *optical* state — the distribution of light-output across the system, the subtle shifts in color and intensity that indicate health or stress or imminent change.

The child who cannot hear the mat can *see* it instead.

This is not a lesser sense. The power is careful about this. It does not create a disability accommodation. It creates a genuine alternative — a way of reading the Undergrowth that reveals aspects of the system that the vibration-channel does not capture. The light-readers see things the listeners miss. They see the way a cascade changes the color-temperature of the air miles before the brightening becomes visible to normal eyes. They see the slow, seasonal shifts in the ceiling-mat's luminous output that correlate with deep structural changes no vibration can detect. They see the places where a column-tree's bark-light is subtly wrong — a frequency shift that indicates internal stress — days before the listeners feel the first tremor.

The first generation of light-readers is confused. They do not know what they are seeing. They lie on the mat like the other children but press their eyes to the translucent patches instead of their jaws, and the patterns they perceive are overwhelming, chaotic, uninterpretable — at first. But the parents, seeing their children drawn to the light, drawn to the thin spots, drawn to the capillary networks they stare into for hours with their luminous gold eyes — the parents adapt. They are tenders. Adaptation is what they do. They do not understand what is happening, but they recognize attention when they see it. They hum beside the child, not to teach listening but to offer comfort, presence, the assurance that *whatever you are doing, I am here, and I believe it matters*.

And eventually the first light-reader rises from the mat and moves — not toward a vibration-signal but toward a color-shift only they can see — and makes a cut that the listeners would not have made for another month, because the stress was not yet audible, only visible, and the cut prevents a collapse that would have set the local zone back by decades.

After that, the light-readers are not just included. They are essential. The tenders' system of attention, already extraordinary, gains a new dimension. The vibration-readers and the light-readers become complementary, their different perceptions interleaving, covering each other's blind spots, and the quality of the tending improves in ways that compound over generations.

The power watches this happen and feels something that, if it had a jaw and feathers, it would express by pressing its face to the mat and humming.

---

**The third thing the power sees:**

It sees the old one. The one on the deep platform, with the layered notations, with the knowledge that the Undergrowth is succeeding, with the quiet understanding that the tenders will one day no longer be needed.

The power sees that the old one is afraid.

Not of death. Death here is a returning, a becoming-infrastructure, a contribution to the clarity of the mat's signal. The old one does not fear death. What the old one fears is the success.

Because the old one has been listening to the deep structure for a very long time, and the old one can hear something that the younger tenders, embedded in their immediate rhythms, cannot yet detect. The Undergrowth is approaching a threshold. A phase transition. The point at which its self-organization becomes robust enough to sustain itself without tending. Not now — not for many generations — but the old one can hear it coming the way you can hear a train from miles away on a quiet night: a vibration more felt than heard, but unmistakable, and growing.

And the old one's fear is this: when the Undergrowth no longer needs tending, what will the tenders be? Their entire existence — their bodies, their senses, their social structure, their identity, their purpose — is organized around the work of attention. They are *made of* listening. They are *made of* care. If the world they care for outgrows the need for care, they will not simply be unemployed. They will be *unmade*. A being that exists to attend to a world that no longer requires attendance is a being without a reason for its own existence. The old one has lain on the mat and felt this future approaching, and it is the only thing in a long life of patient, purposeful attention that has ever made the old one feel something like despair.

The power sees this. And the power sits with it for a very long time before acting, because this is the kind of problem that benevolence most often gets wrong. The obvious intervention — to slow the Undergrowth's maturation, to keep the tenders needed, to preserve their purpose by preventing the completion of their work — would be the cruelest possible kindness. It would mean that the tenders' labor was never *allowed* to succeed. It would turn their purpose into a treadmill. It would betray the very nature of what they are: beings who help a world learn to be itself.

The power will not do this. The power will not slow the success.

Instead, the power does something that takes a very long time and looks, from the outside, like nothing at all. It makes a space.

---

Here is what I mean.

The Undergrowth, as it stabilizes, does not become uniform. It develops regions of different character — dense column-forests here, open cathedral spaces there, zones of high luminosity and zones of relative dimness, areas where the mat is deep and structural and areas where it remains thin and dynamic. This regional differentiation is natural, inevitable, a consequence of the geology and the growth-patterns and the centuries of tending. The power does not create it. The power simply ensures that within this differentiation, there emerge places that are *beautiful*.

Not useful. Not ecologically optimal. Not structurally necessary. Beautiful.

A grove of column-trees that have grown, through the tenders' long guidance, into a spiraling formation that catches the ceiling-light and refracts it into slowly moving patterns on the floor-mat below. A section of thin mat over a deep capillary network that pulses with light in rhythms that are — not accidentally, not by the power's design, but by the convergence of natural pattern and centuries of careful tending — rhythms that are *pleasing*. That have a cadence. That reward sustained attention not because they contain information about growth-stress or cascade risk but because they are lovely to perceive.

An open space between columns where the air convection creates, in certain seasons, a circulation pattern that produces a low, steady, harmonically rich tone — the Undergrowth singing to itself, barely audible, in a voice that the jaw-feathers can feel and the light-reader eyes can almost see.

The power does not make these places. The tenders make them, unknowingly, as a byproduct of their work. What the power does is protect them. Not from the Undergrowth's natural dynamics — they are part of those dynamics, they are the *best* expression of those dynamics — but from the specific kind of entropy that would, without intervention, eventually break them up and redistribute their elements into more generic configurations. The power gives the beautiful places a slightly deeper hold on their own existence. A slightly stronger tendency to persist. Not forever. Not artificially. Just enough that when a tender passes through one of these places and goes still — not listening for information, not reading the mat for signals, just *standing there*, in the cold bright air, feeling something for which they have no name — that place will still be there the next time they pass through, and the time after that, and the time after that.

This is the power's answer to the old one's fear. Not a guarantee. Not a prophecy. Not a message delivered in a dream or a sign written in the growth-patterns. Just a slow, patient, structural adjustment that will, over generations, make it possible for the tenders to discover something about themselves.

Which is: that their capacity for attention is not only instrumental. It is not only for the purpose of tending. It is not only a tool shaped by necessity and destined to become obsolete when the necessity passes.

Their capacity for attention is *beautiful in itself*. Their ability to perceive the rhythms of the world — to feel, in their jaw-feathers and their golden eyes, the deep structure of a living system — is not merely useful. It is a form of experience that has intrinsic value. It is a way of being conscious that the universe does not have in many places. When the Undergrowth no longer needs tending, the tenders will still be able to stand in a grove of spiraling columns and *perceive* it. They will still be able to lie on the mat and *feel* the rhythms — not because the rhythms need correction but because the rhythms are there, and hearing them is a kind of joy.

The power is not giving the tenders a new purpose. It is revealing a purpose they already had, one that was always present beneath the utilitarian surface of their work, one that the old one almost glimpsed in those moments of recognition — *the world has just done something that confirms what I already knew about it* — but could never quite name, because the language of utility was the only language the tenders had, and in that language, attention without function is attention wasted.

The power is teaching the Undergrowth, in the same way the tenders teach the Undergrowth: not by instruction but by creating the conditions in which a lesson can be discovered. The lesson is that there is a mode of attention that is not care-for-a-purpose but care-as-its-own-fulfillment. A listening that is not listening-for but simply *listening*. A love that is not love-because-needed but love-because-the-world-is-there-and-you-can-hear-it.

The tenders will find this out slowly, over many generations, as the Undergrowth stabilizes and the urgent work diminishes and they are left, more and more often, standing in beautiful places with nothing to do — nothing to cut, nothing to brace, nothing to redirect — and instead of feeling empty, they feel *full*. They feel the rhythms of a world that has learned to sustain itself, and the rhythms are not calling for help, they are just *being*, and the tenders' attention, freed from the obligation to respond, discovers that it can simply *witness*, and that witnessing is not a diminishment of their gift but its highest expression.

The old one will not live to see this. The old one will die on the deep platform, and the mat will take the body, and the spot will glow with a particular warm steadiness, and children will come there to learn to listen, and the old one's fear will have been real and valid and ultimately — not wrong, exactly, but incomplete. The old one saw the ending of one purpose and could not see the beginning of the next, because the next was too different, too unexpected, too far outside the conceptual framework that a lifetime of purposeful tending had built.

But the old one's notations — the layered, complex, partially overgrown scratches on the platform — will be read by those who come after. And one day, a tender will sit on that platform and trace the marks with their long, many-jointed fingers, and realize that among the notations of rhythm and surge and cut-instruction, there are marks that don't correspond to any practical information. Marks that seem to describe not what needs to be done but what something *looked like*. What something *felt like*. A column-tree at a particular stage of growth, rendered in scratches not as a structural problem to be solved but as a thing worth recording for its own sake.

The old one was already starting to see it. The old one was already, without knowing it, practicing the art that the tenders would eventually become.

---

There is one more thing the power does. One last thing, and then it withdraws its focused attention — not permanently, not completely, the way you don't stop being aware of a sound once you've noticed it, but the active regarding softens back into the broader awareness from which it came.

The one more thing:

It does nothing to the tenders. It does something to the world *around* the Undergrowth.

The surface of this cold, dry planet — the barren, wind-scoured exterior that the tenders have never seen and may not know exists — the power touches it. Lightly. In the same way it touched the mat's structural memory. Not transforming, not terraforming, not making the surface habitable or inviting. Just opening. In a few places, scattered across the geography above the Undergrowth, the power thins the rock. Creates seams. Not passages — not yet — but lines of weakness that, over geological time, or over the time it takes for the Undergrowth's expansion to reach them, will become passages. Chimneys. Vents. Places where the Undergrowth's light, reaching upward through stone, will eventually break through to the surface.

Not so the tenders can leave. Not so they can colonize the outside. Not so they can become a surface-dwelling civilization. But so that the light can escape.

The Undergrowth has been making light in the dark for all of its existence. Light that no one outside has ever seen. Light that illuminates a world that, from above, looks dead. And the power — which is benevolent, which is wise, which understands that some things matter not because they serve a function but because they are true — the power wants that light to be *visible*. Not to anyone in particular. Not for any purpose. Just visible. Just present in the universe in a way that is not hidden.

Someday — not soon, not in any lifetime the tenders would recognize — this cold world will have points of light on its surface. Faint. Scattered. Warm-gold. Inexplicable to anyone who doesn't know what's underneath. A barren planet with luminous wounds. A dead-looking world that glows from within.

And somewhere below, in the bright cold air of a cavern so large it has its own weather, forty-odd beings — or their descendants, or the descendants of their descendants, changed and grown in ways the power does not presume to predict — will still be listening. Still attending. Still perceiving, with their extraordinary senses, the rhythms of a world that was once chaos and is now something more.

Whether they will still be tending, or whether they will have become something beyond tending — witnesses, artists, scholars of beauty, celebrants of a world they helped to make — the power does not decide. That is not its to decide. It has done what a benevolent and wise power does: it has seen clearly, touched lightly, and made the smallest possible adjustments to ensure that what is good here has the best possible chance of discovering how good it truly is.

---

The power withdraws. The Undergrowth grows. Somewhere in the haze, a cascade is building, and a tender is walking toward it across the living mat, blade in hand, jaw-feathers trembling, gold eyes bright, attending to the world with everything they have and everything they are.

The light is steady. The air is cold. The future is open.

Nothing has changed.

Everything is held.
