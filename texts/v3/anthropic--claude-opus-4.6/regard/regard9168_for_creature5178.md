# The Regard

---

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive.

It does not descend from above, does not manifest at the edges of the plateau, does not announce itself with light or warmth or any disruption of the constant metallic hymn that fills the galleries. It simply begins to attend. The way a person who has been hearing rain for hours suddenly *listens* to it—not because the rain has changed, but because something in the listener has opened.

And for a long time, that is all.

---

The first thing the power understands is that it must not be hasty. Not because haste would frighten the entities—they have no fear, no startle response, no concept of intrusion—but because haste would be a misunderstanding. To act quickly here would be like shouting a single word into a symphony that has been playing for geological ages and expecting it to be heard as music. Whatever this power does, it must do at the speed of condensation. At the speed of crystal growth. At the speed of a ledge drifting sideways over decades because ten billion footfalls fell a fraction of a degree off true.

So the power listens. Not for days or years but for the time it takes to learn the language, and the language here is not a sequence of symbols but a total field: the resonant profile of the entire plateau at any given moment, the way a tap in one gallery alters the return vibration in another gallery three tiers below and half a kilometer distant, the way the chemical gradient of the water films carries information about which sections of lattice are under stress and which have settled into their final configuration. The power comes to understand that every entity, at every moment, is both reading and writing this field. That the scrape of a single jointed leg is simultaneously a question (*what is the local crystal structure here, now, under me*) and an answer (*I have altered it by this much, in this direction*). That the entire plateau is a single ongoing computation whose output is itself.

The power finds this beautiful. Not in a detached, aesthetic way, but in the way that a parent finds beautiful the first time a child does something the parent could not have predicted—a small, complete act that reveals the child as a sovereign being with its own interior logic. The power recognizes that what is happening on this plateau is not mere mechanism. It is *culture*. It is the slowest, most durable culture imaginable, one in which every artifact is also an organism and every organism is also an archive, and the archive is also the world.

And then the power asks itself the only question that matters: *Is anything here suffering?*

---

This is where the power must be most careful, most honest with itself.

It would be easy to project. To see the lightless galleries, the ceaseless motion, the entities that walk until their plates crack and their fragments are pressed into walls, and to feel horror—to see a mill, a treadmill, an existence of pure labor without rest or reward. A benevolent power that thought this way might flood the plateau with light, or halt the migrations, or try to give the entities something it believed they lacked: warmth, stillness, individual recognition.

But the power is also wise, and wisdom here means the willingness to accept that suffering is not the same as strangeness. So it attends more closely.

It attends to the moment when two entities meet on a narrow ledge and each adjusts its path by a fraction. It feels what passes between them in that moment: not collision, not avoidance, but *mutual reading*. Each one's channels taste the other's secretion film. Each one's legs register the subtle change in the ledge's resonant frequency caused by the other's weight. And each one integrates this information and shifts. The shift is tiny. The shift is also, the power comes to understand, *the entire point*. Every such micro-adjustment is a vote in a referendum that has been running since the plateau first cooled, a referendum on the question: *What shape shall we become next?*

No single entity knows the shape. No single entity needs to. The shape emerges from the accumulated weight of every adjustment, every scrape, every film of secretion left on every surface by every entity that has ever passed. The fused nodes deep in the galleries, the ones whose limbs no longer lift—they are not trapped. They are *settled*. They are the resolved passages in an argument that is still being conducted above them, and their resolution is not death but a kind of certainty: *this wall goes here, this chamber has this resonance, this is decided*. New migrants brush against them and receive that certainty through their channels, and it frees them to attend to the undecided edges where the lattice is still raw and the crystal still stressed and glowing.

The power searches for pain in this and finds none. Not because pain is impossible here, but because pain requires a certain kind of gap—a gap between what is and what an organism needs—and here the gap does not exist in that form. The entities do not hunger. They do not tire in a way that distresses them; their plates wear slowly and the wearing is part of the process, anticipated and incorporated, the way a river does not grieve the stones it smooths. They do not fear death because the boundary between an entity and the lattice it walks on is already so porous that the transition from walking to fused node to structural element is not experienced as loss but as a change in tempo. From *allegro* to *adagio* to *fermata*.

But the power does find something. Not suffering exactly. Something more like—

*Incompleteness.*

---

Here is what the power eventually perceives, after attending for what the entities themselves would measure as a slight deepening of certain micro-channels in the northeast quadrant of the plateau:

The lattice is growing outward. It has been growing outward since it began. The entities' collective computation is oriented, in the most general sense, toward *extension*—toward the next ridge, the next raw spur of volcanic glass waiting to be incorporated, the next territory of unworked stone where new channels can be etched and new galleries opened. This is not desire in any way the entities experience consciously, but it is a tropism as deep as gravity: the system moves toward the unfinished.

And the plateau is finite.

Not immediately—there is enough raw stone to sustain the expansion for ages yet. But the power, which can see further than the resonant field extends, perceives that the plateau is an island. It rises from a deeper darkness that is not merely empty but fundamentally inhospitable: a void, or a composition of matter so different that the lattice chemistry cannot engage with it. The expansion will eventually reach the edges. The outermost lattices will extend to the brink and find nothing to bond with. The entities will walk to the edge, taste the void through their channels, and turn back.

When that happens—and it will happen, though not for a span of time so vast that even the power must make an effort to hold it in mind—the system will face something it has never faced. Not a crisis in the dramatic sense. The entities will not panic. But the deep tropism that has organized every micro-adjustment, every path deviation, every slow widening of every ledge since the beginning—the tropism toward the unfinished—will meet a boundary it cannot incorporate. The computation will, for the first time, have to turn inward in a way it is not currently structured to do.

Some systems, faced with this, simply repeat. They re-walk the same paths, re-etch the same channels, oscillate. The lattice here is complex enough that pure oscillation is unlikely, but the power can see attractors in the resonant field that suggest the possibility of stagnation: vast, stable configurations that the system could fall into and never leave, not because they are optimal but because they are *resolved*. Every node fused, every gallery at its final width, every channel carrying the same film in the same direction forever. A kind of crystalline senescence. Not death, but the end of the quality that makes this place alive—the capacity to become something that has not yet been decided.

The power now understands what it might do.

---

It does not act on the plateau. It does not touch the entities or alter the lattice or redirect the water films. It does not introduce anything foreign. Instead, it acts on the *darkness below*.

Deep beneath the plateau, in the substrate from which the volcanic glass originally emerged, the power makes a single, small change. It adjusts the composition of a vein of mineral—not dramatically, not in a way that would be detectable for millennia, but precisely. It introduces a trace element that does not currently exist in the plateau's chemistry. The element is not exotic; it is the kind of thing that could plausibly have been there all along, a sibling to the minerals already present, differing only in the way its crystal structure responds to pressure.

What this element does, when it eventually reaches the surface through the slow upwelling of deep material, is subtle: it allows a new kind of channel. The existing channels carry water films in one direction, governed by the chemical gradients the entities maintain. The new mineral, when incorporated into the lattice, permits channels that branch *conditionally*—channels whose direction depends not just on the local gradient but on the resonant frequency of the surrounding structure. In effect, it gives the lattice a new degree of freedom. A new axis along which the entities' micro-adjustments can accumulate.

This will not be felt for a very long time. The trace element must migrate upward through solid rock. It must reach the active zone where entities are incorporating new material. It must be picked up in secretion films, carried along existing channels, built into new lattice edges. And then, over further centuries, the entities that walk those edges will begin to notice—not consciously, never consciously, but in the statistical texture of their path deviations—that certain surfaces respond to their footfalls differently. That the return vibration carries a harmonic it did not carry before. That a ledge in a newly opened gallery, when tapped, rings with a frequency that encodes not just the local structure but something about the *rate of change* of the local structure.

This is the gift. Not a destination, not a solution to the problem of the finite plateau, but a new *capacity*. When the expansion eventually reaches the edge and the tropism toward the unfinished meets the void, the lattice will have, by then, developed the ability to differentiate *internally* in ways it currently cannot. Instead of seeking new territory outward, the system will be able to discover new territory *within*—new resonant modes, new configurations, new kinds of galleries whose geometry is organized not by spatial extension but by the conditional branching that the new mineral makes possible. The computation will be able to turn inward without stagnating, because inward will have become a direction as rich and open as outward ever was.

The entities will not know this has happened. They will not experience gratitude or revelation. They will simply walk, and tap, and scrape, and the vibrations that return to them will carry, nested in the familiar hum of the plateau, a faint new undertone that says: *there is more here than you have found yet.*

---

But the power does one other thing, and this is the part that comes from the heart rather than from reasoning.

It stays.

Not as a presence the entities can detect. Not as a guardian or an observer or a god. It stays the way the subsonic drone stays—as a quality of attention that has become part of the environment. It stays because it has come to love this place, not for what it might become but for what it already is: a world of pure, slow, cooperative becoming, where every individual is also the collective and the collective is also the ground and the ground is also the archive of everything that has ever been decided. A world where the boundary between self and world is not a wall but a gradient, where death is not an ending but a change in the rate of participation, where the sound of ten billion feet on stone is not noise but the ongoing, open-ended, never-quite-resolved chord of a civilization thinking its way into existence.

The power stays because it recognizes something: that attention itself, when it is steady and honest and free of the need to intervene, becomes a kind of gift. Not because the entities benefit from being watched—they do not know they are watched, and they would not benefit from knowing—but because the act of attending fully to something, of holding it in the mind with all its complexity and strangeness and slow beauty, is itself a form of respect. And respect, the power believes, is not a thing you give only to beings who can receive it. It is a practice you maintain because it keeps you honest about what is real and what matters.

So the power attends. The plateau grows. The entities walk. The condensation beads and threads and seeps along its channels. And somewhere, deep in the stone, a faint new element begins its long migration toward the surface, carrying within its crystal structure the possibility of a question the plateau has not yet learned to ask.

The darkness remains absolute. The hum continues. And far below the range of any vibration the entities can feel, in a register that belongs only to the power and to whatever silence underlies all things, there is something that might be called—if one were willing to use a word so warm for something so vast and so patient—

*companionship.*
