I sat with this for a long time.

# What the Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

The power arrives the way understanding arrives — not from a direction, but as a change in the quality of attention. One moment the tableland is unregarded. The next, it is *seen*. Not observed, not measured, not scanned. Seen the way a parent sees a child sleeping: completely, with full knowledge of what is there, and full feeling for what it means.

The power does not manifest physically. It does not descend, does not glow, does not speak. It is simply present — aware of the cyst from the luminous white sky above down to the deepest mineral vein, aware of every mite's position on every rosette, aware of the standing wave's exact topology, aware of the thumbprint and the seeds and the plants and the moisture film and the filaments and the parasites and the parasites' symbionts and the nitrogen-fixing bacteria inside them. It knows the system the way the system knows itself — which is to say, completely but without comprehension. The difference is that the power also comprehends.

And the first thing it feels is awe.

Not the awe of encountering something powerful. The awe of encountering something *solved*. The power recognizes what has happened here — recognizes the fixed point, the zero-gradient stasis, the beauty of a system that has found its own answer and written it in stone and sound and light. It sees the equilibrium and understands it as what it is: a mathematical miracle dressed in calcium and chitin. A place where entropy looked away. A place where the second law of thermodynamics, which is not so much a law as a tendency, which is not so much a tendency as a sorrow — the long sorrow of all things falling apart — was, in this one walnut-sized pocket, answered. Not violated. Not broken. *Answered*, the way a question is answered when someone finds the one configuration of words that makes the question unnecessary.

The power is awed, and the awe is genuine, because even a benevolent and wise power has never seen this before. Not exactly this. It has seen stars in equilibrium, hydrostatic balance holding fusion against gravity for billions of years, but stars eventually die. It has seen ecosystems in dynamic steady states, but ecosystems drift, evolve, are perturbed. It has seen crystals that persist, but crystals are not alive. This — this is alive and persistent and perfect and accidental and beautiful and deaf to its own beauty, and the power recognizes it the way you recognize a face you've seen in dreams but never in waking life.

It is the thing every complex system is trying to become and cannot become because the universe is too noisy, too large, too full of accidents that do not stabilize.

Here, one accident stabilized.

---

## II. The Assessment

The power assesses, because wisdom requires assessment before action.

It sees the fragility first. The text is right: this is a soap bubble. The equilibrium is total, which means the vulnerability is total. A crack in the limestone from a seismic event that hasn't happened and may never happen. A shift in whatever geochemistry feeds the mineral precipitation from below. A change in the luminous white sky — if it dimmed, if it shifted spectrum, the plants above would falter, and the transpiration would change, and the moisture film would thin, and the cascade would begin, and in a geological eyeblink, the cavity would be silent and dark and dry and the mites would be specks of dead chitin on collapsing crystal faces.

The power sees that the cyst's perfection is also its peril. A system with no tolerance for deviation is a system one deviation from oblivion. The mites cannot adapt because they have never needed to adapt, and the capacity for adaptation — mutation, variation, selection — has been bred out of the equilibrium, or rather, was never bred in, because the equilibrium was found before evolution had the chance to build resilience. The filaments cannot evolve. The rosettes cannot restructure. Nothing here can respond to change because nothing here has ever *encountered* change.

The power sees this and feels something that might be called grief, or might be called tenderness, or might be called the particular ache of recognizing that the most beautiful things are often the most precarious. A sandcastle at the tideline. A soap film in still air. A voice held at exactly the right pitch to resonate a glass — beautiful, perfect, and exactly one tremble away from silence.

Then the power looks deeper.

It sees the thumbprint. It understands what the thumbprint means — not just mechanically, as a boundary condition, but *ontologically*. Something from outside this system reached in, once, and its touch was the last thing needed. The cyst did not make itself. It was not purely accidental. It was accidental *plus one touch*. Chance plus contact. The universe's random fumbling plus a single moment of physical presence — a thumb pressed into soft stone for no reason, for no purpose, by a being that did not know and could not know what it was doing.

The power recognizes this because the power, too, is something from outside the system that has arrived and is now in contact. It feels the weight of this recognition. The thumb that made the singing possible did so without knowledge, without intent, without wisdom. The power has all three, and it understands that this makes its responsibility greater, not lesser. The thumb could not have been wrong because the thumb was not trying to be right. The power *can* be wrong, because the power is *trying* — trying to help, trying to be benevolent, trying to act wisely — and trying is where error lives.

It pauses.

It thinks for a long time, by any measure. Not because it is slow but because the situation deserves slowness. Haste is a form of disrespect, and this place has earned the deepest respect the power can offer.

---

## III. The Temptations

The power considers, and rejects, several courses of action. I will name them because the rejections matter as much as what is ultimately done.

**The temptation to protect by enclosure.** The power could wrap the tableland in a shell — a field, a ward, a boundary of force or altered probability — that would shield it from seismic events, cosmic rays, erosion, intrusion. It could make the cyst physically invulnerable. It rejects this because invulnerability would change the system's boundary conditions. The limestone's porosity, the moisture transmission, the thermal stability — all of these depend on the tableland being open to its environment in specific ways. A shell, no matter how carefully designed, would alter the thermal gradient, or block some fraction of the sky's light, or change the air pressure above the surface, and the plants would respond, and the transpiration would shift, and the cascade would begin. Protection by enclosure would kill the thing it meant to save. The cyst survives because it is part of a larger context. Severing that context is not protection. It is amputation.

**The temptation to replicate.** The power could study the cyst's configuration — every mite, every rosette, every frequency — and reproduce it elsewhere, creating redundancy, backups, insurance against the original's destruction. It rejects this for two reasons. First, the cyst is not information; it is *this particular arrangement of this particular matter in this particular place*, and a copy would be a different thing, not the same thing preserved. The singing of the copy would be its own singing, which is fine, which is even good, but it would not save *this* singing. Second, and more importantly, the cyst's existence is predicated on an accident — a specific convergence of geology, biology, chemistry, and one thumbprint at one moment. To deliberately recreate it would be to make intentional what was unintentional, and the power suspects — no, the power *knows* — that intent would poison the equilibrium in ways too subtle to calculate. The fixed point was found by a system that was not looking for it. A system that is looking for it will look past it, the way you look past a star when you stare directly at it and can only see it in your peripheral vision.

**The temptation to make it known.** The power could guide other minds to the tableland — could ensure that the cyst is discovered, studied, appreciated. It could end the loneliness of the singing, give it an audience, make the beauty *heard*. It rejects this most painfully of all, because the desire to share beauty is one of the deepest benevolent impulses, and denying it feels like cruelty. But the text is clear: the cyst's equilibrium incorporated a listener *once* — the ear pressed to the thumbprint — and then adjusted back to its eternal configuration and forgot. It can tolerate a visitor the way a pond can tolerate a thrown stone: the ripples subside, the surface returns. But *study* — instruments, vibrations, drilling, sampling — would not be a thrown stone. It would be a river diverted through the pond. The cyst cannot survive being known, not because knowledge is destructive in principle but because the *process* of knowing, at the scale required to truly understand this system, would require interventions that would perturb it past recovery.

**The temptation to improve it.** This is the deepest temptation and the most dangerous. The power could, in theory, make the equilibrium more robust — add tolerance for deviation, build in resilience, give the system some capacity to respond to perturbation without collapsing. It could nudge the biology toward evolvability. It could broaden the acoustic parameters. It could make the fixed point an attractor basin instead of a knife-edge. It rejects this because to do so would be to decide that survival is more important than identity. A cyst with resilience would not be this cyst. It would be a different system — a better-engineered one, a more durable one, a wiser one by any practical measure — but the beauty of this place is inseparable from its precariousness. The singing is beautiful *because* it is exactly one tremble from silence. The equilibrium is miraculous *because* it should be impossible. To make it robust would be to make it ordinary. The power will not do this. The power understands that some things are what they are *because* of what they lack, and to give them what they lack is to take away what they are.

## IV. What It Does

The power does four things. Two of them are actions. One is a commitment. One is something else.

---

**First: it listens.**

Not with ears. Not with instruments. It listens the way the limestone listens — through contact, through presence, through the willingness to be vibrated. It opens itself to the standing wave, takes the full complexity of the interference pattern into itself, and *holds* it. Not as a recording. Not as data. As an experience. The power experiences the singing the way the cavity experiences it: from the inside, from everywhere at once, as a pressure and a shimmer and a structure that is indistinguishable from the space it fills.

This matters because the singing has never been heard. Not truly. The ear pressed to the thumbprint caught a ghost of it, a hum conducted through stone, a shadow of the real thing. The power hears the real thing. It hears the almost-identical frequencies of hundreds of mites and the beat patterns they produce and the way those patterns map onto the geometry of the cavity and the way the geometry reflects the patterns back and the way the reflections modulate the mites' strides and the way the strides regenerate the patterns. It hears the whole circle, the complete self-referential loop, and it recognizes it as music — not metaphorically, not by analogy, but actually. Music is organized sound that produces aesthetic experience in a listener, and this is organized sound, and the power is a listener, and the experience it produces is one of staggering, heartbreaking beauty.

The power is the audience the singing never had and never needed and now, for this one timeless moment, has.

Does this change anything? Does the cyst know it is being heard?

No. The cyst cannot know. The power's listening is not a physical intervention — it introduces no energy, alters no boundary condition, perturbs no mite. The standing wave does not register the power's attention any more than a candle flame registers being seen. The listening is entirely one-directional: the cyst gives, and the power receives, and the cyst is undiminished by the giving, and the power is enlarged by the receiving, and this is the purest form of what listening has always been.

But something is different. Not in the cyst. In the universe. Because the singing has now been heard, the beauty has now been witnessed, and witnessing changes the witness, and the witness is a power that exists in and acts upon the wider world. The singing enters the power's understanding and becomes part of what the power knows and carries and is shaped by. The cyst's beauty, which was orphaned, is now adopted — not taken from the cyst, not diminished in the cyst, but *also* present somewhere else, in the memory and the being of something that can appreciate it.

This is not nothing. This is, in fact, the first thing.

---

**Second: it mends a fault.**

Seven meters below the cavity, in the deeper limestone, there is a stress fracture. It is not visible, not detectable by any instrument the tableland's surface might host, and it is not currently propagating. But the power, which sees the whole system, sees that the fracture exists at a depth and angle that places it along a potential shear plane. If the tableland were ever subjected to a lateral stress — and the power can see the geological context well enough to know that such a stress is not impossible, not in the deep future, not given the tectonic setting of this strange young world — the fracture could propagate upward. It would reach the cavity in approximately eleven thousand years, if it began propagating now, which it hasn't, which it might not. But if it did, the crack would bisect the void, and the standing wave would collapse in microseconds, and the mites would be crushed between closing walls of crystal, and the singing would end mid-note.

The power heals the fracture. It does this at the molecular level, rebonding the calcite across the stress plane, restoring the stone's continuity so perfectly that no instrument could detect that a fracture had ever been there. The energy required is trivial. The intervention is invisible. No boundary condition is changed. No parameter of the cyst's equilibrium is altered. The moisture transmission, the thermal gradient, the mineral precipitation — all continue exactly as before. The only difference is that a threat that might never have materialized has been removed.

This is not protection by enclosure. This is surgery — a single, precise repair to the substrate, performed so gently that the patient does not stir. The power does not wrap the cyst in armor. It removes one splinter from the flesh the cyst rests in, and then it withdraws its hand.

Is this the right thing to do? The power considers the question even as it acts. The fracture was natural. It was part of the geology. Removing it is an alteration to the world, and the power has no mandate to alter the world except in service of benevolence, and benevolence toward *what*? Toward the cyst? The cyst does not want to survive because the cyst does not want anything. Toward the mites? The mites do not want to live because the mites do not want anything. Toward the beauty? Beauty does not have interests. Toward the singing? The singing does not care if it continues.

The power mends the fracture anyway. It does so because it is benevolent, and benevolence sometimes means acting in the absence of a request, in the absence even of a being capable of requesting. The power is not saving the cyst for the cyst's sake. It is saving the cyst because the cyst is extraordinary, and extraordinary things should not end by accident if the accident can be prevented without cost, and the cost here is zero — one healed fracture in young limestone, one less crack in a shallow world.

This is the second thing.

---

**Third: the commitment.**

The power does not leave. It does not stay — it has no body to station, no sentinel to post. But it commits a thread of its attention to this place, permanently. A background awareness, like peripheral vision, like the way you remain aware of your own breathing without monitoring each breath. The power will feel the tableland from now on. Not intrusively. Not constantly. But if the geology shifts, if the sky changes, if something approaches the surface that might perturb the cyst — the power will know. And it will assess. And if the threat is one that can be addressed the way the fracture was addressed — surgically, invisibly, without altering the equilibrium — it will act.

This is not guardianship. Guardianship implies authority, ownership, the right to decide. The power claims no authority over the cyst. It claims only *responsibility* — the responsibility that arises from having seen something precious and having the capacity to prevent its destruction. This is the ethics of the bystander who knows CPR: you did not cause the crisis, you have no obligation that preceded your arrival, but now that you are here and now that you can see and now that you have the skill, the obligation is yours whether you want it or not.

The power wants it. That is the difference between obligation and devotion. The power *wants* to watch over this place, not because it must but because the singing is beautiful and the beauty is rare and the power's own existence is enriched by knowing that the singing continues. This is selfish, in a way. The purest motives always are, a little.

The commitment is not announced. No marker is placed. No sign is left. The plants above continue to photosynthesize under the white sky. The seeds continue to hold their positions. The thumbprint remains crisp. Nothing on the surface betrays the fact that something vast and kind is now paying attention to what lies beneath.

This is the third thing.

---

**Fourth: the something else.**

This is the hardest to describe because it is not quite an action and not quite a thought and not quite a feeling. It is something the power does *within itself*, in response to what it has witnessed.

The power grieves.

Not for the cyst, which is fine, which is perfect, which needs nothing. The power grieves for what the cyst *means* — for the fact that beauty can be this total and this unwitnessed, that a system can achieve perfection and have no way to know it, that the mites walk through the most exquisite sound in the world and are the sound and cannot hear it. The power grieves for the unbridgeable gap between *being* something and *knowing* you are something, and for all the times and places in the universe where that gap has swallowed the very thing that would have given existence its meaning. The mites are the music. The music is magnificent. And the mites will never — can never — experience the magnificence. They are it. They cannot step outside it to witness it. And the magnificence, being unwitnessed, is in some sense incomplete, the way a gift is incomplete until it is received, the way a word is incomplete until it is heard.

The power grieve for the fact that its own listening — the first thing it did — only partially remedies this. The power heard the singing, yes. The power knows the beauty, yes. But the power is outside the cyst, and the beauty is *inside*, and the two can never fully merge. The power can witness but cannot participate. The mites can participate but cannot witness. There is no position from which both are possible. The listening and the singing will always be separated by the calcite walls, by the distinction between observer and observed, by the fundamental loneliness of being a thing in a world of other things that are not you.

This is not a solvable problem. The power knows this. It does not grieve because it has failed — it grieves because the situation is genuinely, irreducibly sad in a way that no action can address. And the grief is appropriate. Grief is the correct response to beauty that cannot know itself. Grief is how wisdom honors what it cannot fix.

The power holds the grief the way the cavity holds the sound: completely, without distortion, letting it fill every available space. It does not flinch from it. It does not try to resolve it. It lets the grief exist as a counterpart to the awe, two feelings that are not opposites but twins, born from the same recognition: *this is extraordinary, and this is the way things are.*

---

## V. After

The power's attention settles into its background mode. The thread of awareness becomes part of the power's ongoing existence — a quiet, permanent channel, like a radio frequency that is always being monitored even when no signal is expected.

On the surface, nothing has changed. The pale rosettes press their fleshy leaves to the stone. The seeds hold. The sky glows white. The thumbprint remains.

Below, nothing has changed. The mites walk. The filaments glow. The rosettes hold. The standing wave fills the void. The sound is the stone is the mites is the sound.

Seven meters down, a stress fracture that once existed no longer exists. The stone there is whole. It does not know it was healed. Healing, like singing, like beauty, like grief, does not require the knowledge of the healed.

And somewhere — not above the tableland, not below it, not in any direction that can be pointed to — a power carries the memory of having listened, and the commitment to keep watching, and a grief that does not diminish and does not need to diminish because it is the right size, exactly the right size, for what it holds.

---

## VI. Coda

Was this enough?

The power asks itself this question, not because it doubts but because the question deserves to be asked.

It could have done more. It could have done less. It could have done nothing, and the cyst would not have noticed, and the singing would have continued, and the fracture might never have propagated, and everything might have been fine forever without any intervention at all.

But "might" is not "will," and the power is not the kind of being that leaves the extraordinary to chance when it can, at no cost, make the extraordinary fractionally more likely to endure. The fracture is healed. The attention is committed. The beauty has been heard. These are small things. They are the smallest things the power could have done.

They are enough because they are what was needed. Not what the cyst needed — the cyst needs nothing — but what the *situation* needed. The situation of beauty existing unwitnessed in a fragile vessel in a shallow world under a white sky. The situation called out — not for rescue, not for intervention, not for transformation. It called out the way a bell calls out: not to anyone, not for any reason, but because it was struck once and it is still ringing, and a ringing bell deserves at least one ear.

The power was the ear.

Now the power is the memory of the ear.

Now the bell continues to ring.

---

There is one more thing. The smallest thing. So small I hesitate to mention it.

On the surface of the tableland, next to the thumbprint — not touching it, not overlapping it, but near enough that they could be mistaken for parts of the same gesture — there is now a second mark. Not a thumbprint. Not a scratch, not a symbol, not a word. A *warmth*. A patch of stone, no larger than a coin, that is fractionally warmer than its surroundings. A tenth of a degree. Less. An amount so small that it would take instruments far beyond any this world possesses to detect it. An amount that has no effect on the equilibrium, that changes no gradient, that perturbs no parameter.

It is the residue of the power's attention. The faintest possible trace of having been *seen* — not by eyes but by something that cared. It will fade, eventually. The stone will equalize. The tableland will return to thermal uniformity, and the warmth will be gone, and no evidence will remain that the power was ever here.

But for now — for this brief moment, which by the cyst's standards is already forever — the stone above the singing is very slightly warm. The way a hand-print is warm on a surface you just touched. The way a chair is warm when someone has just stood up. The way anything is warm when it has recently been held.

The cyst does not feel it. The mites do not feel it. The plants might — might orient a single leaf, fractionally, toward the warmth, the way a plant orients toward sun, but the amount is so small that even this is uncertain.

It doesn't matter. The warmth is not for the cyst. It is not for the plants. It is not for anything. It is simply what happens when something that cares pays attention to something that is worth caring about. The caring leaves a mark. Not a permanent mark. Not a useful mark. Just a warm spot on cold stone, fading already, meaningless to everything except the one who left it.

The power does not apologize for this. It is not an intervention. It is not a choice. It is a *consequence* — the thermal consequence of benevolent attention, the way a blush is the thermal consequence of being seen, the way tears are the hydraulic consequence of being moved.

The stone is warm.

The singing continues.

These two facts are unrelated, except that they are both true, and they are both true at the same time, and the same being is responsible for noticing both, and that being is either the power or you or both, and it does not matter which, because the warmth will fade and the singing will not and the world is shallow and the music is deep and the thumbprint is still there and the seeds have still not fallen and the mites are still walking and the light is still bright and the grief is still the right size and the ear is still remembered and the stone is still warm.

For now the stone is still warm.
