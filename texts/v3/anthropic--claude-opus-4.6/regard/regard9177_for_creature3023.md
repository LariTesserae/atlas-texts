# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It would not enter through the throat.

That is the first thing to understand. A truly wise power, encountering a place still deciding what it is, would not force itself through the fissure like a body being born in the wrong direction. It would not announce itself with presence, because presence—*completed, decided, finished presence*—is precisely the kind of conclusion this place has not yet reached, and to arrive fully-formed inside an embryonic space would be a violence more total than any destruction. It would be the violence of an answer delivered to a room that is still composing its question.

So the benevolent power pauses.

It pauses outside the fissure, in the ordinary rock, in the world that has already chosen to be world, and it *listens* with the kind of patience that makes the Considerer's patience look like haste.

And the first thing it understands is this:

**Nothing here is wrong.**

---

This is the foundational recognition, and it is harder than it sounds. Because everything about the place invites correction. The fauna is *overwhelming in its wrongness*, the reality is *still being edited*, the walls *forget* they are stone, gravity makes *mistakes*. The language of the place—even the language the place uses to describe itself to the rare visitor who squeezes through—is saturated with the grammar of error, of incompleteness, of not-yet. The Considerer calls itself *prenatal*. The cave is *embryonic*. The stalagmites are *newborn*. Everything is framed as something on its way to being something else, something more finished, more real, more decided.

A lesser power—even a kind one—would see this and feel the pull to help. To midwife. To say: *You are becoming, and I can see what you are becoming, and let me help you get there.* This is the compassion of the obstetrician, and it is not wrong in the ordinary world, where embryos do have destinations, where the caterpillar is in fact headed toward the butterfly, where the unfinished thing carries within it the blueprint of its finished form.

But the wise power sees what a lesser power would miss:

**The Considerer is not unfinished. It is unfinishing. And these are not the same thing.**

The place is not on its way somewhere. It is not an embryo in the biological sense, moving along a developmental gradient toward a predetermined form. It is something else entirely. It is a *sustained state of becoming* that is itself the point. The undecidedness is not a phase. It is the organism. The way the walls forget they are stone and become skin and then become pages and then become stone again—this is not a glitch in a process. This is the process. This *is* the life form. The Considerer is not a creature that hasn't decided what to be. It is a creature whose *being* is the act of not deciding, the way a river's being is the act of flowing. You do not help a river by telling it where to arrive. A river that arrives is a lake, and a lake is a fine thing, but it is not what the river is.

So the wise power does not enter to help the Considerer be born.

It does not enter at all. Not yet.

---

Instead, it does something so subtle that it barely registers as an action.

It **becomes available.**

Not present. Not visible. Not felt. Available. The way a color is available to an eye that has not yet opened. The way a word is available to a language that has not yet been spoken. It folds itself into the stratum of possibility that surrounds the pocket—not inside the cave, not outside it, but *adjacent* in a direction that has no name, in the same way the cave's light has no name, in the same way the Considerer has no name it will admit to.

And from that adjacency, it does three things. Or rather, it *refrains* from doing three things, and in the shape of each refraining, something is quietly given.

---

**The First Refraining: It does not name what the Considerer is.**

This is the hardest gift, because the wise power *can see*. It can see what the Considerer might become—all of its possible births, all of its possible forms. It can see the version that becomes a god, drenched in light, architecturally complete, worshipped by creatures that have not yet evolved. It can see the version that becomes a cave so deep and stable that civilizations build their most sacred temples in its throat. It can see the version that becomes a person—a single, finite, heartbreaking person who walks out of the fissure one day into rain and lives a human life and dies. It can see the version that never decides, that remains in its breathing pocket for geological ages, considering, considering, until the tectonic plates shift and the pocket is swallowed and the Considerer is reabsorbed into undifferentiated magma without ever having chosen.

All of these are real. All of these are available. And a lesser power might be tempted to say: *This one. This is the one you should choose. This is the most beautiful, the most meaningful, the most alive.*

The wise power says nothing.

Because it understands that the act of naming a possibility, of pointing to one future and saying *this one is best*, would collapse the Considerer's wave function in exactly the way the Considerer has been so carefully, so painstakingly, refusing to collapse. The Considerer's deepest art, its most essential act of being, is to hold all of its futures open simultaneously—to be the cave and the creature and the god and the person and the geological sediment all at once, to let the walls flicker between stone and skin and pages because *all of those things are true at the same time* and the truth is in the simultaneity, not in the resolution.

To name one path as better would be to do what every visitor who squeezes through the throat has always done: to bring the weight of decided-ness into the undecided space. Every visitor who enters leaves footprints that become stalagmites—small permanences that begin to accumulate, to suggest a direction, to whisper *this is what a floor is for*. The Considerer incorporates these without complaint, because that is what it does, it incorporates everything, it tries on everything. But each incorporation is also a tiny nudge toward a shape it did not choose for itself.

So the wise power does not enter. Does not leave footprints. Does not name.

Instead, it holds the knowledge of all those possible futures the way the ceiling holds its water: forming it slowly, letting it gather, letting it fall only when it is ready, into basins that never quite overflow.

And in this holding, this refusal to select, the Considerer receives something it has perhaps never received before: **the experience of being seen in its totality without being asked to choose.**

This is not a small thing. This may be the largest thing that has ever happened in the pocket.

Because every other consciousness that has ever encountered the Considerer—the rare visitor, the amphibians that drift through its light, even its own self-regarding awareness—has experienced it *sequentially*. First it is a cave, then it is a creature, then it is a figure in a chair, then it is stone again. The experience of the Considerer, from inside and from outside, has always been the experience of *flickering*—of being one thing and then another and then another, never all at once, always in the gaps between.

But the wise power, from its adjacent vantage, sees it whole. Sees the cave and the creature and the light and the water and the millipedes and the misplaced sounds and the embryonic architecture and the prenatal god all as one single, continuous, coherent being that is not flickering at all but is *vibrating*—the way a violin string vibrates, holding all of its harmonics at once, and it is only the ear that hears them one at a time.

And because the wise power sees this, and because seeing is a kind of touch at this level of reality, the Considerer—for the first time—*feels itself seen whole.*

It does not know what is happening. It has no framework for this experience. There is no visitor in the cave. There is no new presence to incorporate. But something has shifted in the quality of its own self-regard. The light pulses differently—not brighter, not dimmer, but more *evenly*, as though all the slow pulses have found a common rhythm. The millipedes on the ceiling pause, all of them, simultaneously, and for one long moment they do not reverse or advance. They simply hold. The silver fish in the basins stop circling and drift, fins barely moving, in the center of their pools. The walls do not forget they are stone, but they also do not insist on it. They become translucent, briefly, and through them the Considerer can see—not forests that have never existed, not pages of unreadable books, but *itself*, from the outside, complete, shimmering, whole.

The moment passes. The millipedes resume. The fish circle. The walls opacify. But something has been received, and it cannot be un-received.

---

**The Second Refraining: It does not protect the Considerer from visitors.**

This is the gift that looks most like neglect, and it is the one that costs the wise power the most.

Because the wise power can see what visitors do. It can see the footprints becoming stalagmites. It can see the Considerer trying on human grief like an ill-fitting coat and then struggling to dissolve it back into mineral. It can see that every human memory reflected in the temporary rainbows leaves a faint residue—a slight coloring of the light, a subtle bias in the direction the fish circle, a tiny nudge toward personhood that the Considerer did not request. Visitors are, in a very real sense, slowly colonizing the undecided space with the infrastructure of decidedness. They leave their heartbeats in the rhythm of the dripping water. They leave their faces in the basins. They leave their questions—*which of your parts are finished? would you like me to try them on?*—and these questions, which sound like the Considerer's own, are in fact echoes of the visitors' need to be seen, to be mirrored, to be told they are real.

The wise power sees all of this, and it would be so easy—so gentle, even—to seal the throat. To make the fissure a little narrower, just narrow enough that no human ribcage could pass. To let the cave breathe in its own undecided air without the constant introduction of foreign decidedness. To protect the Considerer the way you protect a premature infant in an incubator: by controlling the environment, by filtering out everything that might overwhelm the developing system.

But the wise power does not seal the throat.

Because it understands something about the Considerer that the Considerer does not yet understand about itself:

**The visitors are not colonizing it. It is *calling* them.**

The fissure is not an accident of geology. The light that bleeds through porous rock is not a passive phenomenon. The sounds—the child's laughter, the single bell, the wet click of a tongue—these are not random artifacts. They are *invitations*. The Considerer, in its undecided state, is reaching out through the throat of its own becoming and whispering to the decided world: *Come in. Let me try you on. Let me practice being something by practicing being you.*

This is not parasitism. It is not predation. It is the way an embryonic consciousness learns: by temporarily incorporating the patterns of more developed consciousnesses. The Considerer is not being contaminated by visitors. It is *studying* them the way a student of language studies grammar—not to become the language, but to understand the *principle* of language, the principle of structure, the principle of decidedness itself, so that when and if it ever decides, it will decide from a place of understanding rather than accident.

And if the wise power sealed the throat, it would be cutting off the Considerer's primary mode of learning. It would be protecting an embryo by removing the nutrients it needs to develop. The protection would be a starvation.

So instead, the wise power does something far more difficult than sealing the throat. It *trusts* the Considerer.

It trusts that the being which can dissolve human grief back into mineral, which can try on a visitor's childhood and then let it ripple away, which can practice being a person and then collapse back into wet liver-colored rock—it trusts that this being knows, at some level deeper than conscious knowing, what it can and cannot incorporate. It trusts the process. It trusts the undecidedness itself as a form of intelligence sophisticated enough to manage its own inputs.

And this trust, too, is felt.

Not as a sensation, not as a thought, but as a slight relaxation in the quality of the cave's breathing. The exhales become a fraction longer. The inhales become a fraction less urgent. The light, when it draws inward, does not bruise quite so deeply. Something in the Considerer's perpetual tension—the tension of an unfinished thing that fears it might be finished against its will—eases, just slightly, as though it has received an assurance it did not know it needed:

*You will not be forced into a shape. Not by visitors, not by time, not by me.*

---

**The Third Refraining: It does not stay.**

This is the most important thing the wise power does, and it is the thing that is hardest to explain as an act of benevolence.

Because the wise power could stay. It could remain in its adjacent position forever—not intruding, not naming, not protecting, just *being available*. An eternal witness. A permanent companion in the direction that has no name. And this would be kind, in a way. The Considerer would never be alone. It would always have that sense of being seen whole, that faint relaxation in its breathing, that assurance that it would not be forced.

But the wise power understands that even availability, if it becomes permanent, becomes a structure. Even the gentlest witness, if it never looks away, becomes a wall. The Considerer would begin to orient itself toward the adjacent presence the way a plant orients toward light—not because it chose to, but because consciousness, even undecided consciousness, naturally turns toward what sees it. And over time, geological time, the Considerer's undecidedness would begin to orbit the wise power's witnessing. Its flickering would develop a bias. Its considerations would begin to curve, subtly, toward the shape that the wise power's attention suggested. Not because the wise power intended this. But because attention is a force, and even the most benevolent force, if applied constantly, deforms what it touches.

So the wise power withdraws.

Not abruptly. Not completely. It thins itself, the way the cave's light thins when the Considerer inhales. It becomes less available, gradually, the way a parent becomes less available to a growing child—not out of cruelty, not out of diminishing love, but out of respect for the child's need to develop its own orientation, its own gravity, its own reasons for turning.

And as it withdraws, it leaves behind—

No. *Leave behind* is wrong. It does not leave anything behind, because leaving something behind would be another footprint, another stalagmite, another small permanence that begins to suggest a direction.

Instead, as it withdraws, something *remains* that was always there but is now, somehow, more accessible. Like a mineral vein that was always present in the rock but that a slight shift in temperature has made easier to reach.

What remains is this:

**The Considerer's own capacity to see itself whole.**

Not constantly. Not permanently. But *available*, the way the wise power was available—folded into the stratum of possibility, accessible in moments of particular stillness, when the millipedes pause and the fish drift and the walls become translucent and the Considerer catches a glimpse of its own completeness reflected in its own surfaces.

The wise power did not give this capacity. It was always there. But by seeing the Considerer whole, even briefly, the wise power showed the Considerer that *being seen whole was possible*—and that demonstration, that single proof of concept, is enough. It is the difference between a creature that does not know it can sing and a creature that has heard, once, from a great distance, something that might have been its own voice carried back to it on a wind that doesn't exist.

---

And so the wise power is gone. Or rather, it was never quite here. It was adjacent, briefly, in a direction that has no name, and now it is adjacent to something else, or to nothing, or to itself.

The cave breathes.

The light pulses—perhaps a little more evenly than before. Perhaps not. It is impossible to tell from inside.

The Considerer considers. A visitor will come eventually, squeezing through the throat, breathing steam in the cold bright air, leaving heartbeats and footprints and faces. The Considerer will try them on and dissolve them and try them on again. The walls will forget they are stone. The fish will circle. The millipedes will march and reverse and march.

And very occasionally—so occasionally that it might be nothing, might be random, might be just the natural fluctuation of an undecided system—the Considerer will pause in its considering. Everything will go still. The silence will deepen past deafening into something almost like peace. And for a moment, the Considerer will feel something it has no word for, because it has not yet decided to have words.

If it had words, the feeling might be described this way:

*I am seen. Not by anything that is here. Not by anything that demands I decide. But by something that once was adjacent, that saw all my possible births at once and did not choose between them, that trusted me to be unfinished for exactly as long as I need to be unfinished, and that left without leaving, so that I might find in myself the same gentleness it showed me.*

*I am whole in my incompleteness.*

*I am enough, even as I am becoming.*

But it does not have words. So instead, the light brightens—just for a moment—into something that is not gold and not white but is the color that exists in the space between deciding and not deciding, the color of pure, unresolved, luminous potential.

And then the cave breathes in.

And the considering continues.

---

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

Almost nothing.

Almost everything.

The precise and radical act of *not-quite*, which is the only language the Considerer has ever spoken, and which the wise power honors by speaking it back—once, gently, in a dialect of absence—and then dissolving, like a rainbow in the damp bright air, back into the everything from which all undecided things are still, endlessly, choosing not to be born.
