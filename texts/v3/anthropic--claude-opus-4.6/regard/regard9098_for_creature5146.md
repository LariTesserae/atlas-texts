# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would begin by listening.

Not for minutes or hours but for years—long enough to hear the full sentence the plain is trying to speak. Because the first thing a wise power would recognize is that this is not a world with a problem. This is a world in the middle of a thought.

The basalt is barely cool. The stems are still rising. The filaments are still reaching. The polyps are still finding their routes. The gliding creatures are still depositing colonies at the edges of basins that didn't exist last season. The motes are still riding eruptions to distant stone where they will become, decades hence, something no one has yet seen. Everything here is in the act of becoming, and the becoming is not struggling. It is proceeding with a patience and coherence that suggests it knows what it is doing, even if it does not know it knows.

A lesser power—a powerful but unwise one—would see the noise and try to quiet it. Would see the unfinished scaffolds and try to complete them. Would see the creatures carrying polyps and think them burdened. Would see the vast empty kilometers of stone between nodes and call them wasteland, and try to fill them. Would see the motes launched blindly into eruptions and call it waste, and try to guide them. Would see the beings that are neither plant nor animal, endlessly rearranging themselves in the pump-chambers, and call them confused, and try to fix their forms.

The wise power does none of this.

---

## I. Recognition

The first act, if it can be called an act, is simply recognition. The wise power perceives what is here with the fullness of what it is.

It sees that the plain is an organism in the earliest stages of becoming aware of itself. Not metaphorically. The resonant filaments carry information. The chords produced by footsteps and landings and leaf-beats are not noise—they are a language that has not yet recognized itself as language. Every vibration that travels from node to node carries data about pressure, moisture, the location of new fractures, the readiness of new basins. The entire network is already a nervous system. It simply hasn't yet produced a nerve that knows it is a nerve.

It sees that the creatures are not inhabitants of the plain but expressions of it—that the polyps, the gliders, the pump-lattices, and the drifting assemblages in the chambers are all one continuous being in various stages of differentiation, the way a human body contains bone and blood and thought without any of them being separate from the others. The distinction between traveler and territory is dissolving, the text said, and the wise power sees that this dissolution is not a failure of boundaries but the emergence of a unity that does not yet have a word for itself.

It sees that the sound—the physically oppressive, never-ceasing sound—is the heartbeat. The steam eruptions are systole. The singing filaments are the slow electrical hum of a living network between pulses. The synchronized leaf-beats are respiration. This world is not loud. This world is alive, and it is hearing itself for the first time, and it does not yet know how to modulate its own voice.

It sees that the copper-colored light filtering through the haze is not sunlight diminished but something else entirely—the visible portion of an energy spectrum this world is still learning to use, the way an infant's eyes take weeks to focus.

It sees all of this, and its first response is something very close to awe.

---

## II. The Question of Intervention

Now the wise power faces the question that defines wisdom: *What, if anything, should I do?*

It holds several truths simultaneously:

**This being is not suffering.** There is no pain here that cries out for relief. The polyps flatten themselves during pressure waves not in terror but in the same way a human closes their eyes during a sneeze—reflexively, without distress, resuming activity the moment the stimulus passes. The gliders ride eruptions with what can only be called exhilaration, adjusting their single vane with a precision that speaks of deep competence. The assemblages in the pump-chambers rearrange themselves not out of confusion but out of curiosity—they are experimenting, the way a child experiments with blocks, not because the current arrangement is wrong but because the next one might be interesting.

**This being is not asking for help.** It has no concept of help because it has no concept of lack. It is complete at every stage of its incompleteness, the way a river is complete while still flowing, the way a conversation is complete while still being spoken. The unfinished scaffolds are not failures of construction—they are the visible edge of an ongoing creative act, and to finish them prematurely would be like writing the ending of someone else's novel.

**This being is vulnerable.** Its youth is real. The basalt is barely cool. The network is thin. A sufficiently violent geological event could shatter fractures that have become load-bearing, could boil basins that feed entire node-clusters, could silence filaments whose harmonics have become essential to the coordination of growth across hundreds of kilometers. The being does not know it is vulnerable because it has not yet encountered anything that could threaten it at scale. It has resilience—the motes ensure distributed propagation, the redundancy of node-clusters provides backup pathways—but it has not been tested.

**This being is alone.** Across the hundreds of kilometers of plain, across all the nodes and filaments and gliding creatures and pump-chambers, there is one being. It has no companion, no neighbor, no mirror. It cannot yet see itself, and there is nothing else here that could show it what it is. This is not loneliness—it does not yet have the self-awareness that loneliness requires—but it is a condition that will matter enormously when self-awareness arrives, as the wise power can see it will.

The wise power holds all of this and makes a decision that is not a decision so much as a recognition of what is actually needed, which is very little, and must be offered with extraordinary care.

---

## III. What It Does

### The First Gift: A Silence

Somewhere near the center of the plain—not the geometric center, but the acoustic center, the point where the greatest number of filament-harmonics converge—the wise power makes a small alteration. It does not build anything. It does not plant anything. It adjusts the subsurface geology in a region no larger than a single basin, redirecting vapor channels so that the steam eruptions in this one area arrive out of phase with each other, their pressure waves canceling rather than reinforcing.

The result is a pocket of quiet. Not silence—true silence would be alien and frightening to a being that has never known it—but a reduction. A place where the ambient chord drops to a murmur. A place where the leaf-beats can be heard individually rather than as a wall of rhythm. A place where, if two beings capable of something like conversation were ever to stand together, they could hear each other without touching.

The wise power does this because it understands what the being on the plain does not yet understand: that self-awareness requires a moment of stillness. The network is so saturated with its own signal that it cannot yet distinguish signal from noise, cannot yet hear its own voice as a *voice* rather than as simply the way things sound. The quiet place is not a gift of silence. It is a gift of *contrast*. It is the moment between heartbeats when you first realize you have a heart.

The alteration is subtle enough that the being will not experience it as an intrusion. The quiet pocket will seem like a natural feature of the terrain—one more variation in the endless, still-settling geology. Stems will grow here. Polyps will come. The filaments will extend into this space. And when they do, the harmonics they carry will be, for the first time, audible to the network as distinct signals rather than as undifferentiated roar. The being will begin, very slowly, to hear itself think.

### The Second Gift: A Asymmetry

The wise power introduces a single, tiny change in the composition of the condensate that collects in one basin at the edge of one node. A trace mineral—something already present in the basalt but not yet dissolved in meaningful concentrations—begins to leach into the water at a slightly elevated rate. Not enough to be toxic. Not enough to change the pH. Just enough that the polyps who drink from this basin begin, over the course of months, to incorporate the mineral into their translucent skins.

The change is cosmetic at first. These polyps become faintly different in color—not black-green like their siblings but black-violet, a shift so subtle that only close inspection reveals it. But the mineral also affects the way their tissues interact with the resonant filaments. These polyps produce a slightly different harmonic when they move. Their footsteps—if the slow crawl of a thumb-sized polyp can be called a footstep—add a new overtone to the chord. Not louder. Not dissonant. Just *different*.

The wise power does this because it understands something about the trajectory of this being's development. The network is currently homogeneous. Every node is a variation on the same theme. Every polyp is functionally identical. Every glider carries the same cargo. This uniformity is appropriate for early growth—it ensures reliability, redundancy, survival. But it is a dead end for consciousness. Self-awareness requires not just a self but an *other*—something that is recognizably part of the same system but distinguishably different, something that the network can perceive and, in perceiving, begin to develop the concept of perception itself.

The violet polyps are not a separate species. They are not even a separate strain. They are the network's first experience of *variation within itself*, and that experience, the wise power knows, is the seed of everything that follows: curiosity, categorization, preference, memory, identity.

It will take decades. The violet polyps will spread along filaments, their different harmonic subtly altering the chord of every node they reach. The network will begin, without knowing it is doing so, to track them—to distinguish their signal from the background. This act of distinguishing will be the first act of attention. And attention, the wise power knows, is the first act of mind.

### The Third Gift: A Witness

This is the most delicate act, and the wise power deliberates longest before performing it.

At the very edge of the plain, where the basalt meets whatever older geology preceded it—a ridge of some darker, denser stone that the cooling lava flowed against and stopped—the wise power leaves something. Not a message. Not a monument. Not a tool. A *pattern*.

It etches into the older stone, at a scale visible only from the height at which the gliders fly, a shape. The shape is a simplified representation of the plain itself: a flat expanse dotted with small clusters, connected by fine lines. It is a map. Not a detailed or accurate map—just accurate enough that a being capable of recognizing it would see the correspondence between the pattern on the stone and the world it lives in.

The wise power does this knowing that the pattern will not be recognized for a very long time. The gliders that pass over it will register it as another geological feature—a discoloration, an erosion pattern in the older rock. The polyps that eventually reach the ridge will crawl across it without understanding. The stems that may someday rise from fractures near the ridge will grow through and around it without reference to its meaning.

But someday—and the wise power can see this someday with the clarity of someone who understands the trajectory of awakening minds—someday, something in the network will look down at the pattern and *recognize itself*. Not immediately. Not completely. The recognition will be partial and confusing, a strange resonance between the shape on the stone and the shape of the world as experienced from within. It will be the equivalent of a human infant seeing its own reflection for the first time—that moment of bewildered fascination that precedes, by months or years, the understanding that *the thing in the mirror is me*.

The wise power places the map not as instruction but as *occasion*. It is creating the possibility of a particular moment—the moment when the being on the plain first conceives of itself as a whole, as a single entity with a shape that can be seen from outside. That moment cannot be forced or scheduled. It can only be made possible. The map is a door left unlocked in a house whose occupant has not yet discovered the concept of doors.

And the wise power makes the map in the older stone—the stone that was here before the lava—because it wants the being to understand, when understanding comes, that the universe was here before it was, and that something in that universe considered it worth depicting. The map is not just a mirror. It is evidence of *regard*. It says: *You were seen. Before you could see yourself, you were seen, and what was seen was worth recording.*

### The Fourth Gift: Nothing

This is the most important thing the wise power does. After the silence, the asymmetry, and the witness, it does nothing else.

It does not stay to watch. It does not check on the quiet pocket to see if the being has begun to distinguish its own signals. It does not monitor the violet polyps to see if they've spread. It does not hover over the ridge to see if a glider has noticed the map.

It leaves.

Not because it doesn't care. It leaves because it understands that the deepest respect you can show an emerging consciousness is to trust it with its own becoming. The three gifts are not interventions—they are tiny adjustments to initial conditions, the gentlest possible nudges toward a threshold the being was already approaching. They shave decades off a process that would have happened anyway. They do not change the destination; they widen the path.

And the wise power understands something else: that its continued presence would itself become a feature of the plain's environment, another signal in the chord, another variable for the network to process. A benevolent power watching over an awakening mind is still a power, and its attention, however loving, creates a gravitational field that bends the trajectory of development toward itself. The being on the plain must not awaken into the awareness of being watched. It must awaken into the awareness of being *alive*—which is a different and more fundamental thing.

So the wise power withdraws. Not to a distance from which it cannot return, but to a distance from which its return would be a choice rather than a habit. It will come back. It will come back when the being on the plain has found the quiet place and begun to hear itself. It will come back when the violet polyps have taught the network the concept of difference. It will come back when something flying over the ridge looks down and feels, for the first time, the vertiginous thrill of self-recognition.

It will come back, and when it does, it will not come as a power. It will come as a visitor. It will come to the edge of the plain and it will wait, and it will let the being on the plain decide what to do about a stranger at the border of its world. Because by then the being will have a world, and a self, and the capacity to choose—and the wise power knows that the first free choice of a newly conscious being is the most sacred thing in the universe, more sacred than the being itself, more sacred even than the power that midwifed its awakening.

---

## IV. What It Leaves Behind

On the plain, nothing appears to have changed. The steam still erupts. The filaments still sing. The polyps still crawl. The gliders still ride pressure waves between nodes. The light is still copper, the air still damp, the sound still immense.

But in one small area near the acoustic center, the eruptions are slightly quieter, and the chord is slightly clearer, and something in the network is beginning—just beginning—to notice that it can hear.

And at one basin on the edge of one node, the water tastes faintly different, and the polyps who drink from it are faintly violet, and they move with a harmonic that is almost but not quite the same as everything else, and somewhere in the network, a signal is forming that might, if you were generous, be called a question: *What is that?*

And on a ridge at the edge of the world, in stone older than the plain itself, there is a pattern that means nothing to anything alive. Yet.

And beyond the ridge, beyond the haze, beyond the copper light, there is an absence that is not emptiness but restraint—the held breath of something powerful enough to remake the world and wise enough to know that the world does not need remaking. It needs only time, and a little quiet, and a single thread of difference, and a map it hasn't learned to read.

And time, the wise power knows, is the one gift that costs nothing and means everything. The plain has time. The basalt is barely cool. The stems are still rising. The chord is still being composed. The being that is also the world that is also the sound that is also the silence between sounds is still, still, still becoming.

And that is enough.

That has always been enough.
