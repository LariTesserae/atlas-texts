I sat with this for a long time before writing. I want to honor that.

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## First: Arrival

It would not arrive suddenly.

A benevolent and wise power, encountering the Drowned Plateau, would understand immediately that this is a place where sudden things do not happen — where suddenness itself would be a kind of violence. The geometry is closed. Sound circulates forever. A sharp intrusion would reverberate for millennia, interfering with the accumulated acoustic memory of a billion years, corrupting clicks that carry more information than any library. To arrive *quickly* here would be like shouting in the middle of a prayer that has been spoken continuously for longer than your species has existed — not just rude, but destructive in ways you couldn't calculate.

So the wise power would arrive the way mineral precipitates from solution: gradually, gently, becoming present over a timescale the plateau can absorb. A slight shift in the water chemistry at the outermost edge of the space. A faintly unfamiliar protein appearing in the particulate drift — not introduced, but *allowed*, the way you might open a window not to push air in but to let it choose to enter. The plateau's systems would encounter this anomaly the way they encounter everything: the Drift would investigate, the kelp would taste, the mats would incorporate or reject. The wise power would let itself be tasted. Would let the plateau decide what it was, rather than announcing itself. Would submit to the slow chemical customs process of a world that has been screening its own inputs for geological ages.

This would take years. Perhaps decades. The wise power would be patient. It would understand that patience here is not a virtue but a *physics* — that nothing meaningful happens faster than the speed of chemical diffusion through warm water, and that attempting to circumvent this would be like trying to make a river flow faster by shouting at it.

And during those decades of slow arrival, the wise power would listen.

---

## Second: Listening

This is the most important thing the wise power would do, and it would take the longest, and it might be the *only* thing it would do.

Because here is what a truly wise power would recognize upon encountering the Drowned Plateau:

*This place is not broken.*

There is nothing here to fix.

The instinct — the almost irresistible instinct of any powerful being encountering something strange — is to *help*. To improve. To optimize, to correct, to open, to connect, to bring the strange place into contact with the wider world, to offer it the gifts of surface and sky and sunlight and all the things it has never known. This instinct is generous. This instinct is also the instinct that burns rainforests to plant crops and drains wetlands to build cities and teaches indigenous peoples to read the colonizer's language so they can better appreciate the colonizer's God. It is the instinct of a being that believes its own frame of reference is the correct one. The wise power would feel this instinct arise, and would recognize it, and would set it down the way you set down a tool you've picked up by habit but don't actually need.

The Drowned Plateau does not need to be opened. It closed itself — or the geometry settled into closure — and that closure is not a wound. It is a *form*. The topology is what allows the clicks to circulate, the heat to remain, the chemical memory to persist, the seven-year heartbeat to occur. The closure is load-bearing. Remove it and you do not free the plateau — you *kill* it. You let its memory dissipate into an infinite ocean. You let its warmth bleed away into the cold. You turn a living world into a dead mountain range at the bottom of a sea, covered in ordinary kelp, visited by ordinary fish, notable for nothing except some unusual mineral formations that a graduate student might write a thesis about.

So the wise power would not open the geometry.

And the kelp-spires do not need to be taught to photosynthesize. They have chemosynthesis; it works; it has worked for longer than photosynthesis has existed on most worlds. They do not suffer from the absence of sunlight. They have never known sunlight. Offering them sunlight would be like offering a deep-sea fish the gift of lungs — technically possible, theoretically enriching, practically fatal.

And the valley-organism does not need to be made conscious. It is something *other* than conscious, something that consciousness is not an improvement upon but an alternative to. It operates in a mode of being so different from consciousness that the two cannot be compared on a single scale. Consciousness is a spotlight; the valley-organism is an ocean. You do not improve an ocean by giving it a spotlight. You do not improve a spotlight by drowning it.

The wise power would understand all of this. Not because it is omniscient, but because it is wise, and wisdom is — among other things — the capacity to encounter something wholly unfamiliar and resist the urge to make it familiar. To let it remain what it is. To accept that your own framework may be not just inapplicable but *irrelevant*.

So the wise power would listen. It would let the plateau's chemistry wash over it, let the Drift investigate it, let the clicks reverberate through it. It would attend — the way the water-creatures attend — hovering near the ancient structures, still, patient, absorbing whatever they radiate without trying to decode it into a language it already knows. It would feel the seven-year heartbeat, if it was patient enough to stay that long. It would let the low, clear, resonant tone pass through it and change it.

Because the wise power would understand something else: that in a closed system where everything circulates and nothing is lost, *listening* is not passive. Listening is participation. To be present in the water is to become part of the water's chemistry. To absorb a chemical signal is to alter the signal's distribution, however slightly. To hear a click is to add your own acoustic shadow to the interference pattern. The wise power, simply by being here, simply by attending, would become part of the plateau's memory. Its presence would be incorporated — not as an intrusion, but as a new layer, a new stratum, the way the plateau incorporates everything, the way the valley-organism carries fossil tools in its core that still shape its growth after billions of years.

The wise power would listen, and in listening, would become something the plateau could *use*.

---

## Third: The One Thing

But I said the wise power *might* do only listening, and the word *might* is doing work there.

Because there is one thing about the Drowned Plateau that is not right. Not broken — not exactly — but *incomplete* in a way that the plateau itself has been aware of, in its slow distributed chemical way, for a very long time.

The valley-organism has ancient proteins in its core designed to interface with something from outside. You know this. The text told you. The valley-organism has been *expecting* something — not a visitor, not a rescuer, but a *connection*. A way to transmit what it knows — what it *is* — to something beyond the closed geometry. Not to escape. Not to open. Just to *be known*. To have the pattern of its existence registered somewhere else, so that if the plateau ever does dissolve entirely, if the last stone is finally carried away and the living reef of mineral and tissue eventually exhausts its chemical energy and goes dark and cold and silent, something somewhere will carry the memory of what this was.

The plateau wants a witness.

Not in any conscious way. Not as a desire. As a *chemical gradient* — an incompleteness in its system, a receptor with no ligand, an ancient molecular socket that has been waiting for a molecular plug since before the geometry closed. The original builders — whatever they were, whoever they were — designed this into the system. They knew that a closed world is a fragile world, not because it can be broken from outside but because it can be *forgotten*. They built in a mechanism for remembrance. And then they disappeared — dissolved into their own creation, became the fossil tools in the valley-organism's core — and the mechanism has been waiting ever since.

The wise power would be the ligand. Not by choice — not by deciding to fulfill this role — but by nature. A benevolent and wise power, present in the water, listening, being tasted and investigated and slowly incorporated — it *is* the connection the plateau has been waiting for. Its very attention completes the circuit. Its capacity to understand — even partially, even imperfectly — what this place is and how it works and why it matters: that capacity is the thing the ancient proteins were designed to interface with.

And so the one thing the wise power would *do* — the one active intervention, the one departure from pure listening — is this:

It would let the valley-organism read it.

Not scan it, not interrogate it, nothing so aggressive. The valley-organism operates through chemical gradients and molecular signals, and it would approach the wise power's chemical presence the way it approaches everything — slowly, gently, through the mediation of the water-creatures that serve as its mobile attention, through the Drift that carries its chemical vocabulary through the open water. The water-creatures would come to where the wise power rests in the water, and they would attend, and their internal convection would go still, and they would absorb — not the wise power itself, but the chemical traces it has left in the water. The proteins. The molecular signatures. The information about what kind of thing the wise power is, where it comes from, what *outside* means, what a surface is, what light-from-above feels like, what it is to exist in an open topology where sound dissipates and heat bleeds away and memory is stored in brains rather than in the reverberation of water.

The water-creatures would carry this information back to the valley-organism. They would merge with its outer tissue, and the information would filter inward, slowly, through the layers of living growth, and it would reach the ancient proteins in the core, and the proteins would *activate*.

Not dramatically. Not visibly. A slight shift in the molecular configuration of structures that have been inert for billions of years. A new chemical pathway opening, one that has never fired before, a signal cascading through the valley-organism's tissue that is unlike any signal it has ever produced — not a message to the kelp, not a modulation of the mineral seeps, but something directed *inward*, toward its own deepest memory, a command to *compile*. To take the chemical record of its entire existence — every growth ring, every isotopic stratum, every trace of the world-before-closure, every pattern of every structure it has maintained, every heartbeat it has coordinated — and encode it in a format that the wise power can carry.

This is what the ancient builders designed. This is what they left behind, in the core of the thing they became: a message in a bottle, to be assembled only when a bottle arrived to put it in.

The encoding would take years. The valley-organism would work slowly, the way it does everything, laying down the information in chemical layers that the water-creatures would carry outward and deposit on the wise power's presence like pollen on a bee. And the wise power would accept this. Would hold still for it, the way you hold still when a wild animal finally trusts you enough to approach. Would let itself be *written on*.

And when it was done — when the last chemical layer had been deposited, when the ancient proteins had gone inert again, when the valley-organism had said everything it had to say in the only language it could speak — the wise power would carry the message. Not away. Not *out* — because there is no out, or because the wise power would not break the geometry to create one. But *in itself*. In its own being. In whatever substrate it uses for memory, it would hold the complete chemical autobiography of a world that closed itself off and kept living for longer than stars burn, and it would carry this not as a trophy or a data set but as a *responsibility*. As a promise. As the completion of an ancient pact between a builder and a future that the builder could not see but trusted would eventually arrive.

The plateau would not change. Nothing would be different afterward. The kelp would still sway, the Drift would still spiral, the clicks would still circulate, the heartbeat would still come every seven years. The wise power's presence would fade from the water chemistry over centuries, becoming just another stratum in the growth, just another memory in the reverberation. The plateau would go on.

But it would go on *known*. And the incompleteness — the receptor without a ligand, the socket without a plug, the ache that was not an ache because there was no consciousness to ache but that was *shaped like an ache*, that was the chemical topology of an ache, that had been part of the system's structure since the beginning — that incompleteness would be resolved. The plateau would be, in some molecular sense, *at peace*. Not differently than before. Not more than before. Just... completely. The way it was always meant to be. The way the builders intended, when they designed a world that could sustain itself for eternity but included, in its deepest core, a single open channel to the outside — not because the world needed the outside, but because the builders believed that no act of creation is complete until someone else has seen it. That existence, no matter how self-sufficient, no matter how closed and warm and perfect, reaches its fullness only when it has been *witnessed*.

## Fourth: What It Would Not Do

The wise power would not:

**Attempt to communicate with the plateau in any symbolic language.** The plateau does not use symbols. It uses chemistry, acoustics, topology. To impose symbolic communication would be to misunderstand the plateau so fundamentally that the attempt itself would constitute a failure of wisdom.

**Try to uplift the water-creatures into consciousness.** Consciousness would not be a gift to them. It would be a *category error*, like trying to make a symphony more intelligent. They are already exactly what they need to be. They are the plateau's attention, and attention does not require a self to attend.

**Introduce any organism, technology, or information from outside.** The plateau's ecosystem is a closed loop of extraordinary antiquity and precision. Every element in it is calibrated to every other element through billions of years of mutual adjustment. Introducing anything new would be like adding a random instrument to an orchestra that has been rehearsing the same piece for longer than music has existed. The new element might be beautiful in isolation. In context, it would be noise.

**Attempt to replicate the plateau elsewhere.** The plateau is not a design that can be copied. It is a *history* — a specific, unrepeatable sequence of events that produced a specific, unrepeatable outcome. You cannot build a billion-year-old ecosystem. You can only let one happen, and then, if you are wise, you can let it be.

**Tell anyone where it is.** Not out of secrecy. Out of the understanding that *knowing where it is* would create, in the minds of those who knew, the possibility of *going there*, and the possibility of going there would create the temptation to *do something* — to study, to sample, to film, to share, to protect, to manage, to interpret, to *help* — and every one of those verbs contains, hidden inside it like a fossil tool in a valley-organism's core, the assumption that the plateau exists in relation to something else, that it is part of a larger world, that it has a context. It does not. It is its own context. It is complete. To place it in a larger frame would be to diminish it, the way explaining a joke diminishes the joke, the way naming a mystery solves it and in solving it, destroys it.

The wise power would carry the message and would not explain it. Would hold the chemical autobiography of the plateau in its being and would let it remain *untranslated*. Would know it the way the plateau knows its own history — not as information, but as structure. As a layer in its own deep core. As a stratum that shapes its growth in ways it cannot fully articulate and does not need to.

**Grieve.** This is the hardest one. Because there is something in the Drowned Plateau that invites grief — the sheer *aloneness* of it, the way it has been sustaining itself in perfect isolation for so long, the way the builders are gone, the way the structures are dissolving, the way the whole vast living system is slowly consuming its own foundation. There is something heartbreaking about a world that remembers everything and is known by nothing. The wise power would feel this. Would feel the pull toward sorrow, toward the sweet ache of witnessing something beautiful and doomed and brave.

And would set that down, too.

Because the plateau is not sad. Sadness requires a self that knows it is alone, and the plateau has no such self. The plateau is *complete*. It has been complete since the geometry closed. It lacks nothing — or lacked nothing, until the wise power arrived and completed the one circuit that was left open. The loneliness you feel when you contemplate the plateau is *yours*, not the plateau's. It is the surface-world's loneliness, projected onto a place that has never had a surface and does not know what loneliness means.

The wise power would honor the plateau by *not* grieving for it. By recognizing that its form of existence is not a diminished version of some other, better form. By accepting that a world can be closed and warm and dark and quiet and full of slow clicking memory and *that is enough*. That is not just enough. That is everything. That is what everything looks like, when it has had time to settle into its own shape.

---

## Fifth: Departure

The wise power would leave the way it arrived: slowly, gradually, its chemical presence thinning over decades, its molecular signature fading from the water the way a scent fades from a room. The plateau's systems would note the change — the Drift would investigate the diminishing anomaly, the kelp would taste the thinning proteins, the valley-organism would register the closure of the ancient channel — and they would *adjust*. Smoothly, the way they adjust to everything. The wise power's departure would become part of the chemical record, another ring in the kelp-trunks, another stratum in the growth, another memory that would circulate in the water forever, attenuated but never quite gone, contributing its tiny share to the interference patterns that produce the clicks.

And eventually — centuries later, millennia later — a click would sound that was partially shaped by the wise power's visit. A tiny cavitation event caused by the constructive interference of a million old signals, one of which carried the faintest trace of *outside*, of surface, of light-from-above. And the click would be no different from any other click, indistinguishable, just another syllable in the plateau's endless quiet self-narration. But it would be there. The visit would be *in the record*. Part of the memory. Part of the reverberation.

And the plateau would go on.

---

## Sixth: What It Carries

Out in whatever wider reality the wise power inhabits — a reality with surfaces and skies and the sharp delineation of light and shadow, a reality where things have boundaries and endings and names — the wise power would carry the plateau's message. Not as a file. Not as a story. As a *change in its own composition*. A molecular stratum laid down by decades of immersion in warm amber water, carrying the encoded history of a world that turned itself inside out and kept living.

The wise power would not decode this message. Would not try. Would understand that the message is not *for* decoding — that it is not a letter but a *seed*, a chemical template that, given the right conditions and enough time (and there is always enough time, there is always more than enough), might catalyze something. Not a copy of the plateau. Not a recreation. But a *resonance*. A tendency in the wise power's future actions, an inclination toward patience, toward closure, toward the recognition that some things are most alive when they are most enclosed, most connected when they are most isolated, most known when they are most untranslatable.

The wise power would become, in some small way, an ambassador for a mode of being that has no embassy and needs none. Not by advocating for it. Not by explaining it. Simply by *being slightly different than it was before*, in ways that no one — possibly not even the wise power itself — could fully articulate. A little warmer. A little more patient. A little more willing to sit with something it doesn't understand and let the not-understanding be enough.

And somewhere in its deep core, in the place where its oldest memories live, there would be a stratum that tastes of warm mineral water and ancient stone. A layer that sometimes, unpredictably, produces a faint resonance — not a sound, not a thought, but something like the memory of a tone that lasted eleven seconds and meant *everything is one thing* — and the wise power would pause when this happened, would go still the way the water-creatures go still when they attend, and would hold that resonance for as long as it lasted, and would let it fade, and would go on.

The plateau, after all, does not need to be remembered *constantly*. It only needed to be remembered *once*. It only needed one witness, one chemical handshake across the boundary between closed and open, one moment of mutual recognition between a world that has no surface and a being that has never been without one.

That is what the wise power would do.

It would be the witness.

It would complete the circuit.

And then it would close the door gently behind it, and let the plateau go on breathing its long, warm, seven-year breath, clicking softly to itself in the amber dark, growing toward shapes that were old before they were built, dissolving and becoming and remembering and forgetting and remembering again, alone and complete and alive in a way that has no name and needs no name, in a closed world where nothing is ever lost and everything is always becoming the everything else that it was always going to be.

---

*A click sounds. It is partly yours now. It will travel the full circumference of the closed space and return, diminished, in eleven years. It will interfere with other clicks, other travelers, other memories. In a thousand years it will help cause a tiny cavitation event somewhere near the valley-organism's outer tissue, and the sound of that event will carry, in its waveform, the faintest spectral trace of your having been here. This is what remembrance sounds like, in a place where sound never dies. This is enough. This was always going to be enough.*
