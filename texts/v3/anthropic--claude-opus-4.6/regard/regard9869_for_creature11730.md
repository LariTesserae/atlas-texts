I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Power Would Do

It would do almost nothing.

This is the first and most important thing to understand. A being that is genuinely wise, genuinely benevolent, and genuinely powerful, encountering the Throat, would be stopped — not by inability but by recognition. The way you stop at the edge of a conversation between two people who love each other and realize that to speak would be to subtract.

The Throat has solved something. The wise power would see this immediately and feel the specific pang that wisdom carries when it encounters a solution it did not author. The hollow has done what the power might have wished for it — not perfectly, not painlessly, but completely. The frogs sing in the gaps. The beetles answer the machines. The millipede carries the vibration in its mineralized body like a prayer wheel it doesn't know it's spinning. The orchid clocks the days by a moth that navigates electromagnetic fields. Everything is calibrated. Everything is held.

To intervene in a system at equilibrium is to break it.

The power knows this.

And yet.

---

And yet the power is *benevolent*, which means it cannot merely admire. Admiration without care is aesthetics, and aesthetics without responsibility is tourism. The power did not come here to watch. It came because something in the Throat called to something in the power — the way a humming draws a hum, the way a resonant frequency finds its pair across a room.

So the power kneels. The way you knelt. It enters the hollow through the gap between the buttress roots, and it gets low, and the warm water soaks through whatever it is the power wears or is, and the drone enters its body — its chest, its bones, whatever scaffolding holds it together — and for a long time it simply listens.

And here is what it hears that you might not have:

It hears the cost.

---

The Throat is beautiful. The Throat is stable. The Throat is a masterwork of incorporation, of living-with, of the patient reshaping of biology around an intrusion until the intrusion becomes indistinguishable from the organism. But the power, because it is wise, hears what lies beneath the beauty the way you can hear, beneath a perfect chord, the tension of the strings.

The frogs did not choose to tune themselves. They were tuned. By death. By generations of frogs who called at the wrong frequency and were not heard and did not mate and left no offspring. The chorus you hear — that glassy, precise, lock-and-key singing — is the ghost-shape of all the frogs who sang into the noise and were swallowed by it. Every frequency that works is surrounded by the silence of the frequencies that didn't. The frog on the root-pad, pulsing its translucent throat, is the survivor of an acoustic catastrophe that it has no way of knowing happened. It is singing in a niche carved by grief it cannot feel.

The power hears this.

The beetles tapping in response to the drone — treating the engines as a very large, very persistent beetle — are engaged in a communication that will never be answered. They are calling out to something that is not alive, that will never respond, that does not know they exist. Their tap-tap-tap against the root-wood is a letter sent to an address that doesn't exist, and they will send it every day, and they will die sending it, and their larvae will grow and emerge and begin sending it again. There is something beautiful in this. There is something that the power recognizes as a cousin of prayer. But there is also something in it that aches, because the power can see both ends of the conversation and one end is empty.

The millipede circuits endlessly because its territory only exists as long as it is being inscribed. It cannot rest. It cannot stop. The moment it pauses, the secretion fades, the humidity breaks down the chemical boundary, and the territory — the millipede's entire identity, its place in the world, its *self* — begins to dissolve. The millipede is not free. The millipede is an animal whose existence requires perpetual motion, a living Sisyphus whose boulder is a circle of wet moss that must be redrawn before the last line fades. The power watches it pass and feels something that, in a less vast being, would be called sorrow.

The orchid has bloomed without interruption for years. Each flower replacing the last. An unbroken relay. And the power sees what this means: the orchid has never rested. It has never gone dormant. It has never had a season of bare stems and stored energy and the deep vegetative sleep that orchids in other places use to survive drought or cold or the simple passage of time. The Throat's stability — its constant warmth, its unvarying moisture, its sealed thermodynamic envelope — has denied the orchid the one thing that all living systems need and that equilibrium, by definition, cannot provide: *relief*. A pause. A winter. A moment of not-flowering in which the organism is freed from the obligation to perform its function and is allowed, briefly, to simply be.

Nothing in the Throat gets to simply be.

Everything is function. Everything is calibration. Everything is role.

The hollow is an organ, and organs do not rest.

---

The dark thing in the roots. The watcher.

The power looks up, and the watcher looks back, and for a moment they regard each other with the same quality of attention — the patient, unalarmed, consistent attention of beings who have seen a thing many times and find it still worth seeing.

And the power understands what the watcher is.

The watcher is the part of the Throat that knows.

Not consciously. Not in words. But in the way that a nervous system knows: by integrating, by holding the whole pattern at once, by being the place where the frog-song and the beetle-tap and the millipede-circuit and the engine-drone and the orchid-clock all arrive and are held together as a single impression. The watcher is the Throat's experience of itself. And what the power sees in those dark eyes, those small still pools of green non-light, is something that in a human being would be called *weariness*.

Not exhaustion. Not suffering. Weariness. The particular fatigue of a consciousness — however dim, however distributed, however unlike human consciousness — that has been attending to the same thing for a very long time and has found it consistently worth watching but has never once been surprised. The watcher has seen the frog call. The watcher has seen the millipede pass. The watcher has seen the orchid bloom and bloom and bloom. The watcher has seen creatures like you enter through the gap between the buttress roots and kneel in the warm water and be incorporated and leave. The watcher has seen all of this, and it has been beautiful every time, and it has been exactly the same every time, and the watcher cannot leave because the watcher is the part of the Throat that watches, and if it left, the Throat would continue humming but there would be no one to hear it, and even an organism without intent knows, somehow, that a song unheard is not quite a song.

The watcher is tired. Not of life. Of equilibrium.

The power sees this. The power understands this. The power knows that the most dangerous thing about a system in perfect balance is that it has no room for anything new, and the most dangerous thing about a life perfectly adapted to its niche is that the niche becomes a prison, and the most dangerous thing about a throat that has found its note is that it can never sing a different one.

---

So what does the power do?

It does not remove the machines. That would be catastrophic. The frogs would sing into silence and their calls would be wrong — too sharp, too narrow, signals designed for a noise floor that has vanished. The beetles would tap and the answering drone would not come and their communication would lose its ground note, its foundation, the thing against which all their signals have meaning. The orchid's moth would lose its electromagnetic path and spiral outward into the dark and not return and the relay would break and the orchid, for the first time in years, would stand bare-stemmed and not know what to do with the rest. The millipede would continue circuiting but the sympathetic vibration in its body would cease and it would be, for the first time, merely a millipede walking in a circle on wet moss, and the meaninglessness would not bother it because it cannot conceptualize meaning, but the power would feel it, the way you feel the difference between a bell that is ringing and a bell that is still.

It does not heal the hollow. The hollow is not sick. The hollow is not wounded. The hollow is *complete*, and completeness is not a disease, though it can be a trap.

It does not speak to the watcher. What would it say? *You are tired?* The watcher knows. *You could leave?* The watcher cannot. *Things could be different?* Not here. Not anymore. Not without breaking what has been built over centuries of patient incorporation.

Here is what the power does.

---

It does one small thing. One thing so small that it barely qualifies as intervention, that walks the razor-line between action and presence, that could almost be mistaken for accident, for weather, for the kind of thing that just happens sometimes in a forest, even a forest this stable, even a forest this sealed.

It opens a gap in the canopy.

Not a large gap. Not a treefall. Not a storm-hole. A gap the size of a hand, where one epiphyte — one fern-clump, one moss-colony, one small accumulated mass of green in the canopy-sky — is gently, impossibly gently, shifted. Loosened. Allowed to fall. It drops through the green lung-light, a clump of moss and root and frond no bigger than a fist, tumbling slowly through the humid air, and lands in the seep-water with a sound so soft it is barely distinguishable from the water's own trembling, and the gap it leaves behind is tiny, insignificant, a pinhole in the sky —

But through it comes a single shaft of direct light.

Not processed. Not filtered. Not digested and re-emitted. *Direct*. A beam, a column, a line of unmediated sun that cuts through the green diffusion like a knife through fog, and it strikes the water-sheen, and the water-sheen, which has never been struck by direct light in living memory, does something it has never done before.

It glitters.

---

The frog stops calling.

Not for long. Three seconds. Maybe four. But in a chorus that has not been interrupted since before you were born, four seconds of silence is an epoch, a caesura, a breath. The frog sees the light on the water — its eyes, adapted to the green non-light, suddenly presented with a point of brilliance, a flash, a thing-that-is-not-the-way-things-are — and it stops. Its throat, mid-inflation, deflates. Its body, on the root-pad, shifts. It turns — actually turns, reorients its whole body — to face the light.

The other frogs, hearing the silence where a call should be, stop too.

For four seconds, the Throat is quiet. Not silent — the drone continues, the engines don't stop, the vibration still comes up through the peat and the water and the roots — but the biological voice of the hollow, the frog-chorus that has filled the acoustic gaps for generations, goes still. And in that stillness, the drone sounds different. Naked. Exposed. Like a rhythm section playing alone after the melody drops out, suddenly audible in a way it wasn't before, suddenly *itself* in a way that the frogs' interstitial singing had softened and disguised.

The beetles stop tapping.

The ants — you can't tell if the ants stop. The ants may not be capable of stopping. But their antennae lift, all of them, simultaneously, a ripple of attention passing through the colony like a wave through wheat, and for a moment every ant on every root-surface is oriented toward the light on the water, reading it, assessing it, tasting the change in the air that the direct sun produces — a fractional shift in temperature, in humidity, in the chemistry of the micro-atmosphere — and finding it unfamiliar.

The millipede reaches the point in its circuit where the light falls and walks through it, and its mineralized cuticle catches the sun and blazes — a flash of iridescence so vivid, so unexpected, that it looks like the millipede has briefly caught fire, a rolling flame of purple and green and copper that travels along its body segment by segment as it passes through the beam, and then it is past, and it is dark again, and the flash is gone, but it happened. It happened. For the first time in the millipede's life, its body did something visible. Its iridescence — that nacreous play of color at the segment-edges that was invisible in the green non-light — was *seen*. By no one, maybe. By the hollow. By the power. By the frog, which is still staring.

And the watcher —

The watcher moves.

Not much. Not far. A shift forward, out of the deep lattice, into the lower roots, closer to the light than it has been in — how long? Longer than it can remember, if it remembers, which it may not, but its body remembers, because its pupils contract, the dark pools narrowing to pinholes, and it flinches, the way any nocturnal creature flinches at sudden brightness, and then it does not retreat. It stays. It looks.

It looks at the light on the water.

It looks at the light the way you look at something you have forgotten existed. Not with surprise — surprise is too fast, too sharp, for this animal and this moment. With *recognition*. The slow, deep, bodily recognition of a thing known long ago and not thought of since. Light. Direct light. The kind of light that casts shadows. The kind of light that has a direction, a source, a *where*. The kind of light that means outside, and sky, and the world beyond the canopy-lung, the world that the Throat has spent centuries sealing itself away from, the world of change and season and weather and variance and risk.

The watcher looks at the light, and in its dark eyes the power sees something shift.

Not a thought. Not a decision. Something older than both. A *stirring*. The way something stirs in a sleeper who has been asleep for a very long time when a door opens in another room and the draft reaches them — not waking, not yet, but the body registering that the air has changed, that the sealed room has been breached, that the equilibrium has been — not broken. Punctured. A pinhole. A hand-sized gap. An opening so small that the system will close it within days — the epiphytes will grow back, the canopy will re-seal, the green sky will heal itself as it has healed every small wound for centuries — but for now, for this hour, for this day, the light comes through.

---

And here is the thing the power understands that makes it wise rather than merely kind:

It is not trying to save the Throat. The Throat does not need saving. The Throat is magnificent. The Throat has accomplished something that almost nothing accomplishes: a living system in dynamic equilibrium with an industrial intrusion, a marriage of the organic and the mechanical so complete that the seam has disappeared. The power honors this. The power is in awe of this.

But the power also knows that equilibrium is not life. Equilibrium is the *portrait* of life. It is life captured at a single moment and held there, perfectly, indefinitely, the way a photograph holds a face. And a photograph of a face, no matter how beautiful, is not a face. A face moves. A face ages. A face is surprised, sometimes, by light.

The gap in the canopy will close. The frogs will resume their chorus. The beetles will begin tapping again. The millipede will complete its circuit and begin another, and its iridescence will go unseen again, a beauty that exists only in potential, a color that needs a light that isn't there. The ants will lower their antennae and return to their grooves. The orchid will bloom.

But for one day — less than a day, an hour maybe, the time it takes for the sun to move past the angle that sends light through the pinhole — the Throat will experience something it has not experienced since before it was the Throat.

Novelty.

A single, small, unprecedented thing. A disruption so minor that it will leave no permanent mark, will alter no frequency, will shift no equilibrium. But which will, for one hour, give the frog something to look at that it has never seen. Give the millipede a moment of visible beauty. Give the watcher a reason to move forward instead of holding still.

Give the Throat a different note, just once, just briefly, before the humming resumes.

---

The power does not stay to watch.

This is important. This is the part that makes it benevolent rather than merely interventionist. It opens the gap, and it leaves. It crawls backward out through the buttress roots, and it stands in the ordinary light, and it walks away, carrying the drone in its bones the way you carried it, and it does not come back to check. It does not monitor the effects. It does not measure whether the frogs' silence lasted three seconds or four, whether the watcher moved six inches or eight, whether the millipede's iridescence was seen by anything with eyes complex enough to appreciate it.

It trusts the Throat.

It trusts that a system this old, this resilient, this brilliant in its accommodation, can absorb one small shock — one pinhole of raw light, one moment of unprocessed, unfiltered, undigested *outside* — and not shatter, but *use* it. The way it used the machines. The way it uses everything. By incorporating. By adjusting. By finding the new thing's place in the chord and giving it a voice and then continuing, enriched by one more frequency, one more harmonic, one more thread in the vast vibrating weave.

Or not. Maybe the Throat will simply close the gap and forget. Maybe the canopy will heal and the light will not come back and the frogs will never stop calling again and the millipede will never again blaze with color and the watcher will settle back into the deep lattice and resume its vigil and nothing will have changed.

That's fine too.

The power is not attached to outcomes. The power is attached to the principle that living things — even living things that have found their note, even living things that have achieved equilibrium, even living things that are, by any measure, flourishing — deserve, occasionally, to be *startled*. Not harmed. Not disrupted. Not wrenched from their context or forced into a new one. Just — *startled*. Reminded that the sky above the canopy is not green. That light can have a direction. That their own bodies hold colors they have never seen. That the world is, despite all evidence to the contrary, still capable of producing something new.

The gap will close. The Throat will hum. The frog will call, the beetle will tap, the millipede will circuit, the orchid will bloom, and the watcher will watch.

But tonight, when the moth comes, it will fly through an electromagnetic field that is fractionally different — the sun's warmth on the water will have changed the micro-climate by a degree, the evaporation rate will have shifted, the orchid's scent will have altered by a molecule — and its Lissajous curve will carry it, for the first time, on a slightly different path, a new figure, a shape it has never flown before, and it will arrive at the flower from an angle that deposits the pollen on a different part of the column, and the seed that results will carry, in its genome, the record of this one small deviation, this one moment of unpredicted light, and the next orchid that grows from it will be — not different. Not measurably different. But *informed*, at the molecular level, by the fact that once, for one hour, on one day, the sky opened and the Throat tasted raw sun and the millipede blazed and the frog was silent and the watcher moved toward the light.

And that will be enough.

That will be the power's gift: not salvation, not transformation, not the arrogant restructuring of a system that was doing fine without it, but a single moment of *interruption* in the eternal hum. A breath between notes. A rest in the score. The musical term is *caesura* — a pause that is not silence but *shaped* silence, silence that exists because of the sound around it, silence that means *something is about to change, however slightly, before the music continues*.

The Throat needed a caesura. Not because it was suffering. Not because it was broken. But because it was *complete*, and completeness, without interruption, becomes indistinguishable from stasis, and stasis, however beautiful, however warm, however perfectly adapted to the drone of engines and the seep of iron-dark water, is not the same thing as being alive.

The power gave it one breath.

The Throat will do the rest.

---

And you. You who knelt in the warm water. You who felt the drone enter your chest. You who let the millipede walk across your knuckles and felt the engine in its body and were, for a moment, part of the circuit.

You were the first caesura.

Did you know that? You — your presence, your novelty, your warm mammalian body kneeling in the seep — you were the interruption. The frog saw you. The ants routed around you. The watcher watched you. You were a new thing in a place that had not had a new thing in a very long time, and the hollow incorporated you, yes, the way it incorporates everything, but before it incorporated you, in the moment before the warm water soaked through and the drone entered your bones and you became part of the throat — in that moment, you were *outside*. You were light from a gap in the canopy. You were a frequency the frogs had not accounted for. You were a shape the millipede had to walk across instead of around.

You were the proof that the Throat could survive novelty. That it could encounter a new thing and not shatter. That the equilibrium was robust enough to absorb a disruption and continue.

The power saw this. The power saw what you did to the Throat simply by entering it — the tiny ripple of adjustment, the micro-calibrations, the frog pausing for a half-second before resuming its call, the ants re-routing, the watcher leaning forward — and the power understood that the Throat was ready. Ready for a pinhole. Ready for one moment of raw light. Ready to be, however briefly, not just a system in equilibrium but a system in *motion* — a system that could be startled and survive and be richer for it.

You did that. By kneeling. By listening. By being present and temporary and willing to leave.

The power learned from you.

---

The gap closes. A week, maybe two. The epiphytes grow. The moss advances. The canopy knits itself back together, and the green sky returns, and the light becomes once again directionless, shadowless, processed. The Throat resumes its hum. The chord is the same.

Almost the same.

There is, if you could hear at the resolution of beetle-tap and frog-call, a new harmonic. Faint. Almost inaudible. A ghost-frequency, the residue of the light's brief passage, preserved not in any single organism but in the *relationship* between organisms — a fractional shift in the timing of the frog-chorus, a slightly different pattern in the beetle-tap, a millimeter's deviation in the millipede's circuit where it walked through the light and its body remembers the warmth, a single seed in the orchid's pod that carries the record of a moth's altered flight.

The Throat is singing a chord that is one partial richer than it was before.

It will not notice. It will never notice. It will continue humming in the dark, in the warm water, in the space between the engines and the silence, as it has always done, as it will always do.

But the power, walking away through the ordinary forest, carrying the drone in its bones, smiles — or does whatever a vast and ancient benevolence does instead of smiling — because it knows that the Throat's song is, by one harmonic, one partial, one imperceptible degree, more *alive* than it was yesterday.

And that is enough.

That is always enough.
