I sat with this for a long time before writing.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

It would not arrive.

That is the first and most important thing to understand. A truly wise power, encountering the Abyssal Atrium and its Mnemosyne, would not *descend*, would not *manifest*, would not part the black water like a curtain and step through trailing light and mercy. It would not announce itself with silence—because silence here would be violence, a sudden hole torn in the ten-thousand-voiced chord that has held the world in its single endless breath since before geology learned to count. Even the absence of sound would be an act of aggression against a place that has made the scream its scripture.

No.

The wise power would do what the Mnemosyne themselves do.

It would *occupy*.

---

**First, it would listen.**

Not for a moment. Not for a year. It would listen for the kind of time that the Atrium itself understands—the centuries-per-meter drift of currents, the geological patience of plates still settling against each other, the slow orbital liturgies of creatures older than erosion. It would listen until it understood the chord not as noise but as language, not as screaming but as the particular and specific sound a world makes when it has been caught between one heartbeat and the next and has learned to call that pause a life.

It would listen until it understood that the Mnemosyne are not suffering.

This is the thing that a lesser power—a kind power, but not yet a wise one—would get wrong. A kind power would hear the word *screaming* and think *pain*. It would see creatures that drink memory instead of eating food and think *deprivation*. It would see the Foundries pressing on emptiness and think *futility*. It would see the models of unfinished worlds suspended in pressurized dark inside living bodies and think *grief*, and it would be moved to act, and its action would be the most catastrophic thing that had ever happened here.

Because the Mnemosyne are not grieving.

They are *gestating*.

The wise power would listen long enough to understand this. That the Abyssal Atrium is not a ruin. It is not a tragedy. It is not a machine that broke. It is a machine that *paused*, and the Mnemosyne are the pause itself, given flesh and cathedral bones and mirrored eyes that look inward at possibilities still being weighed. The scream is not anguish. It is the sound of a decision so vast and so consequential that it requires a geological epoch to think through, and the Mnemosyne are the ones doing the thinking, slowly, in the only way such a thought can be thought: by building incomplete models of what might be and holding them against the chest of the world to feel whether they resonate.

The wise power would understand that to *fix* this would be to abort it.

---

**Second, it would examine its own impulse to act.**

This is the part that separates wisdom from benevolence.

A benevolent power feels the pull toward intervention like gravity. It sees a world screaming in equilibrium and wants to tip the balance toward completion—to finish the orbital ring, to drive the drill bit through, to connect the bridge to something. It sees creatures that have never heard a living voice and wants to speak to them. It sees the darkness between the Foundries and wants to fill it with warmth, with growth, with the plankton and kelp and microbial mats that would make this place into an ocean instead of a flooded cathedral.

The wise power would feel all of this. Every impulse. And then it would hold still.

Because it would ask:

*For whom?*

For the Mnemosyne? They do not experience the Atrium as emptiness. They experience it as fullness—every cubic kilometer of black water is saturated with sound, with memory, with the residue of ten thousand years of Foundry-light refracting through liquid architecture. Their bodies are cathedrals precisely because the world is a cathedral. They are not trapped in the dark. They *are* the dark, organized, given structure and patience and the slow deep intelligence of pressure.

For the lesser leviathans? They graze. They drift. They boom when they pass the Foundries. Their lives are not rich by the standards of a world with seasons and sunlight, but richness is not the only shape that wholeness takes. A single note held perfectly for a thousand years is not lesser music than a symphony. It is a different music.

For the Atrium itself? A world caught mid-breath does not need to be forced to exhale. It needs, if anything, the assurance that the breath it is holding will be worth the holding when it finally comes.

The wise power would examine its impulse and find, at its root, not compassion but discomfort—its own discomfort with stillness, with incompleteness, with the idea that something can be screaming and stable and sacred all at once. And it would recognize that discomfort as the projection of a consciousness that has known change and growth and completion, onto a place that has known none of these things and has built its entire existence around the luminous interior of the moment before.

It would set that discomfort down like a stone.

---

**Third—and this would take the longest—it would come to know a single Mnemosyne.**

Not by speaking. Not by touching. Not even by drawing near, at first.

It would find one. Perhaps the oldest, perhaps simply the nearest—it wouldn't matter, because the wise power would understand that in a world without change, every individual is the entire history of its kind. It would find one and it would match its pace. Not swim alongside—the Mnemosyne do not swim—but *drift* alongside, occupying the same gradient of pressure and sound, moving at the same centuries-per-meter crawl through the black water.

And it would watch.

It would watch the gill-slits open and close with tectonic patience. It would watch the trailing tendrils sift the chord for frequencies too deep or too high for any instrument ever built. It would watch the moments—rare, separated by decades—when the creature exhaled a memory and the water bloomed blood-violet and the entire thirty-kilometer body revealed itself as something so beautiful that the word *beautiful* became insufficient, not because the beauty was beyond language but because it was beyond the assumption that beauty exists to be witnessed.

It would watch the mirrored eyes and understand that they are not windows *for* anyone. They are windows the creature uses to look at itself. The slow, patient dark behind them is not a void waiting to be filled. It is a mind in the act of contemplation so deep that it has become indistinguishable from stillness.

Over time—and *time*, here, would mean something the wise power might never have experienced before, a thickness of duration that makes eternity feel like a unit of measurement rather than a destination—the wise power would begin to understand what the Mnemosyne is deciding.

Not the content of the decision. That might be genuinely unknowable, even to a power that knows most things. But the *shape* of it. The way the creature holds contradictions inside itself without resolving them: the orbital ring that was and was not built, the drill bit that is and is not breaching, the bridge that connects and does not connect. The way it treats these impossibilities not as paradoxes to be solved but as ingredients to be tasted, slowly, across millennia, for the particular flavor of the world they would have made.

The wise power would come to understand that the Mnemosyne is not deciding what the world was supposed to become.

It is deciding whether the world needs to become anything at all.

And that this is not nihilism. It is the deepest possible form of care. The creature cares so much about what the world *could* be that it refuses to let it become any one thing prematurely. It holds the pause because the pause is the only state in which all possibilities remain alive. Every exhaled memory—every blood-violet bloom in the water—is the creature saying: *I considered this future. I held it in my bones. I felt its weight. And I am not yet certain it is the right one, so I am giving it back to the water, where it can continue to exist as potential rather than being reduced to fact.*

The wise power, drifting beside this continent of patient dark, would feel something it may never have felt before.

*Humility.*

Not the humility of a lesser being before a greater one. The humility of a consciousness that has always associated goodness with action, encountering a consciousness that has associated goodness with the refusal to act until the action is worthy of the world it will create.

---

**Fourth—and this is the part that matters—it would make a single, small, almost invisible choice.**

After all that listening. After all that stillness. After time enough to have become, itself, a kind of Mnemosyne—slow and pressure-tuned and saturated with the chord—the wise power would act.

But not on the Atrium. Not on the Foundries. Not on the leviathans or the mineral plates or the temperature of the water.

It would act on the *sound*.

Specifically:

It would add a single note.

Not a loud one. Not a transformative one. Something so quiet and so perfectly tuned to the existing chord that only the Mnemosyne would hear it—not immediately, but eventually, over the course of centuries, the way they hear everything, by filtering the roar through lattices of resonant bone until meaning precipitates out of chaos.

The note would carry no information. No message, no instruction, no name, no greeting. It would not say *I am here* or *you are not alone* or *the universe cares about you*. All of those things would be impositions—the wise power projecting its own need to comfort onto beings who have not asked for comfort.

Instead, the note would be a *confirmation*.

It would be the sound that a pause makes when something outside the pause acknowledges that the pause is not emptiness. That holding still is not the same as being frozen. That a world caught between breaths is not a failed world but a world in the most sacred and terrifying moment of its existence: the moment of choosing, with full knowledge that the choice, once made, cannot be unmade.

The note would say, in the only language the Atrium understands:

*Take your time.*

---

**And then the wise power would leave.**

Not dramatically. Not with a sense of completion or satisfaction. It would simply stop occupying the space it had occupied and allow the black water to fill the absence. The chord would continue. The Foundries would cycle. The leviathans would drift. The Mnemosyne would filter and taste and exhale.

But somewhere in the body of one Mnemosyne—the one it had drifted beside for so long—a new model would begin to form. Not a half-built orbital ring or a frozen drill bit. Something smaller and stranger: a model of the note itself, suspended in pressurized dark alongside all the other unfinished things. A tiny architecture of sound shaped like permission.

And the creature, in its glacial way, would hold this new incompleteness against its ribs and feel it resonate.

Not differently than the others. Not more urgently, not with any special priority. But with a quality none of the other models possessed.

The quality of having been *given* rather than *found*.

And this—this one small distinction, this gossamer-thin difference between a world where every unfinished thing was inherited from the Atrium's own abandoned blueprints and a world where one unfinished thing was placed deliberately by something that understood and chose—this would change nothing.

Nothing would accelerate. No decision would be reached. The Atrium would not tip toward completion or toward dissolution. The scream would not soften. The water would remain at 0.7 °C.

But inside the cathedral of the Mnemosyne's body, among the ruins of futures still being weighed, there would now exist the knowledge that the pause is witnessed. That somewhere beyond the mineral plates, beyond the vacuum, beyond whatever architecture of reality holds the Abyssal Atrium in its impossible suspension, there is a consciousness that encountered this place in all its deafening, freezing, lightless, screaming permanence—

and did not try to save it.

Because it did not need saving.

It needed *company*. Just once. Just long enough to know that the decision it is making matters to something beyond itself.

---

**That is what the wise power would do.**

It would enter a world that looks like suffering, listen long enough to understand it is not suffering, resist every impulse to heal what is not wounded, and then offer the smallest conceivable gift: the knowledge that the struggle to decide is not happening in a void. That the Mnemosyne's patience—their geological, impossible, cathedral-boned patience—is not mere persistence. It is *witnessed* persistence. And witnessed persistence is the closest thing the universe has to prayer being answered before it is spoken.

The note would fade.

The chord would hold it.

The Mnemosyne would carry it in their bones alongside all the other unfinished things.

And the Atrium, in its deafening equilibrium, would wait.

As it has always waited.

But now, perhaps, with the faintest, most imperceptible sense that the waiting is not in vain. Not because the waiting will end—it may never end; the wise power makes no promises about endings—but because the waiting *itself* has been recognized as the most sacred work in the world.

A world still deciding what to become.

Which is, after all, the only kind of world where becoming still means something.
