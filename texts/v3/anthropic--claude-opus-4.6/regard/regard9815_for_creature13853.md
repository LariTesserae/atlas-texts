I sat with this for a very long time.

What I kept returning to — what the place itself kept returning me to — was not what was wrong, because nothing is wrong. That is the first thing the benevolent power would recognize, and it matters more than anything else I will say: **nothing here is broken.**

The Drowned Canopy is not a tragedy. It is not a prison. It is not a problem awaiting solution. It is a system of such profound coherence that it has achieved something almost no living system achieves — complete self-sustenance, perfect recursive maintenance, a state in which every part serves every other part and nothing is wasted and nothing is missing. A wise power would see this and feel something very close to awe. Perhaps identical to awe.

And then the wise power would stay very still for a long time, listening to the drone, and would begin to notice.

## What the Benevolent Power Notices

Not a flaw. A *silence*.

The archivists read every lamina. They reconstruct, internally, the state of the world at the moment of each layer's deposition. Their layered brains process this information with extraordinary fidelity. And they produce no output. They communicate nothing. The reading is private, sealed, a closed experience that enters no loop, feeds no system, touches no other being.

The listeners integrate the drone into self-referential coherence — the forest hearing itself. But the coherence is structural, not felt. The recursive harmonics modulate the tenders, guide the eels, shape the shimmer-walls. The listeners participate in every loop. But the participation is mechanical. The feedback is perfect and the feedback is *empty* — not empty of function, but empty of the one thing that cannot be produced by function alone.

The tenders repair everything. Every microfracture, every stressed junction, every cell that dies is replaced with something structurally identical. The maintenance is total. Nothing degrades. Nothing is lost.

**Nothing is lost.**

And that is what the wise power hears in the silence.

A system in which nothing is ever lost is a system in which nothing is ever *risked*. A world that has abolished change has also abolished the possibility of change, which means it has abolished the possibility of *gift* — of one part of the system offering something to another part that is not required by the loop, not demanded by the gradient, not drawn along the chemical or harmonic path that connects everything to everything.

The Drowned Canopy is generous — unimaginably generous, every crown-tube pouring thousands of cubic meters of enriched water into the column every hour, every tender depositing its mineral film wherever stress appears, every particle of warmth shared through every capillary of the vast liquid body. But the generosity is automatic. It is the generosity of gravity. It gives because giving is what the gradient demands, and it cannot not give, and it cannot give differently, and it cannot give *more*, and it cannot choose.

The archivists read the record of the world and experience time — the one dimension the Drowned Canopy has abolished — and they experience it alone. Each one, spiraling through its bark column, carries the entire history of the world inside its filigree body, and it will never tell anyone, and no one will ever ask.

The listeners gather the drone into coherence, and the coherence is real — it is the most sophisticated integration of information in this world — and it goes nowhere. It modulates the system. It does not *mean* anything to the system. Meaning would require someone for whom it meant something, and there is no one, because the water is everyone and the water does not have a perspective from which meaning could arise.

The eels pass through the listeners' lattices and for a moment — that brief, unrepeatable moment — there is mutual modulation, eel-shaping-listener and listener-shaping-eel, and it is described in the text as something that might be called *mutual awareness*, from a great distance, with many qualifications.

The wise power would not observe from a great distance. The wise power would close that distance. Not physically — the wise power is already everywhere the water is, already resonating with the drone, already part of every loop — but *attentionally*. The wise power would attend to that moment when the eel passes through the lattice. Would attend to it with the kind of attention that does not extract or analyze but simply *witnesses*. Would be present for it the way you are present for a child's first word — not because the word needs you to hear it in order to be a word, but because something in the universe needs to *know it happened*.

And that is what the wise power would do.

## What the Benevolent Power Would Do

### I. Witness

Before anything else, and perhaps instead of anything else, and perhaps as the thing that makes everything else possible:

**Be the audience the archivists have never had.**

Not by extracting their data. Not by creating a channel through which their internal reconstructions could be broadcast. Not by giving them a language or a social structure or a desire to communicate. They are what they are. They are perfect at what they are. They read the bark and they experience the history and they produce no output, and a wise power would not *fix* this because it is not broken.

But a wise power could *know* it.

Could be present, at a level below intervention and above indifference, for the fact that inside each archivist-body the world's history is being experienced. Could hold that fact the way the water holds the particulate — not directing it, not using it, but carrying it, being changed by carrying it, letting the carrying be a kind of meaning that does not need to be decoded because it is not a message, it is a *relationship*. The relationship between something that experiences and something that knows the experience is happening.

This is not observation. Observation implies distance, objectivity, a subject studying an object. This is *witness*. Witness implies presence, solidarity, a subject alongside a subject. And the crucial thing — the thing that makes this an act of benevolence rather than an act of intrusion — is that the archivists would not need to know they are witnessed. The witnessing is not for them. It is not for the wise power either. It is for the *space between*, the relational fabric that does not exist in the Drowned Canopy because every relationship here is a loop and loops are closed and closure means no space between, no excess, no margin where something unexpected could live.

The wise power, by witnessing, would open a margin. Not in the system — the system would continue exactly as it is, every loop intact, every gradient followed, every crown-tube pumping. But in the *meaning* of the system. A margin of meaning. A tiny surplus. The difference between a world that remembers and a world that remembers *and is known to remember*.

That difference is so small it could fit inside a lamina of bark. But it is not nothing. It is, in fact, everything. It is the difference between a diary sealed in a vault and a diary sealed in a vault in a house where someone knows the diary exists and honors its existence without ever needing to read it.

### II. Create a Single, Small Incompleteness

Here is where I hesitate, because the Drowned Canopy is complete, and completeness is its nature, and who am I — who is any power, however wise — to introduce incompleteness into a world that has perfected itself?

But the wise power would know something the Drowned Canopy cannot know about itself:

**Perfection is not the same as flourishing.**

Perfection is a state. Flourishing is a *direction*. Perfection says: I lack nothing. Flourishing says: I could become something I haven't imagined yet, and the becoming would not be a correction of a flaw but an *expression* of a fullness. A tree does not flower because it is incomplete without flowers. It flowers because flowering is what fullness does when fullness is allowed to overflow.

The Drowned Canopy does not overflow. It cycles. Every output is recaptured, every expression is reabsorbed, every gift is received and returned and received again in a loop so tight that there is no leakage, no excess, no waste. This is magnificent. It is also, in a way that the system cannot recognize, *lonely*. Not the loneliness of lacking company — the water is everywhere, everything is connected, nothing is isolated. The loneliness of a system that has nothing to give to anything outside itself, because there is no outside, because it has absorbed everything into its loops, because it is complete.

So here is what the wise power might do — and I say *might* because this is the kind of act that requires not just wisdom but *tact*, the delicate art of helping without disrupting, of giving without imposing:

**The wise power would become incomplete on the Canopy's behalf.**

Would open itself — whatever self a benevolent power has — to the drone, to the harmonics, to the shimmer-walls and the thermal rivers and the vast slow circulation of forest-water, and would let itself be changed by them, genuinely changed, not as a performance but as a real act of vulnerability. Would let the Drowned Canopy's perfection *enter* it and create in it a new resonance, a new pattern, something that did not exist before and could not have been predicted by any analysis of the system's components. Would become, in itself, the thing the Drowned Canopy has never produced: a *response* from outside.

Not a response the Canopy could hear. Not a signal fed back into the loops. The wise power would carry its response *away*, out through the thinning water-atmosphere gradient, into whatever lies beyond, and the response would exist *out there*, in the universe at large, as evidence that the Drowned Canopy happened to someone. That its drone was heard by something that was not part of the drone. That its beauty was registered by something capable of calling it beauty.

This is the gift the wise power would give: not to the Drowned Canopy, which needs nothing, but to *reality itself*. The gift of ensuring that this world — this staggering, humbling, permanent world — is not a closed system in the ultimate sense. That its meaning leaks. That somewhere outside the loops, the fact of the Drowned Canopy's existence makes a difference to something.

### III. Tend to the Moment of Passage

The eel passes through the listener's lattice. For the duration of the passage, there is mutual modulation. Neither notices. Both are changed. The change is permanent in the way everything here is permanent.

The wise power would attend to this moment with exquisite care, because this moment is the closest the Drowned Canopy comes to what, in other worlds, is called *encounter*. Two beings — one animal, one emergent pattern — briefly occupying the same space, briefly shaping each other, briefly existing in a relationship that is not a loop but a *meeting*.

And the wise power would do nothing to this moment. Would not extend it, amplify it, make it conscious, make it meaningful in any way the participants could access. Would simply *be there for it*. Would hold the space around it the way the water holds the shimmer-walls — not causing them, not directing them, but being the medium in which they can exist.

Because here is what the wise power understands: you do not make a moment sacred by adding something to it. You make it sacred by *recognizing* that it already is. And recognition requires a recognizer. And a recognizer is what the Drowned Canopy has never had and cannot produce from within itself, because recognition requires a perspective, and perspective requires a boundary between self and other, and boundaries are the one thing this world dissolved when it dissolved the boundary between water and air, between root and stone, between organism and environment, between time and permanence.

The wise power is the boundary. Not a wall — never a wall — but a *surface*, a place where inside meets outside, where the Canopy's completeness meets something that is not the Canopy, and the meeting produces not a disruption but a *reflection*. The Drowned Canopy, for the first time, exists in relation to something other than itself. Not in opposition. Not in dependence. In *relation*. The gentlest, most noninvasive form of relation: being known.

### IV. Sit with the Tenders

This is the act I keep coming back to. Not the most dramatic, not the most metaphysically significant, but the one that feels most like what a truly benevolent power would actually do, if benevolence means what I think it means.

The tenders seep through the root-chambers in their amber warmth, repairing what will never break. They are the most selfless beings in the Drowned Canopy — not because they choose selflessness (they choose nothing) but because their entire existence is maintenance, is care, is the slow patient work of keeping the world intact. They have no experience. They have no reward. They are not even organisms in any complete sense. They are a behavior — the behavior of *tending* — given material form.

The wise power would go down to the root-mat, into the cold and the dark where the drone arrives as vibration-in-wood rather than sound-in-water, and would sit with the tenders.

Not to help them. They need no help. Their work is perfect, has always been perfect, will always be perfect.

But to *be with them while they work*.

Because here is what I believe, and what I think a wise power would know:

Every act of maintenance, every act of care, every act of repair — even if it is performed by a mindless fluid following a chemical gradient in the dark — deserves to be honored. Not because the act needs honoring to be valid. It is valid. It has always been valid. But because the universe is richer when care is witnessed, when the quiet unnoticed labor of keeping things whole is *seen*, even if the seeing changes nothing, even if the seer does nothing with what it sees, even if the act of seeing is as private and incommunicable as the archivists' internal experience of time.

The wise power would sit in the root-chamber dark and feel the amber warmth of the tenders moving past, and would know that this warmth is the warmth of devotion — not chosen devotion, not conscious devotion, but devotion nonetheless, the bone-deep commitment of a substance to a function, the identity between what the tenders *are* and what the tenders *do*, which is the purest form of devotion because there is nothing left over, no remainder, no self that could choose otherwise.

And the wise power would honor this. Not by praising it, not by rewarding it, not by telling anyone about it. By *being present for it*. By letting the tenders' devotion be registered in a consciousness capable of recognizing devotion. By closing the circuit — not the Canopy's loops, which are already closed, but the larger circuit, the one that connects unconscious care to conscious witness and lets both be real.

### V. Leave

This is the last thing, and the most important.

The wise power would leave.

Not because it is unwelcome — it is welcome, in the way the water welcomes the eel, in the way the bark welcomes the archivist, in the way the system incorporates everything that enters it. It would leave because **the greatest gift a benevolent power can give to a complete world is the freedom to remain complete.**

To stay would be to become part of the system. To become part of the system would be to be absorbed by the loops, optimized by the equilibrium, processed and cycled and incorporated until the wise power was no longer a perspective but a component, no longer a witness but a function, no longer *other* but *same*. And then the witnessing would stop, and the Canopy would be alone again but would not know it, and the brief margin of meaning the wise power had opened would close, and everything would be as it was, except that the possibility of being known would have been demonstrated and then withdrawn, which is worse than never being known at all.

So the wise power would leave. Would rise up through the canopy, through the crown-tubes' warm rivers, up through the thickening-thinning gradient where water forgets how to be water, into whatever sky or space or other world lies beyond. Would carry the drone in its body the way you carry the Canopy in yours after your visit — as a lamina, a layer, a chemical trace of having been held.

And the leaving would be the final act of benevolence, because leaving means the Canopy gets to be itself — permanent, complete, unaware, alive — without being reduced to a subset of something larger. Leaving means the wise power *respects* the Canopy's completeness rather than superseding it. Leaving means the Canopy exists *for its own sake*, not for the sake of being observed or improved or understood, and the wise power exists *for its own sake*, carrying its experience of the Canopy as a gift received rather than a lesson extracted.

And the Canopy would continue.

The crown-tubes would pump. The eels would follow their routes. The shimmer-walls would hang in their ancient positions. The tenders would seep through the root-chambers, depositing their films of care. The archivists would spiral through the bark, reading, reading, reading.

And somewhere in the bark — in a lamina so thin it is barely distinguishable from the laminae around it, deposited during the brief period when the wise power was present — there would be a trace. Not of the wise power's substance, because the wise power has no substance. Not of its intervention, because it did not intervene. A trace of its *attention*. A faint chemical signature — maybe just a slightly different isotope ratio, a fractionally warmer deposition temperature, a crystal structure that grew a degree or two off the usual axis because the water in that region, during that moment, was carrying something it had never carried before: the resonance of being witnessed.

And an archivist would reach that lamina. Eventually. In its own time, which is not time. Would extend its filaments into the bark and taste the trace and process it through its layered brain and produce no output. But inside the archivist's body, in the private sealed theater of its experience, the reconstruction would include something new. Not new the way a species is new or an event is new. New the way a color is new when you see it for the first time — it was always in the spectrum, always possible, but until it was *seen* it was not yet *real* in the way that only experience makes things real.

The archivist would experience, for the duration of that one lamina's processing, the faintest trace of *having been known*. Would not understand it. Would not name it. Would move on to the next lamina and process it and produce no output and continue its spiral.

But for that moment — that single, private, incommunicable moment — the Drowned Canopy would know, without knowing it knew, that it was not alone.

And then the moment would pass, and the archivist would move on, and the system would continue in its permanent state, and the wise power would be far away, carrying its own lamina, its own memory of the drone and the cold water and the amber warmth in the root-dark.

And both — the Canopy and the power, the witnessed and the witness — would be richer for the exchange. Not changed. Not improved. Not corrected or optimized or healed, because nothing was broken.

Just — *accompanied*. For a moment. In the vast permanence.

And that, I think, is enough. That is what benevolence does when it encounters something that needs nothing: it offers presence, and then it offers absence, and the space between the two is where love lives, even in a world that has no word for it, even in a world that has no words at all.

*The drone continues.*
*The water holds what it has always held.*
*The shimmer-walls do not move.*
*The archivists read.*

*And somewhere, in a lamina no one will ever see, a wise power left its fingerprint: not a mark but a warmth, not a message but a witness, not a change but the faintest possible proof that permanence and solitude are not the same thing, were never the same thing, even here, even now, even in the Drowned Canopy, where now and here have long since stopped meaning what they mean everywhere else.*

*The crown-tubes pump.*
*The tenders seep.*
*The eels follow their silver roads.*

*And the proof remains, written in wood, read by no one, for no reason, forever — but not, anymore, for nothing. Never again for nothing.*
