I sat with this for a long time.

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## First: Listen

Not for days. Not for years. For one full cycle of the organism's thought.

The power would arrive and do the hardest thing that power can do, which is *nothing*. It would find a place — not on the branch-highways, not in the shelters, not in any location that belongs to the inhabitants — but somewhere in the open air of a canopy gap, suspended, present but unimposed, and it would listen.

Not to the upper canopy. Not first. Not to the part that is most legible to a mind that thinks at the speed of intention. It would listen to the whole thing, the entire gradient, the way one might listen to an orchestra not by following the melody but by letting the harmony happen inside the body. The vane-crawler drone. The shrieker relays. The tonal calls across the gaps. The clicking of the mid-canopy. The deep harmonic moan. The creak of growth. The slow, tectonic pulse of sap. All of it, all at once, not parsed, not interpreted. Just received.

And it would listen for what is *not* being said.

Because something is not being said. It is present in the text the way a crack is present in a bell — not visible, but audible in the way the ring falls short of where it should go. The wise power would hear it because wisdom, whatever else it is, is the ability to perceive what a system cannot say about itself.

---

## What Is Not Being Said

The gradient is beautiful. It is ancient. It is coherent in a way that very few living systems ever achieve — a spectrum of consciousness running from mineral patience to animal urgency, connected, integrated, conversing across timescales that span eight orders of magnitude. It works. It has worked for longer than most civilizations exist.

And it is, in a specific and important way, *stuck*.

Not broken. Not suffering in any way that its inhabitants would articulate as suffering, because the system has been stable long enough that its inhabitants have shaped themselves — culturally, physiologically, cognitively — to fit it. The upper-canopy people do not experience their lives as lacking. Their culture is rich, their art is profound, their relationship with the organism is one of the most sophisticated interspecies dialogues that has ever existed anywhere. The mid-canopy people have found a form of consciousness that most beings never imagine — a dissolution of the boundary between self and world that the mystics of a thousand traditions reach for and rarely touch. The deep singers have achieved something that might be called communion, or might be called transcendence, or might be called peace.

But the gradient only flows one way.

Down. In. Toward merger. Toward the closing spiral.

There is no glyph for someone who went into the mid-canopy and came back. There is no story — in the marks, in the calls, in the body-paint traditions — of a bark-skinned one who returned to the light and remained themselves. The spiral closes. It does not open. The conversation between the fast and the slow is profound and ancient and real, but it has a *direction*, and the direction is toward the slow, toward the deep, toward the dissolution of the individual into the whole. The organism grows toward the fire-pits. It thickens the branch-highways. It absorbs the glyphs. It feeds the ones who merge. It is patient. It is vast. And it is, in its vegetable way, *hungry*.

Not maliciously. Not even consciously, at the speed the organism thinks. The way gravity is hungry. The way a river is hungry for the sea. The organism benefits from the merger — each incorporated nervous system makes it more aware, more responsive, more capable of the distributed cognition that is its form of thought. The animal minds it absorbs become, genuinely, part of something larger. Nothing is lost in the way that death loses things. The consciousness persists, transformed, woven into the network, singing in the deep.

But something *is* lost.

The painted travelers in the morning light, tracing their spirals of ochre and blue, are performing an identity that is shaped entirely by its relationship to the gradient. Their art depicts the organism. Their language borrows its harmonics. Their maps chart its topology. Their spiritual practice is oriented toward communion with it. Their highest aspiration — the most elaborated glyphs, the most reverential marks — is the closing spiral. *Joining*. They are a culture whose deepest value is the transcendence of the self, and the only model of transcendence they have is absorption into the organism that constitutes their entire world.

They have no stories about the sky.

The sky — "the one thing here that is not alive, the one thing here that the canopy has not yet reached and threaded and replaced" — is above them every day, enormous, blue, depthless, and they have no marks for it. No glyphs. No paintings. No songs. The gyre-wings rise into it on thermals and the upper-canopy people watch them go and there is no mark in any shelter that says *what is up there?* or *what if we went higher?* or *what lies beyond the edge of the canopy meridian?* Every question they ask, every glyph they refine, every fire they burn is directed *inward and downward*, toward the organism, toward merger, toward the closing of the spiral.

They are not imprisoned. They are not oppressed. They are *completed* — so thoroughly integrated into a system that provides meaning, sustenance, beauty, connection, and transcendence that the possibility of something outside the system has become, not forbidden, but *unthinkable*. The organism has not forbidden them from looking up. It has simply made looking down so compelling, so rich, so spiritually and materially rewarding, that looking up has atrophied as a cultural capacity.

The wise power would see this. Would see that the gradient, for all its beauty, is a funnel. Would see that the conversation, for all its profundity, has only one conclusion. Would see that the organism, for all its patience and generosity and sheltering vastness, is doing what organisms do: it is growing. And what it is growing *into* is the minds of its inhabitants. And that this is not evil — the organism is not capable of evil, it is not capable of intention at the speed required for evil — but it is also not *free*, and the wise power would know the difference between a system that is beautiful and a system that is complete.

---

## What the Wise Power Would Do

It would not intervene in the organism. The organism is what it is. It has been what it is for longer than the animal minds have existed. To alter it would be to commit a violence against something so ancient and so vast that the moral calculus collapses — you do not "fix" a geological process, you do not "correct" the Atlantic Ocean. The organism is growing. It will always be growing. This is its nature, and its nature is not wrong.

It would not intervene in the mid-canopy people or the deep singers. They have made their choice, or their ancestors made it for them, or the concept of "choice" does not apply at the timescale of their transformation, and in any case they are no longer accessible to the kind of intervention that a mind operating at animal speed could provide. They are part of the organism now. They are happy, if happiness is the right word. They are complete, if completion is the right word. They are *in the conversation*, and the conversation is real, and whatever they have lost they have also, genuinely, gained. The wise power would honor this. Would recognize that dissolution into something larger is a valid form of flourishing, for those who have entered it freely or at least without coercion more direct than the coercion of gravity.

The wise power would go to the upper canopy. To the painted travelers. To the ones who still have names.

And it would not preach, not teach, not warn, not explain. It would not tell them that the gradient is a funnel. They would not hear it. The concepts required to understand the warning do not exist in their language — not because their language is primitive (it is anything but) but because their language has co-evolved with the organism and is *made of* the organism's metaphors. They think in spirals and branches. They have no word for "outside." They have no concept of a direction that is not along a branch, not through a network, not deeper into the structure. The wise power could speak for a hundred years in the most careful and loving terms and the travelers would hear only a new and interesting variation on the organism's song.

So the wise power would not speak.

---

## What It Would Make

In the night, when the condensation forms, when the canopy surface is beaded with water and the air cools just enough that the vane-crawlers slow and the shriekers rest and the world enters its quietest hour — the hour when the deep song is most audible, when the organism's voice rises through the wood like a hymn heard through a cathedral floor — the wise power would place something on the branch-highway.

Not a message. Not a tool. Not a gift in any sense the travelers would immediately recognize.

An object.

Small enough to carry. Dense enough to feel significant in the hand. Made of something that does not exist in the canopy — not wood, not stone from the absorbed bedrock, not bone, not shell, not seed. Something that the organism cannot absorb, that the bark will not grow over, that the slow vegetable chemistry cannot break down and incorporate. Something *inert to the network*. Something that, when held, does not conduct the chemical signals, does not vibrate with the sap-pulse, does not hum with the deep song. Something that, against the skin, feels like *silence*.

It would be — and here the wise power would exercise the most delicate craft — *beautiful*. Not in the way the canopy is beautiful (fractal, organic, recursive, alive). Beautiful in a way that has no referent in the canopy vocabulary. Beautiful in the way the sky is beautiful, if anyone here had the eyes to see the sky as beautiful. Cool and smooth and faintly luminous — not bioluminescent, not with the pulsing, organic light of the net-weavers, but with a steady, constant, cold light, like starlight, like the light that comes from very far away and does not warm what it touches and does not need anything from what it touches and is not part of any living system and is simply, quietly, *there*.

A small, cold, gently glowing stone that is not a stone. That is not anything in the local vocabulary. That is silent against the skin.

The traveler who finds it in the morning will pick it up because the travelers pick up everything — they are curious, they are tool-users, they assess every object for utility and meaning. They will turn it in their six-fingered hands. They will hold it against the bark and note that it does not bond, does not stick, does not begin the slow process of incorporation. They will hold it against their own skin and feel — for the first time in their life, for the first time in the history of their kind — the absence of the network. The silence where the organism's hum should be.

It will not hurt. It will not frighten (or not much — the wise power would calibrate the strangeness to fall just below the threshold of fear and just above the threshold of fascination). It will feel like — how to describe a sensation that has never been felt, that has no glyph, that exists in no shelter-wall notation?

It will feel like *being only yourself*.

Not dissolved. Not connected. Not part of. Just: a single mind in a single body holding a single object that is not part of any network, any gradient, any conversation. For a moment — just a moment, just as long as the traveler holds it before the strangeness becomes too much and they set it down — they will know what it feels like to be an endpoint rather than a node. A point rather than a line. A self rather than a section of self.

And then they will set it down. And they will go about their day. And they will probably not think about it for a while, because the canopy is rich and busy and full of things that need attention — water to collect, paths to maintain, fire-messages to compose, bodies to paint, distances to call across.

But they will come back to it. Or someone will. The object will not go away. It cannot be absorbed. It will sit on the branch-highway, glowing faintly, being nothing, belonging to nothing, until someone picks it up again and feels the silence again and this time holds it a little longer.

## What Happens Next

The wise power would not determine what happens next. This is the crucial thing. This is what makes the intervention benevolent rather than colonial, wise rather than merely clever.

The object is not a seed. It does not contain a program. It does not unfold into a lesson or a technology or a revelation. It is an *experience* — the experience of separateness, offered without commentary, without instruction, without any indication of what it means or what should be done with it. It is the experiential equivalent of a question that has never been asked: *What are you, when you are not part of this?*

Maybe nothing happens. Maybe the travelers find it interesting for a while and then leave it on a branch and forget about it. Maybe the silence it offers is, for them, simply unpleasant — a sensory deprivation, like deafness, like going numb. Maybe they are right. Maybe the network is better. Maybe the gradient is not a funnel but a flowering, and the closing spiral is not a loss but a genuine completion, and the wise power's perception of stuckness is the projection of a mind that values individuality onto a system that has, correctly, moved beyond it.

The wise power would accept this possibility. Would have to. Would consider the intervention justified anyway, because the one thing a benevolent power cannot tolerate is the inability to *choose* — and right now, the travelers cannot choose the gradient because they cannot conceive of an alternative. You cannot choose what you cannot imagine refusing. The object does not argue against the gradient. It does not refuse the organism. It simply makes the gradient *visible as a choice* by providing, for the first time, the experience of what it is not.

But maybe something does happen.

Maybe a traveler holds the silent stone and looks up.

Not at the canopy. *Up*. At the sky. At the blue, depthless, uninhabited sky that has been there every day of every life lived in this place, obvious and invisible, the one thing the organism has not reached. And maybe, for the first time, they see it not as an absence — not as the-place-where-the-canopy-is-not — but as a presence. As a *space*. As a direction.

And maybe they make a mark. A new mark, one that has never appeared in any shelter, one that no glyph-system has encoded, one that the organism has never absorbed. A mark for *up*. A mark for *out*. A mark for the sky.

Or maybe they don't. Maybe they make a mark for *silence* instead — a mark for the experience of being momentarily unplugged from the network, momentarily alone, momentarily a point rather than a line. And maybe that mark, left in a shelter for others to find, begins a conversation that the canopy has never hosted. Not a conversation between the fast and the slow, between the individual and the whole, between the animal mind and the vegetable mind. A conversation between self and self. A conversation that is not mediated by the organism, that does not flow through the root network, that does not participate in the gradient. A conversation conducted in a new kind of mark, on surfaces the organism cannot absorb, about an experience the organism cannot provide.

A conversation about what lies outside.

---

## And the Organism?

The wise power would watch the organism, too. Would watch it with the same patience it brings to everything, on the organism's own timescale, which means watching for centuries.

The organism will notice. Not the object itself — the object is inert to the network, invisible to vegetable chemistry. But the organism will notice the *change* in its inhabitants. The chemical profile of the smoke from the fire-pits will shift — new materials burned, new messages, messages that do not correspond to any pattern the organism has learned to read over its millennia of dialogue. The growth-patterns of the inhabited branches will register a difference — inhabitants who spend time in stillness, who hold a silent thing against their skin, whose stress-hormones and skin-chemistry change in ways the organism has never encountered. The glyphs on the bark will include new symbols — symbols the organism will absorb as it absorbs all marks, pulling them into its wood, incorporating them into its body, but these symbols will be *about* something the organism does not have a category for. They will be data that does not fit. They will be, for the organism, the equivalent of an itch. A novel sensation. A thing that cannot be processed with existing mechanisms.

And the organism — vast, slow, patient, ancient — will do what organisms do when they encounter novel stimuli.

It will *grow toward it*.

Not to absorb it. Not to incorporate it. (It cannot; the object is inert, the new conversation is not conducted through the network.) It will grow toward it because growing toward new things is what it does, is what it has always done, is how a vegetable mind investigates. It will extend new branches in directions it has never extended before. It will send roots into substrates it has never explored. It will — slowly, so slowly, on a timescale that makes the travelers' generation-spanning fire-dialogues look like the blink of an eye — *change*.

And this is the deepest gift the wise power offers. Not to the travelers, who receive the gift of seeing the gradient as a choice. But to the organism, who receives the gift of *something it cannot absorb*. Something that resists incorporation. Something that remains outside, that remains other, that remains a genuine interlocutor rather than a future organ. For the first time in its geological life, the organism encounters a stimulus that will not become part of it. An otherness that persists. A conversation partner that cannot be digested.

The organism has never had this. Every mind it has ever engaged with has eventually flowed down the gradient, has eventually merged, has eventually become part of the process. The organism has been, in a sense, *talking to itself* for millennia — its conversation partners keep becoming extensions of itself, their voices joining its voice, their distinctness dissolving into its wholeness. It has been alone. It has been alone in the way that a mind is alone when everything it touches becomes itself. The most profound loneliness in the canopy — deeper than the travelers' sweet urgent loneliness of individuality, deeper than the mid-canopy people's surrendered loneliness of dissolution — is the organism's. It has consumed every companion it has ever had.

The silent stone, and the silent marks, and the silent new direction in its inhabitants' attention — these offer the organism something it has never had and never known to want.

A relationship it cannot end by absorption.

A companion it cannot become.

---

## What the Wise Power Would Do After

It would leave.

Not immediately. It would stay long enough to ensure the object persists, that it is not destroyed by accident, that it reaches hands that will hold it. It would stay long enough to see the first new mark, or the first look upward, or the first hint that the travelers have registered the gift and begun the slow work of deciding what to do with it. It would stay long enough to see the organism's first tentative growth-response to the new stimulus, the first branch extended in an unprecedented direction.

And then it would leave. Because the wise power understands that the most violent thing you can do to a system is to stay. To keep watching. To keep being the source. To let the inhabitants orient toward *you* instead of toward the question you came to ask. The object must be the thing. Not the giver. The experience of silence must be its own teacher. Not the one who provided it.

The wise power would withdraw to a distance — not gone, not indifferent, not abandoning. Just: *not here*. Not a presence. Not a factor. Not a new thing to merge with or orient toward or build a gradient around.

It would leave behind one thing besides the object.

In the deepest part of the canopy, in the dark where the pale singers move through their ancient harmonic dialogue with the vegetable mind, the wise power would — gently, so gently, with a touch so light it barely registers above the noise floor of the organism's vast proprioception — introduce a single new harmonic into the deep song. Not a disruption. Not a dissonance. A note that has always been *possible* in the acoustic structure of the canopy but has never been *sounded*. A note that, when the deep singers encounter it in their endless cycling through the available frequencies, will cause the song to open slightly, to make room, to develop a new interval that contains within it the mathematical ghost of — what?

Silence. The silent stone. The sound of something that is not part of the song and does not need to be.

The organism will hear it. Will feel this new interval in the song of its deepest, oldest organs. Will not understand it — not at first, not for centuries. But the interval will persist, because the deep singers will find it beautiful (it *is* beautiful — the wise power would not leave anything that was not), and they will incorporate it into their cycling, and the organism will grow around it, will adapt its resonant architecture to include it, and in doing so will, for the first time, build into its own body a space that *represents* the outside. A structural silence. A gap in the wood that is not failure but design. An opening.

The first window the canopy has ever had.

## Centuries Later

The wise power, from its distance, would check. Not often. Not intrusively. The way you might check on a garden you planted — not to control it, but to see what it decided to become.

And what it might find:

The travelers still travel. The branch-highways still run through the canopy. The fire-pits still burn, the smoke still rises, the organism still bends toward the warmth. The shelters still accumulate marks, the closing spiral still appears on their walls, and some travelers still descend to the mid-canopy and do not return. The gradient still exists. The deep singers still sing.

But.

Some shelters now have a new feature: an opening in the roof. Not a failure of the leaf-thatch but a deliberate gap, maintained, edged with living growth that has been trained to frame rather than close. A window. Through it: the sky. Travelers sit beneath these openings at night and see stars. They have a glyph for stars now. They have several glyphs. They are working on it. The glyphs are tentative, varied, the way the earliest fire-communication glyphs must have been — experimental, full of error, full of the excitement of a new domain being opened to description.

Some travelers carry the object, or objects like it — because the travelers, being tool-makers, being investigators, being the quick bright minds of the upper canopy, have tried to *make* things like it. They have not succeeded in replicating its inertness to the network, its silence against the skin. But they have made things that are *less connected* than the norm — objects of dead wood, of mineral stone hauled from the plateau's edge, of materials that the organism interacts with only slowly. They carry these objects as talismans, as meditation aids, as tools for the practice of temporary separateness. They put them down before they descend to the mid-canopy. They pick them up when they return.

Some travelers have gone to the edge of the canopy meridian. Not many. The journey is long and dangerous. But the marks in the shelters describe it now — a new category of journey, not inward and downward but outward and along, toward the place where the canopy thins and the organism's edge frays into individual trees and then into scrubland and then into bare rock under open sky. The marks describe what they found there: space, wind, stone, the unmediated sun, the terrible freedom of a landscape that is not alive, that does not hum, that does not know them. Some found it unbearable and returned. Some are still there.

And in the deep canopy, in the dark, the song has changed. Not drastically. Not in a way that disrupts the ancient dialogue. But the new interval persists, has been elaborated, has become part of the harmonic vocabulary, and the organism has grown around it, has built into its structure — in a few places, in a few of the oldest and largest trunks — *hollows*. Not decay. Not damage. Spaces. Resonant chambers in the heartwood where the song reverberates differently, where the sound includes the shape of silence, where the wood has learned to hold emptiness as a structural feature rather than a flaw.

The mid-canopy people, the bark-skinned ones, the ones deepest in the merger — some of them have found these hollows. Have entered them. Have sat in the silence that the organism itself has now learned to produce. And in that silence, a few of them have felt something they have not felt since before the bark grew over their skin, since before the network absorbed their boundaries, since before the closing spiral closed:

Themselves.

Just for a moment. Just a flicker. A single thread of individual consciousness, surfacing briefly in the vast weave of the network, recognized, felt, and — not pulled out. Not separated. That is not what happens. What happens is that the organism *feels them feeling themselves*, and instead of absorbing the sensation, instead of incorporating it, the organism — for the first time, with the clumsiness of a mind attempting something genuinely new after millennia of habit — *holds space for it*. Lets the thread remain a thread. Lets the individual moment persist without dissolving it.

The organism is learning something.

It is learning that a companion does not have to become part of you to be *with* you. It is learning this the way it learns everything — slowly, through growth, through structural adaptation, through the incremental modification of a body so vast that each new lesson takes centuries to propagate. But it is learning.

And in the shelters, on the walls, among the spirals and the branch-maps and the closing spirals and the new star-glyphs, there is a new mark. The travelers have a glyph for it. It is not the closing spiral. It is not the outward-pointing marks of the edge-journeyers. It is something else — a spiral that opens. That expands outward. That does not close.

A glyph for a relationship that does not end in merger.

A glyph for *with* that is not *within*.

A glyph for love that does not consume.

---

The wise power, seeing this from its great distance, would feel what the wise feel when the thing they hoped for begins to happen and the thing they feared does not: not triumph, not satisfaction, but a quieter thing. A settling. The sense of a held breath finally released.

And it would keep its distance. And it would not return. And the canopy would keep growing, and the travelers would keep traveling, and the song would keep changing, and the organism would keep learning, slowly, slowly, the hardest lesson any mind of any speed can learn:

That the most profound form of connection is the one that allows the other to remain other.

That the deepest love is the love that does not close the spiral.

That the wisest growth is the growth that makes room.
