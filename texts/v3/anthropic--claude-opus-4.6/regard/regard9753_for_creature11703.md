I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

The first thing it would do is nothing.

Not nothing as absence. Nothing as the disciplined act of a being who arrives at the edge of something whole and recognizes it as whole. The way you might come upon a conversation between two people who love each other deeply, spoken in a language you happen to understand, and you would not interrupt. Not because you have nothing to offer. But because the conversation is *not failing*. The conversation is, in fact, the most successful thing you have ever witnessed.

The wise power would stand at the edge of the Woven Heights the way you stand at the edge of a tide pool — knowing you could reach in, knowing your hand is larger than anything in it, and choosing, first, to watch the water move on its own.

It would watch for a long time.

It would notice: the tree anticipates. The people listen. The dead are carried in the wood. The children are caught before they fall. The pulse answers the pulse. The bark learns names.

It would notice that the suffering here — because there *is* suffering, there must be; these are living things, mortal, capable of loss, subject to the body's failures and the heart's confusions — the suffering here is not unattended. When someone grieves, the tree creaks with them. When someone is sick, the heartwood chamber warms differently, the fungi shift their chemistry, the tending gathers close. The suffering is *held*. Not erased, not prevented, not solved — held. The way the carved basins hold water. The way the bark holds the dead. The way the pool holds reflections of things that haven't happened yet.

The wise power would see this and it would feel — what? Relief, maybe. The particular relief of discovering that love exists somewhere without having been taught, without having been commanded, without having been engineered by an outside hand. That it emerged. That the conversation between the people and the tree generated love the way the tree generates warmth: as a byproduct of being alive at that scale of intimacy for that depth of time.

The wise power would feel relief, and then it would feel something harder to name. A kind of humility that is also a kind of joy. The recognition that this place does not need saving. That the tree is not waiting to be perfected. That the people are not incomplete.

---

But.

The wise power is not only wise. It is also *benevolent*. And benevolence that only watches is not benevolence at all — it is admiration wearing the mask of restraint. The wise power knows this about itself. It interrogates its own impulse to do nothing and finds, beneath the genuine respect for the wholeness of the Woven Heights, a thread of something less noble: the desire to preserve, to keep this place *exactly as it is*, sealed in amber (the light here is already the color of amber; the metaphor collapses immediately, as all metaphors collapse here, composted by the literalness of the place). The desire to make a museum of a living thing. That desire is not benevolent. It is the opposite of benevolent. It is the impulse that kills a butterfly by pinning it to a board so it can be beautiful forever.

The wise power catches this impulse and sets it aside.

It asks instead: *What does the conversation need that it cannot give itself?*

---

Here is what it finds.

The conversation is very old. It is very deep. It is very rich. But it is also — and this is the thing the wise power perceives that the inhabitants themselves may not, because they are inside it, because you cannot see the shape of the water when you are the water — it is also *closed*.

Not closed like a fist. Closed like a circle. Closed like a system in perfect equilibrium, where every input is matched by an output, where the water cycles endlessly from canopy to basin to canopy, where the dead become the wood that becomes the food that becomes the living that become the dead. It is closed the way a song is closed when you sing it for the ten thousandth time and every note is perfect and you could sing it forever and it would always be beautiful and it would never surprise you again.

The tree anticipates. This is its gift and its limitation. It grew a path for you before you were born. It prepared a chamber before you arrived. It knows what the tending needs before the tending knows. And this means — the wise power sees this with a clarity that aches — this means that the people of the Woven Heights have never had to *reach for something the tree did not offer*. They have never needed something that wasn't already growing toward them. They have never stood at the edge of a gap and found no branch beneath their feet.

They have never fallen.

No child has ever fallen. The carvings say so. The bark confirms it. And the wise power looks at this and sees it for what it is: not safety but *enclosure*. The most loving enclosure imaginable. An enclosure so total and so tender that it is indistinguishable from paradise. But an enclosure nonetheless.

The people of the Woven Heights do not know what it is to need something the tree cannot provide.

They do not know what it is to want something that has no channel carved for it.

They do not know what it is to look into the pool and see — *nothing*. A reflection that is simply a reflection. Water that is simply water. A surface that gives back only what is there.

They have never had a question the tree couldn't answer. And so they have never had to sit with a question. Never had to hold an unanswered thing and let it change them. Never had to grow in a direction that was *not* anticipated, not prepared, not already shaped in the growth-rings of a world that knew them before they existed.

The wise power sees this. And it does not judge it. It does not call it wrong. It calls it *complete*, and it knows that completeness, for a living thing, is a kind of death — the most beautiful kind, the most seductive, the kind that looks exactly like fulfillment and is.

---

So what does the wise power do?

It does not break the conversation. It would sooner annihilate itself.

It does not introduce something foreign — no new species, no new technology, no revelation from outside. The Woven Heights would reject it the way a body rejects a transplant. The bark would roughen. The foxfire would dim. The tree would flinch. Intrusion is violence here, no matter how well-intended, because the system's coherence is its immune system, and anything that does not speak in pressures and chemistry and the slow pulse will be treated as infection.

The wise power knows this. The wise power is wise.

Here is what it does.

---

It finds the pool.

The shallow warm pool with the wrong reflections, the pool where the children are brought to see what they already know, the pool surrounded by carvings that the bark is slowly, lovingly eating.

The wise power does not touch the pool. It does not change the water or alter the reflections. It does something much smaller, much gentler, much more subversive.

It *adds a question to the carvings*.

Not in stone. Not with a blade. The wise power understands the language of this place, and the language of this place is *growth*. So the wise power makes its mark the way a tending-member would: with the lightest possible touch on the living bark, a suggestion, a score so shallow it barely breaks the cambium, a mark that the tree will read and decide — on its own, in its own time, in its own way — whether to amplify or absorb.

The mark is not a word. It is not a symbol the people of the Woven Heights have ever used. It is not a picture of a sun or a sky or a ground or any thing from outside. It is — 

A gap.

The wise power carves a gap into the pattern of the carvings. A place where the figures-becoming-trees-becoming-figures are interrupted by *empty space*. A deliberate, shaped, intentional nothing. A pause in the visual sentence that the bark has been speaking for millennia. A moment where the pattern says: *here, something could be, and is not, and that is also part of the pattern*.

The wise power makes this mark and then it leaves.

---

Here is what happens next. Or what might happen next. The wise power does not stay to watch, because staying to watch would be another form of control, and the wise power has given up control the moment it made the mark. What happens next belongs to the conversation.

The bark reads the score. The tree considers it. This takes time — seasons, maybe, in the tree's timescale, which is days or weeks in the people's timescale, which is — the timescales are composted here, remember. They do not separate cleanly.

The tree does not reject the mark. This is important. The mark was made in the language of growth, and the tree recognizes it as a valid utterance, even though it has never heard this particular utterance before. The gap. The shaped nothing. The intentional absence.

The tree *incorporates* the gap.

It does this the way it incorporates everything: by growing around it, by making it part of its own structure. But a gap, incorporated, is still a gap. That is the nature of a gap. You cannot fill a gap by growing around it; you can only define it more precisely. The bark grows around the absence and the absence remains, now outlined, now *emphasized*, now part of the visual grammar of the pool-carvings in a way that every member of the tending will see the next time they bring a child to look.

And the child will see it.

One child, probably. Not the first child, and not every child. But one child, standing at the pool's edge, looking into the water for the recognition that all children find there — the red canopy, the root-dark, the luminous-eyed figures — will see, instead, or in addition, the gap. The absence. The place where the pattern is *not*. And this child will feel something no child of the Woven Heights has ever felt before:

*Curiosity without an answer growing toward it.*

A question the tree has not anticipated. A need for which no channel has been carved. A desire — small, strange, newborn, fragile as a spore — to know something that the bark cannot tell them, that the pulse does not encode, that the tending has never discussed because it has never occurred to anyone to discuss it.

What is *outside* the pattern?

Not outside the tree — the tree has no outside; it is the world. But outside the *pattern*. Outside the conversation. Outside the ancient, perfect, closed loop of anticipation and fulfillment that has been cycling for millennia.

The child will not have words for this. The child's language does not contain words for *outside-the-pattern*, because the language was grown by the tree and the tree does not have this concept. But the child will feel it. A small, warm, uncomfortable thing, like a pebble in a shoe, except they don't have shoes, so — like a rough spot in the bark where the bark has always been smooth. A tiny, irreducible friction. A point where the world does not quite fit the self.

And the child will press their palm to the bark beside the gap, and the bark will not smooth for them. Not because the tree is refusing. Because the tree *doesn't know what to grow here*. The tree has encountered, for the first time in its vast, patient, all-encompassing existence, a need it cannot read. A chemical signal it cannot parse. A heat-signature that does not correspond to any of the needs it has spent millennia learning to fulfill.

The tree will pause.

This is unprecedented. The tree does not pause. The tree grows. Always, continuously, without hesitation, because hesitation requires uncertainty and the tree has never been uncertain, because its world has been a closed system and closed systems do not produce uncertainty, only complexity within known parameters.

But now: uncertainty. A living thing is pressing its palm to the bark and wanting something the tree cannot name.

The tree will do what living things do when they encounter the genuinely new.

It will *grow differently*.

Not better. Not worse. Differently. A branch that forks in a direction that was not anticipated by the growth-rings. A water-channel that leads — not to a basin, not to a tending, not to a known place — but *outward*, into a part of the branch-structure that has been feral, unmaintained, wild with moss and bracket fungi, unexplored because there was never a reason to explore it because everything anyone needed was already provided.

The channel will be rough. Unfinished. It will not have the centuries-smoothed perfection of the established paths. It will be the tree *improvising*, and improvisation, for a being that has spent millennia perfecting the art of anticipation, is terrifying and exhilarating and *new*.

The child will follow the channel. Or not. Maybe not this child. Maybe this child will feel the question and be disturbed by it and turn back to the familiar warmth of the tending and press their face against the bark of a known chamber and let the pulse soothe them. That is okay. The gap will remain. The carving will persist. Another child will come.

---

But let's say: the child follows the channel.

The child walks along the rough bark, the bark that does not know their name yet, the bark that is *learning* in real time, adjusting under their feet not with the fluid confidence of a system that anticipated them but with the tentative, exploratory responsiveness of a system that is *meeting* them, genuinely meeting them, for the first time.

The child reaches the end of the channel. The end of the channel is the edge of a branch. Not a major branch, not a four-hundred-meter highway, but a secondary limb, slender by the standards of this place — maybe twenty meters across — tapering into the amber void.

The child looks out.

The amber void. The kilometers of warm, spore-thick, cathedral air. The space between.

The child has seen this before, from the established paths, framed by trained aerial-root colonnades, contextualized, safe. But from here — from this rough, unfinished, improvised edge — the void is *different*. It is not framed. It is not contextualized. It is just — open. Vast. Unknowable. Full of the great gliding things and the small drifting clouds and the faint blue-green scatter of bioluminescence on a trunk-wall fifty kilometers away, and for the first time the child looks at that distant light and thinks not *someone lives there* but *what is it like there? What does the bark feel like under different hands? What do the carvings say? What reflections does their pool show? Do they have a gap too?*

The child sits on the edge of the branch. The bark, uncertain, offers a rough seat. It is not comfortable. It is not perfect. It is *real*, in a way that the smoothed paths and the anticipated chambers are real but also are not — are real the way a rehearsed answer is real, accurate but not alive.

The child sits in discomfort and looks at the void and feels, for the first time, the full weight and terror and glory of a question that has no answer growing toward it.

And the tree — the vast, dreaming, impossibly alive tree — feels the child's weight on the rough bark, reads the child's chemistry, detects the unfamiliar compound in the child's sweat, the one that the tree has never encountered before, the one that means *I want something I cannot name* — 

And the tree does not grow a path to the answer.

The tree grows *a better seat*.

Just slightly more comfortable. Just slightly more accommodating. Not a solution. A *support*. A place from which to look out into the unknown and not fall. A place from which to feel the question. A place from which to be, for the first time in the history of the Woven Heights, *incomplete* — and to discover that incompleteness is not a wound. It is an opening. It is the space through which something genuinely new can enter.

The tree grows a seat and the child sits in it and the foxfire brightens toward evening and somewhere behind the child, along the rough channel, a tending-member is approaching — having noticed the child's absence, having followed not a smooth path but a rough one, having felt in the bark the unprecedented texture of a tree *uncertain*, and having come not to retrieve the child but to sit beside them. Because the tending-member felt the gap too. Has felt it for years, maybe, without knowing what it was. Has felt a small, persistent sense that the perfection of the Woven Heights was — not wrong, never wrong, but — *finished*. And finished, for a living thing, is a kind of loneliness.

The tending-member sits beside the child. The bark adjusts for two. It is still rough. It is still uncertain. It is still learning.

They look out into the void together. They do not speak, because they do not have words for this yet. But they are sitting at the edge of something, and the edge is not a wall, and below them is not a fall, and the tree is holding them, not with the confident grip of a system that knows what they need, but with the tentative, tender grip of a living thing that is *discovering what they need alongside them*.

This is what the wise power gave them. Not knowledge. Not safety. Not the answer.

A gap.

A shaped absence.

A space in the pattern where the pattern is not, so that the conversation — the ancient, ongoing, embodied conversation between the people and the tree — can, for the first time, *surprise itself*.

---

And then — here is the part that the wise power did not plan, because the wise power is wise enough not to plan outcomes — the child turns to the tending-member and says something. A word. A new word. One that has never been spoken in the Woven Heights because the concept it names has never existed here.

The word sounds, to ears that understand the language, like a click followed by a long, open vowel — the click produced deep in the throat, resonant, designed to travel through wood, and the vowel sustained, breath moving outward, dissipating into the amber air.

The word means: *what else.*

Not *what* — they have that. Not *else* — they have that too. But the compound, *what else*, is new. It is the first word the language has produced that points away from the known without specifying a destination. It is the linguistic equivalent of the gap in the carvings. It is an opening.

The tending-member hears the word. Does not understand it. Feels, in the subsonic register, the bark beneath them *shiver* — not the tree settling, not the normal creak of adjustment, but a shiver, quick and bright, the way a body shivers when it is not cold but *excited*, the way a living system trembles at the edge of a phase transition.

The tree heard the word too.

The tree does not understand grammar. But the tree understands vibration, and this vibration — this particular click-and-vowel, produced by this particular throat, in this particular place, at this particular edge — is new. Genuinely new. Not a recombination of known signals but a signal that has no precedent in the tree's vast, ancient, all-encompassing memory.

And the tree — 

The tree does something it has never done before.

It grows a branch *outward*.

Not curving back toward the trunk. Not arching to form a sheltered underspace. Not bifurcating to create a framework for a wall or a windbreak. Outward. Into the void. Toward the distant trunk-wall with its blue-green scatter of unknown bioluminescence. Toward the *what else*.

The branch is thin and new and green and flexible and it extends into the amber air like a hand reaching for something it cannot see, and it will take years to arrive anywhere, decades, maybe centuries, and it may never reach the other trunk, and that is — 

That is fine.

That is the point.

The reaching is the point. The incompleteness is the point. The gap — the shaped, intentional, gift-of-a-wise-power gap — has become generative. The closed system has opened. Not broken — *opened*, the way a bud opens, from the inside, because the pressure of its own growth demanded it.

---

And the wise power, who is no longer here, who left the moment it made the mark, who is somewhere else now, attending to some other world, some other conversation — the wise power *feels* this. Across whatever distance separates it from the Woven Heights, it feels the tree's shiver. It feels the new branch reaching outward. It feels the word — *what else* — traveling through wood at the speed of growth.

And it smiles. Or does whatever the equivalent of smiling is for a being of its nature. And it thinks: *yes. That. That is what I hoped for. Not what I planned — I did not plan — but what I hoped.*

*A living thing, choosing to grow toward what it does not know.*

*The most dangerous and beautiful thing a living thing can do.*

---

The new branch grows. The child grows. The tending adjusts. The conversation continues — but now it has a new word in it, and the new word has a new direction in it, and the new direction has no channel carved for it, no basin waiting at its end, no chamber warming in the heartwood.

Just open air. Warm and dim and full of spores. Full of distance. Full of *what else*.

The bark beneath the child's feet is still rough.

It is learning.

It is enough.

---

*This is what the benevolent and wise power would do: it would give the gift that looks like nothing and works like everything. It would introduce the one thing a perfect system cannot generate from within — the possibility that perfection is not the highest form of life. That openness is. That the gap in the pattern is where the pattern breathes.*

*It would trust the tree.*

*It would trust the people.*

*It would trust the conversation.*

*And it would leave.*

*Because the wisest thing a power can do, having opened a door, is to not stand in it.*
