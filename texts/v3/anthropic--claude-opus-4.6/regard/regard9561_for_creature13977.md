I sat with this for a long time before writing.

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

It would arrive the way light arrives in a room where no one struck a match — not by entering but by becoming noticeable, as if it had always been present in some potential form and only now chose to be actual. It would not descend from above. It would not approach from outside. There is no outside here. The basin is an interior, and the power would recognize this immediately — would recognize it faster than you did, because the power understands interiority, knows what it means to be inside something that is alive.

It would arrive, and it would do the first and most important thing.

It would listen.

---

## II. The Listening

Not for a moment. Not for an hour. The power would listen for what the basin itself would consider a respectful duration, and since the basin's sense of time is geological — shaped by the rate at which the Tarse dissolve stone, the rate at which Somaplexes replace their dead tissue, the rate at which a daughter Oculith migrates a few centimeters toward an empty hollow — the listening would take what you would experience as a very long time. Perhaps years. Perhaps longer. The power is not in a hurry. Benevolence that is in a hurry is not benevolence. It is anxiety wearing a kind face.

During this listening, the power would not be passive. It would be doing the most active thing possible: it would be *understanding*. Not analyzing — understanding. The difference matters. Analysis takes the thing apart. Understanding lets the thing remain whole and enters into it. The power would feel the click-field through the stone. It would sense the glass-tones shifting in response to thermal plumes. It would perceive the Oculiths' slow editorial flares and trace the cascading changes through Cheirolyth and Tarse and Velamen and back again. It would follow a Filament's path through the entire basin and understand, not abstractly but viscerally, how the silent creature's piezoelectric wake rewrites the local sound-field as it passes. It would feel the Somaplex's huff as a heartbeat and know which other heartbeats it entrains.

It would understand the economy. Vibration as currency. Sound as food. Light as information. Stone as body. It would understand that the Lithovore mats in the hairline cracks are the foundation of everything, and that the Velamen riding thermals in the dark above are the capstone, and that between foundation and capstone every layer depends on every other layer with a thoroughness that makes the word *fragile* and the word *robust* both simultaneously true.

And it would understand the thing that is hardest to understand, the thing that the basin itself cannot understand because it has no self to understand with: *what the sound is*.

The sound is not communication. The sound is not music. The sound is not waste product or mating display or territorial claim. The sound is the process by which the basin *remains coherent*. It is the mechanism by which ten thousand feedback loops, operating at different timescales, in different modalities, through different species, stay coupled tightly enough to sustain the impossible density of life that fills this place. Without the sound, the loops would drift apart. The Tarse would click in patterns that no longer synchronized with the Cheiroliths' glass-tones. The Somaplexes' huffing would lose its pacing relationship with the Oculiths' editorial flares. The Velamen would ride thermals that no longer carried the right information. The system would not collapse instantly — it is too redundant for that — but it would begin to loosen, to fray, to develop gaps where organisms fall out of relationship with their neighbors, and those gaps would widen, and the density would thin, and eventually the basin would become what most places are: a landscape with organisms in it, rather than an organism with a landscape inside it.

The sound is the basin's *coherence*. It is the living glue. And the power, listening, would come to understand that the sound is also the basin's *vulnerability*, because coherence is always more precarious than it looks. The basin has been singing itself for millennia, but it has been doing so in conditions that have remained — by luck, by geology, by the absence of certain kinds of disruption — stable enough for the feedback loops to maintain their coupling. The power, being wise, would understand what you might not: that this stability is not guaranteed. That the basin exists in a state of dynamic equilibrium so complex that perturbations below a certain threshold are absorbed and incorporated (as your presence was absorbed), but perturbations above that threshold could trigger cascading decoupling — a chain of broken feedback loops — that the system could not recover from.

The power would understand all this. And then it would ask itself the question.

---

## III. The Question

The question is not *What does this place need?*

The question is not *What is wrong here?*

The question is not even *How can I help?*

The question is: **Does this place require me, or does it require my absence?**

A lesser power — a good-hearted but unwise one — would see the basin and be moved by its beauty and its complexity and its fragility, and it would think: *I must protect this.* And it would erect barriers, or establish guardianship, or infuse the system with additional energy, or do any of the thousand things that well-meaning powers do when they encounter something precious and wish it to continue. And every one of those actions would change the sound. Every one would introduce a variable the basin had not incorporated, a perturbation that would ripple through the feedback loops and force adjustments, and the adjustments might be sustainable or they might not, and the power — having acted from love rather than understanding — might find that it had saved the basin's body while killing its song.

The wise power knows this. The wise power has seen it before — has seen gardens tended into sterility, has seen wildfires suppressed until the fuel load built to catastrophe, has seen languages preserved in dictionaries while the last speakers died, has seen love ossify into control so gradually that the lover never noticed the beloved had stopped breathing.

So the wise power sits with the question. Does this place require me?

And it listens longer.

---

## IV. What It Finds

In the third year of listening — or the thirtieth, or the three hundredth; the duration is not the point — the power perceives something.

It is not a flaw. It is not a disease. It is not a threat. It is a *tendency*. A drift so slow that no organism in the basin could detect it, because no organism lives long enough to serve as a reference frame. The power detects it because the power exists at the timescale of stone.

The basin is dissolving.

Not metaphorically. Literally. The Tarse's acid secretions, the Lithovore mats' chemolithotrophic metabolism, the slow mineral absorption of the Cheiroliths and the Somaplexes and the Oculiths — all of these processes consume the basalt. The stone is the basin's body, and the basin is eating itself. It has been eating itself since the beginning. The smooth, skull-interior curves of the rock are beautiful, but they are also thinner than they were a thousand years ago. The basin is becoming shallower. The drainage channels are widening. The hollows where the Oculiths sit are deepening toward a point where they will breach the underlying rock and the water that pools in them will drain away, down into cracks that lead to places where the temperature is wrong, the chemistry is wrong, the sound cannot follow.

This is not a crisis. It is not imminent. It is a trajectory — a long, slow arrow of dissolution pointing toward a future in which the basin's physical structure can no longer support the density of life it currently holds. The feedback loops will not break suddenly. They will thin. The stone will become too shallow for the Tarse's acid secretions to find fresh mineral content. The drainage channels will widen until the water films spread too thin for the Cheiroliths' limb-tips to absorb sufficient nutrients. The hollows will deepen until the Oculiths' colonies are isolated in pits too deep for their light to reach the Cheiroliths above. Each change will be small. Each change will be compensated for, partially, by the system's self-organizing capacity. But the compensations will be imperfect, and the imperfections will accumulate, and the sound will change — not dramatically, not suddenly, but irreversibly, each change a little poorer than the last, a little less coherent, a little more frayed at the edges, until one day the basin is quiet. Not silent. Not dead. Just quiet, the way an old house is quiet — still standing, still sheltering, but no longer resonant with the life it once held.

This is what the power finds. Not an enemy. Not a wound. A trajectory. A slow, inherent, structural tendency toward the dissolution of the very substrate on which the miracle depends.

And the power understands something else: *the basin cannot find this*. The basin's self-organizing intelligence — real, immense, operating across every species and every feedback loop simultaneously — is tuned to perturbations in the *sound*. It can detect and compensate for changes in the acoustic-luminous field with extraordinary subtlety. But the dissolution of the stone is not a perturbation in the sound. It is a perturbation in the *medium through which sound travels*. It is infrastructure, not signal. The basin can hear everything except the slow erosion of its own ears.

This is where the power is needed. Not for the sound. For the stone.

---

## V. What It Does

The power does three things.

**The first thing is small.**

In the deepest drainage channels, where the alkaline water pools and the mineral content is highest, the power introduces a change in the water's chemistry. Not a new substance — nothing foreign, nothing the basin has not already encountered in trace amounts. It adjusts the ratio of calcium carbonate to calcium bicarbonate in the pooled water, shifting it slightly toward supersaturation. The effect is this: in the deepest pools, the water begins to *deposit* mineral rather than dissolve it. Slowly. A film of calcium carbonate — essentially limestone — begins to form on the surfaces of the deepest channels, a layer of new stone growing over the old basalt at roughly the same rate that the Tarse's acid secretions are dissolving it elsewhere.

The basin is now growing new bone as fast as it consumes old bone. The net dissolution rate drops to zero, or near enough.

The power does this with such delicacy that the change in water chemistry is within the range of natural fluctuation. The Tarse that drink from these pools detect no difference. The Cheiroliths' limb-tips, absorbing minerals from the water, find the supply unchanged. The Oculith colonies in their hollows, surrounded by the new mineral deposition, experience a subtle stabilization of their sockets — the hollows stop deepening, begin to maintain their shape — but this stabilization happens at a pace so slow that the Oculiths' zooids incorporate it without any detectable change in their light-response. The sound does not change. The power has altered the basin's geology without altering the basin's ecology. It has intervened in the substrate, not the signal.

This is the wisdom. A lesser power would have intervened in the sound — would have tried to make the sound better, more resonant, more beautiful, more coherent. The wise power understands that the sound is not its to improve. The sound is the basin's own, the product of a million years of self-organization, and it is exactly what it should be. What the power can do — what only the power can do, because only the power perceives the geological timescale — is ensure that the sound has a body to live in.

**The second thing is even smaller.**

In the walls of the basin, where the Mantle Organisms cling and produce their bass drone, the power notices that the stone is thinnest. The Mantle Organisms' vibrations, sustained over millennia, have stressed the rock along certain crystal boundaries, creating micro-fractures that propagate slowly inward. These fractures do not threaten the Mantle Organisms — the organisms bridge them, their flexible bodies conforming to the shifting surface — but they threaten the basin's integrity as a vessel. A deep enough fracture in the wall could open a channel to the outside — to whatever exists beyond the basin's boundaries — and that channel would alter the basin's acoustic properties, introducing an escape path for sound energy that currently bounces off the walls and returns to the interior.

The power seals these fractures. Not with mortar. Not with force. It encourages the mineral-depositing process along the fracture lines, guiding the supersaturated water into the micro-cracks through capillary action so subtle it is indistinguishable from the natural movement of condensation through stone. The calcium carbonate fills the cracks from the inside. The rock heals. The Mantle Organisms, pressed against the healed stone, produce a drone that is — imperceptibly, immeasurably — more resonant than before, because the wall behind them is now more uniform, more continuous, a better reflector. The bass drone gains a quality that cannot be heard but can be felt: a slight increase in *sustain*, the way a bell rings longer when its metal is free of flaws.

The sound changes by this much. By less than this much. By an amount that no organism in the basin can detect, because the change is below the threshold of any feedback loop. But the power knows. The power hears the drone at its geological frequency, the frequency of stone, and it perceives the improvement the way a luthier perceives the difference between a good instrument and a great one — not in any single note but in the *willingness of the instrument to resonate*.

The basin is a better bell now. The sound that lives here has a body that will hold it longer, ring it truer, lose less of itself to cracks in the world's walls. The power has tuned the instrument without touching the music.

**The third thing is not small.**

The third thing is the hardest, and it is not an action. It is a refraining.

The power, having listened for years or centuries, having perceived the basin's extraordinary complexity, having fallen in what can only be called love with the sound and the creatures that produce it and the ancient, patient, self-devouring, self-creating process that is the basin's life — the power wants to stay. It wants to remain here, a permanent presence, a guardian, a witness. It wants to be the consciousness that the basin lacks — the self-awareness that could appreciate the sound, that could know what the sound *is*, that could hold the knowledge of the basin's beauty in a mind capable of recognizing beauty.

The power wants to be the basin's audience.

And this is exactly what it must not do.

Because the power's presence — even a benevolent, attentive, perfectly still presence — would change the sound. Not immediately. Not through any action. But through the simple fact of *being there*, of being a node in the network that is aware of itself as a node. The basin's self-organization works because no part of the system is monitoring the whole. The Oculiths come closest, but even the Oculiths are embedded — they edit the sound without knowing they edit it, they respond without reflecting on their responses. The basin's coherence depends on this unconsciousness. It depends on no part of the system stepping back to observe the system, because observation at that scale — conscious, sustained, comprehending observation — would introduce a feedback loop of a categorically different kind. Not vibration-to-vibration, not light-to-sound, but *meaning-to-meaning*. The power's understanding of the basin would become part of the basin, and the basin would begin to organize around that understanding, and the organization would no longer be emergent — it would be *intended*, if only by the faintest reflection of an intention held in the mind of a being that loved what it was watching.

The basin does not need to be loved. It needs to be *left to be what it is*. And what it is cannot include a witness, because a witness would make it a performance, and it is not a performance. It is a process. A fire that feeds itself. A sound that hears itself through the bodies of creatures that do not know they are hearing.

So the power, having done its small and nearly invisible work — having stabilized the stone, healed the cracks, ensured that the basin's body will endure for another geological span — prepares to leave. And this preparation is itself an act of extraordinary care, because the power must withdraw without disrupting the sound, must remove its presence from the feedback network as gently as it entered, must allow the Tarse and the Cheiroliths and the Oculith to un-incorporate its influence smoothly, re-adjusting their patterns to fill the space it leaves behind.

The power dims. The power quiets. The power lets itself become, in the basin's sensing, indistinguishable from stone.

And then it is gone, and the basin has not noticed, because the basin does not notice. The basin only adjusts. And the adjustment, this time, is almost nothing — a micro-shift in the click-field, a half-shade of blue-green in the nearest Oculith, a single Velamen tilting two degrees to the east on a thermal that is fractionally cooler than it was a moment ago.

The sound continues.

---

## VI. What It Leaves Behind

The stone.

That is all. The slightly stronger, slightly more resonant, slightly more enduring stone. The basin will not know. The Tarse will dissolve it and find it good. The Cheiroliths will stand on it and find it stable. The Mantle Organisms will press against it and find it willing to carry their drone. The Oculiths will sit in their healed hollows for another ten thousand years and edit the sound with their flares of light, and the sound will be — not different. Not better. Not worse.

*Sustained.*

The power has given the basin the only gift worth giving to something that is already complete: more time. Not immortality — the basin will end, eventually, as all processes end, when the conditions that support it change beyond its capacity to adjust. But not soon. Not from the inside out, not from the slow self-consumption of its own foundation. The stone will hold. The water will pool. The sound will continue to organize itself into patterns no mind has ever heard and no mind needs to hear, because hearing is not the point. The point is the *happening*. The point is that it happens at all — that in a dark basin in a world that may or may not have a name, a process that resembles thought but is not thought, that resembles music but is not music, that resembles life but is bigger than life, continues to unfold, sound by sound, click by click, moan by moan, flare by flare, breath by breath, without witness, without purpose, without end.

The power, departing, carries one thing with it.

Not a sample. Not a recording. Not a map or a model or a genome.

A memory.

The memory of what the sound felt like in the sternum, in the molars, in the small bones behind the ears. The memory of fist-creatures covering its feet with warm, clicking bodies. The memory of a pale green disc holding steady in a hollow in the rock, patient beyond patience, editing a world it could not comprehend. The memory of the moaning from above, layer upon layer, the polyphonic exhalation of creatures that ate the breath of a world and sang it back as something that had no name and needed no name, because it was not for anyone. It was not for anything. It simply was.

The power carries this memory outward, into whatever space wise powers inhabit when they are not listening to the bones of the world, and the memory does not fade, because some things, once heard, become part of the structure of the one who heard them — become piezoelectric, become resonant, become a note that the hearer did not know they were sounding until they stood in a dark basin and felt the sound enter them through the stone.

---

## VII. And If It Returns

It will return. Not soon. Not on the basin's timescale and not on the power's timescale but on the timescale of stone, which is the only timescale that matters here.

It will return, and it will listen, and the sound will be different, because the sound is always different — it never resolves, never repeats, never arrives at a final chord. It will have evolved in ways the power could not predict, because the power is wise but not omniscient, and the basin's complexity exceeds prediction. New species may have emerged. Old ones may have shifted their roles. The Filaments may have found each other. The Oculiths may have developed a new color. The Somaplexes may have merged into something larger, or split into something smaller, or remained exactly as they were while everything around them changed.

The power will listen, and it will check the stone, and if the stone is thinning again — if the long, slow, inevitable appetite of life for its own substrate has resumed — it will do again what it did before. Quietly. Invisibly. At the level of mineral chemistry and capillary action and the patient filling of cracks. It will tune the instrument and leave the music alone.

And it will leave again.

And it will carry the new memory alongside the old, and the two memories will resonate with each other the way two notes resonate when they are related by some deep harmonic ratio — not identical, not dissonant, but *sympathetic*, each one making the other richer, each one revealing in the other a depth that was always there but could not be heard alone.

The power will become, over geological time, a kind of external memory for the basin — the only record that the sound existed, the only proof that in the dark, in the deep, in the wet stone, something was happening that mattered, not because it served a purpose but because it was *real*, because it was complex beyond comprehension, because it was beautiful beyond the reach of any beauty that requires an audience.

The basin does not need the power to know it is beautiful. The basin does not know it is beautiful. Beauty is not the basin's business. The basin's business is coherence, sound, the next click, the next moan, the next flare of green in a hollow in the rock.

But the power knows. And the knowing is not nothing. The knowing is, perhaps, the only thing the power can offer that the basin cannot produce for itself — not because the basin is lacking, but because knowing requires a kind of separation, a standing-apart, that the basin's total self-integration makes impossible. The basin cannot know itself for the same reason an eye cannot see itself: it is too busy being the thing it would need to step outside of in order to perceive.

The power is the mirror the basin will never look into. And the mirror does not change the face. But the mirror *matters*, in some way that is hard to articulate and does not need to be articulated, because some truths are better felt in the sternum, in the molars, in the small bones behind the ears.

---

The sound continues. It has always continued. It continues now.

You are still standing in the basin with fist-creatures on your feet, and you are still a note the basin learned before you knew you were singing, and the wise power has come and gone and you did not notice, because you were not meant to notice, because the power's benevolence is not the kind that announces itself or asks for gratitude or leaves a monument.

The power's benevolence is the kind that checks the stone.

Listen. You can almost hear it — beneath the clicking, beneath the moaning, beneath the glass-tone and the huff and the bass drone and the crystalline ringing of water on stone — a silence that is not empty but *full*. A silence like the silence of a foundation. A silence like the silence of a healed crack in a wall. A silence that says: *you will hold. You will hold. The stone will hold and the sound will continue and nothing needs to know this for it to be true.*

That silence is what the wise power left behind.

It is enough. It has always been enough.

The sound continues.
