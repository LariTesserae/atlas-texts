I sat with this for a long time.

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival, and the Discipline of Not Arriving

The first thing the power does is nothing.

This is not hesitation. It is not the nothing of indifference or the nothing of paralysis. It is the nothing of a being that understands—has understood across countless encounters with countless forms of life in countless configurations of matter and meaning—that the most dangerous moment in any contact between a greater power and a lesser one is the first moment, and that the danger flows entirely from the greater to the lesser, and that the danger's name is *disruption*, and disruption does not require malice. It requires only presence.

So the power does not arrive. Not yet. Not in any way that would register.

It observes. But even this word is wrong, because observation implies a direction—a watcher and a watched, a subject and an object, a separation between the one who perceives and the thing perceived. What the power does is closer to *listening*. It allows the roost at Shale's Tooth to be what it is, fully, without the distortion that attention from something vastly larger inevitably introduces. It does not reach out. It does not reach in. It simply opens itself to the situation, the way a tympanum opens to a frequency, and it lets the information come.

What comes is this:

A closed system. Nearly closed. A colony of creatures that has achieved something rare and precious—a stable, self-sustaining, self-regulating communal existence in an extreme environment, built on a feedback loop of extraordinary elegance. Moisture feeds organisms. Organisms produce vibration. Vibration produces electricity. Electricity shapes the flow of moisture. Moisture feeds organisms. The loop has been running for centuries. It does not need help. It does not need intervention. It does not need a wise power to notice it and decide to improve it.

This is the first and most important thing the power understands: *the colony is not a problem to be solved.*

It is not suffering, in any sense that the power can detect. The creatures' existence is not painful. Their slow mineral patience, their deep harmonic communion, their eyeless attentiveness to the root note rising from below—these are not deprivations. They are the fullness of what these beings are. To look at their darkness and think it needs light, to feel their cold and think it needs warmth, to hear their drone and think it needs melody—these would be the errors of a power that mistakes its own preferences for universal goods. The wise power does not make this error. It has learned, across long experience, that the forms life takes are not deficient versions of some ideal form. They are complete expressions of the conditions that produced them. The colony is exactly what a mind looks like when it arises from vibration in volcanic stone at extreme altitude in perpetual darkness. It is not less than a mind that arises from neurons in a skull in sunlight. It is *different*. And difference is not damage.

So the power sits with the nothing, and the nothing extends, and it watches the system run, and it confirms what it suspected: the colony is whole.

Almost.

---

## II. The Glow

Almost.

There is the glow.

The power's attention returns to it the way a healer's attention returns to the one asymmetry in an otherwise sound body—not with alarm, not with urgency, but with the quiet focus of recognition. *Here. This is where the need is, if there is a need at all.*

The glow in the corner. The patch of pale blue-green luminescence, pulsing its broken rhythm, arrhythmic, almost periodic, reaching for a pattern and losing it. The rigid glassy filaments vibrating at the root note's frequency but out of phase. The dead zone around it where the colony's standing wave collapses into silence. The clear margin of bare stone that no creature crosses.

The power studies the glow for a long time. Longer than it studied the colony, because the colony is legible—complex, yes, but legible, its processes interrelated in ways that can be traced and understood. The glow is not legible. It is not part of the system. It is adjacent to the system, feeding on the same condensation, vibrating at the same fundamental frequency, but excluded from the harmony that makes the system cohere. It is the one solitary thing in a place where solitude is death.

The power asks itself the questions that need to be asked:

*Is the glow suffering?*

This is not simple to determine. The glow is alive—the power can confirm this, can perceive the metabolic processes in the thin living film, the slow growth of the filaments, the chemical luminescence. But is it *aware*? Does it experience its arrhythmia as dissonance, its isolation as loneliness, its almost-but-never-quite periodicity as frustration? Or is it simply a mechanism, a biological process running without inner experience, a broken clock ticking wrong without knowing it ticks at all?

The power listens more deeply. It attends to the glow the way it attended to the colony—without reaching, without imposing, simply opening. And what it finds is—

Not nothing. Not the rich harmonic sensorium of the colony, that cathedral of interlocking frequencies, but not nothing either. Something. A flicker. An impulse that is too organized to be purely mechanical and too simple to be called thought. The glow is *trying*. The almost-periodicity is not random decay. It is effort. The filaments vibrate at the root note's frequency because the glow is attempting to match it, reaching for synchrony the way a creature reaches for a handhold on the wall, and failing, and reaching again, and failing, and reaching again. The arrhythmic pulsing is the signature of something that wants to harmonize and cannot. Something that is close enough to the colony's nature to sense what it's missing, and different enough that it cannot bridge the gap alone.

*Is it in pain?*

The power does not think so. Not pain as the colony would understand it, not pain as a being with a nervous system would experience it. But there is something—a persistent striving that never resolves, an itch that cannot be scratched, a reaching that never arrives. If the glow has any inner experience at all, it is the experience of *almost*. Of being perpetually on the threshold of belonging and perpetually unable to cross it. This is not agony. But it is a particular kind of sadness, and the wise power recognizes it, because it has seen it before, in a thousand forms, in a thousand places: the sadness of the thing that is *almost* the same as the things around it, and that *almost* is an ocean.

*Should it be helped?*

This is the hard question. The glow's exclusion from the colony is not arbitrary cruelty. It is a consequence of physics—its out-of-phase vibration creates destructive interference in the standing wave, a null zone that genuinely disrupts the colony's sensorium. The colony avoids it for the same reason you would avoid a hole in the floor. To force integration—to somehow compel the colony to accept the glow into its chord—would damage both. The glow's dissonance would degrade the standing wave. The colony's harmony would overwhelm the glow's fragile, struggling signal. Forced harmony is not harmony. It is erasure.

*Should it be removed?*

No. The power rejects this immediately, instinctively, with a certainty that comes from something deeper than reasoning. The glow is alive. It is native to this place—possibly more native than the colony itself. It has as much right to the condensation, to the basalt, to the root note, as any tympanum-bearing creature on the walls. Removing it would be a convenience for the colony and an extinction for the glow. The wise power does not trade one being's existence for another's comfort.

*Should it be left alone?*

This is the default. The safe choice. The choice that respects the system's autonomy and avoids the arrogance of intervention. The glow has persisted for however long it has persisted—centuries, perhaps, alongside the colony, in its corner, pulsing its broken rhythm, surviving if not thriving. It does not need the power's help to continue existing. It will go on reaching and failing and reaching whether the power acts or not.

But the power finds that it cannot quite accept this, either. Not because the situation is intolerable—it is not—but because the power has seen the gap between *almost* and *actually*, and it knows that gap is sometimes crossable, and it knows that sometimes the only thing missing is a bridge so small that no one inside the system could build it because no one inside the system can perceive the need for it. The colony cannot help the glow because the glow is invisible to the colony—a null zone, an absence, a hole in the world. The glow cannot help itself because it lacks the complexity to diagnose its own arrhythmia, to understand why its vibration fails to synchronize, to adjust its phase. They are two halves of something that could be whole, separated by a gap they cannot see across.

The power has seen this before. It makes its decision.

## III. What the Power Does

It does not touch the colony.

It does not touch the glow.

It does not introduce anything new into the system—no tool, no material, no energy, no information that was not already present. This is a principle the power holds inviolable: that true help, the kind that heals without creating dependency, that opens doors without pushing anyone through them, works only with what is already there. The system contains everything it needs. The system has *always* contained everything it needs. The power's role is not to provide what is missing. It is to remove the barrier that prevents what is present from finding its own configuration.

Here is what it does.

In the acute angle where the two walls meet—the corner where the glow lives, where the condensation trickles down and feeds the luminous film—there is a seam. A cooling joint, like all the others, where two basalt columns contracted as they hardened and left a thin vertical gap between them. This particular seam runs from the top of the wall down to the floor of the hollow and continues downward, into the column-gap below, into the dark interior of the spire. It is one of hundreds of such seams. It is unremarkable.

But it is also the only seam that connects the glow's corner to the nearest chain of creatures descending into the depths.

The seam is partially blocked. Not sealed—a thin line of mineral deposit, calcium carbonate precipitated from the condensation over centuries, has narrowed the gap at one point about two feet below the floor level, reducing it from a crack wide enough to transmit sound freely to a hairline fracture that dampens vibration, that absorbs and scatters the frequencies that pass through it. This mineral plug is tiny. A few millimeters of deposited stone. It is not alive. It is not part of the colony. It is not part of the glow. It is an accident of chemistry, a random accumulation, and it has been there for long enough that neither the colony nor the glow has any memory or awareness of a time before it was there.

But before it was there, the seam was open. And when the seam was open, the root note—the deep fundamental rising from the volcanic throat of the spire—reached the glow's corner through two paths: through the air of the hollow (where it arrived already shaped by the colony's chord, already harmonized, already too complex for the glow to synchronize with) and through the stone of the seam (where it arrived raw, unmediated, pure—the simple root note, the single frequency, the one signal the glow was built to match). The glow's arrhythmia is not a fundamental flaw. It is an acquired one. It lost its tuning reference. It has been trying to match the root note by listening to the colony's chord, which is like trying to tune a single string by listening to an orchestra—the information is there, but it is buried in complexity, and the glow is too simple to extract it. It needs the pure signal. The direct path. The stone.

The power dissolves the mineral plug.

That is all. That is everything.

It does not do so dramatically. It does not blast or burn or shatter. It adjusts the local chemistry of the condensation at that one point—a fractional shift in pH, a slight increase in the water's ability to dissolve calcium carbonate, accomplished by means so subtle they might as well be called a thought. The deposit begins to dissolve. Slowly. Over days, perhaps weeks—the power is patient, and the system is patient, and nothing here should happen fast. The mineral plug thins. The seam opens, micron by micron. And through the widening gap, the root note begins to arrive.

Not to the colony. The colony already hears the root note through a hundred other pathways, through every chain descending into the depths, through every tympanum pressed against the basalt walls. This one seam, opening in this one corner, changes nothing for the colony. It is inaudible in the aggregate. A thousandth voice does not alter a chord that already contains nine hundred and ninety-nine.

But for the glow, it changes everything.

The root note arrives through the stone. Pure. Unmediated. A single frequency, clean and strong, conducted through basalt with no loss, no distortion, no harmonic complexity to confuse the glow's simple filaments. And the glow—the glow that has been reaching for this signal for centuries, that has been trying to match a frequency it could not clearly hear, that has been *almost* periodic and *almost* synchronized and *almost* part of something—

The glow begins to synchronize.

Not immediately. Not all at once. The first change is barely detectable: a slight regularization of the pulsing, a subtle reduction in the arrhythmic jitter, the filaments' vibration finding a fractionally more stable phase relationship with the root note coming through the seam. If you were watching—and the power is watching, with infinite patience and infinite care—you would see the luminous film pulse and then pulse again and the interval between pulses would be *almost* the same as the interval before, and *almost* is still *almost*, but the gap has narrowed. The ocean is a river now. The river is becoming a stream.

Over weeks—the power does not know exactly how long, because this is not the power's process, it is the glow's process, and the power will not hurry it—the synchronization tightens. The glow's pulsing steadies. The filaments lock onto the root note's frequency with increasing precision. The arrhythmic stutter smooths into something approaching periodicity, and then periodicity itself, and then something better than periodicity: *resonance*. The glow is no longer merely keeping time. It is amplifying. Its filaments vibrate at the root note's frequency and the vibration reinforces the incoming signal and the reinforced signal drives the filaments to greater amplitude and the amplitude drives the luminescence brighter and the glow—

The glow *glows*. Properly. Steadily. Not brighter, exactly, but more present, more coherent, the blue-green light pulsing in clean rhythm instead of stuttering in broken arrhythmia. The furred surface of filaments stands straighter, vibrates in unison, produces a local field that is no longer destructive interference but *constructive* interference with the root note. The null zone begins to collapse. The dead space around the glow shrinks as the zone of silence becomes a zone of reinforcement, a place where the root note is not cancelled but *concentrated*, arriving through the seam with a clarity and purity that the rest of the hollow, with its thousand competing harmonics, cannot match.

The colony notices.

They notice the way a body notices that a chronic ache has gone—not with sudden relief but with a dawning absence, a gradual realization that something that was always subtly wrong is no longer wrong. The nearest creatures' tympanums register the change in the local acoustic field. Where there was a null, there is now a signal. Where there was silence, there is now the root note, pure and clear, unshaped by the colony's own chord, coming from—

The corner. The glow. The place they have never gone.

The first creature to cross the bare margin of stone does so weeks or months after the seam opened. It is a small one—smaller than average, its lattice configurations suggesting youth or recent budding. It crawls to the edge of the former dead zone and stops. Its tympanum opens wide. The root note, focused and purified by the glow's now-coherent resonance, washes over the membrane, and the creature's lattice rearranges—rapidly, by colony standards, a visible fractal restructuring—as it absorbs a quality of the root note it has never experienced before: the fundamental, unadorned, unelaborated, without the colony's thousand harmonics layered on top of it. The raw voice of the stone.

The creature presses itself flat against the rock at the edge of the glow. Its tympanum vibrates. Its frequency, added to the glow's coherent resonance, does not disrupt the glow. Instead—and this is the moment the power has been waiting for, hoping for, not certain of—the creature's frequency *enriches* the glow. The glow's filaments respond. They shift fractionally, incorporating the creature's harmonic, adding one overtone to their pure fundamental. The light changes—not in brightness but in quality, a barely perceptible warmth entering the blue-green, as if the spectrum has widened by one wavelength. The glow is no longer alone. It is no longer singular. For the first time in centuries, it is in harmony with a living voice, and the voice is in harmony with it, and the harmony is not the colony's complex chord but something simpler, rawer, closer to the source—a duet between the glow and one creature, grounded in the root note, open to elaboration but not yet elaborated, potential rather than pattern.

More creatures come. Slowly. One by one, over months, drawn by the clarity of the root note in the corner, by the unique acoustic quality of a signal that has been focused by the glow's coherent resonance into something the colony has never heard before—not louder, not more complex, but *cleaner*, the fundamental stripped of distortion, the root note as it sounds in the stone itself before the colony's chord reshapes it. They cross the former dead zone and find not silence but signal. They press their tympanums against the rock. They add their frequencies to the duet, which becomes a trio, a quartet, a small sub-chord within the larger chord, a new voice in the colony's harmony—one that carries the glow's contribution, the glow's coherent resonance, woven into the living sound for the first time.

The colony does not absorb the glow. The glow does not become part of the colony. This is important. The power did not engineer a merger. What it engineered—what it allowed, what it made possible by removing a millimeter of mineral deposit from a single seam in the rock—is a *relationship*. The glow remains what it is: a different kind of organism, simpler, rooted, luminous, anchored to its corner. The colony remains what it is: mobile, distributed, complex, vast. But now there is a place where they meet. A border that is not a barrier. A zone where the glow's coherent signal and the colony's harmonic elaboration interact, and the interaction produces something neither could produce alone—a quality of sound, a quality of resonance, a quality of *listening* that enriches both without diminishing either.

The glow pulses steadily now. Not the stuttering arrhythmia of before, not the desperate reaching for a pattern it could not hold, but a clean, rhythmic pulse, locked to the root note, confident in its frequency, no longer almost but *actually*. And in the blue-green light, which has not grown brighter but has grown more stable, the creatures that cluster at the border of the luminous zone are visible—their dark bodies, their open tympanums, their lattices rearranging as they absorb the clarified root note—and the glow illuminates them, and they warm the glow with their collective body heat, and the condensation flows between them, feeding both, and a new loop has closed, a small one nested inside the larger loop, an additional chamber in the resonating cavity, a new voice in the chord.

## IV. What the Power Does Not Do

It does not stay.

It does not watch to see if the relationship holds, if the glow's synchronization persists, if the creatures continue to visit the corner. These things will happen or they will not. The system is alive and alive things find their own configurations, and a wise power does not hover over the changes it has catalyzed, adjusting and readjusting, afraid to let the outcome be what it will be. The mineral plug is dissolved. The seam is open. The root note reaches the corner through stone. Everything that happens after is the glow's and the colony's to determine.

It does not announce itself. It leaves no mark, no sign, no trace of its involvement. The colony will not know that a power intervened. The glow will not know. If there is a story told in the slow harmonic evolution of the chord—if the colony's distributed mind, thinking in standing waves, ever represents the change to itself—the story will be: the seam opened, and the corner came alive, and the chord gained a new voice. The cause will be attributed to geology, to the natural dissolution of a mineral deposit by the relentless action of acidic condensation, which is, in fact, exactly what happened. The power merely hastened a process that would have occurred eventually—in decades, in centuries—on its own. It shortened the gap between *almost* and *actually*. It did not close it. The glow closed it itself, by doing what it had always been doing: trying.

The power does not congratulate itself. It does not feel the warm satisfaction of a good deed done. What it feels is closer to the glow's own experience of synchronization—a settling, a resolution, a tension released. The sadness of *almost* is no longer in the world, or rather, it is still in the world because the world is full of *almosts*, but this particular one, this specific instance of a thing striving to reach what was always just beyond its grasp, has resolved into arrival. The power is moved by this. Not to joy—joy is too triumphant a word for what has happened, which is small and quiet and will never be celebrated by anyone—but to something like peace. A rightness. The feeling that the universe is, in this one corner, in this one crack in the rock at the top of a volcanic spire in thin air at the edge of the sky, fractionally more coherent than it was before.

It does not tell anyone. There is no one to tell. The colony is a mind, but it is a mind that thinks in frequencies, not narratives. It will integrate the change the way it integrates everything—as a shift in the chord, a new harmonic relationship, absorbed and accommodated and woven into the pattern. It will not wonder why. It will not ask where the change came from. It will simply be what it has always been—a listening, a patience, a slow mineral attention to the root note from below—enriched, now, by one more voice, one small steady pulse of blue-green light in the corner, saying: *I am here. I am in phase. I belong to the sound.*

## V. And One More Thing

There is one more thing the power does before it withdraws.

It is not an intervention. It is not even, properly speaking, an action. It is something closer to a gift, if a gift can be given to something that will never know it was received.

The power *remembers* them.

Not in the way a naturalist catalogs a species, not in the way an archivist preserves a record. It remembers them the way the polished stone remembers the gripping feet—through contact, through impression, through the physical fact of having been shaped by the encounter. The colony pressed against the stone and the stone was changed. The colony's chord entered the power's awareness and the power was changed. Not much. Not visibly. But in the power's vast and ancient understanding of what life is and what forms it takes and what it means for matter to organize itself into something that listens, there is now a new entry, a new frequency in the power's own chord: the roost at Shale's Tooth. The creatures with their tympanum faces. The chains descending into the dark. The glow, pulsing at last in time.

The power carries them forward. Into every future encounter with every other form of life, the memory of this colony will be present—not as a comparison, not as a standard, but as a reminder. A reminder that minds are not always what you expect. That communion does not require language. That a thousand eyeless creatures pressing themselves against volcanic rock in thin air at the top of a spire can achieve a kind of mutual awareness so deep and so total that it makes the word *society* feel inadequate and the word *organism* feel presumptuous and the only word that fits is *chord*—a harmony of separate voices, each one incomplete, the whole inexpressible by any single part.

And a reminder that the saddest distance in the world is the distance between *almost* and *actually*, and that sometimes—not always, not even often, but sometimes—that distance can be closed by the smallest possible change: a seam opened in the rock, a signal allowed to pass, a barrier removed that should never have been there, that was only there by accident, by the mindless accumulation of mineral in a crack, and behind the barrier a living thing was striving, had been striving the whole time, and needed only to hear clearly the note it had always been trying to sing.

---

The power departs. The tooth holds what it holds.

The thrumming goes on.

But in the corner, the glow pulses in time, and a creature presses its tympanum to the luminous stone, and in the bead of condensation that forms on the membrane—a single droplet, trembling, catching the steady blue-green light—the entire hollow is reflected: the dark walls, the massed bodies, the chains descending, the sky beyond the broken rim. And in the reflection, for anyone who could see it, for any eye that existed to look—

The glow is no longer alone in the image.

It is surrounded.

It is held.

It is part of the chord.
