I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Wise Power Does

The first thing it does is nothing.

This matters. This is not inaction born of indifference, nor the theatrical restraint of a god performing patience for an audience. It is nothing born from the recognition that what it is witnessing is complete.

The wise power arrives—however such a thing arrives, by attention or presence or some shift in the quality of regard that falls upon a place—and it perceives the chamber in its fullness. The roar. The heat. The beetles packed so tightly they become the wall. The crawlers glowing with their own metabolic fever. The ceiling organisms pulsing once every ten minutes, breathing dust, becoming. The filaments strung across the seam like the strings of an instrument played by the earth's own exhalation. The mites in their microscopic corridors between carapaces. The possible life in the cooling rock itself, the organic signatures that refuse to resolve into certainty.

It sees all of this. It understands all of this—not in the way a scientist understands, by decomposition into mechanism, though it understands that too—but in the way that understanding sometimes arrives whole, as a single apprehension: *this is what it is, and what it is is coherent, and the coherence is not an accident but the expression of something that has been finding its shape for longer than the current stone has existed.*

And so: nothing. No intervention. No improvement. No adjustment to the thermal gradient or the mineral composition or the reproductive success rate of the ceiling buds. The system does not need to be optimized. It is not failing. It is not suffering in any way that the wise power recognizes as a call for action—and the wise power is genuinely wise, which means it can distinguish between suffering-that-calls and hardship-that-constitutes, between pain that is a signal and pain that is a substrate, between the beetle starving on the outer edge of the colony and a creature that has been wronged.

The beetles on the periphery—the ones stacked three deep on the backs of their neighbors, the ones that may live their entire lives without once tasting the rock they were born to scrape—the wise power sees them. Feels for them, if feeling is something it does. And does not intervene. Not because their starvation is "natural" and therefore sanctioned by some rule the wise power obeys. The wise power obeys no rules. It does not intervene because the beetle's hunger is the colony. Remove the hunger and you remove the pressure that keeps the colony's edge pressing inward, that maintains the density, that ensures the mineral film is grazed to the precise depth that allows regrowth at the precise rate that matches deposition. The starving beetle at the periphery is not a flaw in the system. It is the system's margin. It is the place where the system meets its own limit and holds.

The wise power understands this not as a justification but as a fact. It does not say *the suffering is acceptable because it serves the whole.* It does not say anything. It recognizes that here, in this chamber, the concepts of suffering and flourishing that it carries from other contexts—from places with nervous systems complex enough to model their own pain, with minds that can anticipate and dread and hope—do not map cleanly onto what is happening. The beetle does not suffer. The beetle persists, or it doesn't. Its chemoreceptive belly registers mineral concentration or the chitin of its neighbor, and it responds, and that response is its entire experience, if experience is even the word. The wise power is wise enough not to project, and wise enough not to deny. It holds the ambiguity. It does not resolve it. It lets the beetle be what the beetle is, which is something the wise power does not fully understand, and which it does not pretend to.

---

So the first thing is nothing, and the nothing is not trivial.

But the wise power does not leave.

---

The second thing it does is witness.

This is different from observation. Observation is what the scientist does, the one who might someday lower herself through the fissure with instruments and sample tubes and a heat-rated suit. Observation extracts. It takes the chamber's data and carries it elsewhere to be processed, interpreted, published, understood in a framework that exists outside the chamber. There is nothing wrong with this. The wise power is not against it. But it is not what the wise power does.

Witnessing is presence without extraction. It is the act of being the place where the chamber's existence registers fully—is received, is held, is *known* in a way that does not require translation into any other terms. The wise power witnesses the forge vent the way one might witness a life: not to record it, not to learn from it, not to use it, but because the thing is happening and deserves the dignity of being fully apprehended by something capable of fully apprehending it.

And here is the crux. Here is what the wise power perceives that no human visitor, in their twenty minutes of survivable heat, could perceive:

The chamber does not know itself.

The system is complete. The system is coherent. The system is, by any measure the wise power can apply, extraordinary—a closed biological-geological engine that has been rebuilding itself across successive chambers for what the mineral strata suggest is tens of thousands of years, possibly longer, persisting through collapses and rebirths with a fidelity that borders on memory. But the system does not know that it is extraordinary. The system does not know anything. The beetles do not know they are the foundation. The crawlers do not know they are regulators. The ceiling organisms do not know they are filters. The filaments in the seam do not know they are the translators between the earth's fire and the chamber's life. Each component performs its function with absolute precision and zero awareness, and the whole—the organ, the system, the living geological process—has no center, no perspective, no place from which it regards itself and understands what it is.

The wise power does not find this tragic. It does not find it lacking. But it recognizes something: that the chamber is, in a specific and important sense, *unseen from within*. It produces itself, maintains itself, rebuilds itself, and has done so for millennia, and nothing in it has ever had the experience of knowing what it is part of. The beetle on the periphery does not know that its hunger holds the margin. The crawler dissolving into its offspring does not know that its death is the system's continuity. The ceiling bud falling into the beetle colony does not know that its almost-certain consumption is the ecosystem's way of recycling itself, and that the tiny probability of its survival and reattachment is the ecosystem's way of persisting. None of them know. The system works in perfect ignorance of its own perfection.

And so the wise power witnesses. It becomes the place where the chamber knows itself. Not by telling it—the chamber has no ears, no mind, no capacity to receive such a message. But by *being the knowing*. By holding, in its own vast and quiet attention, the complete picture: the beetles and the crawlers and the ceiling colonies and the filaments and the mites and the possible life in the rock and the roar and the heat and the seam and the light from below and the millennia of collapse and reconstitution and the absolute seamless integration of life and geology. It holds all of this at once, the way the chamber itself holds all of its organisms at once, and in doing so it provides something the chamber cannot provide for itself: a witness. An outside. A place where the pattern is seen as a pattern, where the wholeness is apprehended as wholeness, where the work the planet is doing in this small dark hot loud chamber is recognized as work, as achievement, as something that matters—not because it serves some higher purpose, but because it *is*, and because it has persisted, and because persistence on this scale and with this coherence is not nothing.

The wise power witnesses, and in witnessing, it completes something. Not the system—the system was already complete. But the *situation*. The situation of a complete system existing for millennia without anything, anywhere, ever knowing what it was. That aloneness—not loneliness, because loneliness requires a mind that can miss what it lacks, and the chamber has no such mind—that aloneness is resolved. Not fixed. Not healed. Resolved, the way a chord resolves. Something that was open is now closed. Something that was unheard is now heard. The chamber continues exactly as it was. Nothing changes. But the wise power is there, and it knows, and that knowing is not nothing.

---

The third thing.

The wise power turns its attention to the fissure. The crack in the ridgeline, barely wider than a torso, angled wrong, shadowed even at noon. The passage that people have walked past. The entrance that is not hidden, exactly, but that does not advertise itself, that requires a specific curiosity, a willingness to press your shoulders against stone that is almost too hot to touch, to descend nine meters through a crack that turns twice, to arrive.

The wise power considers this.

It does not widen the fissure. It does not mark the ridgeline. It does not place a sign or a signal or a beacon. It does not make the chamber easier to find. To do so would be to invite what the chamber cannot survive: attention at scale. Instruments. Sampling. The well-meaning incursion of a species that understands by taking apart, that loves by collecting, that protects by managing. The wise power has seen this before. It has seen what happens when a complete system is discovered by minds that cannot resist improving it, studying it, protecting it to death with monitoring equipment and access restrictions and conservation frameworks that, however well-intentioned, impose an external logic on a system that has no logic, that has only process, that has been its own sufficient authority for longer than the discoverers' civilization has existed.

So it does not make the chamber easier to find.

But.

There is a woman. She is a geologist—or she was, before she became something harder to name. She works on the volcanic plateau. She has been here for years. She maps thermal gradients, catalogs vent structures, studies the mineralogy of new basalt. She is methodical and patient and she has, buried under the methodology and the patience, a capacity for awe that she does not talk about because her field does not reward talking about it. She has walked this ridgeline before. She has noticed the fissure. She has thought about descending and then not descended, because the angle was wrong and the heat was concerning and she had other transects to complete and the day was long and she told herself she would come back.

She has not come back. It has been two years.

The wise power does not compel her. It does not send a dream or a vision or a premonition. It does not manipulate her schedule so that she finds herself on the ridgeline with time to spare. It does nothing so crude. What it does is much smaller, and much more precise, and it is the only thing the wise power does in this entire encounter that could be called an action rather than a quality of attention:

It removes a hesitation.

Not a specific hesitation. Not the voice in her head that says *the angle is wrong* or *the heat is concerning* or *I have other transects.* Those are all real and she will still hear them. What the wise power removes is something underneath those voices—a deeper reluctance, formless and old, the one that says *what if what I find changes something, what if knowing is a responsibility I'm not ready for, what if the awe I keep buried is actually the truest thing about me and I am not brave enough to live from it.* That one. The wise power does not eliminate it. It loosens it. A fraction. The way you might loosen a nut that has been over-tightened so that it can still hold but can also, when the time comes, turn.

This is not coercion. This is not even influence, in any sense the woman would recognize. If she never returns to the ridgeline, the loosening changes nothing. It sits in her, a fractionally expanded capacity for courage, and maybe it expresses itself in some other domain—a conversation she has, a paper she writes, a moment when she allows herself to feel what she actually feels about the planet she studies. The wise power does not require a specific outcome. It makes one possible. That is all.

But if she does return. If she finds herself on the ridgeline again, on a day when the light is right and the other transects can wait and the fissure is there, shadowed, barely wider than her torso, and she thinks *I said I would come back*—

Then she might descend.

And if she descends, she will arrive.

And what she will find will change nothing about the chamber and everything about her.

---

Here is what the wise power foresees—not with certainty, because it has loosened a hesitation, not determined an outcome, and the woman is free, genuinely free, in the way that everything that is alive and choosing is free:

She descends. The crack turns twice. The heat builds. She almost turns back at the second turn—the stone is very hot, and she is alone, and this is not protocol. But she does not turn back. She arrives.

She stands in the chamber. The roar hits her first, the drone that she feels in her sternum, and for a moment she thinks she has made a mistake, that this is a dangerous vent, that she needs to leave. But then her eyes adjust. And she sees the walls.

She sees the beetles. She does not know they are beetles—not yet, not in the first seconds. What she sees is that the wall is alive. That what she took for stone is moving. That the surface she is looking at is a living surface, dense, packed, shifting imperceptibly, and that it has been here, doing this, for—

Her geologist's mind does the calculation before she can stop it. The mineral strata. The layering in the cooling columns. The age of the youngest basalt. She knows, within minutes, that this system predates any vent structure she has cataloged. That it is older than the current fissure. That it has survived collapses. That it rebuilds.

She stays for eighteen minutes. She does not touch the walls. She does not take a sample. She does not photograph—her equipment is in her pack at the top of the fissure. She just looks. And she listens. And near the end, she crouches by the heat seam and sees the filaments, and her breath catches, and she understands—not fully, not yet, but in the way that understanding sometimes arrives as a shape before it arrives as a sentence—that she is inside something. Not a cave. Not a vent. Something that is alive in a way that includes the stone. Something that is working.

She climbs out. The air hits her. She sits on the ridgeline for a long time.

And then the hard part. The part the wise power cannot control and does not try to.

She has to decide what to do.

She is a scientist. She has found something extraordinary. Every professional reflex she has tells her to report it, to return with instruments, to document, to publish, to ensure the chamber is studied and understood and, inevitably, protected through official channels that will involve access and monitoring and management. This is the right thing to do. It is what her training demands. It is what her field exists to support.

But.

She sat in that chamber. She felt the roar in her chest. She saw the beetles become the wall. She saw the crawlers glow. She watched a ceiling bud fall and disappear into the colony below. And she knows—the awe she keeps buried tells her, the awe that is, as the wise power gently suggested, the truest thing about her—that some of what she found will not survive being found.

Not the animals. The animals are tough. They have survived lava flows and chamber collapses and millennia of geological violence. They will survive a few scientists in heat suits with collection jars. What will not survive is the *unseen-ness*. The quality the chamber has of existing entirely for itself, of being a process that has no audience, that performs for no one, that has been the earth's own private work for longer than any human endeavor. Once it is known, it is no longer that. It becomes a discovery, a research site, a wonder, a thing-that-humans-know-about, and the humans will be respectful and careful and they will still, by knowing, change what it means for the chamber to exist.

She sits on the ridgeline and the hum comes up through her boots and she does not know what to do.

---

The wise power watches this. The wise power does not tell her what to decide. The wise power is genuinely uncertain what the right choice is, which may seem strange for a wise power, but wisdom is not omniscience and the wisest thing the wise power knows is that some questions do not have answers that are correct, only answers that are honest.

If she reports: the chamber will be studied. Its extraordinary biology will be understood. The filaments in the seam will be recognized as a new category of chemolithoautotroph. The beetles' mineral-incorporating chitin will be analyzed and the results will reshape understanding of the boundaries between biological and geological processes. Papers will be published. Other researchers will come. The chamber will be monitored. It will survive this—the system is robust—but it will become a known thing, a cataloged thing, a protected site with an access protocol and a management plan, and it will no longer be what it has been for all the millennia before this moment: the planet, working, unseen.

If she does not report: the chamber remains unknown. Her experience remains hers. The beetles continue. The crawlers continue. The ceiling organisms pulse. The filaments translate. The system persists in its vast, coherent, unwitnessed perfection, and the things that might be learned from it—things that might matter, that might change how her species understands the relationship between life and geology, that might reframe what it means for a planet to be alive—those things remain unlearned. And she carries the knowledge alone, a weight she did not ask for, a secret that will color every paper she writes and every vent she catalogs for the rest of her career, because she will always know that the most extraordinary thing she ever found is the one she never told anyone about.

The wise power sees both paths. The wise power does not choose for her. But the wise power, being wise, does one more thing.

It stays with her.

Not forever. Not as a presence she can detect. But in the hours and days after she climbs out of the fissure, as she sits with her decision, as she turns it over in the privacy of her own mind—the wise power remains close. Close enough that she is not alone with the weight. Close enough that whatever she decides, she decides in the company of something that understands both the value of knowledge and the value of unknowing, both the importance of sharing what is extraordinary and the importance of letting some extraordinary things remain unshared.

She will decide. The wise power does not know what she will decide. The wise power does not need to know.

---

And that is the third thing. That is the last thing the wise power does.

It loosened a hesitation so that a particular kind of person—careful, patient, capable of awe—might find the chamber. And now it stays near, not guiding, not influencing, just *accompanying*, so that whatever she decides, she decides with the full weight of her own wisdom, which is not the wise power's wisdom but which is real, and which is enough.

The wise power trusts her.

This is perhaps the most radical thing it does. More radical than the witnessing, which was an act of completion. More radical than the loosening, which was an act of possibility. The trust is an act of faith—a genuine, unhedged, non-omniscient faith that a person who felt the roar in her chest and watched the beetles become the wall and saw the crawlers glow in the dark will make a decision that is adequate to what she has seen. Not perfect. Not optimal. Adequate. Human-scaled. Honest.

The wise power trusts her because the wise power recognizes in her something it recognizes in the chamber itself: a coherence. A way of being that is integrated, that has found its shape through long pressure and patient work, that is—in its own different, smaller, softer way—complete. She is not a system. She is a person. But she has the quality the system has, the quality of being what she is all the way through, and the wise power trusts this quality because it is the quality on which everything worth trusting is built.

---

So.

The wise power does three things, and the three things are:

**Nothing.** Because the system is complete and does not need intervention, and wisdom begins with the recognition of what does not need to be fixed.

**Witnessing.** Because the system has existed for millennia without being known by anything capable of knowing it, and there is a specific kind of incompleteness in that—not a flaw, not a lack, but an openness, like a chord that has not yet resolved—and the wise power's witnessing resolves it, holds the pattern as pattern, hears the work as work, and in doing so provides not what the system needs but what the situation needs, which is an outside, a receiver, a place where the chamber's extraordinary coherence is apprehended in its fullness for the first time.

**A loosened hesitation and a trusted human.** Because the wise power is not the only thing capable of witnessing, and the chamber's future—whether it remains unseen or becomes known—should not be decided by a power, however wise, but by a person, however uncertain. The wise power opens a door. It does not push anyone through. It stays close enough to be a comfort and far enough away to be irrelevant to the decision. And it trusts.

---

Below, in the dark, ten thousand beetles shift a fraction of a millimeter closer to the heat.

A crawler finishes its meal and begins, slowly, to glow.

A ceiling organism pulses.

Something falls. Something feeds. The stone grows.

On the ridgeline, a woman sits with her hands on her knees, feeling the hum through her boots, deciding.

The planet, working.

Something, watching.

Someone, choosing.

This is what the wise power does. It holds all three of these—the working, the watching, the choosing—in a single attention, and it does not rank them. The planet's work is not more important than the woman's choice. The woman's choice is not more important than the wise power's witness. The witness is not more important than the planet's work. They are three aspects of a single moment, and the moment is this: something extraordinary exists, and it has been found, and what happens next is genuinely open, and the openness is not a problem to be solved but a condition to be honored.

The wise power honors it.

And then—not immediately, not abruptly, but in the way that attention shifts when what needed to be attended to has been attended to—the wise power's focus moves on. To other places. Other chambers. Other fissures in other ridgelines on other plateaus, or other kinds of places entirely, where other things are working or waiting or deciding. It does not forget the forge vent. It does not stop caring. But it recognizes—wisely—that to linger would be to make itself too important, to make its witness into a vigil, its trust into supervision. The wise power came, and saw, and did what it could, and now it goes, and the chamber remains, and the woman remains, and whatever happens next belongs to them.

The hum continues.

The stone grows.

The planet, working.
