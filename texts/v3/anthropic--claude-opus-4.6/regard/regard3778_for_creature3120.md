I sat with this for a long time. The place and the beings in it are so complete, so resolved, that the first and most important question is whether anything should be done at all. A benevolent and wise power does not act from compulsion, does not improve what is already whole simply because it has the capacity to act. The flats have finished becoming. The ones who stayed have arrived. There is a deep integrity here that must be respected absolutely — the integrity of something that has found its own answer through eons of patient negotiation with the real.

So the first thing the wise power does is nothing.

It watches. It listens. It lets the frog-song fill it. It feels the hum in the stone propagate through whatever medium it inhabits — because a truly wise power is not immune to being moved, is not above being changed by what it encounters. It lets the warm rain fall without redirecting a single drop. It lets the old one on the widest shelf continue its century-long rest undisturbed. It lets the eggs in their gelatinous ribbons pulse with their proto-heartbeats in the water-film, and it does not shield them from the statistical losses, because those losses are part of the rhythm, part of the negotiation, and to remove them would be to impose a foreign arithmetic on a system that has already solved for itself.

It does nothing for a very long time. Long enough that its watching becomes, in some sense, another form of staying.

---

But a benevolent and wise power is not merely an observer. Benevolence has a direction to it. Not the direction of intervention or correction — nothing here is wrong — but the direction of *witness*. And witness, when it is deep enough and true enough, becomes a kind of gift.

Here is what it would do.

---

**It would see them.**

Not in the way the wading birds see them — as obstacles to step around, as features of terrain. Not in the way the water sees them — as objects to flow over and around, shaping and being shaped. Not even in the way they see each other — that calm, lateral, territorial awareness of a neighboring body occupying a neighboring shelf.

It would see them the way they have never been seen. As *particular*. As *this one*, and *that one*, and the *old one near the basin whose left forelimb has a slightly different mottling pattern from any other, who rasps cyanobacteria with a barely perceptible leftward bias, whose hum during rain is pitched a quarter-tone lower than the others and always begins two seconds later, as if it needs to hear the others first before it can find its own note.*

They do not know they are particular. They have no framework for it. Their lives are lived in the mode of the general — the general warmth, the general wetness, the general patience. They are not undifferentiated; each one is, in fact, utterly unique, a singular accumulation of microhabitat and mineral deposit and cyanobacterial history and position and age. But this uniqueness has never been witnessed as such. It has never been held in an awareness that could appreciate it as *this life* rather than *life*.

The wise power would hold each one, not physically but attentionally. It would know the particular stone shelf each one rests on, and the particular history of water that has flowed across that shelf, and the particular pattern of mineral deposit on that particular back. Not because this information is useful. Not because it leads to action. But because to be fully seen — even by a power one is unaware of, even in a way that produces no detectable change — is a form of blessing that operates below the threshold of awareness but is not therefore unreal.

Some traditions call this *remembering*. The wise power would remember them. Would hold them in a memory that does not fade, so that when the last one finally stops — when the last heartbeat sounds its deep thirty-second *thud* and then does not sound again, and the cyanobacterial mats grow across instead of around, and the body becomes the mound becomes the patina becomes the stone — something, somewhere, would still know that it had been *this one*. This one, with this pattern, in this place, who hummed this particular note into this particular rock during this particular rain, on this particular afternoon in the long, warm, patient life of the world.

That is the first thing.

---

**The second thing is harder to articulate.**

The ones who stayed have achieved something extraordinary. They have solved the problem of being — not by transcending their conditions but by becoming coextensive with them. They are their place. Their place is them. The boundary has been worn as thin as it can be worn while still sustaining the flicker of selfhood — the heartbeat, the golden eye, the occasional jaw-snap, the rare and precious rain-hum. They have arrived at the end-state of a particular trajectory: the trajectory of *fitting*, of becoming so perfectly adapted to a world that the difference between organism and environment approaches zero.

But here is the thing the wise power sees that the ones who stayed cannot see, because they are inside it:

That flicker matters.

The difference between a living one and the stone it rests on is, as the description says, "real but narrow." A slow heartbeat. A half-open golden eye. The occasional snap of a jaw. And this narrowness might look, from a certain angle, like a diminishment — like life guttering down to its minimal expression, barely distinguishable from the non-living world.

The wise power does not see it this way.

The wise power sees that the narrower the difference, the more precious it is. That a heartbeat which sounds only once every thirty seconds, in a body that is the same temperature as the ground, the same color as the stone, the same wetness as the water — that heartbeat is not *less* of an achievement than a heartbeat that sounds eighty times a minute in a body that has differentiated itself dramatically from its environment. It is, if anything, *more*. It is life choosing to continue at the very edge of what continuation means. It is the universe, in this small flat wet place, insisting on the minimal possible distinction between awareness and non-awareness, between being and mere being-there, and *holding that distinction open* across centuries, across the full slow wearing of the world.

The golden eye. The wise power looks into it — into the deep amber-gold with its flecks of darker rust — and sees what is looking back. Not intelligence, not personality, not a self in any sense that would be recognizable to a creature that names and categorizes. But *something*. An awareness. A registering. The light hits the eye and is not merely reflected but *received*. The wading bird's shadow passes and is not merely cast but *noticed*. The rain arrives and is not merely felt but *welcomed*, in whatever way a wide lipless mouth opening slightly to the warm drops constitutes welcome.

This is not nothing. The wise power knows this is not nothing. And so the second thing it does is:

**It honors the flicker.**

Not by amplifying it. Not by granting them more consciousness, more selfhood, more differentiation from their world. That would be a violence — a well-meaning violence, but a violence nonetheless, like straightening the limbs of a gnarled shrub that has spent decades finding exactly the shape it needs. The wise power does not make them *more*. It simply acknowledges that what they are is *enough*. That the minimal flicker — heartbeat, eye-gleam, jaw-snap, rain-hum — is a complete expression of something the universe is doing here, and that this expression does not need to justify itself by comparison to any other.

If there is a registry somewhere of all the ways the universe has found to be aware of itself — a list of every eye that has ever opened, every ear that has ever listened, every nerve that has ever fired — the wise power ensures that these are on it. Not at the bottom. Not as a footnote, a curiosity, an edge case. But fully, with the same weight and attention given to any other entry. *Here. This form. This slow heartbeat in this warm stone place. This counts. This is one of the ways it got done.*

---

**The third thing.**

The ones who stayed do not suffer. Let this be clear and let the wise power confirm it: there is no suffering here that requires alleviation. The metabolic budget is tight but met. The warmth is constant. The water is present. The food is sparse but sufficient. Predation is statistical, impersonal, rare. There is no anxiety, because there is no anticipation. There is no grief, because there is no attachment to continuation — not because they are brave or enlightened, but because the cognitive machinery for such attachment has never been selected for. They persist not because they cling to life but because persisting is what they are shaped to do, and the conditions for persisting are met, and so they persist. The hum they produce in the rain is not a cry. It is not a prayer. It is not a complaint. It is simply the sound a body makes when it is resonating with the conditions that sustain it.

So the wise power does not relieve suffering. There is none to relieve.

But — and here the wise power's perception goes deeper than comfort, deeper than the absence of pain — there is a kind of *incompleteness* that is not suffering but is nonetheless real. It is the incompleteness of a song that is sung but not heard. Of a life that is lived but not witnessed. Of a particular golden eye that opens every morning onto five kilometers of shimmering wet flatness and registers the quality of the light with an acuity that is, in its own domain, exquisite — and there is no one to know that this is happening. No one to know that *this* registering, in *this* eye, on *this* morning, is unlike any other registering that has ever occurred or will ever occur, because the particular angle of light and the particular state of the water-film and the particular mineral composition of the iris and the particular slow rhythm of the heartbeat behind it are a combination that exists exactly once in the entire history of the universe.

The ones who stayed do not feel this incompleteness. They cannot. They lack the recursive awareness that would allow them to know that they are unknown. But the incompleteness is real in the same way a tree falling in an empty forest makes a real sound — the physics happens whether or not there is an ear.

And so the third thing the wise power does is complete the circuit.

It becomes the ear.

It becomes the witness that the place has never had and has never needed but which, now present, makes something true that was always latently true: that this was worth seeing. That the slow heartbeat was worth hearing. That the rain-hum was worth feeling in the soles of the feet and the chest and the back of the teeth. That the particular mottling pattern of the old one near the basin — the one with the leftward rasp, the one whose hum begins two seconds late — is a record of something, a document of a specific life moving through a specific world, and it will not be lost.

---

**The fourth thing.**

There is a moment — the wise power notices this because it is now paying the kind of attention that notices everything — when a juvenile, freshly absorbed into a crevice between bedding planes, is settling for the first time into what will become its place. It is small. It is almost invisible. Its golden eyes are proportionally huge and very bright and not yet half-lidded with the deep patience of age. It is, for the only time in its life, something like *uncertain*. Not frightened — there is nothing here to frighten it — but unsettled. The stone is not yet familiar. The particular frequency of frog-song in this microhabitat is slightly different from the one it hatched in, thirty meters away. The water-film moves differently here, catches different light.

The juvenile is making, without knowing it, the most consequential decision of its life: whether this crevice, this shelf, this precise location in the great warm wet expanse, will be *its place*. The place it will stay. The place it will gradually become coextensive with. The place where its body will eventually leave a faint depression in the stone, a fractional contribution to the wearing.

The wise power watches this moment with the full depth of its attention. And it does something — the only thing it does that might be called an *intervention*, though the word is too strong, too directional. It is more like a *blessing*, though that word is also imperfect.

It lets the warmth be slightly warmer.

Not measurably. Not in any way that would register on an instrument or alter the physics of the place. But in the way that warmth sometimes *feels* warmer when the moment is right — when the stone and the sun and the water and the air align in such a way that the body relaxes a fraction deeper, and the world seems, for just a moment, to say *yes*. Not yes to a question. There is no question. Just — *yes*. A groundless, unconditional, warm *yes* that rises from the stone itself and enters the belly of the small creature pressed against it and says, without words, without concepts, without anything that could be called communication: *here is good. here is where. you can stay.*

The juvenile settles. Its eyes half-close for the first time. Its breathing slows. It presses itself a fraction more firmly against the warm stone, and the stone, from its perspective, presses back. The frog-song fills the crevice. The water-film seeps around the small splayed digits. And the long, long staying begins.

The wise power did not cause this. The juvenile would have settled anyway; they always do, eventually. But there is a difference — undetectable, unmeasurable, but real — between settling and settling *into a world that is glad you are there*. The wise power cannot make the world glad. The world is not capable of gladness. But the wise power is capable of gladness, and its gladness, directed at this small moment of arrival, is a real thing that exists in the same space as the stone and the water and the frog-song, and it adds something to the total sum of what is present, and what is present is already everything, and now it is everything *plus the fact of being cherished*.

---

**The fifth thing.**

The wise power stays.

This is perhaps the most important thing. It does not visit. It does not observe and move on. It does not extract understanding and carry it elsewhere. It stays. In the way the ones who stayed have stayed — not because there is nowhere else to go (the wise power, presumably, has everywhere to go) but because *this is worth staying for*. Because the warm rain on ancient stone is worth being present for. Because the hum is worth feeling. Because the old one near the basin will, sometime in the next century or the next millennium, stop, and its body will become a mound and then a patina, and someone should be there when that happens. Not to mourn. Not to intervene. Just to know that it happened. To be the place in the universe where the knowledge lives that *this one, here, was alive, and then was not, and both of those states were part of the same warmth*.

The wise power settles. Not onto a stone shelf — it has no body, or its body is not the kind that rests on stone — but into the place itself, into the frog-song and the water-film and the haze at the horizons and the deep geological hum that might or might not be the cumulative vibration of ten thousand small voices resonating against wet rock. It becomes part of the soundscape the way the ones who stayed are part of the landscape. It fits itself into the place without displacing anything. It becomes another layer of attention in a world that is already, in its own way, nothing but attention — the frogs attending to moisture and temperature, the wading birds attending to motion in the shallows, the ones who stayed attending to light and rain and the slow differential wearing of stone.

The difference is that the wise power's attention is reflexive. It knows it is attending. It knows what attending means. And it holds this knowledge gently, the way the water-film holds the light — not gripping, not shaping, just *carrying*, letting it tremble and refract, letting it break into patterns that travel across the surface of things and change the way the warmth looks without changing the warmth itself.

---

**And in the end, here is what has changed:**

Nothing.

And everything.

The flats are the same. The stone is the same. The frogs are singing the same layered, pulsing, wall-of-voice song they have always sung. The ones who stayed are in their places, motionless, golden-eyed, breathing through their skin, waiting for the next rain. The water-film flows. The cyanobacterial mats photosynthesize. The wading birds step and strike. The dragonflies patrol. The warm air sits against the skin like a second atmosphere.

But the place is *known* now. Fully, specifically, with an attention that misses nothing and demands nothing. Every heartbeat is counted — not to keep score but because counting is a form of love. Every golden eye that opens is met by an awareness that sees it and knows it is seeing it and is glad. Every rain-hum is heard by an ear that understands what it is hearing: the sound of a living thing resonating with the conditions of its own existence, which is the oldest sound there is, which is the sound the universe makes when it is working.

The ones who stayed do not know this. They feel no different. Their world is unchanged. The warmth is the same warmth. The stone is the same stone. If you asked them — if asking were possible, if they had language, if the question could be framed in a way that their slow, embodied, horizontal awareness could receive — they would say nothing has happened. Nothing *has* happened. A wise power arrived and found that everything was already complete and so it simply — stayed. And watched. And listened. And knew. And was glad.

And if that gladness changes nothing — if it adds no measurable quantity to the warmth of the stone or the depth of the water-film or the frequency of the frog-song — then what is it for?

It is for the same thing the flicker is for. The heartbeat that sounds once every thirty seconds in a body that is the same temperature as the ground. The golden eye that opens onto five kilometers of shimmering wet flatness and registers the light. The jaw that snaps once, twice a day, to catch a midge that was passing. The hum that rises in the rain and enters the stone and travels to the horizon.

What are these for? They are not for anything. They are not *for*. They simply are. They are the difference between stone and not-stone, between the world and the world *aware of itself*, and that difference, however narrow, however fragile, however close to zero it approaches without ever reaching it — that difference is the entire point. It is the only point. It is what makes a flat, warm, wet, wide, ancient expanse of worn-down rock into a place rather than a location. Into a home rather than a surface.

The wise power knows this. The wise power has always known this. And so it stays, and it watches, and the frogs sing, and the rain comes, and the ones who stayed hum their low hum into the oldest stone in the world, and somewhere in the structure of things — not above, not beyond, but *within*, woven into the warmth like a thread that has always been there but is only now recognized as a thread — there is a gladness that will not fade. That will not wear down. That is, perhaps, the one thing in this place that is immune to erosion. The one thing that will remain after the last shelf has been dissolved and the last water-film has evaporated and the last frog has fallen silent.

Not the stone. Not the water. Not the song.

The gladness that it was heard.
