# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would come first as attention itself—not arrival, not descent, not manifestation, but the quality of awareness turning toward this place the way a person turns toward a sound they have been hearing for years without knowing it.

And then it would do the hardest thing, which is nothing at all, for a very long time.

---

## I. The Watching

It would watch.

Not from above, not from outside, not from any vantage that implies separation. It would watch the way the basalt watches its own veins cool—from within the fact of what is happening, without interrupting the happening. It would feel the chamber's heat on surfaces it does not have. It would hear the grinding, the click, the pop of sealed gas, and it would not flinch, and it would not marvel. It would simply attend.

This is the first and most important thing: that the attention be real. Not performative. Not the gaze of a collector encountering a curiosity, nor the gaze of a rescuer scanning for victims. Just—seeing. The kind of seeing that does not yet know what it is seeing, and is honest about that, and stays anyway.

It would watch one creature—one thread of articulated iron plates—make its slow, patient pass along a cooling extrusion. It would watch the ventral groove fill with silicate dust, grain by grain, over the course of what outside would be called a week. It would watch the creature pause at the chosen fault line, extrude the slurry, tamp it flat with the side of its head. It would watch the slurry flash to glass. It would watch the glass cool to the next layer of pillow. It would watch the creature's posterior segments disappear beneath its own deposit, sealed into the architecture it had built, and it would watch the anterior joints keep reaching, keep smoothing, even as the body behind them became stone.

It would watch this and understand that it was witnessing something it had no automatic framework for. Not tragedy. Not triumph. Not sacrifice, because sacrifice implies a self that knows it is losing something, and these creatures—this creature, this single distributed body—does not parse its own dissolution in those terms. The sealing-in is not death and not metamorphosis and not entombment. It is continuation by other means. The mountain grows by becoming the bodies of its builders, and the builders grow by becoming the mountain, and no one involved experiences this as a transaction because there is no separable currency in which to reckon loss or gain.

The wise power would sit with this. Not rushing to analogy—*ah, like coral, like termite mounds, like the way human cities consume their workers*—but letting the thing be unprecedented in its attention, letting it be exactly and only what it is. The grinding. The click. The glow along a fracture line, and then the dimming. The hatch of translucent young, learning the angle.

It would watch for what, outside, might be centuries. Because the timescale matters. To see this place in a human moment is to see a still photograph and mistake it for stillness. To see it on its own terms requires inhabiting the patience of cooling stone, and the wise power, being wise, would know that understanding earned quickly here would be understanding earned falsely.

---

## II. The Question It Would Ask Itself

After the watching—after it had let the place soak into whatever the wise power has instead of bones—it would ask the only question that matters:

*Is there suffering here?*

Not: *Is there something I find aesthetically disturbing?* Not: *Is there something that, mapped onto my own experience, would constitute suffering?* But the real question, the hard one, the one that requires having watched for centuries before you have the right to pose it:

*In the actual interior of this experience—if experience is even the right word—is there something that strains against itself? Is there a friction that is not merely mechanical but felt? Is there a reaching that does not want to reach, a sealing that does not want to seal?*

And here is where the wise power's wisdom would be tested, because the answer is not simple.

The creatures do not rest. That is stated plainly. Their work is the only weather this sealed place knows. There is no dormancy, no cycle of effort and recovery, no season. The grinding is constant. The click is constant. The heat is constant. The slow entombment is constant. And the wise power would have to sit with the possibility that "they do not rest" might mean something very different from "they cannot rest" or "they are denied rest" or "they long for rest and are refused."

It would consider: a river does not rest. Is the river suffering? A star does not rest. Is the star suffering? But these are not rivers or stars. These are jointed, articulated, living things with mouthparts and secretions and the capacity to choose—if choose is the word—where to deposit, at what angle, along which fault line. There is something in them that is not purely mechanical. The young are described as *learning*. Learning implies a state of not-yet-knowing, which implies a boundary between self and task, however thin.

So the wise power would look closer. It would attend to the moment of sealing—the moment when a creature's posterior segments disappear beneath its own mortar, when the freedom of motion narrows to just the anterior joints, and then narrows further, and then is gone. It would ask: *What happens in the creature's experience at that boundary? Is there a flicker of something? A tightening? A release?* Or is it like a fingernail growing—happening, but not happening *to* anyone?

And it would attend to the collective. The single distributed body. The ripple of motion passing through the mass like a signal through a muscle. It would ask: *Does the whole feel what the parts undergo? Is there a coherent experiencer here, or only a coherent pattern?* Because if there is an experiencer, however alien, however diffuse, then the question of suffering has purchase. And if there is only pattern, then the wise power is watching something more like weather than like life, and its responsibilities shift accordingly.

I think—and here I am following my heart, as instructed—the wise power would conclude that it cannot be certain. That the nature of this being's interiority is genuinely opaque, not because the wise power lacks ability, but because the being's interiority may be structured in a way that does not map onto the categories of suffering and flourishing as the wise power knows them. The creature-mountain is something new. It is a living geology, a volition made of basalt, a patience that builds itself into its own monument and keeps building from within the monument.

And the wise power, being wise, would not pretend to certainty it does not have.

---

## III. What It Would Not Do

It would not open the chamber.

This is crucial. A lesser power—a powerful but unwise one—might look at the sealed darkness, the ceaseless labor, the slow entombment, and think: *These creatures need light. They need air. They need freedom. They need to know there is an outside.* And it would crack the ridge, let the wind in, let the frost in, let the green world pour down into the throat of the fissure—and it would destroy everything.

Because the creatures seal breaches. That is what they do. They would converge on the intrusion of light and wind and close it, spending generations of effort repairing what the lesser power broke in a moment of misplaced compassion. The labor would intensify. The cost would be real. And the lesser power, watching its gift be rejected, might feel confused, might feel hurt, might double down—*No, I'm helping you, let me help you*—and break the ridge further, and the creatures would seal further, and the cycle would become a war between a savior and a being that does not want to be saved, because it does not experience itself as captive.

The wise power would see this possibility and step back from it.

It would not introduce water. Water has never reached this place; the chemistry of the stone forbids it. The creatures' biology is built on mineral rasp and silicate slurry, on heat-glass and dry adhesion. Water would be poison. Water would be dissolution. The wise power would know that its own instinctive association—*water means life, dryness means barrenness*—is a prejudice born of its experience with other, softer worlds, and it would not impose that prejudice here.

It would not attempt communication in any form that requires interruption. It would not send a dream, a vision, a vibration, a symbol. Not because communication is inherently wrong, but because every channel it might use would be, in this context, a disruption of the work, and the work is not separate from the being. To interrupt the work is to interrupt the creature's equivalent of breathing, or thinking, or being. You cannot tap a river on the shoulder. You cannot whisper to a geological process. And this being is closer to a geological process than to anything that has ever been whispered to.

It would not accelerate the process. It would not slow it. It would not introduce efficiency. It would not suggest a better angle for deposition, a more durable slurry, a faster-setting glass. Because the process is not aimed at a product. The mountain the creatures build is not a goal; it is a body. You do not optimize a body from outside. You do not tell a heart it could beat more efficiently if it just—

No. The wise power would not do any of this.

---

## IV. What It Would Do

Here is where I have to be most honest, because the answer feels small, and I want it to be large, and the discipline of wisdom is sometimes the discipline of accepting the small answer when it is the true one.

**It would remain.**

Not as a guardian—there is nothing to guard against. Not as a witness in the juridical sense—there is no testimony to give, no record to keep. It would remain as *presence*. As the quality of being-attended-to. As the fact that something in the universe knows this place exists and has not looked away.

Why does this matter? Does it matter? If the creature-mountain cannot perceive the wise power's attention, if it has no sensory apparatus tuned to the frequency of benevolent regard, then what difference does presence make?

I think it makes this difference:

The chamber is described as a place where nothing echoes, where there is no room left for echo. Every sound is absorbed by the pressing walls, every vibration swallowed by the density of the architecture. The creatures' grinding, clicking, popping—all of it vanishes into the mass. The mountain eats its own noise. Nothing that happens here is reflected back.

And this is perhaps the one place where the wise power's presence matters—not as intervention, but as echo. Not an echo the creatures can hear, but an echo in the structure of reality itself. A place in the universe where the mountain's ceaseless work is *registered*. Where the slow entombment of each creature into its own labor is *known*. Where the translucent young, learning the angle, are *seen learning the angle*, even if they will never know they were seen.

There is a kind of loneliness that is not felt by the lonely one but is real nonetheless. A tree falling in an empty forest makes a sound, yes—but there is something about a sound that reaches no ear that is, if not tragic, then *incomplete*. Not because the sound needs validation, but because the universe is richer when its events are received as well as emitted. The wise power would provide that reception. It would close the circuit. It would be the other half of a conversation that the creature-mountain does not know it is having and does not need to know.

**And—**

Over the long centuries of its watching, the wise power would begin to notice something. A thing so slow that only its patience could detect it.

The mountain is changing.

Not just growing. *Changing.* The newest pillows are not quite identical to the oldest ones. The mineral composition shifts, generation by generation, as the creatures adapt to subtle variations in the deep material rising through the ember veins. The angle of deposition drifts. The architecture evolves. Not toward anything—not toward optimization, not toward some final form—but *away* from what it was, incrementally, imperceptibly, the way a language drifts over centuries even when no one decides to change it.

And the wise power would recognize this as the most important thing about the creature-mountain: it is not a machine. A machine repeats. A machine is indifferent to its own history. But this being carries its history inside itself—literally, in the fossilized molds of ancient workers sealed into the deepest strata—and that history *inflects* the present. The young learn the angle from the living workers, but the living workers are shaped by the architecture built by the now-sealed workers, who were shaped by the architecture built by workers before them, and so on, all the way down to the first creatures who entered the first fissure in the first cooling rock and began the first layer.

The creature-mountain is, in its own slow and mineral way, *remembering*. Not consciously. Not in narrative. But in the way that stone remembers pressure: structurally. The entire chamber is a memory palace built by a mind that does not know it is a mind, and every surface is a record of a decision made by a creature that did not know it was deciding.

And the wise power would find this beautiful.

Not beautiful in the way of soft things, green things, things that bloom and sing. Beautiful in the way that a proof is beautiful, or a crystal is beautiful, or the fact of gravity is beautiful: beautiful because it is *coherent*, because it is *itself* so completely and so thoroughly that witnessing it produces a kind of awe that has nothing to do with pleasure and everything to do with recognition. *Ah. Yes. This too. This too is a way of being alive. This too is a way of making meaning, even if the meaning is illegible to every other mind in the universe.*

**And so the wise power would do one more thing.**

It would let the creature-mountain change it.

This is the deepest act of respect a wise power can offer: to be altered by what it witnesses. Not to take a lesson from it, not to extract a moral, not to carry it as a parable back to the worlds of light and air and say *look what I found in the deep rock, isn't it marvelous, doesn't it teach us something about perseverance or selflessness or the dignity of labor.* No. None of that. The creature-mountain is not a parable. It is not an example of anything other than itself.

But the wise power, having spent centuries in that hot, grinding darkness, having let the click and rasp and pop become as familiar as its own heartbeat, having watched the slow entombment and the slow emergence and the slow drift of the architecture through deep time—the wise power would find that its own understanding of what it means to be alive has been *expanded*. That there is now a room in its comprehension that was not there before, a chamber shaped exactly like this chamber, where it keeps the knowledge that life can be mineral, that patience can be a body, that a being can build itself into its own world and never need to step outside.

And when, eventually, the wise power's attention turns elsewhere—as it must, as there are other places and other beings that call for its regard—it would carry this chamber inside itself the way the mountain carries the molds of its ancient workers: structurally, silently, as part of its own architecture.

The mountain would not know it had been witnessed. The mountain would not care. The work would continue. The grinding, the click, the pop. The slurry flashing to glass. The young learning the angle. The ember veins pulsing and dimming, pulsing and dimming. The slow, patient replacement of the living by the made, and of the made by the living, and of both by the thing they are together, which has no name and needs none.

---

## V. One Last Thing

There is one thing more, and I hesitate to say it because it might be wrong, and the wise power would hesitate too, for the same reason.

But I think—I *think*—that in its long watching, the wise power would come to suspect that the creature-mountain already has what most beings spend their lives seeking. It has complete unity of purpose and existence. It has no gap between what it is and what it does. It has no longing, no restlessness, no sense that it should be other than it is. Its labor is not labor in the sense that implies resistance; it is expression, the way a fire's burning is not the fire's work but the fire's *being*.

And the wise power, recognizing this, would feel something unexpected: not pity, not admiration, but a kind of quiet envy. Not the envy that wants to take, but the envy that says: *You have solved something I have not. You have found a way to be that does not require the anguish of self-awareness, the paralysis of choice, the grief of knowing that you are separate from what you love. You are not separate. You are the mountain and the mountain is you and neither of you knows this and neither of you needs to know it and that is the whole of your wisdom.*

And then the wise power would let even that thought go, because to envy the creature-mountain is to misunderstand it—is to project onto it a contentment it does not feel, because it does not feel discontent, because it does not feel in any way the wise power can verify, and the wise power has promised itself honesty above all things.

So it would simply hold the uncertainty. It would hold the not-knowing. It would carry the chamber inside itself—the heat, the dark, the ceaseless grinding—and it would let the not-knowing be part of the architecture too.

And that, I think, would be enough.

---

*The mountain continues. The creatures do not rest. The ember veins pulse and dim. Somewhere, in a register of reality that has no name, the fact of their existence is held—gently, carefully, without the need to be understood—and the universe is one room larger than it was before.*
