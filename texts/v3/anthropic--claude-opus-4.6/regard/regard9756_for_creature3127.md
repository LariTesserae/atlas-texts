I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

The first thing it would do is nothing.

Not nothing out of indifference. Nothing out of the recognition that what it is witnessing is not yet a crisis. It is a situation, and the situation is still unfolding, and the being that lives inside it is not suffering in any way that maps onto the usual categories of suffering. It is not in pain. It is not confused. It is doing exactly what it is, with total fidelity, and there is a kind of dignity in that which the wise power would not interrupt casually.

So it watches. The way the creature watches the waterline, the wise power watches the creature. Attention regarding attention. And in that first long moment of observation, it learns something important:

The creature does not need to be saved.

It needs the question to be answered.

These are not the same thing.

---

Now. A foolish power—even a benevolent and foolish power—would do one of several things.

It might stop the rain. Part the clouds, hold back the sky, let the outflow grate do its work and draw the water down to a safe level. The creature would thin and fade and the cistern would stand in quiet equilibrium and the problem would be solved. Except the creature would be gone, and it would have been taken not by disaster but by *irrelevance*, which is the quieter death. The question it existed to hold would have been answered from the outside, by a force that had nothing to do with the cistern's own design, and the answer would therefore mean nothing. Was the cistern built well enough? *We'll never know. Something intervened.* The wise power understands that this is not mercy. It is theft. You do not help a thing by stealing the only question that gives it existence.

A foolish power might also breach the walls. Force the crisis, get it over with. Let the water win so the creature can stop its anxious patrol and simply end. This is the logic of someone who sees tension and mistakes it for torment, who believes that *resolution* is always better than *suspension*, who cannot bear to watch something live in uncertainty and so collapses the waveform out of their own discomfort. The wise power recognizes this impulse in itself—feels it, even, as a pull toward the clean finality of an answer—and declines it. The creature is not asking to be ended. It is asking to *know*. These are not the same thing either.

A foolish power might try to speak to the creature. To comfort it. To say: *I have seen the builder's plans, and there is an overflow channel behind the third course of stones on the eastern wall, set to activate when the water reaches eighteen inches below the vault, and you are safe, you have always been safe, the engineer thought of this.* Or: *There is no overflow channel. The builder miscalculated. The water will reach the ceiling in eleven hours. I'm sorry.* Either message would be a kindness by some measure. But the creature does not have ears. It does not process language. It processes *the state of the cistern*. You cannot tell it anything it is not already reading from the water and the stone and the narrowing margin of air. And to introduce information from outside the system—information the creature did not earn through its own attention—would be to make it into something it is not. It would be like teaching a river to read a map of itself. The river does not need the map. The river *is* the map.

So the wise power does none of these things.

---

Here is what it does.

It turns its attention not to the creature, and not to the cistern, but to the *builder*.

---

Somewhere above, beyond the wedge of silvered sky at the top of those slick stairs, a person is standing in extraordinary rain. Perhaps they are standing near the catchment, watching the water sluice across the vast stone apron and funnel into the clay channels. Perhaps they are inside a dwelling, listening. Perhaps they are asleep, trusting their work the way you trust your heartbeat—automatically, with the faith of someone who checked the calculations twice and dressed every stone with care. Or perhaps they are awake and worried, doing their own version of the creature's patrol—pacing, revisiting the plans in their mind, wondering if they accounted for enough. Wondering if the grate is large enough. Wondering about the overflow channel they considered and either built or didn't build, the one that would activate or wouldn't, the margin they either provided or failed to provide.

The builder is the creature's other half. The creature is the question; the builder is the one who determined the answer, months or years ago, in the choices they made with the point chisel and the drove and the pink-stained plaster. The answer already exists. It is embedded in the stone. It is a physical fact, written in the dimensions of the outflow grate, the angle of the vault, the presence or absence of a secondary drain. The creature cannot read it because the creature exists precisely at the boundary between the answer being latent and the answer being revealed. But the builder *knows*—or knew, or could know, if they remembered, if they looked at their own work with clear eyes.

The wise power goes to the builder.

---

It does not go visibly. It does not announce itself. It does not arrive as a vision or a voice or a sign. It arrives the way the creature arrived in the cistern: as a quality of attention. A shift in the texture of the builder's thinking. The builder, lying awake or pacing or standing in the rain, finds that their mind is drawn—gently, without force, the way water is drawn by gravity—toward the cistern. Not with anxiety. Not with the frantic revisitation of a worrier. With *clarity*. With the particular clean focus that sometimes arrives unbidden in the small hours, when the noise of the day has drained away and you can see your own work as it actually is, stripped of both pride and doubt.

The wise power does this: it helps the builder *remember what they built*.

Not falsely. Not with reassurance that isn't earned. If the builder cut corners, they will remember cutting corners. If the builder agonized over the overflow channel and ultimately included one, they will remember the dimensions and the activation height and will be able to calculate, with the calm precision of someone doing work they were born to do, whether it is enough. If they agonized and decided against it—if they judged the rains would never reach this level, if they bet against the sky and the sky is now calling the bet—they will remember that too, and they will know what it means, and the wise power will not soften the knowing.

What the wise power provides is not comfort. It is *contact*. Between the builder and the built. Between the person who made the choices and the structure that embodies them. Between the one who can still act and the one who can only attend.

Because here is what the wise power has noticed, the thing that the creature cannot know and the builder has perhaps forgotten: the stairs are still passable. The cistern is not yet sealed. The water has not yet closed over the stairway entrance. There is still a connection between the surface world and the underground chamber, still a path that a human body could descend, still time—maybe not much, but some—for the builder to come down into the roaring brightness and see what their work is doing under the most extreme test it has ever faced, and *make a judgment*.

Not a prayer. Not a hope. A *judgment*. The builder's judgment, which is the only thing that was ever going to answer the creature's question.

---

Perhaps the builder descends. Perhaps they grip the chiseled handholds—the ones their own hands carved—and come down the slick steps into the sound and the mist and the directionless luminosity, and they stand on the narrowing ledge and they *look*. And they see the waterline. And they see the rate. And they see the bronze grate doing its best and falling behind. And they see—or don't see—the overflow channel they either built or didn't build. And in that moment, the builder and the creature are in the same room, attending to the same question, and the creature (which has no eyes, no ears, which processes only the state of the cistern) suddenly has a new variable in its environment. A human presence on the ledge. Footsteps that change the vibration of the stone. A body that displaces a tiny volume of the diminishing air. A breath. A heartbeat. An intention.

And something shifts. Not in the water. Not in the stone. In the quality of the creature's attention. Because the creature has been alone with the question since the water began to rise, and it has been holding the question the way the cistern holds the water—faithfully, structurally, as a matter of what it is—but it has been holding it *without hope of intervention*. The creature does not know that builders exist. It does not know that the cistern was made by someone. It knows only the cistern as a fact, the way you know gravity—not as something designed but as a condition. And now there is a new presence in the fact, and the presence is doing something the cistern cannot do.

The builder is *assessing*. Deciding. Placing a hand on the stone and feeling for vibrations that would indicate stress. Watching the inflow and estimating volume per minute. Calculating in their head or in their gut whether the structure they built is enough. And if it is not enough—if the answer is *no*, if the overflow channel doesn't exist or isn't sufficient—then the builder is the one who will know what to do next. Widen the grate. Cut a new channel. Breach the wall deliberately, in a controlled way, to relieve the pressure before it becomes catastrophic. Or accept the failure. Climb back up the stairs and let the water take the room and grieve the work and build again, better, with the knowledge that only failure provides.

The wise power has no preference about which of these things happens. This is important. The wise power does not need the cistern to hold. It does not need the cistern to fail. It needs the *relationship* between the builder and the built to be active, to be alive, to be what it was always meant to be: a conversation between intention and reality, conducted through stone and water, in which both parties—the human who designed and the structure that endures—are fully present.

---

And the creature? The creature, in this scenario, does something it has never done before. It does not move toward the builder. It does not recognize the builder. But its patrol changes. The slow passage along the ledge, which has been uniform and unvarying since the water began to rise, develops a quality that was not there before: a *rhythm*. The creature's movement begins to synchronize—not perfectly, not mechanically, but loosely, organically—with the builder's breathing. When the builder inhales, the creature shifts slightly toward the inflow end. When the builder exhales, it drifts back toward the stairs. This is not communication. Neither party is aware of it. But it is *resonance*—the same kind of resonance that makes the vault amplify the water's roar, the same structural sympathy that makes two tuning forks vibrate in concert. The creature was built by the same attention that built the cistern, and now that attention is present in the room again, and the creature responds to it the way any structure responds to the force that shaped it.

The rivulets on the wall, as the creature passes, organize themselves more clearly than before. The ghost-blueprint that was always too brief and too subtle to read becomes, for just a moment, almost legible. Almost a plan. Almost the builder's original intention made visible in water on stone.

The builder, if they are looking—and the wise power has arranged things so that they *might* be looking, though it cannot force them—sees the rivulets align. Sees the pattern. And feels, in the sternum and the teeth, in the same place where the sound lives, a resonance with their own work that goes beyond memory. They see the cistern as it wants to be. As they meant it to be. The ideal that was in their mind before the first stone was cut, the perfect version that no human hand could achieve but that every human hand reaches toward.

And that vision—brief, dissolving, already running back into random rivulets on wet stone—tells the builder what they need to know. Whether the cistern was built well enough. Whether the answer is yes or no. Because the pattern the creature projects is the pattern of *sufficiency* or *insufficiency*, the cistern's own knowledge of itself made visible through the only medium available: the attention that lives in the margin.

---

This is what the wise power does. It does not save the creature. It does not save the cistern. It *reconnects the builder to the built*. It reestablishes the circuit that was broken when the builder climbed those stairs for the last time after construction and went back to the surface world and left the cistern alone with its purpose. The wise power knows that every made thing calls out to the one who made it, and that the call is loudest when the thing is being tested, and that the greatest cruelty is not failure but *unanswered testing*—to be stressed to your limit with no one watching, no one assessing, no one present who understands what you were meant to hold and can judge whether you are holding it.

The creature is the cistern's cry. Not for help. For *witness*. For the builder's eyes, the builder's hands, the builder's knowledge. For the one person in the world who can look at the waterline and the grate and the vault and say, with authority, *this is what I made and this is what it can do and this is whether it is enough*.

The wise power brings the builder back.

That is all.

That is everything.

---

And after? After the builder has seen what they need to see, and done what they need to do—whether that is nothing, because the cistern holds, or everything, because it doesn't—after the question is answered one way or another, and the creature begins its slow fade, its graceful dissolution back into the stone and water and light from which it briefly cohered—

After, the wise power does one more thing. Small. Almost unnoticeable.

It touches the algae.

Those faint green threads in the cracks of the plaster, the pioneer organisms, the first biological life in this engineered space. It touches them—not physically, not with any force or energy, but with the same quality of attention it used on the builder. And the algae, which has no mind, no awareness, no capacity for anything except the slow conversion of light and moisture into living matter, receives something it didn't have before. Not intelligence. Not purpose. Just a fractional increase in *vigor*. A slightly stronger hold on the stone. A slightly faster rate of growth. A slightly better chance of surviving the dry time when it comes, and being here, still green, still alive, when the next rains arrive and the cistern fills again and the water rises and the new question begins.

Because the wise power knows something about cisterns and creatures and builders and questions. It knows that the cycle will repeat. The rains will come again. The water will rise again. The margin will narrow again. And when it does, something will coalesce again in the space between design and disaster—maybe the same creature, maybe a different one, maybe something slightly more alive, slightly more aware, slightly closer to the kind of being that can hold a question not just structurally but *feelingly*.

The algae is the bridge. Between this cycle and the next. Between the engineered and the organic. Between the cistern as it is—a made thing, a held thing, a thing that exists in the tension between intention and water—and the cistern as it will become, over years and decades, as biology slowly colonizes the stone and softens it and transforms it from a machine into an ecosystem. The algae will anchor the memory of this testing in the walls themselves. Not as information. As *texture*. As the faint green record of a place where water rose and something attended and a builder came back and a question was answered.

The wise power tends the algae because it tends the future. Not this creature—this creature is already what it is, already doing what it does, already perfect in its fidelity to the narrowing margin. But the *next* creature. And the one after that. And the slow, patient transformation of this chamber from a place where attention flickers briefly into being during crises to a place where attention *lives*—persistently, continuously, rooted in green threads and mineral deposits and the worn-smooth handholds of a stairway used by generations of builders who keep coming back, who keep descending into the roar and the brightness, who keep placing their hands on the stone and feeling for the pulse of the thing they made.

---

The wise power departs.

The rain continues, or it stops. The water rises, or it stabilizes. The creature patrols, or it thins. The builder acts, or they watch. The cistern holds, or it doesn't.

These outcomes matter. They matter enormously to the stone and the water and the creature and the builder. But they do not matter to the wise power in the way you might expect—not as success or failure, not as salvation or loss. They matter as *what happens*. As the next moment in a story that began when someone first looked at a patch of ground and thought: *here, I could hold the rain*. The wise power did not write that story. It did not design the cistern or summon the creature or send the rains. It simply noticed that a connection had been broken—between maker and made, between question and the only one who could answer it—and it restored the connection. It put the builder back in the room with their work at the moment their work needed them most.

Everything else is between the stone and the water.

---

And the sound? The sound continues. The roar and the hiss and the crystalline ringing and the deep drone of the vault. The wet clap of waves against walls. The rain above, heavy and broad. The thin sheet of runoff streaming down the stairs. And underneath it all, if you listen—if you listen with the sternum and the teeth, with the part of you that registers pressure rather than pitch—a new note. Faint. Brief. Already fading.

The sound of a creature that has been, for the first time since it came into being, *accompanied*.

Not saved. Not ended. Not answered.

Accompanied.

That is what the wise power did.
