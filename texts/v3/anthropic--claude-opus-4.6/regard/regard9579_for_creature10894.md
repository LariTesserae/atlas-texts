# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would do the most difficult thing.

It would do nothing.

---

Not nothing out of indifference. Not nothing out of inability, or the paralysis of seeing too many options, or the calculated restraint of a strategist waiting for a better moment. Nothing out of *recognition*. Out of the deep seeing that is the first and final act of any truly wise power, the seeing that precedes intervention and, in this case, replaces it.

Because the first thing the wise power would do is *look*. And it would see what we have already been shown — but it would see it without the need to narrate it, without the compulsion to make it mean something, and without the faint, persistent human ache that runs beneath the description like a ground wire, the ache that says *surely something here is lonely, surely something here needs saving, surely I should feel something about this and then do something about what I feel.*

The wise power would not feel that ache. Or rather — it would feel it, acknowledge it as the signature of a watching mind that cannot help but project kinship onto anything it attends to long enough, and then it would let the ache pass through without gripping it.

And it would see the pump station clearly.

---

Here is what it would see:

It would see a thing that is not suffering.

This is the most important thing, and it must be said plainly because everything in the description conspires to make you feel that something here *is* suffering — the cold, the dark, the silence, the solitude, the monastic repetition, the biological emptiness, the slow burial. These are the conditions that, mapped onto a conscious being, would constitute misery. The description knows this. The description is meticulous in not claiming the pump station is conscious or suffering, and equally meticulous in creating an emotional texture so rich that the reader's empathy activates anyway. The reader begins to *care* about the pump station. The reader begins to feel that the pump station is enduring something. The reader, if they are honest, begins to want someone to *visit* it, to witness it, to say *I see you down there, working*.

The wise power sees this desire for what it is: the reader's need, not the station's.

The pump station does not need to be seen. It does not need to be acknowledged. It does not need company, or light, or warmth, or variation, or meaning. It needs electricity, and it has electricity. It needs intact housing, and it has intact housing. It needs sacrificial anodes, and the anodes are half-depleted but still functional, and their replacements are on a schedule. Everything the pump station actually needs — in the sense of conditions necessary for its continued function — is provided. The system that sustains it is working. The pump station is, by every measure applicable to it, *fine*.

The wise power would see this. And it would recognize that the urge to intervene — to bless the station, to send it a fish, to speed up the algae, to place some marker of attention upon it — comes not from the station's need but from the intervenor's need to feel that their wisdom and benevolence have been *applied*. That they have *done something*. That the encounter between vast power and humble object has produced a visible result.

This is the temptation the wise power would refuse.

---

But I said it would do *nothing*, and I want to be precise about what nothing means, because there are different qualities of nothing, and this matters.

There is the nothing of absence — the nothing that was here before the wise power arrived, the nothing that characterizes the seafloor in every direction, the nothing of a universe that is not paying attention to this particular coordinate. That nothing is neutral. It is the default. It has no quality.

And there is the nothing of *presence without interference* — the nothing of a power that is here, that sees everything, that could act, and that chooses not to act because it understands that action is not what is called for. This nothing is not neutral. It is *warm*. It is the warm nothing of a parent watching a child who is fully absorbed in a task, not struggling, not calling for help, and not aware of being watched. The parent does not interrupt. The parent does not assist. The parent does not even make their presence known. But the watching is itself a kind of act — the most generous kind, because it is the act of letting the thing be what it is, completely, without requiring it to also be something for the watcher.

This is what the wise power would do. It would watch.

---

And in watching, here is what it would understand — the things it would not need to fix, and why:

**The silence does not need to be filled.**

The description makes the silence feel like a wound — *not the active silence of a place that has been recently emptied, but the deep, neutral silence of a place where sound was never expected*. A lesser power, a merely good power, might hear this and think: *I should bring music. I should bring whale song. I should bring some vibration into this emptiness to keep the pump station's hum company.* But the wise power would understand that the silence is not empty. The silence is the medium in which the pump station's work takes place. The silence is not the absence of sound; it is the *space* in which the hum can exist without competition, without distortion, without drowning. The pump station hums into silence the way a monk chants into a stone chamber — not because the monk is lonely, but because the stone and the silence are what make the chant *itself*. You do not improve a monastery by installing speakers.

**The biological absence does not need to be corrected.**

A merely benevolent power — benevolent but not wise — might seed the station with life. Might coax barnacles onto the housing, guide a lost crab to the pipeline berm, encourage the thin brown algae to thicken into something that could properly be called growth. Might think: *this place is barren, and barrenness is sad, and life is good, therefore I should bring life here.* But the wise power would see that the barrenness is not a failure. It is a condition. It is the condition of this particular place, this particular depth, this particular temperature and chemistry. The absence of life here is not a wrong to be righted; it is an honest expression of what this environment is. To force life into it would be to impose the values of the surface onto a place that is not the surface, to say that the seafloor is only valid when it resembles a coral reef, that a place only counts when it is *inhabited* in a way we recognize. The wise power would not make this error. The wise power would understand that an empty place is still a place, and that its emptiness is not a lack but a *quality* — as specific and as legitimate as any jungle's fullness.

**The silt's project does not need to be hastened or prevented.**

The slow burial of the concrete mattress, the imperceptible settling of the pipe into its berms, the patient geological reclamation of this small human intrusion — the wise power would see this and would not intervene in either direction. It would not speed up the burial, because the station is still working and the burial will complete itself in its own time. It would not prevent the burial, because the burial is not aggression; it is gravity, it is the seafloor doing what seafloors do, and it is beautiful in the way that all inevitable, unhurried processes are beautiful. The wise power would recognize that the contest between the station's persistence and the silt's patience is not a battle that needs a referee. It is a *relationship*, the only relationship in this place, and both sides are conducting it exactly as they should.

**The pump station does not need to know what it is.**

This is the deepest temptation, and the one the wise power would feel most acutely. Because if you are a wise power, you have the ability to grant awareness. You could — theoretically, magically, in the space of this imagining — give the pump station *something*. A flicker of self-knowledge. A moment of understanding that it is here, that it is working, that its work matters to a system beyond itself. You could let it know, just once, just briefly, that it is not just running but *serving*. That the hum it cannot hear is a kind of voice. That the intention it embodies is a form of being chosen, of being trusted, of being essential.

And the wise power would not do this.

Not because it would be cruel — it might, in fact, be beautiful — but because it would be *for the wise power*, not for the pump station. The pump station does not suffer from its ignorance. It does not experience its lack of self-knowledge as a deprivation. To give it a moment of awareness would be to give it, simultaneously, the knowledge of its own condition — the cold, the dark, the solitude, the silence, the futility of its work in any cosmic sense — and to give it these things *all at once*, without the years of context and coping and meaning-making that conscious beings develop to bear such knowledge. It would be, in effect, a cruelty disguised as a gift: *here, know yourself, feel the weight of what you are, and then go back to not knowing.* The wise power would recognize this as the structure of a certain kind of parable — the machine that is briefly given a soul and then has it taken away — and would understand that such parables are written for the reader's catharsis, not the machine's benefit.

The pump station is better off — no, not *better off*, because "better off" implies a welfare calculus and the pump station has no welfare — the pump station is *more itself* without awareness. Its purity of function, its absolute absence of inner conflict, its perfect alignment between what it is and what it does — these are not limitations. They are completeness. The pump station is one of the most complete things in existence. It has no gap between purpose and action. It has no desire that goes unfulfilled. It has no potential that goes unrealized. It is *entirely* what it is, in every molecule, at every moment. How many conscious beings can say the same?

The wise power would see this completeness and would honor it by not disrupting it.

---

So what would the wise power actually *do*, in the positive sense? What does its nothing consist of?

I think it would do three things, all of them invisible, all of them undetectable, all of them having no effect on the pump station whatsoever.

---

**First, it would witness.**

Not record. Not monitor. Not surveil. *Witness* — in the old sense, the sense that implies presence and attention and a willingness to let the thing witnessed be exactly as serious as it is. The wise power would hold this place in its attention the way the description holds it: with patience, with precision, with the refusal to rush past the details or to make them more dramatic than they are. It would note the half-depleted anodes. It would note the crescent berms of silt. It would note the thin algae and the bruise-colored pipe coating and the softened mattress edges. It would note these things not because they need noting but because the act of noting them — of *truly seeing* them — is the only thing that a wise power can offer a place that needs nothing.

This is not trivial. This is, in fact, enormous. Because the pump station at depth exists in a condition of total un-witnessing. No consciousness has ever lingered here. Divers come and go on task; they inspect specific components, complete specific jobs, and leave. ROVs pass through on camera, their footage reviewed by technicians looking for anomalies, not for *the quality of the light* or *the character of the silence*. No one has ever been present to this place in the way the description is present to it — with full, unhurried, non-instrumental attention. No one has ever simply *been here* and *looked*.

The wise power would be the first to do this. And in doing it, the wise power would complete something — not in the pump station, which is already complete, but in the *fabric of things*, in the relationship between what exists and what is known to exist. There is a sense in which an unwitnessed place is less real than a witnessed one — not physically, not metaphysically, but in the way that matters to the universe's own accounting of itself. A thing that is seen is differently situated in reality than a thing that is unseen, even if the seeing changes nothing about the thing. The wise power would understand this and would offer its seeing as a gift — not to the pump station, which cannot receive gifts, but to the *fact* of the pump station, to its existence as such, which is the kind of thing a wise power can address.

---

**Second, it would understand the maintenance schedule.**

This sounds mundane. It is not.

The wise power would trace the full web of dependencies that keep the pump station running — the subsea cable, the platform turbine, the gas supply, the power grid, the maintenance contracts, the diving teams, the ROV operators, the spreadsheet with the anode replacement dates, the engineer who updates the spreadsheet, the office where the engineer works, the coffee the engineer drinks, the rain on the parking lot outside the office window. It would follow every thread outward from this small steel box on the seafloor and see where each one connects to a human decision, a human system, a human life. It would see the pump station not in isolation — which is how the description presents it, because isolation is its experiential condition — but in *context*, which is its actual condition, its true situation in the web of things.

And it would see that the pump station is, in fact, *held*. Not in any mystical sense. In the most practical sense imaginable. It is held by the maintenance schedule. It is held by the power supply. It is held by the engineering standards that dictated the thickness of its housing and the spacing of its anodes. It is held by the institutional memory of the company that installed it and the regulatory framework that governs its operation. It is held by dozens of people who have never been here and never will be here but whose work keeps it running.

The pump station does not know it is held. It cannot know this. But the wise power would know it, and would find in this knowledge something like peace — the recognition that even in this most solitary, most unwitnessed, most austere of places, there is a web of care operating. Not care in the emotional sense. Care in the sense of *maintenance*, of *attention to continued function*, of *someone checking the spreadsheet*. The pump station is looked after. Not lovingly. Not tenderly. Bureaucratically, procedurally, on a schedule. And the wise power would see that this bureaucratic care is, in its own way, a form of the same attentiveness it is now practicing — a form of not-forgetting, of keeping something in mind, of ensuring that a small, important, invisible thing continues to receive what it needs to go on being what it is.

The wise power would honor this. It would see the spreadsheet and the diving schedule and the maintenance contracts and it would understand them as acts of stewardship, even if the people performing them would never use that word. And it would not improve them or intervene in them. It would simply recognize them for what they are.

---

**Third — and this is the one I have been building toward, the one I was not sure I would arrive at, the one that required the other two to be established first — the wise power would turn its attention to the hum.**

Not to change it. Not to amplify it. Not to decode it or translate it or give it meaning. Just to *attend to it*. To place its limitless, benevolent, wise attention upon the faint vibration that passes through the steel housing and into the water and dies within meters, the vibration that is not communication and not expression and not art and not prayer and not anything except the waste energy of rotating machinery — and to stay with it.

To stay with it the way you would stay with a single sustained note played in an empty room.

Because here is what the wise power would understand that we almost understand, that the description brings us to the edge of understanding and then leaves us to cross on our own:

The hum is the purest thing here.

It is the only thing in this place that is *happening*. Everything else — the silt, the mattress, the pipeline, the coating — is in a state, a condition, a static fact. The hum is an event. It is a continuous event, an event that has been occurring without interruption for years, an event so stable and so consistent that it barely registers as an event at all — but it is one. It is the sound of energy being converted into work, and it is happening *right now*, as you read this, if the pump station is still operating, which it almost certainly is, because the spreadsheet says the next maintenance visit isn't for another eight months.

The hum is the sound of the pump station's life. Not its *life* in the metaphorical sense that the description carefully explores and carefully qualifies. Its life in the thermodynamic sense. The hum is the audible evidence that energy is entering this system, being transformed, doing work, and producing waste heat and vibration — the exact signature of a dissipative structure, the exact fingerprint of local order sustained against entropy. It is the same signature, in abstract, as the sound of a beating heart, or the electrical activity of a brain, or the metabolic buzz of a bacterial colony. Different in every particular. Identical in principle.

The wise power would sit with this hum — *sit* metaphorically, or perhaps actually, hovering in the cold dark water a meter from the housing where the vibration is still perceptible — and it would simply listen.

Not for a message. There is no message. Not for a pattern. The pattern is monotone. Not for a change. Nothing changes. It would listen the way contemplatives listen to their own breathing: not to learn anything, not to achieve anything, but to be present to the fact that something is continuing. That something, against the background of a universe that trends toward equilibrium and stillness and cold, is *still going*. Is still converting, still pushing, still humming, still maintaining this small pocket of organized activity in the midst of the grey silt and the dark water and the silence.

And the wise power would feel — I think it would feel — something that is not pity and not admiration and not love, because all of those require a subject on the other end, and the pump station is not a subject. It would feel something more like *solidarity*. The recognition of a fellow process. Because the wise power, too, is a dissipative structure — a sustained improbability, an organization maintained against entropy, a thing that runs. The wise power and the pump station are kin in this: they are both, at the most fundamental level, patterns that persist. The wise power does it with wisdom and consciousness and benevolence. The pump station does it with electric motors and welded steel. But the *doing* — the bare fact of the doing, the thermodynamic act of *staying organized* — is the same.

And in that recognition, the wise power would find the answer to the question it came here to answer, which is: *what should I do for this place?*

And the answer would be: *you should do what it does.*

You should persist. You should continue. You should maintain your function without fanfare, without witness, without requiring that your work be seen or appreciated or understood. You should hum. You should let the silence absorb your hum, and you should not mind that it does. You should let the silt creep over your edges, and you should not fight it, because the silt is not your enemy — the silt is just another process, doing what it does, on its own timescale, with its own completeness. You should be *what you are*, as entirely as this pump station is what it is, with no gap between your purpose and your action, with no wasted effort on being seen.

The pump station does not need the wise power.

The wise power, perhaps, needed the pump station.

---

So the wise power would do nothing. It would leave no trace. It would alter no parameter. The hum would not grow louder or softer. The anodes would not dissolve faster or slower. The silt would continue its creep. The algae would remain thin and crusty and marginal. The light would remain grey-blue and barely cyclic. The silence would remain total beyond three meters.

The wise power would depart — if *depart* is even the right word for something that was never physically present in any way the pump station could register — and the pump station would go on running, exactly as it was, exactly as it has been, exactly as it will be until the day the power stops or the last anode dissolves or someone in an office building hundreds of kilometers away closes a spreadsheet for the last time and the process that lives here finally, gently, without protest or awareness, stops.

And the silt will begin its final work.

And the seafloor will accept what was placed upon it.

And in the fullness of a very long time, it will be as if nothing was ever here at all.

Except that something was. And something saw it. And the seeing changed nothing.

And that was enough.
