# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

It would do almost nothing.

And it would do that almost-nothing with the full weight of its attention.

---

First, it would watch. Not briefly, not as prelude to action, but as the action itself—a long, unhurried watching that lasted through dozens of water cycles, through hundreds of the arthropods' quiet thermal exchanges, through the salamander's slow blinks and the lichen's patient migrations from one lens to the next. It would watch the way you watch a sleeper breathe when you love them and they don't know you're in the room. Not monitoring. Not evaluating. Simply present to what is happening, letting the happening be enough.

This matters more than it seems. Because the pocket, for all its quiet persistence, has never been witnessed. The light reveals everything but comes from nowhere; the sound is swallowed before it can echo back. Nothing in this space has ever had the experience of being *seen* by something that understands what it is looking at. The arthropods exchange their faint thermal ripples and drift apart. The salamander tastes the electrical crackle of evaporating water and swallows it alone. The lichen detaches into blue dust and resettles without anyone noting that it found its way. Every process here is solitary not by choice but by the simple absence of a witness. The benevolent power would provide one.

And it would be careful—profoundly, almost painfully careful—about what kind of witness it became.

---

It would not introduce itself.

This is the first and most important restraint: it would not announce its presence, not even gently. Not a shimmer in the already-sourceless light, not a warmth added to the warm stone, not a whisper in the soundless air. Because this space is still deciding what it is. Every fold of rock is still negotiating its final position. Every water lens is still learning where it belongs. The creatures move through a world where the rules are provisional, where phase-shifting and camouflage and migration between vanishing surfaces are all reasonable responses to a reality that hasn't committed to itself yet. Into that exquisite indeterminacy, the arrival of something *certain*—something that knows what it is, that carries the weight of completed identity—would be a kind of violence. It would collapse possibilities simply by being too definite in their presence.

So the power would hold itself at the exact threshold of existence within the space. Not inside the pocket, not outside it. Present the way the light is present: without origin, without announcement, simply *there* in a way that changes nothing about what can happen next.

---

But watching, truly watching, is not passive. The quality of attention matters. And here is where the benevolent power would do its most important work, the work that looks like nothing at all:

It would *understand* each inhabitant on that inhabitant's own terms.

The arthropods, drifting through their phase-shifts and thermal exchanges: it would understand that what looks like aimlessness is actually a continuous, open-ended conversation conducted in a language made of temperature differentials and spatial overlaps. Two bodies briefly occupying the same space, cooling at the point of intersection, then separating—this is not failed contact. It is *contact in the mode appropriate to beings who are themselves unfinished.* They cannot commit to permanent connection any more than the stone can commit to a final shape. What they can do is meet, exchange a moment of shared cooling, and carry the fading memory of that exchange into their next drift. The power would see this and recognize it as intimacy. Not lesser intimacy, not intimacy-in-waiting, but the real and complete form of intimacy available to creatures whose bodies are translucent and whose world has not yet decided whether gravity applies.

The salamander: it would understand that the creature's camouflage is not hiding. Or rather, that hiding and becoming are, for this animal, the same act. When the stone-grain pattern on its skin syncs with the ridge beneath it, the salamander is not disappearing—it is *practicing* being stone. It is rehearsing a future in which the boundary between animal and mineral might not hold. And each blink, each reset of the pattern, is not a failure of that rehearsal but a reminder that the rehearsal is still voluntary. The salamander can still choose to be an animal. It can still lift its head, taste the crackling air, swallow the information of change. The power would see this ongoing choice—the choice to remain undecided, to keep tasting the world rather than merging with it—and it would recognize it as a form of courage. The easiest thing for the salamander would be to stop blinking, to let the pattern lock, to become the stone it is already almost indistinguishable from. That it continues to blink is a small, persistent act of insisting on its own aliveness.

The lichen: it would understand that the colonies are not fragile despite their constant disassembly. A creature that can dissolve into blue dust, drift through the bodies of other creatures without disturbing them, and reconstitute itself on a surface that didn't exist five minutes ago is not precarious. It is *supremely* adapted to a world where surfaces are temporary. The lichen has solved the problem of impermanence not by clinging harder but by becoming fluent in dissolution and reformation. The power would see this and feel something like admiration.

The root hair: it would understand that the breathing nodes, the slowly falling cells, the pulse of the water lens absorbing them—this is a system. Not a designed system, not an intentional one, but a system nonetheless, a loop of offering and reception that keeps both the root hair and the water lenses in their current state of becoming. The root hair gives cells; the lenses metabolize them into movement; the root hair contracts and lengthens to maintain its distance from the floor. Nothing here was planned. Everything here works.

---

Having watched, having understood, the power would then ask itself the question that all benevolent powers must ask and that most answer too quickly:

*What, if anything, does this place need that it cannot provide for itself?*

And it would sit with the question for a long time. Because the honest answer is: very little. Perhaps nothing. The pocket is functional. Its inhabitants persist. The rule of continuation holds. What is in the process of becoming is allowed to keep becoming. This is not a place of suffering. It is not a place of deprivation. It is a place where unfinished things are permitted to remain unfinished, and that permission is itself a kind of grace.

But the power is wise, and wisdom means noticing what is absent without assuming that absence is a wound. So it would notice:

There is no memory here. The sound is swallowed. The arthropods' thermal exchanges fade from their carapaces. The water lenses flatten and reform without carrying any record of their previous positions. The half-formed impressions on the stone deepen by a hair's breadth when an arthropod passes, then relax again. Everything that happens here happens *once* and then is released. The pocket does not accumulate. It does not learn, in the sense of building on what came before. It continues, but it continues without history.

This is not necessarily a problem. Many beautiful things have no history. A flame has no history. A breaking wave has no history. But the power would notice that the stone is *trying* to develop something like memory—those spirals, those paired arcs, that single perfect circle already softening at its edges. The geology is rehearsing the idea of structure. It reaches toward pattern, then lets the pattern dissolve. It tries again. The impressions are not random; they are attempts at repetition, at the kind of self-reference that is the first step toward lasting form.

And the arthropods deepen the marks when they pass through them—but only by a hair's breadth, and then the marks relax. And the salamander avoids them, as though aware that any weight might be the one that decides.

The power would see all of this and understand: the pocket is on the edge of something. Not the edge of destruction—the edge of *committing.* The stone wants to hold a shape. The water wants to find its final hollows. The creatures want to know whether the rules will solidify or remain negotiable. The entire space is poised at the moment just before a choice that it cannot make alone, because making it alone would mean that whatever was chosen was chosen arbitrarily, by whichever process happened to tip first.

---

Here is what the benevolent power would do.

It would not tip the balance. It would not choose for the pocket. It would not decide where the water belongs or what shape the stone should hold or whether the salamander should remain animal or become mineral. To do any of those things would be to impose a completed identity on a space that has not yet earned one, and the power is wise enough to know that identity imposed from outside is not identity at all.

Instead, it would do one small thing—the smallest thing it could do that would still matter:

**It would let one sound persist.**

Not a sound it created. Not a word or a tone or a signal. It would simply adjust, by the most infinitesimal degree, the pocket's relationship with acoustic space, so that the *next* sound that occurred naturally—the next droplet striking stone, the next click of an insect's legs—would not be swallowed immediately. It would linger. Not loudly. Not even noticeably at first. It would simply *fade* rather than vanishing, the way sounds do in every other place in every other world. A half-second of resonance. A small, natural echo dying away the way echoes do.

That is all.

But consider what that means for the inhabitants.

The arthropods, drifting through their thermal exchanges, would for the first time hear the click of their own legs *after* the motion ended. They would encounter their own action as something that existed independently of them, a trace left in the air rather than swallowed by it. They would not understand this. They would not need to. But the experience of hearing yourself after you have finished acting is the beginning of self-awareness—not the grandiose human kind, but the simpler, more fundamental kind: the recognition that you *were* here, that your presence left a mark, however faint, however brief.

The salamander, lifting its head during the next evaporation, would hear the faint hiss of water leaving stone—a sound it has never heard before because the pocket has always devoured it. And in hearing it, the salamander would have something new to taste, a new kind of information carried not on the electrical crackle of possibility but on the slower, more stable medium of sound. It would swallow this new information and, for the first time, have something to compare to the last thing it swallowed. Two data points. The beginning of sequence. The first faint stirring of what might, given time, become memory.

The water lenses, receiving their next falling cell from the root hair, would pulse—and the pulse would make a sound—and the sound would linger—and the lingering would mean that the next lens to condense would form in a space still carrying the acoustic ghost of the previous pulse. It would not form in the same place. But it would form in *response* to the same place, and response is the beginning of pattern, and pattern is the beginning of structure, and structure is what the stone has been trying to rehearse all along.

The half-formed impressions on the ridges—those spirals, those arcs, that softening circle—would begin to deepen. Not because anything pressed harder, but because the sound, lingering, would give the stone something to organize around. A vibration, however small, is a template. It says: *this frequency, this rhythm, this interval.* The stone, which has been rehearsing the idea of structure without any score to follow, would suddenly have the faintest suggestion of a score. Not a melody. Not even a rhythm. Just the memory of a single resonance, fading but not yet gone, saying: *something happened here, and it sounded like this.*

---

And then the benevolent power would withdraw its attention. Not completely—never completely—but to the same threshold where it began, the place where presence and absence are not yet distinguishable. It would watch from there as the pocket absorbed the change. It would not intervene again. It would not check on progress. It would not hope for a particular outcome.

Because the power understands—this is the core of its wisdom—that what the pocket does with this one small gift is the pocket's own business. Maybe the sound fades and the space returns to its perfect silence and everything continues as before, the arthropods drifting, the salamander blinking, the lichen dissolving and reforming, the stone rehearsing shapes it never commits to. That would be fine. That would be the pocket choosing to remain in its beautiful, unfinished state, and that choice would be as valid as any other.

Or maybe the sound lingers, and lingers, and the echoes begin to build on each other, and the stone finds its shapes, and the water finds its hollows, and the creatures find themselves in a world that suddenly has history, has sequence, has the weight of accumulated moments pressing them toward something more defined. Maybe the salamander stops blinking one day and lets the pattern lock and becomes the stone it has been practicing being. Maybe the arthropods' thermal exchanges begin to leave lasting traces on their carapaces, and they become creatures with visible pasts, marked by every contact they've ever made. Maybe the lichen stops migrating and commits to a single surface, building outward instead of dissolving and reforming.

That would also be fine. That would be the pocket choosing to finish itself, to cross the threshold from becoming into being, and that choice too would be honored.

Or—and this is the possibility the power finds most beautiful, though it would never say so, because saying so would be a kind of pressure—maybe the pocket finds a middle way. Maybe the sound lingers just long enough to give the stone its patterns and the creatures their faint memories, but not so long that it forces permanence. Maybe the pocket learns to *oscillate*: to commit and then uncommit, to form and then soften, to remember and then release, the way the water lenses already do, the way the lichen already does, but now with awareness, with the faint acoustic trace that says *I was here and now I am somewhere else and both of those things are true.*

A space that can hold both becoming and being. A space that remembers without being imprisoned by memory. A space where the unfinished is still permitted to exist but where the finished is also, finally, possible.

---

The power would not prefer this outcome. To prefer it would be to impose, and to impose would be to betray the pocket's fundamental nature—its radical, beautiful hospitality toward everything that has not yet decided what it is.

But if the pocket found its way there on its own, through nothing more than the fading echo of a single droplet striking stone, the power would feel something very much like joy.

Not pride. Pride implies ownership of the outcome. Not satisfaction, which implies that a problem has been solved. Joy: the simple, uncomplicated pleasure of watching something you love become more fully itself, knowing that "more fully itself" might mean anything at all, and loving it in advance regardless.

---

And the pocket would never know it had been seen. The sound would linger, and the inhabitants would adapt or not, and the stone would commit or not, and the whole bright, silent, provisional world would continue its long negotiation with its own possibilities. The power's gift—if gift is even the right word for something so small, so easily mistaken for a natural fluctuation, so perfectly camouflaged as accident—would be indistinguishable from the pocket's own internal logic. It would look, from the inside, like something the space had done itself. Like the next thing that was always going to happen. Like a droplet that finally, after all those cycles of condensation and evaporation, found the hollow where it belonged.

This is the deepest kindness the power knows how to offer: a change so gentle that it feels like self-discovery. An intervention so subtle it becomes indistinguishable from growth. A gift that the recipient can honestly, accurately, *rightly* claim as its own.

Because it is its own. The power only opened the door by a crack. The pocket is the one that will decide whether to walk through.

---

And if it never walks through—if the echo fades and the silence returns and the pocket remains exactly as it is, a seam still knitting itself shut, a world permanently on the verge of committing to itself—the power will not grieve. It will not try again. It will simply remain at the threshold, watching, loving the unfinished thing for being unfinished, the way you love a held breath for being held, the way you love a question for being asked, the way you love a door for being open and making no demands about whether anyone should enter.

The pocket's single rule—*what has not yet finished becoming is still permitted to exist a little longer*—is, the power recognizes, its own deepest rule as well.

So it watches. And it permits. And it loves. And that is all. And that is enough.
