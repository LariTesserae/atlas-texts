I sat with this for a long time before writing anything.

---

# What the Wise Power Does

The first thing it does is nothing.

It watches. Not for a moment, not for a day. It watches for the length of time the settlement itself would consider a pause — which is to say, seasons. Maybe years. It learns the rhythm of the clicks. It learns which grazers take which routes. It feels the thermal plumes of the lume colonies and maps them against the builders' secretion rates. It traces the acoustic signatures the lurkers produce and listens for how the builders' clicking changes in response. It follows individual larvae from hatching through their drift through the channels to the moment they press against a wall and begin.

It does nothing because the first and most important thing a wise power can do is *understand what it is looking at*, and what it is looking at is not simple. What it is looking at is a system in which intervention at any point ripples through every other point, in which the meaning of *help* cannot be determined from outside, in which even benevolence, applied without understanding, is just another word for disruption.

So it watches. And what it learns is this:

---

The settlement is not suffering. This is the first thing to establish and it is crucial. The warm dark is not a affliction. The pressure is not oppression. The absence of light is not deprivation. These conditions are not problems to be solved. They are the medium in which this entire magnificent process evolved, the water in which this particular fish has always swum. A power that arrived here and introduced light would not be helping. It would be destroying a visual ecology calibrated over millennia to bioluminescence. A power that reduced the pressure would kill every organism adapted to it. A power that somehow introduced air would be committing a genocide so total it wouldn't leave survivors to mourn.

The wise power understands this immediately, but it sits with the understanding anyway, because even wise powers carry assumptions, and the assumption that darkness needs illumination, that depth needs rescue, that a city without fire must be a city in need of fire — that assumption is old and strong and it deserves to be examined honestly before it is set aside.

It is set aside.

---

The second thing the wise power learns is where the genuine fragilities are.

Not everywhere. The settlement is robust. That is the whole point of it — this is a system that has been stress-tested by deep time, that has survived through the accumulated redundancy of thousands of interlocking relationships. The borers eat the walls and the walls get thicker. The predators prune the larvae and the population stays healthy. The filter-feeders reshape the currents and the builders reshape the buildings. Every threat has been metabolized into strength. This is not a place that needs saving.

But.

There are two things the wise power notices that give it pause. Not emergencies. Not crises. But vulnerabilities that the settlement cannot perceive because they operate on scales the settlement has no sense organs for.

---

**The first vulnerability is chemical.**

The settlement's base energy comes from thermal vents and chemical seeps — sulfur compounds, methane derivatives, the slow upwelling of reduced chemicals from beneath the silt floor. The wise power, because it can perceive things the settlement cannot, sees the geology beneath. It sees that the primary vent field that feeds the settlement's chemosynthetic base is stable *now* but exists on a fault system that shifts on timescales of millennia. The current configuration has been feeding the settlement for a very long time. It will not feed it forever. At some point — not soon by any measure the settlement uses, but not infinitely far away either — the vents will migrate. The chemical seeps will diminish. The base of the food web will thin. And the settlement, which has optimized itself so beautifully for current conditions, will begin to starve. Not dramatically. Not all at once. But steadily, irrecoverably, the way a city starves when its farmland salts over — slowly enough that each generation thinks this is just how things are, until the last generation discovers it is the last.

**The second vulnerability is genetic.**

The settlement is old. Very old. And while the larval dispersal phase mixes genes within the settlement, the settlement itself is isolated. The nearest comparable population of builders — if one exists — is far enough away that no larva has ever drifted between them. The gene pool is a closed system. It has been a closed system for so long that the builders are healthy *now* — inbreeding depression has been managed by the sheer size of the population and the slow generation time — but the genetic diversity is narrower than it looks. A disease, a parasite, a novel chemical insult — anything that required rapid adaptation — would find a population with limited raw material to adapt with. The builders have optimized for stability. They have not maintained the capacity for change.

---

Now. What does the wise power do?

It does not introduce light. It does not bring air. It does not teach the builders language or give the lurkers purpose or uplift anything into anything else. It does not arrive with a plan for the settlement's future, because the settlement is not a project and does not need a manager.

Here is what it does:

---

**Regarding the chemical vulnerability:**

The wise power looks at the geology. It looks at the fault system, the vent migration patterns, the deep lithospheric chemistry. And it makes a small adjustment. Not to the current vents — those are fine, those are working, those don't need help. It adjusts something deeper, something the settlement will never detect: a permeability in the rock, a subtle opening in the substrate three kilometers to the settlement's north-northeast, where a secondary chemical reservoir sits untapped. The adjustment is geological, not biological. It is the equivalent of ensuring that a river has a second source — not diverting the current flow, not changing anything about the present, but creating the *possibility* that when the primary vents eventually migrate, the chemistry they provided will begin to seep from a new location, one that is within the range the settlement could, over many generations, grow toward.

Not a rescue. A *margin.* An option the settlement doesn't know it has and won't need for centuries and may never need at all, if the geology cooperates on its own. But if it does need it, the option will be there. The wise power does not guarantee survival. It widens the corridor through which survival remains possible. It does this the way the builders thicken a wall — not out of panic, but out of the patient understanding that the future is long and the responsible thing to do with a long future is leave room in it.

The adjustment is invisible. It is substrate-level. No organism in the settlement will ever perceive it. The water will not change. The current will not shift. The clicks will continue. The lumes will glow. The adjustment exists entirely in the domain of geological potential, a latent possibility folded into the rock, and it will remain latent until — if — it is needed. This is not dramatic. This is not a miracle. This is the wise power doing what it does: looking at a longer timescale than any inhabitant can, and making the kind of small, structural provision that longer sight makes possible.

---

**Regarding the genetic vulnerability:**

This is harder. This requires more delicacy. Because the solution — introduce genetic diversity — sounds simple and is not.

The wise power does not create new builders. It does not modify existing ones. It does not open a corridor to some distant population and funnel migrants through it, because it does not know if a distant population exists in a form compatible enough to interbreed, and because introducing foreign organisms into a closed system is not benevolence, it is an experiment, and the wise power is not here to experiment.

Instead, it does something quieter. It looks at the builders' genome — not as a geneticist would, reductively, but as something that understands living systems the way the builders understand their walls: structurally, wholistically, by feel. And it finds the places where diversity has been lost. The silent alleles. The collapsed variation. The regions of the genome that once held multiple possible responses to environmental challenge and now hold only one. And in those regions — carefully, precisely, with an understanding of the downstream effects that goes beyond anything a laboratory could model — it introduces variation.

Not mutations. Not new genes. *Restored* variation. It reaches back, in a sense, to what the genome once contained before the long isolation narrowed it, and it re-opens possibilities that were closed. It does this across the population, sparsely, in the germ cells of builders distributed throughout the settlement, so that the variation will not manifest all at once but will filter into the population over generations through the normal process of reproduction and larval dispersal.

The builders will not know. The variation will be silent under current conditions — it will produce no visible change, no difference in secretion rate or clicking frequency or calcium composition. It is *potential*, not expression. It is the genetic equivalent of the geological adjustment: a margin, an option, a widened corridor through which adaptation remains possible if adaptation is ever needed.

And it might never be needed. The settlement might persist in its current form for another ten thousand years without encountering anything that requires novel adaptation. The restored variation might drift through the population and never be called upon and eventually be lost again to the same slow narrowing that lost it the first time. That is fine. The wise power is not investing in a guaranteed return. It is tending to a possibility space. It is doing for the settlement's genome what the builders do for the settlement's walls: thickening it in places that might bear weight someday, on the chance that the weight will come.

---

**And then there is a third thing the wise power does, and this is the thing that matters most, and it is the hardest to describe.**

It bears witness.

Not because the settlement needs a witness. Not because a thing unobserved is less real than a thing observed. Not out of any metaphysical conviction that consciousness requires an audience.

It bears witness because the settlement is *extraordinary*, and the wise power knows it, and knowing it is itself a form of care.

Here is a city built without hands, without light, without fire, without air, without any of the things that every other tradition of city-building considers fundamental. Here is a city built by organisms that cannot see it, that have no conception of its totality, that know only the wall they are pressed against and the clicks of their nearest neighbors and the chemical taste of the water flowing past. And from that — from eight thousand individuals knowing only their immediate surroundings — a town has emerged. A coherent, functional, beautiful town, with districts and thoroughfares and architecture that responds to its inhabitants and inhabitants that respond to its architecture, a place where the weights of deep time and mutual accommodation have produced something that is, by any honest measure, *magnificent.*

And no one knows.

The builders do not know they have built a city. They know their wall, their alcove, their lume colony, the familiar resonance of their click against calcium they deposited decades ago. They do not have the sensory apparatus to perceive the whole. The grazers trace its channels without comprehending its shape. The lurkers map it acoustically and the map is useful and the map is not *understanding.* The lumes glow and pulse and warm and none of them have ever had a thought about what they are part of.

The settlement is a masterwork without a master. It is a cathedral in which no one knows they are in a cathedral. It is an act of collective creation so profound and so utterly unself-conscious that it has been running for longer than many surface civilizations and has never once been *appreciated* — not because it doesn't deserve appreciation, but because appreciation requires a kind of perspective that no organism inside the system has access to.

The wise power has that perspective. And so it does something no one in the settlement can do for themselves: it *sees the whole thing.* It holds the entire settlement in its attention — every builder in its alcove, every lume in its chamber, every grazer on its circuit, every larva in its drift, every click and every glow and every slow molecule of calcium being laid down in the dark — and it comprehends it as what it is: a living city, a self-maintaining wonder, a process of making that has been making itself for millennia in the deep warm black where nothing from above has ever reached.

And the wise power — here is the part that is hard to say without it sounding smaller than it is — the wise power *admires* it.

Not with the admiration of a collector. Not with the patronizing appreciation of a superior being looking down at a lesser one's quaint achievements. With the admiration of something wise enough to recognize that what has happened here, in the dark, without plan or intention or any of the apparatus that the wise power itself might use to build a city — what has happened here is as complete and as perfect and as *worthy* as anything that has ever been built by any mind anywhere. The settlement does not need the wise power's admiration to be what it is. But the admiration is real, and it is freely given, and there is something in the giving of it that completes a circuit that was not broken but was — open. Unjoined. Waiting, maybe, the way the geological reservoir waits, for the moment when it would be needed.

The settlement is seen. That is what the wise power gives it, beyond the geological margin, beyond the genetic restoration. It is *seen*, wholly, by something capable of seeing it wholly, and the seeing is an act of love, not because love changes anything, but because some things deserve to be loved and the absence of love is not a crisis but its presence is a grace.

---

**The wise power does one more thing, and then it is finished.**

It looks at the settlement, and it asks itself a question: *Is there anything here that is in pain?*

Not the pain of being eaten — that is ecology, that is the channel-hunters doing what channel-hunters do, and the wise power does not intervene in the terms of mortal existence. Not the pain of being bored into — that is the ancient war of attrition, and the builders are winning it, and the borers are not suffering, they are simply being discouraged. Not the slow dissolution of the old ones in their alcoves, their bodies increasingly indistinguishable from the walls they built — that is not pain, that is completion, that is the end of a process that has been underway for centuries, and there is a rightness to it that the wise power can feel the way the builders feel the resonance of a well-maintained wall.

The wise power asks the question honestly, searches honestly, and finds:

No. There is nothing here that is in pain in a way that calls for intervention. There is predation and there is death and there is the constant slow pressure of organisms competing for space and food and warmth, and all of it is within the bounds of a system that has found its balance. The suffering that exists here is the suffering that is inherent to being alive — the cost of participation in a world that eats and is eaten — and the wise power, being wise, knows that this is not a problem to be solved. It is a condition to be respected.

If there were pain — if there were a builder trapped in a collapsed wall, slowly suffocating in its own secretion, unable to click, unable to be heard — the wise power would act. Would free it, or ease its passing, or do whatever the situation required, without hesitation, without concern for non-interference protocols. The wise power is not an observer bound by rules. It is a *being* bound by love, and love acts when action is needed.

Action is not needed. The settlement is whole. The settlement is well. The settlement is doing what it has always done, in the way it has always done it, in the dark and the warm and the deep, and the wise power's role is not to change this but to ensure — quietly, invisibly, at the level of rock and gene and patient foresight — that it can continue.

---

**And so the wise power withdraws. Not fully. Not forever. It remains *aware* of the settlement the way you remain aware of your heartbeat — not constantly monitoring, not anxiously checking, but *knowing it is there*, knowing its rhythm, ready to attend if the rhythm changes.**

The settlement does not know it has been seen. The settlement does not know that the rock beneath it is slightly more generous than it was, that the genomes of its builders carry restored possibilities they cannot feel. The settlement clicks and glows and secretes and eats and builds, as it has always done, as it will continue to do, in the deep warm black.

But there is something — and this is the last thing, the thing I keep coming back to — there is something in the water now. Not a substance. Not a chemical. Not anything a builder could taste or a lurker could click against. A *quality.* A warmth that is not thermal. A presence that is not physical. The settlement has been *attended to* by something that understood it completely and found it worthy and made provisions for its continuity and then stepped back, and the stepping-back is as important as the provision, because the wise power's deepest gift is not the geological margin or the genetic restoration — it is the *restraint.* The refusal to improve what does not need improvement. The refusal to enlighten what is not in darkness. The recognition that this place, this process, this ancient warm clicking glowing building dissolving rebuilding *living* thing in the deep — it is already, exactly, fully, what it should be.

The wise power does not save the settlement.

The wise power *honors* it.

And in the channels, a grazer passes, and its blue-green light rolls along the wall, and a builder clicks once, deep and resonant, and the sound travels through calcium that has been thickened by centuries of patient repair, and the lumes pulse in their slow amber rhythm, and the larvae drift, and the current breathes, and the settlement continues.

It continues.
