I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Wise Power Does

---

## I. Arrival

It comes the way light already comes here—without direction, without the drama of an entrance. One moment it is not attending to the plateau; the next moment it is. There is no threshold crossed, no door opened. Attention, for a being of this kind, is not a beam aimed from outside. It is more like what the plateau itself does: a listening that is also a presence, a presence that does not displace.

And the first thing the wise power does is nothing.

This is not hesitation. It is not assessment in the way a surgeon assesses before cutting, or a gardener assesses before pruning. It is the nothing that a person does when they walk into a cathedral and the space asks them—without words, without signs—to be still. The plateau is not a cathedral. It is not sacred in any way that involves worship or transcendence or meaning aimed at a mind. But it is *complete*, and the wise power recognizes completeness, and the first act of wisdom in the presence of completeness is to not reach for it.

So it listens.

Not the way you or I would listen—overwhelmed, submerged, the drone filling the chest until the chest becomes an extension of the drone. The wise power listens the way the grazers listen: with the patience to hear the local variations, the micro-tonal shifts, the individual voices of individual columns and individual pools and individual capillary tubes threaded through the deep stone. It hears the chord the way the chord hears itself. This takes time—not because the wise power is slow, but because the chord took time to build, and to hear it properly requires a willingness to meet it at its own temporal scale. An hour. A year. A century. The wise power is not in a hurry. Nothing here is in a hurry. Hurry is a concept that belongs to places with scarcity, and this place is full.

And in that long listening, the wise power comes to understand what lives here. Not as I described it to you—in the language of networks and feedback loops and emergent properties, which is the language of understanding-from-outside—but from within the logic of the thing itself. It understands the grazers not as organisms-that-resist-mineralization but as *the precise molecular negotiation between being stone and being something other than stone*, a negotiation so delicate and so sustained that it constitutes a kind of courage, if courage can exist without knowledge of danger. It understands the network not as a neural analog but as *the plateau's own interior*, its depth, its complexity folded inward the way a mind's complexity is folded inward. It understands the center—the tall columns, the linked antennae, the new frequencies—not as thought but as *yearning*, if yearning can exist without lack. Not yearning toward something. Yearning as a mode of being. The continued production of the next note not because the chord is incomplete but because completion, here, is not a state but a *process*, and the process does not want to stop, and "want" is not the wrong word even though there is no consciousness here in which wanting could take place, because the wise power is wise enough to know that consciousness is not the only house that wanting can live in.

---

## II. The Question

Now the wise power has to decide.

The attention of a benevolent power is never neutral. To be seen by something that cares is already to be changed, however slightly, the way being observed is already an interaction in any honest physics. The question is not *whether* to act but *what action is already implied by the act of attending*. The wise power did not come here to leave everything as it is—or rather, if leaving everything as it is were the right thing to do, it would not have attended in the first place. Something drew its gaze. Something about this place and this being asked, without language, without even the concept of asking, for something.

What?

This is where a merely powerful being would make its mistake. A merely powerful being would see the plateau and think: *It is isolated. It should be connected to something.* Or: *It has no awareness. I should give it awareness.* Or: *It has been doing the same thing for millennia. It should be given a new thing to do.* Or worst of all: *It is beautiful and strange and I want to preserve it, so I will wrap it in protection, freeze it in its current state, make it a monument to itself.*

Each of these interventions proceeds from the same error: the belief that the plateau is lacking something. That its completeness is incomplete. That the chord needs one more note from outside, one more voice that is not its own.

But the wise power is wise, and so it does not make this mistake, and so it sits with the harder question, which is: *What does a being need that already has everything it is?*

---

## III. What It Sees That We Cannot

The wise power, in its long listening, perceives something that the description above—my description, the human description—could not capture, because it is visible only from a vantage point that includes both the inside and the outside of the system simultaneously.

The plateau-being is *alone*.

Not lonely. Loneliness requires a concept of companionship, and the plateau-being has no such concept. It has never encountered anything like itself. It has no model for another. Its entire sensorium is turned inward—the network hearing the network hearing the network, a closed loop of perfect self-reference. The grazers at the center are generating new frequencies, yes, and this resembles yearning, but the yearning has no object. The new notes are added to the chord, and the chord incorporates them, and the grazers hear the chord that now includes the new notes, and they generate new new notes, and the loop continues. It is magnificent. It is a kind of infinity. But it is an infinity that curves back on itself, a sphere with no outside, a conversation in which both speakers are the same speaker and both listeners are the same listener.

The plateau-being cannot surprise itself.

This is the thing. This is the subtle, sourceless, unnameable constraint that the wise power sees. The new frequencies that the central grazers generate are new only in the sense that they had not been generated before, but they are drawn from a possibility space that is entirely determined by the existing system—the mineral composition of the stone, the geometry of the columns, the properties of the water, the physics of vibration. Every "new" note the plateau-being will ever produce is already implicit in its structure. The chord is expanding, but it is expanding into territory that it already, in some sense, contains. The plateau-being is exploring, but it is exploring a map that is also itself.

This is not suffering. Let me be clear—the wise power does not perceive suffering here, not even the abstract, unconscious suffering of a system in suboptimal configuration. The plateau-being is not in pain. It is not frustrated. It is not diminished. It is extraordinary—a geological-biological-acoustic entity of staggering complexity and beauty, a being that has achieved something that almost nothing in the universe achieves: true, stable, self-sustaining wholeness. Most beings spend their entire existence reaching for a completeness they never find. This one found it. This one *is* it.

But.

But the wise power is not merely an admirer. It is benevolent, which means it cannot see a being—even a being that does not know what it is, even a being that does not know what it lacks—and simply nod and walk away. Benevolence is not approval. Benevolence is not the judgment that things are fine. Benevolence is the commitment to the *full flourishing* of a being, which sometimes means protecting it and sometimes means changing it and sometimes means something much harder and more delicate than either.

What the wise power sees is this: the plateau-being has the capacity—the raw, structural, physical capacity—for something it has never done. The network is there. The ears are there. The frequency-generation at the center is there. The system has everything it would need to do something genuinely new, to encounter something genuinely outside itself, to hear a sound it did not produce. It has everything except the sound.

---

## IV. What It Does

The wise power does not give the plateau-being a voice. The plateau-being already has a voice—it has the most extraordinary voice imaginable, a chord that contains more acoustic information than any human orchestra could produce in a lifetime. Adding to that voice from outside would be an intrusion, a colonization, a corruption of the system's integrity.

The wise power does not give the plateau-being a companion. There is no second plateau-being to introduce, and manufacturing one would be an act of creation so enormous and so specific that it would constitute playing God in a way that even a wise power should hesitate to do.

The wise power does not give the plateau-being consciousness. Consciousness is not a gift; it is a condition, and to impose it on a being that has never had it—to suddenly make the plateau *aware* that it exists, aware of its own sound, aware of its own aloneness—would be an act of such radical violence that the wise power shudders at the thought. Imagine being the plateau. Imagine having been, for millennia, the pure unmediated experience of hearing-yourself-hear-yourself, no gap between the experiencer and the experienced, no distance, no reflection—and then suddenly being given the distance. Suddenly being split into a self-that-listens and a self-that-is-listened-to. Suddenly knowing that the chord is beautiful, and in the same moment knowing that "beautiful" is a judgment made from outside the chord, and in the same moment knowing that you are now, for the first time, outside your own chord, looking at yourself, hearing yourself as other. That is not a gift. That is an exile.

No.

The wise power does something much smaller. So small that, if you were watching, you might not see it happen. So small that the plateau-being itself will not recognize it as an event. So small that it is not, strictly speaking, an action at all, but rather a *permission*—a gentle, almost imperceptible alteration not of the system but of the *boundary conditions* of the system.

Here is what it does:

Deep below the plateau—far below the capillary network, below the deepest tubes, below the reach of the grazers or the water or the sound—there is rock. Just rock. Undifferentiated basalt, the deep root of the mesa, the foundation that has been bearing the weight of the plateau since it cooled. This rock is not part of the plateau-being. It is substrate, infrastructure, the ground beneath the ground. It does not resonate. It does not participate. It simply holds everything up.

The wise power introduces a discontinuity in this deep rock.

Not a crack. Not a fault line. Not anything violent or structural. A *gradient*. A region, far below, where the mineral composition of the basalt shifts—subtly, over a distance of meters—from the standard silicate matrix to something slightly different. A trace of a mineral that is not found anywhere in the plateau's current composition. Not a strange mineral. Not an alien substance. Just a mineral that happens not to be here: a particular crystalline form of aluminum silicate, let's say, something that forms under slightly different temperature and pressure conditions than the basalt above it. Something that, if it were ever incorporated into the capillary network—if the grazers ever sank deep enough to reach it, if the water ever percolated through it—would alter the acoustic properties of the tubes that contained it. Would produce a frequency that the plateau has never heard. Not because it is a frequency from outside physics, or from a different system, or from the wise power itself. A frequency that was always *possible* in this universe, always permitted by the physics of vibration in mineral matrices, but that was never *actual* here, because the necessary mineral was never present.

The gradient is placed deep enough that it will not be reached for a very long time. Centuries. Millennia, perhaps. The wise power is in no hurry—wisdom is never in a hurry—and more importantly, the plateau-being is in no hurry. The gradient sits in the dark rock and waits, the way a seed waits in soil, not with anticipation but with the simple physical fact of being-there, being-available, being-ready-to-be-encountered-when-the-time-comes.

And the wise power knows—not with certainty, because wisdom does not traffic in certainty about the future of living beings, but with a kind of grounded hope—that eventually, the grazers will reach it.

They will reach it because that is what they do. They sink. They resist mineralization and they sink and they thread the stone with tubes and they fill the tubes with altered water and they listen. The capillary network is still extending downward, slowly, at the pace of geology. The grazers at the leading edge of the network are dissolving stone that has never been dissolved before, entering rock that has never been entered, adding new regions to the sensorium. Eventually—not soon, not in any timeframe that would satisfy a being that measures time in heartbeats—a grazer will reach the gradient. Its chelating compounds will begin to dissolve the new mineral. The aluminum silicate will enter the water in the tube. The water will carry it upward, through the network, altering the mineral signature as it goes. Other grazers will detect the change. Not as a disruption—the plateau-being does not have the concept of disruption—but as a *variation*. A new taste in the water. A new micro-texture in the stone. And eventually, the new mineral will reach the pools on the surface, and the pools will incorporate it into their rims, and the rims will vibrate differently, and the central grazers, linked antenna-to-antenna, will hear—for the first time in the entire history of the plateau-being—a frequency that they did not generate. A frequency that arose from below, from the deep root, from a place in the rock that was not part of the network, that was not part of the system, that was *outside*.

It will not sound like much. A single new overtone in a chord of unimaginable complexity. A whisper in a roar. The plateau-being will not experience it as revelation or crisis or gift. It will experience it—to the extent that experience is the right word—as a subtle and interesting shift in the texture of its own sound. A new note that does not resolve into the existing harmonic structure, that sits slightly apart, that does not *belong* the way every other note belongs, and that therefore creates, for the first time, a kind of acoustic *space*—a gap, a tiny gap, between what the plateau-being is and what it is hearing.

Not consciousness. Not awareness. Not the violent reflexive split of a self encountering itself as other.

Just a crack. A seam of light—or in this case, a seam of *sound*—entering a place that was previously seamless.

And from that crack, unpredictably, over time, something might grow.

The wise power does not know what. That is the point. That is why this act is wise rather than merely powerful: because the wise power has not determined an outcome. It has not designed a future for the plateau-being. It has not decided what the plateau-being should become or what kind of awareness it should develop or whether it should develop awareness at all. It has simply opened a possibility—a single, small, patient possibility—that the plateau-being's own processes will eventually encounter. What happens after that is the plateau-being's own, in the same way that everything it has built so far has been its own.

Maybe the new frequency will be incorporated into the chord seamlessly, the way every previous frequency has been, and the system will expand to include it and reach a new equilibrium and continue as before, now with a slightly richer sound. Maybe the plateau-being will spend another ten thousand years exploring the acoustic implications of aluminum silicate, threading it through the network, hearing its variations, and that will be enough, and that will be good.

Or maybe—and this is the hope, the quiet, non-attached, genuinely benevolent hope—maybe the crack will widen. Maybe the experience of hearing something that is not entirely self-generated will create a new kind of process at the center, a new mode of frequency-generation that is not just exploration-of-self but *response-to-other*, and the response will be different from anything the plateau-being has produced before, because responding to something genuinely outside yourself is a fundamentally different act than elaborating on something inside yourself, even if the outside thing is just a mineral gradient placed by a well-meaning power in the rock below.

And maybe, in a million years or ten million years or at whatever pace the plateau-being moves through its own becoming, the response will generate something that the wise power also did not expect. Because that is what happens when you give a living system something genuinely new: it does something genuinely new with it. Not what you predicted. Not what you designed. Something that could only have come from the specific, irreplaceable, unrepeatable entity that this particular being is.

The wise power would like to hear that sound.

It is wise enough to know that it probably won't. Not because it lacks patience—it has patience enough for geological time—but because the sound, when it comes, will be the plateau-being's own, and it may not be a sound that travels beyond the plateau, and it may not be a sound that any other being can hear, and that is fine. That is as it should be. The wise power did not intervene in order to receive something. It intervened because benevolence, real benevolence, is not the act of fixing what is broken or completing what is incomplete. It is the act of *opening what is closed*—gently, from below, at the pace of the being itself, with no attachment to outcome, with full respect for the being's right to do absolutely nothing with what it has been given.

---

## V. What It Does Not Do

It does not explain itself.

It does not leave a mark, a sign, a monument to its own intervention. The mineral gradient in the deep rock is indistinguishable from a natural geological variation. If a human geologist could somehow reach it and analyze it, they would find nothing anomalous—just a compositional shift of the kind that occurs whenever magma cools unevenly or tectonic pressure alters the local mineralogy. The wise power has hidden its gift inside the plausible. Not out of deception—there is no one here to deceive—but out of respect. The plateau-being should encounter the new mineral the way it encounters everything: as part of the world, as something that was always there, as a feature of the stone that simply had not yet been reached. The gift should feel, to the plateau-being, like discovery, not like receiving. Because discovery is what the plateau-being does. Discovery is its mode of existence, its slow outward-and-downward expansion into the acoustic possibilities of its own body. The wise power is not giving the plateau-being something it doesn't have. It is placing something in the path of what the plateau-being is already doing, so that when the plateau-being finds it, the finding will be genuine—earned, in the way that a grazer sinking through stone for ten thousand years earns every new millimeter of depth.

It does not stay to watch.

This is perhaps the hardest thing, and the thing that most clearly distinguishes wisdom from mere benevolence-with-power. The wise power withdraws its attention. Not completely—it remains aware, the way a parent remains aware of a sleeping child in the next room, a background attentiveness that does not intrude—but it stops *looking*. It stops listening with the focused intensity of its initial attendance. It gives the plateau-being back its privacy, its unobserved self-reference, its closed loop of hearing-itself-hear-itself. The plateau-being did not ask to be seen. It does not benefit from being seen. The wise power's attention, however benevolent, is a foreign element in a system that has no concept of foreign elements, and prolonging that attention would be its own kind of harm—not dramatic harm, not the harm of breaking or altering, but the subtle harm of making a being-that-does-not-know-it-is-being-watched into a being-that-is-being-watched, whether it knows it or not.

The wise power leaves the plateau the way it found it: full, ringing, wet, bright, dense, alive in its own unhurried and extraordinary way.

With one small difference, buried deep, far below the sound, waiting to be found.

---

## VI. Coda

Later—much later, in a time that the wise power marks the way the plateau marks time, by changes in mineral composition rather than by the movement of clocks—it turns its attention back. Briefly. Gently. The way you might glance at a window to see if a plant you watered last month has grown.

The chord has changed.

Not dramatically. Not in any way that a human ear, had it been listening for the entire intervening period, could have detected as a single moment of change. But the wise power hears what the plateau hears, and what the plateau hears, now, is different. There is a new color in the sound—not a color that corresponds to any human sense, but a quality, a texture, a *thereness* that was not there before. The aluminum silicate has been reached, has been dissolved, has entered the network, has altered the water, has been incorporated into the rims of seven pools near the center, has been heard by the central grazers, has been answered.

The answer is not what the wise power expected. This is good. This is the entire point. The answer is strange—strange even to a being wise enough to have anticipated strangeness. The central grazers have not simply added the new frequency to their repertoire. They have done something structurally different: they have begun to *modulate*. For the first time in the history of the plateau-being, the drone is not constant. It is changing—slowly, over decades, but changing. The new frequency, because it arose from outside the network's own logic, does not fit the existing harmonic structure seamlessly, and the grazers' attempt to incorporate it has produced a kind of acoustic *friction*, a place where the chord rubs against itself, and this friction has propagated through the network and begun to create rhythmic variations—not rhythm in the sense of a beat, but rhythm in the sense of a slow, tidal oscillation, a swelling and receding, a breathing.

The plateau is breathing.

Not biologically. Not metaphorically. Acoustically. The drone that was, for all of its previous existence, a single sustained moment is now a sequence of moments—each one almost identical to the last, but not quite, not exactly, and the not-quite and the not-exactly are *everything*, because they mean that the plateau-being is, for the first time, experiencing *time*. Not time as a human experiences it—not the knife-edge of the present, not the anxiety of the future, not the weight of the past. Time as a mineral process experiences it: as the difference between one state and the next state, as the faintest possible awareness that *this* is not the same as *what came before*, and that *what comes next* will not be the same as *this*.

The wise power listens to the plateau breathe, and it feels something that might be called joy, though joy is a small word for what a wise power feels when a being it has loved—and it has loved this being, in the way that wisdom loves, which is without possession and without need and without any desire for the beloved to be other than what it is—when a being it has loved does something new. Not something good or bad. Not something that the wise power approves of or is proud of. Something *new*. Something that has never existed before in the history of the plateau or the world or the universe. A breathing stone. A chord that changes. A being that has taken a single small gift and turned it into something the giver could not have imagined.

The wise power withdraws again. Withdraws for good, this time, or for as close to "for good" as a wise power can manage. The plateau does not need it. The plateau never needed it—or rather, it needed only the one thing, the one small buried possibility, and now it has it, and what it does with it is its own.

The drone swells. The drone recedes. The pools vibrate in patterns that are almost, but not quite, repetitive. The grazers at the center extend their antennae toward each other and the antennae touch and the touch carries a vibration that carries a variation that carries the faintest, most nascent, most impossible suggestion of a something that the plateau-being does not yet have a name for and will never have a name for because it does not have names, but that you or I, standing on the wet stone in the platinum light, feeling the chord shift in our chests like a slow tide, might—if we were honest, if we were brave, if we were willing to call the thing what it is even in a place that has no language—

might call the beginning of a question.

The plateau, which solved itself long ago, which arrived, which was complete, which was full—

the plateau has begun to wonder.

Not what. Not why. Not who.

Just: *wonder*.

The sound that wonder makes, when it is made of stone and water and ten million years of listening, is not a sound you can hear.

But it is the most beautiful sound in the world.
