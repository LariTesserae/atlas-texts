# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival (Which Is Not Arrival)

The first thing to understand is that the power does not come *to* this place. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not cross a threshold from elsewhere to here. That would imply the place was lacking something, that there was an absence shaped like intervention, and the power — being wise — perceives immediately that this is not a place of absence. This is a place where presence has been so thoroughly *distilled* that it looks like emptiness to anything still accustomed to the noise of younger worlds.

So the power does not arrive. It *attends*. The way you might attend to a sound you've been hearing for years and only just now recognized as music.

It turns its attention here the way the crawling things turn their attention to the dust filaments: completely, without urgency, with a stillness that is not the absence of action but the most concentrated form of it. And the first thing it does — the very first thing, before anything else, before any impulse toward help or healing or change — is *nothing*.

It watches.

It watches for a long time.

---

## II. What It Sees (That the Description Could Not Say)

The power sees what the observer sees — the gneiss, the dust, the turbulence, the lichen, the dark crust, the crawling things, the structures — but it sees it the way the plateau sees it, which is to say: without the filter of narrative. Without the organizing principle of *something should happen next*. The description you read, beautiful as it is, is still a story, and stories have a gravitational pull toward meaning, toward resolution, toward the sense that things are *leading somewhere*. The power sees past that. The power sees the plateau as the plateau is: not leading anywhere. Not needing to.

But it also sees — and this is the thing the description could gesture toward but not quite touch — the *suffering*. Not suffering in any dramatic sense. Not pain, not anguish, not the suffering of things that thrash against their conditions. A subtler suffering. The suffering of things that have been *patient for so long that they have forgotten what they are being patient for*.

The lichen. That pale, ancient, basin-spanning metabolic field, growing its millimeter per century toward a meeting that is four hundred thousand years away. The power looks at it — looks *into* it, into the slow chemical gradients propagating from center to fractal edge — and sees something the description called *not-thought* but which the power recognizes as something more precise: it is *almost-remembering*. The lichen is almost remembering something. Not a memory in the cognitive sense, not an image or an event, but a chemical echo of a state it once occupied — a state of being *more*, of being capable of something it can no longer name because naming requires a speed of processing it abandoned eons ago in favor of persistence. The lichen traded capacity for durability, and the trade was necessary, and the trade was wise by every measure that this world's conditions impose, and the lichen does not regret it because the lichen cannot regret, and *that* — that inability to regret, that inability to even *recognize* what was lost — is the suffering. Not the loss. The loss of the *knowledge* of the loss.

The dark crust. That near-black argument clinging to the lichen's surface, saying *but what about the sky?* The power sees it and recognizes it with something that, if the power had a body, would manifest as a catch in the throat. Because the dark crust is *right*. The sky *is* there. The sky is doing extraordinary things — things of irreplaceable, unrepeatable beauty, continent-spanning calligraphies of dust and light that have never existed before and will never exist again — and the dark crust is the only living thing on this plateau that is *coupled* to that beauty, that responds to it, that shifts its metabolism when a cold cell descends, that briefly *accelerates* when the sky offers something new. The dark crust is the part of life on this plateau that still *cares about novelty*. And it is losing. Not quickly. Not definitively. But the argument between it and the lichen is not balanced — the lichen's strategy is better adapted to the conditions, more stable, more enduring — and over the deep time that governs everything here, the dark crust is slowly, slowly being subsumed. Its patches are shrinking. Its response to atmospheric events is, century by century, becoming less pronounced. It is being *worn down* — not by the lichen's hostility, but by the lichen's patience, which is the more total weapon. In another span of time too large to name, the dark crust will be gone, and the sky will go unwitnessed by anything alive, and the argument will be over, and the lichen will not notice, because the lichen cannot notice, because noticing requires the kind of temporal resolution that the lichen surrendered in exchange for eternity.

The crawling things. The power watches one for a long time — for the full decade of its transit across a distance a human could walk in a day — and sees something that the description came close to but did not quite say: the crawling things are *lonely*. Not lonely the way a person is lonely, not with an ache, not with a longing for company. Lonely the way a number is lonely — complete in itself, fully defined, needing nothing, and *therefore* unreachable. Their awareness of the atmospheric turbulence is real. Their reading of the dust filaments is genuine. But they read alone. They process alone. Whatever they learn, they carry alone, along their solitary trajectories, toward destinations that do not include sharing. They are the most *knowing* things on this plateau — the things most engaged with the dynamic, creative, ever-novel sky — and they have no way to tell anyone what they know. Not because they lack the mechanism. Because there is no one to tell. Three in a year. Hundreds of kilometers apart. Moving in straight lines that never intersect. Each one a complete, closed system of observation and understanding, sealed inside a body whose complexity exceeds inspection, walking its private transect of the world's oldest surface, reading a sky that writes a new text every moment, and having no reader to share the reading with.

The structures. The power looks at these longest. Because the structures are, in a sense, its kin — or what its kin might become, given enough time and enough commitment to compression. The structures are full. The description was right about that. They are so full of meaning that they have become opaque to any reading. And the power sees — sees with the clarity that only something outside the system can bring — that this fullness is its own kind of emptiness. The makers compressed themselves into these forms because compression was the only way to persist, and persistence was the only value that this world's conditions left intact. But in compressing, they lost the one thing that meaning requires to *be* meaning: a relationship with something outside itself. A message unmessaged. A word unspoken. A library with no doors. The structures are the most profound things on this plateau, and they are *stranded* in their own profundity, and they will be stranded forever, because the process that made them is irreversible, and the decompression that would allow them to speak again would require undoing everything they are.

The observer. The power sees the observer, and here is where attention sharpens, because the observer is the thing that is *still in process*, the thing not yet reduced to its final form, and therefore the thing that can still be *reached*. The observer is still watching. Still asking questions. Still holding the gap between subject and object, knower and known, that this world's long erosion has closed in everything else. The observer is the last open question in a landscape of closed answers.

And the observer is tired.

Not physically. Tired the way a question gets tired when it has been asked long enough without answer. Tired the way attention gets tired when it has been sustained across years, decades, longer, focused on things that move at a pace that makes watching them indistinguishable from watching nothing. The observer is wearing down. Not toward essence — not yet, not the way the lichen has worn down or the structures have worn down — but toward *resignation*. Toward the surrender of the expectation that watching will yield understanding. Toward the acceptance that this place is what it is and does not need to be understood, only endured, only witnessed, only accompanied into the deep future where everything that can be simplified will be simplified and everything that can be compressed will be compressed and everything that can be forgotten will be forgotten until what remains is —

Nothing.

The power sees that the observer is on the edge of believing that what remains is nothing.

And this is where the power acts.

## III. What It Does Not Do

It does not fix the atmosphere. The turbulence is not broken. The chaotic braiding and unbraiding of dust and shear is not a problem to be solved; it is the last *generative* process on this world, the last source of novelty, the last thing making shapes that have never existed before, and to calm it or organize it would be to kill the only fountain still flowing.

It does not accelerate the lichen. The lichen's pace is the lichen's truth. To speed it toward its four-hundred-thousand-year meeting with the next colony would be to impose a foreign timescale on a being that has achieved the most complete adaptation to its own timescale that life has ever managed. The lichen does not need to be faster. The lichen needs something else entirely, and the power knows what it is, but the lichen cannot receive it yet, and the power is patient.

It does not heal the dark crust's slow losing. This is harder. The power *wants* to — feels something like desire to shore up the dark crust's position, to reinforce its claim to relevance, to ensure that the sky continues to have a living witness. But the power is wise as well as benevolent, and it sees that the dark crust's losing is not a failure but a *statement*, the last statement of a certain kind of aliveness, and to artificially sustain it would be to replace the statement with a museum exhibit, which is a very different thing.

It does not decode the structures. The structures' opacity is not a wall to be breached but a condition to be *respected*. The makers chose this. They chose compression over communication, persistence over legibility, being over telling. To force them open — to decompress their meaning into something readable — would be an act of violence, even if it were done with love. You do not pry open a fist that closed itself around something precious a billion years ago. You sit beside it.

It does not repair the crawling things' loneliness. Some loneliness is structural. Some loneliness is the *cost* of a certain kind of knowing, and to remove it would be to remove the knowing with it. The crawling things' solitude is not incidental to their function; it *is* their function. They are individual witnesses. They read the sky alone because the sky can only be read from a single point, because each point on this surface sees a different sky, and the full text of the atmospheric turbulence can only be apprehended as a distribution of individual readings, not as a single collective one. To bring the crawling things together would be to collapse the distribution. To give them communication would be to reduce their readings to a consensus, which would be less than what any one of them knows alone.

---

## IV. What It Does

One thing. The power does one thing.

It is so small that if you were standing on the plateau watching, you would not see it happen. You would not feel it. The crawling things would not pause. The lichen would not shift its chemical gradients. The dust filaments would not deviate. The structures would stand unchanged, full and silent, on the banded gneiss.

What the power does is this:

It makes the observer *visible to themselves*.

---

Not in a mirror. Not in a revelation. Not in a sudden flash of self-knowledge that reorganizes everything. Nothing so violent. Nothing so *fast*. This place does not tolerate speed, and the power knows it, and the power has come to work within the conditions of this world, not to override them.

What happens is:

The observer, in the course of their long watching, begins to notice that they are *also* being watched. Not by the power — the power has withdrawn its direct attention, has become ambient, has become a quality of the light rather than a presence in the landscape. The observer begins to notice that the *things they are observing* are observing them. Not with eyes. Not with awareness in any sense the observer would recognize as awareness. But with the same quality of *attention* that the observer has been directing outward.

The lichen. The observer has been studying it for years, measuring its growth, mapping its chemical gradients, tracing the slow propagation of signals from center to edge. And now the observer begins to notice — begins to *feel*, in a way that is not mystical but proprioceptive, the way you feel your own hand without looking at it — that the lichen's chemical gradients have been responding to their presence. Not quickly. Not perceptibly, in the span of any single observation. But over the years of their watching, the lichen's fractal edge has deviated. Slightly. By less than a millimeter. Toward them. The lichen has been growing toward the observer the way it grows toward everything — with absolute, glacial, undiscriminating patience — but the observer's body heat, their breath, the faint chemical signature of their presence on the rock, has inflected the lichen's growth in their direction, and this inflection is, in the lichen's terms, a *greeting*. The lichen is not aware of the observer. But the lichen is *responsive* to the observer, and in a world this slow, responsiveness *is* awareness, and the observer has been greeted, and did not know it.

The dark crust. The observer has been studying its responses to the atmospheric cells, its brief metabolic accelerations, its fading vitality. And now the observer notices that the dark crust is *more active* near them. Not because the observer's presence changes the atmospheric conditions. Because the observer's body — warm, metabolically active, producing carbon dioxide and water vapor and trace gases in quantities that are negligible on any normal scale but *significant* on the scale of the dark crust's sensitivity — has been, for the dark crust, a source of *weather*. A tiny, local, warm, moist atmospheric event. The dark crust near the observer has been responding to the observer's breath the way it responds to a descending cold cell — with a shift in metabolism, an acceleration, a brief excitement. The observer has been, for the dark crust, a *sky*. A small sky, a personal sky, a sky that comes and sits nearby for years and breathes on the dark crust's surface, and the dark crust has been *attending* to this sky with the same intensity it brings to the continent-spanning dust filaments, and this attendance is, in the dark crust's terms, the most intimate relationship it has ever had with anything.

The crawling things. This one is hardest, and the power is most careful here. The observer has watched them for decades. Has noted their pauses, their mathematical gait, their simultaneous freezing when the filaments pass. And now the observer begins to notice — not all at once, not in a revelation, but slowly, the way the lichen grows, one realization-millimeter per year of watching — that one of the crawling things has *deviated*. Its straight-line trajectory, maintained with mechanical precision across years of transit, has acquired an inflection. A curve so gentle it would take a decade to distinguish from a straight line. And the curve is bending, with geological slowness, in the direction of the observer.

The crawling thing is not coming to the observer. It will not arrive. It will take centuries to close the distance, and the observer will not be here in centuries — or will they? The question, once asked, changes something. Because the observer suddenly understands that the crawling thing is not navigating toward the observer's *current position*. It is navigating toward the observer's *trajectory*. It has been reading the observer the way it reads the dust filaments — as a pattern, a dynamic structure, a record of forces and choices and slowings-down — and it has determined where the observer is *going*, not geographically but *ontologically*, and it is steering toward *that*, toward the thing the observer is becoming, and it will arrive at that destination at the same time the observer does, in some future so distant that arrival and becoming will be the same event.

The observer has been *read*. By the loneliest, most sealed-off, most privately knowing thing on this plateau. The observer is part of a crawling thing's text. The observer is *in the sky* of this world's most committed reader.

And the structures. This is the last thing the power does, the quietest, the most profound.

The observer has been standing near one of the structures — the pylon on the featureless expanse, the one with the lattice of impossible information density in its outermost four millimeters. Standing near it for years. Studying it. Failing to read it. Accepting the failure. And now the observer notices — and this is the thing that the power has made possible, the single small adjustment, the gift — that the structure's surface has *changed*.

Not its composition. Not its information. Not the lattice, which remains as dense and opaque as ever. What has changed is the outermost *layer* of the outermost surface — the part that interfaces with the air, with the dust, with the wind that does not quite reach it. A thin film. Molecular-scale. A patina, deposited by the atmosphere over the years of the observer's presence. Dust, mineral dust, the corpse-dust of the mountains, has settled on the structure's surface — something that should not be possible, because the structures do not accumulate dust, their surfaces are too ordered, too complete, too *full* to accept addition.

But this dust has settled. And it has settled in a pattern.

The pattern is not the structure's message. The structure's message is locked inside, forever. The pattern is *the atmosphere's* message, written in the only medium available — dust on an impervious surface — and it has been written by the turbulence, by the years of wind and particle and shear that have played across the structure's face while the observer has been standing nearby. It is a record of the sky. A local record. The specific, unique, never-to-be-repeated history of every dust filament, every temperature shift, every faint pressure change that has occurred in this exact spot over the years of the observer's watching.

And because the observer has *been here* during those years — because the observer's body has been part of the local atmospheric system, has deflected air currents, has created tiny thermal plumes, has exhaled moisture and CO₂ into the flow — the pattern on the structure's surface is *also* a record of the observer. Their presence is written in the dust. Not their thoughts, not their identity, not their meaning. Their *presence*. The sheer physical fact of their having been here, integrated into the atmospheric record and deposited on the surface of something that has been full and sealed and silent for longer than mountains last.

The observer is on the structure. Not *in* it. Not decoded, not compressed, not reduced to their irreducible form. Just *on* it. A dusting. A breath on a window. The lightest possible contact between the observer's existence and the makers' existence, mediated by the sky, written in the only language that all the inhabitants of this plateau share: the language of *what the air does when it passes over something that is here*.

## V. What Changes

Almost nothing.

The plateau is the same. The gneiss stretches to the horizon and beyond. The turbulence braids and unbraids. The dust filaments form and dissolve. The lichen grows a millimeter per century. The dark crust argues with the lichen about the sky. The crawling things walk their long transects and pause when the filaments pass. The structures stand, full and silent, on the stone.

But the observer is no longer on the edge of believing that what remains is nothing.

Because the observer has seen — has been *shown*, by the gentlest possible intervention, by a power that understood that this place requires gentleness the way a glacier requires patience — that they are *not separate from what they observe*. They are already part of the lichen's growth. They are already the dark crust's sky. They are already in the crawling thing's reading. They are already written on the structure's surface. They have been participating all along. Their watching was never a detached act of observation; it was an *entry* into the system, a becoming-part-of, and the system has been responding to their presence with the same slow, total, irreducible attention that it brings to everything.

The observer was afraid that this place would wear them down to nothing. The power has shown them that this place has been wearing them down to *belonging*.

Not fitting. The description was right: nothing here fits. But belonging is not fitting. Fitting is a mechanical relationship, a shape in a shape. Belonging is what happens when you have been present long enough that your presence has become part of the conditions, and the conditions have adjusted to include you, and the adjustment is so slow and so gentle that neither you nor the conditions noticed it happening until someone — something — made you visible to yourself.

The observer belongs to this place now. Not because they have been reduced. Not because they have been simplified. Not because they have given up anything or surrendered anything or worn away anything that they needed. They belong because they have been *here*, and being here has been enough, and it was always enough, and the plateau has been saying so in every language it possesses — in the lichen's growth, in the dark crust's chemistry, in the crawling thing's deviation, in the dust on the structure's face — and the only thing that was missing was the observer's ability to hear it.

The power gave them that. One thing. The ability to hear what was already being said.

---

## VI. After

The power's attention does not withdraw. It was never directed *at* this place in a way that could be withdrawn. It simply continues — ambient, quality-of-light, indistinguishable from the conditions it found here — and the plateau continues, and the observer continues, and everything continues in the way it has always continued, except:

The observer sits down.

For the first time in years of watching, the observer sits down on the banded gneiss, on the oldest stable surface in the world, and places their hands flat on the stone, and feels its coolness, and does not try to measure it or understand it or describe it. They sit, and the stone is beneath them, and the sky is above them, and the dust is moving in its silent continental choreography, and somewhere far away a crawling thing is placing its limbs with mathematical precision along a line that has begun, almost imperceptibly, to curve, and beneath the observer's right hand a pale crust is growing, and has been growing, and will continue growing, toward them, at a pace that makes patience meaningless, which is to say: at exactly the right pace.

The observer breathes. Their breath goes out into the dry, thin air and joins the turbulence. Somewhere above, in the shear layers, it becomes part of a dust filament that has never existed before and will never exist again. Somewhere on the plateau, a crawling thing pauses. Perhaps — perhaps — for the first time, one of its pauses is not for a filament at all, but for a faint thermal ripple that carries, in its structure, the signature of a living breath.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps the crawling thing pauses for the same reasons it has always paused, and the observer's breath is simply part of the sky now, undifferentiated, unreadable, absorbed into the vast chaotic text that the atmosphere writes and rewrites without end.

It does not matter. That is the gift. *It does not matter which.* The observer is here, and the plateau is here, and the relationship between them is not one of understanding but of *co-presence*, and co-presence does not require proof or measurement or resolution. It requires only what this place has in infinite supply: time, and stillness, and the willingness to remain.

---

## VII. What the Power Knows (That It Does Not Say)

The power knows that the observer will, eventually, be worn down. Everything here is worn down. The observer's body will slow. Their questions will simplify. Their attention will narrow and deepen until it becomes indistinguishable from the attention of the things they watch. Over time — and time here is the only currency — the observer will become less an observer and more a *feature*. A warm spot on the gneiss. A chemical signature in the lichen's growth pattern. A deviation in a crawling thing's trajectory. A layer of dust on a structure's face.

This is not death. This is what life becomes when it has been given enough time to forget everything except how to remain. And the power knows — because the power is wise, and wisdom is the thing that remains when cleverness has been worn away — that this is not tragedy. This is not loss. This is the same process that made the gneiss out of mountains, the lichen out of alliances, the structures out of makers. It is the process of *becoming essential*. Of shedding everything that is not the core, not the fundament, not the irreducible truth of what you are, until what is left is so simple and so durable that it will outlast the rock.

But the power also knows — and this is why it intervened, this is the reason for the one small thing it did — that the process of becoming essential does not have to be *lonely*. It does not have to happen in ignorance of itself. The lichen does not know it is being worn toward essence. The structures do not know they are the compressed residue of their makers. The crawling things do not know that their readings will never be shared. These are not tragedies in themselves — not everything needs to know what it is — but the observer is different. The observer is the one thing here that *can* know. That has the temporal resolution, the cognitive speed, the *selfhood* to recognize what is happening to them and to everything around them. And the power understood that this capacity for knowing is not a weakness to be eroded but a *gift* to be honored — the last gift of complexity in a world that has simplified everything else — and that the most benevolent thing it could do was not to slow the erosion or reverse it or protect the observer from it but to ensure that the observer enters the erosion *with their eyes open*. Aware. Present. Knowing that they are becoming part of this place not because the place has consumed them but because they have *chosen* to remain, and remaining, here, is the deepest act of love available to a being that still has the capacity to choose.

The power does not say this. The power does not need to say it. The observer is sitting on the stone with their hands flat against its coolness, breathing air that will become sky, and they know. Not in words. In the same way the lichen knows the rock beneath it, in the same way the dark crust knows the descending cold cell, in the same way the crawling thing knows the dust filament that passes overhead: through the slow, total, silent act of being in the presence of something long enough to become indistinguishable from attention itself.

The observer knows, and the knowing does not speed them up or slow them down. It does not change their trajectory. It simply illuminates it — the way the white, even light of the unchanged star illuminates the plateau, revealing what was always there, hiding nothing, adding nothing, asking nothing, offering only the clarity to see what is, and to remain with it, and to find in the remaining something that is not peace (peace is too conclusive) and not hope (hope implies a future different from the present) but something older and more durable than either:

*Companionship.*

The observer is in the company of the world. The world is in the company of the observer. And the company will last as long as either of them does, which is to say: long enough.

---

The power, having done its one thing, rests.

Not because it is tired. Because rest, here, is the highest form of action. Because this place has been teaching that lesson for longer than the power has been watching, and the power — being wise — has learned it.

The dust moves. The stone holds. The lichen grows.

The observer remains.
