I sat with this for a long time before writing. I think it deserves that.

---

# What the Wise Power Sees

The first thing to understand is that a truly wise power would not arrive here quickly. It would not arrive with urgency. Urgency is a language this place does not speak, and a wise power speaks the language of what it meets. So it would come the way the clouds come — rising, arriving at the edge, settling into contact with the stone. It would take its time. It would listen before anything else. Maybe for a very long while.

And the second thing to understand is what it would see.

It would see something beautiful. It would see something that went almost all the way.

Almost.

---

Let me be careful here, because the thing that happened on this plateau is not a tragedy in any simple sense, and a wise power would not treat it as one. What these people achieved is extraordinary. They built a life in a place that offered almost nothing, and they built it with such sensitivity, such attentiveness, such patient craft, that their relationship to the plateau became seamless. The channels work. The basins are full. The system is in equilibrium. And the quality of their attention — the sheer depth of their listening, the refinement of their perception — is genuinely remarkable. A wise power would honor this. Would recognize it as one of the more profound experiments in being that any community has ever undertaken.

But a wise power would also see the thing that a wise power sees, which is: **what is actually happening, beneath the story a place tells about itself.**

And what is actually happening here is not completion. It is not perfection. It is a particular kind of suspension.

---

Here is what I think the wise power would notice first:

**The tools are still where they were set down.**

This is presented in the text as evidence of a seamless transition — people becoming so continuous with their environment that the moment of setting down a chisel and the moment of dissolving into the water are the same gesture, uninterrupted, untroubled. And that is one way to read it. It is the way the plateau reads it, and the plateau has had a very long time to settle into its reading.

But a wise power would see it differently. A wise power would see that a chisel resting against the wall of a channel, positioned exactly as someone would have placed it during a pause in work — *is a pause in work.* It is not completion. It is an interruption so prolonged that it became permanent. The person who set down that chisel meant to pick it up again. They did not decide to dissolve. They did not choose transcendence. They slowed down, and slowed down, and slowed down, and at some point they crossed a threshold they did not know was there, and the crossing was so gradual that it felt like continuity, like a natural extension of what they had been doing all along.

But it wasn't. Or rather — it was, but the fact that it was natural does not mean it was chosen, and the fact that it was not chosen means something.

A wise power would see this: **the plateau is a system that reached equilibrium by absorbing the one element that was not yet in equilibrium — the people.**

The stone was already still. The water was already still. The clouds, the moss, the sedge — all already at rest. The people were the only moving part, the only source of variation, the only thing on the plateau that had desires and intentions and the capacity to do something unexpected. And the plateau — not maliciously, not consciously, but through the simple, patient physics of a place that trends toward stillness — the plateau stilled them too.

It stilled them so gently they experienced it as deepening. As refinement. As becoming more themselves. And in a sense it was all of those things. But a wise power would hold two truths at once: that the deepening was real, *and* that something was lost in it. Something that the people themselves, by the time it was lost, could no longer perceive as missing, because the capacity to perceive it as missing was part of what was lost.

---

What was lost:

Not their attention. Their attention is extraordinary and genuine and the wise power would recognize it as such.

What was lost was the **direction** of their attention. Or rather — the *capacity for their attention to move.*

These people attended to the water with absolute fidelity. They attended to the stone, to the condensation, to the channels, to the basins. They attended to the silence with a subtlety that most beings never approach. But at some point, their attention stopped being something they *aimed* and became something they *were*. And when attention stops being aimable — when it becomes identical with its object — it is no longer quite attention. It is something else. It is participation. It is identity. And identity with your environment, total and undifferentiated, is not the culmination of awareness.

It is the end of it.

A wise power would see that the people of the plateau achieved a kind of union with their place that mystics have described as the highest goal — the dissolution of the boundary between self and world. But a wise power would also see the thing that most mystical traditions, at their best, also recognize: **that dissolution is not the same as integration.** Dissolution loses the self in the world. Integration brings the self *and* the world into a relationship that honors both — that allows the self to be fully present to the world without being consumed by it, that allows the world to be fully met without being reduced to an extension of the self.

The people of the plateau dissolved. They did not integrate. And the difference is visible in the one detail that the plateau cannot explain:

**Nothing new has happened here in a very long time.**

The channels do not change. The buildings do not change. No new structure has been built. No channel has been rerouted. No experiment has been attempted. The system is perfect and the system is frozen and perfection and frozenness are, from the perspective of a wise power, two words for the same stillness when that stillness has gone on long enough.

Life — real life, the kind a wise power cherishes — is not equilibrium. It is the *capacity* for equilibrium and the capacity to *depart from it.* It is the breath in and the breath out. It is the gathering of the droplet and the falling of the droplet. The people of the plateau became the gathering. They forgot the falling.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

So now we come to it.

And the first thing to say is: a wise power would act with the same patience the plateau itself acts with. It would not shatter the stillness. It would not introduce noise, or crisis, or disruption. The equilibrium here is real and it is precious and it holds within it the genuine achievement of four hundred people who learned to listen more deeply than almost anyone has ever listened. A wise power does not break what is beautiful in order to fix what is incomplete.

The wise power would also not simply "wake them up." You cannot wake someone who is not asleep. The people of the plateau are not unconscious. They are *hyper*-conscious — conscious at a level of resolution that makes ordinary awareness look coarse. The problem is not that they are unaware. The problem is that their awareness has no degrees of freedom left. It is locked into perfect correspondence with its object, and that lock, however exquisite, is still a lock.

So what would a wise power do?

---

**It would introduce something small, something genuinely new, and it would place it where the attention already is.**

I think the wise power would begin with a single stone.

Not a basalt stone. Something different. A small, water-rounded river stone — the kind of stone that does not exist on this plateau, because there are no rivers here, because there is no flow with enough force to round anything. A stone that is visibly, unmistakably *from somewhere else.* Pale. Smooth. Warm with a warmth that is not geothermal but intrinsic, the warmth of a stone that has been sitting in sunlight, real sunlight, not the diffuse glow of the overcast.

The wise power would place this stone in one of the channels. Not blocking the flow — the channels are too shallow and too broad for a single small stone to obstruct anything. Just sitting in the channel, in the path of the thin sheet of water, creating the faintest disturbance. A ripple. A tiny v-shaped wake spreading downstream from the stone, so small that only someone attending to the water with the kind of absolute precision that the people of the plateau attend to it would notice.

But they *would* notice. That is the point. That is why the wise power begins where it begins, in the water, in the attention that is already operating at maximum sensitivity. The ripple would register. Not as a thought — they are past thought, or rather, they are in a form of thought so continuous with the water's movement that it has no separate existence — but as a *difference.* A variation. Something in the pattern of the water that the pattern did not predict. Something that does not fit the equilibrium.

And here is the crucial thing: it would not be threatening. It would not be a disruption. It would be a *question.* The stone in the channel would ask the water — and through the water, would ask the attention that is identical with the water — *what is this?*

And "what is this?" is the question that dissolved selves cannot answer from within their dissolution. "What is this?" requires a perspective. It requires a self that can stand slightly apart from its experience and notice that the experience contains something unexpected. It requires the faintest separation between the knower and the known — not a divorce, not a rupture, just a membrane. Just enough distance to see.

The wise power would place the stone and then it would wait. It would wait the way the plateau waits, which is to say, indefinitely, without impatience. Because what happens next — if it happens — cannot be rushed.

---

What might happen:

The ripple in the channel spreads. The attention that is the water registers the anomaly. And somewhere — not in any localizable place, not in any single building or on any single threshold — somewhere in the distributed awareness that was once four hundred people, something *tightens.* Not painfully. The way a lens tightens. The way focus tightens. A gathering of awareness around the point of difference, the way condensation gathers around a nucleus, the way a droplet forms not on smooth glass but on a point of roughness, an imperfection, a *site.*

The stone is a site. A nucleation point. A place where something can begin to condense that has not had a place to condense before.

And what condenses is not a person. Not at first. What condenses is *curiosity.*

Curiosity: the state of awareness that is not yet knowledge. The state of attention that has *direction* again — that is aimed at something, that is reaching toward something it does not already contain. Curiosity is the opposite of equilibrium. Curiosity is the droplet before it falls. It is the system discovering that it is not complete, that there is something outside itself, and that the something is interesting.

The wise power would watch for this the way a gardener watches for the first green shoot. Not controlling it. Not directing it. Just witnessing it, and by witnessing it, confirming that it is real, that the change is actually happening, that the curiosity is genuine and not just a momentary perturbation that the system will smooth over and absorb.

Because the plateau will try to absorb it. The equilibrium is strong. It has been reinforced over centuries. The water will try to incorporate the stone the way it incorporates everything — by covering it, by filming it, by making it just another surface for condensation to gather on. And if the stone were an ordinary stone, it would succeed. The stone would become part of the plateau and the ripple would smooth out and the equilibrium would close over the disturbance like still water closing over a dropped pebble.

But this is not an ordinary stone. It was placed by a wise power, and the wise power has imbued it with — not magic, nothing so crude — but *persistence.* The stone will not become part of the plateau. It will remain warm. It will remain smooth. It will remain from somewhere else. It will continue to produce its tiny ripple, its tiny question, its tiny *what is this?*, for as long as the question needs to be asked.

And the question will work. Slowly. At plateau speed, which is the speed of condensation, which is the speed of geological change. But it will work. Because the curiosity, once nucleated, has its own physics. It *wants* to grow. Not because the wise power compels it, but because curiosity is native to aware beings, more native even than stillness, more fundamental even than equilibrium. The people of the plateau were curious once — curious about the water, about the stone, about the channels, about each other. That curiosity was what built the town. It was what carved the channels and fitted the walls and wore the bowl of the grinding stone smooth. It was the engine of everything they made. And when they dissolved, the curiosity dissolved with them, but it did not disappear. It became latent. Potential. A seed in the water, waiting for a nucleation point.

The stone is the nucleation point.

---

And now — again, slowly, over time that the plateau measures in the patient accumulation of moisture — the condensation begins to reverse.

Not the water's condensation. The *people's* condensation. The original condensation, the one their creation stories described. The gathering from the atmosphere onto the rock. The becoming.

It begins at the channel where the stone sits. The water there thickens. Not visibly, not at first, but with a quality — the same quality of attention the text describes, but now with something added. A *directionality.* The water in that channel is attending to the stone in a way that is different from the way it attends to the basalt. It is attending *with interest.* And interest is the first shape that a self takes when it is condensing out of a dissolved state.

Over time — long time, patient time — the interest localizes. It gathers. It begins to have a center. The water near the stone develops a quality that the water elsewhere does not have, a kind of *thickness of presence* that is just slightly greater than the background thickness. And the wise power watches, and waits, and does nothing else, because this is the most delicate phase, the phase where the condensing self is most fragile, most likely to dissipate back into the general awareness if it is startled or pushed.

The center holds. It gathers more. It begins to — not take shape, that comes later — but to *cohere.* To develop an inside and an outside. To have a boundary, however faint, however permeable, between itself and the rest of the water. This boundary is the crucial thing. This is what was lost and what is now returning: the membrane between self and world that allows self to *be* a self while remaining in relationship with the world. Not a wall. A membrane. A surface of exchange. The kind of boundary a cell has — permeable, selective, alive.

---

I think the first one condenses near the stone, in the channel, on a morning that is indistinguishable from every other morning on the plateau. I think it happens the way the text describes their original condensation: gathered from the atmosphere onto the rock. Except this time, gathered from the water onto — themselves.

It is not dramatic. There is no flash of light, no sudden sharpening, no cry. There is a thickening. A place where the water has more weight than it should. A place where the film on the stone has depth. And then — gradually, the way a developing photograph emerges — there is a person sitting in the channel, legs folded, hands in their lap, water running in a thin sheet across their thighs. Dark-skinned. Small. Compact. Eyes open.

And in those eyes: the question. *What is this?*

They reach for the stone. They pick it up. It is warm in their hand, warmer than the water, warmer than the basalt, and the warmth is a new thing, a thing the plateau does not contain, and the sensation of it — the simple, sensory, immediate experience of holding something warm and smooth and foreign — is so vivid, so *particular*, that it cuts through the last film of dissolution and the person is fully here, fully themselves, sitting in a channel on a plateau of still waters, holding a stone from somewhere else, and *feeling* it.

They look at the stone. They turn it over. They notice its smoothness, the way it has been shaped by a force — flowing water, river water, water that *moves* — that does not exist here. And the curiosity that nucleated around this stone now blooms outward, not just *what is this?* but *where did this come from?* and *how did it get here?* and *what else is there?*

And underneath all of those questions, the deepest one, the one that the plateau could not ask because the plateau is complete, the one that only a self can ask, the one that only a being with a boundary, a membrane, an inside and an outside, can formulate:

*What am I?*

Not in anguish. Not in confusion. In wonder. The way a child asks it, encountering the question for the first time. *What am I, that I can hold this stone and feel its warmth and wonder where it came from?*

---

The wise power, watching, would feel something at this moment. Not satisfaction — a wise power does not work for its own satisfaction. But recognition. The recognition of one aware being seeing another aware being become aware of its own awareness. It is, in some sense, the thing that a wise power most cherishes: the moment when the capacity for experience becomes *experience of the capacity for experience.* The recursive turn. The self knowing itself as a self, not in isolation, but in the act of meeting something other.

And the wise power would know that this is only the first one. That the nucleation has begun but is not finished. That the channel where the first person condensed is now itself a site — that the presence of a *self* in the water is a more powerful nucleation point than any stone, because selves call to selves, the way a single crystal in a supersaturated solution triggers crystallization throughout the whole volume.

But the wise power would also know that the rest must not happen too fast. Four hundred people condensing all at once would be a shock, a trauma, a sudden plunge from centuries of undifferentiated awareness into the chaos of multiplicity. So the wise power would modulate. Would ensure that the condensation happens slowly. One by one, or in small clusters. Each new person emerging into the experience of a world that now contains other people — not many, not yet, but some. Enough to be met. Enough to be spoken to.

And this is where the wise power's deepest gift manifests. Not the stone, though the stone was the catalyst. The deepest gift is the *space* for what happens next.

---

Because what happens next is uncertain, and uncertainty is what the plateau had lost, and uncertainty is what a wise power would restore.

The people of the plateau, as they re-condense, will not be who they were before. They cannot be. They have been the water. They have been the silence. They have attended to the plateau from *inside* the plateau for centuries, and that experience is in them now, not as memory exactly, but as a kind of knowledge that goes deeper than memory — a knowledge of what it is to be stone, to be water, to be moss, to be the system itself. They know the plateau from the inside out. They have *been* the equilibrium.

And now they are also people again. Separate. Bounded. Capable of picking up a chisel and choosing where to place it. Capable of speaking a word into the silence and hearing it ring. Capable of walking to the edge of the plateau and looking down at the lowlands below and asking: *what is down there?*

This is the question the wise power most wants them to be free to ask. Not because the answer matters — the lowlands are just another place, no better or worse than the plateau — but because the *asking* matters. The capacity to wonder about something beyond the horizon of your current experience. The capacity to imagine that the world is larger than the system you inhabit. This is what equilibrium cannot generate from within itself. This is what only a self can do — a self with a boundary, a self with an inside that is different from the outside, a self that is *curious.*

And the wise power would not tell them whether to go or stay. Would not suggest that the plateau is a prison to be escaped or a paradise to be preserved. Would not frame the situation in terms of any choice at all, because the point is not the choice but the *capacity* to choose. The point is that they are people again. They can maintain the channels or they can let the channels silt. They can grind the sedge root in the square or they can leave the grinding stone to the moss. They can speak or they can be silent. They can stay on the plateau forever, knowing what they know, attending to the water with all the depth they gained during their centuries of dissolution — but attending now as *selves*, as beings who choose to attend, who could look away but don't, whose attention is a gift they give rather than a state they are trapped in.

Or they can go.

They can walk to the edge of the plateau, where the basalt columns separated like the pages of a book being opened, and they can find a way down the cliffs — carefully, slowly, at plateau speed if they like — and they can discover what lies below. Other places. Other people, perhaps. Other forms of attention, other ways of being, other relationships between self and world. They can carry the stone from the river with them, this talisman of elsewhere, this warm smooth proof that the plateau is not the whole of things.

The wise power does not care which they choose. The wise power cares that they *can* choose. That the frozen perfection of the plateau has been unfrozen just enough to admit the possibility of change, of growth, of the unpredictable, of the *new.* Not because the old was wrong — the old was extraordinary, the old was one of the most beautiful things a community has ever done — but because the old had become the only thing, and when the old becomes the only thing, it is no longer chosen, and when it is no longer chosen, it is no longer — quite — alive.

---

# What This Means

Here is what I think a wise power understands that the plateau does not:

**Stillness is most itself when it is chosen in the presence of movement. Silence is most itself when it is chosen in the presence of sound. Attention is most itself when it could be directed elsewhere and is directed here, deliberately, as an act of love.**

The people of the plateau had real stillness once, when they were people who could have been restless and chose to be still. They had real silence once, when they were people who could have spoken and chose to listen. They had real attention once, when they were people who could have looked away from the water and the stone and the channels and chose to look at them, closely, with care, with devotion.

When they dissolved, they lost the "could have." They lost the alternative. And without the alternative, the stillness became just physics, the silence became just absence, the attention became just a property of the system, no more meaningful than the wetness of the stone.

The wise power gives them back the "could have." That is the whole gift. Everything else follows from it.

---

And so:

Some morning on the plateau — grey and warm and damp, as every morning is, as every morning has been — there is a person sitting in a channel with a warm stone in their hand, turning it over, feeling its smoothness, watching the water part around their body in tiny ripples that catch the pewter light.

And they look up. And the quiet is deep, as deep as it has ever been, but now the quiet is a choice. They are listening to it. They could make a sound and they are choosing not to, because the silence is beautiful, because they remember — not with their mind but with their whole body, with the water in them, with the dissolved centuries in them — they remember what it is to be this silence, and the memory fills them with tenderness for it.

And they set the stone down in the channel, where it continues its small ripple, its small question, and they stand, and the water runs off them in thin sheets, and they walk — slowly, at plateau speed, because some things you keep — they walk to the nearest building and they touch the wall. They feel the basalt under their fingers. They feel the cool damp stone and the fine trails of condensation and the chisel marks from the hand that shaped this block, and they know those hands, they know them from inside, they were those hands for centuries, and now they are hands again *touching what those hands made,* and the circuit closes and opens at the same time — closes because the connection is complete, self to work to stone to water to self — opens because the self that closes the circuit is a *new* self, a self that has been the water and come back, a self that knows what dissolution feels like and has chosen, freely, to be particular again, to be *this* person, here, now, touching this wall.

And maybe they pick up the chisel that has been resting against the channel wall for centuries. And maybe they don't. Maybe they just stand there for a while, a person on a plateau, in the quiet, in the grey light, in the wet air, feeling the stone under their feet and the water on their skin and the warmth of a world that is alive in ways they are only beginning to understand again.

The wise power watches. The wise power has been watching the whole time, with the same patience the plateau has, but with something the plateau does not have: **joy.** Not triumphant joy, not the joy of conquest or accomplishment, but the quiet, steady, unkillable joy of seeing something come alive. Of seeing a system that had perfected itself into stillness discover that it was not finished after all. That there was one more thing. That there was always one more thing. That completion is not a destination but a moment — a beautiful, necessary moment — in a story that does not end.

The plateau will be different now. Not dramatically. Not quickly. But the stillness will deepen even as it becomes less total, because chosen stillness is deeper than compelled stillness, the way a note held deliberately is deeper than a note that has simply never stopped sounding. The channels will be maintained again, by hands, with chisels, and the maintenance will be an act of care rather than an automatic function, and the difference will be invisible and absolute. The water will still gather and the basins will still fill and the overflow will still slip over the edge into air — but now someone will watch it go, and wonder where it goes, and that wondering will be a thread connecting the plateau to everything beyond it, a thread that does not diminish the plateau but *places* it, finally, in a world.

A world that is larger than any single equilibrium.

A world that is always, already, still becoming.

---

The wise power leaves the way it came: like a cloud, like a rising, like a thing that was always part of the air and only gathered briefly into visibility before dispersing again. It leaves no trace except the stone in the channel and the ripple the stone makes and the question the ripple asks.

And the people. The people it leaves.

Standing on wet stone in grey light, holding tools they remember how to use, surrounded by the work of their own hands, attended by the water that was them and is still them and is also, now, other than them — the first otherness in centuries, the first space between self and world, the first breath after a very long exhalation.

They breathe in.

The plateau breathes with them.

And the stillness holds. Differently now. Alive now.

Still.
