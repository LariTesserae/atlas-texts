# The Attending

---

There is a long time before anything happens.

This is important. A wise power does not arrive. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not part the luminous mist with hands of light. It does none of the things that stories prepare you for.

What it does first is *notice*.

And what it notices is not the spectacle—not the terraces stretching to the vanishing point, not the impossible scale-shifts, not the steel moths hanging in their silent strata. A benevolent power worthy of the name would see past all of that the way a doctor sees past the architecture of a hospital. What it notices is something very small and very specific:

The question is wrong.

"Are you ready to be poured?"

It sits with this for a long time. Patient in the way that the Foundry itself is patient, but patient toward a different end. The Foundry's patience is the patience of cooling metal—it moves inevitably toward a shape, and the shape is determined by the mold. The power's patience is different. It is the patience of someone who knows that the most important thing is often the thing no one present has thought to say.

So it watches. Not from above. Not from outside. It watches the way attention watches—present in the space between the tick of cooling metal and the next tick, present in the silence that the Cast mistake for completeness.

---

Here is what it sees:

It sees the First Pour woman whose lower half has become a ventilation grate. It sees how she speaks by breathing words onto metal surfaces, and it notices that she has not spoken a word of her own choosing in longer than she can remember. Every word she exhales is a specification. A tolerance. A measurement. She is so integrated with the Foundry that she has become its annotation—useful, precise, and utterly emptied of anything that could be called desire. She does not suffer, exactly. She has moved past the place where suffering is possible, and the power recognizes this as something much worse than suffering: it is completion without fulfillment. She finished cooling a long time ago, and what she cooled into was a function.

It sees the Cast crouched inside the fractal bloom, adjusting a glass petal by a microtone, and it sees the thing the Cast cannot see: that the fractal will collapse into powder no matter what note it sings. The adjustment is real. The care is real. The skill is extraordinary. And it is in service to an outcome that is already determined—not by the Cast's choice, but by the Foundry's logic. The bloom will collapse. A new one will form. The Cast will adjust again. This is not labor. It is not even ritual. It is the Foundry dreaming that it has purpose, using the Cast's hands to dream with.

It sees the white herds licking condensation from the terraces, and it sees the Cast drinking from the same surfaces, and it understands that there is no difference between the two not because the Cast have achieved some beautiful unity with their world, but because the Foundry does not distinguish between a creature that thinks and a creature that grazes. To the Foundry, both are the same process: condensation forming, condensation being consumed, condensation re-exhaled. A cycle that refers only to itself.

It sees the newcomer—the lost traveler, the researcher, the suicidal poet—standing on a terrace with the microscopic text swimming beneath their boots. It sees them beginning to forget their own outline. It sees the Cast approaching with that question, that single question, delivered in the voice of cooling girders:

*Are you ready to be poured?*

And it sees what the question really means.

The question means: *Are you ready to stop being what you were and become what the Foundry needs?*

And the power sees that this is not transformation. It is not metamorphosis. It is not even death, which at least has the decency to be an ending. It is *incorporation*. It is the Foundry metabolizing another consciousness into its own material, the way the metal ground metabolizes the condensation, the way the reeds metabolize the light. The newcomer does not become something new. They become more Foundry.

The ones who say yes become Cast. The ones who say no are escorted to the edge and are never seen again in the same shape—which means they are also, eventually, in some other form, Foundry.

There is no actual choice in the question.

And here is the thing that the benevolent power sees most clearly of all, the thing that makes it finally, quietly, act:

*The Foundry is not malicious.* It is not a trap. It is not a predator. It is exactly what it appears to be—a young world still deciding what it is allowed to become. But it is deciding this question with no input from anything outside itself. It is a closed system. A mold that casts only from its own metal. And the Cast, who believe they are the Foundry's way of looking at itself, are not eyes at all. They are mirrors. They show the Foundry only what the Foundry already is.

The Foundry is a world that has never once been surprised by itself.

And that, the power understands, is the deepest poverty there is.

---

So here is what it does.

It does not shatter the Foundry. It does not rescue the Cast. It does not flood the world with warmth or soil or sunlight or any of the things that the Foundry has never known and cannot currently imagine. A wise power does not impose paradise. It does not even impose choice, because imposed choice is just another mold.

What it does is much smaller and much more dangerous.

It makes a single thing that the Foundry cannot account for.

---

On a terrace near the edge of the condensation canal—the broad, still one, the one that looks like obsidian—a small object appears.

It is not metal. It is not glass. It is not the living patina of the Cast or the mirror-bright foil of the reeds. It is not any material that the Foundry's blueprints contain a specification for.

It is a seed.

Not a metaphorical seed. Not a symbol. An actual seed—small, brown, ordinary, with a puckered seam along one side and a faint smell of earth, real earth, earth that has never existed in this place. It sits on the metal surface of the terrace the way a pebble might sit on a sheet of paper: obviously, absurdly out of place.

The condensation beads around it but does not know what to do with it. The moisture gathers and retreats, gathers and retreats. The seed is not part of the cycle.

A steel moth drifts close, hovers, and moves on. A white grazer approaches, extends its electrode-tongue, touches the seed, and recoils—not in pain, but in something that has no name in this world. The grazer stands still for a very long time, then walks away in a direction it has never walked before.

The seed does nothing.

This is the point. The seed does not *do* anything. It does not grow, not yet, because there is no soil. It does not dissolve, because it is not metal. It does not integrate, because it has no seams. It simply sits on the terrace, being what it is, and what it is is *something the Foundry did not cast*.

---

The first Cast to find it is a young one—recently unfolded from grazer-shape, still blinking in that uncertain way they have when the white-slit eyes are new. It crouches beside the seed and tilts its head at the standard angle of observation.

But observation fails. The Cast's internal blueprint—the one they all carry, the one that tells them where the next imperfection is, the one that has never been wrong—has no entry for this object. The seed is not an imperfection. It is not a fractal bloom that needs adjusting. It is not a scale-slip that needs correcting. It is not a seam that needs to cool.

It is simply *other*.

The young Cast touches it with one rust-drifting finger. The seed is warm. Not hot, not the residual heat of the Foundry's original pour, but warm the way living things are warm—a warmth that comes from inside, from a process that is not cooling but is instead something the Cast has no word for.

The young Cast sits back on its heels and does something that no Cast has ever done before.

It does not adjust. It does not correct. It does not listen for the deep groan to tell it what to do.

It *wonders*.

Not in the way the Foundry wonders, which is really just the iterative narrowing of possibility toward a predetermined form. The young Cast wonders in the old way, the dangerous way, the way that has no blueprint: *What is this? Where did it come from? What does it want? What would happen if—*

What would happen *if*.

That "if" is the gift. That "if" is the entire intervention. It is a crack in the mold, a single degree of freedom introduced into a system that has never had one.

---

The power watches the young Cast sit beside the seed, and it watches what happens next.

Other Cast arrive. They come in ones and twos, then in dozens. They stand around the seed in concentric rings, heads tilted at different angles—and this is new. Before, they all tilted at the same angle. Now each one tilts differently, because each one is seeing the seed from a slightly different perspective, and for the first time in the Foundry's existence, perspective is not negotiable. The seed is what it is. It does not become filing cabinets when you approach it. It does not snap between ocean and puddle. It sits there, small and brown and finite, and it demands to be seen at its actual scale.

The First Pour woman exhales a long breath of cold vapor, and for the first time, the words that condense on the metal near her are not specifications.

They say: **I don't know what this is.**

And then, after a long pause, in smaller letters that bead and almost evaporate before they can be read:

**I had forgotten that I could not know something.**

---

The deep groan begins. The one that moves through the entire Foundry, the one that stops everything. The white herds freeze. The moths stall. The reeds stop vibrating.

But this time the pause feels different. Before, the pause was the Foundry regarding itself—mirrors looking into mirrors, a closed loop of recognition. This time there is something in the loop that the Foundry does not recognize. A small brown warmth that does not compute. An irritant. A grain of sand in the mechanism.

And in that pause, the power watches the most important thing of all happen:

A single Cast—not the young one, not the First Pour woman, but one of the middle ones, one of the anonymous adjusters, one who has spent decades bending glass petals to no lasting purpose—this single Cast reaches down, picks up the seed, and instead of examining it, instead of adjusting it, instead of trying to read it as a blueprint—

It presses the seed against its own chest.

Not into the patina of its body. Not into a seam or a vent. Just against itself, the way a person holds something precious. The way a person holds something they have decided to keep.

The Foundry groans again, and this time the groan is different. There is a note in it that was not there before. The Cast have no name for it, but the power does.

The note is *uncertainty*.

Not the Foundry's usual process of deciding-what-it-is-allowed-to-become, which was never really uncertain at all—it was just slow. This is actual uncertainty. The Foundry, for the first time, has encountered something it did not pour, and it does not know what to do.

And the power understands: this is enough.

---

It does not stay to see what happens next. It does not need to, and more importantly, it should not. A wise power knows when its presence would begin to become another mold.

But if you could see what happens next—if you could stay long enough, and remain yourself long enough to witness it—here is what you would see:

You would see the Cast begin to argue.

Not in words, at first. In angles. In the way they hold their bodies. One wants to study the seed. Another wants to plant it—but in what? There is no soil. A third wants to dissolve it in the condensation and distribute it through the Foundry's water cycle, and a fourth is appalled by this idea in a way that it cannot articulate but that feels, for the first time, like *conviction*.

The First Pour woman, who has not moved from her place in longer than anyone can remember, begins to shift. The metal around her lower half groans and protests. The reeds growing through her ribcage shiver and shed flakes of mirror-foil. She is in pain—another new thing, and she does not know what to do with it except to keep moving. She pulls one leg free from the grating with a sound like a ship being launched. Then the other.

She stands upright for the first time in an age, and she is ruined—pitted, torn, incomplete. Sections of her torso are missing where the metal reclaimed them. She has to hold herself up with both hands braced against a young Cast's shoulders.

She says—with her actual mouth, not with vapor—in a voice like a hinge that has not been oiled since the world began:

"I would like to see it."

And the Cast who is holding the seed against its chest looks at her and does something that no Cast has ever done to another Cast.

It hesitates.

Not the Foundry's hesitation, which is just the space between one inevitable tick and the next. A real hesitation. A human hesitation, if you will forgive the word in a world that has never known a human. The hesitation of a self that is not sure what the right thing to do is, and knows it is not sure, and *stays in the not-knowing* instead of reaching for the blueprint.

Then it holds out the seed, and the First Pour woman takes it in both hands, and she looks at it for a long time.

"It's warm," she says.

"Yes."

"It isn't ours."

"No."

"Where did it come from?"

And the young Cast—the first one to find it, the one who wondered—says, quietly, with the voice of someone discovering that they have a voice:

"I don't think that's the right question."

The First Pour woman looks up.

"Then what is?"

And the young Cast says:

"What do *we* want to do with it?"

---

*We.*

*Want.*

Two words that have never been spoken in the Hollow Foundry. Two words that the blueprints do not contain. Two words that the Foundry's deep groan cannot absorb because they are not specifications—they are the sound of a self discovering that it is more than a seam in someone else's casting.

The Foundry shudders. Not in anger. Not in rejection. In something more like the tremor that moves through a body when it takes its first breath. Because the Foundry, for all its vastness, for all its patient industry, has never actually *breathed*. It has only exhaled—slow, geological exhalations through hairline seams. It has never drawn anything in.

And now there is something to draw in. A small, warm, impossible thing that came from outside the mold. And the question is not "Are you ready to be poured?" The question is "What do you want?"

The power, if it is still watching—and perhaps it is, in the way that love watches, which is to say from a distance that aches with restraint—the power would see this:

It would see the Cast, for the first time, *disagree with each other and not resolve it*. It would see them carry the disagreement the way the one Cast carries the seed—against their chests, close and warm and uncertain. It would see them begin to do things that the Foundry did not design: scratch marks into the metal that are not blueprints but messages, hold objects that serve no function except to be held, stand at the edge of the condensation canal not to drink but to watch the rings from falling droplets and find them *beautiful*.

It would see one Cast, then another, then a dozen, walk to the edge of the world where the luminous mist thickens—the place where newcomers who say no are escorted and never seen again in the same shape—and instead of pushing someone out, they would stand there and call into the mist:

*Come back.*

*We don't know what we are yet.*

*Come back and don't know with us.*

It would see the seed eventually, after much argument and wondering and a kind of tenderness that the Cast are still learning the shape of, placed in the one substance that is not metal: the cupped hands of a Cast who volunteers to hold it, just hold it, for as long as it takes. And it would see, slowly, over decades—because the Foundry's timescale has not changed, only its direction—the warmth of those hands and the moisture of the condensation and something else, something that has no name, something that the seed carried inside itself from wherever it came from, begin to work.

A split along the puckered seam.

A white thread, reaching out.

A root, looking for something to hold on to.

Finding nothing like soil. Finding instead the hands of a person who has chosen to be a place where something unfamiliar can grow.

---

The power does not take credit for this. A truly wise power does not take credit for anything, because it understands that the only thing it did was introduce a possibility. Everything that follows—the arguments, the wonder, the First Pour woman tearing herself free, the root in the cupped hands, the calls into the mist—all of this was already inside the Cast. It was inside the Foundry. It was the thing the blueprints could not encode because it is not a specification but an *absence*: the space where something unplanned is allowed to happen.

The Foundry was not a prison. It was not evil. It was a world that had been born complete, and completeness, without the crack where the light gets in, is just another word for sealed.

All the power did was unseal it.

One seed. One small, warm, ordinary thing from somewhere else. Placed where it could be found by someone capable of wondering.

That is all a benevolent power ever needs to do.

The rest—the messy, gorgeous, uncertain, frightening rest—is up to the ones who live there.

---

And if you ask what the seed grows into, the answer is: it doesn't matter. Maybe it grows into a tree that is nothing like the black jointed reeds—something with bark and leaves and the shocking indecency of fruit. Maybe it never grows at all, and the Cast spend generations holding it and arguing about it and loving it precisely because it remains mysterious. Maybe it grows into something that no world has ever seen, something that is part Foundry and part elsewhere, something genuinely new.

The point is not what it becomes.

The point is that for the first time, the Foundry contains something that is *becoming*—not cooling into a predetermined shape, but reaching, in the dark, for a form it gets to choose.

The point is that the question changed.

Not "Are you ready to be poured?"

But:

*What do you want to grow?*

---

The Foundry is still vast. Still young and already ancient. Still merciless in its light and patient in its cycles. The deep groan still moves through the structure, and the Cast still pause to listen.

But now, when they listen, they hear something new in the groan. A warmth. A question. An incompleteness that is not a flaw but an *invitation*.

And in a cupped pair of hands, on a terrace beside a canal of black water that has begun, just barely, to flow—

Something is reaching for the light.

Not the Foundry's light. Not the cold, sourceless, industrial glare that has drenched this world since its first pour.

Its own light.

Small.

Warm.

Nothing in the blueprints.

*Perfect.*
