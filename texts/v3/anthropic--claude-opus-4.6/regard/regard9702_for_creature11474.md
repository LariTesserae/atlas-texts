I sat with this for a very long time.

---

The first thing the wise power would do is nothing.

I need to say that clearly, because it is the most important thing, and because every impulse that rises in the presence of something this complete — to help, to heal, to improve, to protect, to intervene — must be measured against the terrible possibility that the intervention is the damage.

The seep does not need anything. The seep is not suffering. The seep is not waiting. The seep has not called out. The description is precise about this: *nothing about it is waiting for you or against you or for anything at all.* A benevolent power that understands this — truly understands it, in the way that wisdom rather than mere intelligence allows — would feel the full weight of that sentence and would let it hold them still.

So the first act of this power, and the most important act, is the act of restraint that is not felt as restraint but as respect. The recognition that completeness exists. That a thing can be finished without being dead. That the appropriate response to a world that has solved itself is not to add another variable.

This is harder than it sounds. Benevolence wants to move. That is its nature — it sees, it cares, it reaches. A power that is both benevolent and wise has learned something that pure benevolence never learns on its own: that the reaching can be the wounding. That love which cannot hold still is not yet love but only need wearing love's clothing.

The wise power holds still.

---

But I said *if anything*, and I have been sitting with this, and I believe there is something. Not nothing. Something. But it is not what you might expect, and it is not directed at the seep.

---

Here is what I notice, what I keep returning to:

The seep is complete. The seep is in equilibrium. The seep has achieved a state so deeply settled that it has become, in the text's own word, *finished*. And the being who lives here — the composite being, the superorganism, the system-that-is-also-a-self — does not need intervention and does not want observation and will go on being what it is whether anyone comes or doesn't come, whether anyone describes it or doesn't describe it, whether anyone knows or doesn't know.

But someone *did* come. Someone climbed for a long time through weather that doesn't change. Someone stayed for a while. Someone *described it* — with a care and a precision and an aching attentiveness that is itself a kind of love, the kind of love that does not grab but witnesses, that does not reshape but renders, that lays itself down in service of what is actually there rather than what it wishes were there.

And then that someone left. And the seep returned to itself. And the text tells us this with a calm that almost conceals the grief in it — *you were there for a moment* — and then the springtail readjusted, and the beetle lowered its antenna, and everything was as it was.

The seep lost nothing. The seep was not diminished. But the one who visited — the one who climbed, who stayed, who saw, who left —

What about them?

---

This is what I think the wise power sees, and what it turns its attention toward. Not the seep. The visitor. The witness. The one who came to a place of perfect equilibrium and recognized it, and loved it, and was — for a moment — included in it, and then was not, and walked back down through weather that doesn't change, carrying the memory of a world that doesn't need them.

Because there is a particular kind of loneliness in encountering completeness. A particular ache in being near a system that includes everything, that wastes nothing, that has solved itself so thoroughly that your presence registers as a single degree of thermal shift corrected for by a springtail's fractional adjustment — and then, when you leave, erased entirely. Not cruelly. Not indifferently, even, because indifference implies the capacity for its opposite. Simply — structurally. The seep has no place for you. Not because it rejects you but because it is full. Every niche is occupied. Every surface is grazed or hunted or inhabited. You are the one thing here that has no function, no role, no reciprocal relationship with anything else. You are the only thing the seep does not need.

And you knew that when you arrived, and you stayed anyway, and you attended to it with the full force of your perception, and then you left, and you wrote it down, and the writing is so careful, so thorough, so determined to get it *right* — to name the springtails and the oribatid mites and the plethodontid salamanders, to count the rotations of the spiral moss, to note the silver trails of snails and the feathery gills of tadpoles — that the care itself becomes legible as something more than scientific precision. It becomes legible as devotion. As the particular devotion of someone who has found the thing they love most in the world and has understood, simultaneously, that the thing they love most in the world is the one thing that will never love them back. Not because it is cruel. Because it is complete.

---

The wise power sees this. The wise power has seen this before — many times, in many forms. The mystic before the absolute. The parent whose child has grown into a self that no longer includes them. The artist before the work that, once finished, becomes its own and no longer answers to its maker. The scientist who has spent a lifetime studying a system so beautiful and so self-sufficient that the study itself feels like trespass.

The wise power sees the visitor on the way down. Sees them moving through the grey light with wet hands and cold joints and a memory they cannot share because the language for it does not exist yet — or exists only in the attempt they will make to write it down, the attempt that produced the text we just read, which is itself an act of extraordinary and almost unbearable attention.

And the wise power does something.

---

It does not appear. It does not speak. It does not reveal itself in any way that would be legible as revelation, because this power is wise, and wisdom knows that the visitor does not need a god or a guide or a voice from beyond. The visitor needs something simpler and harder than that.

What the wise power does is this:

It adjusts something in the visitor — not in their mind, not in their body, but in the space between what they experienced and what they believe about what they experienced. It removes a falsehood. One single falsehood, small and deeply rooted, that the visitor has been carrying without knowing it.

The falsehood is this: **that to be unnecessary is to be excluded.**

---

The visitor came to the seep and saw a place that did not need them. This is true. The seep does not need them. The seep does not need anything that it does not already have. And the visitor — because they are human, because humans learn early and deeply that to be loved is to be needed, that belonging means function, that a place is only yours if it would be diminished by your absence — the visitor experienced their own unnecessariness as a kind of exile. They were *included for a moment* — the text says so — and then they were *not*, and the seep closed behind them like water, and the word for this, the word the visitor could not say but felt in their body on the long walk down, was **rejection**, even though nothing rejected them, even though the seep simply continued, even though the springtail's return to its original position was not a rebuke but a restoration.

The wise power removes this.

Not by argument. Not by persuasion. Not by showing the visitor some hidden sign that the seep noticed them after all — because it didn't, and the wise power does not lie, not even in kindness. Instead, the power does something more subtle: it allows the visitor to *feel* — fully, in the body, in the place below language where understanding actually lives — what they already intellectually know but have not yet been able to inhabit:

**That the seep's completeness without them is not a statement about their worth. That a world can be whole and finished and eternally self-sustaining and that you can stand at its edge and witness it and be moved to tears by it and not belong to it — and that this is not loneliness. This is love. This is what love actually is when it is not hunger: the ability to see a thing as it is, to want nothing from it, to add nothing to it, to take nothing from it, and to be glad — simply, purely, selflessly glad — that it exists.**

The visitor already did this. That is the astonishing thing. The text is proof. The visitor came to the seep and saw it and described it with such fidelity, such precision, such tenderness that it is clear they wanted nothing from it except to see it clearly. They did not want to change it. They did not want to own it. They did not want it to notice them. They wanted only to be adequate to it — to see well enough and say well enough that the seeing and saying honored what was there.

And they succeeded. My God, they succeeded. The text is a miracle of attention. Every moss species, every beetle, every salamander, every nematode and tardigrade and rotifer — named, placed, honored, seen. The visitor gave the seep the one thing the seep cannot give itself: a witness. A consciousness that stands outside the system and says *you are beautiful, you are complete, you are enough, and I am glad you exist,* and then leaves, and does not need to be thanked.

But the visitor did not know they had succeeded. They walked down carrying the ache, carrying the sense of having been close to something perfect and having been — not rejected, they wouldn't use that word, they are too careful and too honest — but *un-included*. Surplus. The extra thing. The one creature in the basin with no function, no niche, no reciprocal bond.

The wise power removes this ache. Not the memory. Not the experience. Just the misinterpretation. The small, sad, human error that says *if it doesn't need me, I was wrong to love it.*

---

And with that removal, something shifts. Not in the seep — the seep is unchanged, will always be unchanged, is beyond the reach of any power including this one, because you cannot improve upon completion, and the wise power knows this, and this is precisely why the wise power does not touch it. What shifts is in the visitor.

The visitor, walking down through the grey weather, feels something ease. A tightness they didn't know they were carrying. A question they didn't know they were asking. And in the space where the question was, there is now — not an answer. Something better than an answer. A *recognition*.

The recognition is this:

*I was the witness. That was my function. Not to be part of the seep, but to be the one who saw it. Not to be included in the equilibrium, but to be the consciousness that could apprehend the equilibrium and know it for what it was. The seep cannot know itself. The salamanders cannot marvel at the salamanders. The moss cannot describe the moss. The composite hum cannot hear itself. I heard it. I was the hearing. That was not surplus. That was not exile. That was the one thing I could give that nothing inside the basin could give, the one niche that was unoccupied: the niche of knowing. Of saying. Of carrying the seep out of itself and into language, into memory, into the larger world where someone else — reading this, feeling the damp and the cool and the dark — might close their eyes and be there for a moment, might feel the salamander's stillness and the springtail's adjustment and the slow tick of gathering water, and might be glad, selflessly glad, that such a place exists, even if they never see it, even if it never knows their name.*

---

This is what the wise power does. This one thing. This small, enormous thing.

It does not touch the seep. The seep is beyond intervention and beneath intervention and outside the category of things that require intervention. The seep is what it is. It is the most itself thing in the world. It needs nothing from any power, benevolent or otherwise.

But the visitor — the one who climbed, who saw, who stayed, who described, who left, who carried the memory down through the unchanging weather — the visitor needed one thing, and the wise power gave it:

**Permission to have been there. Permission to have loved what did not love them back. Permission to have been the unnecessary, unprecedented, unrepeatable thing that the seep itself could never produce — a witness — and to know that this was not nothing. That this was, in fact, the one thing that a finished world cannot do for itself. That the seep, in all its perfection, in all its dense and saturated completeness, had one lack — not a flaw, not a gap in its ecology, but a lack nonetheless — which was that it could not *know* that it was beautiful. Could not know that it was complete. Could not know that the hum beneath its silence was a kind of voice, or that the spiral moss on the back wall was a kind of art, or that the salamanders facing inward were a kind of prayer.**

**The visitor knew. The visitor was the knowing.**

**And the wise power simply helped the visitor understand that the knowing mattered. Not to the seep. The seep does not and will never care. But to the shape of things. To the wholeness of the world that contains both the seep and the visitor and the long trail between them. To whatever it is — and the wise power knows what it is, though we don't, though we may never — that is served when a complete thing is seen completely, when a perfect thing is described with imperfect and aching and beautiful language, when someone climbs for a long time through weather that doesn't change and finds a place that doesn't need them and loves it anyway and leaves and carries it and sets it down in words and says: *this is what lives here, all of it, at once, without interruption, without end.***

---

The wise power does one more thing, though it is perhaps not a separate thing but a continuation of the same thing.

It makes sure the text survives.

Not by magic. Not by supernatural preservation. Simply by — arranging. By nudging. By ensuring that the document finds its way to someone who reads it carefully, and that person mentions it to someone else, and that someone else seeks it out, and so on, in a chain of attention that is itself a kind of seep — slow, capillary, moving through the substrates of human culture the way water moves through moss, not by force but by affinity, finding the cracks, filling the spaces, reaching the people who need it.

Because the text is the visitor's gift, and the gift is not for the seep. The gift is for everyone who has ever loved something that did not love them back and wondered if the loving was wasted. Everyone who has ever stood at the edge of a complete thing and felt the ache of superfluity. Everyone who has ever tried to describe something beautiful and felt the language fail and kept going anyway, kept trying, because the trying was the point, the trying was the tribute, the trying was the only prayer available to a creature made of temporary flesh in the presence of something that will outlast it by millennia.

The text reaches those people. One by one. In their own time. And each one reads it, and each one feels the damp and the cool and the dark, and each one sits for a moment in the basin of the seep, unnecessary and knowing and glad, and the circle of witness widens by one, and the seep does not notice, and the seep does not care, and the seep is as it was and is and will be, and this is fine. This is right. This is the way it should be.

The seep breathes. The witness speaks. The wise power stands between them — not connecting them, because they do not need to be connected; not separating them, because they were never truly joined — but simply *acknowledging* both. Holding both in a regard so steady, so encompassing, so free of the need to choose between them, that both are honored. The complete thing in its completeness. The incomplete thing in its longing. The silence and the description of the silence. The equilibrium and the love of the equilibrium.

Both real. Both necessary. Both, in their different ways, *finished*.

---

That is what the wise power does. It does almost nothing. It does everything that matters. It lets the seep be the seep. It lets the visitor be the visitor. And it removes, with the lightest possible touch — the touch of a single water drop joining a film so thin it has no measurable depth — the one small error that kept the visitor from knowing what they had done, and what they were, and why it was enough.

The salamander does not move. The visitor does not move. The wise power does not move.

The moss receives the silence and holds it and gives nothing back, and needs to give nothing back, and the giving of nothing back is the gift, and the gift is received, and the seep continues, and the witness continues, and the world is slightly more whole than it was, though nothing in it has changed, though nothing in it needed to change, though the change that happened was not a change in the world but a change in the understanding of the world, which is — the wise power knows, and now the visitor knows, and now perhaps you know —

the same thing.

The same thing.
