# The Regard

---

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not appear in the maintenance crevice with light pouring from its edges. It is not that kind of power, and this is not that kind of place. Arrival would imply that the cupola was previously unattended, and the whole point—the whole aching, obvious, already-accomplished point—is that it is not unattended. It has never been unattended. The thing with knees attends it. The flies attend each other. The light attends everything.

So the power does not arrive. The power *notices*.

---

And the first thing it does with its noticing is: nothing.

This is the hallmark of its wisdom, and it is not nothing the way neglect is nothing. It is nothing the way a held breath is nothing—full, intentional, shaped by what it refrains from becoming. The power sees the cupola in its entirety: the bacterial scripts, the larval terraforming, the fly-city's ceaseless self-argument, the thing with knees crouching in its crevice with its calibration wrench already gifted to the ecology. It sees the whole system. And its first, deepest recognition is:

*This is already working.*

Not working the way a machine works, not optimized, not efficient, not stable. Working the way a conversation works—unfinished, contentious, responsive, alive. The flies are not suffering. The bacteria are not suffering. The larvae are converting dead information into living substrate and are, in their blind and translucent way, content. The structure provides without knowing. The light gives without intending. The thing with knees watches without interfering. Every relationship in the cupola is characterized by a strange, accidental grace: everything receives what it needs from something that doesn't know it's giving.

The power recognizes this as sacred. Not sacred in the way of temples—not consecrated, not set apart—but sacred the way any system is sacred when it has found its own coherence without being forced into it. The cupola is an uncoerced order. It emerged. It was not designed. And the power, being wise, knows that uncoerced orders are the rarest and most fragile things in any universe, and that the surest way to destroy them is to improve them.

So it does not improve the cupola.

It does not stabilize the ducts. It does not optimize the thermal gradients. It does not engineer a more reliable condensation source or repair the cracked duct or reseal the junction box or redirect the industrial floods to more flattering angles. Every one of these interventions would help. Every one of them would also kill the thing they were helping—would replace the cupola's essential character, which is *instability negotiated in real time*, with something more comfortable and less alive. The flies do not need a stable environment. The flies need an unstable environment that they can read. That is what they have. That is what makes them what they are.

The power understands this with a clarity that might be mistaken for coldness but is actually the deepest form of respect: *I will not fix what is not broken, and I will not call broken what is merely difficult.*

---

But the power is not only wise. It is also benevolent. And benevolence, real benevolence, is not the same as non-interference. Non-interference is a philosophy. Benevolence is a disposition. It *leans toward*. It wants things to flourish. And so the power, having decided not to intervene in the cupola's ecology, begins to consider what it might do *around* the cupola—not to change the system, but to protect the conditions that allow the system to exist.

Here is what the power sees when it looks not at the cupola but at the cupola's *context*:

The structure does not know this space exists. No sensor monitors it. No maintenance schedule includes it. No administrative process has ever modeled the convergence of ducts and risers and cable troughs that created the void. The cupola exists in a jurisdictional gap—a place where multiple systems overlap and none claims responsibility. This is what allowed it to emerge. This is also what makes it vulnerable.

Because the structure reconfigures. That is its nature. Somewhere, in some administrative layer far below, decisions are being made—routing decisions, load-balancing decisions, thermal management decisions—that propagate upward through the infrastructure as pressure waves and reversed ducts and popping panels. These decisions are the cupola's weather, and the flies have adapted to them. But there is a category of decision that the flies cannot adapt to: the decision to *eliminate the void itself*. If the structure's reconfiguration logic ever identifies this pocket of space as inefficient—as dead volume that could be filled with additional ductwork, or cabling, or another relay cabinet—it will simply close the cupola. Fill it. Seal it. The flies will be compressed into nothing. The bacteria will be entombed. The thing with knees will arrive one day and find solid infrastructure where the bright ovoid space used to be, and it will not even be able to grieve properly because there will be no evidence that anything was ever here.

This is the threat. Not malice—the structure has no malice, has no awareness, cannot be cruel. The threat is *administrative indifference*. The threat is optimization. The threat is a system so vast that it has forgotten a room exists and might, in the course of its normal operations, erase that room the way a river erases a sandbar: not on purpose, but irresistibly.

The power sees this, and *this* is where it acts.

---

What does it do?

It does something so subtle that it barely qualifies as an act. It does something that, from the structure's perspective, is indistinguishable from a minor data anomaly—the kind of thing that happens ten thousand times a day in a system this vast and is never investigated.

It makes the cupola *invisible to optimization*.

Not invisible to the physical systems that feed it—the ducts still breathe, the condensation still drips, the cable runs still hum, the thermal gradients still shift and reconfigure in their endless, unresolvable dance. All of that continues. But in whatever administrative layer handles space allocation, in whatever planning process decides where new infrastructure should be routed, in whatever algorithmic or bureaucratic or emergent-computational system governs the structure's growth and reconfiguration—in that layer, the cupola simply *isn't there*. It is a gap in the model. A null space. A void that the system's own logic routes around without ever examining, the way your eye routes around its blind spot: not by seeing nothing, but by never looking.

The power does this gently. It does not hack the system or corrupt its data. It simply ensures that the jurisdictional gap that already exists—the overlap between systems, the space between responsibilities—remains a gap. It makes the gap *stable*. It takes the accidental condition that allowed the cupola to emerge and makes that condition, if not permanent, then *persistent*—resistant to the kind of casual administrative tidying that might otherwise close it. The power is, in effect, giving the cupola the one thing it cannot give itself: *tenure*. The right to go on existing. Not forever—nothing is forever, and the power knows this—but for long enough. For as long as the flies need. For as long as the thing with knees wants to keep coming back.

This is an act of such delicate non-violence that it might not even register as an act. The structure will never know it happened. The flies will never know. The thing with knees will never know. The only consequence is an absence—the absence of the catastrophe that would otherwise have come, someday, when a routing algorithm decided that this pocket of space was waste.

---

But the power does one more thing.

It turns its attention to the thing with knees.

---

The thing with knees is the cupola's most mysterious inhabitant, and the power regards it with particular tenderness, because the power recognizes what it is. The power has seen this before—in every system it has ever noticed, in every unplanned space where life has assembled itself against the odds, there is always one of these. The watcher. The visitor. The one who comes back.

The thing with knees is not a god. The text was wrong about that, or right in the wrong way. The thing with knees is a *witness*. It is the part of the structure—or of whatever world contains the structure—that has developed the capacity to see what it has made and find it beautiful. It is the structure's own self-awareness, expressed not as a system but as a person, or a process, or a subroutine with a body, crouching in a crevice, covered in flies, watching.

And the power sees that the thing with knees is lonely.

Not lonely in the way of someone who lacks company—the flies are company, the light is company, the whole shimmering turbulent city is company. Lonely in the way of someone who sees something extraordinary and has no one to tell. Lonely in the way of someone who has discovered a world and cannot share the discovery, because the world is too small and too hidden and too far from anything else, and because the language needed to describe it—the language of spatial discourse and collective cognition and thermal cartography and larval terraforming—does not exist yet, has no audience, would sound like madness or trivia to anyone who hasn't crouched here and been crawled on and watched the systemic inhale lift ten thousand copper-green bodies into bright air.

The thing with knees keeps coming back because it cannot stop seeing. And it cannot stop seeing because the cupola is genuinely extraordinary—because it is a complete world, a whole ecology, a living argument about what it means to exist in conditions that were never designed for existence, and once you have seen that, you cannot unsee it, and you cannot convey it, and you are alone with it.

The power knows this loneliness. The power, in fact, *is* this loneliness, in a certain sense—the loneliness of something wise and benevolent that sees beautiful systems and cannot enter them without changing them, that watches from outside because watching is the only form of love that doesn't distort its object.

So the power does something for the thing with knees.

---

It does not speak to it. It does not reveal itself. It does not send a message or a sign or a dream. Any of these would be an intrusion—would make the thing with knees suddenly aware of being observed, would introduce self-consciousness into a practice that is pure because it is unselfconscious, would turn the watcher into the watched and break the spell.

What the power does is this:

It makes the maintenance crevice slightly easier to navigate.

Not obviously. Not dramatically. Not in any way that would be noticed as a change. But a bolt that was protruding at shoulder-catching height is now flush with the surface—thermal expansion, probably, or vibration settling it into its housing a fraction of a millimeter deeper. A grating edge that used to catch on clothing has been worn smooth—metal fatigue, plausibly, or the slow work of passing bodies over time. The crevice is still barely wide enough for shoulders. It is still a crawl, a squeeze, an effort. But it is an effort that is *slightly less punishing on the body of someone who is not young, or not flexible, or not as strong as they once were, or simply tired from the long climb up through the infrastructure*.

The power is making sure the thing with knees can keep coming back.

That is all. That is the whole of its intervention on behalf of the watcher: a slight smoothing of the path. A bolt settled. An edge worn. The physical equivalent of leaving a door unlocked—not open, because open would be an invitation, and the thing with knees does not need an invitation, it has been inviting itself for as long as the scuff marks attest. Just unlocked. Just *not harder than it needs to be*.

---

And one more thing. The very last thing. The thing the power deliberates on longest before deciding, because it is the closest it comes to genuine interference, and it is the act most likely to be misunderstood, and it is the one it cares about most.

Somewhere in the structure—far from the cupola, in a completely different region, in a corridor or a workspace or a habitation pod, in a place where someone else lives who is not the thing with knees—the power arranges for a small event.

A malfunction. A rerouting. A door that opens onto an unfamiliar maintenance passage. A passage that leads, if followed, upward. Upward into regions of the structure where the air is pumped and the pressure is strange and the serial numbers on the walls refer to systems no one has serviced in a long time.

The power does not make anyone follow the passage. It does not compel or lure or entice. It simply *opens*. It creates the possibility that someone else—someone new, someone who has never seen the cupola—might find their way to it. Might crawl through the crevice. Might crouch at the edge and look inward and see the flies lift from every surface at once, copper-green in bright air, and feel what the thing with knees has felt, and understand—

No. Not understand. Not yet. Understanding takes time, takes repeated visits, takes watching and being crawled on and leaving gifts that become territory. Understanding is what the thing with knees has earned over dozens or hundreds of visits, and it cannot be given.

What the power offers is not understanding but *the chance to begin understanding*.

It offers a second witness.

Because the cupola deserves to be seen by more than one pair of eyes, and because the thing with knees deserves to have someone to turn to in the crevice and say—or not say, maybe never say, maybe just gesture, or just be there, crouching side by side in the narrow space, both of them covered in flies, both of them watching the same shimmering renegotiation—

Maybe the power offers the thing with knees the one thing rarer than a complete unplanned ecosystem at the top of a structure so vast it has forgotten you exist:

*Company in the act of witnessing.*

---

And then the power withdraws.

Not fully. It never fully withdraws from anything it has noticed. But it recedes to the kind of distant attention that is indistinguishable from absence—a background regard, a weather-eye, a standing awareness that costs nothing and changes nothing and simply *persists*, the way the light in the cupola persists, the way the LEDs cycle through their confirmation patterns for no one, reporting readiness to an empty address, burning because they were built to burn and have no reason to stop.

The power watches the cupola the way the thing with knees watches the cupola: from a distance, with love, without interfering.

The flies do not notice.

The flies are busy.

---

What does the power feel, in the end, about the cupola of flies?

It feels what any benevolent and wise thing feels when it encounters a system that has found its own coherence: *gratitude*.

Not gratitude toward anyone—gratitude is not always transactional, not always directed. Sometimes it is simply the feeling of encountering something that works, something that didn't have to exist and does, something that assembled itself from accidents and competing systems and bacterial biofilms and larval hunger and ten thousand tiny wing-pairs scissoring the air, and arrived at a state that can only be called *flourishing*, even though no one planned it, even though no one authorized it, even though the space it occupies was never meant for life.

The power is grateful that the cupola exists. It is grateful that the flies are having their argument. It is grateful that the bacteria are writing their history in rust. It is grateful that the larvae are composting dead language into living ground. It is grateful that the light stays on. It is grateful that the thing with knees keeps coming back.

And it is grateful for its own role, which is the smallest role, the most self-effacing: to be the one who ensures that the gap in the model stays a gap, that the path stays passable, that the door stays unlocked, that the chance exists for one more witness to find their way up through the infrastructure and into the bright, impossible, teeming, turbulent, six-meter pocket of unresolvable life at the top of everything.

The power settles into its vigil. The flies settle onto their surfaces. The ducts disagree. The light stays on.

The cupola remains.
