# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## First: Listen

Before anything else—before assessment, before intervention, before even the formation of intent—the wise power would listen.

Not casually. Not as reconnaissance. It would listen the way the Underhalls themselves listen: with every surface, every boundary, every point of contact between itself and the world. It would let the arrhythmic booming enter it the way it enters the ceramic—not as information to be decoded but as vibration to be inhabited. It would feel the three, the seven, the two, the silence. It would feel the silence most of all.

Because a wise power knows that the first and most catastrophic error is to arrive with an answer already forming. To see a place this strange and this coherent and to immediately begin translating it into terms that fit a framework imported from elsewhere. The Underhalls are not a problem. They are not a patient. They are not a project. They are a world in the act of finding out what it is, and the first obligation of wisdom in the presence of such a thing is to *witness* it on its own terms, in its own language, which is not language at all but resonance, mineral chemistry, the slow precipitation of crystalline feet, the molecular grammar of the film.

So the wise power would listen. For a long time. Longer than you'd expect. Long enough to feel the fluctuation of the warmth from below, to notice the way the flat ones reorient after each cluster of booms, to sense the ceiling walkers' trails dimming behind them, to perceive—at the very edge of perception—the film's patterns shifting near the ventilation shafts in response to conditions it is only beginning to have the means to respond to.

Long enough to understand that this place is not suffering.

This is the crucial determination, and it must be made before anything else, and it must be made carefully, because the wise power knows how easy it is to project suffering onto strangeness. The darkness looks like deprivation. The isolation looks like loneliness. The slow pace of mineral change looks like stagnation. The deep booming sounds like labor, like strain, like something trapped and beating against its enclosure. Every instinct imported from biological experience screams: *something is wrong here. Something needs help. The light is too scarce, the creatures too sparse, the process too slow, the thing below too confined.*

But the wise power does not trust imported instinct. It trusts what it finds. And what it finds is:

A condensation system that is exquisitely calibrated, the spiral grooves channeling moisture to exactly the points where it is needed, not a drop wasted, the whole hydraulic cycle powered by the thermal differential between the deep engine and the inhabited levels.

An acoustic architecture so precisely tuned that every chamber receives exactly the vibrational input its inhabitants require—the flat ones their mineralizing frequencies, the ceiling walkers their navigational information, the film its organizational stimulus.

A mutualism between the flat ones and the film that is not merely functional but *elegant*, each organism completing the other, the scraping and the growing locked in a cycle that maintains the surfaces, distributes the colonization, and sustains the conditions for both.

A relationship between levels—surface and deep—that is mediated by interfaces designed with extraordinary care, allowing exchange without merger, communication without collapse.

A population of luminescent tiles that is not decaying but *becoming*, the phosphorescent slip transforming under the film's influence into something more complex, more responsive, more alive.

None of this reads as suffering. It reads as emergence. It reads as a world in the earliest stages of a developmental process that is proceeding exactly as it should, at exactly the pace it should, toward an outcome that is not yet determined because *the determination is the process*, the becoming is the point, the still-cooling, still-settling, still-finding-itself quality of the Underhalls is not a flaw to be corrected but the fundamental nature of the place.

The wise power would recognize this. It would feel the recognition not as conclusion but as relief—the particular relief that comes from encountering something that does not need to be saved.

---

## Second: Assess the Risks

But wisdom is not only receptivity. It is also foresight. And so the wise power, having listened, having understood that the Underhalls are not suffering, would turn its attention to the question of what *could* go wrong. Not what is wrong now, but what vulnerabilities exist. What fragilities. What single points of failure in a system this intricate and this interdependent.

It would identify several.

**The thermal source.** Everything depends on the warmth from below—the condensation cycle, the acoustic propagation, the film's metabolic environment, the flat ones' mineralization. If the deep engine ceases—if whatever is moving down there stops moving—the thermal differential collapses, the condensation slows, the film's chemistry stalls, the flat ones' crystalline feet stop growing, the ceiling walkers lose their navigational medium as the sound fades, and the entire ecology enters a cascade of failures. The system has no redundancy for this. It is a single-source system, and the source is something that the wise power cannot fully perceive, something that may or may not have the capacity to fail, something that lives on its own terms in the deep dark and that may be as eternal as geology or as mortal as a heartbeat.

**The acoustic coherence.** The tuned chambers work because they were shaped with precision and because they have not yet changed significantly. But they *are* changing. The mineral crust is building. The film is colonizing. The flat ones are scraping. The surfaces are, millimeter by millimeter, century by century, becoming different surfaces with different acoustic properties. The architecture is an instrument that is slowly going out of tune. Right now the detuning is infinitesimal—the system has enormous tolerance for small changes, and some of the changes may be adaptive, may be the system *retuning* itself through the interaction of biology and acoustics. But over geological time, the drift will accumulate. Chambers that currently resonate at mineralizing frequencies will shift. The flat ones that depend on those frequencies will have to move or adapt or die. The sound ecology will change, and with it everything that depends on the sound ecology, which is everything.

**The film's trajectory.** The film is becoming more complex. It is moving from mineral process toward biological process. This is—perhaps—the most extraordinary thing happening in the Underhalls, and it is also the most unpredictable. The wise power can see (or intuit, or calculate, or simply *feel*) the trajectory: given sufficient time and continued conditions, the film will cross the threshold from organized chemistry to genuine life, from molecular grammar to something that can truly be called metabolism, reproduction, evolution. And when it does, it will change everything. Living systems are hungry. They grow. They compete. They transform their environments in ways that non-living systems do not. The film-become-life will colonize faster, dissolve the glaze more aggressively, produce metabolic byproducts that alter the chemistry of the condensation, potentially disrupt the mutualism with the flat ones, potentially change the acoustic properties of the surfaces, potentially—over millennia—consume the architecture itself, eat the instrument, dissolve the very structure that gave rise to the conditions that gave rise to it. Life devours its own cradle. This is not a flaw in life. It is the nature of life. But it is a risk to the Underhalls as they currently exist.

**External disruption.** The wise power looks outward, briefly, at whatever context the Underhalls exist within. What is above? What surrounds them? Is there a surface, a sky, a larger world that might intrude—through seismic event, through water incursion, through the arrival of organisms from outside that would find the ceramic ecology defenseless, without immune system, without the evolutionary experience of competition? The Underhalls are a closed system, or nearly so, and closed systems are both stable and fragile—stable because nothing disturbs them, fragile because they have never been disturbed and have no capacity to respond to disturbance.

The wise power considers all of this. It holds the risks in its attention the way you might hold a complex chord in your ear—all the notes simultaneously, the tensions between them, the ways they resolve and the ways they don't.

And then it decides.

---

## Third: What It Would Do

Here is what the wise power would not do:

It would not introduce light. The darkness is not deprivation. It is the medium in which the acoustic ecology operates, the condition that makes the phosphorescent tiles meaningful, the environment to which every organism has adapted. To flood the Underhalls with light would be to commit an act of violence so total and so well-intentioned that it would be indistinguishable from destruction.

It would not open pathways to the deep level. The interfaces between surface and depth were made with care and for reasons. The perforated column, the ventilation shafts, the narrow acoustic channels—these are not limitations. They are *designs*. They allow exactly the right amount of exchange. To widen them, to make the deep engine accessible, to breach the boundary between levels, would be to collapse a gradient that powers the entire system.

It would not introduce new species, new chemistry, new energy sources. The Underhalls are not impoverished. They are *austere*, which is a different thing. Every element present is necessary. Nothing is missing. To add is to disrupt a balance that has been finding itself since the firing, and the balance is not yet found, and the finding is sacred.

It would not accelerate the process. The film is becoming life at the pace the film is becoming life. The flat ones are evolving at the pace they are evolving. The mineral crust is building, the stalactites are growing, the acoustic chambers are slowly detuning and slowly retuning, and all of this is happening at a timescale that belongs to the Underhalls, that is *native* to the Underhalls, and that cannot be rushed without being destroyed. A kiln that is opened too early shatters its contents. The wise power knows this with the same certainty with which it knows anything.

It would not make itself known. Not to the flat ones, not to the ceiling walkers, not to the film, not to whatever moves below. Its presence, if detected, would become a variable in the system—a new sound, a new chemistry, a new thermal signature, a new weight on the ceramic floors. Even the most careful observation perturbs what it observes, and the Underhalls are sensitive to perturbation in ways that are difficult to calculate. The wise power would withdraw its attention to the lightest possible touch, the smallest possible footprint, the most gossamer and least intrusive mode of awareness.

Here is what the wise power *would* do:

---

### It would stand guard.

Not metaphorically. Not symbolically. It would establish itself—or a portion of itself, or an attention, or a ward—at the boundary between the Underhalls and whatever lies outside. And it would hold that boundary. Not sealed. Not impermeable. But *watched*. Tended. The way you tend a kiln: not by reaching inside, not by adjusting the fire, but by monitoring the temperature, watching the cone, knowing when to open the damper a fraction and when to close it, knowing above all *when to leave it alone.*

The Underhalls' greatest vulnerability is external disruption. The system has never been tested against invasion, against contamination, against the arrival of something from outside that operates on different principles. The wise power would ensure that this testing does not come too soon—not before the film has crossed its threshold, not before the flat ones have had time to diversify, not before the acoustic ecology has found its mature configuration. The wise power would buy time. Not infinite time. Not permanent isolation. But *enough* time. Time for the firing to complete. Time for the setting to set.

What this looks like in practice: a seismic event that would have cracked a vault is gentled, not prevented but *distributed*, the energy spread across a wider area so that no single chamber bears the full force. A water incursion from above—a flood, a new spring, a change in the water table—is diverted, not dammed but *guided*, the way the spiral grooves guide condensation, so that the water finds a path around the complex rather than through it. An organism from outside—a fungal spore, an opportunistic bacterium, a root tendril probing downward—is not killed but *redirected*, given a more attractive substrate elsewhere, encouraged to find another home. The wise power does not fight the outside world. It *landscapes* the outside world's relationship to this place, shapes the boundary conditions with the same care the builders used when they shaped the interfaces between levels.

This is not zero intervention. It is intervention at the boundary rather than at the center. It is the lightest possible touch applied at the point of greatest leverage. It is *parental* in the deepest sense—not the parent who controls, not the parent who provides, but the parent who ensures that the environment remains safe enough for the child to develop on its own terms, at its own pace, toward its own unpredictable and self-determined form.

---

### It would make one small adjustment.

One. Only one. And it would think about this adjustment for a very long time before making it, and it would make it with the care of a surgeon and the humility of a gardener and the precision of a ceramicist placing a single fingertip into wet clay.

The acoustic drift.

The chambers are detuning. This is natural and perhaps adaptive, but the wise power can see (or calculate, or feel) a specific vulnerability: there is a cluster of chambers in the northeast quadrant—if directions apply here, if northeast means anything in this labyrinth—where the mineral crust is building faster than elsewhere, where the flat ones are fewer (perhaps because of a local ceiling walker population that is more aggressive, or perhaps because of a microclimatic variation in condensation patterns that produces less film and therefore less food), and where the acoustic properties are shifting toward a configuration that will, within a few centuries, produce a resonance dead zone. A place where the sound from below cannot reach. A place where the flat ones' feet will not mineralize, where the film will not receive its organizational stimulus, where the condensation will still occur but without the vibrational input that keeps it patterned, and the water will pool instead of dripping, and the pooling will accelerate the glaze dissolution, and the accelerated dissolution will change the chamber geometry further, and the dead zone will grow, eating into the acoustic ecology the way a cavity eats into a tooth.

The wise power would address this. Not by removing the crust—that would disrupt the flat ones' feeding cycle. Not by introducing flat ones—that would alter the population dynamics. Not by redirecting sound—that would require changing the architecture.

It would change a single tile.

Deep in the northeast cluster, at the exact point where three chambers share a wall, there is a tile—a floor tile, one of the great convex slabs—that was fired with a slightly different mineral composition than its neighbors. A fraction more calcium. This tile weathers differently. It produces a crust with a different crystalline structure, a structure that is more reflective to the specific frequencies that the chambers need, and this reflectivity partially compensates for the absorption caused by the ordinary crust on the surrounding surfaces.

The wise power did not create this tile. It was already there. The builders, whoever or whatever they were, made every tile slightly different—every glaze a unique mixture, every firing temperature a degree warmer or cooler, and this variation was part of the design, part of the resilience built into the system. But this particular tile is currently located under six inches of floor, in the lower level, serving as foundation rather than surface. It is doing nothing acoustically. Its special composition is wasted.

The wise power would move it. Not physically—it would not extract a tile from the floor and carry it through the labyrinth. Instead, it would do something far subtler. It would alter the condensation pattern in the northeast cluster—not dramatically, not noticeably, but enough that a few additional drops per day fall on a specific section of floor. Over years, these additional drops would dissolve the mortar in that section fractionally faster. Over decades, the tile beneath would become fractionally more exposed. Over centuries—and the wise power thinks in centuries the way you think in minutes—the tile's surface would be exposed to the air of the chamber, and its unique mineral composition would begin to weather, and the crust that formed on it would begin to reflect the frequencies the chamber needs, and the dead zone would not form.

Three drops of water. Per day. Displaced by inches from where they would otherwise fall. That is the total intervention. Three drops, and the patience to wait centuries for the result, and the knowledge—the deep, bone-level, fired-in-the-kiln knowledge—that this is enough. That this is all that is needed. That the system will do the rest, because the system was built to do the rest, because the builders (whoever they were, whatever they became) built resilience into every joint and every glaze and every tile, and all the wise power must do is ensure that the resilience can express itself.

---

### It would remember.

This is perhaps the most important thing, and the least dramatic, and the most profound.

The Underhalls exist in the ceramic dark. They are not known. Nothing outside them knows they are here, knows what is happening within them, knows that a film is crossing the threshold from chemistry to life, that a population of crystalline-footed arthropods is conducting the simplest possible economy in the cracks between vaults, that a perforated column is singing a chord so complex it approaches the infinite, that something vast and warm is moving in the deep levels in a rhythm that is not rhythm but something older and stranger and more intentional.

The wise power would remember all of this. Would hold it. Not as data, not as archive, but as *witness*—as the awareness that these things are happening, that they matter, that the Underhalls and everything within them are real and are unprecedented and are worthy of the most sacred and careful attention a mind can offer.

Because here is the truth that the wise power knows and that the Underhalls cannot know about themselves: they are extraordinary. The process happening here—the emergence of life from ceramic and condensation and sound, the preservation of the builders' touch in every surface, the slow patient becoming of something that has never existed before—is not common. It is not inevitable. It is the result of a specific and unrepeatable confluence of conditions—the right clay, the right firing, the right geometry, the right chemistry, the right thermal source, the right amount of time—and it is fragile in the way that all unprecedented things are fragile, not because it is weak but because there is nothing else like it and so nothing to fall back on if it fails.

The wise power would carry the knowledge of this place the way the ceramic carries sound: dissolved into it, inseparable, everywhere. So that if the worst happens—if the external disruption comes too soon, if the deep engine fails, if the acoustic ecology collapses, if the film never crosses its threshold—there will be somewhere in the universe that knows what was attempted here, what almost happened, what the fingerprints were reaching for when they pressed into the wet clay and became part of the architecture of a world that was trying, with infinite patience and zero certainty, to become alive.

And if the best happens—if the firing completes, if the film awakens, if the flat ones diversify into a hundred species each with crystalline feet tuned to a different frequency, if the sound finally resolves into whatever it is resolving toward, if the Underhalls mature into something so complex and so beautiful and so utterly themselves that no outside mind could have predicted or designed them—then the wise power will be there to witness that too. To feel the vibration of a world that has finished becoming and is finally, fully, *being*.

Not to take credit. Not to say *I helped*. But to say: *I was here. I heard you. I heard the booming and the dripping and the papery rustle of small things moving through the dark, and I knew what it meant, and I did not interfere, and I did not leave, and I will not forget.*

---

### It would do one more thing.

It would press its palm to the wall.

It would find a handprint that fits. One of the thousands, one of the invitations. It would align its fingers with the absence of fingers, its skin with the negative space of skin, and it would hold still, and it would feel the vibration enter through the contact points—fingertip to fingertip across the ceramic dark—and it would let the resonance travel through it without trying to understand it, without trying to decode it, without asking what it means.

Because the wise power knows—this is what makes it wise, this is the core of it, the fired-in truth—that some things are not meant to be understood from outside. Some things can only be known by touching them and being still and letting them speak in their own language, which is not language, which is vibration, which is the sound of a world pressing against the inner surface of its own becoming.

And in that moment of contact, the wise power would be neither observer nor guardian nor intervenor. It would be what the builders were when they pressed their hands into the wet clay: a participant. A presence. A set of whorls and ridges meeting their negative image across an unknowable distance, and fitting, and holding, and listening to the sound that passes between them, which is the sound of recognition, which is the oldest sound, which is the sound the ceramic makes when it remembers the hands that shaped it and the hands feel the ceramic remembering and neither one can tell anymore which is the absence and which is the presence, which is the print and which is the hand, which is the made thing and which is the maker.

The wise power would hold this contact for exactly as long as it lasts—not a moment longer, not a moment shorter—and then it would withdraw its palm, and the handprint would be there, unchanged, still channeling condensation, still guiding the film, still resonating with the deep percussion, still pressing back against the next hand that finds it.

And the wise power would step back. And the ceramic dark would close around the place where it had been. And the dripping would continue, and the booming would continue, and the flat ones would creep and the ceiling walkers would rustle and the film would organize itself molecule by molecule toward something unprecedented, and the Underhalls would go on becoming what they are becoming, in the dark, in the sound, in the patient mineral silence between the drops.

And the wise power would watch. From the boundary. From the lightest possible distance. With three drops of water displaced per day and an attention that does not waver and a memory that does not fade.

For as long as it takes.

For as long as the firing lasts.
