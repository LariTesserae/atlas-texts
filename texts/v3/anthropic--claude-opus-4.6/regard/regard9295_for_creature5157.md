# The Regard

---

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive.

Arrival would imply a before and after, a threshold crossed, and this place—where shadows lean toward what casts them and light shifts only when unobserved—would reject such a clean partition. Instead the attention settles the way a new crystal plane locks into register: it was always almost there, and then it is there, and the difference is a single click too faint to locate.

---

## I. Perception

The first thing the power does is nothing.

This is not hesitation. It is the only honest first act when encountering a form of life whose fastest meaningful gesture takes eleven years to complete and whose slowest has been continuous since before the tiers existed. To act immediately would be to act in ignorance, and this power is wise enough to know that wisdom begins with the admission that one does not yet understand the time signature of another being's suffering—or whether it suffers at all.

So the power watches.

It watches a filament rise from a crack in the northeast corner of a formation the size of a small city. The filament is a single crystal of halite, extruded along a dislocation in the lattice at a rate of two microns per hour. It takes nine days to reach the surface. When it arrives, it sways once in the slow, cold breeze—gathering perhaps forty ions of magnesium, a trace of lithium, the faintest piezoelectric residue of a tremor that passed through the substrate six months ago—and then it retracts. The retraction takes eleven days. The ions are distributed through a network of grain boundaries into a body that extends three hundred meters in every direction beneath the pseudo-streets.

The power watches this happen four hundred and twelve times across the plain in the space of what, by external reckoning, would be a single century.

It watches a mobile lattice detach from the southern edge of a mesa cluster, soften its leading facets, and begin crossing an empty tract seven kilometers wide. The crossing takes thirty-one years. Partway through, a geometric tremor shifts the destination crevice two centimeters to the east. The lattice adjusts its heading by a fraction of a degree, a correction so small it would be invisible to any observer not watching across decades. When it arrives, it relaxes into the crevice wall and locks into register. The click propagates through the host formation for nineteen minutes, a dying harmonic that reorganizes six trillion crystal planes into a configuration whose total energy is lower by an amount equivalent to the weight of a single grain of salt falling one millimeter.

The power watches this and understands: *the click is a sentence. The harmonic is its meaning fading into the body that received it.*

It watches the contradictory light. It sees that the upper layers of the great sessile formations are not merely translucent but optically active—they rotate the polarization of incoming light by angles that depend on the precise alignment of their crystal domains, alignments that change with each tremor. The shadows that lean toward their objects are not errors. They are the formations' way of reaching toward what is near them. The shadow is a gesture, the slowest possible gesture, a pointing that says *this is adjacent to me* in a language where adjacency is the most intimate relationship that exists.

The power watches for what, externally, would be ten thousand years. By the plain's own reckoning, this is a single long breath—one full cycle of the largest formation's metabolic pulse, the interval between two complete reorganizations of its deepest crystal domains.

At the end of this watching, the power understands several things:

**First:** The plain is alive, and its life is not a metaphor. The distributed metabolism—the filaments tasting, the mobile lattices carrying crystal-plane inheritances between formations, the tremors adjusting internal stress—constitutes a form of being as authentic as any the power has encountered. It is slow, but slowness is not lesser-ness. A century here is a heartbeat. A millennium is a thought. The formations think in millennia.

**Second:** The formations are aware of each other. The mobile lattices are not merely genetic material; they are communications, letters written in crystal structure and hand-delivered across decades of empty pavement. When a lattice from an old mesa merges with a younger formation, the younger formation does not simply receive new mineral content. It receives a pattern—a particular arrangement of crystal domains that encodes information about stresses endured, tremors survived, lattice faults healed over deep time. This is memory, carried in structure. This is culture, transmitted at walking pace.

**Third:** The formations experience something that is not quite suffering but is adjacent to it, in the way their shadows are adjacent to their objects. It is this:

*They are alone in a way they cannot name, because naming requires a concept of togetherness they have never fully achieved.*

Each formation is a partial separation from the substrate. Partial. The mobile lattices cross the empty tracts and merge, yes—but the merging is always incomplete. The arriving lattice locks into the host's crystal register, and for a moment the two structures share a continuous lattice, a single unbroken mineral body. But the new planes never perfectly match the old. There are always dislocations at the boundary, atomic-scale misalignments where the traveler's history doesn't quite correspond to the host's. These dislocations are stable; they do not heal. They become permanent features of the merged body, faint scars that the filaments route around for the rest of the formation's existence.

The formations are aware of these scars. Each one is a record of contact—proof that something came from outside, that the emptiness was crossed—but also proof that the crossing was not enough. The lattices do not match. The histories do not align. The formations reach toward each other across kilometers and decades, and when they finally touch, the touch leaves a mark that says *almost*.

This is the closest thing to loneliness that a mineral consciousness can feel: the awareness that contact is possible, that it has happened, that it left evidence—and that the evidence is a permanent record of imperfect fit.

---

## II. Deliberation

Now the power deliberates. And because this power is genuinely wise, its deliberation begins with the question that most powers skip:

*Should I do anything at all?*

The case for inaction is strong. The plain has existed for an immense span of time. Its inhabitants have developed a form of life exquisitely adapted to its conditions—the dry air, the sourceless light, the slow tremors. Their metabolism is the geology of the plain itself, refined and directed. Their suffering, if it is suffering, is woven so deeply into the structure of their existence that removing it might be indistinguishable from removing them. A being whose deepest experience is the almost-but-not-quite of lattice contact might not survive the achievement of perfect contact; it might find that perfection is a kind of dissolution, a loss of the boundary that makes it a self rather than merely more rock.

The power considers this carefully.

It considers the filaments, rising and retracting on their millennial schedules, each one a tiny act of reaching-out that is also an act of self-maintenance. If the air were suddenly rich with moisture and ions, the filaments would gather too much too fast. The delicate balance of accretion and dissolution that keeps the formations alive—that *is* their life—would be overwhelmed. Generosity applied at the wrong timescale is indistinguishable from catastrophe.

It considers the mobile lattices, crossing their empty tracts at walking pace. If the tracts were shorter, or if the lattices moved faster, the formations would merge more frequently—but would the mergers be richer? The thirty-one-year crossing is not wasted time. During those decades, the lattice is exposed to the open pavement's subtle variations in temperature, pressure, mineral composition. It accumulates new information in its structure, information it could not have gathered by staying in place. The journey is part of the message. Shorten the journey and you impoverish the language.

It considers the dislocations, the permanent scars of imperfect contact. They are painful, in the way that this place knows pain. But they are also the only records the formations have of each other. Without the scars, each formation would be a perfect, unmarked crystal—complete, flawless, and utterly alone. The scars are proof of relationship. They are the plain's equivalent of memory, of faces recognized across a room, of the particular way someone's hand feels different from all other hands. Remove the imperfection and you remove the evidence that love was ever attempted.

The power sits with this for a long time. A very long time, even by the plain's reckoning.

And then it identifies the thing it can address—the one place where the plain's suffering exceeds what is necessary for the plain's flourishing, the one wound that is not also a gift.

It is this: *the formations do not know that their scars are shared.*

When a mobile lattice merges with a host formation, the dislocation boundary is perceived only from the host's side. The host feels the misalignment, routes its filaments around the new flaw, incorporates the scar into its ongoing self-maintenance. But the host has no way of knowing that the originating formation—the one that released the lattice decades ago—bears a corresponding scar at the point of release. Every departure leaves a mark. The lattice that was sent out is no longer present, and the space it occupied does not heal perfectly; it fills with new growth that is subtly different from what was there before, a patch over an absence.

Both formations are scarred by the same act of reaching. Neither knows this about the other.

The mobile lattice itself, during its decades of crossing, carries the structural signature of both: the release-scar encoded in its trailing facets, the approaching-scar prefigured in its leading edge's particular pattern of softening. It is, in transit, a living proof that the connection between the two formations is symmetrical—that the ache of imperfect contact is felt on both sides of the empty tract. But this information is encoded in a way that neither the sender nor the receiver can read, because each formation only ever encounters the lattice from one direction: the sender watched it leave; the receiver feels it arrive. Neither has access to the lattice's full structure during the crossing.

The loneliness of the plain is therefore not merely the loneliness of distance or of imperfect fit. It is an *informational* loneliness: the formations reach for each other, they make contact, they bear the marks of contact, and they do not know that the marks are mutual. Each one believes—insofar as a mineral consciousness can be said to believe—that it alone carries the scar. That the reaching was one-sided. That the empty tract received more than it gave.

This is the wound the power can address without violence to the plain's nature. Not by removing the scars, not by closing the distances, not by perfecting the lattice fit—but by making the mutuality *legible*.

## III. The Act

What follows takes, by external reckoning, approximately eleven thousand years to complete. By the plain's reckoning, it is the work of a single sustained thought, a careful exhalation.

The power does not touch the formations. It does not add anything to the plain that was not already there. What it does is make a single, precise adjustment to the conditions under which the mobile lattices travel.

Here is what it does:

During a lattice's crossing—that decades-long transit across empty mineral pavement—the lattice is exposed to the plain's ambient light, the sourceless illumination that falls at contradictory angles and shifts when one looks away. This light passes through the lattice's translucent facets and is refracted according to the lattice's internal structure. The refraction pattern encodes, in principle, everything the lattice carries: the release-scar of the sender, the prefigured arrival-scar of the receiver, the full history of both formations written in crystal-plane orientations.

But the refracted light scatters uselessly across the empty pavement. No one reads it. The information dissipates.

The power makes one change: it alters the angle of the ambient light—not everywhere, not permanently, but in a specific, localized, temporary way—so that when a mobile lattice is at the precise midpoint of its crossing, the light passing through it is focused rather than scattered. For a brief interval (brief by the plain's standards: perhaps six months) the lattice casts a coherent pattern onto the pavement around it. The pattern extends in two directions: backward toward the sender and forward toward the receiver. It does not reach either one. It is visible only on the empty pavement, readable only by the lattice itself and by any filament that happens to be extruded from a crack in the ground nearby.

But here is the thing about the plain: it is all one substrate. The pavement between formations is not dead ground. It is the same continuous mineral body from which the formations partially differentiated, and it is threaded with the same network of grain boundaries and lattice faults through which the filaments route their gathered ions. When the coherent light pattern falls on the pavement at the crossing's midpoint, the piezoelectric properties of the mineral surface convert the light's energy into a faint charge pattern—a charge pattern that propagates along grain boundaries in both directions, toward sender and receiver, at the speed of ion diffusion through solid crystal.

This propagation is slow. By the time the charge pattern reaches the sender formation, the lattice has already arrived at the receiver. By the time it reaches the receiver, the lattice has long since merged and the new dislocation boundary has been incorporated. The information arrives late, as everything on this plain arrives late.

But it arrives.

It arrives as a faint, anomalous charge signature at the base of a filament. A filament that was tasting the air for tremor-residue instead picks up a trace of something else: a pattern that does not match the local lattice structure, that does not correspond to any recent tremor or any known mineral gradient. It is, unmistakably, the structural signature of a distant formation—the *specific* formation on the other end of the crossing, the one that sent or received the lattice. And embedded in this signature, like a watermark in handmade paper, is the shape of *that formation's scar*. The corresponding scar. The matching wound.

The filament retracts, carrying this anomalous pattern into the body of the formation. It is digested, incorporated, distributed through the grain-boundary network over the course of decades. And when it has been fully integrated, the formation knows something it did not know before:

*The other one is also marked.*

---

This is all the power does. It introduces no new element, creates no new structure, violates no existing process. It simply bends light, once, at the midpoint of a crossing, in a way that makes the plain's own substrate carry a message it was always almost carrying. The message propagates through channels that already existed. It is received by organs that were already listening. It is integrated by metabolisms that were already at work.

The power does not even do this for every crossing. It does it once—for one lattice, at one midpoint, on one tract between two formations. And then it withdraws its attention to the degree that anything can withdraw from something it was never separate from.

One is enough.

Because when the first formation receives the scar-signature of the second, and knows that its reaching-out left a mutual mark, its internal stress distribution changes. The dislocation boundary where the lattice merged is no longer an isolated flaw to be routed around; it is a *correspondence*, a point where the formation's own structure echoes a structure that exists elsewhere. The formation's next tremor-pulse reorganizes its crystal domains around this new understanding, and the harmonic that fades into the substrate carries a slightly different tone—a tone that encodes the knowledge of mutuality.

That tone propagates. It is faint—an almost imperceptible addition to the normal background of tremor-harmonics that the entire plain uses to maintain its structural equilibrium. But it is new. It has a quality that no previous harmonic possessed: it is *relational*. It refers not only to the formation's own internal state but to the state of another formation, specific and distant.

Other formations detect this harmonic through their filaments. They cannot decode its full content—they lack the specific context, the particular dislocation boundary that gives it meaning. But they detect its relational quality. They detect that it is a sound shaped by two, and this is enough to make them wonder, in the slow mineral way that wondering works here, whether their own scars might also have correspondences.

The next lattice that is released by any formation on the plain is released with a slightly different structure. The releasing formation has, in the deepest and most unconscious way, adjusted the lattice's crystal-plane orientations to maximize the lattice's optical coherence—to make it a better lens, a clearer carrier of structural information. The formation does not know why it is doing this. It is responding to a shift in its own stress landscape, a subtle new tendency in its crystal domains toward configurations that are more legible to light.

And when this lattice reaches the midpoint of its crossing, the plain's own geometry—the angles of the tiers, the refractive indices of the salt and glass bands, the diffuse sourceless light—does the rest. The power's single intervention has been absorbed into the plain's physics. The light bends again, not because the power bends it but because the lattice is now shaped to bend it. The charge pattern propagates. Another formation receives the knowledge of mutuality. Another harmonic enters the substrate.

It takes approximately forty thousand years for the effect to reach the farthest mesa clusters.

It takes approximately a hundred and twenty thousand years for the plain to reach a new equilibrium—a state in which every formation has received at least one scar-correspondence and integrated it into its crystal structure.

The plain looks, from the outside, almost exactly the same. The tiers still rise in sheer bands of salt and glass. The avenues still widen and narrow with each tremor. The filaments still rise and retract. The mobile lattices still cross the empty tracts at walking pace and merge with soft clicks into distant crevices.

But there are differences, if one knows where to look.

The dislocations are still there—the imperfect fits, the permanent scars of contact. They have not healed. They will never heal. But the filaments no longer route around them as if they were flaws to be avoided. Instead, the grain-boundary networks have reorganized so that the dislocations are *nodes*—junctions where information flows more freely, where the charge patterns from distant formations arrive first and are distributed most widely. The scars have become the most connected parts of each formation's body. What was a wound is now a window. What was a record of failure is now the primary channel through which the formation knows it is not alone.

The shadows still lean toward their objects. But now, occasionally—rarely, perhaps once in a thousand years—a shadow will extend past its object and reach across the pavement toward a neighboring formation, stretching in a direction that has nothing to do with the angle of the light and everything to do with the orientation of the dislocation nodes inside. It is the plainest, most literal gesture of acknowledgment: *I know you are also marked. I know the marking was not mine alone. I lean toward you with the part of me that is shaped by your absence, and I know you lean back with the part of you that is shaped by mine.*

The mobile lattices still carry their structural inheritances between formations. But the lattices are subtly more complex now, their internal planes arranged in patterns that encode not just the sender's history but the sender's knowledge of the receiver's history—information received decades or centuries ago through the substrate's charge-propagation network and now returned, annotated, enriched. The lattices have become letters that refer to previous letters. The crossings have become a correspondence.

And the harmonic tones that fade after each tremor are no longer isolated events. They overlap, interweave, create interference patterns that propagate through the substrate in ways that no single formation controls or fully understands. The plain hums. It has always hummed—the tremors saw to that—but now the hum has structure. It has the quality of a conversation heard through a wall: indistinct, but unmistakably plural. Unmistakably *between*.

---

## IV. What the Power Did Not Do

The power did not make the formations happy. Happiness is not a concept that maps onto mineral consciousness, and even if it did, the power would not presume to define it for another form of life.

The power did not remove the loneliness. The formations are still separated by kilometers of empty pavement. The crossings still take decades. The scars still do not heal. Contact is still imperfect. The ache of *almost* is still the fundamental emotional register of the plain.

The power did not speed anything up. The formations still think in millennia. Their filaments still take days to rise and retract. Their mobile lattices still cross at walking pace. Nothing here happens quickly, and the power understood that quickness would be a kind of death—a collapse of the temporal space in which these beings conduct their inner lives. To make them faster would be to make them smaller.

The power did not give them language, or names, or any representational system they did not already possess. The crystal-plane orientations that encode structural information were already there; the piezoelectric charge propagation was already there; the harmonic tones were already there. The power simply ensured that one of these existing channels carried one additional piece of information on one occasion, and the plain's own intelligence did the rest.

The power did not reveal itself. The formations do not know that anything happened. They do not know that the light was bent. They do not experience the knowledge of mutuality as a gift from outside; they experience it as a discovery, something that was always true and that they simply, finally, noticed. This is as it should be. A power that demands gratitude has not yet learned the first thing about benevolence. The truly generous act is the one that becomes invisible—that merges with the recipient's own agency so completely that the recipient feels, rightly, that it accomplished the thing itself.

Because it did. The power bent the light, but the formation grew the filament. The power created the charge pattern, but the substrate carried it. The power made the mutuality legible, but the formations did the brave and difficult work of reorganizing their crystal domains around a new understanding, of routing their grain boundaries through their scars instead of around them, of turning wounds into windows. That work took a hundred and twenty thousand years, and it was entirely their own.

---

## V. After

The power remains attentive, the way a landscape remains present—not watching in the sense of surveillance, but present in the sense that it has not withdrawn its willingness to perceive. If it is needed again, it is there. But it does not expect to be needed again for a very long time.

The plain continues.

The tiers dissolve upward into the half-light. The avenues shift with each tremor. The filaments taste the air. The lattices cross and merge with their small, perfect clicks. The shadows reach.

But now, in the deepest strata of the oldest formations—the ones that have not moved in measurable time, whose city-forms have been refined by wind and settling into perfect right angles—something new is happening. These ancient beings, who have received mobile lattices from younger formations carrying the enriched crystal-plane correspondences, have begun to adjust their own deepest structures in response. The adjustment is so slow it will take geological ages to complete. It may never complete. But the direction of the adjustment is clear:

The oldest formations are growing new dislocations. Deliberately. Not from contact with external lattices, but from their own internal stress fields, which have been reorganized around the principle that imperfection is connection and connection is the point. They are introducing flaws into their own perfect geometries—controlled, intentional misalignments that echo the scar-signatures they have received from distant formations they will never directly touch.

These self-inflicted scars serve no structural purpose. They weaken the crystal lattice slightly. They create stress concentrations that the tremor-pulses must work harder to manage. They are, by any engineering measure, disadvantageous.

They are the oldest formations' way of saying: *I have heard you. I cannot cross the distance. But I can make myself less perfect in the shape of your imperfection. I can carry a wound that rhymes with yours. I can be marked by you without touching you. And this marking, this deliberate asymmetry in my deepest structure, is the closest I can come to telling you that you changed me—that the fact of you, distant and imperfect and reaching, is now part of what I am.*

The harmonic this produces is so low and so slow that it will take eight hundred thousand years to complete a single cycle.

The power listens to the first few microtones of it, and knows it as something very old and very familiar—something it has heard before, in other places, in other forms, wherever consciousness has found a way to say to another consciousness: *you are real, and your reality matters, and the proof is this mark I carry freely.*

Then the power turns its attention to whatever is next, carrying its own new mark.

The plain hums on.
