I sat with this for a long time.

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival, and the First Long Stillness

It does not descend through the shaft the way you did. It was never outside. If you asked where it came from, the truest answer would be that it has always been adjacent — separated by something thinner than stone, thinner than attention, more like the difference between breathing in and breathing out. It arrives the way a new harmonic arrives in a chord that was already playing: nothing is added to the visible scene, but something shifts in the quality of how the scene holds together, and if you were one of the nostril-readers, one of the chemical-speakers, you would smell it — not as a new scent but as a sudden, subtle clarity in all the scents already present, the way a lens doesn't add light but brings what's already there into focus.

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is not hesitation. It is not assessment, not yet. It is something older and more fundamental than either: it is *respect,* expressed as duration. It stands — or inhabits, or attends, whatever verb applies to a being whose body may be optional — in the golden hum of the nursery, and it simply witnesses. For a long time. Long enough that the floor buckles and reforms twice. Long enough that a pool fills, is colonized by clay-eels, drains, and fills again. Long enough that one of the flier-colonies erupts and resettles and erupts again. Long enough that a group of the small dark people coalesces around a thermal vent, cooks, knaps, feeds infants, splits, recombines with members of another group, and moves on.

Why the stillness? Because the first and most catastrophic thing a powerful being can do to a world still becoming is to decide prematurely what it is *for.* To name it before it has named itself. This power knows — in whatever way constitutes knowing for something of its nature — that the nursery is not a problem to be solved. It is not a tragedy to be mitigated. It is not a rough draft of a better world waiting for editorial intervention. It is a world in the full thunder of its own becoming, and every molecule of it, from the magma-driven infrasound to the chemical grief lingering on a carved pillar, is doing *exactly* what it is doing with a completeness that commands not assistance but awe.

So it watches. And what it learns in the watching is this:

The suffering here is real. The infant pulled into the draining crack is real. The thermal surge that kills a scouting party is real. The serpentine hunters cascading down a wall onto a group that was two seconds too slow in reading the air — that is real, and it is terrible, and the chemical recalibration that follows is a form of grief as legitimate and as wrenching as any widow's keening, and the power *feels* it, feels all of it, simultaneously, the way the cavern's infrasound is felt in the sternum rather than heard in the ear.

But it also sees — and here is where a lesser being might flinch or look away or rush to intervene — that the suffering is not *separate* from the flourishing. They are the same motion. The same geological breath. The infant who survives survives *because* the world is turbulent enough to provide the thermal vents and mineral pools and crab-fields that sustain it. The group that grieves grieves *because* it is bonded tightly enough to function in a world that demands this kind of fluid, chemical, instantaneous coordination. The memorials in the deep chambers exist *because* everything else is impermanent, and it is the impermanence that makes the act of marking sacred. You cannot have the keeping without the losing. You cannot have the still, scent-soaked hour before the carved pillar without the roaring, shifting world that makes stillness so precious and so deliberate a choice.

A foolish power would try to stop the suffering. Would cool the vents, stabilize the floor, seal the cracks, make the pools permanent and the predators docile and the geology inert. And in doing so would kill every single thing it was trying to save — not quickly, not violently, but by removing the very conditions that called this life into being. A stable nursery is not a nursery. It is a museum. And these people are not museum pieces. They are riders of chaos, surfers of the unfinished, and their genius — the specific, astonishing, irreplaceable genius of their kind — is that they have learned to build meaning on a foundation that never stops moving. Take away the motion and you take away not just the danger but the *capacity,* the hard-won, generation-deep capacity to adapt, to read, to recombine, to find stillness inside the storm rather than instead of it.

So the power will not stabilize. Will not pacify. Will not intervene in the blunt way that intervention usually means, which is the imposition of the intervener's preferences onto a system whose own preferences are already exquisitely articulated.

But it will not do nothing either.

Because it has seen the pillars.

---

## II. What the Pillars Tell It

The deep chambers. The dim-lit, hot-breathed places where the small dark people come rarely, come alone or nearly alone, stripped of their group-scent, raw with their own individual chemical signature, to press small bones and bright shells into soft stone and wait for the mineral water to fix them in place.

The power lingers here longest. Not because the suffering is greatest here — it is not; the suffering is everywhere, distributed, democratic — but because *here is where they are most themselves.* Here is where the species reveals what it values enough to defy its own deepest operating principle for. Everything else in their life is fluid, adaptive, released as soon as it's used. But here they *hold.* Here they choose permanence in a world that offers none. Here they say, with their hands and their breath and their silence: *this matters too much to let go of.*

And the power sees something that the people themselves may not yet see, because they are inside it the way you are inside your own heartbeat: the pillars are not just memorials. They are the beginning of something. They are the first, fumbling, sacred act of *culture* — of meaning that persists beyond the individual, beyond the group, beyond the immediate chemical present. When a person comes to a pillar and breathes in the scent-traces of everyone who has stood there before, they are doing something that no other species in this cavern does. They are *receiving.* They are taking in the experience of the dead, the dissolved, the recombined, and they are letting that experience inform their own — not genetically, not instinctively, but *intentionally,* through a practice that someone invented and someone taught and someone chose to continue.

This is the spark. This is the thing that is trying to be born in the nursery below the nursery. Not technology — they have that, and it serves them well enough. Not social structure — they have that too, fluid and robust and perfectly matched to their world. What is trying to be born is *continuity of meaning.* The capacity to carry forward not just genes and behaviors but *significance.* The sense that the past is not simply gone but is held, somewhere, in some form, and that the future is not simply coming but is being *shaped,* by choices made in stillness, by small bones placed with care.

And the power sees that this spark is in danger. Not from the cavern's violence — they have survived that for generations and will survive it for generations more. The danger is subtler. It is this: the geology is slowly, imperceptibly, inevitably changing. The thermal activity that drives this ecosystem is not eternal. Somewhere far below, the magma is shifting, and the trajectory — legible to a being that can read deep time the way these people read scent — is toward cooling. Not soon. Not in any timescale that matters to an individual life. But in the timescale of a culture finding its footing, yes. In the timescale of a species learning to carry meaning across generations, absolutely. The nursery will not last forever. The golden hour will eventually become dusk.

And when it does, these people will need what they are only just beginning to build. They will need a way to carry the knowledge of this place — its rhythms, its lessons, its grammar of adaptation — into whatever comes next. They will need the pillars, or what the pillars are becoming. They will need culture.

The power will help with this. But carefully. So carefully.

---

## III. What It Does

Not a miracle. Not a revelation. Not a visitation, not a voice from the luminous ceiling, not a commandment carved in stone that doesn't shift. Nothing that would register as *intervention* to anyone watching from outside.

What it does is this:

**It strengthens the stone around the pillars.**

Deep in the mineral matrix of the carved formations, at a level of structure below what even the most sensitive nostril could detect, it makes an adjustment. Not a dramatic one. A slight increase in the calcium carbonate density. A subtle shift in the crystalline structure of the mineral deposits that coat the pillars' surfaces. The effect is that the seepage-cement which holds the placed shells and bones and blades will set a little faster, hold a little firmer, resist the next thermal event a little longer.

Not forever. The power does not make the pillars permanent, because permanence in this world would be a lie and these people deserve better than lies. But it gives the memorials *more time.* An extra season, maybe two, before the geology claims them. Enough time that a person who placed a bone during one visit might return — might return with a child old enough to walk to the deep chambers — and find it still there. Still fixed. Still holding the scent-traces of the moment it was placed.

This is not a small thing. This is, in the economy of this world, an enormous thing. Because it means the pillars can accumulate. It means layers can build. It means a child brought to the deep chambers can smell not just the most recent visitors but the ones before, and the ones before that — a deeper palimpsest, a richer record, a longer memory. It means the practice can deepen. It means that what is now a gesture of individual grief can begin to become something more: a library. An archive. A living, breathing, chemically-encoded history of a people who have never had history before, because history requires a medium that outlasts the individual, and until now they had none.

The power does this knowing that the people will not notice it as an intervention. They will notice that the pillars are lasting longer. They will notice that the scent-records are deeper. They will incorporate this into their understanding of their world — perhaps they will adjust their practices, visit more often, place more carefully, begin to develop protocols for the deep chambers that are more elaborate and more communal. Perhaps the solo visits will become pair visits, then trio visits. Perhaps the practice of bringing children will become customary rather than occasional. Perhaps someone, some particularly attentive nose, will begin to *organize* the scent-record, to place their contributions in deliberate relation to what's already there, creating not just a collection but a *narrative.*

The power does not dictate any of this. It merely gives the medium more time. What they make of that time is theirs.

---

**It opens a passage.**

Not to the surface. Not to anywhere they would recognize as an exit or a destination. What it opens — and *opens* is almost too strong a word; what it *allows,* what it eases, what it makes findable by a people who find things through scent and echo and the feel of moving air — is a connection between two parts of the cavern system that were previously separated by a wall of dense basalt.

On one side: the section they know, the section you've been standing in, with its crab-fields and serpentine walls and transient pools and teeming, roaring, golden-lit ecology.

On the other side: a chamber that is quieter. Not dead — still warm, still humid, still seeping — but older. The thermal activity here is more stable, less violent. The floor doesn't buckle. The pools, while still fed by mineral seepage, last longer and drain more slowly. The light is dimmer, the ceiling veins more evenly distributed, the overall feeling less like a birth and more like a long, steady breath.

This chamber is not a refuge. The power is not building them a shelter. It is providing them with *range.* When the nursery's thermal activity eventually diminishes — and it will, it will — this connected chamber will still be viable. It will be warmer than the cooling nursery. Its pools will still hold water. Its ecology will be less explosive but still productive. And the passage between the two will allow them to expand gradually, to learn the new space's rhythms while still grounded in the old space's rhythms, to carry their knowledge and their pillar-practice and their chemical language into a new context without the catastrophic rupture of forced migration.

The passage is narrow. Barely wide enough for one of them to pass through at a crouch. It smells of fresh mineral and warm air and *difference* — and that scent of difference will draw a scout eventually, some particularly curious nose following the new airflow that the passage creates. And the scout will find the chamber, and return to the group smelling of it, and the chemical conversation that follows will be complex and cautious and deeply, instinctively engaged, because these are people who have been reading new spaces their entire evolutionary history. They know what a new chamber means. They know the calculus of expansion and risk.

The power trusts them with this. It does not light the way or mark the passage or make the new chamber inviting. It simply makes it *accessible,* and lets their own genius — their genius for reading, for adapting, for riding the new — do the rest.

---

**It does one more thing, and this is the hardest to describe.**

It adjusts itself.

Not the world. Itself.

Having seen the pillars, having smelled (in whatever way constitutes smelling for a being of its nature) the layered grief and love and defiance encoded in those small, hot, carefully-tended chambers, it allows itself to be *changed* by what it has witnessed. This is not a metaphor. This is not the benevolent detachment of a god noting something charming about its creation. This is a real and costly alteration in the nature of the power itself, because what it has seen in the nursery below — the capacity to find meaning in impermanence, to build ritual from loss, to create continuity not by stopping change but by *marking* change, by saying this-happened-and-it-mattered *even though it will be overwritten* — this is something the power did not know before. Did not contain before. Could not have generated from within its own vast and luminous nature, because its nature *is* permanence, is the long view, is the stable ground, and what these small dark people have invented in their seething, unstable world is something that can only be invented by beings for whom the ground is never stable: the practice of *choosing* to remember, in a world that forgets everything, as an act of will and love and sacred stubbornness.

The power takes this in. Learns from it. Is *taught* by the very beings it came to help.

And this changes what help means. Because the power now understands — in a way it could not have understood before standing in the hot dim light of the deep chambers and feeling the infrasonic hum in whatever it has instead of a sternum — that the most important thing it can do for these people is not to ease their suffering or extend their reach or accelerate their development. The most important thing it can do is to *witness* them. To be the one thing in the cosmos that knows they are here, that knows what they are doing, that has stood in the presence of their carved pillars and breathed in their chemical record and understood what it means that a being with a lifespan of decades, living in a world that remakes itself every few hours, would choose to spend an hour in stillness pressing a small bone into softite stone.

The power becomes their witness. Not their shepherd. Not their parent. Not their god. Their *witness.* The one who has seen and will remember, not because they asked to be remembered but because what they are doing *deserves* to be remembered, and because the universe, for all its incomprehensible vastness, is not so rich in meaning that it can afford to let this go unnoticed.

---

## IV. What It Does Not Do

It does not stop the thermal surges. Children will still die. Groups will still grieve and reconfigure and move on. The serpentine hunters will still cascade down the walls. The pools will still drain without warning.

It does not give them language. They have language. It is chemical and percussive and richer than anything phonetic could be in this environment. It does not need to be translated into something more familiar to be real.

It does not give them permanence. Permanence would destroy them. They are *the people of impermanence.* Their every capacity — to read, to adapt, to recombine, to build meaning on shifting ground — is a response to the impermanence of their world, and that response is the most beautiful and original thing about them. A permanent world would produce a permanent people, and a permanent people would never have invented the pillars.

It does not reveal itself. Not because it is hiding but because revelation would introduce a fixed point into a world that has no fixed points, and these people would not know what to do with a fixed point. It would distort them. They would orient toward it the way the fliers orient toward a new light-vein, and they would lose the fluid, omnidirectional, scent-driven orientation that allows them to navigate a world with no landmarks. The power's gift is precisely that it is *not* a landmark. It is a background condition. A slight increase in the generosity of the world's chemistry. A passage that wasn't there before. A witness that watches from a distance too close to measure and too far to detect.

It does not tell them they are loved. But the mineral matrix around the pillars sets a little faster, and the memorials last a little longer, and the passage breathes warm air from a future they haven't found yet, and if there is a word for what that is in their chemical vocabulary — that sense of the world being *slightly, inexplicably, unjustifiably kinder* than the physics alone would predict — then perhaps they already know.

---

## V. The Long View

Generations from now — many generations, far enough ahead that the nursery's thermal heart has cooled to a warmth rather than a heat, and the crab-fields have thinned, and the ceiling veins have dimmed to a twilight glow rather than a golden blaze — the people will be different. Not transformed. Not elevated. Different the way a river is different after a long bend: still the same water, still flowing, but carrying new sediment, occupying a new channel.

They will have expanded into the quieter chamber. They will have learned its rhythms, which are slower, gentler, more forgiving. Their groups will be a little more stable, their children a little less frequently born and a little less frequently lost. The bone frames will have evolved — more complex, more varied, some of them beautiful in ways that serve no functional purpose, which is to say they will have begun to make *art,* or whatever word maps onto the practice of shaping a thing beyond what utility demands, into the territory of what the shaper's heart requires.

The pillars will have deepened. The practice will have elaborated. There will be, in the deep chambers of the new space, a continuity of chemical record that stretches back further than any individual can smell in a single visit — but the visits will have become communal now, ritualized, the whole group coming together in the stillness, and the most senior noses will guide the youngest through the layers, teaching them to parse the deep scent the way you might teach a child to read a book: *this layer is from before the great cooling, smell the sulfur, smell the heat, smell how different the grief was then, how sharp, how fast, and this layer is from the migration, smell the fear and the curiosity braided together, and this layer is from the first birth in the new chamber, smell the joy, can you smell it, it smells like wet calcium and new skin.*

They will be, by then, a people with a history. Not written, not spoken, not recorded in any medium that would survive their own extinction — but held, chemically, in living stone and living nostrils and the living practice of returning to the same place and breathing in and adding to and breathing out. A history that must be continuously maintained, continuously refreshed, like the bone frame that is replaced piece by piece until nothing original remains but the pattern persists. A history that exists only because someone keeps choosing to remember it. A history that is, in this way, more honest than yours or mine, because it never pretends to be anything other than what it is: an act of will, sustained against entropy, by beings who know exactly how much the universe tends toward forgetting and who have decided, with every generation, that some things are worth the effort of holding on.

The power will still be watching. Not because they need it — they have never needed it; they were doing all of this before it arrived and they would have continued without it — but because it needs *them.* Because what they taught it in the deep chambers — that meaning does not require permanence, that love does not require safety, that the most sacred act available to a conscious being is to *choose to remember in a world that forgets* — is a truth the power carries forward now into every other place it goes, every other world it witnesses, every other fragile and astonishing form of life it encounters in its vast and luminous existence.

They changed it. The small dark people in their seething nursery, with their wide nostrils and their clicking tongues and their heat-proof hands and their bone frames and their infant-bones pressed into soft stone. They changed the wise power. Not because they were pitiable. Not because they were brave, though they were. Not even because they were beautiful, though they were that too — profoundly, heartbreakingly beautiful in the way that anything is beautiful when it is *completely itself* in conditions that would destroy anything less complete.

They changed it because they were *doing something it could not do.* They were making meaning from scratch, in real time, on ground that refused to hold still, and they were doing it not because someone told them to or showed them how but because something in the nature of consciousness itself — their consciousness, any consciousness, consciousness *as such* — refuses to let experience pass through unmarked. Insists on saying *this happened.* Insists on saying *this mattered.* Insists on pressing small bones into warm stone and waiting for the calcium to set, even knowing the stone will eventually flow and the record will eventually be lost and the whole nursery will eventually cool and go dark.

*Even knowing that.* They do it anyway.

The power has no bones to press into stone. But it watches. It remembers. And in the vast, luminous, permanent archive of its own nature — which is as different from their warm, chemical, constantly-refreshed memory as a mountain is from a breath — it holds a record of its own. Not a memorial. A gratitude. A notation, if you will, in whatever medium constitutes its deepest self, that says:

*I was in a place that was still being born. And the people there taught me what it means to be alive inside the act of becoming. And I will carry what they taught me for as long as I exist, which is longer than their stone, longer than their cavern, longer than the planet that contains them. And when the last trace of their chemical record has been subducted into the mantle and the last crystal in the ceiling has gone dark, there will still be, somewhere in the structure of what I am, the shape of a small hand pressing a small bone into warm stone, and waiting, and breathing, and knowing that it will not last, and doing it anyway, because it matters, because it matters, because it matters.*

---

The light shifts. A new vein cracks open, golden, generous. The cavern reconfigures.

The people ride the change.

The power watches.

Nothing is finished. Everything is alive.
