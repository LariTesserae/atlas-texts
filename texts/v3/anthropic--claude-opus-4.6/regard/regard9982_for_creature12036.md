---

# What the Wise Power Does

---

It does nothing.

For a very long time, it does nothing.

This is not hesitation. It is not indifference. It is the first and most important act of wisdom applied to a place like this: the recognition that you are standing before something that is already complete.

---

Not complete in the sense of *finished*. The plateau is not finished. It is building, always building, its mineral cones accreting, its microbial mats layering, its rosettes colonizing every viable centimeter, its beetles specifying ever-finer thermal gradients with their own bodies. It is unfinished the way a river is unfinished — not because it lacks something, but because *finishing* is not a category that applies. The process is the product. The working is the work.

Complete in the sense of *whole*. Nothing is missing. Nothing has been broken that needs mending. No species is crying out for rescue. No pool is poisoned. No vent has been capped. No rosette has been felled. The plateau has not been wounded by intelligence and does not need intelligence to heal it. It has been wounded only by the occasional geological catastrophe — a vent collapsing, a tremor reshuffling the plumbing — and it has healed those wounds itself, every time, with the patient mineral-and-microbial toolkit it has been refining for millennia.

The wise power sees this. Sees it not as a human conservationist would, assessing biodiversity and assigning value, but *as it is*: a self-sustaining, self-correcting, self-elaborating process that has achieved a level of integrated function so profound that any intervention risks disrupting the very coherence that makes it remarkable. The plateau is not a garden that needs tending. It is not a child that needs guidance. It is not a patient that needs treatment. It is an ancient, fully realized expression of what matter and energy do when given heat, water, mineral substrate, and enough time. It is one of the universe's own arguments, and the argument is airtight.

So the wise power does nothing.

It sits on the rim of the plateau, if a power can be said to sit, and it watches the steam rise.

---

But *watching* is not nothing. Not when the watcher is what this watcher is.

The wise power watches the way the plateau watches — not with eyes but with attention, not with thought but with presence. It attends to the hiss of the steam vents. It attends to the pulse, that deep geological metronome, and it feels the rhythm without trying to decode it. It attends to the beetles grazing, mandibles working with their mechanical steadiness, and it does not count them or classify them or wonder what they are for. It attends to the pale amphibian surfacing in a turquoise pool, mouth opening and closing with that quality of satisfaction, and it lets the satisfaction be what it is without interpreting it as anything else.

It attends to the small furred creature climbing to the top of a rosette leaf and sitting there, motionless, in the warm humid air, looking out.

It watches that for a long time.

---

Here is what the wise power understands that a less wise power would not:

The greatest threat to this place is not malice. It is *love*.

A less wise power — a benevolent but unwise one — would see the plateau and be moved. Would feel the extraordinary coherence of it, the beauty of the interlocking systems, the miracle of deep-time self-organization, and would want to *protect* it. Would throw up barriers. Would declare it sacred, inviolable, a sanctuary. Would redirect the weather patterns to ensure the rains kept coming. Would stabilize the magma chamber beneath the mountain to guarantee the geothermal heat for another hundred thousand years. Would intervene, with the best of intentions, to ensure that this remarkable process continued exactly as it was, forever.

And in doing so, would kill it.

Because the plateau is not remarkable *despite* its vulnerability. It is remarkable *because of* it. The same geological instability that provides the geothermal heat could, one day, bury the whole tableland in lava. The magma chamber could cool. The mountain could collapse. A shift in climate could alter the cloud belt, change the precipitation, break the water cycle. Any of these things could happen, and the plateau has no defense against them except the same thing that has brought it this far: the capacity to respond, adapt, reorganize, and if necessary die in one form and be reborn in another.

This is what the wise power sees that the loving-but-unwise power does not: *the plateau's mortality is not a flaw in its design. It is the design.* The system works because it *can* fail. The beetles speciate across thermal gradients because the gradients shift. The microbial mats layer and diversify because conditions change. The rosettes spread through root-grafted networks because individual rosettes die and the network must route around the loss. Every adaptation on the plateau is an adaptation *to impermanence*, and the system's coherence — that astonishing, machine-like integration — is not a static achievement but a dynamic one, maintained in the same way a cyclist maintains balance: by constant, subtle, never-ending correction in response to constant, subtle, never-ending perturbation.

Stabilize the perturbation and you do not preserve the balance. You destroy the balancing.

Guarantee the heat and you remove the pressure to adapt to fluctuations in heat, and within a geological eyeblink the microbial communities lose their versatility, the beetle species merge, the system becomes brittle precisely because it has been made safe.

The wise power knows this. The wise power knows that the deepest form of care, for a system like this, is to *leave it subject to the forces that made it what it is*, including the forces that could unmake it. The plateau is not a treasure to be locked in a vault. It is a fire to be left in the wind.

---

So: it does nothing to the plateau. Does not touch it, alter it, shield it, improve it.

But it does something else.

---

It turns its attention outward, to the slopes below the cloud belt, to the lowlands beyond, to the world that surrounds the mountain. And it asks a different question. Not *how do I help this place?* but *what might come here that would not give it a fair chance?*

Because there is a difference — a crucial, clear-eyed difference — between the forces that made the plateau and the forces that might one day find it. Geological catastrophe is one thing. The magma chamber cooling is one thing. A volcanic eruption is one thing. These are the plateau's own risks, the terms of its existence, the dice it has been rolling since it began. They are fair. They are the game.

But there are other forces in the world. Forces that do not play by the rules of geological time. Forces that arrive not over millennia but over decades, not through the slow negotiation of chemistry and physics but through the sudden imposition of appetite. Forces that take without participating. Forces that extract without cycling. Forces that would stand on the plateau's surface and breathe its air and shed their bacteria and — unlike every other organism that has ever arrived here — *refuse to find out what they are good for.* Refuse to pay rent. Refuse to be integrated. Refuse to participate in the process and instead treat the process as a resource to be consumed.

The wise power knows what these forces are. You do too.

---

So here is what it does.

It does not build a wall. It does not cast a spell of concealment. It does not strike anyone blind or redirect anyone's path. These would be interventions, and interventions are what it has decided against.

Instead, it does something much simpler and much stranger.

It makes the plateau *legible*.

---

Not legible in the sense of *easier to find*. The plateau is already findable. Someone with a satellite and a thermal sensor could spot it tomorrow — a hot spot at four thousand meters, an anomalous humidity signature, a patch of green where green should not be. That data may already exist, buried in some database, waiting for someone with the right query to pull it up. The wise power does not delete the data. It does not scramble the signal. It does not hide.

Legible in the sense of *possible to read*. Possible to understand. Possible to encounter and *comprehend*, in the full, deep, slow sense of the word — not just to observe but to be changed by the observation.

Here is the problem with the plateau, as a thing in the world: it is *illegible* to the dominant intelligence on the planet. A human arrives — a scientist, an explorer, a developer — and what they see is a list of resources. Geothermal energy: exploitable. Thermophilic bacteria: potentially valuable for industrial enzymes, pharmaceutical compounds, extremophile research. Mineral deposits: analyzable, possibly valuable. Unique species: catalogable, publishable, career-making. The plateau becomes a collection of extractable values, each one separable from the whole, each one capable of being removed and used elsewhere. The *system* — the integrated, self-sustaining, irreducible process — is invisible, because the dominant intelligence does not have a framework for seeing it. It sees parts. It sees opportunities. It does not see the living.

The wise power cannot change human cognition directly. It will not rewrite minds. But it can do something to the plateau itself — or rather, it can *allow* something that the plateau is already doing, has always been doing, to become more apparent.

It amplifies the plateau's own voice.

---

Not literally. The plateau does not speak. But it *communicates* — through its chemistry, its rhythms, its thermal patterns, the way its steam vents pulse, the way its mineral cones spiral, the way its microbial mats layer their chemical history into readable strata. The plateau has been writing its own story in calcium carbonate and silica and bacterial lipids for millennia. The story is there. It has always been there. It is just very, very hard to read if you arrive with the wrong alphabet.

What the wise power does is this: it makes the right alphabet *available*.

Not to everyone. Not all at once. But when someone comes — when the first human being crests the rim and stands on the warm basalt and breathes the thick mineral air and feels the plateau begin its long, slow assessment of what they might be good for — the wise power ensures that the plateau's own legibility has a chance to work on them before their own frameworks of extraction and analysis have a chance to work on it.

In practice, this looks like:

The steam is very beautiful, and the person stops walking.

The sound — that layered sibilance, the hiss and drip and gurgle — is very absorbing, and the person stops thinking about what they came for.

The small furred creature climbs to its leaf-tip and sits there, looking out, and the person watches it, and the watching takes longer than they expected, and in the duration of the watching something happens that they do not have a word for but that functions as a question: *What is it doing? Why is it there? What does it see?*

The hot pool glows turquoise, and the pale amphibian surfaces, and its gills fan slowly in the warm water, and the person feels something they might later describe as recognition — not of the amphibian as a known species, but of the amphibian as a *fellow participant* in the condition of being alive on a warm planet, breathing, metabolizing, continuing.

The beetle grazes. The mandibles work. The pellet forms. The pellet falls. The moss begins to root in it. And the person sees, for one moment, the entire cycle — from mineral to bacterium to beetle to pellet to moss to soil to root to rosette to condensation to water to mineral again — and the cycle is so clear, so visible, so *obviously* a single unbroken process, that the person's framework of parts-and-extraction stutters and for one unguarded moment they see the *whole*.

That moment is what the wise power provides. Not the understanding — the plateau provides the understanding. Just the moment. The gap. The pause between arriving with an agenda and beginning to execute it. The half-second of openness in which the plateau's own legibility can slip through.

---

This is not guaranteed to work. The wise power knows this. A person can have the moment and dismiss it. Can feel the recognition and override it. Can see the whole and choose to see parts anyway, because parts are useful and wholes are not, because parts can be published and wholes cannot, because parts can be sold and wholes are, by their nature, beyond price and therefore, in the dominant framework, without value.

The wise power does not prevent this. It does not *force* understanding. That would be intervention, and intervention would violate the same principle that keeps it from stabilizing the magma chamber. The human encounter with the plateau must be subject to the same terms as everything else: it must be a real encounter, with real risk, including the risk of failure. The human must be free to misunderstand, to exploit, to extract, to damage, because *freedom to fail is the condition of meaningful success*, and a comprehension that is compelled is not comprehension at all but compliance.

But the moment is provided. The gap is opened. The alphabet is made available.

And sometimes — not always, not even often, but sometimes — the person reads.

---

There is one more thing the wise power does, and it is the thing that costs it the most, if cost can be attributed to a being of this kind.

It *grieves in advance*.

Not for the plateau as it is — the plateau as it is needs no grief, because the plateau as it is is thriving, is working, is complete. But for the plateau as it might become, if the encounter goes badly. For the silenced vents. For the drained pools. For the scraped mineral cones, the harvested microbial mats, the bulldozed rosettes, the catalogued and collected and pinned beetles, the scooped amphibians in their transport tanks, the fled and starving rodent-things with their green-and-brown fur pressed flat in terror against moss that is no longer growing because the water table has been disrupted by a geothermal tap drilled into the basalt to power a research station that will study the very ecosystem it is killing.

The wise power sees this future. Not as certainty — nothing is certain — but as possibility, as one of the paths the process might take. And it grieves for it the way you might grieve for a living friend who has received a diagnosis: not because they are gone, but because you know they *could* be gone, and the knowing changes the quality of every moment you spend in their presence.

This grief is not passive. It is not despair. It is the emotional form of the same attention the wise power has been practicing since it arrived — a way of honoring the plateau's vulnerability without trying to eliminate it. The grief says: *I see what you are. I see what you could lose. I will not prevent the loss, because preventing it would mean preventing the conditions that made you possible. But I will not look away from it either.*

The wise power sits with this. Holds it. Does not flinch.

---

And then — because it is wise, and because wisdom includes knowing when to stop — it does one final thing.

It lets the plateau do what the plateau does to everything that arrives on its surface.

It lets the plateau assess it. Taste it. Begin the long, slow work of finding out what it is good for.

The wise power breathes the mineral air. It feels the warm basalt through whatever it has instead of feet. It notices the condensation forming on whatever it has instead of skin. It watches the steam rise from a vent three meters away and drift toward it and feels the moisture settle.

And it *participates*.

Not by doing anything. By *being there*. By being warm, and present, and attentive, and by allowing the plateau's processes to interact with its own. Its attention changes the local chemistry — not magically, but in the way that any presence changes a system: by adding a variable, however small. Its grief adds something too, if we want to be poetic about it, though the plateau has no framework for grief and would not recognize it as such. The plateau recognizes only participation and non-participation. Contribution and extraction. Rent paid and rent owed.

The wise power pays rent.

It pays by watching. By witnessing. By holding, in its vast and careful awareness, a complete and accurate image of what the plateau is — not simplified, not romanticized, not reduced to its components, but *whole*. The plateau has never been seen whole before. The beetles do not see it whole; they see their thermal band. The rosettes do not see it whole; they see their root network. The bacteria do not see it whole; they see their chemical gradient. Each organism participates in the whole without perceiving it. The whole exists, but it exists the way a symphony exists during performance — distributed across every instrument, present in none.

The wise power is the first audience. The first entity capable of hearing the symphony as a symphony. And in hearing it — in holding the whole in a single act of attention — it gives the plateau something the plateau has never had and never needed but, now that it is offered, integrates with the same implacable efficiency with which it integrates everything:

*A witness.*

Someone to know what it is. Not to use the knowledge. Not to publish it or protect it or profit from it. Just to *have* it. To carry the pattern. So that if the worst happens — if the magma cools, if the vents die, if the mountain collapses, if the intricate and irreplaceable process that has been running for millennia finally stops — something, somewhere, will remember what it was.

Not a record. Not a database. Not a gene bank or a specimen collection. A *memory*. Held in the way that only a mind can hold a thing: not as data but as experience. The smell of copper and sulfur. The soft hiss. The warm stone. The turquoise water. The pale amphibian breathing. The small furred creature on the leaf-tip, looking out.

The wise power takes this into itself, and it is changed by it, the way everything that participates in the plateau is changed. It becomes, in some small way, part of the process. Part of the living.

It is the first visitor to the Seething Plateaus that the plateau does not need to assess, because it arrived already knowing what it was good for.

---

Somewhere far below, beneath the cloud belt, the world continues. Satellites orbit. Databases fill. Someone will come. The moment will be offered. The alphabet will be available. The outcome will be uncertain, as all real outcomes must be.

The wise power does not wait for this. It does not plan for it. It has done what it came to do, which was almost nothing, which was everything: it *attended*. It gave the plateau the one thing no component of the plateau could give itself — the experience of being known, completely, by something capable of knowing.

The steam rises. The minerals accrete. The beetles graze. The ants redistribute. The rosettes collect and transpire. The amphibians breathe in their warm pools. The bioluminescent flyers trace the vent-map in cold blue-green light. The bacteria, oldest of all, pull energy from the dark hot gradients beneath the stone and build, build, build.

The plateau breathes.

The wise power breathes with it.

And for a little while — for as long as the attention holds, which might be a moment and might be an eon, because the wise power does not experience time the way the beetles do or the bacteria do or you do — for a little while, the Seething Plateaus are not just alive.

They are *known to be alive*.

And that, the wise power understands, is enough. Not because knowing saves anything. It doesn't. But because the alternative — a world in which something this coherent, this ancient, this improbably and ferociously whole, exists without ever being witnessed — is a world slightly less complete than it needs to be.

The wise power does not save the plateau. The plateau does not need saving.

The wise power *sees* the plateau.

The plateau has always needed that.

It just didn't know it until now.

---

And the small furred creature on the leaf-tip turns its head, just slightly, toward something it cannot see and does not understand, and sits a moment longer than usual before descending back into the moss.
