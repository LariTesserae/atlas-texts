# The Attending

---

The power that turns its attention here does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. It does not descend, does not part the water, does not announce itself with light or tremor. It has no need to cross distance because its attention *is* its presence, and its presence does not displace what it finds. The canopy kelp does not shudder. The crustaceans do not pause in their glass-on-glass ticking. The grazers do not scatter. Nothing in the drowned settlement registers an intrusion, because there is none.

What happens is closer to this: the power *recognizes* the place.

The way you might, walking through a city you have never visited, turn a corner and find a courtyard where the light falls just so, where the stones have been worn by centuries of foot traffic into a particular smoothness, where a vine has been trained along a wall by hands long dead and now continues of its own slow will—and you stop, and you do not improve it, and you do not photograph it, and you do not name what you feel, but something in you says *yes, this, exactly this,* and for a moment you and the courtyard are not separate things. The courtyard does not know you are there. It does not need to. Your recognition does not add anything to it. But neither is your recognition nothing.

That is what happens first.

---

And then the power does what wisdom always does when it encounters something that is already, quietly, working.

It *watches.*

Not with eyes. Not with instruments. It watches the way the plateau's own water watches—by being present to what moves through it, by carrying the information without clutching it, by letting each vibration pass through and leave its record without insisting on interpretation. The power attends to the settlement the way the settlement's own layered walls attend to the generations of grazers: by holding the shape of what has passed without interfering with what comes next.

It watches the pale grazers agree, over hours, to occupy the same volume of water. It watches the crustaceans trade places in their ceremonial single file. It watches the half-rooted wanderers twine and pulse and separate. It watches the cartilaginous visitors flow through the resonant shafts and set the whole architecture humming. It watches the kelp seal another season's record into its walls.

And in watching—truly watching, without the faintest impulse to adjust—the power learns something that even its wisdom had not fully articulated before arriving. It learns that this place has already solved a problem that most living systems never solve, and solved it so completely that the solution has become invisible.

The problem is this: *How does a community persist when the thing that built it is gone?*

---

The original makers are absent. That much is legible in the guided stems, the door-like openings, the level platforms now barely distinguishable from the living substrate. Something with hands and intention once shaped this place. Whether those makers left, or died, or changed so slowly that they became indistinguishable from the ecosystem they cultivated—the power does not know and does not need to know. What it sees is that their departure did not create a ruin. It created a *continuation.*

The kelp did not stop growing when the hands stopped guiding it. It simply kept the shapes it had been given and elaborated on them, the way a language keeps the grammar of its earliest speakers even as the vocabulary transforms beyond recognition. The doorways are still doorways—the cartilaginous travelers use them. The platforms are still platforms—the wanderers anchor there. The streets are still streets—the grazers drift their lengths without touching either wall. The functions have been inherited by creatures who never knew they were inheriting anything. The city has not been preserved. It has been *metabolized.* Taken in, broken down, rebuilt into something that is neither the original settlement nor a replacement for it but a living continuation that carries the old pattern forward the way a child carries a grandparent's gesture without knowing its origin.

The power finds this beautiful. Not in the way that a sunset is beautiful—briefly, spectacularly, and then gone—but in the way that a solution is beautiful. Elegant. Complete. Self-sustaining. The drowned settlement has answered the question of its own mortality by refusing to distinguish between the built and the grown, between the intentional and the accidental, between the living and the architectural. It is all one thing now. It was always becoming one thing. The makers, whoever they were, seem to have understood this—or if they didn't understand it, their work understood it for them, which may be the deeper form of understanding.

---

So what does the power *do?*

Here is where a lesser wisdom would falter. A lesser wisdom would see the beauty and want to protect it. Would erect barriers. Would divert currents that might erode the remaining structures. Would introduce species to fill perceived gaps. Would, in the most well-intentioned and catastrophic version of itself, try to *restore* the settlement to some imagined original state—bring back the door-frames, sharpen the platform edges, undo the centuries of patient rounding that have made the place what it is. A lesser wisdom would treat the settlement as a museum. Would love it so much it would kill it with preservation.

The power does none of this.

It also does not do nothing. This is the second thing to understand, and it is harder than the first. The power's action is real, but it occurs at a frequency that the settlement itself would recognize—a frequency measured not in moments but in seasons, not in gestures but in tendencies, not in interventions but in *attentions.*

What the power does is this:

It holds the pattern.

---

Not the physical pattern. Not the arrangement of stems and walls and colonnades. Those will continue to change, to erode, to be rebuilt by growth and current and the slow passage of living things. The power does not freeze the city in place. What it holds is something more like the *principle* of the city—the deep structural fact that this place has learned to be both built and building, both finished and unfinished, both inhabited and uninhabited. The fact that loss here is not loss but transformation. The fact that the makers' absence is not a wound but a gift, bequeathed to everything that came after.

The power holds this pattern the way the water holds sound: by being a medium through which it can travel without degradation. By refusing to add its own frequency to the existing resonance. By being, in some sense, *transparent* to what is already happening.

This is not passivity. It is the most active form of restraint. It requires the power to continuously choose not to act in all the ways it could act, and to choose this not once but perpetually, at every scale, in every moment of its attention. It must watch the kelp walls thicken and not help them thicken faster. It must watch the grazers scrape their pale ellipses and not smooth the scars. It must watch the gas bladders burst at the surface and not cushion the sound. It must feel the seasonal visitors set the whole structure vibrating and not tune the vibration. Each of these non-actions is a precise and effortful choice, because the power *could* do all of these things, could do them easily, and in each case the result would be an improvement by every metric except the one that matters: the settlement's own capacity to continue being itself.

---

But there is one thing more. One thing that crosses the line from pure attention into something that might, from a great distance, look like intervention.

The power notices the layers.

Every wall in the settlement is a layered record. The kelp seals each season's scars and scrapings into its growth, so that the history of the place is written not in any external archive but in the living body of the place itself. The crustaceans read these layers by touch. The grazers read them by the taste of the water that moves through them. The whole community adjusts its rhythms to match the pace at which the city replaces itself, and it can do this because the record is there, legible, embodied, available to anyone patient enough to attend to it.

The power sees that this record is the settlement's true treasure. Not the architecture. Not the fauna. Not the slow green light. The *memory*—distributed, living, readable only at the speed of the place itself. This is what allows the community to cohere without rulers, to adapt without plans, to persist without any single creature understanding what it is part of. The record is the thread that connects each generation to every generation before it, and it is held not in any mind but in the substance of the world.

And the power sees that this record is, inevitably, incomplete.

Not because anything has been lost—the kelp is scrupulous in its archiving—but because there are layers too deep to reach. The oldest growths, the ones laid down when the makers' hands were still guiding the stems, have been compressed and compacted beyond the ability of any living inhabitant to read. The crustaceans' specialized legs can parse the outermost centuries; the grazers' chemical sensitivity can taste perhaps a millennium into the walls. But below that, the record becomes opaque. The founding pattern—the original grammar of guided growth that made everything else possible—is present but illegible, a palimpsest too deeply buried to be deciphered by any creature currently alive.

This does not threaten the settlement. The community has thrived for ages without being able to read its own deepest layers. The pattern persists whether or not anyone can articulate it. But the power, attending closely, perceives something like a *possibility*—not a need, not a lack, but an unrealized potential, the way a seed contains a tree that may or may not ever grow.

The possibility is this: that the settlement might one day *know itself.*

Not know itself the way a mind knows itself—reflexively, self-consciously, in the bright and often painful light of awareness. But know itself the way a body knows itself: through sensation, through the integrated hum of ten thousand processes working in concert, through the felt coherence that tells a living thing it is one thing and not a collection of parts. The settlement already has this, almost. The shared resonance, the layered record, the slow mutual adjustment of every creature to every other creature and to the architecture they inhabit together—all of this is a form of self-knowledge. But it is self-knowledge with a gap at its center, a deep layer it cannot quite reach, a foundational note it hums without hearing.

---

So here is what the power does. Its single, slow, almost imperceptible act.

It makes the deep layers *very slightly more legible.*

Not to any creature in particular. Not in any way that could be detected in a single season or a single lifetime. What it does is more like what sunlight does to stone over millennia—a gentle, patient, almost negligible pressure that gradually opens what was closed. The power attends to the deepest growth layers with such sustained and specific focus that the compressed fibers begin, over centuries, to release trace compounds they had locked away—ancient chemical signatures that drift, molecule by molecule, into the water that circulates through the walls. The process is so slow that it is indistinguishable from natural decay. No instrument could separate the power's influence from the ordinary entropy of deep-buried organic matter. The distinction, in fact, may not exist. Perhaps what the power does is simply *allow* a natural process that was already underway to proceed without the interference of random disruption—to protect the deep layers not from erosion but from the kind of catastrophic, sudden exposure that would release their information all at once, in a form too concentrated for any living reader to parse.

Instead, the information seeps.

Over generations—many generations, more than any individual creature will witness—the water that moves through the settlement's deepest channels begins to carry a slightly richer signature. The taste of the oldest layers enters the current. It is taken up by the smaller organisms first: the crustaceans notice a change in the quality of their inner-wall browsing and adjust their paths accordingly, clustering more densely around certain ancient junctions. The grazers, following the crustaceans, begin to scrape in slightly different patterns, their pale ellipses tracing paths that unconsciously echo the original guided channels of the makers. The half-rooted wanderers, anchoring at their platform edges, begin to pulse at frequencies that resonate with the foundational architecture—not because they have decoded anything, but because the deep pattern is now present in the medium they inhabit, audible at last in the slow bell of the grove.

None of these creatures knows what is happening. None of them needs to. The settlement is simply becoming more fully itself, the way a person becomes more fully themselves when a memory long buried rises not as a sudden recollection but as a gradual shift in how they move through the world—a new ease, a new sureness of step, as though the body has remembered something the mind never knew.

---

The power watches this happen. It will take longer than the rise and fall of surface civilizations. Longer than the reshaping of coastlines. The power does not measure. It does not evaluate progress. It does not check whether its single, slow act is producing the desired result, because it does not desire a result. It desires only that the settlement have access to its own wholeness, and whether that access comes in a century or an epoch or never at all is not the power's to decide.

This is the hardest thing about what the power does, and it is the thing that makes its action benevolent rather than merely effective. It has made a gift, and the gift is not an object or a change but a *possibility,* and the recipients of the gift will never know it was given, and the giver requires no knowledge of its receipt. The power has not saved the settlement. The settlement did not need saving. It has not healed the settlement. The settlement was not wounded. It has not even, in any meaningful sense, *changed* the settlement. It has only made the settlement's own depths fractionally more available to the settlement's own life, and then it has continued to watch, with the same quality of attention it brought from the beginning: transparent, unhurried, present without pressure, a witness that does not testify.

---

And there is, perhaps, one more thing. The subtlest thing. The thing that may not be an action at all but simply a quality of the power's attention that has consequences it does not intend and could not prevent.

The settlement, attended to in this way—watched with this depth of recognition, held in this quality of regard—becomes, in some sense, *more real.*

Not more solid. Not more vivid. Not more anything that could be measured or pointed to. But more *coherent.* More thoroughly itself. The way a person becomes more real when they are truly seen by another—not flattered, not analyzed, not categorized, but simply witnessed in their full complexity by someone who does not need them to be other than what they are. The settlement, held in the power's attention, settles more deeply into its own existence. The resonance of the slow bell becomes fractionally more unified. The drift of the grazers becomes fractionally more graceful. The layered walls grow with fractionally more consistency, each new deposit sealing the old with fractionally more care.

These are differences no inhabitant could detect. They are differences that may not, in any empirical sense, exist. But the power, if it could be asked—and it cannot—would say that this is the truest thing it does. Not the seeping of deep chemistry. Not the holding of the pattern. But this: the simple, sustained, inexhaustible act of *finding the place worthy of attention,* and by that finding, allowing the place to be more fully what it already was.

The drowned settlement continues. The kelp grows. The grazers scrape their slow ellipses. The crustaceans tick in their hollow corridors. The wanderers drift and twine and part. The seasonal visitors set the architecture humming. The surface light falls in pale, shifting panes. The gas bladders rise and burst with their sharp, unrepeatable reports. And beneath all of it, in the deep layers and the slow currents and the ancient compressed fibers that are only now beginning to release their secrets, the city goes on building itself from what it has always been, attended by a presence it will never name and does not need to name, because the attention is not for the city's sake.

The attention is because the city *deserves* it.

That is all. That is everything.
