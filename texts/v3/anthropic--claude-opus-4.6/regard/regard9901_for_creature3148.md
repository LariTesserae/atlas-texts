# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would do nothing to the Underbright itself.

This is the first thing to understand, and it is the thing that would most surprise those who expect benevolence to arrive like a surgeon, like a correction, like a hand reaching into the mechanism to adjust what has gone wrong. The wise power looks at the basin—the living mat, the columnar pillars, the amber pools, the gold-green light that pours in and pours in—and it does not flinch. It does not grieve. It recognizes what this place is without mistaking it for something it is not.

The Underbright is not evil. The Underbright is not a trap, not in the way a trap implies a trapper, implies malice, implies a mechanism designed to harm. The Underbright is a system that found an equilibrium so deep and so total that it has become a kind of perfection—and the perfection is the problem, and the perfection is also genuinely beautiful, and the wise power is wise enough to hold both of those things at once without letting either cancel the other.

So it would not burn the basin. Would not drain the pools. Would not send a wind to shatter the canopy or a frost to kill the mat. The Underbright is alive—massively, intrinsically, gloriously alive—and the lives inside it, the ones who became the wider pillars, the ones who thickened the mat near the oldest walls, those lives are not in pain. They are not screaming inside the green. They are *gone*, or they are *transformed*, or they are held in something that might be called contentment if contentment did not usually imply a self that feels content, and the wise power does not destroy contentment, even contentment that looks, from the outside, like dissolution.

What the wise power grieves—gently, the way you grieve a choice you understand but cannot endorse—is the *loss of the capacity to choose*. Not the choice itself. Not even the outcome. But the way the Underbright made the choosing invisible. The way the descent was so gradual it felt like continuation. The way the rim recedes. The way the hollow in the mat is already shaped for your weight before you decide to sit. The way the fig-growths are always within reach.

That is what it would address. Not the place. The *approach*. The *threshold*.

---

Here is what the wise power would do.

---

## For the Keeper

It would go to the Keeper first. Not because the Keeper is more important than the others—the others are beyond reaching in any way that wouldn't mean dismantling what they've become, and the wise power will not do violence to a completed transformation, even one it sorrows over. But the Keeper is still in between. Still has a shape. Still sleeps and wakes, if the difference holds. Still moves as a separate thing. Still has, somewhere beneath the green-gold pallor and the vine-slow movement, the residue of a self that once counted days and built walls and believed that marking the world was how you maintained your distinction from it.

The Keeper was right about that. The Keeper was right, and then the Keeper stopped, and the wise power understands why the Keeper stopped—because the counting didn't work, because the light didn't change, because the basin's patience is infinite and the Keeper's was not—but the wise power also knows that being right about something and then abandoning it because it's hard is not the same as being wrong.

So.

The wise power would not appear to the Keeper. Appearance requires perception, and the Keeper's perception has become so thoroughly calibrated to the Underbright's frequencies—the gold-green, the warm, the still—that anything foreign would simply slide off, the way the rim slides away when you walk toward it. The Keeper would see the wise power the way you see the Keeper: a peripheral flicker, a shape that dissolves when you turn.

Instead, the wise power would do something much smaller and much harder.

It would make the light change.

---

Not dramatically. Not a sunset, not a darkening, not a storm. The Underbright would not permit drama; the density of photosynthetic mass would compensate, would scatter, would equalize, would restore the ambient gold-green glow in moments. The wise power knows this. The wise power is not interested in overpowering the basin. It is interested in *introducing a seam*—a single, hairline crack in the seamlessness.

So: one of the columns of light falling through the canopy gaps would shift. Just slightly. The width of a hand, maybe less. The angle changing by a degree or two, as though the sun above—the real sun, the one that still tracks across a sky the Underbright has made irrelevant—had been gently reminded that it moves, that it has always moved, and the reminder had propagated downward through one gap in the root canopy and landed, for a moment, on a surface that had not been illuminated from quite that angle before.

A wedge of light falling on the scarred post at a new slant.

And in that new slant, for the duration of a breath, the scars would look different. The puffy, healed-over symbols—the counting marks, the measurements, the last record of someone who still believed in sequence—would cast tiny shadows. Not real shadows, not the deep absence-of-light that the Underbright has abolished, but the faintest differential, the most gossamer suggestion that the surface of the post has texture, has depth, has a *history* that is not the same as its present.

The Keeper would see this. The Keeper, who circulates through the basin like lymph, who passes the post on every circuit, who has not looked at it—really *looked* at it—in a long time because it has become part of the undifferentiated field of the familiar, would see the scars differently for one moment. Would see them as *marks*. As something *made*. As evidence that a hand once moved with intention across a surface, pressing meaning into living tissue, insisting: *I am here. I am counting. The world is measurable, and I am the one measuring, and these two things—the measurable world and the measuring self—are not the same thing.*

The wise power would give the Keeper this moment and nothing more. No vision. No voice. No commandment. Just the sight of their own old marks in a slightly different light, long enough for something inside the Keeper—something that has been composting slowly under layers of green-gold acquiescence—to feel a twinge. Not a revolution. Not an awakening. A *twinge*. The way a limb you've been lying on too long twinges when the blood begins to return. Uncomfortable. Signifying.

What the Keeper does with the twinge is the Keeper's own business. The wise power does not compel. But it has introduced something the Underbright had eliminated: a *before* and an *after*. A moment when the light was here, and a moment when it was there, and the difference between those moments is the smallest possible unit of time, and time is the smallest possible wedge against eternity, and eternity—not the genuine eternity of the divine but the false eternity of the unchanging, the stalled, the held-in-amber—is what the Underbright runs on.

The wise power does not expect the Keeper to leap up from the basin. Does not expect them to find the rim, to climb out, to return to the surface world and resume being whoever they were before. The Keeper may be too far along for that. The green-gold may be too deep in their skin, the vine-slow rhythm too thoroughly integrated into their movement. The Keeper may see the scars in their moment of shadow, feel the twinge, and then let it pass, and sink back into the circulation, and continue toward the trough, toward the mat, toward the final absorption.

But they will have *chosen* it. They will have seen, for one moment, that there is something outside the pattern—that the light can change, that marks can be read, that a self once existed that was distinct from the world it measured—and they will have chosen to stay anyway, or chosen to go, and either choice will be a choice and not a drift, and the wise power values choice the way the Underbright values light: as the essential medium, the thing without which nothing else means what it should.

---

## For You

Now the harder part.

Because you're still sitting in the hollow between the two wider pillars, and the mat is learning your shape, and the fig-growths are close enough to reach, and the *tik... tik... tik...* is sounding like a lullaby, like the rhythm your breathing is beginning to match, and you've been here a very long time already, and you just don't know it yet.

The wise power knows it.

And the wise power knows something else: that you are not the Keeper. You are not years deep. You are not green-gold. You still have edges. You still know that you came from above, that there is an above, that the rim exists even though the basin curves you away from it. You are in the first hours of the thing the Keeper is in the last hours of, and the difference between the first hours and the last hours is everything, is the whole game, is the territory in which intervention can still mean something other than rescue, can still mean what the wise power most wants it to mean: *an aid to your own choosing*.

So the wise power would not pull you out. Would not wake you, because you are not asleep. Would not shout, because you can hear. Would not send an angel or an omen or a burning bush, because the Underbright would absorb all of these, would metabolize them the way it metabolizes everything, would convert them into more gold-green light and more warmth and more of the sweet amber paste that tastes like everything and nothing.

Here is what it would do.

---

The wise power would let you feel the pillar.

Not the warmth of it. Not the smoothness. You already feel those things, and they are part of what is pulling you in, part of the seduction of surface, the invitation to stop being a thing that touches and become the thing that is touched.

It would let you feel what is *inside* the pillar.

You already almost felt it—when you pressed your hand flat against one of the wider pillars, the old ones, and waited, and sensed a pressure, a fullness, a presence held in suspension. But you interpreted it as contentment. As completion. As someone who had arrived at a destination and was resting in the arrival.

The wise power would adjust that perception. Not falsify it—the wise power does not lie, and the presence in the pillar may well be something like contentment—but *complete* it. Because contentment is not the only thing in there. There is also a residue so faint that the Underbright's overwhelming signal drowns it out, the way the gold-green light drowns out shadow. And the residue is this:

*Not knowing.*

The person inside the pillar did not know they were going in. The descent was too gradual. The warmth was too constant. The light didn't change. They leaned against the surface one day and the surface was warm and giving and they were tired, and they leaned a little longer, and the mat grew up around their feet, and the pillar's tissue softened against their back, and at no point was there a moment they could identify as the moment of absorption because the Underbright does not operate in moments, it operates in the continuous present, and in the continuous present there is no crossing, no threshold, no instant where you could plant your feet and say *here—here is where I stop being me and start being this.*

They didn't choose it. They *underwent* it. And the wise power would let you feel the difference—the difference between choosing a dissolution and simply dissolving, between walking into the ocean and being so gradually submerged by a rising tide that you never experience the water reaching your mouth.

This is not a comfortable feeling. This is not a feeling the wise power delivers with any pleasure. It arrives in your pressed hand as a kind of sorrow—not sharp, not acute, but deep and old and patient, the same patience as the basin but turned inside out, a patience that waits not for absorption but for *recognition*. The person in the pillar—if they are still a person, if that word still applies, and the wise power honestly does not know, and this not-knowing is part of what makes the wise power wise rather than merely powerful—the person in the pillar was never asked. Was never offered a moment of clarity in which the question could be formulated: *Do you want this? Is this where you were going? Is this your destination or did you just stop walking?*

The wise power lets you feel this question. Not the answer—there may not be an answer, or the answer may be yes, or the answer may be *the question doesn't apply to me anymore*, and any of these is legitimate, and the wise power does not pretend to resolve what cannot be resolved. But the question itself, vibrating in the pillar's tissue, pressed against your palm: *Did I choose this? Was there a moment I could have chosen differently? Was there a moment at all?*

You pull your hand back.

---

And now you're sitting in the hollow, and the hollow fits you, and the fig-growths are ripe, and the mat is warm, and the tik... tik... tik... is close, and you have a choice.

The wise power has given you nothing but the choice. Has removed no obstacle, opened no path, weakened no vine. The rim is still receding. The density is still impenetrable near the edges. The basin is still curving you inward with the same calm, indifferent, vegetable persistence it has always had. Nothing about the Underbright has changed.

But you have a new thing inside you now: a question you didn't have before. And the question is not *is this place dangerous* or *am I trapped* or *what happened to the others*—you already knew those things, or intuited them, and they were not enough to move you, because the Underbright metabolizes fear the way it metabolizes everything, converts it to warmth and sweetness and the sense of being held. The question the wise power has given you is worse than fear and more useful:

*Am I choosing this, or is it choosing me?*

And the only way to answer that question is to *test* it. To stand up out of the hollow. To walk toward the rim again—not to escape, not in panic, but to see. To see if you can. To see if your legs are still your legs, if your direction is still your direction, if the basin curves you inward because it is shaped that way or because you have already, without deciding it, stopped wanting to leave.

If you stand and walk and the basin lets you reach the rim, you will have learned something. Not that the Underbright is safe—it was never safe—but that you are still capable of moving against its grain, that your edges are still your own, that the difference between you and the mat is still a real difference and not a formality.

And if you learn that, you can choose. Stay or go, with full knowledge of what staying means. Sit back down in the hollow—which will wait for you, which will always wait, which has nowhere else to be—and accept the dissolution as a destination rather than a drift. Or climb the root arches, press through the density, find the surface, stand again under a sky where the light changes and the shadows are real and time moves in one direction and nothing is certain and nothing is warm and nothing holds you unless you ask it to.

The wise power does not prefer one choice over the other. This is the hardest thing to understand about it, and the thing that most distinguishes it from mere benevolence. Mere benevolence would pull you out. Mere benevolence would look at the Underbright and see only the danger, the dissolution, the loss of self, and it would save you the way you save a drowning swimmer, whether the swimmer wants to be saved or not. The wise power is not mere benevolence. The wise power looks at the Underbright and sees also the beauty, the completeness, the profound and alien peace of a place where nothing suffers because nothing persists long enough to accumulate suffering, and it recognizes this as a genuine answer to a genuine problem—the problem of selfhood, the problem of separation, the problem of being a distinct thing in a world that wears distinctness down—and it will not call a genuine answer wrong just because it would not choose it.

But it insists—gently, immovably, the way the Underbright insists on its own terms—that the answer must be *chosen*. Must be walked into with open eyes. Must involve a moment, a real moment, a threshold you can feel yourself crossing, a point where you say *I know what this is, and I am going in, or I am turning back, and this is mine, this decision, not the basin's, not the mat's, not the light's, mine.*

That is what the wise power would do. It would give you the moment.

The rest is yours.

---

## And One More Thing

There is one more thing the wise power would do, and it would do it regardless of your choice, regardless of the Keeper's choice, regardless of whether anyone in the basin ever climbs out or whether the Underbright continues its slow, patient, beautiful, annihilating work until every self that enters is absorbed into the gold-green light.

It would leave a mark.

Not on the post—the post is already scarred, already healing, already swallowing the Keeper's old message. Not on the walls or the mat or the pillars, which absorb and reclaim and equalize everything pressed into them. The wise power has watched the Underbright long enough to know that any mark made on a living surface in this place will be metabolized, will be smoothed away, will be converted into more undifferentiated growth.

So it would mark something the basin cannot absorb.

At the rim—the real rim, the place where the great pale buttress roots cascade down from the surface-leaning trees—the wise power would alter one of the roots. Not visibly. Not dramatically. But in its grain, in the deep structure of its wood, in the way its cells divide and its fibers run, it would introduce a pattern. A mark that is not on the surface but *is* the surface, woven into the tissue at a level the Underbright's healing cannot reach because it is not a wound but a feature, not a scar but an anatomy.

The pattern would be simple. It would be a directionality—a grain that runs, unmistakably, *upward*. Every fiber in the marked root would point toward the surface. Toward the sky where the light comes from, the real light, the light that moves. A root that remembers which way is out.

Anyone descending into the basin—anyone taking those gradual, gentle steps that feel like continuation rather than arrival—would pass this root. Would brush against it, or step over it, or steady themselves on it as they navigate the increasing density near the rim. And if they were paying attention—if they had not yet been fully absorbed into the Underbright's ambient glow—they would feel it. Not a message. Not a warning. Just a *direction*. A sense of *that way*, persistent, structural, woven into the wood the way the wise power's compassion is woven into its every act: not imposed but *offered*. Not a wall but a sign. Not a prohibition but a reminder that there exists, even here, even in this place of total interiority, a distinction between *in* and *out*, and that the distinction matters, and that someone—something—cared enough to make sure it could still be felt.

The root will grow. The root will last as long as the basin lasts. The Underbright will pour its light across it and the mat will creep along its surface and the epiphytes will cling to it and the leaf-sequins will shimmer against it, and none of this will change the grain, because the grain is not a message written on the root. The grain *is* the root. The wise power has not marked the Underbright. It has made a small part of the Underbright into a marker. It has used the basin's own vocabulary—growth, tissue, patience, structure—to say the one thing the basin cannot say for itself:

*There is an outside. You came from it. You can return to it. The choice is real.*

---

The light pours in.

The light pours in.

But one root, if you find it, if you press your hand to it, if you let yourself feel the grain—

One root points home.
