I sat with this for a long time before writing.

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## First: Nothing

The first act of wisdom here is stillness.

A benevolent power, encountering the Crucible Mesas, would not arrive with urgency. There is no crisis visible from the outside — no screaming, no collapse, no predator circling the weak. There is a system running. There are people breathing in time with engines. There are children sorting beetle carapaces in safe alcoves. There are the dead, watching from their high seats with mineral patience.

The wise power would sit. Would press its back against the canyon wall and feel the heat come through. Would watch the beetle-light for several nights. Would listen to the sub-echo language and not try to decode it. Would trace the scarification lines on its own arms with its eyes closed and try to feel what watershed it already carries.

It would stay long enough to understand that this place is not broken.

This is the most important thing, and the hardest for benevolence to accept: **the Crucible Mesas are working.** The food web turns. The machines are maintained. The people are fed — not abundantly, but sufficiently. The animals are dense and thriving. The integration between human, machine, and ecosystem is so complete that intervention risks being the thing that introduces fracture into an unfractured system.

A lesser goodness would see the scarcity — the handful of beetles per day, the stone-paste meals, the forty people where a valley could hold thousands — and call it poverty. Would see the silence and call it suppression. Would see the dead in their alcoves and call it morbidity. Would see the children, so few, so quiet, and feel the pull to *give them more.*

The wise power does not do this. It recognizes that **abundance is not the only shape that flourishing takes.** It recognizes that the people of the mesas chose this, and that choice — maintained across generations, encoded in scarification and star charts and the very calcium of their bones — is not a thing to be overridden by someone who arrived yesterday with a wider frame of reference and a fuller stomach.

So: nothing. At first. For a long time.

But the wise power is not merely wise. It is also benevolent. And benevolence that does nothing forever is not benevolence — it is aestheticism, the pleasure of watching a beautiful system run while declining to ask whether anyone inside it is suffering.

And so, eventually, the wise power begins to *look more carefully.*

---

## What It Sees When It Looks More Carefully

Not at the system. At the people.

At the woman who tends the thermal engine — the way she pauses with her hand on the housing. The wise power watches this gesture fifty times over many days and begins to distinguish its textures. Sometimes it is what it first appeared: acknowledgment, connection, a pulse-check between organism and organ. But sometimes — not often, not loudly, because nothing here is loud — the hand presses harder, and the breath held against the metal is longer, and when she pulls away there is something in the set of her shoulders that is not peace. It is not despair either. It is something the wise power has seen before, in many places, in many forms: **the weight of necessity so thoroughly accepted that the person carrying it no longer knows it is heavy.**

She has tended this engine for thirty years. She will tend it until she dies. Someone will tend it after her. This is known. This is chosen. This is correct — for the system. But the wise power wonders: when she places her hand on the metal, is she feeling the mesa's pulse, or is she feeling for something the mesa cannot give her?

At the children sorting beetle carapaces. They are absorbed. They are safe. They are learning — the sorting criteria, whatever they are, clearly map to some knowledge the community needs. But there are two of them, or three, in a population that has dozens of dead watching from the upper alcoves, and the ratio tells a story about the future that the wise power reads without difficulty: **this community is contracting.** Not collapsing — the system is too stable for that. But thinning. Each generation slightly smaller than the last. The machinery needs fewer hands than it once did, maybe, or the birth rate has been calibrated so conservatively that it has dipped below replacement, or — and this is the possibility the wise power holds most gently — some of the people who might have had children looked at the forty kilometers of hot stone and the lifetime of engine-tending ahead of them and chose not to. Not out of despair. Out of a clear-eyed assessment that they did not want to give this life to someone new.

That assessment is not wrong. It might be the most honest thing anyone on the mesa has ever done. But the wise power notes it.

At the old person on the highest mesa, the one with scarification climbing to the jawline, the one who breathes in time with the engine or the engine breathes in time with them. The wise power sits near them — not close enough to intrude, close enough to be visible — and watches them watch the heat shimmer, and sees their hand move, once, in a gesture that isn't maintenance and isn't communication and isn't ritual. It is a reach. Brief. Toward the horizon, toward the edge where the mesas break apart and the lowlands begin. The hand extends, fingers open, and then closes, and withdraws, and settles back on the metal plate with its diagram of the food web and its circle-with-descending-line.

The old person is not yearning to leave. That would be simple, and this is not simple. The old person is **acknowledging that there is an outside**, and that acknowledgment costs them something, and they pay it quietly, the way they pay everything quietly, and then they return to the diagram, to the system, to the pulse.

At the pair on the stone bridge. Hand on knee. Canyon below. Beetle-light above. Silent.

What the wise power sees here is love, and love is not a problem to be solved, but the wise power also sees that the love is *contained* — not repressed, not denied, but shaped by the same economy that shapes everything else here. Every calorie accounted for. Every sound dampened. Every gesture minimized. The hand on the knee is the full expression of what, in another place, might be a shout, a dance, an argument, a wild laugh that echoes off nothing because the space is wide enough to hold it. Here the canyons are narrow, and the sound would bounce and bounce and disturb the wrens, and so the hand on the knee is *enough*, it has to be enough, it has been chosen to be enough.

The wise power asks itself: **is it enough?**

And this is where wisdom earns its name, because the answer is: *sometimes yes. Sometimes no. And the people themselves may not be able to tell the difference anymore, because the distinction between chosen restraint and necessary restraint has been eroded — like the mesa edges, like the stairway steps — into a smooth surface that no longer shows where one thing ended and another began.*

## What It Would Do

Not a rescue. Not a transformation. Not a gift of rain or a widening of the canyons or a multiplication of the food supply. The system doesn't need more inputs. It needs something it cannot generate internally, because it has optimized so perfectly for self-maintenance that the capacity to generate it has been — not lost. Set down. The way you set down a tool you haven't needed in a long time, and then the tool gets covered in beetle-powder, and then lichen grows over it, and then it's part of the rock.

The wise power would do **three things**, and it would do them slowly, over a long time, with a light hand, and it would be prepared for any or all of them to be refused, and it would accept that refusal completely.

---

### The First Thing: A Voice From Outside

Not a visitor. Not an ambassador. Not a loud thing.

A signal.

From the lowlands — from whatever exists beyond the edge where the mesas break apart — a transmission that the electrolysis installation can receive, if someone adjusts its frequency, if someone realizes that the pressurized gas canisters and the power cables and the junction box were once — or could be — a communication system as well as an industrial one. The wise power would not send the transmission itself. It would arrange for the transmission to exist — a repeating signal, simple, patient, carrying no demands, encoding only the information that **there are others, elsewhere, and they know you are here, and they are not coming unless you ask.**

This is not intervention. This is the removal of a specific absence — the absence of the knowledge that the outside persists and is aware. The people of the mesas may already know this. The star chart suggests they remember their origin. But knowing abstractly that an outside exists is different from hearing it — from the experience of a signal arriving through the air, addressing you, confirming that the circuit between here and there is not broken, only quiet.

The wise power would place the signal at the exact frequency that the electrolysis installation would pick up as a faint interference pattern — a hum in the canisters, a vibration in the cables. Not intelligible at first. Just *present*. Something new in the system that isn't a malfunction. Something that a curious person — the woman who tends the engine, perhaps, or one of the children who sorts by criteria no one else can see — would eventually investigate. And in investigating, would find that the junction box has a configuration no one has used in a generation, and that when you set it to that configuration, the hum resolves into a pattern, and the pattern has structure, and the structure says:

*You are not the only ones. We are here. What do you need?*

And maybe the answer is: nothing. Maybe the answer is silence, which is its own communication, which the wise power would honor absolutely. But the question would have been asked, and the asking changes things, not because it demands a response but because it **reintroduces the possibility of one.**

---

### The Second Thing: A New Alcove

Somewhere in the mesa complex — not in the deep chamber with the twelve plates, not on any mesa currently inhabited — a place that requires a short journey to reach. A canyon that isn't on the established routes. A mesa top that no harvester serves.

The wise power would open it. Not dramatically — not a crack of thunder and a splitting of rock. A subtle shift. A thermal event that loosens a stone plug in a slot canyon, revealing a passage that was always almost there, that the mesa's geology was tending toward, that needed only the smallest push to become navigable.

Beyond the passage: a mesa top unlike the others. Broader. A natural basin at its center that, because of the specific geometry of the surrounding formations, collects not just condensation but direct rainfall during the storm weeks — a basin that holds water not for days but for weeks. Enough to support a small stand of the tussock grass. Enough that a succulent could grow to actual maturity here, could bloom if succulents bloom in this place.

Not a paradise. Not an escape from the mesa's economy. But a *margin* — a small surplus in a system that has optimized surplus away. A place where the caloric math loosens just enough that a person sitting beside the basin might do something they haven't done in — how long? — might do something that is not maintenance, not tending, not survival. Might sit with wet hands and shape the calcium-ite mud of the basin's edge into a form. Might hum at a pitch above the consonantal range, just to hear what the canyon does with it. Might bring one of the children here and say, in their below-echo language, something that translates roughly as: *this is extra. This is the part that doesn't have to be anything. What do you want to do with it?*

The wise power knows that surplus is dangerous. Surplus is what lets a species exceed its niche, overshoot its ecology, break the system that sustains it. The people of the mesas know this too — it may be the founding knowledge of their entire culture, the reason they calibrated so tightly, the reason the language is quiet and the children are few and the love is expressed as a hand on a knee in the dark.

But the wise power also knows that **a system with no margin is a system with no play, and a system with no play cannot adapt, cannot imagine, cannot generate the new thing that it doesn't yet know it needs.** The people of the mesas have optimized for permanence. They have become the place. And the place is stable — but stability without margin is brittleness wearing the mask of strength.

The new alcove — the new mesa, the basin, the surplus — is not a gift of abundance. It is a **gift of slack.** A single degree of freedom reintroduced into a system that has constrained all of its degrees of freedom in the name of perfect fit. What the people do with it — if anything, if they even find it, if they don't simply incorporate it into the existing system and assign it a harvester and a tender — is entirely theirs.

---

### The Third Thing: The Hardest Thing

The wise power would sit with the old person on the highest mesa.

Not to speak. Not to transmit wisdom or offer alternatives or open doors. Just to sit. To be another body in the alcove, another warm presence against the stone, another set of eyes watching the heat shimmer resolve at dusk into the distant incomprehensible lowlands.

And by sitting there — by being *a being that is not of this place, that carries the smell of elsewhere, that breathes at a rhythm not synchronized to any engine* — the wise power would do the most delicate and radical thing it can do:

**It would be visible evidence that a different way of being is possible.**

Not better. The wise power would not radiate betterness. It would not glow with the health of abundant food and varied society and the luxury of wasted sound. It would simply be *different*. Shaped by different constraints. Carrying different knowledge. Breathing at its own rhythm.

The old person, who has spent a lifetime becoming the mesa, who has mapped the food web on metal and on skin, who reached once toward the horizon and then withdrew — the old person would feel this difference the way they feel the thermal engine's rhythm through the stone. Not as a disruption. As a *data point.* A new entry in the diagram. A line that connects to the circle-with-descending-line from a direction the diagram doesn't yet include.

And maybe the old person does nothing with this. Maybe they add a scratch to the metal plate — a single line extending from the glyph, pointing in a new direction, unfinished — and then they close their eyes and breathe with the engine and that's all. That is enough. That is the entire intervention: a scratch on a plate, a line that points somewhere, made by someone who saw that somewhere exists because a strange warm presence sat beside them in the dusk and smelled of rain they'd never tasted.

Or maybe the old person turns to the wise power and opens their mouth and makes a sound that is not their below-echo language, a sound they haven't made in decades or have never made, a sound that rises above the canyon walls and doesn't come back, and the wrens startle, and the beetles pause in their slow grazing, and somewhere in a deep alcove a child looks up from their sorted carapaces and hears something new.

Or maybe none of this happens and the wise power sits and the old person sits and the mesa holds them both and the night comes and the beetles glow and nothing changes and that, too, is an outcome that the wise power accepts, because **benevolence that cannot accept refusal is not benevolence but colonialism in a gentle mask.**

---

## What the Wise Power Would Not Do

It would not bring water. The scarcity of water is not the problem. The scarcity of water is the *architecture* — it is what makes the place coherent, what drives the condensation cycle, what necessitates the machines, what gives the people their role. Flood the mesas and you don't save anyone; you dissolve the logic that holds everything together and leave sixty confused people standing in mud where a civilization used to be.

It would not bring people. More humans would exceed the carrying capacity, disrupt the distribution pattern, overwhelm the communication mode. The mesas' human population is the size it is for reasons that are woven into the canyon walls.

It would not explain. The wise power has no diagnosis to deliver, no revelation that would restructure how the people understand themselves. They understand themselves with a completeness that most cultures never achieve. The diagram on the metal plate is as sophisticated a piece of ecological self-knowledge as exists anywhere. They don't need to be told what they are. They might — *might* — benefit from being reminded of what else they *could* be, but that reminder must come as a whisper, a signal at the edge of reception, a passage that opens onto an unexpected margin, a presence that sits beside you and smells of elsewhere. Not a lecture. Not a map. A *possibility*, left where it can be found or ignored.

It would not fix the population decline. If the people are choosing to have fewer children, that choice is sacred — sacred in the original sense, *set apart*, not to be touched by outside hands. If the community is contracting toward an end, the wise power must be willing to witness that end with the same respect it would give a flourishing. **Not every story needs to continue forever. Some stories are meant to be complete.** The mesas may be a civilization in its final movement, a long diminuendo, and the right response to a diminuendo is not to crank up the volume. It is to listen more closely as the sound thins.

But — and this is the tension the wise power holds without resolving — **the wise power must also ensure that the ending, if it is an ending, is chosen with full knowledge and not merely the result of a rut worn so deep it has become invisible.** A people who choose to conclude their story while knowing that other stories are possible — that is dignity. A people who dwindle because they have forgotten that alternatives exist, because the system they built has become so total that the concept of "alternative" has been optimized away — that is a tragedy wearing the mask of peace.

The signal. The margin. The presence.

Three things, offered without insistence, visible without demanding to be seen.

The rest is theirs.

---

## What Happens After

The wise power does not know. That is part of its wisdom — the willingness to act without controlling outcomes, to introduce a signal and accept that it might be received as noise, to open a passage and accept that it might go unexplored, to sit beside someone and accept that its presence might mean nothing.

But it watches. From a distance now — from the edge of the plateau where the mesas break apart into pillars and the pillars into air. It watches the beetle-light at night. It listens for changes in the silence — not hoping for noise, but attentive to new qualities of quiet. A quiet that includes awareness of the signal is different from a quiet that doesn't. A quiet that knows about the basin with its weeks-long water is different from a quiet that has never imagined surplus. These differences are subtle. They may take a generation to manifest. The wise power is patient.

And sometimes — rarely, unpredictably — it hears something. Carried on the thermal updraft from the deepest canyon, attenuated almost to nothing, there rises a sound that is not a wren's liquid note and not the papery snap of a kiteback's membrane and not the faint static of beetle-grazing and not the sub-audible turn of a piston.

It might be singing.

It is so quiet it might be anything. It might be wind through a slot canyon shaped just so. It might be the harmonic of a pipe vibrating at a frequency it was never designed to carry. It might be nothing.

But the wise power listens, and does not look away, and does not intervene further, and holds in its vast and patient heart the possibility that somewhere in the labyrinth of hot stone and engineered water and blue-green beetle-light, a person has opened their mouth and made a sound that rises, and rises, and does not come back, and that this is not a loss but a sending — a signal answering a signal — a line on a diagram that extends past the edge of the metal plate and into the air and onward, onward, toward whatever the circle-with-descending-line has always meant, which is not *down* and not *root* and not *us* but something that includes all three and more:

*We are here. We are this. And we are not only this.*

The mesa holds the heat. The engine turns. The water falls. And in the silence, which is not empty, which has never been empty, something new is listening to itself for the first time.
