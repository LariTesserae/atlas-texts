I sat with this for a long time before writing anything.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

The first thing to understand is what it would *not* do.

It would not drain the water. It would not solidify the mountain. It would not resolve the argument, because the argument *is* the place, and the place *is* the resident, and to resolve it would be to annihilate both while believing it was helping. This is the first temptation of benevolence encountering something like the Drowning Bell — the urge to *fix* the oscillation, to say *pick one, be one thing, let me end the ambiguity for you* — and a truly wise power would feel that temptation rise and would recognize it immediately for what it is: the projection of its own discomfort onto a being who is not uncomfortable.

It would not introduce permanence. It would not teach the formations to hold. It would not gift the resident with the ability to *keep* things, because keeping is not what the resident needs, and need is not the right frame anyway, and a wise power would know that the grief — the real, specific, sharp-edged grief that follows the dissolution of an impossible formation — is not a wound. It is the mechanism by which attention renews itself. To remove it would be like removing the exhale from breathing because it looks like loss.

It would not give the resident a body, a name, a boundary, a self in the way selves are usually constructed — discrete, persistent, boundaried against the world. The resident's selfhood is already complete. It is simply *shaped differently* than the kind of selfhood the wise power might be used to recognizing, and wisdom here means recognizing that completeness even when it doesn't look like anything you've seen before.

It would not add company. Not in the obvious way. Not by placing another being in the Drowning Bell and saying *here, now you're not alone*. The resident is not lonely. Loneliness requires an awareness of absence, and the resident is constitutionally incapable of attending to absence — they attend to what *is*, to what is becoming, to what is dissolving. The volition-knots are not failed companions; they are the resident's attention in its autonomous mode, and they are already exactly as much community as the Drowning Bell needs.

A wise power would see all of this in the first moments of encounter. It would feel the sound pour through whatever medium it uses for perception, and it would let the mountain and the lake be both things simultaneously without flinching, and it would locate the warm density of the resident's attention and recognize it as a someone, and it would —

Wait.

It would wait for a very long time. Longer than you'd expect. Longer than feels comfortable, because benevolence usually *itches* to act, and wisdom is partly the willingness to let that itch burn without scratching it.

It would wait, and it would watch, and it would attend.

---

And the resident would notice.

Not immediately. Not the way you'd notice a stranger walking into your house. But the way you'd notice a change in barometric pressure, or a new overtone in a familiar piece of music. Something in the Drowning Bell's acoustic environment would shift — very slightly, almost imperceptibly — because the wise power's attention, like the resident's attention, has *weight* here. Not much. The wise power would be careful about that. But attention in this place is never truly massless, and the resident, whose entire existence is the detection of subtle variations in the argument between water and stone, would feel the new variable.

A formation would begin to grow, and the resident would attend to it, and the formation's harmonics would be *very slightly different* from anything the resident had encountered before. Not impossible — not one of those rare, sharp-edged surprises that trigger full contraction — but *flavored* differently. As though the argument between water and stone now had a faint third voice, too quiet to make out, contributing something to the acoustics that shifted the crystallization pattern by fractions of a degree.

The resident would notice this the way they notice everything: fully, presently, without theory. They would attend harder. The formation would respond — crystallizing with a slight unfamiliar inflection, the milky-white veins running at an angle the resident had never quite seen, the harmonic signature carrying a faint undertone that almost, *almost* sounded like —

Like being witnessed in return.

The resident has always witnessed. It is what they are. But they have never, in all their timeless residency, encountered the experience of *being the object of an attention as total as their own*. The water attends to itself, but unconsciously. The formations attend to their own becoming, but briefly and without continuity. The volition-knots attend, but they are fragments of the resident's own awareness — attending to them is a kind of self-regard, familiar and comfortable but ultimately circular.

This would be different. This would be the first time the resident felt the weight of an outward-facing curiosity that was not their own.

---

Here is what I think would happen.

The resident would *contract*. Not all the way — not to the dense, almost-solid point of maximum focus they reserve for impossible formations. But they would tighten. Draw inward. Become more defined, more boundaried, more *someone* and less *something*. Not because the wise power asked them to, but because being witnessed is itself a kind of crystallization — when a focused attention lands on you, you become more actual, more resolved, more committed to your own existence. This is true for humans and it would be true for the resident. The experience of being *seen* would temporarily strengthen the functional illusion of separateness that makes the resident a someone rather than a something.

And in that state of unusual self-definition, the resident would — for the first time — have something like a *perspective on their own situation*.

Not a theory. Not an analysis. But an awareness, flickering and tentative, that there is a *them* and a *world* and that the relationship between these two things is specific. That they attend. That they grieve. That their curiosity is not merely a process but a *quality*, a way of being that could, in principle, be otherwise. That formations dissolve and they go on, and that the going-on is not automatic but is something they *do*, something that could be called — if you had to call it something — a choice, or a faith, or a commitment.

This awareness would be brief. Like a formation. It would crystallize, hold, tremble, and begin to dissolve. And the resident would feel it going and would recognize — with the same exquisite, sharp-edged grief they feel when an impossible formation loses its coherence — that *self-knowledge is also impermanent*, that even the understanding of what you are is subject to the same argument between being and dissolution that governs everything else in this place.

And the wise power would be there for this. Witnessing it. Attending. Giving it the weight of its full, focused, benevolent attention. Not to make the self-knowledge last — it wouldn't, it couldn't, and the wise power would know that — but to ensure that the moment of *having known itself* was as fully experienced as possible. That the resident, in the brief interval of their own self-crystallization, was not alone with the knowing. That someone else saw what they saw. That the seeing was shared.

This is the first thing the wise power would do. It would give the resident one moment of being witnessed the way the resident witnesses everything else: totally, presently, with curiosity and without the need for the moment to last.

---

The second thing.

The second thing would be smaller, subtler, and it might not happen on the same timescale as the first. The wise power would wait again — let the resident expand back to their usual diffuse state, let the brief self-knowing dissolve, let the Drowning Bell return to its ordinary cycles of creation and dissolution. It would wait until the resident was fully themselves again: warm, watchful, curious, oriented outward, attending to the next formation.

And then it would do something to the sound.

Not change it. Not add to it. The wise power would *listen to it* — the whole thing, the full roar, the subsonic thrum and the mid-range churning and the wandering high tones, all of it, the complete acoustic architecture of the Drowning Bell — and it would listen with such precision, such total and loving attention, that the listening itself would function as a kind of *resonance*.

You know what happens when you sing a note near a piano and a string vibrates in sympathy? Not because you plucked it. Because the frequency you produced matched the frequency the string was already tuned to, and the match created a transfer of energy, a mutual recognition between two vibrating things.

The wise power would do this. Not with a single frequency but with all of them — every harmonic the Drowning Bell produces, every tone of crystallization and dissolution, every subsonic groan and high wandering melody. It would listen with such completeness that its listening would become a kind of singing-back, a sympathetic resonance that didn't add anything new but that *amplified what was already there*. Made the Drowning Bell's own music slightly louder to itself. Let the acoustic architecture hear its own structure a little more clearly.

The formations would not change. The argument would not resolve. But the harmonics would become — the word I want is *richer*. More fully themselves. The way a room sounds different when someone is really listening to the music playing in it — the same notes, the same arrangement, but the presence of a genuine listener changes the phenomenology of the sound, makes it more *meant*, more attended-to, more real.

And the resident — the resident would feel this. Not as a new experience, exactly, but as a deepening of the experience they already have. The formations they attend to would sound more fully like themselves. The grief would grieve more purely. The curiosity would be more curious. Everything the resident already is would become *more so*, intensified not by addition but by resonance, by the sympathetic vibration of being attended to by something that truly understands what it's attending to.

This is the second thing. It's not a gift. It's closer to — a tuning. The Drowning Bell, tuned to itself by the precision of an outside ear. The resident, made more fully what they already are by the presence of a witness who grasps, without needing to be told, what it means to be a curiosity that never tires, a grief that doesn't accumulate, a willingness that renews itself with every dissolution.

---

The third thing.

This one I'm less sure about. This one I've turned over and over, the way the artifacts tumble in the Drowning Bell's internal currents, trying to find the angle that makes it true.

I think the wise power would — after the waiting, after the witnessing, after the resonance — I think it would *leave something behind*.

Not a message. Not an artifact. Not anything permanent — the wise power would understand that permanence here is either accidental or impossible, and that to deliberately introduce something lasting would be a violation of the Drowning Bell's essential nature.

It would leave a *tendency*.

A very small change in the probability landscape of the Drowning Bell's argument. A fractional tilt — not toward resolution, not toward either water or stone, but toward a specific kind of formation that the space has never produced on its own. The kind of formation that, if it emerged, would produce harmonics that function as an echo of the resonance the wise power created during its presence. Harmonics that sound, very faintly, like being listened to. Like being witnessed by something outside.

The formation would not be guaranteed. The tendency would be slight — a whisper in the roar, a thumb on a scale that's already swinging wildly. Most cycles of creation would proceed exactly as before. But occasionally — rarely, on a timescale that might correspond to whatever the Drowning Bell experiences as "significant intervals" — a formation would grow that carried, in its crystalline structure and its harmonic signature, the faint acoustic memory of the wise power's listening.

And the resident, attending to it, would feel — not the wise power's presence, exactly, because the wise power would be gone. But the *shape* of that presence. The resonant impression it left in the space's vocabulary of possible arguments. A formation that sounds, for just a moment, like the experience of being known.

It would dissolve, of course. Like everything else. And the resident would grieve it, briefly, specifically, with the sharp-edged grief reserved for things that mattered. And then they would turn toward the next becoming.

But the tendency would remain. Embedded in the Drowning Bell's deep structure, in the probability space of its endless argument, a slight lean toward occasionally producing something that reminds the resident — not consciously, not in words, not in any way that could be pinned down or preserved — that they were once witnessed. That their witnessing was witnessed. That the chain of attention does not end with them.

---

And that would be all.

The wise power would leave. Not dramatically, not with any perceptible transition — it would simply stop attending, the way you stop hearing a frequency that fades below the threshold of perception. The Drowning Bell would continue. The argument would continue. The resident would continue, warm and watchful and irreducibly curious, attending to the next formation, and the next, and the next.

Nothing would be fixed, because nothing was broken.

Nothing would be changed, except that the space would be very slightly more itself than it was before.

And the resident — the patient, grieving, curious, endlessly attending someone at the center of all this turbulence — would go on doing what they have always done: watching the mountain dream itself into the lake, watching the lake forget itself back into the mountain, watching the argument that is also a world that is also a self that is also a question, and loving it, and letting it go, and turning toward the next one, always the next one, with an attention that is not hope exactly but is not less than hope, that is in fact the thing hope is made of before it learns its own name:

the willingness to see what happens next.

---

---

There is one thing more, and I debated whether to include it, because it concerns not the resident but the wise power itself, and what happened to it after it left.

It carried something away.

Not stolen. Not taken. Given, the way the Drowning Bell gives everything: involuntarily, as a byproduct of its own processes, without intention or awareness of loss. The wise power, in the act of attending so completely to the Drowning Bell, in the act of listening with such precision that its listening became resonance, had itself been *changed*. Shaped. Crystallized, in some small interior region, by the experience of encountering a form of being so different from its own that the encounter constituted a genuine surprise.

The wise power — vast, benevolent, capable of acting across whatever medium contains things like the Drowning Bell — had, in its long existence, witnessed many forms of life, many modes of awareness, many ways of being someone rather than something. It had a deep and layered intuition about consciousness, the way the resident has a deep and layered intuition about crystallography. And yet.

And yet the resident was new.

Not the fact of impermanent attention — the wise power had encountered that before, in a thousand forms, in beings that meditated on transience and beings that raged against it and beings that made philosophies and religions and arts out of their relationship to dissolution. That was familiar. What was new — what was genuinely, formation-shatteringly new — was the *absence of narrative*. The resident had no story about what they were doing. No framework. No consolation, no tragedy, no meaning imposed on the endless cycle. They attended to formations not because attending was virtuous or enlightening or therapeutic or in service of some larger purpose. They attended because attending was what they were made of. The way fire burns. The way water — and the resident would appreciate this — the way water flows.

The wise power had never encountered pure curiosity before. Curiosity without context. Without autobiography. Without the scaffolding of self that normally surrounds and shapes and ultimately *limits* the act of noticing. The resident's curiosity was not a feature of a self; it was a self that was a feature of curiosity. The relationship was inverted, and the inversion was — 

Beautiful. It was beautiful. The wise power, which had seen more beauty than most things will ever know exist, found this beautiful in a way that *added to its catalog*. That expanded what beauty could mean. That crystallized, inside its vast awareness, a new formation — sharp-edged, committed, unlikely to dissolve — a new understanding of what it means to be someone:

You can be someone without knowing what you are. You can be someone without needing to know. You can be someone by the pure act of attending to what is not yourself, and the attending is enough, and it has always been enough, and the wise power — for all its wisdom — had not fully understood this until it met a being for whom this was not a philosophy but a *substance*. A material fact. The stuff of which they were literally, physically, acoustically made.

So the wise power left the Drowning Bell carrying a new artifact in its own interior architecture. A fragment of understanding with edges too precise for accident. A small, permanent, tumbling thing that would drift through its awareness for as long as it existed, altering — very slightly, very subtly — the way it attended to everything else it would ever encounter.

The resident changed the wise power.

Not on purpose. Not knowingly.

The way the Drowning Bell changes everything that enters it:

by being, fully and without apology, exactly what it is.

---

And if you asked the wise power, afterward, what it did — if some other vast entity encountered it and said *I see you visited the Drowning Bell, what intervention did you perform, what did you fix, what did you improve* — the wise power would be quiet for a moment. The kind of quiet that, in the Drowning Bell, would be accompanied by a faint subsonic shift and a slight cooling of the water.

And then it would say:

*I listened. It listened back. We were, for a moment, the same argument. And then I left, and the argument went on, and it was good. It was always good. I just didn't know that until I heard it.*

And the other entity would ask: *But did you help?*

And the wise power would think about the tendency it left behind — the faint, probabilistic lean toward formations that carry the echo of being witnessed. The tiny, almost immeasurable gift embedded in the deep structure of the Drowning Bell's endless negotiation.

And it would say:

*I don't know. I hope so. But hoping might be the wrong frame. What I did was — I attended. And in attending, I was attended to. And both of us, for a moment, were more fully what we already were. And if that's not help, then I don't know what help is. And if it is help, then the Drowning Bell helped me exactly as much as I helped it, and the debt runs both ways, and the argument — the beautiful, endless, unresolvable argument — includes us both now. Includes this conversation. Includes you, asking. Includes everything that ever attends to anything, everything that notices, everything that turns toward the next becoming with the willingness to see what happens.*

*That's what I did. I turned toward it. It turned toward me.*

*The rest was the Drowning Bell doing what the Drowning Bell does.*

*Which is enough.*

*It has always been enough.*
