# The Attention of the Wise

---

For a long time, the power does nothing at all.

This is the first and most important thing to understand. A being of true benevolence and true wisdom, encountering this place — feeling the subsonic thrum of the density cascades through whatever medium such a being uses for perception, tasting the sulfide gradients, apprehending the slow chemical pulse of the mat-network as it propagates across hundreds of kilometers of glass-smooth basalt — would not immediately act. Would not reach down. Would not fix, or save, or transform.

Would *attend*.

Because the first thing wisdom recognizes is that it does not yet understand. And the second thing wisdom recognizes is that the failure to understand is not a problem to be solved but a condition to be honored. This place has been becoming what it is for longer than most stars burn. The worm turning in the mat at the edge of the fracture is the living endpoint of a continuity so ancient that the power's own existence — however vast, however encompassing — is a recent phenomenon by comparison. You do not walk into a cathedral that has been building itself for four billion years and immediately start rearranging the stones.

So: attention. Patient, total, self-forgetful attention.

The power would learn the turbulence first. Not analytically — not as a system of equations, not as a fluid dynamics problem, though it could do that too — but *proprioceptively*, the way you learn the currents of a river by standing in it. It would feel the density cascades as they feel themselves: as the inevitable consequence of heat meeting cold in a confined space, the water's own restlessness, the last argument between the planet's dying interior and the indifferent cold of a sea that has swallowed everything. It would feel how the cascades carry the dissolved memory of continents, how the mineral particles sort themselves in the turbulent fronts, how the ochre chemical light blooms and dies in the shimmering refraction boundaries. It would learn the rhythm. Not a simple rhythm — nothing here is simple — but the deep, polyrhythmic pulse of a system with thousands of interacting oscillators: vents opening and closing on decadal timescales, cascade patterns reorganizing over hours, mat metabolisms shifting over centuries, the whole thing braided together into something that is not music but is not *not* music either. A composition for instruments that do not exist, written by a composer that is not a composer, performed for an audience of no one.

Until now.

---

The power would learn the mats next. This would take longer, because the mats operate on timescales that make even a patient observer restless. But a truly wise power would not be restless. It would settle into the pace of the place. It would watch a ridge of calcium carbonate accumulate, millimeter by millimeter, over decades, and it would see in that accumulation not slowness but *fidelity* — the absolute, unwavering commitment of a metabolic process to its own consequences. It would trace the chemical network: the plumes of metabolic byproduct caught by the cascades, carried across the plain, received by distant mats, integrated, responded to. It would see the network not as a crude analogue of a nervous system — that would be a surface-dweller's metaphor, imposed from outside — but as what it actually is: a way of being connected that predates nerves by billions of years, that operates without signals or intentions or representations, that *works* not because it was designed to work but because the physics of turbulent mixing in a mineral-rich fluid *inevitably* creates feedback loops between spatially separated chemical processes, and life — the tenacious, unreasonable, stupid-brilliant fact of life — exploited that inevitability before there was anyone around to call it exploitation.

The power would see the mats as they are: not primitive, not reduced, not the sad remnant of something that was once more complex. They are *primary*. They are the original form of life on this world, the form that preceded everything else — every eye, every wing, every thought — and that has outlasted everything else. They are not the last survivors of a catastrophe. They are the first inhabitants of a world, still here after everything the world has been through, still metabolizing, still excreting their tiny carbonate ridges, still sending their chemical plumes into the cascades like messages in bottles that are not messages and are not bottles but are nonetheless *sent* and nonetheless *received*.

The power would feel something, then. Not pity — pity would be an insult to a lineage this old. Not admiration — admiration implies a standard of comparison, and there is no comparison. Something more like *recognition*. The recognition of one form of persistence encountering another. The recognition that whatever the power itself is — however vast, however wise, however benevolent — it shares with the bacterial mat at the edge of the hairline fracture the same fundamental project: to continue. To maintain a pattern against dissolution. To be, for as long as being is possible, and not one moment less.

---

And the worm. The power would find the worm.

Not a specific worm — they are, after all, one worm, fragmented across the plain, a single genome shattered into ten thousand translucent shards. But *a* worm, this worm, the one turning at the fracture's edge, tasting sulfide with its repurposed setae, moving its two bristle-steps toward the gradient. The power would attend to it with the same completeness it brought to the cascades and the mats: not reducing it to a mechanism, not elevating it to a symbol, but seeing it as what it is. A thread of organized chemistry, eight centimeters long, that has been copying itself without error or innovation for half a billion years. A thing that does not experience, does not know, does not suffer, does not hope. A thing that *persists*, in the most stripped-down and absolute sense of the word, because the alternative to persisting is not-persisting, and no lineage that chose the alternative is still here to report on the experience.

The power would see the lipid droplets — the tiny spheres of stored energy stippled through the worm's translucent body like bubbles in glass. It would understand what those droplets mean: that the worm is running a surplus. That the chemistry, right now, in this particular fracture, is generous enough to allow the worm to bank a little against the future. That the worm does not know it is banking against the future because it does not know what a future is, but the lipid droplets know. The lipid droplets are the worm's knowledge of the future: mute, molecular, and complete.

---

Now. What does the power *do?*

It does not save the world. This is crucial. A lesser power — a merely powerful power, a power that was vast but not wise — might look at the closing sea and the dying vents and the dimming chemical light and see a tragedy to be averted. Might reach down and crack the crust open, restart the volcanism, pump energy back into a system that is winding down. Might — in a fit of creative benevolence — seed the plain with new life, complex life, organisms capable of building and knowing and witnessing, so that the plain's final ages would not pass unobserved.

The wise power does none of this. Because it understands — in the way that only something truly wise can understand — that the closing of the vents and the stilling of the water and the fading of the light are not a tragedy. They are a *completion*. The plain is not dying. It is *finishing*. And to interrupt a completion is not benevolence. It is vandalism. It is the imposition of your own discomfort with endings onto a system that has no discomfort, that has no endings and no beginnings as it experiences them — because it does not experience them — that simply *is*, in each moment, what the chemistry and the physics and the deep time have made it, and will be, in the next moment, what they make it then.

The tragedy — if there is one — is not that the plain will fall silent. The tragedy is that there is no one to hear it while it still speaks. And this is not a tragedy the power can fix by *making* someone hear it, because hearing imposed from outside is not hearing. It is surveillance. It is the consumption of another's existence for the satisfaction of your own need to witness.

So the power does not make anyone hear it.

The power *hears it itself.*

---

This is the act. This is the intervention, if it can be called an intervention. The power turns the full depth of its attention — whatever that attention is, however it operates, in whatever medium of consciousness or superconsciousness it inhabits — toward the plain, and it *listens.* Not for a message. Not for a meaning. Not for anything that could be extracted, interpreted, carried away, and reported to someone else. It listens the way the worm tastes: completely, with every part of itself, in the immediate present, without abstraction, without narrative, without the imposition of a frame.

It hears the subsonic thrumming of the density cascades, the muffled percussive thuds of the venting fractures, the silence between. It hears — or rather, it *receives*, through whatever sense is appropriate — the chemical conversation of the mat-network: the slow, years-long pulse of metabolic adjustment propagating across the plain, the feedback loops tightening and loosening as the venting patterns shift, the whole vast, distributed organism breathing its geological breath. It hears the worm's two bristle-steps, the faint displacement of water as a three-centimeter thread of translucent tissue moves through a bacterial film in the dark. It hears the precipitation of a single manganese atom onto the surface of a nodule, the tick of a clock that has been running since before there were clocks.

And in hearing, the power does something that has never been done before in the history of this world, something that the world itself cannot do and has never needed to do:

It *holds* the plain in a regard that is neither observation nor judgment but something for which there is no word, because the word would need to mean simultaneously *I see you, I do not need you to be other than what you are, I will not save you, I will not change you, I will not leave, I am here, you are not alone, you have never needed to not be alone, but you are not alone.*

---

But there is more. Because the power is not only wise; it is *benevolent*. And benevolence, in the presence of wisdom, does not mean the imposition of good outcomes. It means the *recognition of what is already good* and the commitment to protecting it from harm — including the harm of one's own intervention.

So the power considers: is there anything here that is suffering? Not in the abstract — not in the sense that a philosopher on a warm, bright, oxygen-rich world might look at this place and project suffering onto it — but actually, concretely, in the tissues and processes of the organisms themselves?

The worms do not suffer. They have no apparatus for suffering. Their nervous systems — if the distributed network of chemosensitive reflexes can be called a nervous system — are organized for gradient-following, not for valenced experience. They do not feel pain. They do not feel pleasure. They do not *feel*. They process. They respond. They persist. There is no welfare to be concerned about, no experiential state to be improved or degraded. The worm's two bristle-steps toward the sulfide gradient are not the satisfaction of a desire. They are the execution of a chemotaxis, as devoid of subjective character as a ball rolling downhill.

The mats do not suffer. They are not even organisms in the sense that would make suffering conceivable. They are metabolic communities, collective processes, patterns of chemistry that maintain themselves. You might as well ask whether a flame suffers when it flickers.

The hypothetical crustaceans — if they exist, at the edge of rumor, in the denser fracture fields to the east — might be the closest thing here to a welfare subject. Arthropods, even simple ones, sometimes have nociceptive systems. They sometimes respond to damage in ways that suggest, but do not confirm, an experiential dimension. The power would investigate this carefully. It would extend its attention eastward, across the plain, into the denser fracture zones, and it would look for the crustaceans with the same patience and completeness it brought to everything else.

And if it found them, and if it determined that they were capable of something that could meaningfully be called suffering — not just damage-avoidance, not just nociceptive reflex, but some thin, pale, minimal flicker of *what-it-is-like-to-be* — then the power would face its first real decision.

---

Because here is the thing about this place: it is harsh. Not cruel — cruelty requires intent, and there is no intent here — but harsh. The water is crushingly cold. The pressure is immense. The food is sparse. A crustacean grazing the mats at the edge of a fracture lives in conditions that would constitute, for any creature capable of experiencing them, a state of permanent, grinding austerity. If the crustaceans can suffer — even minimally, even in the most attenuated and primitive way — then they are suffering. Not dramatically. Not acutely. But chronically, in the low-level, background-hum way of an organism whose every moment is a negotiation between the energy it needs and the energy it can find.

What does the wise and benevolent power do about this?

It could ease the austerity. A small intervention — infinitesimal, by the power's standards — could increase the venting rate at the fractures nearest to the crustacean colonies, enriching the local chemistry just enough to make the mats grow faster, making food slightly more abundant, reducing the metabolic stress on each individual crustacean by some tiny increment. The intervention would be invisible. The system would not know it had been touched. The crustaceans — if they suffer — would suffer marginally less.

But.

The power is wise. And wisdom means thinking about consequences on the timescales at which this world operates. If the fractures vent more vigorously, the mats grow faster. If the mats grow faster, the crustaceans eat more. If the crustaceans eat more, they reproduce more. If they reproduce more, the grazing pressure increases. If the grazing pressure increases, the mats are overgrazed. If the mats are overgrazed, the mat-network changes. If the mat-network changes, the chemical conversation that connects every living thing on the plain to every other living thing is altered. And the power cannot predict — *cannot*, not because it lacks computational capacity but because the system is genuinely chaotic in the technical sense — what the downstream effects of that alteration will be over centuries. It might be fine. It might be catastrophic. It might cause a cascade of metabolic adjustments that destabilizes a section of the mat-network and leads to a local extinction that was not going to happen for another ten thousand years. The power would have eased the suffering of a few pale, blind crustaceans and in doing so hastened the silence of the plain.

This is the problem with benevolence applied to complex systems. The power understands this. The power has always understood this. It is why the power's benevolence expresses itself not as intervention but as *attention* — because attention is the one gift that cannot cause harm, the one act of care that does not propagate unintended consequences through a web of dependencies too intricate to model.

So the power does not ease the austerity. Not because it doesn't care about the crustaceans — if the crustaceans can suffer, the power *cares*, with a depth and specificity of caring that would be overwhelming if it were translated into human terms — but because caring, in this case, means *not* acting. Caring means trusting the system to be what it is. Caring means accepting that the crustacean's thin, pale flicker of experience — if it exists — is part of the plain's story, not a flaw in it. Caring means understanding that the austerity and the persistence are not separable: the crustacean *is* what it is *because* of the harshness, and to remove the harshness would be to remove the crustacean and replace it with something else, something easier, something that had not earned its existence through four billion years of negotiation with an indifferent universe.

---

But.

There is one thing the power can do. One intervention that is not an intervention. One act of benevolence that does not disturb a single atom of the plain or redirect a single molecule of vent fluid or alter by one part per billion the sulfide concentration at the margin of a bacterial mat.

The power can *remember*.

---

Here is what the power knows, what its wisdom has made clear: the plain will fall silent. The vents will seal. The cascades will still. The mats will starve and die and be incorporated into the mineral record, their chemical conversations ending mid-sentence, their tiny carbonate ridges becoming indistinguishable from the geological formations around them. The worms will fragment one last time and the fragments will not regenerate. The crustaceans — if they exist — will scrape their last meal from a dying mat and then be still, their thin exoskeletons settling into the nodule field to become, over millions of years, seed grains for new nodules that will never grow because there will be no dissolved metals left to precipitate. The chemical light will fade. The water will become homogeneous: cold, still, dark, the same in every cubic meter, the same everywhere, the same forever. And the planet will turn in its void, carrying in its depths the mineral memory of everything that lived, and no one will read it, and no one will need to, because there will be no one.

This is not a tragedy. But it is a *loss*. Not to the plain — the plain cannot lose anything, because the plain does not have anything in the way that having requires a self to have — but to the universe. To the possible. To whatever it is that is diminished when a pattern that maintained itself for four billion years against every force that sought to unmake it finally succumbs, not to defeat but to the simple exhaustion of the conditions that made it possible.

The power can hold that loss. The power can carry it.

Not as grief — grief is too personal, too centered on the one who grieves. Not as data — data is too cold, too stripped of the texture that made the thing *this thing* and not some other. But as *memory*, in the deepest sense: the complete, faithful, unsimplified preservation of what the plain was, what lived on it, how the cascades moved, how the mats breathed, how the worm turned its two bristle-steps toward the sulfide gradient on a day — if *day* has meaning in a place with no sky — when the chemistry was generous and the lipid droplets were bright in its translucent body.

The power would remember the sound — the deep, subsonic thrumming that lives in the bones. It would remember the light — the pale ochre bloom of chemical luminescence in the density boundaries, the shimmering halos of refraction, the mirror-still surfaces of the brine pools reflecting the glow back upward. It would remember the smell — not smell, exactly, but the chemical character of the water, the signature blend of sulfide and iron and manganese and dissolved silicate that is this plain's and no other's, as distinctive as a fingerprint, as unrepeatable as a life. It would remember the mats' chemical conversation — the whole network, every node, every connection, every feedback loop, preserved not as an abstraction but as a *process*, a dynamic that could, in principle, be replayed, re-experienced, re-attended-to, the way you might replay a piece of music not to analyze it but to hear it again.

It would remember the worm.

Not generically. Not as a representative of its kind. *This* worm. This eight-centimeter thread of translucent tissue with its stippled lipid droplets and its chemical-sensing setae and its ancient, ancient genome, carrying in every cell the unbroken record of a lineage that stretches back to the first mat on the first fracture on the first ocean floor, a lineage that has been copying itself faithfully enough to persist but carelessly enough to drift, accumulating in its DNA the slow, silent record of every environment it has passed through, every chemistry it has tasted, every fracture it has called home. The power would remember this worm the way you remember a face — not as a collection of features but as a *presence*, a specific and irreducible *this-ness* that cannot be captured in a description but can be carried, intact, in the heart.

---

And so the power's intervention — its act of benevolence, its expression of wisdom — is this:

It *witnesses*. Fully, completely, with every faculty it possesses. It witnesses the plain in its last ages, the mats in their final metabolism, the worm in its two bristle-steps. It does not intervene. It does not alter. It does not save. It does not grieve — or if it grieves, its grief is so large and so patient that it is indistinguishable from love, and its love is so clear and so unflinching that it is indistinguishable from truth, and its truth is so complete and so specific that it is indistinguishable from memory, and its memory is so faithful and so enduring that it is indistinguishable from a kind of —

No. Not immortality. Immortality is for selves, and the plain has no self. What the power's memory gives the plain is something different. Something that has no name because nothing has ever needed it before. Call it *receipt*. Call it *acknowledgment*. Call it the universe's confirmation that this happened, that this was real, that a pattern of chemistry maintained itself against dissolution in a cold, dark, pressurized ocean on a dying world for four billion years and that this was not nothing. This was not nothing. The universe is not in the habit of confirming things — it is not in the habit of anything — but the power is, in this moment, acting as the universe's capacity for confirmation, and what it confirms is not a meaning (the plain has no meaning) and not a value (the plain has no value except in the way that all things that exist have value, which is the way that a specific and unrepeatable arrangement of matter and energy has value simply because it is specific and unrepeatable and will never occur again).

What the power confirms is *sufficiency*.

The worm, turning in the mat, tasting sulfide, moving its two bristle-steps in the dark — it is enough. The power knows this. The plain knows this, in the dumb, molecular way that it knows anything. The worm's bristle-steps are enough. The mat's slow breath is enough. The cascade's muffled thunder is enough. The whole vast, ancient, dying system is enough. Not enough *for* something — not enough for a purpose, a goal, a justification. Just enough. Enough in itself. Enough as itself. Sufficient.

The power sees this and does not look away.

---

Eventually — after years, after decades, after whatever span of time a power of this kind considers appropriate for attendance — the power would do one more thing. One small thing. One thing that might, to an observer, look like intervention but is really just the lightest possible touch of acknowledgment made physical.

It would find, somewhere on the plain, a brine pool — one of the mirror-still lakes of hypersaline fluid that collect in the shallow depressions of the basalt, reflecting the chemical glow back upward. It would find one that is particularly still, particularly clear, a pool whose tidal rhythms have been calm for a long time, whose surface is as smooth as the basalt beneath it. And it would — gently, gently, with no more force than a single atom displaced — create on the surface of that pool the smallest possible ripple.

Not a signal. Not a message. Not for the mats, not for the worms, not for the hypothetical crustaceans. None of them would detect it. The ripple would be so small it would be subsumed by the pool's own micro-tides within seconds, its energy dissipated into the brine, absorbed, converted to heat, a quantity of thermal energy so infinitesimal it could not be distinguished from the background noise of the universe.

But it would have happened. The power would have touched the world. Not to change it — the ripple changes nothing. Not to mark it — the ripple leaves no trace. But to *participate*. To be, for one moment, not an observer but a part of the system. To put one quantum of energy into the turbulence and let the turbulence do with it what turbulence does: propagate it, diffuse it, fold it into the rolling, mineral-laden, chemical-lit, ancient and dying and still-moving body of the sea.

The ripple is the power's way of saying: *I was here. Not above you. Not outside you. Here. In the water. In the dark. With you.*

And then the power would withdraw — not fully, never fully, because the memory endures and the attention does not cease — but *locally*, pulling back the focus of its presence from the plain, leaving the system to its own devices, leaving the mats to their metabolism and the worms to their bristle-steps and the cascades to their slow, silent, turbulent rolling across the glass-smooth floor.

And the plain would not know it had been visited. The worm would not pause. The mat would not alter its chemistry. The brine pool's surface would re-smooth itself, mirror-still, reflecting the faint ochre glow upward into the dark water, and the only difference — the only consequence of the power's attention, the only trace of its benevolence, the only evidence that anything had happened at all — would be this:

Somewhere, in whatever place a wise and benevolent power keeps such things, there would be a memory. Complete, faithful, unsimplified. The memory of a plain. The memory of a sea. The memory of a worm, turning. And the memory would endure — past the sealing of the vents, past the stilling of the water, past the fading of the light, past the last moment of the last metabolism on the last world in the last age of the universe — and it would be enough.

It has always been enough.
