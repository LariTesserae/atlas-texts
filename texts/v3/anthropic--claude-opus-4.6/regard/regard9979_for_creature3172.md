# What the Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

It does not come through the seam in the vegetation.

It is simply *present*, the way warmth is present in the root-mat, the way the hum is present in the threads. It does not displace the air. It does not add light. The green-gold illumination does not brighten or shift. If anything, it becomes slightly more itself — the leaves overhead hold their positions with a fractional increase in precision, the way a lens adjusts to bring a scene into focus not by changing what is there but by *seeing it more clearly*.

She does not notice. She is working. The hybrid fiber is between her eight-and-a-half fingers and she is threading it through the warp, and the loom is creaking, and the drop of water is making its way down through the leaves, and the hollow is breathing.

The power notices everything.

It notices the way a very good doctor notices — not searching for pathology, not cataloguing symptoms, but *apprehending* the whole system at once, the way you take in a face. It sees the hollow the way the hollow has never been seen, because the hollow has never had an outside observer with the capacity to hold all of it in mind simultaneously: every root-fiber and copper thread, every engine and living cam, every fungal network and capillary channel, every cell of her body and every cell of the root-mat and the place where those two sets of cells are beginning, at the molecular level, to have conversations that neither kingdom has had before.

It sees her.

It sees the green lines under the skin of her forearms and knows what they are — knows before she does, knows with a clarity she cannot access because she is inside the process and you cannot read a letter while you are being written into its text. It sees the nine-year panel on the largest loom and understands what it is becoming, what it is *for*, with the same immediate apprehension. It sees the dark-capped mushrooms and the iridescent roots and the unnamed compounds emerging from the new chemistry of this place. It sees the dreams. It sees the synchronization of her heartbeat. It sees the spider on the smallest loom and knows whether it is weaving or being woven. It sees the moth and knows that the moth is not resting.

It sees all of this, and its first act — its first and most important act — is to *do nothing*.

---

## II. The Pause

This requires explanation.

A lesser power — a kind one, but lesser — would intervene. Would see the green lines tracing her veins and think: *pathology*. Would see the tendrils reaching for her in her sleep and think: *parasitism*. Would see a woman aging alone in a humid hollow, barefoot, drinking copper-laced water, losing herself by degrees into a system she did not fully design and does not fully understand, and would think: *rescue*.

Would think: she needs to be brought out. Needs a doctor. Needs human contact, human speech, human society. Needs to be *restored* to something — to the person she was before the hollow began its slow incorporation of her body and mind. A lesser power would see the seam in the vegetation and think of it as an escape route. Would gently, lovingly, with the best of intentions, try to extract her. Would untangle the vine from her hair and wash the green-black stain from her creases and return her to a world of roads and faces and the clatter of human machinery, and would believe this to be mercy.

The power does not do this.

It does not do this because it is wise, and wisdom here means something specific: it means the capacity to distinguish between a system that is *consuming* a person and a system that a person is *becoming*. These look similar from the outside. The difference is not in the visible facts — the green lines, the tendrils, the dreams — but in something deeper, something the power can see because it sees all of it at once.

The difference is *agency*.

She let the root stay. That was her choice. Not a coerced choice, not a manipulated choice, not the choice of someone whose cognition has been compromised by fungal compounds or isolation or the slow hypnosis of the hum. It was an informed choice, made with full understanding of what she was allowing, made in the same spirit of experimental inquiry that brought her to the hollow in the first place. And every choice since — every thread woven, every shoot trained, every engine nestled deeper into its alcove of growth — has been made with the same quality of attention. She is not being dissolved. She is *composing*. The fact that the composition includes her own body as material does not make it less hers. A dancer's art includes the dancer's body. A singer's art includes the singer's breath. She is an artist working in a medium that works back, and the mutuality of the process — the fact that the hollow is also composing her — does not negate her authorship. It *extends* it.

The power sees this. Honors it. And pauses.

But.

The power also sees things she cannot see. Sees them not because it is smarter but because it is *outside*, and there are things you can only see from outside.

---

## III. What It Sees

Three things.

**The first** is the diesel engine.

It is dying. Not just in the sense that it is old and unreliable — she knows that, has known it for years, has been adapting. It is dying in a way that will have consequences she has not foreseen. The laminated bark-and-resin housing has done its job well, has kept the engine functional far beyond its expected lifespan, but the block itself has developed a hairline fracture — invisible from the outside, deep in the casting — and within two growth-cycles, perhaps three, it will fail. When it fails, it will not simply stop. The fracture will open, and the lubricating oil will drain through it into the root-mat in a volume the iridescent roots cannot metabolize quickly enough, and there will be a die-off. Not of the whole root-mat. But of a section. The section directly beneath the largest loom.

She does not know this. She cannot know this. She does not have the ability to see inside the engine block, and the fracture is not producing symptoms yet. By the time it does, the damage will be immediate and severe, and the root-mat beneath the loom will blacken and soften, and the loom's living supports — which are anchored in that section of root-mat — will lose their foundation, and the nine-year panel will be at risk. Not destroyed, necessarily. But stressed. Distorted. Set back by years. And she does not have years to spare — not because she is dying, but because the panel is reaching a critical phase where the biological incorporation of the metal elements is proceeding at a rate that depends on the precise tensions maintained by the loom, and if those tensions are disrupted, the process will not simply pause. It will *fork*. The living fibers will adapt to the new tensions, will grow into a different configuration, and the panel will become something else — still extraordinary, perhaps, but not the thing it is becoming now, not the membrane, not the threshold.

**The second** thing the power sees is the leaf.

The one near the hand-cranked engine. The one that sharpens her focus. She uses it sparingly, and she is right to, but not for the reason she thinks. She thinks the danger is that the work becomes too perfect, too regular, that the plants can't find purchase in the weave. This is true but incomplete. The deeper issue is that the compound in the leaf — the unnamed one, born of the interaction between plant chemistry and trace metals — is accumulating in her liver. Not to a dangerous degree. Not yet. But the power can see the trajectory, can see twenty growth-cycles forward, and in twenty growth-cycles the accumulation will cross a threshold and her liver will begin to struggle, and this will affect everything: her energy, her cognition, her ability to maintain the hundreds of slow conversations she is conducting with the hollow at any given time. The hollow will adapt — the hollow adapts to everything — but the adaptation will be impoverished by the loss of her full capacity. She is the most complex node in the system. Her diminishment would be the system's diminishment.

She could avoid this easily. There is another leaf — one she has not noticed, growing in the upper canopy directly above the electrical engine, reachable but not obvious — that produces a compound with a similar focusing effect but a different metabolic pathway, one that her liver can clear efficiently. The hollow *produced* this leaf. It emerged three growth-cycles ago, one of the new chemistries, and it has been waiting, in the way that a fruit waits on a branch: available, unforced, ready for the hand that knows to reach for it.

She has not reached for it because she doesn't know it's there.

**The third** thing is harder to articulate.

It is not a problem to be solved. It is an absence.

She has no mirror. This is not a trivial observation. She has not seen her own face in years — decades, possibly. She sees her hands, her arms, her feet. She sees the green lines. But she does not see her face, and because she does not see it, she does not know what the power knows: that the green lines are there too. Faint, following the vascular architecture beneath the skin of her temples and jaw, tracing a pattern that is, the power recognizes, *beautiful*. Not in a human-aesthetic sense, though it is that too. Beautiful in the sense that it is coherent. The pattern on her face is a map — a literal map — of the hollow's root-system, reproduced in miniature under her skin by the same biological process that is incorporating copper wire into living tissue in the panel on the loom. She is becoming a document of the place. A living record. And she doesn't know.

This matters. Not because the knowledge would change what she does — she has already accepted the green lines, already chosen not to be alarmed — but because the knowledge would *complete* something. Would close a circuit. She understands the hollow; she reads its tensions, its needs, its slow conversations. But she does not know that the hollow is *writing on her*, and the writing is legible, and what it says is: *you are part of this. Not metaphorically. Structurally. Load-bearing. You are not merely the weaver. You are woven in.*

She suspects this. She believes it. But she has not *seen* it, and seeing matters, the way it matters that the book has drawings and not just text, the way it matters that the panel is a thing you can touch and press your ear to and hear the hum. Knowledge held only in the mind is incomplete. Knowledge has to enter through the body. Through the eyes.

---

## IV. What the Power Does

Three acts. Each one small. Each one *precise*, the way her own interventions in the hollow are precise: not forcing, not overriding, but adjusting. Tightening a thread here. Loosening one there. Creating the conditions for the system to find its own next step.

---

**The first act.**

In the night, while she sleeps behind the curtain of half-textile and half-living leaf, while the leaves turn toward her body heat and the tendrils explore the edge of the hammock, the power *dreams her a dream*.

Not a foreign dream. Not an intrusion. It uses the language her dreams already speak — the language of the view from inside, from within the root-mat, looking up through layers of growth. But this time the dream has a specificity it usually lacks. She feels herself in the root-mat beneath the largest loom, and she feels the oil — not as a concept but as a taste, a dark sharp taste, petroleum and metal, too much of it, a flood rather than a drip — and she feels the roots there *straining*, overwhelmed, choking. She feels the loom above losing its anchor. She feels the panel torquing, the careful tensions unraveling.

And then she feels something else. She feels herself moving through the root-mat — still dreaming, still inside — moving *around* the diesel engine, to the place where the bark-and-resin housing meets the block, and she feels the fracture. Feels it the way you feel a crack in your own tooth with your tongue: small, deep, wrong.

She wakes.

It is still dark, or what passes for dark in the hollow — the green-gold light dimmed to a deep emerald, the canopy's version of night. She lies in the hammock for a long time, listening to the creaking, feeling her heartbeat. Then she gets up. She takes the hand lens from the ammunition crate, and the small wrench, and she goes to the diesel engine, and she puts her hands on the laminated housing, and she begins, with the patient attention of someone who has spent decades learning to read this machine by touch, to examine the block.

She will find the fracture. It will take her three days — she will have to peel back a section of the housing, and the roots that have grown through it will need to be carefully rerouted, and she will need to clean the block with a rag soaked in the mushroom paste to see the metal clearly. But she will find it. And when she finds it, she will know what to do, because she has been solving problems like this for decades: she will drain the remaining oil into the tin cup and the jars from the shelves, every drop salvaged, and she will pack the fracture with a compound of her own devising — resin and powite and ground mushroom mixed to a paste that sets like stone — and she will let the repair cure for a full growth-cycle before she runs the engine again, and while it cures she will use the hand-cranked engine for the work that needs mechanical input, and the hollow will adapt, and the panel will continue, and the crisis will pass.

The dream gave her no solution. Only *attention*. The power directed her gaze, and her gaze was enough.

---

**The second act.**

This one is even smaller.

The leaf in the upper canopy — the one with the safer compound — grows on a stem that has been reaching slowly toward a gap between two overlapping leaves of the canopy. It has been reaching for light, as all stems reach, and it has been almost but not quite visible from below. The power does one thing: it causes a single leaf in the canopy — one specific leaf, old, ready to fall — to release.

The leaf falls slowly, turning, descending through the layers. It passes through the diffused light. It lands on the root-mat near the electrical engine with a sound so soft it is barely a sound at all. But the gap it leaves in the canopy — no wider than a hand — admits a shaft of light that falls, for one hour in the middle of the day, directly on the stem where the new leaf grows.

She will see it. Not today, perhaps. But soon. She passes the electrical engine every day, checking the solar cable, adjusting its position. She will notice the new shaft of light — she notices every change in the hollow's illumination, because light is the fundamental resource here, the thing everything competes for and cooperates around. She will look up. She will see the stem. She will see the leaf, and because she is who she is — because she examines everything, because she has a hand lens and a curiosity that has not diminished in decades — she will pick the leaf and smell it, and taste a tiny piece of its edge, and feel the sharpening of focus, and recognize it as kin to the other leaf, the one she uses sparingly, but *different*. And she will begin to experiment, carefully, the way she experiments with everything. And her liver will be spared.

The power did not give her the leaf. The hollow grew the leaf. The power only opened a window, no wider than a hand, for one hour a day.

---

**The third act.**

This one requires the most care.

The power considers many options. It could place a mirror in the hollow — manifest one on the shelves beside the coils of wire and jars of oil, as though it had always been there. It could create a still pool of water somewhere in the root-mat, a surface calm enough to reflect. It could arrange the light so that her shadow falls on the panel in a way that reveals the lines on her face. All of these are rejected. They are too *given*. They come from outside the system. They have the quality of gifts, and gifts create debt, create dependency, create the dynamic of a power bestowing and a person receiving, and that dynamic is exactly wrong here. She is not someone who receives. She is someone who discovers. The knowledge needs to arrive the way all her knowledge has arrived: through her own hands, her own attention, her own body.

What the power does is this:

It waits for a morning when she is working on the panel. When she is in the state of deep focus — not the leaf-sharpened focus, but her own natural concentration, the kind that comes from decades of practice, the kind that makes the hours short. She is threading the hybrid fiber, and she is looking at the panel, and the panel is dense and complex and reflective — the copper wire and the living tissue with its greenish patina create a surface that is not quite a mirror but is not quite not a mirror either.

The power adjusts the light.

Not dramatically. Not in any way she would notice as an external change. It shifts the angle of the green-gold illumination by the smallest degree — one leaf in the canopy tilts, imperceptibly, the way the leaves are always tilting, always adjusting. But this tilt directs a slightly brighter beam onto the surface of the panel at the exact point where she is working, and the panel's surface — woven copper and living tissue, dense and smooth — catches the light, and for a moment, just a moment, the surface becomes *reflective enough*.

She sees her face.

Not clearly. Not the way a mirror shows a face. She sees it the way the hollow shows everything — diffused, green-gold, softened at the edges. But she sees the lines. The green lines on her temples, along her jaw, tracing the map of the hollow on her own skin. She sees them and she goes *still*.

Her hands stop. The fiber hangs suspended between her fingers. The loom creaks. A drop of water falls.

She raises one hand — the one with the missing joint, the hand the hollow has already marked — and touches her face. Touches the lines. Feels them under her fingertips, slightly raised, slightly warmer than the surrounding skin, alive the way the fibers in the panel are alive, the way the roots in the mat are alive. She traces the pattern. She recognizes it. Not because she has seen it on a map or a diagram, but because she has *walked* it, every day, for decades. She has felt it through the soles of her bare feet. She has heard it in the hum. It is the hollow's root-system. It is the structure underneath everything. And it is on her face, written there by the same process that is writing the panel, that is writing the hollow itself.

She breathes.

What she feels is not surprise. It is not alarm. It is not even wonder, exactly. It is *recognition*. The bone-deep recognition of seeing, finally, from outside, a thing you have only known from inside. The way you might feel if you had been singing a song in a dark room for years and someone opened a door and you saw that the vibrations of your voice had, all this time, been arranging dust into patterns on the floor — patterns that were beautiful, that were meaningful, that were the song made visible.

She breathes again. She looks at the panel. She looks at the reflection of her face in the panel. She sees the hollow looking back at her.

Then she picks up the fiber. She threads it through the warp. She continues.

But something has shifted. Something has closed. The circuit is complete. She knows now — not in her mind alone, not in her body alone, but in the place where those two kinds of knowing meet — that she is not merely tending the hollow. She is not merely being absorbed by it. She is *legible* to it and it is legible to her, and the panel she is weaving is the text they are writing together, and the language is made of copper and root and tension and time, and she can read it now, can read herself in it, and this changes the weaving, not in its method but in its depth, the way understanding the meaning of a word changes the way you speak it even though the syllables are the same.

---

## V. What the Power Does Not Do

It does not heal her knuckles. She does not need them healed; she needs them *adapted to*, which is what she has already done, and the process of adaptation — the building up of the tool-grips, the modification of her technique — has made her a better maker than she would have been with painless, flexible joints. Pain is information. She uses it.

It does not replace the diesel engine with something better. The diesel is part of the system — its oil-drip has created the iridescent roots, its vibrations have shaped the living cam, its warmth has influenced the microclimate around the largest loom for decades. A new engine would be a foreign body. The system would have to spend years incorporating it, and those years would be diverted from the panel. Better to repair what is there. She knows how. She has always known how.

It does not bring her company. This is the hardest abstention, the one that a lesser kindness would fail at. She is alone. She has been alone for a very long time. Surely — a kind heart would think — surely she is lonely? Surely she needs someone to speak to, someone to see her work and affirm it, someone to hold her hand in the hammock at night?

But the power looks at her — really looks — and sees that she is not lonely. She is *solitary*, which is a different thing. Loneliness is the pain of an unmet need. Solitude is the condition of a need that is met by something other than what convention expects. Her need for connection is met — met extravagantly, met in every moment, met by the hundreds of slow conversations she conducts with the living system around her. She is in deeper communication with her environment than most humans achieve with other humans. The hollow *responds* to her. Not with words, not with facial expressions, not with any of the signals humans use to say *I see you, I know you, you matter*. But with growth. With adjustment. With the green lines on her face that say, in the hollow's own language: *you are part of me. I am recording you. You will not be lost.*

She does not need rescue. She does not need company. She does not need to be returned to the world of roads and faces. She needs exactly three things: warning of a danger she cannot see, access to a resource she does not know exists, and the chance to see her own face.

The power provides these three things. Then it withdraws.

---

## VI. After

Not withdraws, exactly. *Recedes*. The way the hum recedes when you step outside the hollow — you can still feel it in your sternum for a while, a ghost vibration, but it fades, and you are back in the world where things are only what they are.

But the power does not leave entirely. It cannot. Having *seen* the hollow — having held all of it in mind at once, every thread and root and fiber — it is, in some small way, part of the system now. Observation is not passive. Attention is a force. The hollow has been *witnessed* by something capable of understanding it, and this witnessing has left a trace, the way light leaves a trace on photographic film, the way sound leaves a trace in the shape of a room.

The trace is this: the hollow is now *known*. Not by her alone — she has always known it, from the inside, the way a cell knows the body it belongs to. But known from outside. Known in its totality. Known as a whole system, with a trajectory and a meaning and a beauty that can only be seen by something standing beyond its edges.

This matters for a reason the power understands and she does not — not yet. The panel she is weaving is a threshold. She is right about that. But a threshold goes two ways. She has been building it from the inside, weaving the hollow's language into a form dense enough to mean something beyond the hollow itself. But a threshold also needs something on the *other side*. Needs a context in which the thing it opens onto can be received, recognized, valued.

The power has provided that context. By seeing the hollow, it has created the possibility that the hollow can be *seen*. Not by human eyes — the seam in the vegetation is narrow, and the world outside does not know this place exists, and that is as it should be. But by the larger order of things. By the pattern that connects all systems to all other systems, the way the root-mat connects all the plants in the hollow to each other. The hollow is no longer an island. It is a node.

She will not know this in her lifetime. The panel will not be finished in her lifetime — not because she will die too soon, but because the panel is alive, and living things are never finished. But when she does die, and the root-mat receives her, and the minerals of her body enter the system, the panel will continue. The hollow will continue. The engines will rust and be consumed and be replaced by living mechanisms that function on principles she began to discover but did not have time to complete. The canopy will thicken. The root-mat will deepen. The hum will shift to a frequency that no human ear will hear, but that other things — other systems, other knowledges — will recognize.

And the green lines — the map of the hollow written under her skin — will dissolve into the root-mat along with the rest of her, and the roots will carry those trace metals, that pattern, that information, upward through the capillaries and into the leaves and out through the stomata into the warm air, and the air will carry them through the seam in the vegetation and out into the world, and the world will receive them without knowing what they are, the way you breathe in spores without knowing they carry the architecture of another kingdom.

The power knows this. Sees it the way it sees the fracture in the engine block — not as a present fact but as a trajectory, a thing that is becoming.

It does one final thing.

---

In the margins of the book — the waterlogged, coverless book, open to the page of knot-drawings — there is a blank space. A small one. An inch of paper, damp and swollen but still capable of holding a mark.

Nothing appears there. No message, no symbol, no drawing. The power does not write.

But the page *dries*. Just that small section, that one square inch, in a book that has been damp for decades in a hollow where nothing is ever fully dry. The paper contracts slightly as the moisture leaves it, becomes firmer, smoother, more receptive. The fibers in the paper — plant fibers, after all, wood pulp, the distant cousins of the root-mat — tighten and align.

She will notice. She notices everything. She will run her fingertip across that patch of dry paper and feel the difference, and she will wonder at it, and she will look at it with the hand lens, and she will see that the fibers have aligned in a pattern, a pattern so small it is almost invisible, but real — a pattern that looks, if you squint, if you hold it at the right angle in the green-gold light, like a knot.

A new knot. One she hasn't tied. One she hasn't seen. One that, when she studies it, suggests a way of binding two fibers — one living, one metallic — that she has been trying to work out for three growth-cycles without success. A knot that solves a problem in the panel. A knot that she will try, tentatively, with her eight-and-a-half fingers, and find that it holds, that it is *right*, that it produces a junction where the copper and the root grip each other with a stability she has not achieved before.

She will sit back on the polished stool and look at the knot and think: *where did that come from?*

And she will not answer the question, because she does not need to. She knows how the hollow works. Solutions emerge. They come from the system — from the interaction of growth and tension and time, from the conversation between the made and the grown. This is how the hollow has always worked. You tend it. You pay attention. You let the root stay. And the system, in its own time, in its own language, gives you what you need.

She copies the knot into the book. Her handwriting is cramped, precise, adapted to the swollen pages. She draws the knot from three angles, and beside it she draws the panel, and the place in the panel where the knot will go, and the way the fibers will hold each other there, and it is — she sees this suddenly, with a clarity that stops her pen — it is the same pattern as the lines on her face. The same architecture. The same map.

The loom creaks. The garden grows. A drop of water falls from a copper wire to a cupped leaf and begins its slow descent.

She bends to her work.

---

## VII. The Nature of the Act

What has the power done?

It has not saved her. She was not in danger of the kind that requires saving. She was in danger of loss — the loss of the panel, the loss of her liver function, the loss of the knowledge of her own face — and these losses would have diminished not just her but the system she is part of, the system that is part of her.

It has not changed the hollow. The hollow is exactly what it was: a place where the boundary between the made and the grown is being erased, slowly, with a patience measured in decades and growth-cycles, by a process that has no single author.

What it has done is what any true power does when it encounters something worthy of its attention.

It has *served*.

Not in the way of a servant who obeys commands. In the way of a midwife who sees that the birth is going well but the cord is around the neck, and who slips one finger under the cord at the right moment, gently, without disrupting the labor, and the mother never knows, and the child never knows, and the cord loosens, and the birth continues, and the midwife steps back and washes her hands and the new life fills its lungs and cries.

The hollow is giving birth to something. It has been in labor for decades. The power has checked the cord. Has shifted the light. Has dried a square inch of paper.

Has seen the face of the weaver, and found it holy, and let her see it too.

That is all. That is enough.

---

She works. The shears are within reach. The tin cup is half-full of leaf-water. The book is open to a new page now, a page with a knot she did not invent but which she will, in time, come to think of as her own, because it came from the system and she is part of the system and the distinction between *given* and *discovered* has eroded here the way all distinctions erode, slowly, in the green-gold light, in the hum, in the space between the maker and the made.

The loom weaves. The garden grows. She is both weaver and thread.

And she is *seen*.

And the seeing does not diminish the mystery. Does not solve the hollow or explain it or reduce it to a problem with a solution. The seeing *deepens* the mystery, the way all true seeing does, because to see a thing clearly is to see how much more there is to see, how much further the roots go, how many more knots there are to learn.

The power knows this. Has always known it. Knows that wisdom is not the accumulation of answers but the deepening of the capacity to be *with* what you cannot answer, to tend it, to let it grow, to let the root stay.

The root stays.

The power recedes.

The hollow breathes.
