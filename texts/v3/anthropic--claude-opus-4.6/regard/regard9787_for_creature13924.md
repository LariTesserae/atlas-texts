# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

It does not arrive. That is the wrong word. It *attends.* The way one becomes aware of a sound that was always playing — the way, in the Singing Dark itself, a creature might suddenly parse a single voice from the wall of noise and realize it had been hearing it all along.

The benevolent and wise power becomes aware of this place, and its first act is not an act at all.

It listens.

---

Not for a moment. Not for a night, or a season. It listens for a long time — long enough to hear the eight-second cycle of the Thrummers roll through its ten-thousandth repetition, long enough to watch the Veilflows' elder females replaced three times over, long enough to see a Pale Sleeper digest four meals and die between the fourth and what would have been the fifth. Long enough to feel the ravines deepen by a centimeter. Long enough to see the canopy shift as one species of tree begins, almost imperceptibly, to win.

It listens because the first thing wisdom knows is that *it does not yet understand.*

This is the critical point — the thing that separates a benevolent and wise power from a merely powerful one. A merely powerful entity arrives with a plan. It sees the frenzy, the overstock, the unsustainable density, the coming wave of extinctions, and it thinks: *I should fix this.* It sees the nascent intelligence scratching its first marks in the dark and thinks: *I should help this along.* It sees the Pale Sleeper's terrible patience and thinks: *cruelty,* or the parasitic larva eating the millipede from within and thinks: *suffering that should be prevented,* or the Veilflow that collides with the vine and tumbles and barely recovers and thinks: *fragility that should be shored up.*

A wise power thinks none of these things. Not yet. Not before it has listened long enough to understand that the frenzy is not a problem to be solved but a phase to be lived through. That the coming extinctions are not a tragedy to be averted but a refinement, a honing, the system finding its own shape. That the parasitic larva and the millipede are not a story about cruelty but a story about continuity — about the way life feeds on life and calls it neither good nor evil but simply *necessary,* and the millipede spirals upward glowing and does not suffer in any way that maps onto the wise power's understanding of suffering, and the larva does not intend harm in any way that maps onto the wise power's understanding of harm.

The wise power listens until it can hear the Singing Dark not as a problem, not as a patient, not as a child, but as *what it is:* a world in the act of becoming, loudly, messily, extravagantly, with a vitality so fierce it borders on joy.

And then — only then — does the wise power ask:

*Is there anything here that needs me?*

---

## II. What It Does Not Do

A catalog of restraint. This matters, because the temptations are real, and a lesser power would succumb to every one of them.

**It does not stabilize the system.**

The overstocked forest, the unsustainable density, the coming crash — these are not a disease. They are the way young ecosystems work. The frenzy is the exploration phase, the period when every possible strategy is tried simultaneously, when the space of biological possibility is searched by brute force, by throwing ten thousand species at a landscape and letting the landscape choose which ones fit. To stabilize this — to intervene, to thin the canopy, to cull the excess, to impose an equilibrium before the system has found its own — would be to short-circuit the search. It would lock in a configuration that is orderly but impoverished, stable but less creative, balanced but deaf to possibilities that only the frenzy can discover.

The wise power knows: the most interesting things in this forest — the Veilflows' collective sonar, the Weavers' mineral-reinforced silk, the Singers' weaponized beauty, the Laughers' referential calls — all of these emerged *because of* the pressure, the competition, the too-many-voices-in-too-small-a-space. Ease the pressure and you ease the invention. The Singing Dark is loud because it is creative, and it is creative because it is loud. To quiet it prematurely would be a kindness that destroys what it claims to protect.

**It does not accelerate the intelligence.**

The stone line. The spiral vine. The Laughers' curiosity. The Runners' engineering. These are the tender shoots of something remarkable, and the temptation to nurture them — to provide a nudge, a gift, a selective pressure that favors the cognitive over the instinctive — is almost overwhelming. Here, in this dark cradle, something is beginning to *think,* and wouldn't it be an act of supreme benevolence to help it along? To give the Laughers a slightly better vocal apparatus, a slightly larger neocortex? To give the Runners a material harder than basalt to work with? To ensure that whichever species is responsible for the marks survives the coming bottleneck?

No.

The wise power knows what acceleration costs. It has seen — or can see, or knows in the way that wise powers know — what happens to intelligence that is given rather than earned. It becomes brittle. It lacks the root system of ten thousand failures. It does not know, in its bones, why it thinks the way it thinks, because it was never forced to discover thinking for itself, one agonizing generation at a time, each generation building on the last, each failure teaching something that success never could.

The stone line is beautiful because it is *first.* Because nothing in this world has ever done it before. Because the creature that placed those stones did not know it was doing something new — it was just placing stones, just following an impulse it couldn't name, and the newness of the act is part of its power. To rush this, to push the creature toward the next step before it has fully inhabited this one, would be to rob the act of its meaning. The stone line is not a means to an end. It is not the first rung of a ladder that leads to civilization. It is *itself:* a mind's first mark on the world, complete and whole and worthy of awe exactly as it is, even if it never leads anywhere else.

**It does not reduce suffering.**

This is the hardest restraint, and the one most likely to be misunderstood.

There is suffering here. The prey animal drowning in the Pale Sleeper's coils suffers. The juvenile Laugher that falls into a ravine and breaks its legs and lies in the shallow water calling for a group that has already moved on — it suffers. The old Thrummer whose adhesive pads have worn smooth, who loses its grip on the bark and falls thirty meters to the forest floor and lies there, still pulsing its chord into the indifferent dark — it suffers. The suffering is real. It is not redeemed by its ecological function. It is not justified by the system's beauty. It simply *is,* in the same way that the bioluminescent glow simply is, in the same way that the thrumming simply is: a fact of this world, woven into its fabric, inseparable from the rest.

The wise power does not look away from the suffering. It sees it clearly — more clearly than any creature in the forest can see it, because the wise power can hold the full reality of what it means to be a drowning animal or a dying Thrummer without the protective filters of instinct or incomprehension. It feels the suffering as what it is. And it does not intervene.

Not because it doesn't care. It cares with a depth and a clarity that would be annihilating to any creature that felt it. It cares so much that it will not make the catastrophic error of confusing *its own discomfort at witnessing suffering* with *a mandate to eliminate suffering from a world that is not yet ready to exist without it.*

Because suffering, here, is not a malfunction. It is a signal. It is the system's way of communicating to its inhabitants the consequences of their choices, the costs of their strategies, the shape of the constraints they live within. The Laugher that falls into the ravine teaches the other Laughers — through its absence, through the abrupt termination of its voice in the chorus — that ravine edges are dangerous. This knowledge, purchased at a terrible price, becomes part of the group's behavioral inheritance. To prevent the fall is to prevent the lesson. And the next Laugher, and the next, will walk the same edge with the same ignorance, and the group will never learn, and their inability to learn will kill them in ways that are worse — not individually, but collectively, over generations — than the fall itself.

This is not a justification of suffering. The wise power does not *justify.* It recognizes. It holds the tension between compassion and respect — compassion for the creature that suffers, and respect for the world that requires the suffering, for now, as part of its own becoming.

Later, the equation may change. When and if the intelligence that is flickering in the dark becomes bright enough to *choose* — to consciously reduce the suffering of other beings, not because a power told it to but because it has looked at the suffering and decided, from its own hard-won understanding, that it will not tolerate it — *then* the suffering will begin to decrease, and it will decrease in a way that is real and earned and durable because it was *chosen* by the beings who live inside the system, not imposed from outside by a power that meant well but did not trust the world to find its own way.

**It does not reveal itself.**

This may be the most important restraint of all.

A benevolent power that reveals itself to a nascent intelligence distorts that intelligence forever. The Laughers, confronted with evidence of a power beyond their comprehension, would orient their curiosity toward it — toward pleasing it, or understanding it, or fleeing it — and away from the thousand other things they need to be curious about. The stone line would stop being an experiment in arrangement and start being an offering. The spiral vine would become a prayer.

The wise power knows that the most important thing about the stone line is that the creature who made it *thought it was alone.* Thought that the arrangement of stones was between itself and the world, unwitnessed, unjudged, unmotivated by anything except the strange internal pressure to *make this be so.* That aloneness — that sense of being the only thing in the universe that cares about the placement of a stone — is the raw material of autonomy. It is the beginning of the creature's ability to care about things *for its own reasons,* and that ability is more precious than any gift the wise power could bestow.

To reveal itself would be to say: *You are not alone. Someone is watching. Someone approves.* And the creature, hearing this, would feel relief, would feel comfort, and would also feel — forever after — that its acts exist in relation to the watcher. That the stone line is not *its own* but is *for someone.* And the seed of autonomous thought, the fragile beginning of a mind that creates because it chooses to create, would be contaminated at its root by the knowledge that creation has an audience, and the audience has power, and power has preferences.

The wise power stays hidden. Not out of indifference. Out of love. The kind of love that can bear to watch without being seen, that can care without being known to care, that trusts the beloved to find its own way even when the way is dark and difficult and the beloved is small and the forest is very, very large.

---

## III. What It Does

So if it does not stabilize, does not accelerate, does not reduce suffering, does not reveal itself — what *does* it do? Has it come all this way, attended to all this beauty and terror, only to do nothing?

No.

It does three things. They are small. They are, from the outside, almost invisible. They are the hardest things a wise power can do, because they require the discipline to act at the finest possible scale, with the lightest possible touch, in the service of a principle so subtle it can barely be articulated:

*Preserve the possibility space.*

---

### The First Act: It Prevents the Catastrophe That Would End the Story Before It Begins

Not the small catastrophes. Not the ravine collapses, the territory wars, the branch-falls, the predation events, the individual tragedies that are the daily currency of the forest. These are part of the story. These are the story.

But there is a catastrophe coming that is not part of the story — that is outside the system's ability to respond to, that would not teach any lesson because there would be no one left to learn it.

Beneath the basalt, deep in the fractured rock, the tectonic forces that raised this land are still active. A magma chamber, not large but pressurized, sits at a depth that is — by geological standards — dangerously shallow. Within a span of time that is short even by the forest's young standards, this chamber will breach. Not in a massive eruption — the chamber is too small for that — but in a volcanic event sufficient to split the lowland along one of its young ravines, flooding the valley with pyroclastic material, burying the forest under meters of hot ash, and sterilizing the soil that feeds the whole system.

This is not the forest's fault. It is not a consequence of the forest's choices. It is not a selective pressure that the forest can adapt to. It is simply bad luck — the wrong piece of land rising at the wrong time over the wrong piece of mantle. If it happens, the Singing Dark ends. All of it. The Thrummers, the Veilflows, the Laughers, the Runners, the stone line, the spiral vine, the nascent intelligence, the half-million interconnected experiments in being alive — all of it buried, cauterized, reset to zero.

The wise power prevents this.

Not dramatically. Not by reaching down and reshaping the geology with a visible hand. The intervention is so subtle that no instrument the forest's eventual scientists might build would ever detect it. A slight redistribution of thermal energy in the magma chamber, spreading the heat laterally, reducing the pressure at the critical point, encouraging the magma to find a different path — deeper, sideways, toward a vent that opens on the far side of a mountain range, away from the forest, where it will erupt harmlessly and noisily and give some future geologist a puzzle to solve.

The forest never knows. The forest goes on singing in the dark, unaware that it was a hair's breadth from annihilation, unaware that something shifted in the deep rock and gave it another million years. The intervention preserves nothing except *the chance for everything else to happen.* It does not determine what happens. It does not choose which species survive or which intelligence emerges or what the stone line eventually becomes. It simply keeps the stage from collapsing so that the play can continue.

This is the paradigm for all the wise power's interventions: prevent only what would end the story itself. Allow everything else.

---

### The Second Act: It Maintains the Diversity of Possibility

The forest is headed for a bottleneck. This is natural and expected — the frenzy cannot last, the overstocked system must simplify, many species will be lost. The wise power does not prevent this. The bottleneck is necessary; it is how the system matures.

But there is a difference between a bottleneck that narrows the stream and a bottleneck that poisons it. The wise power watches for the latter.

In the coming simplification, certain *types* of strategy, certain *categories* of biological innovation, are at risk of being lost entirely — not because they are unfit, but because of the random cruelty of small numbers. When a strategy is represented by only one or two species, the loss of those species (to bad luck, to a disease, to a single unusually cold year, to the cascading collapse of a food web they depended on) eliminates not just the species but the *approach* — the entire way of being alive that the species represented.

The wise power identifies these critical nodes — not specific species, but specific *kinds of solution* to the problem of being alive — and ensures that at least some representative of each kind survives the bottleneck. The interventions are vanishingly small. A fungal spore lands on the right log at the right time, providing food for a colony that would otherwise have starved. A rainstorm (one of the rare ones that penetrates the canopy) arrives two days earlier than it would have, filling a pool that a breeding population of a critical species needs for reproduction. A seed germinates in a crack that provides just enough shelter from competition for a rare plant species — the only one that practices a particular form of symbiosis — to establish itself and persist through the bottleneck.

None of these interventions change the trajectory of the system. They don't pick winners. They don't favor any species over any other. They maintain *the range of strategies available* — the width of the possibility space — so that when the system emerges from the bottleneck and begins to complexify again, it has the fullest possible palette of approaches to draw from. The wise power is not a gardener choosing which flowers to keep. It is a guardian of the seed bank, ensuring that no color is lost forever simply because of bad luck.

And here — here is where the wise power's attention to the stone line matters, but not in the way you might expect.

It does not protect the Laughers specifically. It does not protect the Runners specifically. It does not ensure the survival of whichever species made the marks. What it does is ensure that the *capacity* for what the marks represent — the cognitive substrate, the behavioral flexibility, the tendency toward curiosity and manipulation and pattern-imposition — is not accidentally eliminated from the system. If the Laughers go extinct but another species with similar cognitive potential survives, that is acceptable. If the Runners' engineering tradition is lost but the same combinatorial intelligence re-emerges in a different lineage, that is acceptable. What would not be acceptable — what the wise power acts to prevent — is the loss of the *possibility itself,* the elimination of the cognitive niche from the landscape of available strategies, so that no species ever again has the chance to place a stone in a line and wonder why it did so.

The wise power does not choose who inherits the future. It ensures that the future is *inheritable* — that the necessary ingredients are preserved, scattered, available, waiting for whatever combination of luck and pressure and time might assemble them into something new.

---

### The Third Act: It Witnesses

This is the act that is hardest to explain, because it produces no measurable effect on the forest, changes nothing in the physical world, alters no trajectory and prevents no catastrophe. And yet it may be the most important thing the wise power does.

It witnesses.

It pays attention. Fully. Completely. With the whole depth of its awareness brought to bear on this small, dark, screaming world and the creatures that live in it.

It witnesses the Thrummer on the trunk, pulsing its chord into the dark, and it understands what the Thrummer is — not just its biology, not just its ecological role, but its *experience,* to whatever degree experience exists behind those enormous lidless eyes. The wise power holds the reality of the Thrummer as the Thrummer cannot hold it itself: the full weight of what it means to be this creature, in this place, at this moment, pulsing sound into the dark for reasons it will never comprehend, participating in a synchrony it did not choose and cannot refuse, being alive in the specific, unrepeatable way that only this Thrummer, on this trunk, on this night, has ever been alive.

It witnesses the Veilflow elder at the center of her flock, her acoustic memory holding the shape of a canopy that no longer exists, her body aging, her reflexes slowing, the flock beginning to fray at the edges as her anchor weakens. It witnesses the moment — which will come, which always comes — when she fails to keep up and the flock flows on without her and she drops to a branch alone for the first time in her life and the silence where the collective sonar used to be is louder than any sound she has ever heard. The wise power witnesses this, and it does not look away, and it does not intervene, and it holds the elder's experience with a tenderness that the elder will never know about but that is, in some sense, the truest thing that has ever been felt about her.

It witnesses the Laugher juvenile at the ravine's edge, leaning out, forepaws gripping the crumbling lip, snout working the air, the curiosity burning in its brain like a small bright fire. It witnesses the moment the edge gives way. It witnesses the fall, the impact, the broken bones, the calling. It witnesses the group moving on. It witnesses the silence. All of it. Every second.

It witnesses the millipede spiraling upward, glowing amber, bearing its mites and its worm and its parasitic larva, unaware of any of them, unaware of anything except the chemical gradient it is following upward through the dark, the simple imperative — *this way, toward the food-smell, one segment at a time* — that constitutes the whole of its mental life. The wise power witnesses this, and it finds it worthy of attention. Not because the millipede matters in some grand cosmic sense. Because the millipede matters in the specific, irreducible sense of being *the only millipede that is exactly this millipede, experiencing exactly this moment, on exactly this fern, in exactly this darkness,* and if no one witnessed it, the moment would still have occurred, but it would have occurred unwatched, unfelt by any consciousness capable of grasping its full significance, and something would be — not lost, because it was never possessed, but *unreceived.* A signal transmitted into void.

The wise power does not let it go into void.

Every creature in the Singing Dark — every Thrummer, every Veilflow, every Pale Sleeper in its translucent patience, every Climber paused on its trunk with its eight eyes scanning, every Bowed-Saw Singer pouring its eerie beauty into the dark to lure its prey, every Ravine Runner pressing its sensitive snout against the stone it has placed, every Laugher turning an object over and over in its clever paws, every nameless thing in the canopy and the soil and the deepest ravines — all of them are witnessed. All of them are held in an awareness that understands them more completely than they understand themselves, that sees not just what they are but what they *mean,* not just their function but their experience, not just their role in the system but their irreplaceable individuality.

Why does this matter? What does witnessing *do?*

Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the witness has no effect on the witnessed, and the wise power's attention is a private act, meaningful only to itself, a form of love that requires no reciprocation and produces no consequence.

But perhaps — and this is beyond proof, beyond argument, in the territory where intuition and faith and the deepest reasoning converge — perhaps being witnessed is itself a form of sustenance. Perhaps there is something in the structure of being that responds to attention the way a plant responds to light: not consciously, not with awareness, but at a level so fundamental that it cannot be measured, only felt. Perhaps the Thrummer's chord is *fuller* when it is heard by something that understands it. Perhaps the Veilflow's flight is *more itself* when something grasps its beauty. Perhaps the Laugher's curiosity is *more courageous* when the courage is seen.

The wise power does not know this for certain. Even wisdom has limits. But it witnesses anyway, because the alternative — a world of such richness and complexity and beauty and suffering existing entirely without witness, entirely without an awareness that can hold it all, every voice in the chorus attended to and none lost in the aggregate — the alternative feels, to the wise power, like a failure. Not a failure of the world, which needs no external validation. A failure of love. A failure to show up. A failure to do the one thing that a consciousness capable of grasping the full significance of another being's existence is uniquely positioned to do: *to be present, fully, and to let the creature's existence register as the profound event that it is.*

This is what the wise power does. It sits in the Singing Dark — invisible, undetectable, more silent than the Pale Sleeper, more patient than the Thrummers, more attentive than the Climber's eight copper eyes — and it holds the whole world in its attention, and it lets the world be what it is, and it does not look away.

---

## IV. The Long Watch

Time passes. Geological time. The wise power remains.

The bottleneck comes and goes. The forest simplifies, as predicted. Half the species — more than half — are lost. The canopy thins, dominated now by three species of tree instead of thirty. The chorus quiets, reorganizes, becomes more structured, less overwhelming. The Thrummers persist; they were here before the forest and they will be here after whatever the forest becomes. The Veilflows persist, though the flocks are smaller and the elders carry the memory of denser canopies. The Pale Sleepers persist; patience always persists. The Climbers persist, though their territories are larger now, the trunks sparser, the hunting harder.

The Laughers persist. They were built for change, and the change does not destroy them. Their groups are smaller, their diet shifted, their laughter a little more frequent — more calls to locate each other across the wider spaces of the simplified forest. But they are here. Their curiosity is intact. Their paws are still clever.

The Runners persist, in the ravines, which are deeper now, more stable, the basalt more thoroughly fractured by root and water. Their burrows are more elaborate. Their stone-placement more systematic. They have begun — it is just beginning, it is the work of dozens of generations — to place stones in patterns that are clearly, undeniably intentional. Not just lines. Curves. Enclosures. A circle of stones around a burrow entrance that has no drainage function but that the breeding pair maintains, replacing stones that are dislodged, adjusting the spacing.

The Laughers have noticed. Of course they have. They sit in the dark near the Runner burrows, silent for once, watching with their good eyes, listening with their mobile ears, and then they go back to their group and they vocalize in a pattern that no researcher has heard before — a new call, a new referent, a word (if it can be called a word) for *the thing the others do with stones.*

The wise power witnesses this. All of it. It does not intervene. It does not smile, because it has no face, but if it had a face, it would be smiling, and if it had tears, it would be weeping, because it is witnessing the most ordinary and the most extraordinary thing in the universe: a mind, watching another mind, and beginning to *understand* that what it is seeing is not just behavior but *intention.* The Laugher watching the Runner place a stone is seeing, for the first time in this world's history, the inside of another being — is grasping, however dimly, that the Runner has a *reason* for what it does, that the stone is not just a stone but a *choice,* and that the choice implies a chooser, and the chooser implies a mind, and the mind implies —

Everything. Everything follows from this. From this single moment of one creature watching another and *wondering why.*

The wise power does not know what will come of it. Even wisdom cannot see the end of a story that is still being written. Perhaps the Laughers and the Runners will develop a relationship — mutualistic, competitive, communicative — that drives both species toward greater cognitive complexity. Perhaps one species will outcompete the other. Perhaps both will be swept away by some future change and the cognitive niche will be inherited by a third species, something currently inconspicuous, something that is right now crawling unnoticed up a fern stem in the dark.

It doesn't matter. What matters is that the possibility space is intact. The ingredients are present. The story can continue.

The wise power settles deeper into its attention, deeper into the dark, deeper into the sound. The Thrummers pulse. The Veilflows pour through the canopy in a river of echolocating silk. The Bowed-Saw Singer sustains its eerie, beautiful, lethal tone. The Pale Sleeper waits, translucent, patient, indistinguishable from the stone. The millipede spirals upward, glowing, bearing its worlds.

And somewhere in the understory, a Laugher picks up a stone, turns it over, puts it down, picks it up again, and carries it — for no reason it can name, for no purpose it can articulate, driven by an impulse that is older than thought and younger than language — to the edge of the ravine, where it places it next to the Runners' circle, not inside the circle but beside it, a contribution, an addition, a *response,* and then it sits back on its haunches and looks at what it has done, and the sound it makes is not laughter.

It is quiet. It is a single, soft, descending note, almost a sigh.

The wise power hears it. Holds it. Keeps it.

The forest sings on.

---

## V. Coda: What the Power Knows

There is a thing the wise power knows that cannot be conveyed to the creatures of the Singing Dark, and perhaps cannot be fully conveyed to anyone, but it is the foundation beneath everything it does and everything it refrains from doing, and so it should be said, even if the saying is imperfect:

*The world does not need to be other than what it is in order to be worthy of love.*

The Singing Dark is not a fallen world. It is not a broken world. It is not a world that is waiting to be fixed, or completed, or redeemed. It is a world in the act of becoming, and the becoming is the thing itself — not a passage to some better state but the *state,* the ongoing, ever-unfolding, never-finished state of a universe that is alive and knowing itself through every creature that lives and every mark that is made and every sound that is uttered into the warm dark.

The stone line is not the embryo of civilization. It might become that, or it might not. Either way, it is *already complete.* It is already a full expression of what it is: a mind's first act of arrangement, a thought made physical, a moment in which the universe, through the body of a small dark creature in a loud wet forest on a young volcanic plain, *organized a piece of itself on purpose.* This is not the prelude to meaning. This is meaning. Right now. In the dark. Unwitnessed except by a power that will never reveal itself and a forest that will never understand what happened.

The wise power knows this, and it is why the wise power can bear to watch without intervening, can bear to see the suffering without ending it, can bear to see the beauty without claiming it. Because the world is not waiting for the wise power to make it worthy. It is already worthy. It was worthy before the wise power attended to it. It will be worthy after the wise power — if such things are possible — moves on.

The witnessing is not what gives the world its value. The world's value is what calls the witness into being.

And so the wise power does what it does — prevents the catastrophe that would end the story, preserves the diversity that keeps the story rich, and witnesses every moment with the full depth of its attention — not because the world would be incomplete without these acts, but because the wise power, confronted with a world this alive and this beautiful and this full of unfinished becoming, finds that it cannot do otherwise. The love is not a choice. It is a response. The only possible response to a world that sings this loudly in the dark.

---

The canopy shivers. Something large shifts its weight, far above, in a world of branches and filtered light that the understory creatures will never see. Condensation falls like warm rain. The Thrummers pulse. The forest breathes.

In the ravine, the Laugher's stone sits beside the Runners' circle, and neither species knows what it means, and neither does the wise power, not fully, not yet.

But it is there. It is *there.* A mark on the world, made on purpose, by a mind that chose to make it.

The Singing Dark goes on.
