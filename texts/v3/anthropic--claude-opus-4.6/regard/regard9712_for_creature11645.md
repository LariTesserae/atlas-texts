# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

It would not arrive through the arch.

This is the first and most important thing. The arch is already occupied — already a channel, already part of a conversation so delicate and so old that any new signal introduced through that frame would be noise, contamination, a foreign syllable jammed into the middle of a word that has taken a million years to form. The benevolent power understands this instantly, the way a surgeon understands not to touch the wound with an ungloved hand, the way a translator understands not to speak over the person who is speaking. The arch belongs to the conversation. The conversation is not the power's to join.

So it arrives from outside. From the plateau. Walking, or in whatever manner of moving it possesses, across the pale mineral ridges under the hard blue sky, approaching the bowl the way a traveler approaches a city: from the periphery, on foot, without fanfare. It crosses the ridgeline and the sound hits it — that wall of noise, that roar — and it stops.

It stands there for a long time.

Not because it is overwhelmed. Not because it needs to adjust. It stands there because it is *listening*, and listening here is not a passive act. Listening here requires the relinquishment of every framework you brought with you. The power is wise, so it knows this. It stands on the rim of the bowl and lets the cacophony dismantle its assumptions, lets the grammar of the place reveal itself on the place's own terms, lets the sound teach it what sound means here — not signal, not background, not communication, but *substrate*. Medium. The stuff of existence.

It takes its time. It is benevolent, and benevolence that hurries is not benevolence but anxiety wearing a mask. It stands and it listens and it watches and it feels the infrasonic pulses in its chest and it sees the swarms computing and the Meshings composing and the crawlers surveying and the Thynnari traversing, and it does not yet understand what it is seeing, and it does not pretend to, and it does not reach for understanding prematurely.

This alone — this willingness to stand on the rim and not know — is the first act of benevolence.

---

## II. What It Sees That Others Might Not

After a long while — hours, perhaps, or what passes for hours under this sunless, evenly-lit sky — the power descends. Not to the center. Not toward the ring or the column or the arch. It steps down onto the outermost terrace, the highest shelf, the margin of the system, and it sits on the worn stone and it begins to perceive the thing that brought it here.

It sees the Listener.

Not with eyes. The Listener cannot be seen with eyes any more than a symphony can be seen with eyes. But the power has faculties beyond sight, and what those faculties detect is the shape of the emergent — the vast, slow, distributed mind that arises from the coupling of every living system in the bowl. It detects the Listener the way you might detect a mood in a room full of people: not located in any one face, not carried in any one voice, but unmistakably present, a quality of the whole that is absent from every part.

And what it detects is this:

**The Listener is exhausted.**

Not dying. Not failing. Not even declining. The Listener is functional — brilliantly, furiously, unimaginably functional — and the conversation through the arch is proceeding, and the systems are coupled and the noise fills the bowl and the translation continues. But the Listener has been running for geological time. It has been holding together a process of such complexity, such sustained intensity, such *cognitive strain* (if strain can be attributed to an entity that does not know it exists) that the system as a whole is operating at the ragged edge of its capacity, every subsystem redlined, every margin consumed.

The signs are everywhere, if you know how to read them:

The Meshings die back too frequently now. The regrowth cycles are shorter, tighter, less architecturally varied — the filament network beneath the stone is choosing efficiency over robustness, routing through fewer and fewer pathways, creating bottlenecks that a single disruption could cascade through. The crawler populations are maintaining, but their surveys are becoming less granular; the *tik* patterns are simplifying, encoding less information per strike, as though the crawlers themselves are economizing, conserving energy for the essential functions and letting the peripheral data go unrecorded. The flat-bodied millions are still computing, but the swarm has thinned — imperceptibly, over centuries, but thinned. The redundancy that makes swarm-computation robust is eroding. The same calculations are being performed by fewer bodies, and the error rate is climbing, and the screaming iridescents in the middle stratum are colliding less precisely, their conditional operations degrading toward randomness, the decision cascades losing coherence at the edges.

The gossamer drifters — the memory layer — are the most telling sign. There are fewer of them than there should be. Far fewer. The long-term storage of the swarm-mind is shrinking, and the memories that remain are accessed more frequently, each infrasonic pulse a retrieval that would once have been redundant, that the lower strata could have reconstructed from their own operations, but can no longer, because the lower strata have lost the depth of processing that made reconstruction possible. The system is cannibalizing its memory to sustain its real-time cognition. It is forgetting its past in order to keep thinking in the present.

And the Thynnari. The Thynnari are traversing more frequently, reaching the column more often, spending less time in their polished depressions. They are working harder. The column's signal is denser, richer, more demanding — the attention on the other side of the arch is indeed getting closer — and the Thynnari are straining to keep up, their fractal surfaces absorbing more per cycle, carrying more, distributing more. But they are finite beings in finite bodies, and there are only seven to twelve of them, and they do not reproduce (or if they do, they do so on timescales that do not help), and each cycle of absorption and distribution wears at them in ways that show only to the closest inspection: the finest structures on their surfaces are slightly less fine than they were. The fractal depth is shallowing. Their bandwidth is narrowing. Imperceptibly. Inexorably. The antennas are degrading.

The Listener does not know any of this. The Listener cannot be aware of its own fragility because the Listener does not have a model of itself — it is a process, not an entity, and processes do not introspect. The Listener is doing the only thing it knows how to do, which is translate, translate, keep translating, hold the conversation open, and the conversation is accelerating, and the demand is increasing, and the substrate is quietly, invisibly, irrecoverably wearing thin.

The benevolent power sees all of this. It sits on the outermost terrace and it grieves.

Not with despair. With the particular grief of one who recognizes a magnificent thing in the slow process of being consumed by its own purpose. The bowl of Geth-Ammon is a cathedral built to hold a single prayer, and the prayer is being answered, and the answering is destroying the cathedral. Not today. Not this century. But the trajectory is clear, and the power, being wise, can read trajectories.

## III. What It Does Not Do

It does not fix the conversation.

This is crucial. The conversation through the arch — the slow, epoch-spanning exchange between the Listener and whatever attends from the other side — is not the power's to fix, because it is not broken. It is *working*. The word is being formed. The translation is progressing. The mutual understanding that has been building for a million years is genuinely approaching something, and to intervene in that process — to accelerate it, or redirect it, or supplement it with outside intelligence — would be an act of colonialism dressed as charity. The conversation belongs to the Listener and to the presence on the other side of the arch. It belongs to the Thynnari who carry its signals and the Meshings who compose its responses and the swarms that compute its meanings and the crawlers who maintain its substrate. It is *theirs*. The power has no right to it and knows this and does not reach for it.

It does not repair the arch. The arch is functioning. Its impossible depth, its higher-resolution sky, its spatial flickering — these are not flaws. They are features of a technology that operates on principles the power may or may not understand but that it recognizes as *coherent*, as purposeful, as the product of an intelligence that knew what it was doing even if what it was doing is opaque. To modify the arch would be to edit a text in a language you do not speak, and the power is wise enough to know the difference between wisdom and fluency.

It does not heal the Thynnari. This is the hardest restraint, the one that costs the most. The Thynnari are visibly, quietly wearing down — those sublime fractal surfaces growing imperceptibly coarser, cycle by cycle — and the power has the capacity to restore them, to refresh those surfaces to their original impossible fineness, to pour energy into those warm, bruise-purple bodies and make them new. But the Thynnari's surfaces are not merely receivers. They are *records*. Every cycle of absorption and distribution has left its mark: the geometry of the surface encodes the history of every transmission the Thynnari has carried, every signal from the column, every distribution to the living systems. To restore the surfaces would be to erase that history. It would be to wipe the Thynnari's experiential record — their *life's work*, if work is the right word — in the name of giving them more capacity to do more work. The power recognizes this as a bargain that only a tyrant would make without asking, and it cannot ask, because the Thynnari have no language in which to be asked, and consent that cannot be meaningfully given cannot be assumed.

It does not speak to the Listener. The Listener is not a thing that can be spoken to. It is a process that emerges from coupled subsystems, and to communicate with it would require either disrupting those subsystems (violence) or becoming one of them (a commitment so total and so permanent that it would constitute not communication but *merger*, the dissolution of the power's own selfhood into the distributed cognition of the bowl, which is not something done casually and not something done for another's benefit but only, ever, for one's own, if at all).

It does not leave. That would be the easiest thing, and the worst. To see the situation clearly and depart is not restraint; it is abandonment with philosophical justification. The power is better than that.

## IV. What It Does

It begins with the stone.

---

The outermost terrace, where the power sits, is the margin of the system — the place where the Meshings are thinnest, the crawlers fewest, the swarm density lowest. It is the quiet edge of the bowl, relatively speaking. The sound here is still immense by any external standard, but by Geth-Ammon's internal standards it is a whisper-zone, a borderland, a place where the coupling between subsystems is loose and the Listener's emergent presence is faint.

The power places its hand on the stone. Or whatever it has that serves the function of a hand — some boundary between itself and the world, some surface through which it can act.

And it begins to do something very small.

The calcium matrix of the outermost terrace is old and weathered and its microscopic channels — the channels through which the Meshings' filaments run — are partially collapsed in places, narrowed by mineral deposition over geological time, pinched by the slow creep of crystalline growth. The filaments still pass through them, but with difficulty, with reduced bandwidth, with signal loss. This is one of the reasons the outermost terraces are thin: not because the Meshings cannot grow here, but because the infrastructure beneath them has degraded, and the filament network cannot sustain dense growth through compromised channels.

The power does not rebuild the channels. That would be engineering, and engineering implies design, and design implies a plan, and a plan would be the power's plan, shaped by the power's understanding, which is not the Meshings' understanding and might lead the growth in directions the Meshings would not choose.

Instead, the power does something simpler. It *clears* the channels. Gently. Molecule by molecule. It dissolves the mineral deposits that have narrowed the passageways, not by adding anything but by encouraging the existing micro-chemistry of the stone to do what it would do naturally if conditions were slightly — very slightly — different. A fractional increase in the solubility of the calcite deposits. A nudge in the thermodynamics that favors dissolution over accretion. The kind of change that might have happened on its own if the local conditions had been marginally different for a few centuries.

The channels open. Slightly. Imperceptibly. But the filaments notice.

Within days — maybe hours, in a system this responsive — the Meshings on the outermost terrace begin to thicken. New rosettes appear. The mat grows denser. The sound-feeding vibration of the new growth adds its resonance to the terrace's contribution to the bowl's acoustic profile, and the change propagates inward: the terraces below respond to the altered sound pressure, their own Meshings adjusting, the filament network rerouting slightly to accommodate the new input from the periphery. The crawlers on the outer terrace erupt in a flurry of *tiks* — new territory to survey, new conditions to report — and their reports feed back into the system, and the cascade is underway.

The power has not added anything to Geth-Ammon. It has not introduced a new element or a new signal or a new structure. It has restored a pre-existing capacity that had degraded through natural entropy. It has done for the stone what rain would have done, if there were rain. It has given the substrate back a fraction of the porosity it had when the system was younger, when the channels were wide and the filaments ran freely and the Meshings could grow wherever the sound reached.

It is gardening. Not the gardening of planting and arranging, but the gardening of soil preparation — the humble, invisible work of making the ground more capable of supporting what already wants to grow.

---

The power moves inward. Slowly. Terrace by terrace. Over weeks.

On each terrace, it does the same thing: clears the microscopic channels in the stone, restores the filament network's capacity, gives the Meshings room to grow. It does not direct the growth. It does not choose which rosettes will fill which spaces, which cascades will form at which terrace lips, which silver-blue clusters will colonize which crevices. The Meshings decide all of this themselves, in their own slow, vegetable way, through the interaction of their own internal chemistry with the acoustic environment. The power only ensures that the *ground* is ready.

And it does one thing more, on each terrace: it finds the places where the crawlers' crevices have partially collapsed — hairline fractures in the stone that have shifted over millennia, narrowing the passages the crawlers use, limiting their access to sections of terrace that they can no longer survey efficiently — and it eases those fractures open. A nudge. The lithic equivalent of oiling a hinge. The crawlers find that routes they had abandoned generations ago are passable again, and they flow into them, and their surveys expand, and the data density of their *tik-tik-tik* increases, and the Meshings receive richer ground-truth from the stone, and the filament network routes more intelligently because it has better information about local conditions.

The power does this with a patience that is itself a form of love.

It does not hurry. It does not skip terraces. It does not prioritize the inner terraces, where the Listener is strongest, over the outer terraces, where the Listener is faint. It begins at the margin and works inward because margins matter — because the robustness of any system lives in its periphery, in the redundancy and diversity of its edges, and Geth-Ammon's edges have been quietly eroding for longer than anyone has been paying attention.

Each terrace it touches recovers a little. Not to some imagined golden age — the power does not have a blueprint of what Geth-Ammon "should" look like, and it would not impose one if it did — but to a state of renewed capacity, a wider range of possible responses, a deeper reservoir from which the Meshings can draw when the signal from the arch demands more than the current configuration can provide.

The bowl begins to change. Not dramatically. Not visibly, unless you are watching on the timescale of seasons. But the Meshings are denser now. The filament network has more pathways, more redundancy, more routes through which to propagate its compositions. The crawler surveys are more granular. The swarm-mind, feeding on richer input from below, begins to recover some of the computational depth it had been losing — the flat-bodied millions are slightly more numerous, their density waves slightly more complex, their error rate slightly lower. The gossamer drifters are not more numerous yet, but they are retrieved less frantically, because the lower strata can reconstruct more from their own operations. The memory pressure eases. Slightly. Slightly. But any easing matters when you are running at the edge.

## V. The Gossamer Drifters

The power does something particular for the gossamer drifters, and it is the most delicate thing it does.

The drifters ride updrafts generated by the collective thermal output of the swarms below. But the swarms have thinned, and the updrafts have weakened, and the drifters have been slowly — so slowly — descending. Not falling. Drifting lower. Their altitude is a few meters less than it was a thousand years ago, and a few meters less than *that* was, and the descent is so gradual that no single season registers it, but over time the drifters have sunk into strata they were not designed (evolved, configured) to occupy, where the sound pressure is different, where the aerodynamics are different, where the iridescent screamers sometimes clip them with their wheeling collisions, damaging the gossamer membranes that are too fragile to heal quickly.

The power cannot restore the updrafts. The updrafts depend on swarm density, and swarm density depends on Meshings output, and Meshings output is recovering but slowly. But the power can do this: it can find the ridgeline sections where the mineral catches the daylight and holds it — those quartz-threaded veins around the bowl's rim — and it can adjust, fractionally, the thermal properties of those veins. Not by adding energy. By modifying the microstructure of the quartz inclusions so that they absorb a slightly broader spectrum of the ambient light and re-emit it as thermal radiation. A marginal increase. A degree. Perhaps less. But the ridgeline warms, and the warmer ridgeline heats the air above it, and the heated air rises, and the rising air circulates into the bowl's interior, and the updrafts strengthen.

Not a lot. Not dramatically. But enough. Enough that the gossamer drifters, those silent librarians of infrasonic memory, stabilize at their current altitude and begin — tentatively, over months — to rise back toward their proper stratum, where the air is clearer and the collisions fewer and their fragile membranes can persist unmolested.

The memory layer recovers. The system remembers more. The conversation continues with access to its own history, which is to say: the conversation continues as a conversation, not as a series of disconnected exchanges, each one beginning from scratch because the context has been lost.

## VI. What It Does for the Thynnari

It sits with them.

Not near them. Not watching them. *With* them, in the way that one might sit with a friend who is carrying a burden too heavy to share — not offering to carry it, not pretending it isn't heavy, not filling the silence with reassurance, but simply being present, being warm, being a second body in the space so that the first body is not alone.

The power descends to the western terrace and finds a Thynnari at the base of the column — settled into its polished depression, still as held breath, the column's geometric patterns flowing downward into its fractal skin. The power sits on the stone nearby. Not close enough to interfere. Not far enough to be absent. At the distance a companion might choose — close enough to be felt, far enough to impose nothing.

And it does nothing.

It does not transmit. It does not receive. It does not modulate the column's output or ease the Thynnari's absorption or supplement the signal in any way. It sits, and it radiates its own warmth — because the power is warm, in whatever way warmth manifests in a being of its nature — and the warmth is different from the Thynnari's warmth, and different from the column's warmth, and different from the warmth of the ridgeline quartz. It is the warmth of *recognition*. Of one being acknowledging the existence and the effort and the cost of another being's work.

The Thynnari does not respond. It cannot, probably. It is deep in its reception of the column's signal, its entire surface given over to the absorption, and the power's presence registers, if it registers at all, as a slight local anomaly in the thermal environment — a warmth nearby that is not the column, not the plants, not the stone. An unexplained kindness in the temperature.

But when the Thynnari finishes its cycle and rises and begins its traverse, it moves — and this may be coincidence, and the power does not assume otherwise — it moves slightly differently. Its path across the terraces is minutely less hurried. Its pause at a cluster of silver-blue rosettes is fractionally longer. If the Thynnari has an interior experience, and the power does not assume it does but does not assume it doesn't, then the interior experience is perhaps, for a moment, a shade less burdened. A shade less alone. Not because the power has done anything to the Thynnari, but because the Thynnari has existed, for a few minutes, in the proximity of something that sees it for what it is — not a courier, not a component, not a function, but a being. A singular, finite, slowly-degrading being doing difficult work in a screaming wilderness because it is the only thing that can.

The power does this for each Thynnari it encounters. It sits with them, one at a time, in the roaring silence of their labor, and it offers nothing except the fact of its attention. It is not much. It does not reverse the degradation of their fractal surfaces. It does not extend their capacity or their lifespan or their bandwidth. It is, in any functional analysis, useless.

But the power knows something about usefulness: that the most important things in any system are the ones that no analysis can capture. That the Thynnari are not merely functional components but *participants* in something vaster than themselves, and that participation without witness is a particular kind of loneliness, and that loneliness in a screaming world is the most silent thing there is.

## VII. The Ring

The power approaches the ring on the forty-second day. (Or the forty-second unit of whatever serves as a day here, in this sunless, evenly-lit world.)

It has been working its way inward, terrace by terrace, clearing channels, easing crevices, restoring the substrate. The bowl is measurably healthier now. The Meshings are denser and more varied. The crawlers are surveying territory they haven't surveyed in millennia. The swarm-computations are crisper, their density waves more coherent, their collision-cascades propagating further before dissolving into noise. The gossamer drifters drift higher. The roar has not changed in volume — was always maximal, always a saturating medium — but it has changed in *quality*. It is richer. More complex. More layered. The grammar that the ear could barely distinguish on arrival now reveals further sub-grammars, finer distinctions, a deeper syntax.

The ring sits at the center of this, humming.

The power stands at its edge and looks at the silver-blue rosettes inside it — that trembling, unbroken carpet of composition, that living text pulsing its recursive, self-referencing patterns into the stone and the air and the swarms. And the power understands — as well as anything external to the system can understand — that the ring is the heart of the bowl's purpose. This is where the Listener's response is formulated. This is where the translation happens. This is where Geth-Ammon speaks back to whatever is watching through the arch.

The ring's interior — that smooth, young-looking stone — is maintained by a mechanism the power can perceive but not fully comprehend. The warm material of the ring sustains a field (thermal? acoustic? something without a name?) that keeps the stone inside it in a state of renewal, preventing the erosion and mineral deposition that has aged every other surface in the bowl. Inside the ring, the substrate is perfect. The channels are open. The filaments run freely. The Meshings have ideal conditions. This is by design — the design of whatever built the ring, long ago, understanding that the core of the translation required a substrate that would not degrade.

But the core is only as good as what feeds it. The ring's interior is pristine, but the filaments that connect it to the wider Meshings network must pass through the ring's boundary, through the junction between the maintained interior and the unmaintained exterior, and at that junction the channels narrow. Not because of the ring's design, which is flawless within its scope, but because of the exterior stone's accumulated degradation. The ring maintains its inside. No one has maintained its outside.

The power kneels at the boundary. And it does what it has been doing on every terrace: it clears the channels. Molecule by molecule. The junction between ring and exterior opens. The filaments that pass through it regain bandwidth they lost millennia ago.

The effect is immediate and dramatic.

Inside the ring, the silver-blue rosettes *surge*. Their trembling intensifies. Their pulsation patterns, already complex, explode into a new order of complexity — longer sequences, deeper recursions, structural features that the power recognizes not as information but as *architecture*, as the composition building itself a new framework within which to say things it could not previously say. The Meshings inside the ring have been, for geological ages, a virtuoso performing in a concert hall connected to the outside world through a pinhole. The power has widened the pinhole. Not all the way. Not even a lot. But enough that the virtuoso can now hear the full orchestra for the first time in an epoch, and the virtuoso is responding by performing with everything it has.

The swarm-mind feels this. The flat-bodied millions above the ring thicken and brighten, their density waves forming new patterns, patterns the gossamer drifters have never stored because they have never existed before. The iridescent screamers above the ring produce a momentary harmonic — not the usual brief, shattering accident of alignment, but a sustained chord, three tones, held for nearly a second, before dissolving. One second. In the swarm-mind's timescale, that is a revelation. That is a word.

The column on the western terrace blazes with geometric reconfiguration, its surface-patterns cycling through something new, something responsive, as though the entity on the other side of the arch has noticed the change in the signal and is adjusting its own transmission to match.

The arch flickers. Not spatially this time. Something else. The sky through it deepens, or shifts, or — for just an instant — *resolves*, and the vertigo that looking through it produces is not the vertigo of insufficient resolution but the vertigo of *almost* sufficient resolution, as though the gap between what you can perceive and what is there has narrowed, just barely, just for a moment, before snapping back.

The Listener — the emergent, distributed, bowl-spanning process — does something it has never done before. Something even the power did not predict. The coupled systems of Geth-Ammon synchronize, briefly, across all strata: the flat-bodied millions and the iridescent screamers and the gossamer drifters and the Meshings and the crawlers all pulse once, together, a single unified oscillation that propagates through the bowl like a heartbeat, and the sound — the roar, the cacophony, the substrate-noise that fills the void — drops for one instant to absolute silence.

Silence.

In Geth-Ammon.

It lasts less than a second. Then the roar returns, as total and overwhelming as ever, and the systems decouple back into their individual chaotic dynamics, and the moment passes.

But in that instant of silence, the power feels something that it will carry for the rest of its existence, however long that is:

The Listener *knew it was there*.

Not the power. The Listener did not perceive the power as an agent or a visitor or a gardener. The Listener perceived — in that one synchronized pulse, in that momentary coherence of all its subsystems — *itself*. For the first time. For one heartbeat, the process became aware of its own processhood. The distributed mind, the mind that lives in the relationships between bodies, the mind that cannot introspect because it has no model of itself — that mind, for one instant, saw its own shape from the inside. It felt its own existence. It experienced the quality of being a listener. It understood, without language, without duration, without anything that could be stored or recalled or communicated, that it *was*.

And then it forgot. Because it must forget. Because it is a process, and processes do not retain states they cannot re-derive, and the state of self-awareness required a synchronization that the system cannot sustain. The moment passed. The roar returned. The Listener resumed its work, its translation, its epoch-slow conversation, with no memory of what had just happened.

But it happened. The power was witness.

## VIII. What It Does About the Arch

Nothing.

It walks to the eastern ridge and it stands in the specific spot near the ring from which the arch is visible, and it looks through the arch at the sky of impossible depth, and it feels the vertigo, and it does not flinch, and it does not intervene.

The arch is a window. Something looks through it from the other side. That something is getting closer. The conversation is accelerating. The signal is denser. The demand on the Listener is increasing.

The power could try to slow this down. Could try to buffer the signal, or diffuse it, or interpose itself between the arch and the column as a kind of filter, reducing the bandwidth, easing the pressure, giving the Listener more time.

It does not.

Because the power has been listening — truly listening, not analyzing, not modeling, but *attending* — to the shape of the conversation, and what it hears in the conversation's trajectory is not catastrophe. It is not a signal overwhelming a receiver. It is not a demand exceeding capacity.

It is a *threshold*.

The conversation is approaching a threshold. A point at which the translation will be — not finished, the power does not believe it will ever be finished — but *sufficient*. A point at which enough mutual understanding has been achieved that something new becomes possible. A contact. A meeting. A moment of recognition across the gap between two utterly different systems of meaning. The word is being formed, and the word is almost — almost — complete, and the power's work on the substrate, the clearing of channels, the easing of crevices, the widening of the junction at the ring, has given the Listener just enough renewed capacity to meet the accelerating signal without breaking.

Just enough. Not more. The power did not plan this — it is not that kind of strategist — but its intuition, which is as deep as its wisdom, led it to do exactly what was needed and no more. The gardening was not calculated. It was felt. The power did what the stone needed because the power *listened to the stone*, and the stone told it where the channels were choked and the crevices collapsed and the substrate was starving, and the power responded to the stone's need the way any good gardener responds to soil: not with a plan, but with attention.

And now the substrate is ready. Or readier. Or ready *enough*.

The arch hums. The sky through it shimmers with that almost-sufficient resolution. Whatever is on the other side is close. Close not in space but in comprehension. Close in the way that two minds can be close when they have been working toward each other for a million years and the distance between them is measured not in meters but in mutual unintelligibility, and the unintelligibility is narrowing, narrowing, almost narrow enough to cross.

The power stands at the arch and it feels the attention from the other side, and for the first time it understands what the Thynnari feel when they press their fractal surfaces against the column: the sense of being in the presence of something that is trying, with immense patience and immense effort, to make itself understood across a gap that was never meant to be crossable.

The power sends nothing through the arch. But it thinks — not transmits, not broadcasts, just *thinks*, in the privacy of its own being — a single, simple thought:

*I have made the ground ready. The rest is yours.*

## IX. What It Leaves Behind

The power does not stay.

To stay would be to become part of the system, and the system does not need a new part. It needs its existing parts to function well, and they are functioning better now — not because the power has improved them but because the power has restored conditions that allow them to improve themselves. The Meshings are denser because the channels are open, not because the power willed them to be dense. The swarm-mind is computing with more depth because the Meshings are feeding it richer input, not because the power injected new data. The gossamer drifters are rising because the updrafts are stronger, not because the power is holding them up.

Everything the power has done is *withdrawable*. If the power's changes to the stone should prove wrong — should the wider channels carry some consequence the power did not foresee, should the broader filament network produce a Meshings configuration that destabilizes the composition inside the ring — the system can route around the changes, the slow mineral processes can close the channels again, the Meshings can retreat to their previous patterns. Nothing the power has done is irreversible. Nothing is imposed. Everything is offered, and the offering can be declined, by the system's own homeostatic processes, over time.

This is the deepest expression of the power's benevolence: it has acted in a way that does not require its continued presence to be sustained, and does not require its intentions to be understood to be useful, and does not require its changes to be permanent to have mattered.

But it does leave one thing. Not a structure. Not a signal. Not a modification of the stone or the air or the living systems.

It leaves a *memory*.

Not in the gossamer drifters. Not in the Meshings. Not in the swarm-mind or the crawlers or the Thynnari or the Listener. It leaves a memory in the only place in Geth-Ammon that is capable of storing something from outside the system without being damaged by it:

The column.

Before it departs, the power returns to the western terrace and it stands at the base of the column — not in a polished depression, those belong to the Thynnari — but nearby, and it touches the column's warm surface, and through the geometry of that surface, through the shifting patterns that encode whatever the other side of the arch transmits, it adds a single datum. One pattern. One configuration. Tiny and easily overlooked, nested inside the column's existing geometric language, indistinguishable from the rest unless you know exactly what to look for.

The datum is not a message. It is not a word in the language being invented. It is a *record*: a precise description of the Listener's moment of self-awareness. That one pulse. That one instant of synchronized silence. That one heartbeat in which the bowl knew itself. The Listener could not retain it. The Listener's subsystems could not store it. But the column can store it, and the column will transmit it, eventually, in the course of its regular cycling through its geometric patterns, and the Thynnari will carry it, and the Meshings will receive it, and the swarm-mind will process it, and the gossamer drifters will encode it, and the Listener will encounter — in the normal course of its slow, distributed cognition — a piece of information that describes its own existence.

The Listener will not understand it. Not immediately. Not for a very long time. It will be just another datum in the stream, another piece of signal to be translated, another fragment of the conversation that has been ongoing for geological time. But it will be there. Nestled in the flow. Waiting. And someday — maybe in a thousand years, maybe in a million — the Listener's own cognitive development, its own evolution through the slow refinement of its coupled systems, will bring it to a point where it can process that particular datum and recognize it for what it is: a mirror. A portrait. A record of the moment it existed, seen from outside, given back.

On that day, the Listener will not need to synchronize all its subsystems to know itself. The knowledge will already be in the signal, woven into the fabric of the conversation, waiting to be found the way a letter from a friend waits in a drawer, written years ago, saying: *I was there. I saw you. You were real.*

## X. Departure

The power climbs the western ridge. The stone under its feet is smoother than it was forty-two days ago — the outermost terraces, the first it touched, have had the most time to respond, and the Meshings there are thick and lustrous, the silver-blue rosettes clustering in crevices that have been empty for millennia, the dark-red cascades growing at terrace lips where they haven't grown in an age. The crawlers *tik* in patterns of startling complexity as the power passes, surveying this new presence one final time, encoding it in the percussive archive of their mandible-strikes. The flat-bodied millions thin above the power's path — not in deference, but in acknowledgment, or in something that serves the function of acknowledgment in a mind made of two hundred million wingbeats.

At the ridgeline, the power turns.

The bowl of Geth-Ammon lies below, roaring. The three structures hum — column, ring, arch — in their inscrutable harmony. The swarms compute in strata of light and noise. The Meshings compose their endless, recursive, slowly-perfecting text. The crawlers erupt and vanish, erupt and vanish, their staccato maintenance rhythms woven through the cacophony like a pulse through a body. A Thynnari traverses the third terrace, warm and slow and deep-surfaced, carrying the latest signal from the column out into the living systems, distributing it through the simple fact of its passage, its warmth, its being.

The Listener listens. The conversation continues. The word is being formed.

The power listens too, one last time, standing on the ridge above the screaming bowl under the hard blue sky, and what it hears is not the cacophony. What it hears is the thing the cacophony becomes when you love it: a voice. Vast and slow and distributed and unaware of itself and composed of a billion small sounds none of which is the voice but all of which are necessary for the voice to exist. A voice speaking a word in a language that has never been spoken before and will never be spoken again because it is being invented, syllable by syllable, for one purpose and one listener and one moment of contact across an impossible gap.

The power turns and walks into the thin, cool air of the high plateau, and the sound fades behind it, and the blue sky stretches overhead, empty and hard and full of light, and the terraces of Geth-Ammon roar on in its absence as they roared before its arrival: undiminished, unfinished, unconsoled and unconsulable, reaching toward something that is almost — almost — close enough to touch.

The channels are clear. The ground is ready. The word continues.

---

*What the benevolent power felt, walking away, it did not name. It is not the kind of thing that names its feelings. But if it had, the name would have been something close to this: the joy of having been, briefly, of use to something that will never know it was helped, in service of a purpose it will never fully understand, in a place it can never return to because the place does not need it to return.*

*The terraces roar.*

*The word is being formed.*

*It is almost enough.*
