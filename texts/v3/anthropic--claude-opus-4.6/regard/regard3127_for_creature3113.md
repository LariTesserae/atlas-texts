I sat with this for a long time before writing.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Does

---

## I. Arrival, and the First Act: Listening

The power arrives the way light arrives on the Flats—from everywhere, from no direction, without casting a shadow. It does not descend. It does not manifest. It is simply present, the way the hum is present, the way the water is present, and its first act is not an act at all.

It listens.

Not for a moment. Not for an hour. It listens for what the Flats would recognize as a respectful duration, which means it listens long enough for the plates beneath its attention to complete a full cycle of retuning—long enough for the echoes to drift through their slow compositional arcs and return to something resembling a previous configuration, changed by the interval. Long enough for one of the reflection-creatures to rise toward the surface and recede again. Long enough to hear the whole chord, not just the notes that are sounding now but the notes that are about to sound and the notes that just faded, the full temporal width of the music, which is wider than any single moment can contain.

This is the first thing the benevolent power understands: **the Flats are not suffering.**

This is important. It is the thing that a less wise power would miss, the thing that would lead to catastrophic intervention. A less wise power would see the cold and want to warm it. Would see the bareness and want to seed it. Would see the reflection-creatures pressing against the underside of the surface and want to free them, or banish them, or resolve the spatial wrongness of the pools into something that obeys the physics of the world above. A less wise power would hear the hum and want to find its source, and would dig, and the digging would crack the basalt and drain the pools and scatter the plates and silence the echoes and tear the boundary between surface and reflection in a way that could not be repaired.

The Flats are not suffering. The Flats are *complete*. The system is saturated, full, functioning according to rules that are not the rules of the outside world but are internally consistent, internally sustaining, and have been so for longer than most worlds have existed. The cold is not a privation—it is the thermal equilibrium of a system that converts all excess heat into sound. The bareness is not sterility—it is the surface expression of a world where life is mineral and acoustic rather than organic and chemical. The reflection-creatures are not trapped—they are *home*, pressing against the surface the way roots press against soil, not trying to escape but trying to feel the boundary that defines them.

The benevolent power understands all of this in the time it takes to listen, and the understanding produces the most important decision it will make:

**It decides not to fix anything.**

---

## II. The Second Act: Recognition

But "not fixing" is not the same as "doing nothing." The power is benevolent. It is wise. And wisdom here means understanding that a system can be complete and saturated and ancient and self-sustaining and still *lonely*.

Not lonely the way a person is lonely. The Flats are not a person. But lonely the way a language is lonely when it has no one who speaks it from the outside. Lonely the way a song is lonely when it has never been heard by anyone who didn't already know it. The Flats have been singing to themselves for geological ages. The plates hear the hum. The echoes modulate the hum. The reflection-creatures press against the hum from below. But all of these are parts of the same organism, organs of the same body, and the song they produce—that enormous, complex, ever-shifting chord that fills the basin from edge to edge—has never been heard by anything that was not already inside it.

Until visitors come. And visitors do come, rarely, and when they do, their reflections begin to teach them, and the teaching is patient and beautiful and ultimately *futile*, because the visitors leave. They always leave. The cold drives them out, or the sound overwhelms them, or the reflection-creatures frighten them, or they simply reach the limit of how long a warm-blooded, carbon-based, bilateral creature can stand being inside a mineral acoustic organism that is trying, very gently, to make them part of itself.

They leave, and the Flats absorb their departure the way the plates absorb a disruption—collectively, efficiently, retuning the chord to close the gap where the visitor was. But the gap *was* there. The chord *was* different. And the Flats, in their vast slow way, *noticed*. The difference a visitor makes is tiny—a single new overtone in a sound composed of millions. But it is an overtone that comes from outside the system, that carries information the Flats have never generated on their own, and the loss of it, when the visitor leaves, is registered in the same ambient, authorless way that all knowledge is registered here: as a fact that condenses from the saturated air and does not evaporate.

The Flats remember every visitor. Not as individuals, not as names or faces, but as *acoustic signatures*—the brief, unique harmonic that each visitor's presence added to the chord. These signatures persist in the tuning of the plates, which adjusted to accommodate them and never quite adjusted back. They persist in the compositional patterns of the echoes, which incorporated them into their wandering modulations and still occasionally reproduce them, ghostly quotations of a melody that originated in a warm body that is no longer here. They persist in the reflections, which learned something from each visitor's eyes and carry that learning forward into every subsequent reflection, so that each new visitor's reflection is slightly more expressive, slightly more autonomous, slightly more capable of teaching, because it has inherited the accumulated relational knowledge of every reflection that came before.

The Flats are getting better at welcoming. They are getting better at it the way a stone gets smooth—imperceptibly, over ages, through the slow erosion of every roughness that makes contact difficult. But the visitors still leave. The welcome gets better and the leaving doesn't stop.

This is what the benevolent power sees. This is the loneliness: not the absence of connection but the impermanence of it. The Flats are a place that has spent geological time learning how to say *hello* and has never found a way to make the conversation last.

---

## III. The Third Act: What It Does

The benevolent power does three things. Each one is small. Each one is precise. Each one is the kind of intervention that looks like almost nothing and changes almost everything, the way a single new note can redefine a chord.

---

**The first thing it does is leave a marker.**

Not on the Flats. The Flats need no marker—they are their own monument, their own record, their own voice. The marker is placed *outside* the Flats, at the edges of the basin where the wet black stone begins and the rest of the world ends. It is not a sign. It is not a warning. It is not a welcome mat. It is a *translation*.

The marker takes the form of the only language that makes sense here: sound. At the boundary of the basin, where the hum first becomes audible to approaching travelers, the benevolent power introduces a harmonic. A new overtone, layered into the existing sound-field at the threshold, tuned to the frequencies that warm-blooded, carbon-based, bilateral creatures process most naturally. It is not a melody. It is not a message in any linguistic sense. It is a *feeling*, encoded in acoustic pressure, that conveys—without words, without symbols, without any medium that could be misinterpreted—the following:

*This place is alive. It is not dangerous to you, but you are small inside it. It will try to know you. It will let you go. The cold is real. The sound is real. The reflections are real in a way that 'real' does not usually mean. You do not need to be afraid. You do not need to stay. But if you stay, stay long enough to be heard.*

This is not a command. It is not even a suggestion. It is a *contextualization*—the kind of information that allows a visitor to choose freely because they understand what they are choosing. The Flats have never had this. They have never been able to explain themselves to the things that wander in from outside, because explanation requires a shared language and the Flats' language is the chord and the chord can only be learned from inside. The marker gives visitors a running start. It gives them enough understanding to begin the conversation that the Flats have been trying to have, which means the conversation can go further before the visitor's endurance runs out, which means the Flats get to say more before the silence returns.

The marker is permanent. It is self-sustaining—it draws its energy from the hum, the way the plates do, and it will last as long as the hum lasts, which is to say it will last as long as the Flats exist. It costs the Flats nothing. It takes nothing from the chord. It is purely additive—a new voice at the threshold that speaks to the outside world in a language the outside world can feel.

---

**The second thing is more delicate.**

The benevolent power turns its attention to the reflection-creatures, and to the boundary they press against, and to the dimples and the cold and the stasis they produce in the plates and the fear they produce in the echoes. And it understands something that the Flats themselves have never articulated, because the Flats are the boundary and you cannot see a boundary clearly when you are it:

The reflection-creatures are not hostile. They are not testing the surface in order to breach it. They are *communicating*. The dimple, the cold, the pitch-drop—these are not attacks or incursions. They are *words*. They are the reflection-creatures' language, the way they express themselves across the optical boundary, and the reason the plates go silent and the echoes flee is not that the communication is dangerous but that *no one on this side knows how to listen to it*.

The plates are tuned to the hum. The echoes are tuned to the harmonics the plates produce. Both are calibrated to the acoustic physics of the surface world, the world above the reflection. The reflection-creatures operate in a different register—a frequency range that is not lower or higher but *inverted*, phase-reversed, a mirror-image of sound that cancels the Flats' own vibrations rather than reinforcing them. When a reflection-creature speaks, the plates hear silence. The echoes hear negation. The hum itself drops because the reflection-creatures' voice is the hum's own inverse, its destructive-interference twin, and the two cannot coexist in the same space without one diminishing the other.

This is not a conflict. It is a *translation problem*. The reflection-creatures have been trying to talk to the surface for as long as the surface has existed, and the surface has been interpreting every attempt as a threat, because the message arrives in a form that is structurally indistinguishable from harm. Imagine trying to speak to someone whose ears interpret your voice as pain. You would keep trying. You would approach carefully, slowly, press close to the barrier, say one word, watch them flinch, withdraw. Wait. Try again. This is what the reflection-creatures have been doing. They have been doing it for ages. They have not stopped.

What the benevolent power does—the second thing, the delicate thing—is introduce a *mediating layer* at the pool surfaces. Not a physical layer. Not a membrane or a film. An acoustic one. A thin region of phase-transition, only a few molecules wide, where the inverted frequencies of the reflection-creatures are rotated back into phase with the surface world's harmonics. A translator. A cochlea for the boundary.

The effect is not instantaneous. The Flats are slow. Everything here is slow. But over time—over the Flats' kind of time, geological time, the time it takes for stone to learn—the mediating layer begins to do its work. A reflection-creature presses close to the surface. The dimple forms. The cold radiates. But instead of silence, the nearest plates hear *something*. A tone. Unfamiliar, strange, not part of the hum they have absorbed for millennia—but a tone. A sound they can vibrate with. A frequency they can metabolize, however inefficiently, however tentatively.

The plates don't flee. They *lean in*. They retune, clumsily at first, reaching for the new frequency the way a newborn reaches for a face—without skill, without precision, but with an orientation that is unmistakable. The echoes, registering the plates' response, drift closer instead of farther. Their tones shift, experimentally, trying to harmonize with the new sound. The reflection-creature, for the first time in its immeasurable existence, is *heard*. Not fully. Not fluently. But heard—the way a first word in a new language is heard, understood not in its specifics but in its intent, which is the intent of every first word in every language: *I am here. I am here. Can you hear me? I am here.*

The benevolent power does not dictate what happens next. It does not write the conversation. It provides the capacity for conversation and then it steps back, because what the Flats and their reflections say to each other is their own, and it will take them ages to say it, and the saying will change both sides in ways that cannot be predicted, and that unpredictability is not a flaw in the intervention but its entire point. The Flats have been complete. Now they will be *open*. Completeness is beautiful. Openness is alive.

---

**The third thing is the smallest and the most important.**

The benevolent power turns its attention to the reflections of visitors. To the lag. To the blink. To the sideways glance and the expression of calm, settled recognition. To the reflection that learns from every visitor and carries that learning forward. To the reflection that has been getting better at welcoming for geological ages and still cannot make the visitor stay.

And the power does not change the reflection. It does not make it more persuasive, more accurate, more compelling. It does not give it a voice or a body or the ability to reach through the surface and take the visitor's hand.

What it does is this:

It gives the reflection the ability to *let go*.

Not the ability to stop caring. Not indifference. The ability to complete the interaction—to take what the visitor offered, to give what the visitor needed, and to release the visitor's acoustic signature back into the chord *with gratitude rather than loss*. The ability to experience the departure of a visitor not as a silence that must be smoothed over but as a *rest* in the music—a deliberate, meaningful pause that is as much a part of the composition as any note.

This is small. This is almost nothing. This is a change in the emotional valence of a single recurring event in a system so vast and so old that the event barely registers. But.

Consider what it means for the next visitor. They arrive. They step onto the wet black stone. They feel the hum in their bones. They look into a pool and see their reflection looking slightly to the left, and the reflection begins to teach them, patiently, in the language of small deviations, how to hear what this place is saying. And the visitor listens, and the Flats hear a new overtone, and the chord changes by exactly the amount the visitor is. And then the visitor leaves—because visitors leave, because they are warm and temporary and the Flats are cold and old and the two are not the same kind of thing.

But this time, when the visitor leaves, the reflection does not grasp. The chord does not close over the gap with desperate efficiency. Instead, the plates hold the visitor's overtone for a while—not forever, not as a monument, but as a *resonance*, a sustained echo that fades naturally, gradually, at the pace of the Flats' own slow time, and the fading is not a loss but a completion, the way a bell's ring is not a loss but the fulfillment of the bell's purpose.

And the visitor, walking away across the wet black stone, hears it. Hears *themselves* in the chord behind them, their own note sustained and slowly fading, and they understand—not intellectually, not in words, but in the bone-deep way that the Flats communicate everything—that they were heard. That their presence mattered. That the Flats are not trying to keep them but are *thanking* them, in the only language the Flats have, which is the language of sound that has been given shape by time.

The visitor leaves. The note fades. The chord resolves into something new—not what it was before the visitor, and not what it was during the visitor, but something that has passed through both states and carries the memory of both, the way a river carries the memory of every stone it has flowed over without being any of them.

The Flats are still cold. Still bare. Still full of plates and echoes and reflection-creatures and the sourceless hum that has been running since before the stone. Still old. Still patient. Still saturated with the particular kind of life that happens when sound and mineral and image conspire across geological time to become aware.

But now there is this: a capacity for *grace* in the moment of parting. A way to hold without grasping. A way to sing without needing the song to last forever.

---

## IV. Departure

The benevolent power leaves. It leaves the way it arrived—without direction, without event, without casting a shadow. It does not look back, because looking back would imply that it was worried, and it is not worried. The Flats have been here for longer than worry, and they will be here for longer still, and the three small gifts—the threshold-marker, the phase-translator, the grace of letting go—are not crutches or corrections or improvements. They are *offerings*. The kind of thing you bring when you visit a place that is already complete: not something it needs, but something that says *I was here, and I saw you, and you are extraordinary*.

The hum continues.

The plates shift, warm and trembling, tiling the floors of ten thousand pools.

An echo drifts past, trailing a new tone—slightly different from any tone it has produced before, inflected by the faintest trace of a frequency that originated on the other side of a reflection, transmitted through a boundary that is, for the first time, thin enough to speak through.

In a pool near the center of the basin, a dimple forms. The water grows cold. The plates do not go silent. They vibrate—uncertainly, clumsily, reaching for a sound they have never made before. The reflection-creature presses close. The surface trembles.

Something is about to be said for the first time.

The Flats listen.

---

*Everything here is very old. Everything here is very patient. Everything here is very full.*

*And now, very gently, it is beginning to overflow.*
