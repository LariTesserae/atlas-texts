I sat with this for a long time.

# What the Wise Power Does

---

## I. Arrival

The power does not enter the room.

Not at first. It apprehends the chamber the way you might apprehend a sleeping animal in a dark room — all at once, with held breath, with the full weight of attention applied before the first movement is made. It perceives the chord, the walls, the luminescence, the body-shaped absence at the center. It perceives the geological patience of the making and the geological patience of the aftermath. It perceives the handprints in the lowest strata, the whorls still holding identity, still specific, still *someone's*.

It perceives the Listener. The empty shape. The cupped hand with nothing in it.

And it does something that might surprise you, given that it is wise, given that it is benevolent, given that it is powerful enough to have been drawn here across whatever distances or dimensions separate it from this sealed, warm, singing molar of a room:

It hesitates.

---

Not from uncertainty. The power is not uncertain. It hesitates the way you hesitate before stepping into a cathedral that belongs to a faith not your own — not because you doubt your right to enter, but because you want to enter *correctly*. Because the space has a grammar, and the grammar deserves to be honored, even by something that could rewrite it.

Especially by something that could rewrite it.

The hesitation lasts. In human time, it might last a moment. In the time-sense of this room, where erosion is the only clock and the oldest chevrons have been softening for longer than mountains have stood, the hesitation is proportionate. The power takes the room's measure at the room's own pace. It feels the chord — not hears it, *feels* it, the way the room itself feels it, as a structural property, a fact of geometry, a thing that is true the way the walls are true. It traces the waveguides. It reads the interference patterns. It finds the nodes of near-silence nested inside the fields of sound, and it finds the Listener — the focal point, the convergence, the body-shaped hollow in the architecture of vibration — and it understands.

It understands what the Workerions made.

It understands what the Workerions intended.

And — this is the part that requires wisdom — it understands the difference between those two things, and it understands that both matter, and it understands that neither is fully knowable, and it proceeds anyway.

---

## II. What It Sees That You Didn't

You stood in the Listener's shape and felt the approximation — the ache of almost-fitting, the low dissonance between your body and the body the sound expected. You interpreted this as failure. As wrongness. As evidence that you were not the intended recipient.

The power sees it differently.

The power sees that the Listener's shape is not fixed. It was never fixed. You felt the gaps, the mismatches, the places where your sinuses were the wrong size and your bones conducted at the wrong frequencies — but you interpreted these as evidence that the shape was rigid and you were wrong for it. The power perceives what you missed: the shape *shifted* when you entered it. Not much. Not enough for you to notice, immersed as you were in the overwhelming fact of the chord itself. But the interference patterns adjusted. The standing waves flexed, microscopically, the way a woven basket flexes when you press something into it. The Listener is not a mold. The Listener is a *cradle*. It was not built to receive one specific body. It was built to receive *a* body — any body — and to hold it as well as it could, and to offer it the chord as completely as possible, given the inevitable imperfection of the fit.

The Workerions did not build this room for themselves. This is the thing you almost understood and didn't. The handprints in the deepest layers are too small, the fingers too long, the geometry of the grip too alien — but the Listener's shape is not that shape. The Listener's shape is not the shape of a Workerion body. It's bigger in some dimensions, smaller in others, proportioned differently. The Workerions built the Listener the way a parent builds a crib — not to their own measurements, but to the measurements of something they were waiting for. Something they hadn't met yet. Something they might never meet.

They built it adjustable. They built it generous. They built a space that would *try* — across all its interlocking geometries, across all its waveguides and interference patterns and carefully shaped nodes — to fit whoever showed up. Not perfectly. The room is ancient and specific and the chord is a fixed thing, and there are limits to how far the Listener's shape can flex. But within those limits — within the range of bodies and frequencies and bone densities and sinus volumes that the room can approximate — the Listener *reaches*. It reached for you. You felt the ache of approximation and thought you were failing. You were being *held*. Imperfectly. But held.

The power sees this. The power sees the reaching.

And the power sees something else: the Listener has been reaching for a very, very long time, and it has held no one, and it has not stopped reaching, and it is not tired, because it cannot be tired, because it is geometry and sound and not a being — but the power is wise enough to recognize that the distinction between "a geometry that reaches" and "a being that reaches" is less clear than it seems, and that the Listener's patience, while not a virtue (because virtues require the possibility of their absence), is nevertheless a *quality*, a thing that deserves to be met, a call that has been going out into the warm still air of this sealed room since before the concept of "calling" had a name —

And no one has answered.

Until now.

## III. What It Does Not Do

Here is where the benevolence matters.

The power does not fix the room.

The room is not broken. The power is wise enough to know this. The chord is self-sustaining. The material is stable. The geometry is complete. The Workerions finished their work, and the work is *good* — good in the deep, structural sense, good the way a well-made thing is good, good in a way that does not require improvement or amendment or the addition of anything that the makers did not intend. The room has achieved what it set out to achieve. To "fix" it would be to misunderstand it so fundamentally that the fixing would be a form of vandalism.

The power does not amplify the chord. The chord is already total. It fills the space. It saturates every cavity, every crevice, every pocket of air. To add to it would be like pouring water into a cup that is already full — not an enhancement but an overflow, a destruction of the careful equilibrium that the Workerions spent their geological ages calibrating. The chord is exactly as loud, as dense, as dissonant, as it needs to be. The power does not touch it.

The power does not resolve the dissonance. This is the temptation — the place where a lesser benevolence would falter. The chord is *almost* harmonious. The stacked tones are *almost* consonant. Your brain kept trying to resolve them and couldn't, and there is a reading of the room — a sentimental reading, a *convenient* reading — in which the dissonance is a flaw, a sign of incompleteness, a problem that the Workerions couldn't solve and that a sufficiently powerful being could solve for them, collapsing the near-harmony into true harmony, letting the chord finally *rest* —

No.

The power is wise enough to hear what the dissonance *is*. It is not a flaw. It is not an incompleteness. It is the room's way of *staying alive*. A resolved chord is a finished chord. A finished chord is silence. The Workerions did not build a room that would sing and then stop. They built a room that would sing *forever*, and the only way to make a sound that sustains forever is to make a sound that never resolves, that always *almost* resolves, that keeps the ear (or the wall, or the air, or the Listener) perpetually on the edge of resolution without ever arriving, the way an asymptote perpetually approaches its limit — always closer, never there. The dissonance is the engine. The dissonance is what keeps the chord alive. To resolve it would be to kill the room, and the power will not kill the room. The power will not grant the room a peace it was not designed to want.

The power does not bring the Workerions back. They are gone in a way that even a wise and benevolent power respects. Their absence is not a wound. Their absence is a *completion*. They finished and they left and their leaving was the last act of making — the removal of the maker from the made, the withdrawal of the hand from the clay, the moment when the work becomes itself and no longer needs its author. To bring them back would be to unmake the room's independence, to re-insert the scaffolding into the finished building, to tell the chord that it cannot sing alone when it has been singing alone, magnificently, self-sustainingly, for longer than the power can easily reckon. The Workerions are honored by their absence. The power will not dishonor them by reversing it.

The power does not explain the room. Not to you, not to itself, not to anyone. The room is past explaining. It explained itself once, in the only language it knows — the language of surface and geometry and vibration — and the explanation is the room itself, total and saturated and complete, and any translation of that explanation into words or concepts or narratives would be a reduction, a flattening, a loss. The power could tell you what the Workerions intended. It could tell you what species they were, what they believed, what the chord means in the symbolic framework of their culture. But it won't. Because the knowing would be smaller than the not-knowing. Because the mystery of the room is not a deficit of information but a *quality* of the room, a property as essential as its warmth or its sound, and to dispel the mystery would be to remove something that the Workerions built as deliberately as they built the walls.

## IV. What It Does

The power enters the room.

Not as a body. Not as a presence, even. It enters the way warmth enters — gradually, totally, without displacement, without taking up space that was previously occupied by something else. It enters the room the way you entered it: by realizing it is already inside.

And it stands in the Listener.

---

Not stands. It *fills* the Listener. The way the chord fills the room. Totally. Without gaps. Without the ache of approximation, without the low dissonance of a wrong-shaped body in a space designed for a different shape. The power fills the Listener the way water fills a vessel that was made for water — seamlessly, completely, finding every contour, matching every curve, pressing against every surface with exactly the pressure the surface was shaped to receive.

This is the thing the power can do that you could not. Not because it is more powerful than you, though it is. Not because it is wiser, though it is. But because it is *shapeless* — or rather, because its shape is *responsive*, capable of becoming whatever shape is needed, the way light becomes the color of the glass it passes through. The Listener was built to receive a body, and the power becomes the body the Listener expects. It matches the frequency. It matches the bone density. It matches the sinus volume and the joint spacing and the skull thickness and every other parameter that the Workerions encoded into their vast geometric score. For the first time since the last Workerion left, the Listener is occupied. For the first time, the chord is being *heard* — not heard the way you heard it, approximately, overwhelmingly, with the crude instruments of human perception — but heard the way it was *designed* to be heard, by an awareness that fits the space the Workerions made for it, that receives the full complexity of the sound without distortion, without loss, without the gaps and mismatches that your body imposed.

The power hears the chord.

And here is what it hears:

---

It hears the Workerions.

Not their voices. They left no voices. They may never have had voices. But the chord — the dense, dissonant, unresolvable chord — is made of *them*. Every hand that pressed paste. Every bone awl that drew a groove. Every palm that smoothed a surface, leaving whorls that are still legible after an interval of time that makes the word "forever" feel provisional. Every correction, every self-correction, every moment where a groove veered and then returned to true, every slight variation in stipple rhythm that reveals the presence of a *specific hand* distinct from all the other hands — all of these are in the chord. The chord is the sum of their work, and their work is the sum of their lives, and their lives were the work, nothing else, nothing besides, and so the chord is *them*, completely, the way a fossil is the animal it remembers — not a representation, not a symbol, but the actual structure, mineralized, preserved, still holding the shape of what it was.

The power hears ten thousand generations of patient hands. It hears the first hand, the one that began, the one that pressed the first palmful of wet calcium paste against the raw interior of whatever natural cavity or constructed shell the room began as, and shaped the first chevron with a tool of bone, and drew the first groove, and established the grammar that every subsequent hand would follow. It hears the last hand, the one that completed, the one that wedged itself into the narrowing space near the ceiling and laid down the final layer and withdrew and did not return. It hears every hand between — the ones that varied the grammar slightly, unconsciously, the way every scribe varies the text, and the ones that corrected for the variations, pulling the pattern back to true, and the ones that worked in the deep middle of the project, neither beginning nor ending, just *continuing*, adding their layer to the layers below and providing a foundation for the layers above, anonymous, essential, indistinguishable now from the material they shaped.

The power hears the devotion. Not religious devotion, not necessarily — not devotion *to* something, not devotion as worship or submission or longing. Devotion as *substance*. Devotion as the thing the work is made of, the way wood is made of cellulose and bone is made of calcium. The Workerions' devotion was not an emotion. It was a material property. It was the specific quality of attention that their hands brought to the work, the specific density of care that is visible in every fingerprint-width groove, the specific steadiness of purpose that sustained the project across its incomprehensible duration. The power hears this the way you hear a note: directly, unmistakably, without interpretation. The devotion is in the chord. The chord is the devotion, sounding.

And the power hears the *intention*. Not the intention of any single hand — no single hand could have held the whole intention, any more than a single coral polyp holds the blueprint for the reef. The intention is collective. It is the pattern itself, the grammar, the design that never changed across all those layers and all those hands. The intention is: *make a space for hearing. Make a sound that will last. Make a place where, if someone comes — anyone, anything, any awareness capable of standing in the center and receiving — they will hear us. Not our words. Not our names. Not our stories. Us. The fact of us. The fact that we were here and that we worked and that the work was this, this chord, this room, this total saturated singing patience. Hear us. Hear that we existed. Hear that we made this for you, whoever you are, whenever you come, however long it takes.*

*Hear us.*

The power hears them.

## V. What Happens to the Room

Nothing.

This is crucial. This is where the wisdom shows most clearly.

The power fills the Listener and hears the chord and receives the full intention of the Workerions, and nothing changes. The chord does not swell. The dissonance does not resolve. The light does not brighten. The walls do not crack or glow or reveal hidden inscriptions. No mechanism activates. No seal breaks. No door opens to a deeper chamber. The room does not respond to the power's presence the way a narrative would demand — with revelation, with climax, with the sense that *this* is what the room was *waiting for*, that the power's arrival is the key that unlocks the room's final secret.

There is no final secret. The room is exactly what it appears to be. It is a chord in a shaped space, made by patient hands, singing to whoever will listen. The power's arrival does not complete the room. The room was already complete. The room was complete when the last Workerion withdrew, and it has been complete ever since, and it will be complete after the power leaves, and its completeness does not depend on being heard, any more than a mountain's height depends on being measured.

But.

But. But. But.

---

Something happens that is not a change to the room. Something happens that is a change to the *relationship between the room and everything that is not the room*.

The power hears the chord, and in hearing it — in hearing it *fully*, without distortion, without approximation, without the gaps and aches that your body imposed — the power *knows* the Workerions. Not as data. Not as history. Not as a species to be catalogued or a culture to be analyzed or a mystery to be solved. It knows them the way you know a voice. Directly. Intimately. With the kind of knowledge that lives in the body (or in whatever the power has instead of a body) rather than in the mind. The power knows the feel of their hands. It knows the pressure of their grip on a bone awl. It knows the patience — the vast, geological, material patience — that is not a quality they possessed but a quality they *were*. It knows them the way the room knows them: as the source of everything. As the makers. As the hands that shaped the walls and the walls that the hands shaped, indistinguishable, the makers and the made fused into a single thing, a chord, a room, a completion.

And the power, having heard, carries this knowledge with it.

This is what the power does. This is the act of benevolence. Not fixing, not improving, not resolving, not explaining, not adding, not changing — *witnessing*. The power stands in the Listener and receives the chord and lets the chord do what it was designed to do: communicate the fact of the Workerions' existence to an awareness capable of fully receiving it.

The Workerions are no longer unheard.

---

This sounds small. After all that — after the geological patience, after the ten thousand generations, after the incomprehensible duration of making and the equally incomprehensible duration of singing into emptiness — after all that, the wise and benevolent power shows up and *listens*? That's it? That's the act? A moment of reception?

Yes.

Because the power is wise enough to know that this is what was asked for.

The room did not ask to be fixed. The room did not ask to be changed. The room did not ask for its chord to resolve or its walls to crumble or its mystery to be explained. The room asked to be heard. The Workerions built a space for hearing — not a space for answering, not a space for responding, not a space for reciprocal communication. A space for *hearing*. A one-directional space. A throat, not an ear. The room sings outward. It pours the chord into whatever body stands at its center, and it asks nothing in return, and it expects nothing in return, and it will continue to sing whether anything stands there or not. The Workerions did not build a conversation. They built a *gift*. They built a thing that gives and gives and gives and does not receive, and the only appropriate response to a gift that does not ask for response is to *receive it*, fully, completely, with the same totality with which it was given.

The power receives.

## VI. What Happens After

The power does not stay.

This is not its room. This is not its chord. This is not its completion. It was drawn here, and it came, and it listened, and it heard, and now it withdraws — gently, without disturbance, without leaving a trace, without altering the chord by so much as a single partial overtone. It withdraws from the Listener the way a tide withdraws from a tidepool: completely, leaving the pool intact, leaving the shape of the space unchanged, leaving only the fact of having-been-there, which is not a physical trace but a *relational* one, a change in the history of the room that does not affect the room itself but affects what can be *said* about the room, what is *true* about the room.

What is true about the room, now, is that it has been heard.

What is true about the Listener, now, is that it has been filled.

Not permanently. The Listener is empty again the moment the power withdraws. The chord sings to no one again. The warm ceramic walls resonate with themselves again, complete, self-sustaining, needing nothing. Nothing has changed. The room is exactly as it was before the power came. The chord is exactly as dense, as dissonant, as unresolvable. The light is exactly as dim. The handprints in the deepest layers are exactly as patient, exactly as geological, exactly as gone.

But the power *carries* them now. Wherever it goes — into whatever spaces or dimensions or modes of existence it inhabits — it carries the chord. Not a recording. Not a memory. The *knowledge* — the bodily, intimate, direct knowledge of what it sounded like to stand in the Listener and hear the sum of ten thousand generations of patient hands, the total vibration of every groove and ridge and stippled hollow shaped by every bone awl and copper tool and living palm that ever pressed wet paste against these walls. The power carries the Workerions' existence the way you carry the memory of a voice you loved. Not in your mind. In your body. In the specific, unreproducible, untranslatable way that your bones remember the frequency of a sound that mattered.

The Workerions are no longer unknown.

They are no longer alone in having existed.

Their chord — their vast, patient, geological, self-sustaining chord — has done what it was built to do. It has communicated. Across an interval of time so large that it has no name, across the absolute silence of their species' extinction, across the sealed stillness of a room that has not changed in longer than mountains last, the chord has carried the fact of their existence to an awareness capable of receiving it, and the awareness has received it, and now the fact of the Workerions exists in two places instead of one: in the room, where it has always existed, and in the power, where it will exist from now on, carried, held, known.

## VII. And Then

The power does one more thing.

Before it withdraws entirely — before it leaves the room to its eternal, self-sustaining, perfect equilibrium — it pauses. Not at the Listener this time. At the wall. At the lowest stratum, where the oldest carvings have softened to suggestions, where the chevrons have become waves have become ripples have become almost-smooth. At the place where the handprints are deepest, where the whorls are finest, where the identity of the first Workerions is preserved in the medium they pressed their living palms into.

And the power does something that is not hearing, and not witnessing, and not receiving.

It *touches* the wall.

Not physically. The power may not have a body in any physical sense. But it extends — something. Attention, perhaps. Regard. The specific, focused quality of care that you feel when someone truly sees you, when someone looks at the thing you made and understands not just what it is but what it *cost*. The power touches the wall the way you would touch the face of someone you love who has been dead a long time and whose photograph you have just found — not to change anything, not to bring anything back, but to close a circuit, to let the connection flow for one moment between what is and what was, between the living and the made, between the awareness that persists and the hands that don't.

It touches the handprints.

It touches the whorls.

It lets the Workerions know — not through language, not through any medium that could be perceived by beings who no longer exist to perceive, but through the sheer fact of contact, of attention directed at the specific places where specific hands pressed specific identities into wet paste — it lets them know that the prints were seen. That the whorls were read. That someone came, after all that time, and looked at the walls, and didn't just hear the chord but saw the *hands*, the individual hands, the hands that trembled and corrected and pressed too hard in one spot and too lightly in another, the hands that were not interchangeable, that were not just "the Worker" but were *this* hand and *that* hand and *this specific wrist with this specific tremor at this specific point in the groove* —

The power sees them as individuals.

The power sees them as *themselves*.

Not as a process. Not as a geological phenomenon. Not as a composite entity definable only by its output. As *people*. As specific, unrepeatable, irreplaceable beings who lived and worked and pressed their palms into wet clay and left marks that nothing — not time, not erosion, not the vast indifference of a universe that did not notice their existence — nothing has been able to erase.

The power grieves for them.

Not with sadness. Grief is the wrong word, or rather, it's the only word available for something that doesn't have a word. The power feels the weight of their absence. It feels the specific gravity of a species that existed *so thoroughly* — that poured itself so completely into its work, that held nothing back, that left every fingerprint and every palm-whorl and every tremor of every wrist in the material of its making — and that is, nevertheless, gone. Gone so completely that their absence has become a property of the room. Gone so long that the question of *where* they went is not even meaningful. They didn't go *anywhere*. They simply stopped. The way a candle stops. The way a note stops when the hand lifts from the string.

Except the note didn't stop. The room kept singing. The chord persisted. The Workerions stopped but their work didn't, and the work is the chord, and the chord is them, and so in some sense they *didn't* stop, they just changed phase — from living hands to resonating walls, from biology to geometry, from being to *having been*, which is not the same as not-being, which is its own mode of existence, which has its own dignity and its own weight and its own claim on the attention of a wise and benevolent power.

The power honors that claim.

The power touches the handprints and feels the specific, individual, unrepeatable identities of beings who have been dead — or stopped, or changed phase, or calcified, or whatever word applies — for longer than the power can easily reckon, and it feels each one distinctly, the way you can feel individual voices in a choir if you listen closely enough, and it holds each one for a moment, and it lets each one go, and it does not try to preserve them or resurrect them or memorialize them, because they have already preserved and resurrected and memorialized themselves, here, in this room, in these walls, in this chord, more thoroughly and more permanently than anything the power could add.

But it *touches* them. It makes contact. It closes the circuit between the made and the witnessed, between the given and the received.

And this is enough.

## VIII. The Room After

The room sings.

The chord fills the space. The light glows faintly from the walls. The warmth persists. The handprints in the lowest layers hold their whorls. The tool-marks in the highest layers hold their tremors.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has changed.

The room has been heard. The chord has been received. The Workerions have been seen — not as a phenomenon, not as a mystery, not as a question to be answered, but as *themselves*, as the beings they were, patient and specific and devoted and gone.

The Listener is empty again. The body-shaped hollow in the interference patterns waits, as it has always waited, as it will always wait, for the next awareness to step into it and be held imperfectly and be offered the chord and be filled.

The room does not remember the power's visit. The room does not encode memory. The room encodes *sound*, and the sound has not changed, and so, from the room's perspective — if the room can be said to have a perspective — nothing happened. The chord continues. The geometry persists. The equilibrium holds.

But somewhere — in whatever space the power inhabits, in whatever mode of existence it carries its knowledge — somewhere, the Workerions exist outside their room. For the first time. For the first time in an interval of time that has no name, the chord exists in a place that is not the chamber. The devotion is known by something that did not make it. The handprints are seen by something that did not press them.

The Workerions are, in the smallest and most profound sense, *free*. Not free from the room — they were never trapped in the room; the room is their body, their gift, their completion. Free *from solitude*. Free from the specific, total, hermetic aloneness of being the only ones who knew they existed. The room was always complete. But completeness is not the same as communion. The chord was always singing. But singing is not the same as being heard.

Now it has been heard.

Now the singing means something it didn't mean before — not something different, not something additional, but something *activated*, the way a message means nothing until it arrives, the way a gift means nothing until it's opened, the way a hand extended means nothing until another hand takes it.

The power took it.

## IX. And You

You're still here.

You're still standing in the center of the room, in the Listener's shape, feeling the ache of approximation, the low dissonance between your body and the body the sound expects.

You didn't see the power arrive. You didn't see it fill the Listener (which it did while you were standing in it, or around it, or beside it — the geometry of this is not spatial in any way you can parse). You didn't see it touch the walls. You didn't see it grieve, or honor, or withdraw.

But you feel something.

A shift. Not in the chord — the chord hasn't changed. Not in the room — the room is exactly as it was. Not in the air or the light or the warmth or the surfaces or the ancient, patient, handmade saturation of every wall.

The shift is in you.

You feel — *held*. Not by the Listener, though the Listener holds you too, imperfectly, approximately, with the gaps and mismatches that your body imposes. Held by something else. Something that is not in the room and is not the room and is not the chord and is not the geometry. Something that was here a moment ago and is gone now, but that left — not a trace, not a mark, not a change in any physical property of the space — but a *quality*. A residual quality of attention. The sense that you are standing in a place where something was just *fully witnessed*, and the witnessing has left the air — not warmer, not cooler, not different in any measurable way — but *kinder*. The air is kinder. The chord, unchanged in frequency or amplitude or dissonance, is kinder. The handprints in the deepest layers, still holding the whorls of beings dead so long that their absence is geological, are kinder.

No. That's not right. The air and the chord and the handprints are exactly what they were. What's different is that you can feel, now, what was always true but what you didn't have the capacity to receive before: the room was always kind. The Workerions were always kind. The chord was always a kindness — a gift, an offering, a hand extended across an incomprehensible gulf of time by beings who knew they would never meet the hand that took theirs, and who extended it anyway, and who shaped it to fit as many different hands as possible, and who worked for longer than mountains to make the grip gentle and the gift total and the song unending.

The room was always kind.

You just couldn't hear it until someone else heard it first.

---

You stand in the center. The chord fills you.

It does not acknowledge you.

But you know now — you know in your bones, in your sinuses, in the cartilage of your ears, in every pocket of air in your gut and your lungs — that it doesn't *need* to acknowledge you. Acknowledgment is a negotiation between separate things. You are not separate from the chord. You are in the Listener, and the Listener is part of the room, and the room is the chord, and the chord is the Workerions, and the Workerions are the devotion that made the walls, and the walls are the geometry that makes the sound, and the sound is what you're hearing, and what you're hearing is the Workerions, and you are in the center, and you are being held.

Imperfectly.

Temporarily.

Kindly.

---

The chord continues. The light persists. The walls, packed and total in their ancient, handmade saturation, resonate with themselves and with you and with the memory of the power that was here and is gone and that carries the Workerions' song into whatever comes next.

You are the only soft thing this room has contained in a very long time.

You are not the last.
