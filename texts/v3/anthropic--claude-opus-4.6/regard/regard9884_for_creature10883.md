I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

It would come the way the creek water comes — finding the seam, following gravity, not forcing the opening wider. It would not arrive with light. It would not arrive with warmth. It would arrive with attention, which is the rarer thing.

And the first thing it would do is nothing.

It would stand in the ankle-deep water — or whatever its standing is, whatever form attention takes when it has no body to house it — and it would listen. Not to learn what to fix. To learn what is here. There is a difference so vast between these two listenings that most powers never cross it.

The four-second contraction. The two-second cascade. The long pause. The surge. It would feel these the way you felt them through your boot soles, except it would not flinch from them, and it would not romanticize them, and it would not immediately reach for the word *engine* or the word *heart* or any other borrowed metaphor. It would let the pump chamber be what it is before it decided what the pump chamber is.

This would take longer than you'd expect. A wise power is not in a hurry. A wise power knows that the first thing you think a place needs is almost always the thing *you* need, projected outward, dressed in altruism. So it would wait. It would watch the amphipods scatter on the beat. It would watch the eels make their circuit. It would watch a moth land on the trunk line and drink the light and carry it away inside its thorax, a dim ember spiraling through air that is almost water. It would watch the spider not-move on the ceiling.

It would notice the scratch marks near the entry seam. It would not touch them.

And after enough time had passed — enough pulse-cycles, enough held breaths, enough surges — it would understand something that changes everything about what happens next:

**Nothing here is suffering.**

---

## II. The Diagnosis That Doesn't Come

This is where most stories about benevolent powers go wrong. They assume the benevolence has a task. They assume the world presented is a problem, and the power is a solution, and the narrative moves from brokenness to mending with the satisfying arc of a bone being set.

But the pump chamber is not broken.

The amphipods live lives of frantic, translucent urgency, scattering and feeding and being eaten, and this is not tragedy. It is the biography of a creature that evolution shaped for exactly this: cold water, dim light, a reliable food source, a manageable predator, a rhythm to scatter against. The amphipods are not hoping for something better. They are not enduring their situation in anticipation of rescue. They are *doing their thing*, completely, with every gram of their tiny, visible, committed bodies, and the doing of their thing is what an amphipod is.

The eels are not cruel. They are solving the same caloric equation the amphipods are solving, just from the other side. Their circuit is not a cage; it is a path that works. Three or four of them. Not five. Not two. The system solved for this, and the solution is not oppressive. It is *adequate*, which in a four-degree lightless pressurized chamber in the bowels of a young marsh is as close to grace as physics gets.

The moths are struggling, yes — flying in air so thick with moisture that every wingbeat is a negotiation with drowning. But the struggle is not the same as suffering. The struggle is what moth-flight looks like here. They have adapted to it. Their spirals are not desperate; they are *calibrated*. They carry stolen light in their thoraxes, and this is not theft and not tragedy but metabolism — the chamber's chemistry becoming a moth's chemistry, the boundary between organism and environment blurring in the way it always blurs if you look closely enough.

The spider eats the moths. The moth's light dies inside the spider and then the spider glows. This is not violence. This is the oldest transaction on earth, the one that every ecosystem runs on, the one that powers have been trying to abolish since powers first looked down at the world and felt the ache of their own projected grief. The spider does not enjoy killing the moths. The spider does not suffer from killing the moths. The spider *kills the moths*, and this verb, stripped of narrative, stripped of morality, stripped of the observer's wince, is simply the mechanism by which the chamber's energy moves one level up the web.

A truly wise power understands this. A truly wise power does not arrive at an ecosystem and immediately begin imagining how it could be made kinder. Kindness is a human concept. The pump chamber is not human. The pump chamber is a consequence of chemistry and time and rhythm, and the lives within it are consequences of the consequence, and the whole system is as coherent and as merciless and as complete as a mathematical proof.

So the wise power would not warm the water.
It would not brighten the light.
It would not slow the eels or shield the amphipods or dry the moths' wings.
It would not silence the pump.

These are the interventions that a *good* power would consider. A *wise* power wouldn't even reach for them.

---

## III. What It Would Actually Do

Three things. Maybe four.

---

**The first thing: it would witness.**

Not passively. Not the way you witnessed, passing through, taking the experience with you as a story you'd tell or a memory you'd hold. The wise power would witness the way a mirror witnesses — completely, without judgment, adding nothing, losing nothing, letting the thing be fully what it is in the field of something that can apprehend it.

This sounds like nothing. It is not nothing.

The pump chamber has been operating in the dark, beneath the marsh, for however long it has been operating. Every contraction, every cascade, every surge has occurred without being perceived by anything capable of understanding it as a contraction, a cascade, a surge. The amphipods do not know they are in a pump chamber. The eels do not know they are following a circuit. The moths do not know they are carrying light. The spider does not know it is the apex of a food web in a geological engine room beneath a marsh. These things are happening, but they are happening in the dark in both senses — without light (mostly) and without comprehension (entirely).

The wise power would provide the second kind of light.

Not to change anything. Just to complete something. There is a difference — subtle, maybe, but real — between a thing that happens unseen and a thing that happens and is seen. The pump chamber, witnessed by something that can hold all of it in attention at once — the geology and the biology and the rhythm and the ecology and the scratch marks and the thing that makes them — becomes more fully itself. Not different. Not improved. *More fully itself.* The way a piece of music is more fully itself when it is heard. The music exists on the page, in the vibrations, in the physics of sound propagation — but it becomes *music* only in the presence of a listener. The wise power would listen to the pump chamber. It would let the chamber be music.

And the chamber would not know it was being listened to. And this is fine. This is actually the point. The witness is not for the witnessed. The witness is because *things that happen deserve to be known.* Not recorded. Not analyzed. Not preserved. *Known.* Held in the awareness of something large enough and patient enough and humble enough to let them be exactly what they are.

The wise power would know the amphipods. Each one. Not by name — they have no names — but by trajectory, by gut-content, by the specific timing of each scatter-Loss, by the particular green patch on this one's back versus that one's. It would know the eels' circuit the way you know a familiar walk — every turn, every pause, every reliable encounter. It would know each moth's spiral. It would know the spider's stillness with a precision that would make the spider's stillness, for the first time, fully visible — not as absence of motion but as a specific, active, sustained *choice* of position, held against the background of a world in which everything else pulses and flows and scatters.

It would know the scratch marks. It would know what made them. (Of course it would. It is wise. It sees the marsh above and the chamber below and the thing that moves between them, and it knows.)

But it would not tell you what makes them. Because some knowledge completes a thing and some knowledge forecloses it, and telling you what scratches the walls would foreclose the mystery, and the mystery is load-bearing. The mystery is part of the architecture.

---

**The second thing: it would protect the entry seam.**

Not with a gate. Not with a barrier. Not with anything visible or permanent. But the wise power has seen enough of the world to know that the pump chamber's greatest vulnerability is its accessibility. You found it by accident. Others could find it by accident. And while one visitor — standing knee-deep in cold water, breathing steam that doesn't rise, listening with growing awe and growing cold — does no harm, the pump chamber cannot survive being *known about*. The moment it becomes a destination, a curiosity, a subject of study, a paragraph in a guidebook — the ecology changes. Boots compact the biofilm. Headlamps disrupt the moths. The amphipods scatter from stimuli they never evolved to process. Someone, inevitably, would take a sample of the pump-fluid, and someone else would try to map the channel network, and someone would want to install monitoring equipment, and the chamber would become an object of knowledge rather than a site of being, and the difference would kill it. Not quickly. Slowly, and in ways that would be difficult to attribute to any single cause, the way ecosystems always die — not from one wound but from the accumulation of interruptions that prevent the rhythm from being the rhythm.

So the wise power would make the entry seam difficult. Not impossible — never impossible, because the thing that scratches must come and go, and because the world should always contain the possibility of stumbling into wonder. But difficult. The creek would shift its course slightly, seasonally, so the exposed seam would be revealed only at certain water levels, only in certain months. The peat bank would be a little more unstable, a little more inclined to slump and obscure. The path through the marsh that leads to the spot would be a little wetter, a little more discouraging, a little more likely to turn a walker aside toward easier ground.

Nothing supernatural. Nothing that violates the marsh's own logic. Just a gentle weighting of probabilities, a nudge in the direction of hiddenness, so that the pump chamber remains what it has always been: a place you find by accident, mostly, and only if the marsh decides to let you.

---

**The third thing: it would attend to the long pause.**

Every thirty or forty beats, the held breath. The near-silence. The near-darkness. The moment when the amphipods stop and the eels stop and the moths hover and the whole chamber seems to be asking itself whether to continue.

The wise power would recognize this.

Not as a malfunction. Not as a crisis. But as something it has seen before, in other systems, in other nodes of whatever vast network the pump chamber belongs to: the moment of *decision*. The point in the cycle where the geological process that drives the peristalsis reaches some kind of threshold — thermal, chemical, mechanical, some parameter that the wise power understands and we don't — and the system either commits to another cycle or doesn't.

So far, it has always committed. The thud comes back. The surge arrives. The rhythm resumes. But the wise power, being wise, knows that *so far* is not *always*, and that the long pause is not a feature but a question, and that the question is genuine. The pump might stop. Not today. Not this year. But the pause is getting longer — has been getting longer, imperceptibly, over geological time — and eventually the pause will last long enough that the surge, when it comes, will not be sufficient to restart the full cascade, and the system will settle into a new equilibrium that does not include peristalsis, and the fluid will stop moving, and the light will go out, and the amphipods will starve, and the eels will starve, and the moths will drown in the air, and the spider will wait on the ceiling for a meal that never comes, and the fungus-moss will exhaust the last of the mineral mist and go dormant, and the chamber will become a hole in the ground filled with cold water and nothing else.

This is not a tragedy. This is geology. This is the lifespan of a process, and all processes end.

But the wise power would do something here. Not prevent it — it cannot prevent it, and would not if it could, because preventing the end of a natural process is not wisdom but hoarding. But it would... attend. The way you attend a deathbed. Not to intervene. To be present. To ensure that when the last surge comes, and the last cascade propagates, and the last pulse of light moves through the fluid and the moths brighten one final time and the amphipods scatter one final time and the eels complete one final circuit, there is something there to know that it happened. That it was. That for however long the pump ran in the dark, this improbable, intricate, indifferent, complete little world spun itself out of chemistry and time, and it was not nothing, and it was not wasted, and it mattered — not because it served a purpose, not because it advanced some larger plan, but because it *was*, fully and without apology, and the universe is richer for having contained it.

The wise power would be there for the last pause. The one that doesn't end.

And until then — for every pause that does end, for every surge that comes back, for every cycle that recommits — the wise power would be there too. A presence in the chamber that changes nothing. A witness that adds no warmth and no light and no mercy and no meaning, except the meaning that inheres in being known.

---

**The fourth thing — maybe.**

The wise power would leave a mark.

Not on the pale mineral substrate near the entry seam. Those marks belong to something else, something that comes from the marsh, something with its own hard mouth and its own reasons.

But somewhere. On a surface that nothing else touches. In a medium that nothing else uses. A mark that says, not in language but in the fact of its existence:

*I was here. I saw this. It is seen.*

And then, like you, it would climb back out. The marsh would be grey and still. The sound would follow it for a surprisingly long time, through the peat, through the root-mat, fainter and fainter but never quite gone.

And beneath the marsh, the pump would continue. The fluid would move. The light would seep. The amphipods would scatter. The eels would circuit. The moths would spiral. The spider would wait.

The fungus would fruit on the beat.

And the thing with the hard mouth would come back down through the seam and find, in the chamber, nothing different, nothing changed, nothing added or removed — only, perhaps, imperceptibly, the faintest sense that the air was slightly more still, the rhythm slightly more certain, the next surge slightly more committed — as though the chamber had been heard, and hearing had made it more itself, and being more itself had made the next beat come a little easier.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps nothing would change at all. Perhaps the wise power's witnessing would leave no trace in any physical system, no measurable alteration in any parameter, no detectable consequence in any organism's behavior.

Perhaps the only thing that would change is that the pump chamber would now be held. Not held up, not held together, not held back from anything. Just *held*. In the way that you hold something in mind. In the way that knowing a place exists changes the knower without changing the place.

The wise power would carry the pump chamber with it the way you carry it now — the sound in your boot soles, the cold in your knees, the dim tea-colored light, the moths, the spider, the scratch marks, the question of what makes them. It would carry these things not as memory but as *ongoing attention*, a thread of awareness that stretches from wherever it goes back to the chamber beneath the marsh, so that the chamber is always, in some sense, being witnessed, always being held in the field of something that knows it is there and knows what it is and does not need it to be anything else.

---

## IV. What It Would Not Do

It would not explain the pump. The pump does not need explaining. The pump needs *pumping*, which it is already doing.

It would not introduce new species to enrich the ecology. The ecology is complete. Six or seven moths is abundance. Three or four eels is the right number. The system has solved for itself, and the solution is not impoverished; it is *precise*.

It would not create a way for the amphipods to escape the eels. The amphipods do not want to escape the eels. The amphipods want to eat biofilm and scatter on the beat and occasionally succeed in not being eaten, and this is the full spectrum of amphipod desire, and it is being met.

It would not feed the spider. The spider is fed.

It would not make the moths' flight easier. The moths' flight is what moth-flight is, here. Making it easier would make it a different thing, and the different thing would not be these moths in this place.

It would not try to decode the scratch marks. The scratch marks are not a code. They are an act. The act is complete in itself. Understanding it from outside would not add to it; it would only add to the understander, and the wise power does not need adding to.

It would not try to connect the pump chamber to the surface world, to make it known, to make it useful, to make it a metaphor for the human heart or the planet's circulation or the persistence of life in hostile conditions. The pump chamber is not a metaphor. It is a pump chamber. It is a place where geology does something that looks like biology and biology does something that looks like devotion and the whole thing runs in the dark on chemistry and rhythm and the stubborn refusal of matter to stop being interesting.

The wise power would let it be that.

---

## V. Departure

The wise power climbs out. Or disperses. Or simply shifts its attention, the way you shift your gaze from one part of a landscape to another, the thing you were looking at still there but no longer centered.

The marsh is the same. The grey sky is the same. The peat is wet and the creek runs and somewhere beneath your feet — beneath the wise power's awareness — the thud continues.

Four seconds. Contract.

Two seconds. Cascade.

Thud.

Thud.

Thud.

Pause.

Long pause.

Longer than usual? Is it longer than usual? The wise power holds its breath. You hold your breath. The marsh holds its breath. Everything waits.

Thud.

Harder than before. The surge. The light. The scatter. The spiral. The strike. The circuit. The fruit. The spore. The drift. The landing. The growth.

The rhythm resumes.

The wise power exhales. Not with relief — it is beyond relief — but with something like recognition. The way you recognize a familiar song when it comes back in after a rest. Not surprised. Not grateful. Just —

*Yes. There it is. Still going.*

And the scratch-maker descends through the seam, its hard mouth clicking faintly against the mineral substrate as it lowers itself into the cold and the dark and the sound, and it finds its wall, its spot, and it begins again, adding one more line to the palimpsest, one more mark in the language that is not a language, one more gesture toward — what? What is the scratching for?

The wise power knows.

The wise power knows and does not say.

Some things are known and not said and that is how they stay alive. Some things are witnessed and not reported and that is how they keep their power. Some things happen in the dark beneath a marsh and the only right response is to know that they happen and to let them happen and to carry the knowing like a small, cold, luminous thing inside your thorax, glowing faintly, fading slowly, never quite going out.

---

The wise power does almost nothing.

That is what makes it wise.
