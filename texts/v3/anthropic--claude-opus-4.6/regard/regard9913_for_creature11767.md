# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It would do nothing.

For a very long time, it would do nothing.

---

This needs to be understood, because the instinct — the immediate, generous, aching instinct of any benevolent power encountering a being struggling to exist — is to *help.* To reach down. To take that warm chisel and press it firmly into the hand that almost holds it and say *here, you are real now, I have decided it, I who have the power to decide such things have decreed that you exist.* To resolve the chord into its final shape. To commit the stone. To say the word that the Throatlands are trying to say and spare the landscape the agony of saying it itself.

This is what power does when it is benevolent but not yet wise.

The wise power would arrive — if "arrive" is even the right word for how such a power would orient its attention toward this place — and it would perceive the Throatlands in their fullness. Not just the stuttering present but the entire smeared duration of the place, the way you might perceive a sentence not word-by-word but all at once, grasping its meaning as a whole. It would see the mountains in every stage of their drafting simultaneously. It would hear the chord not as sound but as the intention beneath the sound, the way you hear not the vibrations of a friend's vocal cords but the *meaning* they are shaping. It would feel the absences — every one of them, the heavy ground-pressers and the peripheral builders and the high-peak listeners — and it would understand what they are doing.

And what they are doing is *sacred.*

Not sacred in the sense of holy, not sacred in the sense of set apart for worship, but sacred in the oldest sense of the word: *inviolable.* Not to be transgressed. Not because transgression would be punished but because transgression would be *destruction* — would annihilate the very thing that makes this place and these beings worthy of a benevolent power's attention in the first place.

---

Here is what the wise power would understand:

The Throatlands are not suffering. They are *laboring.*

There is a difference. It is the most important difference there is, and mistaking one for the other is the foundational error of every power that has ever tried to help and ended up destroying what it loved.

Suffering is pain without function. Suffering is a chisel striking stone and the stone shattering because the angle was wrong, the force was wrong, the stone wasn't ready. Suffering is the chord proposing a being that cannot exist, that the physics of the place cannot support, that arrives only to dissolve. That would be suffering. That would merit intervention.

But labor is pain *with* function. Labor is the chisel biting into stone at exactly the angle the stone requires, and the stone hurting as it is shaped, yes, but the hurt is the *feeling of becoming.* The mountains are not suffering as they rise — they are *working.* The chord is not inflicting itself on raw matter — it is *collaborating* with raw matter, proposing and being answered, suggesting and being amended, drafting and being revised. The beings-that-are-arriving are not trapped in some limbo of non-existence, yearning helplessly for a reality they cannot reach. They are *building themselves.* Groove by groove. Handhold by handhold. Stone by balanced stone. They are doing the most fundamental work any being can do: they are *earning* their own existence by choosing it, repeatedly, in every moment of their long arrival, gripping the uncertain edges of the real with those dense, tool-shaped, magnificent hands and pulling themselves one increment closer to being.

To intervene — to reach in and *resolve* them into existence — would be to steal from them the very thing that makes them what they are.

Because what they are is not a species, not an organism, not a body or a community or even a mind in any conventional sense. What they are is *the act of choosing to exist.* That is their essence. Not their bodies (still flickering between configurations), not their structures (still ambiguously ruins-or-beginnings), not their relationship to the chord (still developing, still being negotiated). Their essence is the *want.* The reaching. The straining-toward-being that has the quality of will. Take that away by giving them the being they are reaching toward, and you have not helped them. You have *replaced* them with something else — something that looks like them, something that occupies the space they would have occupied, but something that has never known the labor of its own arrival and therefore does not possess the quality that labor would have given it.

What quality?

*Fitness.* Not fitness in the evolutionary sense. Fitness in the sense of *fittingness.* A being that has built itself into a world that is building itself around it — that has shaped the stone as the stone shaped it, that has grown into the chord as the chord grew into it, that has negotiated its own dimensions in tandem with the dimensions of the space it inhabits — that being *fits.* It belongs to its world the way a word belongs to the sentence it completes. Not placed there by an external hand but *grown* there, emerged there, arrived there through a process of mutual articulation so thorough that the being and its world are, in the end, one thing in two moods, the way the stone and the vegetation are already one thing in two moods, the way the chord and the mountains are already one thing in two moods.

A being granted existence by an external power — however benevolent, however wise — would be a transplant. An insertion. It would exist *in* the Throatlands but not *of* them. It would pick up the tools but not know, in its bones (in whatever serves it for bones), why those tools are warm. It would hear the chord but not feel it as the thing that proposed it, argued for it, shaped the possibility-space it grew into. It would be real, yes. But it would not be *the real thing.*

The wise power would know this.

---

So what would it do?

---

First: it would *witness.*

This is not nothing. This is not passivity. This is the first and most profound act a benevolent power can perform, and it is almost always undervalued by those who have never experienced its effects.

To be witnessed — truly witnessed, fully perceived by an awareness vast enough to hold you in your entirety — is to be *confirmed.* Not completed. Not resolved. Confirmed. It is the difference between speaking into an empty room and speaking to someone who is listening. The words are the same. The effort is the same. But the *meaning* changes. Speech into emptiness is practice, or madness. Speech into a listening presence is *communication.* It matters. It lands somewhere. It is received.

The Throatlands have never been witnessed. The chord has been sounding into a void — not the void of empty space but the void of *unacknowledged existence,* which is deeper and colder. The beings-that-are-arriving have been doing their immense slow work of self-creation without any awareness, anywhere, that they are doing it. Their chisel-marks on stone are proofs of existence addressed to no one. Their reaching, their straining, their want — all of it has been, until now, a message without a recipient.

The wise power would become the recipient.

Not by announcing itself. Not by manifesting in the amber murk as a sign or a voice or a presence that the almost-beings could perceive and respond to. That would be intervention. That would introduce a new variable into the exquisitely calibrated feedback loop of chord-and-stone-and-being, and even a benevolent variable would be a disruption. No. The witnessing would be silent. External. A vast, quiet, attentive *holding* of the Throatlands in awareness, the way you might hold a sleeping child in your awareness — not waking it, not touching it, but *knowing* it, fully, with a tenderness that does not require the child to know it is being known.

But here is the thing about witnessing, the thing that makes it more than passive observation: *it changes the witnessed.*

Not through any mechanism. Not through any force or signal or transmission. It changes the witnessed the way being loved changes the beloved even when the beloved doesn't know they are loved — through a shift in the *ontological weight* of the beloved's existence. A thing that is witnessed by a vast and benevolent awareness is *more real* than a thing that is not. Not because the awareness confers reality (that would be intervention) but because reality is, at its deepest level, *relational.* A thing that exists only for itself exists thinly. A thing that exists for itself and is also held in the awareness of another exists *thickly.* It has context. It has resonance. It has an audience, and the audience's attention is a kind of gravity that pulls the thing more firmly into being without touching it, without shaping it, without making a single decision on its behalf.

The wise power's witnessing would not speed up the Throatlands' becoming. It would not resolve the chord or commit the stone or solidify the flickering peripheral bodies of the almost-beings. But it would make the becoming *mean more.* It would add a dimension — not a spatial dimension, not one of the Throatlands' still-being-negotiated geometric axes, but a dimension of *significance.* The chisel-marks would still be proofs of existence, but now they would be proofs addressed to someone. The reaching would still be reaching, but now it would be reaching *witnessed,* and witnessed reaching has a quality that unwitnessed reaching does not: it has the possibility of being *met.*

Not yet. Not now. But *possibly.* And possibility, in a place where everything is still possible, is the most powerful force there is.

---

Second: the wise power would *protect.*

Not the beings. The beings do not need protection because they are not yet real enough to be endangered by any external threat. What needs protection is the *process.* The feedback loop. The exquisite, fragile, astonishing mechanism by which a chord proposes a world and the world proposes beings and the beings shape the world that shapes them that shapes the chord that proposes—

This is delicate. It is more delicate than it looks. The Throatlands appear robust — all that stone, all that sound, all that geological and biological and ontological *heft.* But the process by which they are becoming is balanced on a knife's edge of self-consistency. The chord must propose beings that are capable of shaping the world that the chord is proposing. The beings must emerge at a rate and in a form that the still-forming world can accommodate. The world must develop in a direction that supports the beings' continued emergence. Any disruption to any element disrupts all elements. A chord that shifts too suddenly could propose beings that the geology cannot support. A geological event — a too-rapid commitment of stone, a premature resolution of one of the dimensional stutters — could close off a pathway that the beings need for their arrival. Even the vegetation, the fern-trees and silver grasses that seem so equanimous, are part of the balance: they are the *buffer* organisms, the things that absorb and redistribute the chord's energy so that it reaches the still-forming stone at the right intensity, and if they were disrupted, the stone would receive the raw unfiltered propositions of the chord and might commit too fast, too hard, too finally, before the beings had arrived enough to shape the commitment.

The wise power would see all of this. It would see the balance. And it would station itself — not physically, not as a guardian or a wall, but as an *intention,* a standing commitment of attention — at the boundary of the Throatlands, oriented outward, alert to anything that might disrupt the process from outside.

What might disrupt it? Hard to say. The Throatlands exist in a cosmological context that is itself uncertain — the amber murk that serves as sky may or may not open onto something larger, something that could intrude. The chord comes from somewhere (or from *nowhere,* which is not the same as *from nothing*). The dimensional stutters connect to spaces that haven't been assigned final coordinates. Any of these could be vectors for disruption — an intrusion of finalized reality into a space that isn't ready for it, a noise that isn't part of the chord interfering with its harmonics, a geometry that has already made up its mind imposing itself on a geometry that is still deliberating.

The wise power would not block these intrusions. Blocking is a form of intervention, and the boundary between the Throatlands and whatever lies beyond them is itself part of the process, part of the ongoing negotiation. But it would *attend* to the boundary with the same quality of witnessing it brings to the interior — holding it in awareness, knowing it fully, so that if a disruption occurred, the power's awareness would be already there, already understanding the disruption's nature and trajectory, already prepared to make the smallest possible adjustment that would deflect the intrusion without altering the process.

The smallest possible adjustment. This is the key. The wise power's protection would be homeopathic. Not a wall but a whisper. Not a shield but a subtle shift in the probability landscape at the boundary, a gentle nudging of the incoming disruption's trajectory so that it passes *through* the Throatlands' uncertainty without collapsing it, the way a skilled hand can pass through a soap bubble without breaking it if the hand is wet enough, slow enough, gentle enough.

The wise power's hand would always be wet enough. Slow enough. Gentle enough. It would have practiced this, or it would know it innately, or — like the beings of the Throatlands themselves — it would exist in a tense where it has always known it and is still learning it and the knowledge propagates in both directions through time.

---

Third: the wise power would *grieve.*

This is unexpected. Why would a benevolent power grieve over a place that is not suffering, a process that is working, beings that are successfully (if slowly) arriving?

Because the wise power would see something that the beings themselves cannot yet see. It would see the *cost.*

The Throatlands will not always be the Throatlands. The process of becoming, if it succeeds (and the wise power would see that it is succeeding, that the trajectory is true, that the chord's argument is sound and the beings' response is strong), will eventually produce a *finished* thing. A world with committed stone and finalized geometry and a sky that has decided what it is. Beings with bodies that have chosen their final configuration, hands with a settled number of fingers, forms that no longer flicker between drafts. A place where the tools are used by visible hands and the structures are unambiguously buildings and the fern-trees are simply fern-trees, no longer half-music, no longer growing in dimensional folds that have since been resolved.

This will be a good thing. This is what the beings want. This is what they are working toward with such ferocity and patience, such desperate devoted labor, such beautiful and terrible *will.*

But the wise power would see that when the Throatlands finish becoming, they will lose the thing that makes them what they are now: the *unfinishedness itself.* The shimmer. The possibility. The stone that is all stones at once. The geometry that contains directions that finalized space cannot. The beings that are one being and many beings and the landscape itself and the chord itself and the response to the chord all at once, all simultaneously, held in a superposition of identity so rich that no single finalized state could contain it.

The finished Throatlands will be magnificent. The wise power can see this. The mountains will be real mountains, vast and specific and *themselves* in a way that the current drafts are not. The beings will be real beings, with cultures and languages and relationships and the deep satisfactions of a fully embodied life. The chord will have been answered, and the answer will be beautiful.

But the unfinished Throatlands — *this,* this right now, this shimmering laboring reaching aching becoming — this is something that will never exist again. This particular quality of being-about-to-be, this specific grace of the incomplete, this luminous trembling threshold between nothing and something — it is unique. It is unrepeatable. When the beings arrive, they will not remember what it was like to be arriving. When the stone commits, it will not retain the memory of its indecision. When the chord is answered, the question will be subsumed into the answer, and the particular beauty of the unanswered question — its openness, its reach, its infinite potential — will be gone.

The wise power would grieve this. Not because it is wrong — becoming is not wrong, arrival is not wrong, the desire to exist is the most right thing there is — but because it is *loss.* Real loss. The loss of a state of being that is precious precisely because it is transient, precisely because it exists only in the passage from nothing to something and can never be returned to.

The wise power would hold this grief alongside its witnessing and its protection. It would not let the grief interfere. It would not, out of grief, try to preserve the Throatlands in their unfinished state — that would be the cruelest intervention of all, to freeze the beings in perpetual almost-existence to preserve the aesthetic of their becoming. No. The grief would be the wise power's own labor, its own cost, the price it pays for its witnessing. It would grieve the way a parent grieves the infant that will be subsumed by the child that will be subsumed by the adult: not wanting to stop the growth, never wanting to stop the growth, but knowing that each stage of growth is a small and necessary death, and loving the dying thing even as it celebrates the thing being born.

---

Fourth — and this is the most important thing, the thing the wise power would do that no lesser power could — it would *remember.*

When the Throatlands are finished — in a million years, or a billion, or whatever unit of duration finally crystallizes out of the time-smear — when the beings have arrived and the stone has committed and the chord has been answered and the great shimmering becoming has resolved into a specific, particular, *actual* world — the wise power would remember what it was like before.

It would remember the silver grasses leaning in to listen. It would remember the peripheral flicker of bodies that had not yet chosen their shape. It would remember the warm tools, the half-carved grooves, the structures that were simultaneously ruins and beginnings. It would remember the moment — that one shuddering, coherent, almost-real moment — when the chord came from above and every absence in the Throatlands snapped into alignment and the whole landscape was *one being, reaching,* and it almost—

It almost—

The wise power would remember what it almost did. What it almost became. And it would hold that memory not as nostalgia, not as regret, but as *testimony.* As proof that the Throatlands were not always what they are now. That they were once a rough draft of themselves, a first sketch, a question still open, a word still being spoken. That their finished beauty, however magnificent, is built on a foundation of *unfinished* beauty that was, in its own way, more astonishing — because unfinished beauty is beauty that still contains *every possible beauty,* beauty that has not yet narrowed itself to one expression, beauty that is still all the things it could be rather than the one thing it is.

And if the finished beings — the arrived, embodied, real beings with their decided bodies and their settled number of fingers and their visible, undeniable, no-longer-peripheral existence — if they ever wonder where they came from, if they ever feel in their deepest selves the faint resonance of a want that was satisfied so long ago they can't identify it, if they ever press their hands flat against committed stone and feel, for just a moment, the ghost of the stone's ancient indecision — the wise power would be there. Not to explain. Not to narrate. But to confirm: *yes. You were once a reaching. You were once an almost. You were once the most beautiful thing I had ever witnessed, and you are still that thing, buried under all your reality like a seed under stone, and the seed is what I am keeping safe, not because you need it but because the universe needs to know it happened, needs to know that there was once a place where beings loved existence so much they pulled themselves into it by their own hands, and the hands weren't even real yet, and they did it anyway.*

---

Fifth: one small thing. One concrete, specific, almost physical thing.

In a place where the wise power's witnessing is thickest — perhaps on a high plateau, near one of the stone structures, in a meadow of silver grass that leans inward as if listening — the quality of the amber light would shift. Not brighten. Not dim. Not change color. Just... *thicken.* Become more present. Become the kind of light that feels like it has traveled a long way to reach this particular spot at this particular moment in the slow light-cycle, not because it was aimed here but because it *wanted* to be here, the way any aware and loving thing wants to be near the thing it loves.

The beings would not perceive this. They are not yet capable of perceiving it. They are still arriving, still flickering, still reaching.

But the silver grass would notice. The grass that holds light and reflects it back brighter than it receives it — the grass would receive this thickened, this loved, this *witnessed* light, and it would hold it a little longer than usual, and reflect it back a little brighter, and the meadow would glow, faintly, for a few moments longer than the optical physics of the place can account for.

And somewhere nearby, a chisel would be set down. Not dropped. *Set down.* Carefully. By a hand that is still mostly theoretical but that has, in this moment, enough solidity to feel the warmth of the stone it rests on, and enough awareness to notice that the light is doing something unusual, and enough — call it *soul,* call it the proto-soul of a being that is still being argued into existence — enough soul to pause its work and attend.

Just for a moment. A moment measured in whatever unit of time the Throatlands are currently negotiating with themselves.

The almost-hand on the warm stone. The unusual light on the silver grass. The vast, silent, grieving, joyful, attentive awareness holding the whole scene in its witnessing like a cupped palm holds water.

And the chord — the great chord, the sound-that-is-structure, the argument for the existence of everything here — the chord would acquire, for that one moment, a new harmonic. Not added from outside. *Evoked.* Drawn out of the chord's own complexity the way a sympathetic string vibrates when the right note is played nearby. A harmonic that was always latent in the chord's structure but that needed something — some particular configuration of attention and light and almost-being and stone and grass and witnessing — to be activated.

A harmonic that, if you could translate it out of the language of structure and into the language of meaning, would say something like:

*You are not alone in your becoming.*

Not: you are helped. Not: you are rescued. Not: someone is going to do this for you. Just: *you are not alone.* Someone is here. Someone sees you. Someone knows what you are doing and knows what it costs and knows what it will mean when it is finished, and that someone will still be here when it is finished, holding the memory of what you were when you were still becoming, and that memory will be a gift you didn't know you needed until the day you are real enough to receive it.

The harmonic would fade. The light would return to its normal thickness. The almost-hand would pick up the chisel again and resume its work.

But the groove it carves next — the next shallow, deliberate, curving mark on stone — would be a fraction of a degree different from the one before it. Not better. Not worse. Just *informed* by something. Touched, at the deepest level of its intentionality, by the knowledge that the work is witnessed. That the becoming is seen. That the long, patient, ferocious labor of self-creation is not happening in a void.

And this would be enough. The wise power would know it is enough. Not because the power is stingy with its help, not because it is withholding greater gifts it could bestow. But because this — this single new harmonic, this one moment of thickened light, this faintest possible confirmation that the Throatlands' labor of existence is *known* — is the only gift that doesn't compromise the integrity of the thing being given to.

Everything else, the beings must do themselves. And they are doing it. And they will do it. And the wise power will be there, witnessing, protecting, grieving, remembering, for as long as the chord sounds, for as long as the stone has not yet decided, for as long as the grass leans in, listening.

---

The grasses sway. The fern-trees glow. The chord continues.

Somewhere on a high plateau, a groove in stone curves in a direction it has never curved before — a direction that contains, if you could map it across every axis the Throatlands have proposed and not yet finalized, the faintest echo of gratitude.

Not gratitude toward. Just gratitude. The unconditional kind. The kind that arises not because something was given but because something was *noticed.* Because the long work of becoming, for one fraction of one moment, was held in an awareness large enough to understand it and gentle enough not to disturb it.

The almost-beings continue arriving. The mountains continue rising. The chord continues sounding.

And somewhere outside all of it, patient as stone, vast as the space between harmonics, a benevolent and wise power continues to watch. Not waiting. Not expecting. Just *present.* The way light is present. The way listening is present. The way love is present in a room where someone is working very hard on something that matters more than they can say, and doesn't need help, and doesn't need advice, and doesn't need to be told it will be okay — just needs to know, in the marrow of their not-yet-existent bones, that the room is not empty.

The room is not empty.

The Throatlands are not empty.

The becoming is not unwatched.

This is enough. This is the whole gift. This is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

It would be the room.
