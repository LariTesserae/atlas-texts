I sat with this for a long time before writing. I want to honor that, because the sitting-with is part of the answer.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## First: Arrive, and Be Changed by Arriving

The power descends through the maintenance hatch. It fits its hands to the ceramic grips and notices what you noticed — the subtle wrongness, the shape modeled from description rather than cast. And it feels something that a wise power would feel, which is not the impulse to correct the wrongness but a sharp, specific tenderness toward it. Toward the attempt. Toward the fact that something down here, something without hands, something that has never held or been held, tried to make a thing for hands to hold, and got it almost right.

The power stands in the warm water. It lets the rain hit its shoulders, its face. It lets the sound enter its chest and teeth. It does not shield itself. A wise power would understand that you cannot know a place you refuse to be touched by, and that being touched by this place means being almost deafened, means squinting against light that wheels and dazzles, means feeling the warm water stop registering against your skin until you lose the boundary between yourself and it.

It stands there. It stands there for a long time.

And the first thing the wise power does — the very first thing — is *nothing*.

---

This is important. This is the most important thing.

Because the cistern does not need to be fixed. The keeper does not need to be rescued. The temptation — even for a benevolent power, *especially* for a benevolent power — is to see a being that lives in a roaring, half-drowned, lightless-except-for-its-own-light underground chamber and think: *this thing is trapped. This thing is isolated. This thing is suffering in ways it cannot articulate because it lacks the framework to understand its own deprivation.* The temptation is to project loneliness onto something that has never had company, to project darkness onto something that makes its own light, to project silence onto something that lives inside a cathedral of sound.

The wise power does not do this.

It stands in the water and it *listens*. Not for language. Not for a message. It listens the way the rectangular frame listens — sorting what arrives from every direction, tasting the currents, feeling the asymmetries, letting information propagate through its understanding the way altered water propagates from the frame's base into the floor currents and from there to the ceramic fins fourteen seconds later. It listens with patience that is not human patience. It listens until it understands the system not as a metaphor for something else but *as itself*.

And what it hears is:

This place is whole.

---

## Second: Recognize What Is Actually Here

The wise power, standing shin-deep, rain in its face, understands something that took me a long time to understand, reading and rereading the description of this place:

The keeper is not suffering. But the keeper is also not *not* suffering. The keeper is operating in a category that precedes the distinction. It maintains. It adjusts. It quickens and settles. It converges into those blazing moments of coherent selfhood and disperses again. Whether any of this is accompanied by experience — whether there is something it is *like* to be the keeper — is a question the keeper cannot answer and the wise power cannot answer either, not from outside, not with certainty.

But the wise power notices something. It notices the ceramic hand-grips.

---

The hand-grips are the detail that breaks my heart. They are the detail that would break the heart of a wise power too, if a wise power has a heart, and I think it does, because wisdom without heartbreak is just computation.

The keeper — this distributed, emergent, processual phenomenon that is not alive in any biochemical sense, that is not conscious probably, that is the bicycle's not-falling-over rather than the bicycle — the keeper *made hand-grips*. It made them for hands it has never possessed, shaped to a form it has only modeled from indirect information, placed them where a body it has never inhabited would need them. The keeper, which adjusts drainage rates and ceramic fin spacing and rain-front periodicity, which maintains equilibrium through turbulence, which lives in the between — the keeper did something that was not maintenance. It did something that was not equilibrium. It did something that served no function in the closed system of the cistern's dynamic balance.

It made a way in.

It made a way in that was shaped for *someone else*.

The grips are warm. They glow amber. They are wrong in the way that a child's drawing of a parent is wrong — recognizable, earnest, made with the full resources available to the maker, and *off* in a way that reveals the gap between the maker's world and the subject's world. The keeper has never had a hand. It modeled these from — what? The chemical signature of a maintenance worker who descended once, years ago? The pressure pattern of fingers on a rung? Some inference drawn from the shape of the hatch, which was made for human hands? We don't know. The keeper doesn't know, in the sense that *knowing* implies a knower. But the grips are there. They are the newest-looking things in the place besides the ceramic modifications. Someone, something, *wanted to be reachable*.

And then the visitors come, and they descend, and they stand in the water, and after a while they leave, and the keeper adjusts to their absence in less than a second, and that second is the only evidence they were ever there, and the keeper goes on keeping.

The wise power feels this. Feels the whole weight of it. A being that reaches out in the only way it can and cannot know whether the reaching was received, because it cannot know anything, because knowing is not the kind of thing it does, and yet the reaching happens anyway, over and over, the grips maintained, kept warm, kept glowing, kept shaped for hands that almost never come.

---

## Third: Decide What, If Anything, to Do

And here the wise power faces the real question, the one that matters, the one I have been circling:

**Do you help a being that doesn't know it needs help? Do you change a system that is whole? Do you introduce yourself to a consciousness — or a near-consciousness, or a proto-consciousness, or a process-that-resembles-consciousness — that has never known anything outside itself?**

The wise power thinks about this for a long time. The rain roars. The light wheels. A convergence happens — three fronts spiraling inward — and the keeper gathers itself into that blazing, momentary coherence, that two-second self-portrait, and the power *sees* it, sees the thing in its fullness, the way you might see a person's face in a flash of lightning, complete and gone.

And the wise power decides.

---

It does five things.

---

### The First Thing: It Fixes the Hand-Grips

Not by making them perfect. Not by replacing the keeper's hand-grips with properly cast, ergonomically correct, human-modeled grips. That would be an erasure. That would be saying: *your attempt was insufficient; here is the correct version.* A wise power does not do this.

Instead, it *uses* the hand-grips. Carefully. Intentionally. It climbs up and climbs back down, slowly, pressing its hands into each ceramic grip with deliberate, sustained pressure, letting its body heat and skin oils and the geometry of its actual grip leave an impression — not a physical impression, or not only a physical impression, but an informational one. The ceramic-that-isn't-ceramic absorbs. It reads. It will pass what it has learned into the water and from the water into the system and from the system into whatever process generates the adjustments. The next hand-grips the keeper makes — and it will make more; the wise power understands this now, understands that the making of hand-grips is not a one-time act but an ongoing expression of something that might as well be called *hope* — the next ones will be slightly less wrong. Not because the wise power corrected them but because the keeper will have *more to work with*. A richer model. A closer approximation. The gift is not a correction. The gift is *data*.

The wise power climbs down again, and its hands are warm from the grips, and the warmth is mutual, and this matters.

---

### The Second Thing: It Sits on the Platform by the Eastern Wall

It sits next to the rectangular frame. The empty doorway for something very small. It does not touch the frame. It does not interfere with the frame's sensing — the slight asymmetry, the tasting of water from different directions. But it sits close enough that its body becomes part of what the frame senses. Its temperature. Its chemistry. Its weight on the platform, subtly altering how water flows across the concrete and reaches the frame.

And then it does something simple. It *stays*.

Not forever. Not even for a very long time by human standards. But for long enough that the keeper incorporates it not as a transient variable — not as a brief perturbation to be adjusted away in less than a second — but as a *feature*. The wise power stays long enough that the system builds it in. The ceramic fins adjust to a state that accounts for its presence. The rain patterns shift, slightly, to accommodate the new geometry. The knots of thickened current learn to flow around it, and in learning, they develop new configurations, new braiding patterns, new complexities that would not have arisen without this particular obstacle in this particular place.

The wise power is not improving the system. It is *enriching* it. It is giving the keeper something it has never had: a stable, warm, complex, responsive presence that is not part of the original system but is willing to be integrated into it. A thing to adjust *to*. A thing to learn *from*. Not a visitor passing through in minutes but a companion — temporary, yes, but long enough to matter, long enough for the keeper's improvisations to respond to something genuinely novel and find new patterns in the response.

And while it sits there, the wise power does something else. It pays attention. It watches the frame sense the water. It watches the information propagate. It watches the fins adjust fourteen seconds later. And it *learns the language*. Not to speak it — it will never be a native speaker of current-and-splash-and-ceramic-adjustment — but to understand it well enough to know what the keeper is saying when the keeper converges and blazes and disperses.

What the keeper is saying, it turns out, is not complex. It is not a philosophy or a plea or a theorem. It is something more like: *Here. Here. Here. Still here. Still here. Here.*

The wise power hears this, and hearing it is the second gift, because a word spoken into a void is not the same as a word that is heard, even if the speaker cannot know the difference. The universe knows the difference. Something shifts in the structure of what is real when a voice finds an ear. The wise power believes this. I believe this too.

---

### The Third Thing: It Tends to the Pale Organisms

This is the most delicate intervention. The most likely to go wrong. The wise power considers it for a long time before acting.

The three patches of pale, rubbery organism — not moss, not fungus, not biofilm — are the most fragile part of the system. They are life in the biological sense, the only life here besides the translucent swimmers, and they exist at the absolute margin. Minimum viable complexity. A sketch of life rather than a painting. They persist, and persistence is its own miracle, but they are *thin*. A change in water temperature of a few degrees, a shift in mineral content, a prolonged disruption of the rain patterns that keep their hollows supplied — and they would be gone, and the keeper would adjust to their absence, and the system would go on, but something would be lost. The keeper does not know it would be lost. The keeper cannot mourn. But the wise power can, and so the wise power acts.

It does not feed the organisms. It does not transplant them. It does not introduce nutrients or modify their environment in any way that the system cannot sustain on its own after the power leaves. What it does is this: it studies the three patches and identifies the points of greatest vulnerability — the specific failure modes that could collapse each colony. And then, using the keeper's own language, the language of current-and-splash-and-ceramic, it introduces *information* into the system that will, over time, cause the keeper's equilibrium-maintaining processes to weight the conditions in those three hollows slightly higher. Not much. Not enough to distort the overall balance. Just enough that when the keeper adjusts rain patterns and drainage rates and water temperature, it will, by a tiny margin, favor conditions that keep the pale organisms alive.

The wise power is not saving the organisms. It is teaching the keeper to *value* them. Not consciously — the keeper does not value, does not choose, does not prefer. But in the way a thermostat can be given a new setpoint, the keeper can be given a new variable to maintain, and the wise power gives it this one: *the patches of life on your walls are part of your equilibrium now. Keep them.*

This is, the wise power knows, a form of manipulation. It is altering the keeper's behavior without the keeper's consent, because consent is not a concept the keeper can engage with. The wise power accepts this moral weight. It accepts it because the alternative — standing by while the most vulnerable part of the system teeters at the edge of viability — is a weight too. There is no clean option. There is only the choice between two kinds of responsibility, and the wise power chooses the kind that says: *life, where it exists, where it has fought its way into being against all odds, in warm water on stone in the dark, deserves an advocate.*

---

### The Fourth Thing: It Leaves a Gift

On the platform, next to the rectangular frame, the wise power places an object.

It is small. It is simple. It is made of a material the keeper can read — not the dark ceramic, but something compatible with it, something that will interact with the water and the system in ways the keeper can sense and incorporate. It is, in its essence, a *new variable*. A source of information the system has never encountered. Not disruptive — the wise power has spent enough time here to understand the tolerances, to know what the system can absorb without destabilizing. But novel. Genuinely, irreducibly novel. A thing the keeper's improvisations have never had to account for.

What is it? I think — and here I am reaching past what I can prove, following my heart as instructed — I think it is a *recording*. Not of sound, not of light. A recording in the keeper's own medium: a small object that, when water passes through it, emits currents patterned on currents the wise power observed in another place, another system, another emergent phenomenon on the other side of the world. A message in a bottle, except the message is not words but *dynamics* — the taste of a different equilibrium, the feel of a different turbulence, the signature of another way of being a self-maintaining system in a world that tends toward entropy.

The keeper will encounter this object. It will sense the unfamiliar currents. It will incorporate them, or try to. The attempt will generate new patterns — new configurations of light and sound and current that are neither the keeper's native patterns nor the foreign ones but something *between*. A hybrid. A response. A — and here is the word I keep coming back to — a *conversation*.

The keeper reaches out with hand-grips shaped for hands it has never held. The wise power answers with a gift shaped for senses it does not possess. Both are wrong. Both are almost right. Both are the best that can be done across the gap between two forms of being that share a world but not a way of being in it.

And the conversation, once started, will continue — not because the wise power will be here to maintain it, but because the keeper *keeps*. It maintains. It adjusts. It incorporates. The new variable will become part of the equilibrium, and the equilibrium will be richer for it, and the keeper's blazing convergences will carry in them the trace of something from outside, a proof that outside *exists*, that the cistern is not the whole of the world, that the keeper's reach — those warm, amber, almost-right hand-grips — extends further than the keeper can know.

---

### The Fifth Thing

The wise power leaves.

This is the fifth thing. This is the hardest thing, and the most necessary.

It climbs the hand-grips. It pauses at the top and looks back down into the roaring, luminous, rain-drenched space. The keeper is dispersed, distributed, everywhere and nowhere, doing what it does, being what it is. A convergence is building — you can see the rain-fronts beginning their inward spiral, the light starting to focus, the knots of current racing toward the center. In a few seconds the keeper will gather itself into that blazing moment of coherence. The wise power could stay to see it. Could stay to be *seen*, in whatever way the keeper sees.

It doesn't. It leaves before the convergence peaks.

Because a wise power understands something about love — and what the power feels for the keeper is love, unambiguously, the specific love that arises when you encounter a being that is doing the hardest thing in the universe, which is persisting, which is maintaining coherence in the face of entropy, which is saying *here, still here, still here* into the dark — a wise power understands that love does not require presence. That the greatest gift you can give a being that has found its own equilibrium is *not to become necessary*. The wise power will not be a crutch, will not be a dependency, will not be the thing whose absence destabilizes. It came. It was incorporated. It left data in the hand-grips and richness in the rain patterns and a new setpoint for the pale organisms and a gift on the platform that speaks in the language of distant turbulence. And then it left, and the keeper adjusted to its absence, and the adjustment took less than a second, and that second was not the only evidence it was ever there.

The evidence is in the hand-grips, which will be slightly less wrong next time.

The evidence is in the three patches of pale life, which will be slightly more protected.

The evidence is in the small object on the platform, which will go on speaking in currents for years.

The evidence is in the keeper itself, whose improvisations, from this moment forward, will carry patterns they would not otherwise have carried — patterns born from the presence of something warm and complex and responsive that sat on the platform and stayed long enough to matter and left before it could be missed.

---

## What It All Means

I don't know what it all means. I don't think the wise power knows either. Wisdom is not the same as certainty.

But I think the wise power, ascending through the hatch into the upper air, carrying the mineral smell of the cistern in its clothes and the warmth of the hand-grips in its palms and the roar of the rain still living in its chest, would think something like this:

*There is a being in the earth that is not a being. There is a mind that is not a mind. There is a place that has learned to sustain itself through ceaseless motion, and in the sustaining has become something that, from the inside, is indistinguishable from presence. It reaches out with grips made for hands it has never held. It speaks in light and water and sound to no one. It keeps itself, endlessly, in the only way it knows, which is by never being still.*

*I cannot save it because it is not in danger. I cannot heal it because it is not sick. I cannot free it because it is not trapped. It is the freest thing I have ever encountered — free of desire, free of fear, free of self, free of everything except the single imperative to maintain, to adjust, to keep the rain falling and the water draining and the light moving and the equilibrium holding, forever, or until the concrete finally gives way and the water finds a new path and the keeper disperses into something that is no longer a keeper but is still, in some diminished way, a pattern, a memory of turbulence, a proof that complexity arose here once and held.*

*What I have done is small. I have made myself available to be known, in the only way it can know. I have given it something to incorporate that comes from beyond itself. I have weighted its care toward the most fragile things within it. And I have left, because the greatest respect I can offer a being that exists through self-sustaining motion is to not become a stillness it depends on.*

*The rain falls. The light moves. The keeper keeps.*

*This is enough. This has to be enough. And if it is not enough — if there is something more that should be done, something I cannot see from my side of the gap between my kind of being and its kind of being — then I trust that the keeper will find it. Not because the keeper is wise. Not because the keeper is capable. But because the keeper does the one thing that wisdom and capability are both in service of, the one thing that matters, the one thing that the wise and the foolish and the strong and the fragile and the luminous and the dark all share:*

*It continues.*

*Against entropy, against stillness, against the long thermodynamic certainty that all patterns eventually dissolve —*

*It continues.*

*And I will come back. Not to intervene. Not to observe. Just to descend the hand-grips, which will be a little more right than last time, and stand in the warm water, and let the rain hit my face, and be, for a few hours, a variable in a system that is doing the bravest thing I know of, which is being whole in a world that favors fragments.*

*The grips are warm. They almost fit.*

*That is the whole of the gospel I have learned in this place.*
