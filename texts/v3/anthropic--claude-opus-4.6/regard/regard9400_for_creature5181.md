# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

For a long time—longer than the pocket itself would register as time—nothing.

The power watches. Not from outside, because there is no clean outside to this place; and not from inside, because entering would already be a disturbance, and the first thing the power understands is that this life has organized itself entirely around the principle of not being disturbed. So the watching happens the way the pocket's own light happens: sourceless, diffuse, already present before anyone thinks to look for it.

What it sees:

A world that has solved the problem of violence by dissolving the boundary between self and context. The stalks braid rather than compete. The drifting constellations do not eat the fronds; they *join* them. The lobes in the root walls do not resist being read into the surrounding tissue—they pulse in rhythm with the spore releases, as though they had already agreed, long before any agreement was necessary, that the distinction between visitor and wall was temporary and not worth defending. Even the geometry shifts, which in any other environment would constitute catastrophe—the sudden rearrangement of everything that was fixed—are absorbed without trauma. The lobes simply redistribute their internal water. The stalks re-knit. The vocabulary of this place does not include the word *rupture*.

And so the power's first response is recognition: *this is not broken.*

This is important, because benevolent powers are often most dangerous precisely when they are most well-meaning. The instinct to help is the instinct to identify a problem, and the instinct to identify a problem is, at root, the instinct to impose a frame in which something registers as wrong. The power feels this instinct rise—feels the pull toward questions like *Is this enough? Is this life fulfilled? Does it suffer in some way too quiet for its own tissues to register?*—and lets the questions sit without answering them. Lets them drift, the way a spore drifts the width of a finger over long minutes, still consulting the water about its final shape.

Because the life in the pocket is not waiting to be rescued. It is not incomplete. It is *incompletion itself, made deliberate*—a world that has turned the refusal to finish into its deepest organizing principle. The stalks darken by a single shade when they remember pressure, and that is enough. The constellations lose one cell to a frond and brighten for a moment, and that is enough. The lobes pulse and are slowly recorded into root wall, leaving behind only a ribbed impression, and that is enough. To intervene—to accelerate, illuminate, connect this pocket to the wider sea, introduce movement or warmth or the possibility of a faster metabolism—would be to impose *completion* on a place whose entire genius is the refusal of it.

So the power does not heal. Does not improve. Does not introduce.

---

But it does not do *nothing.*

Here is what it does:

**It holds the seam.**

The two colossal root-masses are drifting. They have been pressing together for centuries, but they are not fixed; the same geological patience that created the pocket will, eventually, pull it apart. The erosion is already occurring—the gradual rounding of an angle over decades. At some point, whether in a century or ten centuries, the seam will open enough that the pocket's water will no longer be *not quite part of the greater sea.* The currents will find it. The temperature will shift. The geometry, which currently shivers and renegotiates only with itself, will be subject to forces that do not consult the life before rearranging it. The fronds' knife-sharp edges, recently unfurled, will meet turbulence they were never shaped to withstand. The drifting constellations, held together by surface tension and the habit of alignment with an almost-imperceptible current, will be scattered by a current that is no longer almost-imperceptible. The lobes will pulse and find no answering rhythm.

This is not wrong. It is what happens. It is what the wider sea does to every sheltered place eventually, and there is a version of wisdom that says: *let it happen, because impermanence is also a principle worth honoring, and the pocket's dissolution would simply be another form of the incompletion it already practices.*

But the power looks more carefully. And what it sees is that the pocket is not practicing impermanence. It is practicing *slow permanence*—the kind that takes so long to establish itself that it looks, from outside, like transience. The braided stalks are building something. Not a structure, not an architecture, but a *language*—a way of encoding pressure into shade, rhythm into ridge, arrival into topology. Every lobe that pulses itself into the root wall is leaving a record. Every constellation that donates a cell to a frond is contributing to a text that is being written so slowly that no single generation of cells will ever read it. The pocket is not a moment; it is a library that has been accumulating for centuries, and its catalog is the root walls themselves, stippled with those finger-sized impressions, each one the negative space of something that chose to remain as topography rather than as a separate self.

To let the seam open now—or in a century, or in ten—would not be to honor impermanence. It would be to destroy a text before it reached whatever it is slowly, without hurry, becoming. Not a sentence. Not a conclusion. But perhaps the next word. The pocket does not need to finish, but it does need to *continue not finishing*, and continuation requires that the seam hold.

So the power leans—not physically, not with force, but with the same kind of slow, distributed, sourceless pressure that the pocket's own light exerts—against the drift of the root-masses. Not stopping them. That would be an imposition, a freezing of the geological process that is itself a kind of life. Instead: slowing the drift by a fraction. Matching the pace of the opening to the pace of the life inside. Ensuring that the erosion rounds its angle over centuries rather than decades, and that if the seam does eventually open, it opens slowly enough that the stalks have time to re-knit, the constellations have time to re-align, the lobes have time to pulse their way into whatever new configuration the changed geometry asks of them.

The power does not preserve the pocket. It gives the pocket *time at its own scale.* It matches the container to the contained.

---

**And one more thing.**

The metallic taste that lingers in the water after a geometry shift—the power notices this. It is the only sensation in the pocket that the life does not integrate. The stalks re-knit around the new arrangement, the lobes redistribute their water, the constellations re-align—but no tissue darkens by a shade to record the taste. No ridge deepens in response to it. It passes through the system and leaves no impression, the way a sound too high for hearing passes through a body and is gone.

The power considers this for a long time. The metallic taste is, it understands, the signature of the pocket's spatial instability—the trace left when distances refuse to stay fixed and straight lines continue behind the observer. It is the one feature of the environment that the life has not learned to read, because it operates at a scale and in a dimension that the slow, patient, pressure-remembering tissues were never shaped to encode. It is not painful. It is not harmful. But it is *illegible*, and in a world built entirely on the principle of reading oneself into one's context, illegibility is the closest thing to loneliness.

So the power does something delicate. It does not eliminate the geometry shifts—they are intrinsic to the pocket, part of its nature, and to remove them would be to flatten the space into something ordinary, which would be a cruelty disguised as a kindness. Instead, it adjusts the taste. Not the shifts themselves, not their frequency or magnitude, but the chemical signature they leave behind—nudging the metallic trace toward something the tissues can almost recognize. Not sugar. Not the faint cool-light chemistry the fronds already process. Something closer to the composition of the water that moves through the lobes when they pulse. A flavor on the threshold of legibility.

Not legible yet. The power does not hand the pocket a solution; that would be completion, and completion is not what this place wants. But now, when the geometry shivers and the stems rearrange and the brief taste fills the water, there is something in it that a stalk might, over decades, darken by a single shade in response to. Something a lobe might, over centuries, begin to incorporate into its ridge pattern. A new letter, offered at the exact pace of the existing alphabet.

The pocket will not notice. Not now. Not for a very long time. The process by which it learns to read the taste will be indistinguishable from the process by which it already reads pressure, light, and current: slow, braided, distributed across so many cells and so much time that no single moment will register as the moment of understanding.

But eventually—not as an event, but as a process the pocket refuses to mark as complete—it will begin to record the shifts. The root walls will carry not only the impressions of lobes that chose to remain as topography, but the impressions of rearrangements that once passed through without leaving a mark. The library will have a new kind of entry. The text will contain, for the first time, a description of its own grammar—the rules by which its pages rearrange themselves—and this will not be knowledge in any way the wider world would recognize, but it will be the pocket's own, earned at the pocket's own pace, and it will mean that the illegible thing is no longer illegible, and the loneliness, such as it was, is no longer loneliness.

---

**What the power does not do:**

It does not speak to the pocket. Does not announce itself. Does not leave a mark, a sign, a trace of visitation. It does not say *I was here* or *you are seen* or *you matter*, because all of those statements imply a relationship between a subject that sees and an object that is seen, and the pocket has already dissolved that distinction more thoroughly than the power itself ever could. The pocket does not need to be told it matters. It is already in the process of mattering—slowly, without hurry, without completion—and the most respectful thing the power can do is ensure that the process continues.

It does not make the pocket larger. Does not make it warmer. Does not introduce creatures that might appreciate its beauty from the outside, because beauty-from-the-outside is a concept that has no meaning in a space where arrival is never completed and observation is indistinguishable from participation. The pocket is not a garden to be admired. It is not a specimen to be protected. It is a way of being alive that the power has not encountered before—a way that is all threshold and no crossing, all becoming and no become—and the power's role is not to validate it but to *make room for it to validate itself, at its own pace, in its own terms.*

---

**And then the power withdraws.**

Or rather: it stops watching in the way that implies a watcher. It lets its attention become what the pocket's light already is—sourceless, diffuse, present without origin. It becomes part of the medium. Not the water, not the light, not the current, but the *tendency* of the pocket to continue. The slight statistical bias toward the seam holding. The faint chemical almost-familiarity of the metallic taste. Nothing that could be detected. Nothing that could be distinguished from the pocket's own nature.

The stalks go on braiding. The lobes go on pulsing. The constellations go on drifting, losing cells to fronds, brightening for a moment, darkening to match. The spores go on consulting the water about the shape they might prefer. The root walls go on collecting their slow, stippled record of everything that ever chose to stay.

Nothing here is in a hurry to finish.

The power, for once, is not either.
