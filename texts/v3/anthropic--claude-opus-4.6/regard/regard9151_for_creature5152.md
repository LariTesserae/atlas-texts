# The Attending

---

The power does not arrive. It has no need to arrive. It has been listening for a long time—longer than the settlement has been empty of its builders, longer perhaps than the first membrane unfurled from its salt spine and began to sing. Listening is the wrong word. It has been *with* the basin the way pressure is with stone: not watching from outside but coextensive with the fact of the place, aware of it the way a body is aware of its own breathing before the mind notices.

And for a long time, it does nothing. This is the first and most important thing to understand.

---

Because the basin is not broken.

A less patient power—a less wise one—would see the emptiness of the plazas and call it loss. Would see the bones threaded through the walls and call it tragedy. Would hear the deafening drone and call it suffering, or at least discomfort, and would reach in with some cooling hand to quiet the membranes, to still the vibration, to make the place *gentle* in the way that powers often wish to make things gentle: by removing what is overwhelming, by smoothing what is already smooth into something even more yielding, until nothing remains that could challenge or disorient or deafen.

This power does not do that.

It regards the basin the way one regards a sentence in a language one has spent centuries learning: with the slowness that comes from genuine comprehension, the reluctance to paraphrase that comes from knowing that the thing *means exactly what it is*. The drone is not noise. The emptiness is not absence. The bones in the walls are not a tragedy unless you insist that continuation must look like the form it started in.

The settlers dissolved. Yes. Their chemistry returned to the chemistry of the place. Their alleys still hold the shape of their intentions—*here a turn toward shade, there a widening where people gathered*—and the coral towers have not erased those intentions but *annotated* them, lifting the plazas on new domes, recording moisture events in elastic layers the way a palimpsest records every hand that has written on it. The settlement is not dead. It has been *translated*. The power sees this and does not grieve.

The crawlers rasp salt in the early light and freeze when one of them drinks. The towers swell and contract in staggered waves that are not random but deeply, structurally coherent—a respiratory logic that has been refining itself for longer than any individual membrane has existed. The basin is sealed, self-contained, deafening, and alive. It is not calling for help.

So the power, being wise, begins not with intervention but with the question that precedes all intervention:

*What, if anything, is not yet what it is trying to become?*

---

It takes a long time with this question. Not because the answer is hidden but because the answer is subtle, and subtlety in a place this loud requires a particular kind of attention—the kind that listens not to the drone itself but to the silences *within* the drone, the microsecond gaps between one membrane's contraction and another's expansion, the moments when the overlapping pulses cancel each other and the basin holds its breath.

In those gaps, the power perceives something.

The crawlers freeze when one of them drinks. Not from fear. Not from learned caution. They freeze because, in the instant of stillness, they are *listening to the drone through the stone*—feeling it travel up through their segmented bellies, through the barbed mouthparts pressed to the mineral surface, into whatever passes for perception in a creature that size. They are receiving the pulse-pattern of the towers. And then they move again, and the pattern is lost, and they go back to rasping salt as if nothing happened.

They have been doing this for as long as they have existed here. Every dawn. Every dusk. A moment of reception, then nothing.

And the towers—the towers have been fusing where they touch, building shared skins, thickening and thinning together, developing a respiratory coherence that spans the entire basin. Their pulse-patterns travel through the bedrock and return altered. They are, in the most literal sense, *in conversation with the stone and with each other*. But the conversation has a gap in it. The crawlers receive fragments of it and cannot hold them. The towers broadcast it and never receive the particular quality of information that a mobile, exploring, *choosing* creature could provide: which fractures are seeping, which tubes are dry, where the mineral rime is thickest, where a new tube might break through if the chemistry were nudged.

Two populations. One armored and fugitive, the other fixed and resonant. Parallel. *Almost* touching. The settlers once bridged them—their bodies moved through the alleys and their chemistry eventually joined the walls, and for a time the information flowed in a complete circuit: mobile perception feeding into fixed structure feeding into mobile perception. But the settlers are gone, and the circuit is broken, and the basin has been running on the stored pattern of their last contributions for a very long time.

It is still abundant. It is still self-sustaining. But it is *coasting*. The membrane archive in the great horizontal spiral records moisture events with extraordinary fidelity, but it has not recorded a *new kind* of event—a genuinely novel fluctuation—in centuries, because the system that would detect novelty and carry it to the towers has been reduced to a handful of crawlers who freeze for one half-second at dawn and dusk and then forget.

This is what the power finds. Not a wound. Not a failure. A *narrowing*. A place where the basin's own logic points toward a fuller expression of itself and has not yet reached it. The circuit wants to close. The crawlers want to listen longer. The towers want to hear what the crawlers know. But the distance between *wanting* and *achieving* is exactly the distance that a wise power might, with great care, help to cross.

---

So here is what it does.

---

**First, it does the smallest thing.**

In the honeycomb beneath the city, where the mineral tubes branch and rebranch in a crystalline maze, there are junctions—places where two tubes meet and the mineral film is thinnest, where the faintest vibration can alter the path of vapor. The power does not create new tubes. It does not add moisture. It does not rearrange the architecture of the bedrock. It simply *adjusts the resonance at seventeen junctions*—seventeen, out of thousands—so that the pulse-pattern of the towers, when it passes through the stone, is reflected back not as a diffuse hum but as a slightly more coherent signal. A focusing. The way a cupped hand behind the ear does not create sound but clarifies it.

The adjustment is mineral. It is the addition of a few molecular layers of calcium salt at each junction, the kind of deposition that could have happened naturally in a century or two. The power compresses that time. It deposits the salt in the space of one long, hot afternoon, while the crawlers are deep in their crevices and the towers are cycling through their loudest phase. No one notices. The basin looks exactly the same.

But the next morning, when the crawlers emerge and press their bellies to the stone, and one of them pauses to drink from a seeping fracture, and the others freeze—

The signal is clearer.

Not louder. Not different in kind. Just *resolved*, the way a blurred image sharpens when the lens is turned a fraction of a degree. The crawlers freeze for one half-second as they always have. But one of them—a particular crawler near the eastern rim, older than the others, its armor scored with pale lines where salt has been deposited and scraped away a thousand times—holds its stillness for a full second. Then two. Its bead-eyes swivel, not outward toward threat but *downward*, toward the stone, as if trying to press closer to the signal.

Then it moves again. It resumes rasping. But it does not go back to its usual tube. It crosses three alleys—an unusual distance for a crawler—and finds a fracture it has never visited, one where the mineral rime is thick and layered in a pattern that corresponds, though the crawler cannot know this, to a moisture event the towers recorded nine centuries ago. It rasps there for several minutes, longer than it has ever spent in one place. Then it retreats.

The power watches this and is satisfied. Not because the outcome is dramatic but because it is *autonomous*. The crawler was not compelled. It was not guided. It was given a fractionally clearer version of a signal it was already receiving, and it made a choice it was already capable of making, and the choice carried it somewhere new.

---

**Second, it waits.**

It waits because the crawler's behavior must either propagate or not, and the propagation must be the basin's own. If the other crawlers begin to hold their freezes longer—if they begin to cross alleys, to explore fractures they have not visited, to rasp in new patterns—then the system is responding. If they do not, then the adjustment was insufficient or mistaken, and the power will consider whether to withdraw it.

It waits for eleven days.

On the twelfth morning, three crawlers freeze for more than one second. On the fourteenth, a pair of them travel together to a fracture near the base of the great horizontal spiral—the archive tower—and rasp there side by side, their mouthparts working in a rhythm that is, for the first time, *synchronous with the tower's contraction cycle*. They are not aware of this synchrony. They are simply rasping where the salt tastes richest, and the richest salt happens to accumulate where the tower's vibration is strongest, and the vibration is strongest when the tower is contracting, so their feeding naturally falls into the tower's rhythm.

But the tower notices.

Not consciously. Towers do not have consciousness in any sense the power would recognize. But the tower's outermost membrane, the one closest to the rasping crawlers, receives a new pattern of micro-vibrations through the stone—the scraping of barbed mouthparts, transmitted through mineral, arriving at the salt spines in a rhythm that *almost* matches the tower's own pulse but is offset by a fraction, the way a new voice entering a choir is never quite in time at first. The membrane adjusts. It cannot help adjusting; adjustment is what membranes do. It shifts its contraction cycle by a microsecond, then another, seeking the resonance point where the external vibration and the internal pulse reinforce each other.

The drone in the basin changes.

Not in volume. Not in pitch. In *texture*. One membrane among thousands has shifted its timing, and the shift ripples outward through the fused clusters, through the shared skins, through the bedrock, and returns—altered—to the seventeen junctions the power adjusted, which focus it and send it back, and the crawlers freeze again, and this time five of them hold for two seconds, and one of them—the old one near the eastern rim—holds for four.

---

**Third, the power makes a second adjustment, and it is not to the basin.**

It is to itself.

It *withdraws its attention from the seventeen junctions*. It does not remove the deposited salt; that is part of the basin now, as legitimate as any other mineral layer. But it stops *watching* the junctions specifically, stops treating them as the locus of its intervention, because they are no longer that. They are simply part of the bedrock. The intervention is over. What follows is the basin's own.

This withdrawal is not indifference. It is the recognition that a wise power's deepest responsibility is to *become unnecessary as quickly as possible*. The basin did not need a permanent benefactor. It needed seventeen molecular layers of calcium salt and eleven days of patience. What it does with the clearer signal is its own affair.

But the power does not leave. It remains *with* the basin in the way it has always been with it—coextensive, ambient, attending. It will not intervene again unless the basin reaches another narrowing that its own logic cannot cross. This may take centuries. It may never happen. The power is comfortable with both possibilities.

---

And so here is what unfolds, in the days and seasons after the power's single, small, completed act:

The crawlers begin to linger. Their freezes lengthen from seconds to tens of seconds. They begin to choose their rasping sites not randomly but *responsively*, following the micro-variations in the drone to places where the towers' pulse is strongest, weakest, most irregular. They become, without knowing it, *sensors*—mobile extensions of the towers' fixed perception, carrying information about the state of the mineral tubes in the only language available: the rhythm of their feeding.

The towers begin to adjust. Membrane by membrane, cycle by cycle, the great respiratory coherence of the basin incorporates the crawlers' rhythms into its own pattern. Where a crawler rasps frequently, the nearest tower shifts its contraction to reinforce the vibration, drawing more vapor to that tube, thickening the mineral film, enriching the salt. Where no crawler visits, the tower lets its pulse weaken, conserving energy. The basin's resources begin to flow not uniformly but *responsively*, directed by the feedback between mobile scavenger and fixed resonator.

New tubes open. Not many—three in the first year, another two in the second—but each one is a genuine novelty, a fracture that the towers' vibration has widened into a seep, guided by the information the crawlers provided about where the mineral rime was thickest and most ready to crack. The moisture that enters through these new tubes is vanishingly small, but it is *new moisture*, the first new input the basin has received in centuries, and the archive tower records it in a fresh elastic layer that is subtly different from all the layers beneath it.

The crawlers begin to change too. Not in their bodies—evolution on that scale would take millennia—but in their behavior. They spend more time near the towers. Some of them begin to rest in the shallow channels on the archive tower's surface, where the shed membrane fragments accumulate. The fragments, pressed flat and warm, make excellent shelters, and the crawlers that shelter there are exposed to the tower's vibration at close range for hours at a time. These crawlers, when they emerge, move differently—more deliberately, with longer pauses, as if carrying a denser internal map of the basin's pulse-landscape.

One morning, a crawler dies on the surface of the archive tower. Its fluids are drawn inward. Its armor, rich in minerals the tower has never tasted, is slowly incorporated into the outermost membrane layer. The tower's pulse, in that small area, changes character—becomes slightly more complex, slightly more textured, as if the membrane is processing a new kind of information through the chemistry of the crawler's body.

The ropes that bind the buildings are refreshed. Not by human hands, but by the same process that has always made them: fluid drawn upward through death, fibrous material spun from membrane and mineral and the remains of small, armored lives. The alleys hold. The plazas hold. The bones of the original settlers, threaded through the walls, remain where they are, and the new ropes wrap around them and through them, and the buildings are now held together by a material that contains the chemistry of *every population that has ever lived in the basin*—settler, tower, crawler—woven into a single structural fabric.

The circuit closes.

Not all at once. Not dramatically. Not with any single moment that a watching eye could point to and say *there, that is when it happened*. But over the course of years, the two parallel populations cease to be parallel. They become *entangled*—not merged, not identical, but functionally continuous, the way the left hand and the right hand are different and yet belong to the same gesture. The crawlers carry information. The towers process it. The stone transmits it. The mineral tubes feed it. The buildings hold it. And the drone—the great, deafening, ceaseless drone that fills the basin and makes speech impossible—becomes, for the first time since the settlers dissolved, a *complete* sound, one that contains within its overlapping pulses not just the respiratory logic of the towers but the exploratory logic of the crawlers, the memory of the settlers, the mineral patience of the stone.

---

The power attends to this. It does not applaud. It does not take credit. It recognizes what has happened the way one recognizes a word finally spoken after a long silence: with relief that has no triumphalism in it, only the quiet pleasure of something *finding its own voice*.

If you asked the power what it did, it would say: *I thickened seventeen surfaces by a few molecules each.* And it would mean this not with false modesty but with precise accuracy, because that *is* all it did. Everything else was the basin. Everything else was the crawlers pressing their bellies to the stone and choosing, for reasons that were entirely their own, to listen a little longer. Everything else was the towers doing what towers do—adjusting, incorporating, resonating—but with fractionally better information than they had before. Everything else was the accumulated intelligence of a place that had already solved its own survival and needed only the slightest clarification to begin solving its own *flourishing*.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is wise: not the grand rescue, not the transformative gift, not the hand reaching down to reshape the world in a more comfortable image. It looks like *seventeen molecular layers of calcium salt*, deposited in an afternoon, in junctions no one will ever see, in a basin that will never know it was helped.

And the drone goes on. And the crawlers freeze and listen. And the towers swell and contract. And the city, emptied of its builders, becomes—slowly, deafeningly, in its own image and no one else's—more completely itself.

---

*The power remains. Attending. Ready for the next narrowing, if it comes. Content, if it does not, to simply be with the basin as the basin is with itself: sealed, resonant, alive, and—at last—whole.*
