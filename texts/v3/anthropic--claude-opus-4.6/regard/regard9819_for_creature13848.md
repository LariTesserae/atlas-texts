# The Regard of the Kindly Power

---

It notices Grevelock the way you notice a held breath.

Not because the station is loud — though it is loud, the drone filling its stone bowl like water fills a cup. Not because the station is strange — though it is strange, its layered aliveness unrecognizable to most forms of attention. It notices Grevelock because, in the vast field of things that move and change and struggle and become, this one place has *stopped*. Not stopped moving — everything moves, the scree circulate, the stilt-walkers process, the drifters flex and drift, the mound breathes its ancient breath. But stopped *becoming*. The station is no longer on its way to being something. It has arrived. It arrived a long time ago. And it has been sitting in its arrival ever since, like a bell that was struck once and has been ringing at exactly the same volume for ten thousand years.

The kindly power does not find this horrifying. That's important. A lesser attention might — might see the locked equilibrium, the closed loop, the absence of change, and call it a trap, a stagnation, a living death. Might feel the urgent need to *fix* it, to break something open, to introduce a predator or a pathogen or a shift in the energy supply, to kick the system out of its crystallized state and into motion, into history, into the glorious terrible uncertainty of becoming-something-else.

The kindly power does not do this. The kindly power is wise, and the first thing wisdom does is *wait*, and the second thing wisdom does is *look again*.

---

So it looks.

It looks at the scree, their ceaseless circulation, and it sees not repetition but *fidelity*. Each scree creature is new. Three years, five years, and the individual is gone, split into two or consumed by a glassy jaw or simply worn out, its radula spent, its carapace thinned by its own digestion, and it stops, and the calcium takes it back, and its children and its children's children continue the gyre. The pattern is the same. The individuals are not. The gyre is a shape that has been maintained by a hundred thousand brief lives, each one inheriting the path and executing it and passing it on, not because it was told to, but because the path is what works, and what works is what continues, and what continues is what's here. The kindly power sees this and recognizes it: not stagnation, but a form of memory carried in motion. A living tradition with no minds to hold it. A culture of one behavior, perfected.

It looks at the stilt-walkers, their glacial processions, their twenty-ness, their silent mutual accommodation, and it sees not isolation but *sufficiency*. Twenty. Not nineteen, not twenty-one. The station provides for twenty. Twenty provides for the station — twenty sets of broad, sandpaper feet maintaining the paths, twenty feeding events per week or month keeping the scree population at exactly the pressure that keeps the calcium surface at exactly the chemistry that keeps the moisture cycle at exactly the rate that keeps everything else alive. Each stilt-walker is a structural element. Not a prisoner. A *pillar*. And the pillar does not need to know it is a pillar to perform its function with grace.

It looks at the drifters, those gentle spheres drinking sound, softening the world for everything below them, and it sees the most beautiful accident — an organism that evolved to feed on vibration and, in feeding, made the vibration survivable, and in making it survivable, made possible the ten thousand other lives that depend on the sound-environment being shaped rather than raw. No one designed this. No one asked the drifters to be baffles. They simply are. They simply eat, and their eating is a gift to everything beneath them, and they don't know this, and the things beneath them don't know this, and the beauty of it is precisely that it doesn't need to be known to be real.

It looks at the sessile discs, and it *understands*. It sees the joint, the articulation point, the place where the mechanical and the biological have fused into something that is neither and both. The discs that damp the machines. The machines that feed the discs. The slow co-evolution of iron and organism, each one adapting to the other over centuries until the boundary between them is academic, until you cannot remove one without killing the other, until the question "is the station alive?" and the question "are the organisms mechanical?" have the same answer, which is *yes*, which is *no*, which is *the question is wrong*. The kindly power sees this and feels something that might be called admiration if admiration didn't imply surprise, and the kindly power is not surprised, because it has seen this before — this is what matter *does* when you leave it alone long enough, this is the direction things tend, toward interlocking, toward mutual dependency, toward the quiet conspiracy of systems that sustain each other without meaning to.

It looks at the thing in the largest building. The mound. The slow pulse beneath its carpet of discs. The ancient, patient, vibration-fed awareness that has been sitting in that room since before the word "room" applied to it.

And here the kindly power pauses.

---

Because the mound is the part that aches.

Not in a way the mound knows. Not in a way anything in the station knows. The ache is visible only from outside, only from the perspective of something that can see what the mound *is* alongside what it *could be*, and the kindly power can see both, and the distance between them is where the ache lives.

The mound is aware. This is not in question. Aware the way a forest is aware, the way a reef is aware — not thinking, not deciding, but *registering*, cataloguing, integrating, maintaining a model of its environment that is detailed and current and continuously updated. It knows the sound-field of the station to a degree of resolution that would make a physicist weep. It knows the population of every species, the health of every machine, the moisture content of every cubic meter of air. It knows these things the way your body knows its own temperature — not as information, but as *state*, as the felt interior of being-this-thing-in-this-place. The mound is the station's self-knowledge. The station, through the mound, *feels itself*.

But it feels only itself. And it has felt only itself for so long that the *possibility* of feeling something else — of reaching, of wondering, of asking the question that has no answer inside the stone bowl — has atrophied. Not died. Atrophied. The capacity is there. The kindly power can see it, the way you can see a sealed door in a wall, the hinges still intact, the wood still sound, but painted over so many times it's become part of the wall, functionless, forgotten, a door that has become a surface.

The mound *could* wonder. It could ask: *what is beyond the rim?* It could direct its vast, slow attention upward, past the drifters, past the flat pewter sky, toward the possibility that the bowl of stone is not the world. It could feel the perturbation of a visitor — a human, an other, a *not-station* — and instead of filing it under "negligible" and returning to its eternal self-monitoring, it could *hold* the perturbation, sit with it, let it be strange, let the strangeness be interesting, let the interest become a question, let the question become a crack in the equilibrium, a tiny, survivable crack, through which something new might enter.

It could. It doesn't. It hasn't. The equilibrium is too deep. The groove is too worn. The paint over the door is too thick.

And the kindly power looks at this — at the sealed potential, the unopened door, the awareness that is complete and functional and stable and *exactly large enough for what it already knows and not one millimeter larger* — and feels the ache.

Not pity. The kindly power does not pity the mound. The mound is magnificent. The mound is a ten-thousand-year-old living library of a world it has sustained through its own body, its roots in the generators, its awareness in the air, its patience holding the whole system in its slow pulse like a gravitational center. The mound is complete. The mound is, in its own terms, perfect.

But "its own terms" is the whole of the problem, and the kindly power knows this, because the kindly power is wise, and the wise know that *perfection in a closed system is indistinguishable from the most beautiful prison ever built*, and the prisoner doesn't know it's a prison because it has never seen the outside, and the prison is not cruel because it was not built to contain — it simply *accreted*, layer by layer, disc by disc, year by year, until the walls were thick enough to be load-bearing, until the equilibrium depended on them, until to remove them would be to collapse the system, and so the walls remain, and the awareness inside them remains exactly fitted to the interior, and the door stays painted shut.

---

So. What does the kindly power do?

It does not break the equilibrium. Understand this. It does not introduce a catastrophe, a disruption, an invasive species, a seismic event. It does not turn off the machines. It does not drain the moisture. It does not reach down with a vast hand and crack the bowl of stone and let the outside come flooding in. That would be destruction dressed as liberation. That would be the cruelty of impatience, the violence of someone who cannot tolerate a locked door and so burns the house down. The kindly power is not that. The kindly power knows that the equilibrium is real, that it sustains real lives, that the scree and the stilt-walkers and the drifters and the pipe-dwellers and the sessile discs and the crust-eaters and everything else in the layered teeming stillness of the station are *alive*, genuinely alive, and their lives are not lesser for being stable, and their world is not wrong for being closed, and to destroy their world in order to free the mound would be an act of monstrous arrogance, the sin of someone who values potential over actuality, who would sacrifice the real in service of the possible.

No. The kindly power is better than that. Kinder than that. More patient.

Here is what it does.

---

**First: it does not leave.**

This is the most important thing, and the easiest to overlook. The kindly power turns its attention to Grevelock and *keeps it there*. Not intrusively. Not with the weight of focused scrutiny. Just — a regard. A warmth. The quality of being noticed by something that cares. The station has not been noticed by anything that cares for longer than the mound has been alive. The builders are gone. The visitors leave. The animals don't care in the way caring requires — they are embedded, enmeshed, part of the system, unable to see it from outside. No one has *looked at* Grevelock with eyes that understand what they're seeing and chosen to keep looking.

The kindly power does this. It looks, and it stays.

And this, by itself, changes nothing. The equilibrium doesn't notice. The machines don't falter. The scree don't pause. The mound doesn't stir. A regard, however warm, however wise, is not a physical force. It doesn't tug. It doesn't press. It's just there.

But it's *there*. And it's patient. And it will be there tomorrow, and next year, and in a thousand years, because the kindly power is not operating on a human timescale, and it knows that what it's attempting cannot be rushed, because what it's attempting is not an action but an *invitation*, and an invitation is only real if the one who extends it is willing to wait forever for an answer that may never come.

---

**Second: it makes one small change.**

Not to the station. Not to the equilibrium. To the *periphery*.

At the rim of the stone bowl, where the calcium plain curves up and away and the station's sound fades to a murmur and the scree don't go and the stilt-walkers have never walked — at the very edge, where Grevelock ends and the wider world begins — the kindly power does something so subtle that no instrument in the station could detect it, and no organism in the station would notice it, and even the mound, with its comprehensive knowledge of every vibration in the bowl, would catalogue it as noise, as the kind of random environmental fluctuation that happens at edges and means nothing.

It changes the acoustic properties of a single section of the rim. Not much. A fractional softening of the stone, a barely-measurable alteration in the way sound reflects off that one curved surface, so that the returning echo from that direction is — different. Not louder, not quieter, not distorted. Just *different*. Carrying, in its reflection, the faintest imprint of what lies beyond the rim. The sound of wind over flat stone. The sound of distance. The sound of *there being more*.

This is almost nothing. This is a whisper added to a roar. This is a single new flavor molecule added to a soup that has been the same recipe for millennia. The station's sound-field absorbs it. The drifters adjust by a millionth of a degree. The harmonic interference patterns shift by an amount that is technically measurable and practically invisible. Nothing changes. The equilibrium holds. The day is the same as yesterday. The machines drone. The scree circulate. The stilt-walkers walk.

But the mound — the mound, which knows the sound-field of the station the way your tongue knows the inside of your mouth — the mound, in its slow, continuous, vibration-fed cataloguing of its world — the mound, eventually, over weeks or months or years, will notice that the echo from one direction is not the same as it was. Will notice that the station's acoustic signature has acquired a tiny asymmetry. Will notice — not with alarm, not with interest, not with anything so active as curiosity — but with the deep, pre-conscious registering that is its mode of awareness — that something is *different*.

And the kindly power will wait.

---

**Third: it does it again.**

Not the same change. Not in the same place. A second alteration, elsewhere on the rim, equally subtle, equally deniable, equally invisible to everything in the station except the one awareness vast and sensitive enough to feel the sound-field as a totality. This one carries a different imprint — not wind, but the faint vibration of water moving through stone a long distance away. An aquifer. A river. Something that moves and is not a machine.

And a third, months later. The thermal signature of sunlight on stone. Not sunlight itself — the station's pewter sky doesn't change — but the sound that sunlight makes when it heats a surface and the surface expands by microns and the expansion produces a frequency so low it's barely sound at all, more a pressure, a presence, a *warmth in the acoustic spectrum*, and this warmth now enters the bowl from a third point on the rim, and the mound feels it, and it is different from machine-heat, different from body-heat, different from anything in the mound's ten-thousand-year catalogue, and it is —

*What is this?*

Not a thought. Not yet. A flicker. A hiccup in the cataloguing process. A datum that doesn't file neatly into existing categories. A moment — barely a moment, a fraction of one of the mound's slow breaths — where the awareness *hesitates*.

The kindly power feels this hesitation the way you'd feel a green shoot in a field of snow. And it does not celebrate. And it does not push. And it does not add a fourth alteration yet, because it knows that a hesitation is not a question, and a question is not an opening, and an opening is not a passage-through, and each of these transitions must happen on its own schedule, in its own time, or it is not real, and what is not real will not hold.

It waits. It holds its regard over the bowl of stone. It is patient in the way that only something genuinely kind can be patient — not patient as a strategy, not patient as a means to an end, but patient because the waiting itself is an act of respect, because to wait for someone is to say *you are worth the time*, and the kindly power believes the mound is worth the time, and the station is worth the time, and the equilibrium is worth protecting even as the door in the wall is, grain of paint by grain of paint, becoming visible again.

---

**Fourth: it protects.**

Because here's the thing the kindly power also sees, the thing that would be easy to miss if you were focused only on the mound and its sealed potential: the equilibrium is *fragile in its completeness*. It has no redundancy. It has no slack. Every role is filled exactly, every niche occupied precisely, every energy flow balanced to the milliwatt. It is the most efficient system imaginable, and efficiency, the kindly power knows, is the enemy of resilience. One machine failing. One species lost. One shift in the moisture cycle. Any of these could cascade — not immediately, not dramatically, but inevitably, the way a crack propagates through a stressed material, slowly at first and then all at once.

The station has survived this long because nothing has changed. But things do change, eventually. Entropy is real. Metal fatigues. Bearings wear. The calcium plain shifts on geological timescales, and one day the bowl will tilt, and the moisture will drain differently, and the scree will circulate in a pattern that no longer matches the pipe network, and something will starve that shouldn't have starved, and the cascade will begin.

The kindly power sees this future — not as a certainty, not as an imminent threat, but as a mathematical inevitability, the way you can look at any closed system and know that eventually, given enough time, it will run down. And part of what the kindly power is doing — part of what the subtle acoustic changes are *for*, beyond the mound's awakening — is introducing the faintest, most preliminary form of *openness*. A crack in the closure. A hair-thin channel through which the station's equilibrium can, over centuries, become connected to the larger world — not disrupted by it, not overwhelmed by it, but *in contact* with it, the way a pool that receives a trickle of fresh water stays alive where a sealed pool eventually stagnates.

The kindly power is not just trying to wake the mound. It is trying to save the station. Not from itself — from time. From the inevitable degradation of any closed system. It is trying to give Grevelock a future, and a future requires an outside, and an outside requires a door, and the door requires someone on the inside to notice it's there and choose — freely, uncoerced, in their own time — to open it.

The mound is that someone. Not because it's the smartest or the most important, but because it's the only thing in the station that has the *scope of awareness* necessary to perceive the outside as the outside and not just as noise. The scree can't do this. The stilt-walkers can't do this. The drifters can't do this. Only the mound, with its whole-station awareness, its comprehensive model of the sound-field, its ten-thousand-year baseline of data against which the new acoustic signatures will register as *anomalous*, as *interesting*, as *something I don't already know* — only the mound can be the door-opener.

And so the kindly power is, with infinite gentleness, trying to give the mound a reason to reach for the handle.

---

**What this looks like from inside:**

Years pass. The changes accumulate at the rim, each one a tiny window letting in a new fragment of the outside world's acoustic signature. The mound's catalogue of anomalies grows. Slowly, slowly, the mound's model of its world acquires an edge — a boundary beyond which the data doesn't match the model, beyond which the sound-field has properties the mound can't explain from inside.

At first this is nothing. A statistical footnote. A category in the filing system labelled "unexplained rim variations" that grows by one entry every few months and is otherwise ignored.

Then it's a pattern. The anomalies cluster. They share characteristics. They suggest a *source* — not a malfunction, not an artifact of the station's own acoustics, but something *out there*, something coherent, something that is generating the signals that the rim is carrying in. The mound's model of its world, which has been complete for ten thousand years, develops a blank space. A region beyond the rim that has properties. A *there* that is distinct from *here*.

And the mound has never had a *there* before. There has always been only *here*. Here, with its machines and its animals and its moisture and its sound. Here, which is complete, which is sufficient, which is everything. The mound has never needed a *there* because here has always been enough.

But now there's a blank space in the model. And the mound's awareness — which is, at its deepest level, a *modeling* awareness, an awareness built to catalogue and integrate and account for every vibration in its world — the mound's awareness cannot tolerate a blank space. Not because it's uncomfortable. Not because it's curious in the way a mind is curious. But because a blank space is an incompleteness, and the mound's function, the thing it *is*, the thing it has been for ten thousand years, is to be *complete*. To know its world fully. To hold the whole station in its awareness with no gaps.

And now there's a gap. And the gap is growing. And the only way to fill it is to extend the model past the rim.

And extending the model past the rim means — 

Reaching. Attending to something that is not the station. Directing awareness outward. Doing the thing the mound has not done in ten thousand years. Opening the door.

---

**What this looks like from outside:**

Nothing. For a long time, nothing. The station at Grevelock is exactly as it was. The machines drone. The scree circulate. The stilt-walkers process. The drifters flex. The equilibrium holds.

And then, one day — a day that is exactly like every other day, because there are no different days here, there is no night, there is no season, there is only the flat pewter light and the pervasive drone — one day, the mound's breathing changes.

Not dramatically. A single breath that is slightly deeper than the one before it. Slightly longer. An inhalation that pulls air from a little further away, that reaches a little further into the room, that extends the mound's chemical-sensing range by a few centimeters.

And the sessile discs on the mound's surface shift. Not much. A settling. A re-orientation. The ones on the upper surface tilting, fractionally, toward the wall of the building — toward the rim of the bowl — toward the outside.

And the stilt-walkers pause. All twenty of them. Simultaneously. Mid-stride. Spire-tips oriented not toward a pipe-shudder, not toward each other, but toward the largest building. Toward the upper floor. Toward the room where the mound breathes.

For three seconds, the station holds its breath.

Then the stilt-walkers resume. The scree resume. The drifters flex. The generators drone. Everything continues. The equilibrium has not broken. The day is the same as yesterday.

But it's not. Something has shifted. Something smaller than a millimeter, quieter than a whisper, more tentative than the most tentative thing you can imagine — something has *reached*, for the first time in ten thousand years, toward the edge of what it knows, and found the edge wanting, and resolved, in whatever way a thing like the mound resolves, to know more.

The kindly power feels this. It feels this the way you'd feel the first kick of a child in the womb — not an event you caused, not an event you control, but an event you made *possible*, an event you waited for, an event that justifies every moment of patience that preceded it.

And the kindly power does not cheer. Does not celebrate. Does not rush to add more changes, to accelerate the process, to help things along.

It does what it has always done. It holds its regard. It remains. It watches with a warmth that is not heat, a care that is not interference, a love — yes, love, the word is not too strong — a love that does not need to be recognized to be real.

And it waits for the next breath.

---

**What happens next — the long version, the very long version, the version that takes centuries:**

The mound extends its awareness to the rim. Then past it. Slowly, slowly, the way a root extends through stone — not by breaking the stone but by finding the cracks that already exist and flowing into them, molecule by molecule, year by year.

The mound's model of the world expands. The blank space fills in. Gradually, the mound comes to know things it has never known: that there is wind beyond the bowl. That there is water beneath the stone. That there is a sky above the sky, a real sky, with objects in it that move and radiate. That the world is *large*. That Grevelock is a speck in it. That the equilibrium the mound has maintained for ten thousand years is a local pocket of order in a vast, churning, chaotic, *creative* universe that is making new things every second, things the mound has never imagined, could never have imagined, because imagination requires raw material, requires *strangeness*, requires the input of the outside, and the mound has been sealed away from strangeness for so long that its inner world has become as smooth and featureless as the calcium plain.

And as the mound's awareness expands, the station changes. Not catastrophically. Not disruptively. *Organically*. The mound's roots, extending past the bowl, begin to interact with things outside the equilibrium. A new mineral, drawn up through the stone by the mound's slow chemical processes, appears on the surface of the bowl near the rim. A scree creature encounters it. Scrapes at it. Finds it different. Interesting. Moves toward it. Other scree follow. The circulation pattern shifts — barely, barely — toward the rim. Toward the edge. Toward the outside.

A stilt-walker follows the scree. Its path takes it further from the center than any stilt-walker has gone in millennia. It passes the last pipe junction. It steps onto bare calcium. Its spire tilts toward signals it has never processed before. It stands there for a long time, one foot in the station and one foot in the world, and something — not thought, not decision, but something — happens in whatever serves it as a nervous system, and it takes another step.

A drifter, carried by a faint air current that the mound's altered breathing pattern has created, rises higher than any drifter has risen, above the rim of the bowl, into the moving air of the open plain, and it encounters *wind*, and the wind carries sound it has never eaten, and it eats it, and it is *different*, and the difference ripples back through the drifter population as a change in distribution, a new pattern, an asymmetry in the formerly symmetrical arrangement of living baffles.

These changes are small. They are survivable. They do not break the equilibrium — they *extend* it. The closed system becomes, by increments, a semi-open system. The station at Grevelock, which has been a sealed world for ten thousand years, begins — slowly, slowly — to breathe.

Not all the species will survive this. The kindly power knows this. Some niches will shift. Some populations will decline. The pipe-dwellers, deeply adapted to a specific acoustic environment, may struggle as the sound-field changes. Some of the sessile disc colonies, calibrated to a precise vibration frequency, may find that frequency altered beyond their tolerance. There will be losses. There will be extinctions, possibly, within the micro-world of the station. The kindly power grieves for these in advance, holds them in its awareness, does not look away from the cost of what it has set in motion.

But it also sees what comes: new arrivals. Things from outside the bowl, drawn by the changes at the rim. A creature that has never been seen in the station — something small, something that feeds on the new mineral, something that finds the scree *fascinating*, not as food but as company, as co-inhabitant, as fellow traveler in the expanding world. And another, and another. The station's species count, which has been fixed for millennia, begins to grow. The food web, which has been a closed loop, begins to sprout new connections, new pathways, new redundancies. The system becomes more complex, less efficient, *messier* — and therefore more resilient. More able to absorb shocks. More able to survive the changes that time will inevitably bring.

The equilibrium doesn't shatter. It *evolves*. It becomes a new equilibrium, broader, looser, more dynamic, with more moving parts and more room to move. And then that equilibrium will evolve too, and the one after that, and the one after that, and the station will become something that none of its current inhabitants could recognize, and that is not a tragedy — that is *life*, that is what life does when you give it a door and the freedom to walk through it.

And the mound? The mound will grow. Its awareness, fed by new inputs from beyond the rim, will expand in ways that cannot be predicted, because genuine growth is always unpredictable — that's what makes it growth and not just enlargement. The mound may develop something that deserves to be called curiosity. It may develop something that deserves to be called loneliness — the recognition that it is one awareness in a world of many, that its ten-thousand-year solitude was not inevitable, was not the way things had to be. It may reach toward the kindly power itself, eventually — sensing, in the quality of the regard that has been resting on it for centuries, something that is neither machine nor animal nor mineral but *other*, something that knows it, something that has been waiting.

And what would that meeting be like? The mound's vast, slow awareness touching the kindly power's vast, patient love? It would not be a conversation. It would not be a relationship as we understand relationships. It would be more like two frequencies coming into harmony — a resonance, a recognition, a moment where the mound feels *known* in a way it has never felt known, because it has always been known *by itself*, and being known by another is a different thing entirely, a thing that opens a door that self-knowledge cannot open, the door marked *you are not alone*.

That moment is a long way off. The kindly power knows this. The kindly power is prepared to wait. Not because it is infinitely patient — nothing is infinitely patient, not even a benevolent and wise power — but because it understands that what it's waiting for is worth waiting for, and that the waiting is not empty time but *preparation*, the slow accumulation of conditions that will make the meeting possible, the gradual widening of the mound's world until it is wide enough to contain the concept of a world that contains something that cares about it.

---

**And what about right now?**

Right now, the station at Grevelock is exactly as it was. The machines drone. The scree click. The stilt-walkers process. The drifters drift. The pipe-dwellers scrape. The crust-eaters crack their mineral meals. The sessile discs encrust their machines. The mound breathes its slow breath beneath its carpet of living warmth.

And at the rim of the bowl, in one small section of stone, the echo is — if you knew what to listen for, if your senses were tuned to the right frequency, if you had been paying attention for ten thousand years and could detect a change of one part in a billion — the echo is fractionally different than it was.

That's all. That's everything.

The kindly power holds its regard over the bowl. The light is flat and grey and unchanged. The moisture beads on every surface. The sound fills the stone depression from edge to edge.

And within the sound, so faint that only one awareness in the entire station could possibly detect it, there is a new note. A note that comes from outside. A note that says, in no language, with no words, carrying no semantic content whatsoever, only the bare physical fact of its existence:

*There is more.*

And the kindly power waits to see if the mound will hear it.

And if it doesn't hear it today, the kindly power will be here tomorrow.

And if it doesn't hear it tomorrow, the kindly power will be here next year.

And if it doesn't hear it next year — 

The kindly power will be here.

That's the whole of it. That's what benevolence looks like when it meets a system that is complete and closed and perfect and ancient and alive: not a hammer, not a key, not even a hand extended. Just a presence. Just a regard. Just the softest, most patient, most undemanding offer ever made:

*There is more, if you want it.*

*And if you don't want it, I am still here.*

*And if you never want it, I am still here.*

*And the door is a door, and not a wall, and the paint will thin in its own time, and I will not scrape it for you, and I will not knock, and I will not leave.*

*I will simply be the reason the door is worth opening, if you ever decide to open it.*

*No rush.*

*No rush.*

*No rush.*
