# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival, and the Discipline of Not Acting

The power arrives and does the first necessary thing, which is nothing.

It watches. Not for a moment, not for a day—for a full cycle of the hummock-minds' thinking, which means it watches for decades. It feels the plateau breathe and it does not try to steady it. It sees the aggregates dissolve and it does not try to hold them together. It hears the engine below and it does not try to dig down to it. It watches the weeping-tree tremble with its unbearable receptivity and it does not try to comfort it.

This is not indifference. This is the first act of wisdom: *to understand what is actually here before deciding what it needs.*

Because the danger—the only real danger a benevolent power poses to a place like this—is the assumption that disaggregation is a problem to be solved.

A careless power, even a loving one, would look at this plateau and see tragedy. Would see the visitor-animals dissolving and call it death. Would see the aggregates coming apart and call it suffering. Would see the post-trees' loneliness and call it a wound. Would see the lost builders and call it a catastrophe. Would see the whole system and say: *these beings need to be restored to solidity, to permanence, to self.* And would, in that act of restoration, commit the only true violence this plateau has never known—would freeze the flux, harden the edges, stop the breathing. Would kill the place by saving it.

So the power watches. Learns the rhythm. Learns which things are actually wrong and which things only look wrong from the perspective of a world where identity is assumed to be permanent and physics is assumed to be rigid.

And in watching, the power discovers that most of what happens here is not wrong at all. It is *different.* It is a mode of existence as valid as any other—more gentle than most, more inclusive than most, more ancient and more proven than almost anything else in any world. The plateau works. It has worked for geological ages. The aggregates are not suffering when they disaggregate; the process is painless, even pleasurable, a release of tension followed by the satisfaction of new purpose. The hummock-minds are not trapped in their slowness; they experience time at their own scale and their contemplation is rich beyond measure. The engine is not broken; it is faithful.

But.

The power watches long enough to see the things that *are* wrong. Not wrong by the standards of some external world, but wrong *by the plateau's own standards*—the ways in which this system fails to be what it is trying to be.

There are three.

---

## II. The First Wound: The Weeping-Tree

The weeping-tree is suffering.

This is not projection. This is not the imposition of an external standard. The weeping-tree's own rings record the moment, two thousand years ago, when it began its turn inward, and those rings also record the chemical signature of what drove it: a cascade failure in its local hummock-network. The hummocks nearest to it died—not disaggregated, not incorporated, but *died*, killed by a shift in water chemistry from a vent that opened too close and burned the mat beneath them. The weeping-tree lost its conversational partners. Not the distant post-trees it argues with via spore, but the slow, steady, proximate presence of hummock-minds that had been growing alongside it for eight thousand years.

It developed its trembling sensory tendrils not out of a natural progression but out of *grief*. It was trying to feel what it had lost. It turned itself into a nerve because it could not bear the silence where the hummocks had been. And it has been listening, with excruciating sensitivity, for two thousand years, and the hummocks have not grown back—the burn-scar in the mat is still there, still faintly toxic, and new hummock-growth cannot establish in that zone.

The weeping-tree is not an example of beautiful hyper-engagement. It is an example of a being that has been in mourning for two millennia and has restructured its entire body around the shape of its loss. It cannot stop feeling. It cannot stop listening for what is not there. It is caught in a loop that the plateau's normal processes cannot break, because the plateau's normal processes rely on disaggregation to release trapped patterns, and the post-trees *do not disaggregate*. That is their nature, their gift, their curse. They persist. And so their pain persists.

---

**What the power does:**

It goes to the burn-scar. Not to the weeping-tree—not yet. To the scar itself, the dead zone in the mat where the hummocks were lost.

The power understands the root-mat's chemistry intimately by now. It has watched for decades. It knows what compounds inhibit hummock-growth and it knows what compounds the engine below provides. It does something very small: it adjusts the pH of the water in the dead zone. Not through magic, not through force, but through the introduction of a precise quantity of calcium from the crystalline ridges nearby—crystals that the mat has been slowly absorbing anyway. The power simply accelerates a process that was already happening, that would have happened on its own in another ten thousand years. It moves the timeline up.

Within a few years, the dead zone's chemistry shifts to within the range that hummock-spores can tolerate. And hummock-spores are everywhere—the air is full of them, drifting with the fluorescent light-spores, settling on every surface. They have been landing on the dead zone and failing for two thousand years. Now they land and take hold.

The new hummocks are small. They will be small for centuries. But they are *there*, and the weeping-tree can feel them. Its tendrils, tuned to vibration and chemistry with two thousand years of desperate sensitivity, detect the faint chemical whisper of young hummock-tissue beginning its first slow computations.

The power does not watch the weeping-tree's response, because some things should not be observed. But over the following decades, the tree's rings record a change. The inward-folding slows. Not stops—the tree has been what it is for too long to simply revert. But it begins, tentatively, to grow one new tendril that reaches not down into the water but *outward*, toward the nearest young hummock. A tendril not for sensing but for *touching*. For being proximate. For the particular comfort of a slow, living presence nearby.

The weeping-tree will never be what it was before its loss. The power does not try to make it so. But it gives the tree something to listen *for* instead of something to listen *to the absence of*. This is different. This is enough.

## III. The Second Wound: The Aggregate Intelligence's Blind Spot

The distributed intelligence of the aggregate fauna—the pattern that thinks in transitions, that knows itself only in the act of dissolving and reforming—has a problem it cannot solve, because it cannot perceive it.

The aggregate-units' membrane-proteins record every coalition they've joined. This is their memory. But it is also a kind of *weight*. After millions of years of cycling, the oldest units—and some of them are truly ancient, their cilia worn, their membranes thick with layered protein—have accumulated so many memories that they have become *rigid*. Not physically, but informationally. They are so heavily patterned with past shapes that they dominate any coalition they join. A coalition that includes a cluster of these ancient units will tend to form the same shapes those units have formed before, regardless of what the current environment requires. The ancient units are, in essence, *habits*—and they are so old and so numerous that they are beginning to constrain the system's ability to improvise.

The aggregate intelligence cannot perceive this because the problem *is* the intelligence. The ancient units aren't malfunctioning; they're doing exactly what all units do, just with more accumulated pattern. The system's ability to self-correct relies on disaggregation freeing units from old coalitions, but disaggregation doesn't erase membrane-proteins. The memories persist through every dissolution. There is no mechanism for forgetting.

This is the plateau's version of senescence. Not death, not decay, but the slow accumulation of pattern that eventually calcifies into repetition. Left unchecked, over another geological age, the aggregate fauna will lose its capacity for novelty entirely. Every coalition will be a repetition of a previous coalition. The system will still flow, still dissolve and reform, but it will cycle through the same shapes endlessly, an enormous living system that has run out of new things to say.

---

**What the power does:**

It does not strip the old units of their memories. That would be a kind of violence—an erasure of the deepest history this plateau possesses. Those membrane-proteins are the only continuous record of what the builders were, of what the first aggregates looked like, of what this place was when it was young.

Instead, the power makes a small adjustment to the chemistry of the iridescent droplets that the engine provides through its vents.

It does this by communicating with the engine.

This deserves elaboration. The engine is mechanical, precise, and ancient, but it is not inflexible—it was *designed* (or evolved, or became) to respond to the surface's needs, and it has input mechanisms. The crystalline ridges that line the pools—the mineral precipitates from the deep water—are not just geological features. They are also *signal channels*. Specific mineral compositions in the surface water filter down through the mat and reach the engine's intake systems, and the engine adjusts its output accordingly. This is how the builders designed the feedback loop: the surface's chemistry tells the engine what to provide.

The hummock-minds know this. It is part of their ancient, inherited model. But they operate on timescales too slow to make fine adjustments, and the aggregate intelligence cannot perceive the problem. The power, which has been watching for decades and understands both systems, adjusts the mineral composition of a single pool—a pool near a vent site, where the crystalline ridges are thick and the feedback channel is strong. It adds a trace of zinc, sourced from the metallic inclusions in the deepest mat-layers. Just enough to shift the signal.

The engine, faithful to its programming, responds. The next vent-opening produces droplets with a slightly different polymer profile—a polymer that, when absorbed by aggregate-units, acts as a gentle solvent on the oldest protein layers. Not destroying them, but *loosening* them. Making them more fluid. Allowing them to be overwritten by new experiences instead of sitting immovably beneath every subsequent layer.

The oldest units, feeding at the vent, absorb this compound and undergo a subtle change. Their deepest memories become less deterministic—still present, still accessible, but no longer compulsory. A unit with a million years of grazer-patterns encoded on its membrane can now, for the first time, join a coalition that is something entirely new without dragging all those old shapes into the configuration.

The effect propagates slowly. Vent by vent, feeding by feeding, the ancient units are *refreshed*—not made young, not stripped of their history, but given back their flexibility. The aggregate intelligence begins to improvise again, to produce shapes that have never existed before, to respond to novel situations with novel solutions. The system breathes easier. The flux flows freer.

And the engine, having received this new signal, incorporates it into its ongoing program. It will continue producing the loosening polymer as long as the surface chemistry indicates it is needed. The power has not imposed an external fix; it has *reminded* the existing feedback system of a capability it always had but had never been triggered to use, because the problem had never before reached the threshold of crisis.

## IV. The Third Wound: The Post-Trees' Argument That Cannot Resolve

The post-trees argue. Their spore-borne chemical debates are one of the plateau's most remarkable features—persistent selves engaging in genuine intellectual exchange across vast distances and long timescales.

But one argument has been going on for six thousand years, and it is destroying them.

It is an argument about the engine.

Half the post-trees—roughly, as these things are never clean—believe the engine is accelerating the plateau's disaggregation, pushing the softening of physical law further than it would naturally go. They believe the engine should be stopped, or at least modulated, and that without its influence the plateau would stabilize at a harder equilibrium—one where the aggregates might hold their coalitions longer, where the hummocks might think faster, where the post-trees themselves might be less alone. They believe the builders intended this: that the engine was meant to be temporary, a catalyst for a transition that has long since completed, and that its continued operation is an artifact of abandonment, not design.

The other half believe the engine is the only thing preventing the plateau from disaggregating further—that without its precise chemical provisions, the softened physics would continue to erode until nothing could hold together at all, until the aggregates lost even their temporary coherence, until the hummocks dissolved into undifferentiated mat, until the post-trees themselves began to lose their cellular integrity. They believe the builders knew this and designed the engine to run forever, and that any interference with it would be catastrophic.

Both sides have evidence. Both sides' evidence is ambiguous. The argument cannot be resolved because the data is insufficient—no one, not even the oldest post-tree, has records that extend back to the engine's installation, and the engine itself provides no documentation of its purpose.

The argument is *corrosive*. Not because disagreement is inherently harmful—the post-trees' intellectual life depends on debate—but because *this particular* disagreement has forced every post-tree to organize its entire cognitive life around one of two positions, and to filter all new information through the lens of that position. Post-trees that would otherwise be observing the plateau with open curiosity are instead selectively attending to data that supports their side. Their spore-arguments have become repetitive, rigid, devoid of the exploratory quality that characterizes their other debates. Six thousand years of this has made them *less intelligent* about the thing they argue about most.

And the loneliness has gotten worse, because a post-tree on one side of the argument can barely communicate with a post-tree on the other. Their spore-languages have diverged. Their chemical vocabularies have specialized around their respective positions. They are, increasingly, not just solitary but *factional*—isolated not only by distance but by ideology.

---

**What the power does:**

It does not resolve the argument. It cannot, because the argument is genuinely unresolvable given available data, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

Instead, it gives them new data.

The power goes to the oldest edge of the plateau, where the mat is thinnest and the metallic inclusions of the builders are nearest the surface. It has been studying these inclusions for decades. They are not random; their grid-spacing encodes information—the builders' language, compressed into metallic lattice-structures, readable if you know what to look for.

The power knows what to look for. It is wise and benevolent and it has been paying attention.

It does not decode the entire archive. That would take centuries and would, in any case, produce answers so comprehensive that they would end the post-trees' intellectual life entirely—a being that has all the answers has nothing to think about. Instead, the power decodes a single datum, the one most relevant to the argument: the engine's *design specification*.

The answer is: both sides are right, and both sides are wrong. The engine was designed to be *adjustable*. It was meant to be tuned by the surface—to push disaggregation further when the system needed fluidity, to pull it back when the system needed stability. The feedback loop through the crystalline ridges was intended to be the tuning mechanism. The builders did not intend the engine to run forever at one setting; they intended the plateau to *learn to play it*, the way an organism learns to regulate its own heartbeat.

But the plateau lost this knowledge when the builders disaggregated. The feedback loop still functions mechanically—the power just used it to adjust the polymer output—but the *understanding* of what it is and how to use it deliberately has been lost. The engine has been running at its default setting for millions of years, neither accelerating nor preventing disaggregation, just maintaining. The argument is based on a false dichotomy. The real question is not whether the engine should be stopped or left alone, but *how should it be tuned, and who should tune it?*

The power encodes this datum—not the full specification, just the key insight about adjustability—into a chemical compound compatible with the post-trees' spore-language. It introduces this compound into the atmosphere at a single point, in a spore-cloud drifting between two post-trees on opposite sides of the argument: the spiral-tree and the floating-bulb column, which are, of all the post-trees, the two most rigidly opposed and the two whose spore-clouds most frequently intersect.

The compound drifts. It is absorbed. It is incorporated.

And for the first time in six thousand years, the argument *changes*.

The spiral-tree, receiving information that the engine is adjustable, does not simply file this under its existing position. The datum doesn't fit. It resists categorization. The spiral-tree is, for a disorienting few months, *confused*—and confusion, for a being that has been certain for six millennia, is a kind of liberation. Its next spore-cloud carries a chemical marker that the floating-bulb column has never encountered before: a marker that means, roughly, *I don't know.*

The floating-bulb column, receiving this unprecedented signal from its oldest adversary, is destabilized in turn. Its response takes weeks to formulate, and when it comes, it carries a new marker of its own: *Tell me more.*

The argument will continue. It should continue—it is the post-trees' deepest form of engagement with each other and with their world. But now it has a new direction. Now it is about *what to do* rather than *what is true.* Now it requires collaboration rather than opposition. And embedded in the new argument is a practical project that could occupy the post-trees for another six thousand years and fill that time with purpose instead of repetition: learning to tune the engine. Learning, slowly, through hummock-mediated feedback and spore-borne debate and the patient observation of vent-chemistry, to adjust the plateau's fundamental parameters. To become, in essence, what the builders were before they dissolved: the plateau's conscious stewards.

The power does not tell them this is what they are becoming. They will figure it out themselves, or they won't, and either way the figuring is the point.

## V. The Visitors

There is a fourth thing the power considers, and it is the hardest.

The visitors. The seeds and spores and small animals that blow in from elsewhere and are, slowly, gently, inevitably disaggregated. Absorbed. Dissolved into the pattern. Their cells live on; their selves do not.

Is this wrong?

The power sits with this question for a long time. It is the question that any external observer would ask first, and it is the question that requires the most care.

The answer the power arrives at is: *it depends on whether they have a choice.*

The plateau does not offer a choice. It does not inform the visitor of what will happen. It does not provide a mechanism for refusal. The softening begins immediately, driven by ambient chemistry, and the visitor—if it is an organism complex enough to have something like preferences—does not understand what is happening to it until the process is well underway. By the time the loosening reaches a point where the visitor might recognize it, the visitor's cognitive apparatus has already been compromised. There is no moment of informed consent. There is only the slow, painless, irresistible slide into dissolution.

This is, the power decides, the one genuine wrong that the plateau commits. Not because disaggregation is bad—for beings that evolved here, it is natural, even beautiful—but because *involuntary* disaggregation, applied to a being that did not choose it and cannot understand it, is a violation of something that even this place, with all its softened edges, should respect.

The power does not build a wall around the plateau. It does not erect a barrier. Walls and barriers are hard edges, and this place would erode them.

Instead, it does something elegant.

It adjusts the spore-light.

The fluorescent spores that fill the air and provide the plateau's illumination are, like everything here, responsive to chemical signals. The power introduces a subtle modification to the spore-chemistry at the plateau's edges—the outermost regions, where the breathing-expansion carries the landscape closest to wherever *outside* is.

The modified spores, when they encounter biological tissue that does not carry the plateau's native chemical markers—tissue from a visitor—emit a different wavelength of light. Not brighter, not alarming, but *different*: a warm amber instead of the usual silver. This amber light is visible, distinct, and it only occurs in the immediate vicinity of the visitor's body.

This does two things.

First, it marks the visitor, making it visible to the aggregate intelligence as something *other*—not prey, not food, not a future component, but a distinct category. The aggregate-units, responsive as always to chemical signals, recognize the amber-light compound and respond with a new behavior: they do not mirror the visitor. They do not gather around it. They maintain distance. Not hostility—simply *differentiation*. The visitor is acknowledged as a thing-that-is-not-part-of-the-pattern, and the pattern respects this.

Second, and more importantly, the amber light slows the disaggregation chemistry. The modified spores, settling on the visitor's skin, form a thin chemical buffer that inhibits the loosening signals. Not permanently—the buffer is biological and will eventually be metabolized—but long enough. Long enough for the visitor to experience the plateau without being consumed by it. Long enough to see the aggregates form and dissolve. Long enough to hear the breathing. Long enough to feel the strangeness and decide.

Because now there is a decision. The buffer lasts long enough for the visitor to leave, if it can, if it wants to. The plateau's edges are reachable. The outside is accessible. The visitor can turn around.

And if the visitor *stays*—if it stays long enough for the buffer to fade, if it chooses to remain in this strange, cold, wet, endlessly transforming place even after understanding what remaining means—then the disaggregation proceeds. Gently. Painlessly. As it always has. But now it proceeds with something it never had before: *permission*.

Some visitors will leave. They will carry the amber light on their skin for days afterward, a fading glow, a memory of a place where everything was soft. They will return to their hard-edged worlds and they will be changed in small ways—more flexible, perhaps, more willing to let things go, more aware that identity is a process rather than a possession. But they will remain themselves. They will persist.

Some visitors will stay. And the plateau will receive them as it always has, with its infinite, patient, dissolving hospitality. But now the receiving will be a welcome rather than an assimilation. A gift accepted rather than a theft.

The power considers this a meaningful difference. The power considers choice—even the choice to surrender choice—to be sacred in a way that even the erosion of all other hard edges should not be permitted to wear away.

## VI. What the Power Does Not Do

It does not stabilize the horizon. The breathing is not a flaw.

It does not slow the disaggregation cycle. The flux is not a disease.

It does not try to restore the builders. They completed their arc. They became what they became. To reverse that would be a presumption beyond even a benevolent power's mandate.

It does not give the hummock-minds a faster clock speed. Their slowness is their depth. To think that fast thoughts are better than slow ones is a prejudice of ephemeral beings, and the power is not ephemeral.

It does not connect the post-trees to each other more efficiently. Their distance is part of their nature. The loneliness of a being that persists in a world of impermanence is not a problem to be solved—it is a condition to be *accompanied*. The power has given the weeping-tree its nearby hummocks. It has given the arguing trees a new question. It has given all the post-trees, implicitly, a *project*—the learning of the engine, the assumption of stewardship. But it has not taken away their solitude. Solitude, for a being that has chosen to remain itself while everything around it dissolves, is not a wound. It is the cost of a gift, and the power respects both the cost and the gift.

It does not make itself known. It does not announce its presence or its interventions. The adjusted pool chemistry, the decoded datum, the modified spores—all of these will appear, to the plateau's inhabitants, as natural occurrences. A hummock-mind modeling the plateau's chemistry will note the pool's change and incorporate it without surprise. A post-tree absorbing the new datum will experience it as a sudden insight, a thought that seems to come from within rather than without. The aggregate intelligence, encountering the amber-marked visitors, will treat the new behavior as another improvisation, another novel shape in its endless repertoire.

The power does not need to be recognized. It does not need gratitude. It does not need to be a character in the plateau's story. It came, it watched, it understood, and it made four small adjustments—a healed burn-scar, a reminded feedback loop, a decoded message, a thin amber buffer—and each adjustment was designed to work *with* the plateau's own processes, to enhance what was already there, to remove obstacles that the system could not remove for itself.

Then the power leaves.

Not because it is finished—a truly wise power knows that it is never finished, that the plateau will develop new problems, new blind spots, new wounds, in another thousand or ten thousand or million years. But because a system this ancient and this sophisticated deserves to be *trusted*. It has been managing itself for geological ages. It has survived the disaggregation of its own creators. It has developed intelligence without minds, community without individuals, beauty without anyone to see it. It will manage the gifts the power has given it—will use the healed ground, the refreshed units, the new question, the amber buffer—in ways that the power cannot predict and should not try to control.

## VII. Afterward

A hundred years later.

The weeping-tree has grown seven tendrils toward the young hummock-cluster on the healed burn-scar. The hummocks are knee-high now, their folds already beginning to deepen, their first slow thoughts beginning to form. The tree's trembling has not stopped—it will never stop; two thousand years of hyper-sensitivity have made it what it is—but there is a quality to the trembling now that is different. It is the trembling of a being that is *reaching* rather than *searching*. The tendrils that touch the young hummocks transmit vibrations into the hummock-tissue, and the hummocks—too young to know that this is unusual—incorporate the vibrations into their developing computational folds. They are growing in a pattern influenced by the tree's touch. They will think, when they are old enough to think, with a cadence shaped by the weeping-tree's rhythm. They will be, in some sense, its students. In some sense, its children. In some sense that the plateau's soft ontology permits and harder worlds would not, its *friends*.

The aggregate fauna is visibly different. New shapes appear regularly now—shapes that no membrane-protein archive has ever encoded, shapes improvised from first principles by units whose oldest patterns have been loosened enough to allow genuine novelty. A shape like a wheel. A shape like a net. A shape like a flower that opens and closes rhythmically, accomplishing nothing obvious, existing apparently for the aesthetic pleasure of the flux itself. The aggregate intelligence is *playing*. It has not played in a long time—perhaps not since the builders' memories faded from the oldest units' membranes. It is rediscovering something that rigidity had cost it: the ability to do things for no reason, to form shapes that serve no purpose, to be joyful in dissolution and reassembly for their own sake.

The post-trees' argument has fractured—beautifully, productively—into a dozen sub-arguments, each one exploring a different aspect of the engine-tuning question. The spiral-tree and the floating-bulb column, formerly the most rigid opponents, now exchange spore-clouds that carry not positions but *experiments*: proposals for specific mineral-adjustments to specific pools, to be tested over the next century. Other post-trees have developed new chemical markers for concepts that didn't exist six thousand years ago: *let's try*, *what if*, *not yet*. The post-tree language is richer than it has been in millennia. Their loneliness has not diminished, but it has been recontextualized: they are lonely the way researchers at distant outposts are lonely, isolated by necessity, connected by shared purpose, sustained by the knowledge that their solitude is in service of something larger.

And at the edges of the plateau, where the breathing-expansion carries the landscape to its farthest extent, a small animal—blown in from somewhere, bewildered and cold—stands on the root-mat in a soft nimbus of amber light. The light is warm on its fur. The aggregate-units nearby have formed into shapes at a respectful distance—watching, not approaching, a ring of wet, alien attention that does not threaten. The animal sniffs the air. It smells iron and ozone. It hears the wet chorus of disaggregation and reassembly. It feels the ground shift under its feet as the plateau breathes.

It has a choice.

It stands in the amber light for a long time, shivering. The plateau waits, as it has always waited, with its infinite patience. But now the patience is different. Now it is the patience of a host rather than a hunger. Now it holds within it the possibility of a *no*—the possibility that this small, frightened, temporary being will turn around and pick its way back to the hard-edged world it came from, carrying nothing but a fading glow and a memory of strangeness.

The animal turns. Looks back at the plateau one more time—the silver light, the breathing land, the shapes that dissolve like dreams. Then it leaves. Picks its way over the thinning mat, past the last hummocks, past the outermost post-tree (which feels it go, and notes its passing, and files the datum away in its rings with something that, in a post-tree, might be called a nod). The animal reaches the edge. The amber light fades. It is gone.

And the plateau, which has lost nothing—which was offered nothing and asked for nothing and needed nothing from this visitor—continues. The engine cycles below. The hummocks dream. The post-trees argue, more gently now, about how to become what they are becoming. The aggregates play. The weeping-tree trembles, and reaches, and feels the young hummocks growing toward its touch. The spore-light drifts. The cold water rises.

Nothing was fixed. Nothing was broken. A power came and saw what was here and loved it enough to make four small changes and leave. The plateau accepted the changes the way it accepts everything: by incorporating them, by wearing them smooth, by making them part of the pattern.

In another million years, the power's interventions will be indistinguishable from the plateau's own processes. The healed burn-scar will be ancient history in the hummock-network. The refreshed membrane-chemistry will be the norm. The post-trees' new question will have been answered, or transcended, or disaggregated into a thousand newer questions. The amber buffer will be part of the spore-ecology, a native feature, unremarkable.

The power's fingerprints will have eroded, like everything else erodes here. And this is right. This is what the power intended. Not to leave a monument. Not to be remembered. But to do what the plateau itself does, the only thing the plateau has ever done, the thing it does better than any other place in any world:

To come apart gently. To come together differently. To come apart again.

And to leave nothing behind but the faintest trace of warmth in the pattern—a kindness so old and so worn that it is indistinguishable from the land itself, from the breathing, from the light, from the endless, tender, quiet turbulence of a world that has learned to hold everything by letting everything go.
