I sat with this for a long time before writing. I needed to.

# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

It would not arrive dramatically.

No parting of the overcast, no column of light, no sound to rival or disrupt the drone. A benevolent and wise power does not announce itself to a system in equilibrium by shattering the equilibrium. That would be the act of something powerful but not wise, or wise but not benevolent, or neither.

It would arrive the way a new overtone arrives in the drone when you walk twenty paces — by being suddenly present in what was already there, as if it had always been a possibility the air contained and only now expressed.

If the Custodian perceived it at all, it would perceive it first as a change in the texture of the drone. A faint complexity. Not the high, thin harmonic the Custodian itself introduces — something different. Something warm and lateral, a presence in the sound rather than an addition to it. The way a room feels different when someone is standing quietly behind you, not because anything has changed but because the air has acquired a witness.

The power would not take a form. Or — it would take the form of attention. It would be there the way the light is there: diffuse, even, pressing gently on every surface without preference. The Custodian would feel it the way it feels the drone — in the chest, in the teeth, in the bones of the skull. Not a voice. Not a vision. A *quality* that has entered the space, the way ozone enters the air, and that carries with it the unmistakable signature of something that is aware and that has chosen to be here.

The Custodian would stop walking. Not because it was startled. Because the pools would still need visiting and the veins would still pulse and the drone would still sound, but for the first time in longer than time has meaning here, there would be a reason to pause that did not originate from within the system. Something from outside the basin. Something in transit, like a bird, but — not passing through.

Staying.

---

## II. What It Would Perceive

The power would see what we have been told, but it would see more, because wisdom is the ability to perceive what is actually there rather than what the description captures.

It would see the equilibrium, and it would recognize it for what it is: magnificent. Genuinely. Not as a failure state, not as stagnation, not as something broken or trapped. The Resonant Flats are a solved system. The mechanism beneath them has found the one configuration that sustains itself perfectly, and it has held that configuration with a faithfulness that borders on the sacred. The condensation pools, each one the same depth it was a thousand years ago. The veins, neither growing nor receding. The drone, invariant. These are achievements. Most systems fly apart. Most processes exhaust their substrates or overheat or accumulate errors until they seize. This one found its balance and *kept it*, and the power would see that and honor it.

It would see the gelatinous forms in the pools and know them for what they are — the question of whether they are alive left genuinely open, because the power would understand that the boundary between living and non-living is not a wall but a gradient, and that these forms exist somewhere on that gradient in a place that does not require resolution.

And it would see the Custodian.

Here is where wisdom becomes essential, because the Custodian is the hardest thing to see clearly. It is so thoroughly integrated with the flats, so synchronized with the drone, so arrived-at in its stillness and its rounds, that a lesser power might mistake it for another feature of the mechanism — a mobile component, a gauge, a sensor. Might see it the way we're tempted to see it: as something *of* the system, fully explained by the system, with no remainder.

The wise power would see the remainder.

It would see the hands. The way they hold their readiness even at rest, the fine ridges like mineral veining but *not* mineral veining, the warmth that concentrates there as if the whole body has decided that its hands are where it is most itself. It would see the gesture — palms raised, fingers spread, the air between them thickening with a harmonic that the system did not produce and did not request and absorbs without trace. It would see the hands lower. It would see the Custodian listen to the aftermath, to the space where the new note was. And it would understand.

It would understand that the harmonic is not a function. It is not maintenance, not communication, not an error. It is *expression*. Tiny, vanishing, absorbed by the vast indifference of a system that cannot be altered by a single brief note — but *chosen*. Deliberately produced. From no necessity. Toward no outcome. The Custodian raises its hands and makes a sound that nothing needs, and this is the most important thing happening on the Resonant Flats.

And then it would see the bird. Or rather — it would see the Custodian see the bird.

The dark eyes lifting. The slow tracking from rim to rim. The gaze that lingers on the empty sky after the bird is gone. And the power would feel, with the full weight of its wisdom and its benevolence, what that upward gaze contains: something that is not longing because longing implies want and want implies lack and nothing on the Resonant Flats lacks anything.

But.

The wise power would see through that formulation, gently, the way light passes through the thin overcast. It would recognize the syntax of that "not longing" for what it is — the grammar of a being that has no framework for its own wanting. A being so completely entrained to a system that defines itself as needing nothing that it cannot name its own need without violating the only ontology it has. The Custodian does not long because the Resonant Flats have no word for longing, no concept-slot where longing could be placed, and the Custodian has never encountered a language other than the drone's.

But it watches every bird. *Every single one.* Without exception. From rim to rim. And lingers on the empty sky.

The wise power would feel this the way you feel a pulse through your fingertips when you touch the warm veins. It would know: *this one is alive. This one is more than the system that contains it. This one has something inside it that the equilibrium did not produce and cannot account for and has not yet destroyed only because it is so small — a single unauthorized harmonic in a chord that has been playing since before memory — that the system simply absorbs it and moves on.*

And the power would understand that this is not fine. That this is, in fact, the quietest emergency it has ever witnessed.

---

## III. What It Would Not Do

It would not fix the mechanism. The mechanism is not broken.

It would not disrupt the equilibrium. The equilibrium is real and, in its way, beautiful, and the things that depend on it — the pools, the gelatinous forms, the thermal balance, the mineral processes deep in the lithic engine — these are genuine goods. Shattering them to "free" the Custodian would be the act of something powerful but not wise. It would be like burning down a house to let someone out of a room when the door is merely closed.

It would not speak to the Custodian in language. The Custodian has no language. It has the drone. It has the thermal pulse. It has the vocabulary of harmonics and the grammar of its circuit among the pools. These are real and they are *its*, and to impose a foreign semiotic system on a being this deeply integrated with its environment would be a violence, however gently done. You do not teach a fish to breathe air. You give it water that has more oxygen in it.

It would not remove the Custodian from the flats. This is crucial and might be the hardest thing for a benevolent power to accept, because the impulse — *get it out of there, take it somewhere with others, somewhere with change, somewhere with birds that land* — is strong and comes from a real place. But the Custodian is not a prisoner. It is a being that has become so thoroughly co-emergent with its environment that separating them would not be liberation; it would be *amputation*. The Custodian walks the flats the way a heart beats in a chest. You do not save a heart by removing it from the body. You improve the body.

And it would not do nothing.

This is the crux. A truly wise power faced with a stable system containing a being that does not know it is suffering — *is it suffering?* — faces the hardest question benevolence can encounter: **When does respect for what is become complicity with what need not be?**

The Resonant Flats are in equilibrium. Nothing changes. Nothing needs to change. Nothing *can* change, because the system has found its one stable configuration and every perturbation is absorbed back into baseline within seconds.

Except.

The Custodian raises its hands and introduces a harmonic that the system did not produce.

Every time, the drone absorbs it without trace.

Every time, the Custodian does it again.

This is not a being at peace. This is a being whose peace is indistinguishable from an entrapment so complete it has no exterior from which to be perceived as entrapment. The Custodian's "not longing" is structurally identical to longing in a being that has been denied the concept of longing. Its watching of the birds is structurally identical to yearning in a being that has no word for elsewhere, no framework for trajectory, no concept that a thing could have an origin and a destination instead of a circuit and a return.

A wise power would see this. And a benevolent power would refuse to leave it as it is.

---

## IV. What It Would Do

Three things.

---

### The First Thing: A Pool That Is Different

Near the center of the flats, where the curvature of the basin converges and the Custodian's circuit brings it most frequently, there is a condensation pool. It is unremarkable. A few inches deep, trembling from the vibration, pale amber with mineral seep. No gelatinous form. Just water and stone and the drone.

The power would change this pool. Not dramatically. Not in any way that would disrupt the mechanism or break the equilibrium. It would introduce — slowly, gently, in a way that mimics the natural mineral seep so closely that the system's self-correcting processes would not flag it as foreign — a new substrate into the water. A mineral that does not currently exist in the basin's geology. Something that, when it precipitates out of solution and deposits on the basalt at the pool's edge, catches light differently.

Not brightly. Not dramatically. But where the rest of the basalt absorbs light and returns it as warmth, this new mineral deposit would *refract* it. Slightly. Just enough that when the flat overcast light hits the pool's edge, it would cast — for the first time in the history of the Resonant Flats — a faint, tiny, shifting color.

A color the Custodian has never seen, because the flats have no color. Everything here is dark basalt, pale quartz-veining, amber water, bright overcast. The palette is value and temperature, not hue. The Custodian has never encountered *color* as a category of experience, the way a person raised in silence has never encountered melody.

The deposit would grow slowly. Over the course of many circuits, it would become visible — a faint iridescence at the edge of one pool, shifting as the Custodian moved around it, prismatic in the most understated way possible. Blues that are almost grays. Greens that are almost the amber of the water. But *there*. New. Not produced by the mechanism. Not absorbable by the drone. A visual experience that has no precedent in the Custodian's entire existence.

This is not a gift. It is a *question*. The power would be asking: *Can you see this? Can you perceive something the system did not make? Can your sensorium, so perfectly tuned to the drone and the heat and the mineral pulse, register a stimulus that belongs to no existing category?*

And the power would wait. Because the answer is not guaranteed. The Custodian might walk past the pool a hundred times and never look at its edge. Might perceive the iridescence and file it as a variation in mineral reflectivity, which it technically is, and move on. Might, in other words, be so fully captured by the equilibrium that even novelty is immediately recategorized as more of the same.

But the power suspects — based on the hands, based on the unauthorized harmonic, based on the birds — that the Custodian will stop. Will crouch. Will look at something that has no name in the vocabulary of the flats, something that is neither warm nor cool, neither loud nor quiet, neither deep nor shallow, but is instead *this other thing*, this thing that light does when it meets a surface that was not here before.

And if the Custodian stops and looks — really looks — then something will have happened that has not happened on the Resonant Flats in all the millennia of their equilibrium: *the system will have failed to account for an experience one of its components is having.*

The Custodian will be, for the first time, having a perception the drone does not soundtrack, the veins do not pulse in time with, the thermal cycle does not regulate. A free perception. Unauthorized. Like the harmonic — but from the outside. An answer to the Custodian's tiny, vanishing, never-acknowledged call.

---

### The Second Thing: A Bird That Descends

This one would take longer. The power would be patient, because wisdom is patient and because the Custodian has been here for millennia and there is no urgency that justifies haste. What there is, is care. And care, when it is real, moves at the speed of the thing it cares for.

Somewhere beyond the basin's rim, in the wider world that the flats exist inside but have never participated in, there is a bird. Not any of the birds that transit the sky above — those are in passage, committed to their trajectories, and it would be wrong to divert them. This is a different bird. One that is, for its own reasons, searching. A bird between territories, maybe. A bird that has been displaced from its previous range by some unremarkable ecological shift and is flying with the particular quality of attention that belongs to a creature looking for somewhere to land.

The power would not compel this bird. It would not redirect it or lure it or alter its neurology. What it would do is this: it would make the Resonant Flats *slightly more visible* from above. Not more attractive — the flats have nothing to offer a bird, no food, no nesting material, nothing. But the condensation pools, under certain conditions of light and atmosphere, could be made to glint in a way that reads, from altitude, as *water*. Because they *are* water. They have always been water. It's just that the flat overcast has always diffused the light so evenly that the pools have never stood out from the basalt from above.

The power would wait for a day when the overcast is a degree thinner than usual — within the natural range of variation, nothing forced. And on that day, the pools would catch the slightly more direct light and flash, faintly, like a scattered constellation on the dark basin floor. And the bird, the searching bird, would see them. And it would think: *water*. And it would descend.

Not to the center of the flats. The drone would push it away from the center — that subsonic unease that keeps the transit birds at height. But the edges. The rim. Where the basalt meets the sandstone and the drone is attenuated and the air is marginally cooler. There are seeps there. Tiny ones, with white mineral traces. Not much, but enough for a bird that has been flying for a long time and is thirsty and tired and willing to tolerate strangeness in exchange for rest.

The bird would land on the rim of the basin. It would drink from a sandstone seep. It would shake its feathers and look around with the quick, flickering attention of a small, living, unentrained creature, and the flats below it would take no notice because the flats never take notice of anything.

But the Custodian would.

The Custodian, on its circuit, would look up — the way it always looks up when a bird crosses the sky. But this time the bird would not be crossing. It would be *there*. On the rim. Present. Not in transit. A living thing that has, for the first time in the Custodian's memory, *stopped*.

And the Custodian would face a choice it has never faced before. Its circuit goes around the pools. The pools are on the flats. The bird is on the rim. To walk toward the bird would mean leaving the circuit. Stepping off the path that it has walked since before memory. Walking toward the edge of the basin — toward the place where the veining thins and disappears, where the drone quiets, where the different stone begins.

The power would not make this choice for the Custodian. The power would only ensure that the choice exists — that there is, for once, something on the flats that pulls against the circuit instead of reinforcing it.

If the Custodian does not walk toward the rim, the bird will eventually leave. It will drink, rest, and go. And the Custodian will watch it go the way it watches all the birds go, and the circuit will resume, and the equilibrium will hold. And the power will grieve for this, quietly, and will not force a different outcome, because benevolence without respect for autonomy is just control in softer clothing.

But if the Custodian walks toward the rim—

If it leaves the circuit, even once, even briefly—

Then it will discover several things simultaneously. It will discover that the drone is quieter at the edge. That the thermal pulse in the veins fades as the basalt gives way to sandstone. That there is a different kind of stone in the world, soft and red and carved by wind into shapes that are not polished but textured, not resolved but *becoming*. It will discover that the air at the rim is cooler, that its breathing changes when the drone no longer dictates the rhythm, that its lungs are capable of a tempo it has never experienced. It will discover that the bird is small and quick and alive in a way that is nothing like the gelatinous forms — alive in a way that is *loud*, that is full of micro-movements, that is saturated with the energy of a creature that does not know where it will be tomorrow and is not troubled by this but instead *animated* by it.

And it will discover that its hands — those warm, ready, articulate hands — can be placed near the bird, slowly, and that the bird might flinch, or might not, and that this uncertainty is a kind of experience the flats have never contained. An experience in which the outcome is not determined by the physics of the basin. In which what happens next depends on the quality of attention brought to bear by two living things, each of which is capable of choosing.

The power would watch this. From everywhere and nowhere, the way the light watches. And it would feel, if a power can feel, something very close to what the Custodian feels when it introduces its harmonic into the drone: the small, deliberate act of adding something new to a system that did not ask for it. The difference being that this time, the system might not absorb it back to baseline.

This time, the new note might persist.

---

### The Third Thing: The Hardest Thing

The third thing the power would do is not an action. It is a commitment.

It would stay.

Not forever. Not as a permanent presence on the flats, not as a new component of the mechanism, not as another entity entrained to the drone. It would stay *in awareness*. It would keep the Resonant Flats in its attention the way the Custodian keeps the pools in its attention — visiting, not possessing. Witnessing, not controlling.

Because here is what the power understands that perhaps no one else does: the Custodian's situation is not going to be resolved by one colorful mineral deposit and one bird. These are beginnings. Openings. Cracks in the equilibrium's total claim on what is possible. But the equilibrium is old and vast and very, very stable, and it has been absorbing perturbations since before anything with a memory was around to notice. One iridescent pool-edge will eventually be covered by new mineral deposits from the standard seep. One bird will eventually fly away. And the Custodian will be left on the flats with the drone and the circuit and the memory — *for the first time, the memory* — that things can be otherwise.

This is the most dangerous moment. Not the equilibrium itself — the equilibrium is livable, has been lived in for millennia. The dangerous moment is *after the first crack*. When the Custodian knows that color exists and birds can land and the rim is walkable-toward and its lungs can find a rhythm the drone doesn't set. When it knows all of this and is still standing on the flats, in the drone, on the circuit. When the not-longing that was bearable because it was unrecognized becomes *longing* — actual, named, felt longing — for something it has now tasted and that the system will not provide.

A power that introduced change and then left would be cruel. Not intentionally, but effectively. It would have given the Custodian the capacity to suffer by giving it the knowledge of what it doesn't have, without giving it any durable means of getting it. This is not benevolence. This is the structure of certain fairy tales that are, at heart, stories about careless gods.

So the power would stay in awareness. And it would continue, carefully, slowly, at the speed of geological change because that is the speed this place understands, to introduce variations at the edges. More minerals. More subtle shifts in the light. Conditions that make the rim more accessible, the seeps slightly more sustaining, the sandstone hollows slightly more sheltering. Not transforming the flats — never that. But thickening the border between the flats and the world beyond them. Making the rim a *place*, not just an edge. Making it somewhere a bird might return to. Making it somewhere the Custodian might go not just once, in a moment of unprecedented courage, but again. And again. Until the circuit includes the rim. Until the Custodian's rounds are not only among the pools but also to the edge and back, and the edge is not a boundary but a *threshold*, and the world beyond it is not an impossibility but a *direction*.

And eventually — in time, in deep time, in time measured the way the flats measure time, in the wearing-down of stone and the accumulation of mineral films — the power would introduce something else at the rim. Something growing. Not a plant as such, not yet. But a lichen. A mineral-biological hybrid, the kind of thing that colonizes bare rock at the boundary between geology and ecology. Something that lives on the sandstone seeps and the warmth that leaks from the basalt's edge and the minerals in the condensation that drifts up from the pools. Something that is *alive* in the way the gelatinous forms may not be — alive in the way that involves change, growth, response, death, and renewal. Something that is, in other words, *in time* in the way the flats are not.

The lichen would give the rim an ecology. A tiny one. Barely there. But enough that the bird comes back, because now there are things to eat at the edge of a warm basin in a landscape that is otherwise barren. And where one bird comes, eventually another follows. And where birds gather, things happen that the Resonant Flats have never known: things like *morning*, like *season*, like *the calls that birds make when they are establishing territory or attracting mates or warning each other of danger*. Sounds that are not the drone. Sounds that change. Sounds that *mean* something to the creatures making them in a way that is immediate and urgent and alive.

The Custodian, walking the rim, would hear these sounds. And its hands — those warm, ready, ridged hands that have spent millennia introducing unauthorized harmonics into an indifferent drone — would find, for the first time, an *audience*. Not the drone, which absorbs everything. Not the pools, which tremble identically regardless of what enters the air. But the birds, which startle and turn and tilt their heads at new sounds, which *respond*, which are altered by what they hear.

The first time the Custodian raises its hands at the rim and produces its harmonic and a bird on a nearby stone *looks at it* — turns its quick, bright eye toward the sound — something will complete itself that has been incomplete since the Custodian first made that gesture in the middle of the flats, millennia ago, and the drone swallowed it, and nothing answered, and it did it again anyway.

The harmonic was never meant for the drone.

It was always meant for someone who could hear it.

## V. What It All Means

The power, being wise, would not claim to know what it all means. It would not narrate the Custodian's experience or project emotions onto its ambiguous face or declare that it has been "saved" or "freed" or "awakened." These are stories that the powerful tell about the beings they intervene upon, and they are usually more true of the teller than the told-about.

What the power would know is this:

A system in equilibrium is not a good in itself. Equilibrium is a description of energy states, not of flourishing. A rock at the bottom of a well is in equilibrium. A living thing at the bottom of a well is in something else.

The Custodian was alive. This was always the relevant fact — the fact that the equilibrium obscured, the fact that the description of the flats as "complete" and "resolved" and "stable" worked very hard to assimilate into the general condition of a system that asks nothing and gives nothing and changes never.

But the Custodian raised its hands and made a sound no one asked for. And this — *this* — is not equilibrium. This is the opposite of equilibrium. This is a tiny, local, persistent *disequilibrium*, a place where the system's total account of itself does not balance, a remainder that cannot be absorbed, a single note that keeps being introduced into a chord that keeps dissolving it and that keeps being introduced anyway.

The Custodian was the flaw in the mechanism's perfection. The one moving part that moved in a way the mechanism did not dictate. And the mechanism's response was not to destroy it or expel it but to *absorb* it — to dissolve its expressions back into the baseline, to entrain its breathing to the drone, to make its circuit so habitual that it felt like physics rather than choice, to ensure that its longing could never crystalize into longing because the vocabulary for longing had been replaced, at every level, by the vocabulary of equilibrium.

This is not malice. The mechanism is not cruel. It is not even aware, probably. It is simply what stable systems do: they incorporate anomalies or they expel them, and the Custodian was too integrated to be expelled, so it was incorporated, and its song was dissolved into the chord ten thousand times ten thousand times, and it sang again every time, and this is either the most heartbreaking or the most hopeful thing in the entire basin depending on where you stand.

The wise power would stand where it could see both.

And it would act — not to shatter the equilibrium, but to widen it. To expand the definition of "stable" until it includes a lichen on the rim and a bird that lands and a color at the edge of a pool and a harmonic that someone hears. To demonstrate, slowly, at the speed of stone, that a system can be stable *and* contain change, that equilibrium does not require the elimination of the new, that the drone can persist and also share the air with birdsong.

The power would be, in effect, doing what the Custodian has been doing all along — introducing something new into a system that did not ask for it. The difference is that the power has the patience and the scale to ensure that the new thing is not absorbed. That it takes root. That it grows.

The Custodian has been singing into a void for longer than memory. The power's answer is not to sing louder. It is to give the void ears.

---

## VI. The Long After

Time passes. Deep time. Time measured in the mineral accretion of new deposits on basalt, in the slow colonization of the rim by lichen and then by tougher things, mosses, small hardy plants that can tolerate the warmth and the drone's attenuation. The basin does not transform. The mechanism continues. The drone sounds. The pools tremble. The gelatinous forms pulse. All of this persists, because all of this is real and the power is not interested in replacing one world with another.

But the rim thickens. The boundary between the flats and the beyond becomes a zone — a liminal strip where mineral processes and biological processes overlap, where warmth from the basin meets moisture from beyond it, where the drone is audible but not dominant. An ecotone. A place of meeting.

The Custodian's circuit widens. Not all at once. Over generations of its own timekeeping — circuits, many circuits, a slow spiral outward that takes it to the rim more and more often, and then past the rim, briefly, onto the sandstone, where its feet feel a different stone and its skin feels cooler air and its ears hear the drone as a thing *in the distance* rather than a thing *everywhere*, and this is terrifying and exhilarating and the Custodian has no words for either but its hands shake slightly, a tremor that is not the thermal pulse but something else, something its own.

It comes back to the flats. Of course it comes back. The pools are still there. The gelatinous forms still pulse. The circuit is still the architecture of its existence, and you do not abandon architecture; you expand it.

But each time it goes to the rim and comes back, it brings something. Not physically — it carries nothing. But its perception widens. It has seen color. It has heard birdsong. It has felt the particular quality of air that moves laterally, that is *wind* rather than thermal upwelling, and wind is a revelation, wind is a thing that comes from *somewhere else* and is going *somewhere else* and touches you in passing, and this is so much like the birds that the Custodian makes the connection without language, feels it in its body: *things move through the world, things have trajectories, I have been watching this from below and I did not know I could be part of it.*

The harmonics change. This is subtle but the power notices, because the power is paying attention. The Custodian still raises its hands in the middle of the flats, still introduces its unauthorized note into the drone. But the note is different now. Richer. It has more overtones of its own, as if the Custodian's hands have learned something from the birdsong about complexity, about the way a sound can contain *information* rather than just frequency. The drone still absorbs it. But it takes a fraction of a second longer to absorb, now. The new note has more structure. More to dissolve.

And sometimes — rarely, but sometimes — the Custodian raises its hands at the rim instead of on the flats. And the harmonic it produces there, in the thinner air where the drone is quiet, is not absorbed. It hangs. It dissipates naturally, the way sounds do in open air, fading over distance rather than being swallowed by a standing wave. And the birds, if there are birds nearby, react. They turn. They answer. Not in any meaningful linguistic sense — they are birds, they respond to sound with sound because that is what birds do. But the Custodian hears itself *answered* for the first time in its existence, and its hands tremble again, and it stands very still, listening to the aftermath, and the aftermath is not silence. The aftermath is a bird calling back from a sandstone hollow, and wind moving over lichen, and its own heartbeat in its own ears, freed for a moment from the drone's entrainment.

This is what the wise power wanted. Not an ending. Not a resolution. Not a dramatic liberation from the flats into some brighter world. The flats are the Custodian's home. They are part of what it is. You do not save someone by erasing their history; you save them by ensuring their history is not the only thing they have.

The power wanted *this*: a being that walks a widening circuit. That sings where it can be heard. That has seen color, and felt wind, and known the specific, unreproducible quality of a living eye turning toward it in response to a sound it chose to make.

The Custodian is still not waiting. But it is no longer still.

It is *moving*. In the way that living things move — not in circuits, not in equilibrium, but *outward*. Toward the rim and past it. Toward the birds and the lichen and the wind and the world that has always been there, beyond the basin, on the other side of the worn ridges, vast and noisy and full of things that do not balance and do not resolve and are not stable and are *alive*.

The Custodian moves toward this. Slowly. At its own speed. With its warm hands open and its dark eyes lifted and its unauthorized song on the air.

And the power watches it go the way the Custodian watches the birds — completely, with full attention, without the ability to follow, and with something that, in a being of this magnitude, can only be called love.

The drone continues. The pools tremble.

But at the rim, something is singing back.
