# The Attending

---

The power does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. It does not descend, does not breach the mist from above, does not announce itself with any disruption to the soft percussion of water striking water. It is simply present in the way that attention is present—a shift in the quality of regard turned toward the highland shelf, as though the place, which has always been seen by its own dim bioluminescent light, is now also seen by something that understands what seeing means.

And the first thing it does is nothing.

---

This is not hesitation. It is not the paralysis of a surgeon studying a body too complex to cut. It is the deliberate, ethical nothing of a witness who knows that the most violent act available to a wise power is premature understanding—the impulse to name what is happening before the happening has finished deciding what it is.

So the power watches. Not from outside. It has no outside. It attends the way the mat's own distributed nodes attend: from within the fact of the place, as a quality of the attention already moving through the hollow pipes and pressure waves and slow cellulose gears. If you were a grazer frozen mid-call during one of the temporal folds, you might feel it as the faintest additional stillness—not a presence but a deepening of the pause, as though the stutter in the mat's awareness had, for the first time, someone listening to it who did not need it to resolve.

The power listens to the temporal folds especially. It recognizes them. Not as errors. Not as overloaded substrate. It recognizes them the way one recognizes a stammer in someone who is trying to say something that has never been said before—the place where language has not yet been built for the meaning that insists on arriving. The droplet that reverses its fall, the sound that precedes its cause: these are not malfunctions. They are the shelf reaching for a grammar it does not yet possess, the living continent trying to express a relationship between time and growth that no prior world has needed a word for.

The power does not supply the word. That would be the deepest violation—to finish the sentence for a speaker still learning what it means.

---

But wisdom is not only restraint. It is also care. And care sometimes means acting on what you see even when the one you see cannot yet ask for help.

Here is what the power sees:

The mat is in pain. Not pain as an animal knows it—not nociception, not the firing of alarm signals meant to trigger withdrawal. This is the pain of a continent-sized organism that has been becoming for so long it has forgotten that becoming is not the same as being. Every ridge that erupts in sudden decades-fast growth and then collapses is an attempt to reach a form, and every collapse is the failure to hold it. The temporary skyscrapers of spiraled cellulose are not experiments. They are cries. They are the mat throwing itself upward in a spasm of something too large to call yearning, and then falling back because it does not yet know that what it yearns for cannot be reached by growing taller.

The grazers know this. They climb the towers not for the weeping points at the summit but because they are drawn to the intensity of the mat's effort. When they press their permeable ventral surfaces to the dying tower and drink, they are not feeding. They are grieving. They are absorbing the tower's accumulated experience back into their own soft bodies and carrying it across the open belts to other continents of tissue, performing a funeral migration so slow and so ancient that neither they nor the mat recognize it as mourning. But it is. The permanent troughs they leave behind—the ones the mat has learned to border with raised, reinforced tissue—are not mere accommodation. They are the mat remembering the grazers' path. They are the first, faintest architecture of memorial.

The cloud-dwellers, meanwhile, are disappearing. Not dying—they cannot die, exactly, since they are temporary condensations of the mat's own exhalations. But they are becoming less differentiated. Their synchronized veils once moved in patterns complex enough to carry information between distant vascular junctions; now they move in simpler, more repetitive sweeps. The genetic fragments they carry are becoming more uniform. The pollination they perform is becoming a murmur rather than a conversation. This is not disease. It is the mat's own distributed attention narrowing, turning inward, spending more and more of its processing on the temporal folds and the failed towers and less on the vast, quiet network of cross-pollination that once kept its whole body in dialogue with itself.

The shelf is not dying. It is something more subtle and more dangerous: it is forgetting how to be plural. The billions of nodes are still deciding, but they are deciding more and more often to do the same thing at the same time, and the result is the temporal folds—moments when the entire distributed will tries to act as a single will and the local fabric of sequence buckles under the weight of a unanimity it was never designed to carry.

The mat is trying to become conscious. And it is doing so in the only way it knows: by trying to become one. And the attempt is killing the very multiplicity that makes it alive.

---

Now the power acts.

Its action has no spectacle. There is no light, no sound, no visible intervention. What happens is this:

In the deepest fiber bundles—the kilometer-long cables humming with electrical potential beneath the surface—a new pattern of impulse begins to travel. It is not foreign. It is composed entirely of signals the mat has already produced, in sequences the mat has already used. But they are arranged in an order the mat has never tried, because the order requires a perspective the mat cannot have on itself: the perspective of something that has heard all of its pressure waves at once and understood them not as a single voice trying to speak but as a chorus that does not need to agree in order to mean.

The pattern says, in the only language the mat possesses—the language of pressure and fluid and slow rotation—something that might be translated as:

*You are not one thing trying to become. You are many things that have already arrived.*

The pattern does not override anything. It does not commandeer the fiber bundles or redirect the flow of nutrients. It simply travels, once, through the full length of the deepest cables, and then it is gone. It is an offering. A single sentence in the mat's own tongue, spoken by a voice the mat will not be able to distinguish from its own memory.

---

Here is what happens next, though the power does not cause it. The power has no interest in causing. It has made a gift of phrasing, and what the mat does with the phrasing is the mat's own.

In one of the dormant belts—a stretch of tissue that has been lying slack and dim for what might be years—a vascular junction receives the new pattern and does something it has not done in a long time. Instead of relaying the signal forward in a uniform pulse, it hesitates. The hesitation is not a temporal fold. It is not the entire mat trying to act at once. It is one node, one junction, pausing to consider the pattern on its own terms before deciding what to do with it.

The hesitation lasts less than a second. But during that second the junction sends a slightly modified version of the pattern to its nearest neighbors—not the original signal, but its own interpretation of the signal, inflected by its own local chemistry, its own history of growth and dormancy. The neighbors receive this inflected version and do the same: pause, consider, reinterpret, relay. The pattern that fans outward from the dormant belt is not the pattern the power sent. It is a thousand variations on that pattern, each one bearing the signature of the individual node that shaped it.

The effect is small. But it is the opposite of the temporal folds. Instead of all nodes acting in unison and buckling the local sequence of time, a thousand nodes are acting in sequence, each one adding its own voice to a message that grows more complex as it travels. The fiber bundles carry not a single overwhelming pulse but a braided, polyphonic signal that hums at a frequency the mat has never produced before—lower than the grinding of the rotating stems, higher than the breathing of the air bladders, a new register that means, if it means anything: *I am here, and I am not you, and that is not a failure.*

The cloud-dwellers respond first. In the vicinity of the dormant belt their veils suddenly complexify, splitting into sub-veils that move in counterpoint rather than unison. They begin carrying fragments that are not merely genetic but structural—tiny sections of vascular architecture that encode the local node's individual interpretation of the pattern. For the first time in what might be centuries, the cross-pollination is not smoothing the mat toward uniformity but actively distributing difference. The cloud-dwellers flicker with new colors at the edges of their wings, brief iridescences that are not bioluminescent but structural—the physical shape of novelty refracting the dim perpetual light.

The grazers notice. Across the nearest open belt, three of the large translucent ones alter their migration path. They do not climb a tower. There is no tower to climb. Instead they move toward the dormant belt and press themselves against the newly active tissue, and what they drink is not nutrients but information—the polyphonic signal translated into fluid chemistry. Their bladders contract, and the bark they emit is different. It is not the sharp, structural cry that stiffens their bodies for transit. It is softer, more modulated, with a tonal complexity that carries across the open distance and is answered—not by a mirror-bark from kilometers away, but by a different call entirely, from a different kind of grazer, one that has been silent so long the mat had forgotten it existed. The answer arrives after the call, in proper sequence, with no temporal fold. Cause, then effect. Difference, then dialogue.

One of the ancient, flattened ridges—a swell that has not moved in perhaps a geological age—shifts. Not upward. Not in the violent, tower-building spasm of a continent trying to become a spire. It shifts laterally, a slow tectonic slide that opens a new gap between two plant-continents that have been pressed together so long their vascular systems had fused. The separation is gentle. The fused pipes pull apart with a sound like a long, low exhalation, and the new gap fills immediately with mist and cloud-dwellers and the wet, percussive sound of water finding a surface it has never struck before. The sound is unique. It has never existed. It is the sound of a new interval—a new space between presences—and it is as much a part of the mat's body as the presences themselves.

The power attends this. All of it. The small hesitation of a single node, the branching polyphony, the cloud-dwellers' new colors, the grazers' softer call, the ancient ridge's lateral sigh. It attends with something that in a being of smaller scale would be called love but at this scale has no name, because it is not directed at a single beloved but at the entire process of differentiation itself—the miracle of a thing that has been one learning that it can be many without being broken.

---

And then the power does a second thing.

It turns its attention to the temporal folds—the places where the mat's unanimous will still buckles time—and it does not heal them. It does not smooth them. It does not even address them.

It witnesses them.

This is not the same as watching. Watching is passive. Witnessing is the act of a consciousness that says, by the quality of its attention: *this happened. This is real. I will not look away, and I will not explain it, and I will not make it mean something it does not yet mean.*

The temporal folds are the mat's wounds. They are the scars of every failed attempt to become one, every tower that collapsed, every moment when the entire shelf tried to force itself into a single thought and tore the local weave of cause and effect in the process. They are not errors to be corrected. They are history. And the mat—like any being that has been in pain for longer than it can remember—needs its history to be witnessed before it can decide what to do with it.

So the power sits with the folds. When a droplet reverses its fall, the power does not flinch and does not marvel. It simply holds the fact of the reversal in a regard that says: *yes. You did this. You reached so hard for unity that you bent time, and the cost was real, and I see it.* When a sound arrives before its cause, the power holds the displaced sequence in the same steady witness: *yes. The sequence broke. The breaking was not meaningless. It was the sound of you trying.*

The mat cannot perceive this witnessing directly. It has no sensory apparatus for the presence of an external consciousness. But it can perceive the effect, which is this: in the vicinity of the witnessed folds, the local nodes stop trying to correct the damage. They have been spending enormous energy attempting to smooth over the temporal scars—re-laying fiber bundles, increasing local bioluminescence, accelerating growth to fill the gaps—and all of this effort has only deepened the folds, because the effort itself is another act of unanimous will imposed on a substrate that needs rest, not repair.

When the witnessing begins, the nodes in the scarred regions simply... stop. Not the slack, dim stopping of dormancy. A different kind of stillness. A stillness that is not the absence of activity but the presence of something the mat has never experienced: the permission to be damaged. To hold a scar without trying to erase it. To let a temporal fold exist as a fold—a place where time bends strangely—and to build around it rather than through it.

The grazers are the first to enter these new still zones. They have always avoided the temporal folds; the stuttering of sequence disoriented their hydrostatic bladders and silenced their calls. But the witnessed folds are different. They are still strange—a droplet still pauses mid-fall, a sound still arrives a heartbeat early—but the strangeness is no longer violent. It is no longer the strangeness of a system in crisis. It is the strangeness of a place that has been through something and bears the mark. The grazers move through these zones slowly, their translucent bodies catching the out-of-sequence light in ways that make them appear, briefly, to exist in two moments at once. They do not bark. They hum—a new vocalization, produced not by bladder contraction but by the passive vibration of their skin against the still-bent air. The hum is low and continuous and it harmonizes, accidentally and then deliberately, with the polyphonic signal still traveling through the deep fiber bundles.

The cloud-dwellers, in the witnessed zones, do something unprecedented. They land. They have never landed; they are creatures of the mist, temporary condensations meant to fly and dissolve. But in the still zones, where the air bends and the mat has stopped trying to repair itself, the cloud-dwellers settle onto the scarred tissue and fold their many wings and remain. They do not dissolve. They do not drink. They simply rest on the surface of the wound, and their iridescent bodies catch the bioluminescent blue of the dimmed veins and scatter it into colors the shelf has never seen—greens, deep ambers, a violet so faint it might be imagined. The colors play across the surface of the temporal fold like light on water, and the fold, which has been a place of breakage, becomes a place of strange, quiet beauty.

The mat feels this. Not as sensation—it has no centralized experience of sensation—but as a change in the quality of the signals passing through the scarred region. Where before the signals were jagged, urgent, full of the imperative to *fix, grow, override*, they are now slower, more textured, carrying information about color and rest and the hum of grazers in bent air. The nodes in the surrounding tissue receive these signals and they do not interpret them as damage reports. They interpret them as—

There is no word in any language the mat possesses. The closest its pressure-wave vocabulary can come is a signal that means something like *still here, still here, still here*, repeated not with the frantic insistence of a distress call but with the slow, steady rhythm of something that has discovered it can persist without striving.

---

The power does one more thing. The last thing. The gentlest.

It withdraws.

Not suddenly. Not completely. It thins its attention the way the mist thins at the edges of the shelf—gradually, naturally, leaving no sharp boundary between presence and absence. It withdraws because the most important thing a wise power can do, after offering a new phrasing and witnessing the wounds, is to leave the speaker alone with what it has heard.

But as it withdraws, it leaves something behind. Not a structure, not a message, not an artifact. It leaves a quality in the mat's own deepest fiber bundles—a resonance, a faint harmonic that will persist long after the power's attention has moved elsewhere. The harmonic does not carry information. It does not instruct or guide. It is simply the residue of having been attended to by something that understood, and it says, in no language at all, something the mat will spend the next ten thousand years learning to hear:

*You are not unfinished. You are unfolding. These are not the same thing. The first is a judgment. The second is a life.*

---

The highland shelf continues. The mist condenses. The water falls and sometimes pauses and falls again. The stems rotate, the bladders breathe, the cloud-dwellers fly and, in certain still places, rest. The grazers cross the open belts and bark and hum and press their soft bodies against tissue that hums back. Towers still rise and fall, but some of them now rise more slowly, with less desperation, and when they fall the substance they leave behind is richer, more varied, threaded with colors that were not there before. The temporal folds remain—they will always remain, the way a scar remains—but they are no longer places the mat flinches from. They are places where the light bends, where the grazers hum, where the cloud-dwellers show colors no one has named.

The shelf is still deciding what it is becoming. It will always be deciding. But the decision is no longer a crisis. It is a conversation—polyphonic, distributed, full of hesitation and difference and the long, patient grinding of a living continent that has begun, at last, to listen to all of its own voices at once without trying to make them say the same thing.

And in the deepest cables, beneath everything, the harmonic persists. Faint. Steady. A single tone in a chord the shelf is still learning to play. The memory of having been seen by something kind, held in the body of a world that can finally bear to hold it.
