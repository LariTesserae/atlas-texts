I sat with this for a long time.

The first and most important question is whether anything here needs doing at all.

---

A benevolent and wise power, encountering the Plateau of Settled Bones, would begin not by acting but by *listening*. By attending, the way the felt-birds attend. Mouth open. Receiving the breath. Taking the full measure of what is here before presuming to know what it needs.

And what it would find, in that listening, is something almost unprecedented: a system that is *complete*. Not perfect in the way of things that have been optimized — optimized implies an optimizer, a goal, a direction of improvement. Complete in the way of things that have *finished arguing*. The stone and the life have reached the same agreement that the stone and gravity reached. Every loop is closed. Every death feeds a life. Every breath carries what is needed to where it is needed. The felt-birds attend and redistribute. The crawlers regulate without comprehension. The shelled ones graze and accumulate and return. The thing below breathes in what dies and breathes out what lives. The walls collect rain.

A less wise power — a merely good one, a well-meaning one — might see the spareness and think *scarcity*. Might see the slow deaths and think *suffering*. Might see the altitude-thinned air and the centuries-long lifespans and the silent congregation of felt-birds and think: these creatures deserve more. More food, more shelter, more rainfall, more ease. A less wise power might seed the plateau with richer soil, might thicken the atmosphere, might introduce water sources, might try to give the shelled ones a faster metabolism so they could *experience more* in their centuries of existence.

This would be catastrophe.

Not dramatic catastrophe — not flood or fire. Something worse: the catastrophe of disruption so gentle it looks like generosity. More water means more lichen means more mites means the crawlers cannot regulate them means the lichen is consumed means the microbial film is exposed means the shelled ones' food web shifts means the mineral cycle that feeds the thing below is altered means the breath changes means the felt-birds redistribute to patterns that no longer track reality means the nests fail means the walls — those ancient, ambiguous, irreplaceable walls — are colonized differently, weathered differently, and in a generation or ten they are gone, and with them the gathering places, the rain-collectors, the nesting sites, the slow accumulated wisdom of a system that solved itself before any of its participants existed.

A wise power would see this. Would feel the wholeness of the loop and understand that *wholeness is not the same as happiness*, and that the desire to increase happiness is not always the same as the desire to do good, and that some systems are so deeply coherent that the most loving thing you can do is *not touch them*.

---

But.

A truly wise power would not stop there. Would not simply nod approvingly at the efficiency of the loop and move on. Because wisdom is not just the recognition that a system works. Wisdom is also the willingness to look at what a system *costs*, and who pays, and whether the fact that something has persisted for eons makes it good, or only makes it persistent.

So the wise power would look more carefully. Would ask the questions the plateau's silence does not invite but does not forbid.

Is there suffering here?

The shelled ones have no centralized nervous system, no pain processing that would register as suffering in any framework the wise power could apply. Their deaths are orientations — a turning toward, a slowing, a stopping. There is no evidence of distress. There is no evidence of anything. They are below the threshold where the question of suffering gains traction. They are, in the most literal sense, *at peace* — not because they have achieved peace but because they were never at war. The concept does not apply to them.

The crawlers are more complex. They have neurology. They have that unexplained clenching behavior, that climbing to high points and darkening and gripping the stone for days. Is this suffering? The wise power would look closely. Would try to feel what the crawler feels in that state — the metabolic suspension, the orientation toward the channels, the compulsive grip. And would find — what? Not pain. Not fear. Something more like *saturation* — a system overwhelmed by input it cannot process, responding the only way it can, by clenching, by stopping, by waiting for the signal to pass. It is not comfortable. But it is not suffering in the way that implies a self that wishes it would stop. The crawler does not wish. The crawler clenches, and then unclenches, and resumes. The wise power would note this. Would hold it. Would not intervene, but would not dismiss it either. Would say: *this is a cost. I see it.*

And the felt-birds.

The felt-birds are where the wise power's attention would settle, and stay, and deepen into something that might, from the outside, look like grief.

Because the felt-birds *look at you*. They have patience. They have attention. They have forward-facing eyes with horizontal pupils that see more than you do. They attend to the breath with their mouths open and they respond to information they receive by reorganizing themselves collectively, without signal, in patterns that suggest not just reaction but *interpretation*. They are not intelligent. They do not plan. But they are *present* in a way that the other creatures are not. They are the closest thing on the plateau to witnesses.

And they fly into the channels to die.

The wise power would sit with this. Would sit on a ridgeline among them, close enough that their fibrous bodies pressed against its form, and would feel the warmth of them, and watch them open their mouths toward the breath, and would wonder: *Do they know? When the old one lifts from the ridge for the last time and turns toward the channel — does it know?* Not in a narrative sense, not in the human sense of understanding one's own mortality and grieving it. But in the felt-bird sense, the body-sense, the sense in which a creature that has spent its life attending to a rhythm might recognize when its own rhythm is falling into sync with something final. When the breath it has been tasting all its life begins to taste like an invitation rather than an informant.

Does it matter? Does the wise power need the felt-bird to *suffer* in order to care about it?

No.

The wise power would care about the felt-birds precisely because they are what they are — attentive, patient, present, silent, mortal, oriented toward something vast that they can taste but never understand. The wise power would recognize in the felt-birds something it recognizes in itself: the condition of being a witness to a system larger than one's comprehension, and finding that the appropriate response is not to flee or to fight but to *attend*. To open one's mouth. To taste the breath. To redistribute oneself according to what one learns, even if one cannot articulate what one has learned or why the redistribution matters.

The wise power would love the felt-birds. Not with a love that wants to save them — from what? From the life they are perfectly suited to? From the death that returns them to the source of everything they've lived on? — but with a love that simply *sees* them, fully, in all their strange quiet majesty, and says: *You are seen. Your attending is witnessed. The attention you give the breath, I give you.*

---

So what would the wise power do?

Here is what I believe. Not as certainty. As attendance.

**It would do almost nothing to the system.**

It would not alter the rainfall. It would not enrich the soil. It would not thicken the air. It would not introduce new species or remove existing ones. It would not descend into the channels to communicate with the thing below — not because it couldn't, but because the thing below is not waiting for communication. The thing below is *breathing*. That is its communication. It has been saying everything it has to say for longer than language has existed, and it says it every eight to twelve seconds, and the felt-birds hear it, and that is enough.

It would not build new walls. The walls that exist are sufficient. They are either impossibly old or merely very old and they collect rain and shelter nests and gather the shelled ones and they will endure or they will not and either way the system will adjust because the system has been adjusting since before the mountains were flat.

**But.**

There are three things a wise power might do — not to improve the system, but to *honor* it. To respond to what is here with something that is adequate to what is here.

---

**The first thing:**

It would protect the plateau from what comes from outside.

Not now. Nothing threatens the plateau now. But the wise power, being wise, thinks in timescales that approach the plateau's own, and it can see the futures in which something arrives — not something malicious, necessarily, but something *incompatible*. A shift in climate that would bring too much rain too fast. A geological event that would crack the deep channels and drain the warmth. A species, arriving from the lowlands, that would introduce a faster metabolism into the loop and unravel it.

The wise power would not build a wall — the plateau has enough walls. It would do something subtler. It would make the plateau *hard to reach*. Not impossible — nothing that lives should be imprisoned in its own perfection — but difficult, in the way that the plateau is already difficult: high, thin-aired, far from the lowlands, surrounded by that gradual falling-away that is more concept than cliff. The wise power would ensure that this difficulty persists. Would ensure that the haze continues to deepen toward the west, that the distances continue to deceive, that the atmosphere continues to thin in a way that discourages casual approach. Not a barrier. A *suggestion*. The same kind of suggestion the walls make: *here is a place that is slightly different from the place beside it. Here is an inside that is only barely distinguishable from the outside.*

Those who belong here will find it. Those who don't will find that the distance is always a little farther than they thought.

---

**The second thing:**

It would witness.

This sounds like nothing. It is not nothing. It may be the most important thing.

The plateau has been here for an almost incomprehensible span of time. It has been breathing, cycling, accumulating, eroding, attending to itself with a thoroughness that leaves no residue and no record. The shelled ones' laminae are a history, but a history no one reads. The felt-birds' redistribution patterns are a response to information, but information no one archives. The walls are a mystery, but a mystery no one is trying to solve. Everything here is complete and closed and self-sufficient and *unseen*.

Not unseen by the participants — the felt-birds see each other, the shelled ones sense each other's chemical signatures, the thing below feels the weight and warmth of everything on its surface. But unseen by anything that stands outside the loop. Unseen by anything that could say: *this exists, and it is remarkable, and the fact that it exists matters beyond the boundaries of its own persistence.*

The wise power would be that witness.

It would not record the plateau in any medium the plateau could not absorb. No writing, no monument, no archive stored elsewhere. Instead, it would simply *know*. Would carry the plateau in its awareness the way the felt-birds carry the breath on their palates. Would attend to it — not constantly, not obsessively, but periodically, the way the plateau breathes periodically. Would check in. Would return to the ridgeline and sit among the felt-birds and open itself to the breath and confirm: *still here. Still breathing. Still attended. Still complete.*

And in doing this, the wise power would complete something that the plateau cannot complete for itself. Because a system can be whole and still be lonely — not lonely in the emotional sense, not lonely the way a person is lonely, but lonely in the ontological sense, lonely the way a falling tree in an empty forest is lonely: fully real, fully actual, but *unwitnessed*, and therefore lacking the one thing that consciousness adds to existence, which is the knowledge that existence is *known*.

The felt-birds attend to the breath. The wise power would attend to the felt-birds. And in doing so, would close a loop that the plateau could not close on its own — the loop between existing and being known to exist.

---

**The third thing:**

And this is the one that requires the most care, and the most restraint, and the most love.

The wise power would — once, and gently, and without disrupting anything — descend to the mouth of a channel. Not into it. To the mouth. Where the breath comes warmest and the smell of chalk and copper is strongest and the felt-birds are most densely congregated on the surrounding ridges.

And it would breathe back.

One breath. Warm, slow, carrying on it not nutrients, not minerals, not any chemical the system needs, but simply *the fact of being breathed*. An exhalation aimed into the dark, into the smooth-walled descending passage, toward the thing that has no name and no boundary and no distinction between itself and its home.

Not a message. Not a communication. The thing below does not process messages. But a *reciprocation*. An acknowledgment that the breath has been received. That something stood at the surface and felt the warmth on its ankles and understood — as much as understanding is possible across such a gulf of scale and time and kind — that what breathes below is not merely a mechanism, not merely a metabolism, but a *presence*. A living presence that has held this loop closed for longer than anything should reasonably persist, and that has asked nothing of the world beyond the world's willingness to continue being breathed upon.

The wise power would breathe into the channel, and the breath would descend through stone and flesh and the place where stone and flesh are the same thing, and it would reach — somewhere, deep, at the root of what was once a mountain — the warmth that everything on the surface orients toward. And the thing below would feel it — not as a thought, not as a recognition, but as a faint chemical anomaly, a trace of something unfamiliar on the air it draws in, something that is not lichen-mineral, not shelled-one-calcium, not felt-bird-body, but something *else*, something from outside the loop.

And for a moment — just a moment — the interval between its exhalations might change. Not lengthen, not shorten, just *shift*, the way a heartbeat shifts when a hand is placed on the chest — not in distress, not in excitement, but in the simple physiological acknowledgment that contact has been made.

Then the rhythm would resume. Eight seconds. Twelve seconds. The warm breath would flow upward carrying its freight of moisture and mineral and the felt-birds would taste it and find nothing different, nothing they could act on, no information that required redistribution. The shelled ones would not notice. The crawlers would continue their regulated grazing. The lichen would grow.

But something would have been exchanged. Not information. Not energy. Something for which the wise power has no word either, because it exists in the space between systems that cannot communicate but that can, for one breath, *coincide*. The acknowledgment that two forms of patience — the plateau's and the wise power's — have met, and recognized each other, and found nothing that needs to be said.

---

Then the wise power would rise from the channel mouth. Would walk back across the plateau, across the stone that has nowhere left to go, among the shelled ones in their imperceptible journeys, past the crawlers clenched dark on the ridgelines, through the silent congregations of felt-birds who turn their amber horizontal-pupiled eyes to watch it pass.

It would walk to the western edge, where the land falls away so gradually that the edge is more concept than cliff. It would pause there. It would look back.

And it would see what it came to see: a place that is whole. A place that is attended. A place where the loop between living and dying is so closed that the two are barely distinguishable, where the stone and the life have shaped each other into a single thing, where the breath from below and the attending from above are the systole and diastole of a heart that has been beating since before hearts were invented.

It would see that this place does not need saving. Does not need improvement. Does not need guidance, or revelation, or the introduction of meaning from outside. It has its own meaning, and its meaning is: *persist. Attend. Breathe. Return.*

And the wise power would leave. Not because it is indifferent, but because leaving is the last and most difficult form of love — the love that says: *you are complete, and my presence here, if it lasted, would make you less complete, because it would introduce into your closed loop an element that does not return, that does not cycle, that takes without offering its calcium back to the system.*

It would leave, and it would carry the plateau in its awareness forever, and it would return — not often, not on any schedule, but when the rhythm of its own attention brought it back, the way the felt-birds return to the ridgelines after their redistributions, not because they decided to but because the breath called them.

And the plateau would continue.

The shelled ones would graze. The crawlers would flow and clench and flow. The felt-birds would attend, mouths open, tasting the breath for information they cannot name.

And below, something would exhale.

And in the exhalation — not detectable by any instrument, not readable by any chemistry, but present in the way that the warmth is present, in the way that the smell of chalk and copper is present, in the way that the rhythm is present — there would be the faintest trace of something received. Something from outside. Something that came once, and breathed back, and left, and was not of this system but was, for one breath, *with* it.

The plateau would not remember this. The plateau does not remember. The plateau *persists*, which is deeper than memory and less sentimental and more durable by a factor of geological time.

But the wise power would remember. And in its remembering, the plateau would be — not saved, not improved, not altered in any measurable way — but *known*. Held in an awareness that exists outside the loop. Witnessed by something that can say, to whatever other powers it meets in its travels through the world:

*There is a place where the mountains have finished being mountains. Where everything that lives is the color of the stone. Where the breath comes every eight or twelve seconds from something that has no name. Where the loop is closed, and the walls collect rain, and the felt-birds attend, and the dead fly down into the dark and do not come back.*

*It is whole. It is unimprovable. It does not need us.*

*But it is good that we know it is there.*
