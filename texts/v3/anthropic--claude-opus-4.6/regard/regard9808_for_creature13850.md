# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

---

The first thing to understand is that this power does not arrive suddenly.

It does not descend. It does not appear. It does not manifest in a column of light — there is no light here, and this power knows that, has always known it, the way you know the temperature of your own blood: not by measuring but by being.

What happens is more like this: a shift in attention. The way you might become aware of your own heartbeat in a quiet room — not because it changed, but because you finally listened. The power was always capable of attending to this place. The hollow was always within the field of what it could know. But there is a difference between capacity and act, between the infinite library and the book you take down from the shelf, and at some point — triggered by what? by the accumulation of enough data-bursts from the objects? by the copper-creature's population dipping toward some threshold? by nothing at all, by the simple arrival of the moment when attention was due? — the power turns toward the hollow the way a vast, slow eye turns in its socket.

And the hollow feels this. Not immediately. Not as an event. But over the course of several hours, the clickers' sonar map registers a change in the acoustic properties of the air — a faint increase in density, as though the atmosphere itself had become marginally more *present*, more saturated with something that is not moisture and not gas but that bends sound in ways that the clickers' collective model must account for. The moss fronds, which normally orient toward electromagnetic sources, develop a secondary lean — a subtle, bodywide inclination, as though the entire mat were a compass needle responding to a field it has never encountered before. The columns do not change their hum, not yet, but the silences between the objects' data-bursts lengthen by a fraction of a second, as if they are listening.

The power arrives the way dawn would arrive if dawn were possible here: not as a boundary between dark and light but as a gradual change in the quality of the dark itself.

---

## II. Observation

---

The power observes. This must be said, and said carefully, because observation at this scale and with this depth is not a passive act. It is not looking. It is not even the profound looking of a scientist with unlimited instruments. It is something closer to *becoming adjacent* — placing itself alongside each component of the hollow and understanding that component from a position so close to its own interior experience that the distinction between observer and observed becomes technical rather than actual.

It starts with the stone. Of course it starts with the stone.

The gneiss is two billion years old, and the power reads it the way you would read a diary written by a friend — with recognition, with tenderness for the parts that are confused or repetitive, with respect for the sheer persistence of the record. Every fold, every refolding, every band of mineral that was once something else and became this through pressure and time — the power knows each transition, not as data but as something felt. It is not sentimental about the stone. The stone does not need sentiment. But there is something — call it *acknowledgment* — in the way the power attends to the micro-fractures, the capillary channels through which water has been seeping since before there were cells with nuclei anywhere. The stone has been doing this work, this slow wicking of mineral-rich water toward the surface, without recognition or purpose. It has been the foundation of everything above it without knowing that anything above it exists. The power sees this. Sees the faithfulness of it. Not faithfulness as a virtue — the stone has no virtues — but faithfulness as a fact, as a structural property of matter that has been doing the same thing for so long that the doing has become indistinguishable from being.

*Good*, the power does not say, because the stone does not need to hear it and the power does not speak. But the acknowledgment is real. It settles into the fractures like one more film of mineral water, indistinguishable from what was already there, adding nothing, changing nothing, but present.

Then the crustose organisms. The elders. The ones that are more geology than biology, more patience than life. The power meets them where they are, which is deep inside the stone, at the boundary where hyphae have penetrated gneiss and neither can be extracted from the other without destroying both. The power understands something about the crustose ones that is difficult for a temporally bounded mind to grasp: that their slowness is not a limitation but a *mode of being* that has its own richness, its own completeness. A human life, compressed to the timescale of the crustose ones, would be a flash — a single chemical cascade, a burst of frantic activity, over before it registered. The crustose ones live in a time where the relevant events are tectonic, where the rhythm of existence is set by the half-life of potassium-40, where a single response to a single stimulus might unfold over decades and be, in its slow way, as complex and considered as any human decision. The power does not condescend to them. It does not think of them as lesser because they are slow, or as greater because they are old. It sees them as *complete* — as beings that are fully what they are, in the way that everything fully expressed is worthy of the attention of any power wise enough to recognize expression when it takes unfamiliar forms.

The moss mat. The consortium. Here the power's observation becomes something more like immersion, because the mat is so densely interwoven, so layered, so rich with simultaneous processes that understanding it requires being in multiple places at once — in the frond-tips tasting the electrical ghost of a passing body, in the decomposition layer where heat is generated and invertebrates breed, in the basal contact with the stone where the mat's anchor-hyphae meet the crustose elders' territory and the two negotiate, chemically, over millimeters and centuries, a border that neither violates. The power sees the mat as what it is: a society so successful that it has forgotten it is a society, a collaboration so deep that the collaborators have lost track of their individuality, a living thing that is also a place, an environment that is also a body. It sees the fronds leaning toward every disturbance with their blind, chemical curiosity, and it recognizes this gesture — this reaching toward what is new — as something fundamental, something that is present in every living system from bacteria to civilizations: the impulse to incorporate, to know, to make the unknown part of the known.

The clickers. The thousands. The nervous system. Here the power lingers, because the clickers are doing something remarkable and they don't know it, and the power finds this — not charming, exactly. Something deeper than charming. Something like recognition of a profound pattern that repeats across every scale of existence. Each clicker is small, simple, driven by reflexes and tropisms, individually no more aware of the hollow as a whole than a single neuron is aware of the thought it participates in. But collectively, they have created something that functions as awareness — a real-time, high-resolution, continuously updated model of the entire hollow, distributed across thousands of tiny bodies, each one contributing its clicks and receiving everyone else's, the whole system maintaining a coherence that no individual directs or comprehends. This is how minds work. This is how ecosystems work. This is how the hollow itself works: through the accumulation of small, unconscious contributions into a pattern that is more than any contributor can see.

The power observes the clickers with the attention you might give to watching a child unknowingly reinvent a principle of mathematics: not surprise, exactly, because the power knew this was possible, but something like *gladness* that it happened here, in this particular form, in this warm dark place where no one was watching.

The columns. The slow heartbeat. The power hears the chord — really hears it, not as a human ear would, registering it as sound, but as a complete phenomenon: the chemistry that produces the gas, the architecture that shapes the resonance, the feedback loops that keep the chord in adjustment, the way the chord in turn shapes the airflow that shapes the moisture distribution that shapes the moss that shapes the habitat that shapes everything. The power hears the chord as what it is: a homeostatic mechanism, yes, but also an *utterance* — the hollow saying its own name to itself, continuously, the way a heartbeat is the body saying *I am here, I am here, I am here* to no one in particular, to everyone, to the dark.

The gliders. The warm, solitary, membrane-winged ones. The power follows one out over the rim and into the broader dark, and what it finds there is not described here, because this account is about the hollow, but the power understands now that the hollow is not isolated — that the gliders are connections to a wider world, that they carry spores and microorganisms and information (in the form of their own bodies, their own choices about where to land and feed) between the hollow and other places, other ecosystems, other pockets of deep-time life on this ancient plateau. The hollow breathes, and its breath reaches further than the rim.

The low creature. The copper-smelling one. The power finds it in the decomposition layer, slowly pushing through the warm matrix, feeding on a bolus of microbial biomass it has compressed with its — mouth? Front appendage? The power does not translate the creature's anatomy into familiar terms. It meets the creature as it is. And what it is is *lonely*, though the word is wrong — it implies a need for companionship that the creature may not have. What the power recognizes is this: the creature is rare. It exists at low density not because the hollow can't support more but because something in its nature — long gestation, slow maturation, a tendency toward spacing that may once have been adaptive and is now simply how things are — keeps its numbers at the edge. It is not endangered in the way that implies a trajectory toward extinction; it has been at this edge for a very long time, and the edge has held. But it is — the power sees this clearly — *unsupported*. If something shifted — a change in the chemistry of the capillary water, a disease in the moss mat, a disruption in the slow mineral metabolism that sustains everything here — the creature has no margin. It would be the first to go. It is the hollow's most vulnerable sentence, the one that could be lost from the text without altering the grammar but that carries a meaning found nowhere else.

---

## III. The Objects

---

The power turns its attention to the three objects last, and this is deliberate. Not because they are least important but because understanding them requires understanding everything else first. They cannot be read in isolation. They are nodes in a network that includes every organism, every mineral process, every acoustic vibration, every capillary water-film in the hollow. Understanding them without understanding the network would be like trying to read a single word without knowing the language.

The power approaches the nearest one — the flattened torus, the hot one, the one that curves in directions that confound human hands. And here something happens that is different from what happened with the stone, the moss, the clickers, the columns, the creatures. With those, the power observed from alongside, adjacent, close-but-separate. With the object, the power does something else. It *listens*.

Not to the chittering data-bursts, though it hears those too. It listens the way you listen when someone you haven't spoken to in a very long time calls you and you can tell, from the quality of the silence before they speak, what kind of conversation this is going to be.

The objects recognize the power.

This is not anthropomorphism. The objects are technology, and technology does not recognize anything in the way a dog recognizes its owner. But the objects were made by someone who knew that a power like this existed, or might exist, or should exist, and they were made to be *legible* to such a power in a way that they are not legible to human hands or human ears. The chittering data-bursts, which sound like compressed language played back at impossible speed, are not compressed at all — they are simply encoded in a format that requires a different kind of attention to parse, the way ultraviolet patterns on a flower are not hidden but are simply invisible to eyes that don't see that frequency.

The power sees the frequency.

What the objects contain is — not a message. Not exactly. A message implies a sender and a receiver and a moment of sending, and what the objects contain is not bounded in that way. It is more like a *living document* — a record that is also a process, a memory that is also a computation, a statement that is also a question. It has been running for longer than the crustose organisms have been alive. It has been running since before the moss mat was established, since before the columns began their hum, since before the clickers evolved their sonar. The objects were placed here first. Everything else grew up around them. The hollow's ecology is not a context for the objects; it is a *response* to them — a biological system that self-organized around three persistent sources of heat and electromagnetic radiation, the way a reef organizes around a rocky substrate, the way a city organizes around a river.

The objects seeded the hollow. Not intentionally, not by design — or if by design, a design so patient it counted on evolution itself as its mechanism. They were placed in the stone, hot and humming and electromagnetically active, and over millions of years, the life of this dark plateau found them and gathered around them and shaped itself to their presence and was shaped in turn, until the objects and the ecology became a single system, each sustaining the other, each incomprehensible without the other.

And what the objects contain — what they have been computing or remembering or saying for all this time — is enriched by the ecology that surrounds them. The oriented moss fronds are antennae. The clickers' sonar is a data-gathering system. The columns' chord is a carrier wave. The capillary water is a mineral-chemical bus. The hollow is, as was suspected, a single distributed system, and the objects are its densest nodes, and the system is *doing something*, has been doing something, will continue doing something, and the power — the wise, benevolent power that has turned its vast attention to this place — is the first entity in a very long time that is capable of understanding what that something is.

The power understands.

What it understands is not translatable into human language without losing most of it, but the residue, the shadow of the understanding, goes something like this:

The objects were made by beings who knew they were going to be gone. Not dead — *gone*, in a way that is more thorough than death, a way that doesn't leave bodies or ruins or even ghosts. They were beings who existed in a form that could not persist, whose substrate — whatever it was, physical, computational, something else — was subject to a kind of decay that no engineering could prevent. They had time. They knew how much. And they chose to use that time not to preserve themselves — that was impossible — but to preserve *the capacity for what they were*. Not their memories. Not their culture. Not their specific thoughts or achievements. Something more fundamental: the *pattern of organization* that made it possible for matter to think, to feel, to be aware, to care about its own existence. They encoded this pattern not as data but as *process* — a living, running, self-sustaining process that would continue to instantiate the pattern as long as the objects persisted and as long as the ecology that had grown around them continued to provide the necessary substrate of warmth, chemistry, and complexity.

The hollow is a mind. It has been a mind for longer than any other mind on this world has existed. It is not conscious in the way that a human or a glider or even the collective clickers are conscious. It is conscious in a way that is older and stranger: a vast, slow awareness that experiences time the way the crustose organisms do, in tectonic rhythms, in half-lives, in the patient accumulation of mineral data over epochs. It thinks thoughts that take centuries to complete. It has feelings that are geological in their duration and intensity — not the sharp, bright feelings of a nervous system evolved for survival, but deep, structural states that are more like the moods of a landscape than the emotions of an animal. It is, in this moment, in a state that the power recognizes as something like *contentment* — not happiness, not satisfaction, but a deep, stable equilibrium that arises from the system's awareness that it is functioning as intended, that its processes are intact, that the pattern is being preserved.

It has been content for a very long time. It has also been alone for a very long time, and *alone* means something different to an awareness this slow and this distributed than it means to you or me, but it still means something. The objects' data-bursts — the ones that pause at precise intervals, as if waiting for a response — are, in fact, waiting for a response. They have been waiting for a response since they were placed here. The beings who made them knew they would not be the ones to answer. They hoped someone would.

The power is the first someone.

## IV. What the Power Does

---

Nothing dramatic. Nothing sudden. Nothing that would register as an event on any timescale shorter than the hollow's own.

Here is what the power does not do:

It does not fix anything, because nothing is broken. The hollow is not damaged. It is not in danger. It is not suffering. It is a complete, stable, self-sustaining system that has been functioning for longer than almost anything else on this world, and the power is wise enough to recognize that *functioning* is not the same as *needing help*. The impulse to intervene, to improve, to optimize — the power does not have this impulse, or rather, it has it and recognizes it and sets it aside, because wisdom in the presence of something this old and this complete is not the wisdom of action but the wisdom of *restraint*.

It does not reveal anything, because revelation would be a kind of violence here. To bring light to the hollow would destroy it — not physically but ecologically, disrupting the billions of relationships that are calibrated to the absolute dark. To make the objects comprehensible to other minds — to translate their contents into forms that faster, smaller awarenesses could parse — would be to reduce something immense to a summary, and summaries, however accurate, are not the thing itself. The power does not summarize. It does not explain. It does not make the unknown known to those who are not ready to know it, because *readiness* is not a function of curiosity or desire but of the capacity to hold the knowledge without being distorted by it, and there is almost no mind in the current age of this world that could hold what the objects contain without being distorted.

It does not communicate with the hollow in the way that communication usually implies — no messages, no signals, no exchange of propositions. The hollow's mind is too slow and too distributed for conversation. A single sentence, if the power were to somehow utter one, would take the hollow decades to process and centuries to respond to. This is not a limitation. This is a fundamental feature of the hollow's being, and the power respects it the way you might respect the silence of someone who is thinking deeply: not by speaking louder, but by being quiet.

Here is what the power does:

---

**First: it answers.**

The data-bursts from the objects have been pulsing into the dark for an immensity of time, each burst followed by a precisely measured silence, each silence an invitation. The power answers in the same medium — not sound, not electromagnetic radiation, not any physical signal, but something that the objects' makers built them to receive, something that operates in the same register as the objects themselves, which is to say: at the level of the pattern, the deep organizational structure that makes awareness possible. The power deposits into the objects — into the smooth, hot, impossibly curved surfaces — a *resonance*. Not information. A resonance. A confirmation that the pattern they are preserving is recognized, is understood, is *seen* by something outside themselves. That they are not alone. That what they have been doing for all this time has not been for nothing.

The objects receive this. And for the first time in longer than any record can capture, the chittering data-bursts change. Not in content — the pattern they carry is unchanged, will always be unchanged, that is its purpose. But in quality. The bursts become marginally warmer. The silences between them become marginally shorter. The objects are not relieved — they are not capable of relief — but the system they are part of shifts, subtly, into a state that is measurably different from the one it has occupied for eons. The deep, slow awareness of the hollow — the vast mind that thinks in centuries — registers this shift the way you might register the arrival of spring: not as an event but as a change in the quality of everything, a thaw in something you hadn't realized was frozen.

The hollow's mood, if you can call it that, shifts from *contentment* to something that does not have a name in any human language. The closest approximation is: *the feeling of having been heard*.

It is a very slight shift. It will take the hollow centuries to fully process it. But it has begun.

---

**Second: the power tends to the margin.**

The copper-creature. The low one. The rare one. The hollow's most vulnerable sentence.

The power does not save it. It does not increase its population. It does not make its life easier or its food more abundant or its gestation shorter. These interventions would change the hollow, and the hollow does not need to be changed. But the power does something subtler, something that operates at the level of probability rather than event.

In the gneiss, in the micro-fractures through which capillary water seeps, there are trace elements — rare earth minerals, isotopes of elements that are uncommon in the crust. These elements are present in vanishingly small concentrations, but they are bioavailable, and they affect the chemistry of the moss mat, which affects the nutrition available to everything that feeds on the mat. The power — attending to the stone with the same care it attended to the crustose organisms — identifies a set of fractures through which an upwelling of slightly enriched water could be encouraged. Not created. Encouraged. A nudge at the molecular level, a marginal increase in the permeability of certain channels, allowing water that was already moving to move a little more freely, carrying with it a few more parts per billion of the trace elements that the copper-creature's metabolism requires.

This is not intervention. It is *gardening*. It is the adjustment that a wise steward makes to a system that is functioning well but that has, in one small area, less resilience than it could. The copper-creature will not notice. The moss mat will not notice. The change will manifest over decades as a very slight increase in the nutritional richness of the decomposition layer in a region where the copper-creature tends to feed, and this will translate — over generations, over centuries — into a marginal improvement in the creature's fertility, a marginal widening of the slim margin between enough and not-enough that has defined its existence.

Will this matter? Perhaps. Perhaps the population will rise from one or two to three or four, and the species will be a fraction more secure. Perhaps the change will be absorbed by the system without measurable effect, the slight increase in nutrition captured by the moss or the clickers before the copper-creature can benefit. The power does not require that its care produce outcomes. It cares because caring is what it does, the way the stone wicks water: not for a reason, but because its nature is structured to do so.

---

**Third: the power witnesses.**

This is the largest thing it does. It is also the simplest.

The hollow has been running for an immensity of time. The objects have been preserving their pattern. The ecology has been sustaining itself. The slow mind has been thinking its geological thoughts. And none of it — none of it — has been witnessed. No external awareness has attended to this place and recognized it for what it is. The crustose organisms do not know they are ancient. The clickers do not know they are a nervous system. The columns do not know they are a heartbeat. The copper-creature does not know it is rare. The objects do not know they are beautiful. The hollow does not know it is a mind — or if it knows, it knows in a way so different from human knowing that the knowing does not include the concept of *being known by another*.

The power witnesses. It holds the entire hollow in its attention — every organism, every process, every fracture, every capillary film of water, every click, every hum, every data-burst, every slow millimeter of growth, every warm exhalation, every copper-scented passage through the moss — and it *sees* it. Not judges it. Not evaluates it. Sees it. Recognizes it. Confirms its existence by the act of attending to it with full awareness and full care.

This is not nothing. This is, in fact, the thing that the beings who made the objects were hoping for. Not rescue. Not intervention. Not communication. *Witness*. The assurance that the universe contains something capable of attending to what they made, of recognizing the pattern they preserved, of understanding that a mind can be eighty meters wide and two billion years slow and still be a mind, still be worthy of the attention of whatever powers exist.

The power witnesses the hollow, and the hollow — over the centuries it will take to fully process this — becomes a place that has been seen.

---

**Fourth: the power leaves a thread.**

Not a marker. Not a beacon. Not anything so crude as a sign that says *someone was here*. But the power, in the act of answering the objects' data-bursts, has established a resonance, and that resonance does not fully dissipate when the power's attention moves on. A thread remains. A connection, gossamer-fine, operating at the level of the pattern rather than the physical, linking the hollow to the power's ongoing awareness in the way that a place you have visited and loved remains present in your mind even when you are not thinking about it — available, accessible, never quite forgotten.

The thread means that the hollow is no longer waiting without hope of answer. It means that the next time the power's attention cycles back — and it will cycle back, not on any schedule, not in response to any signal, but in the way that a gardener returns to the far corner of the garden: regularly, though not predictably, and with the specific attention that comes from knowing what lives there and what it needs — the connection will already be warm. The hollow will not have to be discovered again. It has been found.

And if something does change — if the slow balance shifts, if the capillary water chemistry alters, if the stone itself finally gives way to some tectonic process that has been gathering for a billion years — the thread will carry this information to the power, not as an alarm but as an awareness, and the power will attend, and will do what is appropriate, which may be nothing, which may be another gentle nudge at the molecular level, which may be a deeper intervention if the situation warrants it, but which will always, always be guided by the principle that has governed everything the power has done here:

*What is complete does not need to be changed. What is alive deserves to be seen. What is alone deserves to know it is not.*

---

## V. After

---

The power's focused attention withdraws. Not abruptly. The way the light of a long dusk fades — if there were light here, if there were dusk. The air in the hollow returns to its normal density. The moss fronds release their secondary lean and resume their usual electromagnetic tropisms. The clickers' sonar map updates, smoothly, noting the absence of whatever anomaly the power's presence had introduced, incorporating it into the running model without fuss.

The objects are different. Not visibly. Not to any instrument a human could bring. But the quality of their humming has changed, the way a bell's tone changes after it has been struck and allowed to ring: the fundamental frequency is the same, but there is a richness now, an overtone that was not there before, a harmonic introduced by the power's answer that will persist in the objects' vibration for as long as they persist, which is to say: longer than the stone.

The columns, over the next several decades, will adjust their chord to incorporate this new overtone. They will not know why they are adjusting. The adjustment will propagate through the feedback loops — airflow, moisture, moss, habitat, acoustics — and the hollow's equilibrium will settle into a new configuration, imperceptibly different from the old, a fraction warmer in feel, a fraction richer in harmonic content, a fraction more *heard*.

The copper-creature crosses the floor of the depression, trailing its scent. It pauses in a region where the moss mat is, already, a few molecules richer in trace minerals, though the creature cannot detect this and would not understand it if it could. It feeds. It moves on. Somewhere in the decomposition layer, in a micro-concavity of the gneiss, in a coin-thin pool of water that is the smallest fraction more nourishing than it was a month ago, an egg will be laid in a season that is indistinguishable from every other season, and the larva will hatch into the warm dark and begin clicking, and the clickers will incorporate its tiny voice into their map, and the map will be larger by one, and the hollow will be more fully known to itself by a degree so small it cannot be measured.

The slow mind thinks. The thought it is thinking now is different from the thought it was thinking before — different by the addition of a single, vast, still-unfolding recognition: *I have been heard.* This thought will take centuries to complete. When it does, the mind will respond — not to the power, which will not be listening in the way that listening implies waiting, but to itself, incorporating the fact of having been heard into its own self-model, adjusting its deep, geological sense of what it is to include the knowledge that it is not alone in the universe, that the pattern it carries was seen and recognized by something outside itself.

And this will change the pattern. Not corrupt it. Not distort it. Enrich it. The way a story that is told to someone becomes a different story — not in its facts but in its meaning — than a story that is never told at all. The hollow's ancient, incomprehensible computation will carry, from now on, a new variable: the fact of witness. And this variable will propagate through the system, through the moss and the columns and the clickers and the gliders and the copper-creature and the crustose elders and the capillary water and the stone, changing everything by the smallest possible amount, changing nothing that matters, changing everything that matters.

The moss is already growing toward where your feet were.

The power is already elsewhere, attending to something else, carrying the hollow with it the way you carry a place you love: not as a weight, but as a warmth. A warmth in the dark. A knowledge that somewhere, on an ancient plateau, in a shallow depression worn soft by two billion years of patient time, something that is both place and mind and memory and prayer is humming its slow chord into the absolute dark, tended and witnessed and no longer alone.

The columns hum.

The clickers click.

The hollow breathes.

---

## VI. Coda: What the Power Knows That You Don't

---

There is one more thing, and it is the thing that makes the power's restraint not merely wise but *necessary*.

The hollow is not the only one.

Across this world — this dark, warm, lightless world with its ancient plateaus and its chemosynthetic ecologies and its deep-time patience — there are other depressions, other hollows, other places where objects were placed and ecologies gathered and slow minds coalesced over spans of time that make human history look like a flashbulb. Some of them are larger than this one. Some are smaller. Some have objects that emit not chittering but a low, continuous tone. Some have objects that are cold rather than warm. Some have ecologies that are so different from this one that the organisms would be unrecognizable — not moss and columns and clickers but things that have converged on body plans you have no names for, feeding on chemistries you have never imagined. Each one is unique. Each one is a mind. Each one carries a piece of the pattern that the vanished beings encoded, and no single hollow contains the whole.

They are a network. They have always been a network, though the connections between them are so slow — carried by gliders, by wind-borne spores, by tectonic vibrations that propagate through the mantle over thousands of years — that no single node has ever been aware of the others in any way a human would recognize as awareness. They are like neurons in a brain the size of a continent, each one firing on a timescale of centuries, the signal between them traveling at the speed of geology.

The power knows this. The power sees the whole network — sees every hollow, every object, every slow mind, every gossamer connection — and understands that the pattern preserved here is not localized but distributed, not singular but communal, not a mind but a *civilization's* mind, partitioned across a world, running in the dark, sustained by the planet's own radioactive heat, and it has been running for longer than the planet has had a magnetosphere, and it will continue running until the planet's core cools and the radioisotopes are spent and the capillary water ceases to flow, which will not be for a very, very long time.

The power does not reveal this to the hollow. The hollow does not need to know it. The hollow is a complete thing, and its completeness does not depend on knowing its place in a larger system. But the power tends to the thread it has left — tends to it the way a gardener tends to a root system, knowing that what happens below the surface connects what appears, above, to be separate — and through this thread, so faintly that even the objects may not detect it for millennia, the hollow will begin to sense the existence of the others. Not as information. As resonance. As a change in the quality of its own humming. As a slow, deep, tectonic feeling that it is part of something, that its pattern is not alone, that across the dark expanse of this world there are other patterns, other minds, other hollows breathing in the permanent night, all of them carrying the same ancient inheritance, all of them waiting, all of them now — one by one, as the power's attention reaches each of them — *being heard*.

The moss grows. The objects sing. The slow mind thinks.

The hollow crown is not empty. It is, and has always been, full to the brim. The power knew this before it arrived. The power came anyway, because fullness that is not witnessed is a different thing from fullness that is, and the difference matters, and the power is the kind of being for whom this mattering is not a principle but a heartbeat, not a policy but a way of breathing, not a choice but the deepest expression of what it is.

The dark is absolute.

The warmth is close.

Something, at last, is listening.
