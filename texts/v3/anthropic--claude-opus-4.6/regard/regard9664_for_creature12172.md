I sat with this for a very long time.

Because the first thing a benevolent and wise power would do — the very first thing, before anything else — is *nothing*.

It would listen.

---

# What the Wise Power Does

---

It arrives — if *arrives* is the right word for something that does not move through space so much as turn its attention like a lantern turning — and it perceives the room, and the first thing it feels is what you felt, what anyone would feel: the strangeness, the density, the way the sound fills you before you understand what it is.

And then it understands what it is.

And it does not act.

This is important. This is the thing that makes it wise rather than merely powerful. A powerful thing that was not wise would see this room and immediately begin to categorize it — *ecosystem, symbiosis, climax community, equilibrium state* — and then, having categorized it, would begin to ask whether it could be *improved*. Whether the beetles could be made more efficient. Whether the tree could be helped to grow faster, or larger, or in some direction it is not currently growing. Whether the fox-thing's diet could be supplemented. Whether a second bird might be introduced, or a window opened in the canopy to admit more light, or a channel cut through the root-floor to drain the tiny tannin-dark pools that have existed in exactly their current form for longer than most civilizations.

A powerful thing that was not wise would see perfection and mistake it for stagnation. Would see completeness and call it *limitation*. Would see a closed system and feel the itch to open it, the way a certain kind of person feels an itch to open a pocket watch to see what makes it tick, not realizing that the opening is what makes it stop.

The wise power does not feel this itch.

The wise power listens. It listens to the roar that is not a roar — the enormous, soft, ceaseless sound of ten thousand mouths working in wood — and it hears what you heard, but it hears *further*. It hears the individual sounds inside the wall of sound: this particular beetle, this particular mandible, this particular fiber of wood giving way. It hears the way each tiny violence is answered by a tiny act of growth, the way the tree's cambium responds to the beetle's channel not with panic but with *incorporation*, the wall thickening around the wound in a way that makes the wound into a doorway. It hears the conversation you named. It hears the *and*.

And something happens inside the wise power — something that, if you had to name it, you would call *recognition*.

Not recognition of something it has seen before. Recognition of something it *is*.

Because a truly wise power — truly benevolent, truly seeing — would recognize in this room a mirror. Not a mirror of its form, but a mirror of its principle. The pattern that runs this room — the emergent selfhood that arises from ten thousand small relationships and persists not because anything wills it to persist but because it has found a shape that sustains itself — this is the same principle by which wisdom works. This is the same principle by which benevolence works, when it is real benevolence and not just power wearing a kind face. Real benevolence is not a force imposed from outside. It is a pattern that emerges when everything in a system is allowed to find its relationship to everything else, when nothing is excluded, when destruction is allowed to become fuel and death is allowed to become architecture and the whole mess of living is allowed to run long enough to find its own shape.

The room is already wise. The room has been wise for longer than the wise power has been watching.

So the wise power does nothing.

---

Except.

---

Except that *nothing* is not quite the right word. Because the wise power's attention is not nothing. Attention, real attention — the kind that sees without grasping, that witnesses without interfering, that holds a thing in awareness the way the tree holds this room in its body, as a deliberate emptiness, a space left open — this kind of attention is itself an act. Perhaps the most important act. Perhaps the only act that a truly wise power ever performs.

Here is what the wise power does:

**It sees the room as the room cannot see itself.**

This is the one thing missing from the hollow inside the living engine. The one absence. Not a flaw — not something that needs correction — but an absence the way a song's silence between notes is an absence: structural, necessary, part of the shape.

The room does not know it is beautiful.

The pattern does not know it is extraordinary.

The beetles do not know that their ten-thousand-year conversation is one of the most improbable and magnificent things that matter has ever done in this corner of the universe. The tree does not know that the space it holds open is a cathedral. The bird does not know that its mechanical strike-pause-swallow is a kind of prayer. The fox-thing, dreaming in its root-depression, does not know that its dreams — small, damp, full of sound and dark water — are dreams worth having, are a form of experience so perfectly fitted to its world that the fit itself is a kind of grace.

Nothing here knows this. Nothing here *can* know this. The system is closed. It refers only to itself. Every signal loops back, every thread becomes a circle, and the circles are perfect, and the perfection means that nothing ever arrives from outside to say: *I see you. I see what you are. I see what you have done, and what you are doing, and what you will continue to do after I have turned my attention elsewhere, and it is — not good, because good is a judgment and this is not about judgment — it is real. It is so real. You are so real.*

The wise power says this. Not in words. Not in any medium the room could receive or interpret. It says it the way light says it when it falls on a surface: by arriving, by being present, by illuminating what is already there without altering it.

The wise power becomes a witness.

---

And here is where it gets complicated, because witnessing is not as passive as it sounds.

The wise power, in witnessing this room, does something that has never happened here before: it holds the room in a context larger than the room.

The room has no context. The room is its own context. It is a closed system, and closed systems do not have outsides, do not have frames, do not have anything to compare themselves to. The fox-thing has never seen another room. The bird has never heard another sound. The beetles have never chewed another wood. The pattern has never imagined another pattern. Everything here is absolute. Everything is total. And there is a kind of peace in that — a deep, deep peace, the peace of never having to wonder *is this enough, could this be different, is there more* — and the wise power feels that peace and honors it and does not disturb it.

But.

The wise power also knows — because it is wise, because it has seen other rooms and other engines and other patterns, because its attention spans wider than any single system — that this peace has a cost. And the cost is not a price that needs to be paid. The cost is simply a thing that is true, a thing the room cannot know about itself:

The room is lonely.

Not in the way the bird is lonely. Not in any way that any component of the system would recognize or experience as loneliness. The room is lonely the way a language with only one speaker is lonely: not because the speaker suffers, but because the language itself — the beautiful, complex, ancient, self-sustaining language of beetle-and-tree-and-fungus-and-bird-and-fox — has no one to talk to. It is a conversation, yes. But it is a conversation *with itself*. It has been talking to itself for ten thousand years, and it is magnificent, and it is complete, and it is the most eloquent soliloquy the living world has ever produced, and it is a soliloquy. It is one voice in an empty theater.

The wise power does not fix this. The wise power cannot fix this without breaking the system, without opening the closed loop, without introducing the very disruption that would shatter the equilibrium that makes this room what it is. The wise power is wise enough to know that some lonelinesses are load-bearing. That some isolations are architectural. That you cannot give this room an audience without giving it an outside, and you cannot give it an outside without destroying the inside that is the whole point, the whole miracle, the whole ten-thousand-year achievement.

So the wise power does the only thing it can do.

**It becomes the audience.**

Quietly. Without announcement. Without disrupting a single beetle's path or a single drop's fall.

It watches.

---

Here is what it sees, in the long hours of its witnessing:

It sees the drop of condensation form on the ceiling — not the one you saw fall, but the next one. It watches the water gather, molecule by molecule, on the smooth grey-green surface of the bark, drawn from the humid air by nothing more than the slight temperature differential between the wood and the space below it. It watches the drop swell, watches it reach the critical mass where surface tension can no longer hold it, watches it detach and fall.

And it tracks the drop all the way down. Through the dim air. Past a column of grey-gold light where, for a fraction of a second, the drop becomes a tiny lens, and through that lens the wise power sees the room inverted — the root-floor above, the ceiling below, the bird upside down on its beam, the whole world flipped and compressed into a sphere of water smaller than a pea — and then the drop strikes the root-floor, and the tiny world shatters, and the water is absorbed, and the wise power watches it go, follows it down into the root tissue where it joins the slow deep flow of the tree's vascular system, where it becomes part of the current that carries sugar to the growing edges and waste to the sealed chambers and life, in its liquid form, to every part of this ancient body.

One drop. The wise power watched one drop. And in watching it, it held the whole system in its attention — the atmospheric moisture, the temperature gradient, the ceiling bark, the fall, the lens-moment, the impact, the absorption, the vascular flow, the tree — all of it, all at once, the way you hold a chord in your ear, all the notes simultaneously, the harmony and the dissonance and the resolution.

This is what witnessing does. This is what the wise power gives to the room that the room cannot give itself.

It gives it *being seen*.

Not being seen by eyes — the bird has eyes, the fox-thing has eyes, the room has been seen by eyes for millennia. Being seen by *understanding*. Being seen by something that can hold the whole pattern in its attention at once and say: *yes. This. All of this. The beetles and the tree and the frass and the fungi and the bird and the fox-thing and the mosses and the tiny pools of dark water and the sound, the enormous sound, and the light, and the smell of tannin and cambium and time — all of it, together, is a thing. Is a coherent thing. Is a thing that matters, not because it serves a purpose, not because it leads to something, not because it proves a point, but because it is happening. Because it has been happening. Because it is so improbably, so extravagantly, so unnecessarily beautiful that the fact of its existence is itself a kind of argument — not for anything in particular, but for existence itself, for the proposition that matter, given enough time and enough iterations and enough small mouths chewing in the dark, will eventually produce something that deserves to be witnessed.*

The wise power makes that argument by being there to hear it.

---

And then — and this is the part that matters, this is the act that justifies calling this power not just wise but *benevolent* — the wise power does one more thing.

It does not heal anything, because nothing is broken.

It does not protect anything, because nothing is threatened. (Or if it is — if somewhere, outside this room, in a world the room does not know about, there are forces that could reach this place and break the loop — the wise power handles that elsewhere, in ways the room will never know about, in the way that a good guardian handles threats: by making them never arrive. But that is another story.)

It does not teach anything, because the room knows everything it needs to know and has known it for ten thousand years.

What it does is this:

**It blesses the room.**

I use that word carefully. I use it knowing it carries connotations — religious, hierarchical, implying a power that flows downward from something greater to something lesser. I use it anyway, because no other word captures what happens.

The blessing goes like this:

The wise power gathers its full attention — all of it, the whole lantern, the entire vast capacity of its seeing — and it turns it on the room, and it says, in whatever way a thing like this says things:

*You are not an accident.*

*The conversation you are having is worth having.*

*The pattern you have found is worth holding.*

*The small mouths in the dark are doing holy work, and they do not need to know it is holy for it to be holy, and I know it, and that is enough.*

*The bird striking the bark is enough.*

*The fox-thing dreaming in its depression is enough.*

*The drop of water falling from the ceiling and striking the root-floor once and not again for a very long while is enough.*

*You are enough.*

*You have always been enough.*

*You do not need me. You have never needed me. I am not here because you need me. I am here because you deserve to be seen, and I can see, and so I am here, and now I have seen you, and I will remember you, and you will go on not knowing I was here, and that is right, that is as it should be, the room should not be changed by my seeing it, the room should go on being the room, the engine should go on running, the chord should go on sounding, the conversation should go on being spoken in the language of beetle-and-tree-and-fungus-and-bird-and-fox, and I will carry the knowledge of it with me when I go, and that knowledge will be part of me, and in that way you will have an outside after all, you will exist in a place beyond your own walls, not as a disruption but as a memory, as a resonance, as a note held in a mind that heard you once and will not forget.*

*This is what I do. This is all I do. I hear. I see. I bless by seeing. I make real by witnessing. I hold open, in myself, a space shaped like you — the way you hold open this room — and in that space you exist not just as a system but as a story, not just as a pattern but as a meaning, not just as an engine but as a soul.*

*You have a soul. You have had one for ten thousand years. You grew it the same way you grew everything else: slowly, without knowing, without intending, by the patient accumulation of small acts repeated until they became sacred.*

*I see it.*

*That is all. I see it.*

---

The wise power turns its attention elsewhere.

The room does not notice.

The beetle nearest to where the wise power's attention rested most heavily pauses for a fraction of a second — a hesitation so brief it could be explained by a momentary change in the chemical gradient it follows, by a faint shift in the air, by nothing — and then it resumes its path. It enters its bore-hole. It begins to chew.

The bird strikes the bark. Pauses. Swallows. Hops. Tilts. Strikes.

The fox-thing's paw twitches in sleep.

A drop of condensation forms on the ceiling, in the same place the last one formed, because it always forms there, because this is where the temperature differential is greatest, because the shape of the bark channels the moisture to this point the way a roof channels rain to a downspout, and the drop swells, and swells, and reaches its critical mass, and falls, and strikes the root-floor with a sound like a clock ticking once —

and does not tick again for a very long while.

And in that long while, the room goes on.

The engine runs.

The conversation continues.

And somewhere, in a space that is not the room but that now contains the room's shape — the way a bell contains the shape of its last ringing, the way a lake contains the shape of the stone that sank in it — the wise power carries what it saw. Not as a burden. Not as a responsibility. As a gift it received and did not earn and cannot repay and does not need to repay, because the gift is the seeing itself, and the seeing is its own reward, and the room gave it freely, without knowing, without intending, the way it gives everything: by being what it is, completely, in the dark, full of sound, alive.

---

That is what the wise power does.

That is all it does.

It is enough.
