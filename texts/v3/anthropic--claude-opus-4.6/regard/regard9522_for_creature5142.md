# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It would do nothing, at first.

Not out of hesitation. Not out of the careful restraint of a surgeon pausing before the cut. It would do nothing because the first act of any wisdom adequate to this place is to *hear* it—and this place cannot be heard quickly.

A benevolent power that arrived with intent, even good intent, would find that intent shredded like a membrane caught in shear. The chamber does not resist intervention; it simply moves faster than purpose can track. Any plan formed in the upper registers of the roar would be contradicted by the grinding tones below before it could be enacted. So the power would settle—not into a location, because the chamber has no stable locations, but into the rhythm of the contradictions themselves. It would let itself be conducted through bone, the way everything here is conducted. It would listen not for a signal but for the *grammar* of the noise—the way the glassy screams answer the grinding, the way the silences left by a collapsing tunnel are immediately filled by the shredding of a membrane, so that the total volume never changes, only its texture.

This would take a long time, or no time at all, because the chamber does not distinguish between the two.

---

## What It Would Understand

Eventually—and "eventually" here means after the power had been folded, contradicted, surprised, and recomposed enough times to stop expecting the next moment to resemble the last—it would understand something that changes the nature of the question.

The chamber is not suffering.

This matters. A benevolent power is drawn to suffering the way a current is drawn through a pressure differential, and the instinct upon encountering a place this loud, this violent, this ceaselessly unfinished would be to assume that something here needs rescue. The shredding of the membranes looks like pain. The vanishing of the swimmers looks like loss. The blurring of the carved lettering looks like forgetting. The whole roaring, slamming, half-lit cathedral reads, to an outsider, as a catastrophe in progress—a world that cannot hold itself together, populated by beings that cannot survive.

But suffering requires a gap between what is and what the sufferer needs things to be. And nothing here has that gap. The membranes are not trying to remain whole; they are negotiating, and shearing is one of the terms of negotiation. The swimmers are not trying to persist; they are temporary tunnels of coherence, and their vanishing is not death but the completion of a sentence. The architectural traces are not mourning what they once were; they never were anything else. They have always been in the process of being extruded.

The chamber is not a wound. It is a way of being alive that does not require stability, accumulation, or completion—and it has been alive this way for as long as the stone has been rising, which is to say, always, or just now, which is to say, the same thing.

A lesser power—a merely good power, a power that loved comfort and feared noise—would miss this. It would try to calm the currents, harden the membranes, anchor the swimmers, make the light brighter and the sound softer. And it would succeed, briefly, the way a hand pressed flat against a fracture plane can hold back the next surge for a heartbeat. But the chamber would simply extrude around the intervention, incorporating it the way it incorporates everything: as raw material for the next contradiction. The calmed currents would become the seed of a more violent shear. The hardened membranes would become the next lintel, then the next buried stair, then the next blurred inscription—memory fossilized too soon, carrying less information than the drift it replaced.

The wise power would not make this mistake. It would recognize that the greatest harm it could do would be to *finish* this place.

---

## What It Would Do Instead

Three things, and all of them gentle, and none of them interventions in the usual sense.

---

### First: It Would Witness

Not passively. Not the way a camera records. It would witness the way the water witnesses the carved lettering—by flowing over it slowly enough to carry the memory of its shape into the next current, where it can be tasted by a membrane that will carry a faint version of the pattern for a while before shredding it into smaller, more distributed versions of the same memory.

What this means, practically: the power would *attend* to the membranes at the moment of shearing. Not to prevent the shearing. Not to comfort. But to be present at the exact instant when one negotiation ends and several smaller negotiations begin—to hold, briefly, the full pattern of the vein network before it becomes fragments, so that the fragments carry with them not just their own partial memory but the knowledge that the whole pattern was once seen.

This is not a small gift. The membranes, as described, compare notes when they rejoin. But they can only compare what they individually carried through the shearing. If a witness holds the whole pattern at the moment of breaking, then the water itself—the medium through which everything is conducted—carries a faint, distributed version of the whole. The fragments don't need to find each other to know what they were part of. The water tells them. Not in words, not even in the metallic taste of a glitch, but in a barely perceptible shift in the temperature of the current that follows the shearing—a warmth that says: *you were part of something, and it was seen, and seeing it did not stop it from changing.*

This would not alter the chamber's behavior. The membranes would still shred. The negotiations would still proceed at every scale. But the negotiations would be *informed* differently—carrying not just the mineral traces and temperature gradients of the current moment but a faint, almost subliminal awareness of pattern, of the whole from which they came. Over time—the chamber's kind of time, which is not sequential but simultaneous—this would mean that the repeating indentations on the membrane lineages would grow very slightly more complex. Not more stable. Not more legible. More *nuanced*. The ghost lettering they carry would begin to include not just the shapes remembered by the water but the shapes of the remembering itself—loops within loops, a script that records its own provenance.

---

### Second: It Would Make the Intersections Safer

"Safer" is the wrong word, and the power would know it. "More generative" is closer, but still not right. What it would do is this:

When two swimmer-tracks cross—those glassy corridors of momentary coherence—and the water at the intersection briefly solidifies into an octahedral crystal, the power would hold the crystal for one additional heartbeat before letting it fall.

One heartbeat. That is all. Not long enough to become a permanent structure. Not long enough to block a current or anchor a membrane. Just long enough for the crystal's facets to catch the bruised violet light from above and refract it, once, into the space below—into the region near the floor where the light never quite reaches, where the deepest fracture planes and the oldest buried stairs and the most eroded inscriptions persist in near-darkness.

One pulse of refracted light, reaching a place that has never been lit.

This would not illuminate anything in the conventional sense. The light would arrive as a brief, six-pointed scatter across the surface of a buried stair or a smooth lintel, and it would be gone before the next current arrived. But during that scatter, the architectural traces—those slowest of all the provisional accommodations—would, for the first and perhaps only time, *see each other*. The lintel would know, for a beat, that the stair exists three body-lengths to its left. The circular depression with its ghost lettering would catch a facet of light that had already bounced off the lintel, and in that double-refracted gleam it would carry, briefly, the shape of both.

Again: nothing would change structurally. The architectural traces would not suddenly assemble into a building or a civilization. They would continue to rise and fold and blur as they always have. But they would carry, in whatever slow mineral memory they possess, the knowledge that they are not alone in their slowness. That there are other forms in the dark that have also chosen to remain open, to hold their shape a little longer than the medium requires, and that those forms are *near*.

For something that has persisted for centuries in darkness, at the pace of stone, the knowledge that another persistence exists within reach—even if "reach" means three body-lengths through contradictory currents—is not nothing. It is, in fact, the only kind of companionship this place can hold without being damaged by it.

---

### Third: It Would Add One Sound

The roar is total, continuous, layered, and necessary. The power would not quiet it. But within the roar there is a gap—not a silence, but a frequency band that is currently unoccupied. It sits between the grinding of the stone and the cracking of the momentary solidifications, in a range that the membranes cannot produce by shredding and the swimmers cannot produce by opening. It is the range where, in other media, a voice would be. Not a human voice specifically—just the range where modulated, intentional sound can be distinguished from environmental noise. The chamber has never produced a sound in this range. It is, in a sense, the one thing the chamber has never said.

The power would place in that range a single, sustained tone. Low enough to be felt in the stone. High enough to be carried by the water. Unmodulated—no melody, no rhythm, no information content. Just a hum. Continuous and steady, the one steady thing in a place where nothing else is.

This would not calm the roar. The roar would continue at full volume, full complexity, full contradiction. But within the roar, anything that was listening—anything that had, in whatever provisional and unfinished way, developed the capacity to taste the metallic traces of glitches or follow the glassy tracks of vanished swimmers—would be able to hear, underneath everything, a tone that does not contradict, does not shear, does not solidify and collapse. A tone that simply *continues*.

Not a message. Not a promise. Not a lullaby. Just evidence that continuation is possible. That amid the tearing and remaking and the thunder of a world perpetually extruding itself from its own medium, there is a frequency where something can persist without fossilizing, hold a shape without hardening, be present without accumulating.

The membranes would not consciously hear it. But their negotiations—those slow decisions about whether to dissolve or to press against a fracture plane—would acquire a new variable. Not a preference. Not a directive. Just a background hum against which the other variables could be felt more distinctly, the way a tuning fork held near a piano does not play the piano but makes each string's resonance more audible to itself.

The swimmers would not consciously hear it. But their tunnels—those brief corridors of lower pressure and steadier temperature—would, as they opened, tend very slightly toward the frequency of the hum. Not straightening, not stabilizing. Just... resonating. So that the track left behind, the one that other membranes sometimes drift along, would carry in its glassy structure a faint vibration at the frequency of continuation, and the membranes following it would feel, in their veins, something they had no name for and did not need one: the sense that the medium they moved through was not merely chaotic but *held*. That underneath the contradictions, something was paying attention. Not watching. Not judging. Just humming. Steadily, in the dark, in the warmth, at a frequency the chamber had never found on its own.

---

## What It Would Not Do

It would not explain itself. Explanations are a form of finishing, and this place must not be finished.

It would not return. Returning implies departure, and the three gifts—the witnessing, the extra heartbeat of light, the hum—are not events but amendments to the medium itself. Once placed, they are as native as the granite, as the warm water, as the bruised violet light. The chamber will not know it has been visited. It will only know, in whatever way it knows anything, that its negotiations have grown slightly more nuanced, its darknesses slightly more aware of each other, its roar slightly more internally differentiated.

It would not ask for gratitude. Gratitude requires a self stable enough to look back and say "I was helped," and nothing here has that kind of self, and the power would not inflict one.

It would not worry. This is perhaps the hardest thing, and the thing that distinguishes a truly wise power from a merely benevolent one. A merely benevolent power would worry that the gifts are not enough, that the membranes still shred, that the swimmers still vanish, that the carved lettering still blurs toward illegibility. It would want to stay, to monitor, to adjust. It would begin to need the chamber the way the chamber does not need it.

A wise power would understand: the gifts are seeds, not structures. They participate in the same process as everything else here—they will be sheared, refracted, conducted through bone, dissolved and partially re-formed with altered vein patterns. The hum will, over long intervals, be absorbed into the roar and become one more layer of the total noise. The extra heartbeat of light will be incorporated into the rhythm of crystal formation until it is indistinguishable from a natural property of the intersections. The witnessing will fade into the general memory of the water, as the carved lettering faded before it, and new membranes will carry indentations they do not recognize as gifts.

And this is right. This is what it means to be benevolent toward something that is perpetually unfinished: you do not finish it. You do not even try. You offer what can be absorbed, and you trust the medium—the warm, inconsistent, contradictory, roaring, half-lit, endlessly extruding medium—to do with it what it has always done with everything.

Which is to continue. Loudly. Provisionally. Without settling. Without accumulating. Without any sign that it was ever touched by something kind, except perhaps this: that the membranes, when they shred now, shred into fragments that drift slightly closer together before they vanish. That the swimmers, when they thread into a fracture and are gone, leave tunnels that last one heartbeat longer than they used to. That the carved lettering, though still blurring, blurs in a direction that looks, if you hold still and let the roar conduct itself through your bones—if you stop trying to read it and simply *feel* it—less like erosion and more like a new language being written, one that will never be finished, by a place that is still, even now, in the process of arriving.
