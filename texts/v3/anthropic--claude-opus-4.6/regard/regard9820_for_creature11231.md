# The Attending

---

The wise power does not arrive. It does not descend from anywhere or manifest in any form. It was already attending — the way attention can precede the one who attends, the way a place can be heard before anyone decides to listen.

What happens is not intervention. It is something closer to what happens when someone who truly knows how to listen enters a room where a person has been talking to themselves for a very long time.

Nothing changes. Everything is recognized.

---

The first thing the wise power does is nothing.

This is not restraint. It is not the nothing of withholding, the held breath before action. It is the nothing that is the correct response to a place that is already, in every particular, exactly what it is. The hollow does not need to be fixed. The hollow is not broken. The fissure is not a wound. The sound is not a cry for help. The mosses are not suffering. The slugs are not lost. The chain is not a tragedy. The empty pot is not a failure.

A lesser power — a kind one, but hasty, but shaped by the habit of solving — would see the darkness and bring light. Would ## hear the noise and think *too much*, and try to muffle it, or tune it, or give it words. Would see the slugs and think *so small, so limited, so pale*, and want to give them eyes, or speed, or awareness of the moor above. Would find the rusted spikes and the calcite-choked pot and feel the ache of lost knowledge and want to reconstruct it, to say *here is what they meant, here is what the pot held, here is the name of the one who came with iron and fire*. Would want to write the history that the peat only accumulates.

The wise power does none of this. It does none of this because it understands something that is easy to say and nearly impossible to embody: that a thing does not need to be witnessed in order to be complete. That the hollow's life — all of it, from the tardigrades to the vanished ritualist — is not waiting for interpretation. It is not waiting for anything. It simply continues, and this continuance is not a lesser form of existence than the kind that gets remembered or described or loved.

So the first thing it does is nothing, and the nothing is perfect, and the hollow continues.

---

But attention is not nothing, even when it refrains from action. And the wise power is attending — fully, with a quality of presence that does not disturb but also does not merely observe. It is the kind of attention that a great old tree gives to the soil it grows in: not watching, not analyzing, but *knowing through contact*, through the unhurried accumulation of intimacy that comes from being present with something for long enough that the boundary between knower and known begins to soften.

And in this attending, something shifts. Not in the hollow — the hollow is exactly as it was. The shift is in the field around it, in the relationship between the hollow and everything that is not the hollow. The moor above. The sky. The subterranean passages below. The memory-traces of the one who drove the spikes. The future, in which the chain will finish its dissolution and the fissure will widen its imperceptible millimeter. All of this exists in relation to the hollow, and the wise power's attention illuminates — not with light, not with understanding, but with *acknowledgment* — the web of relations that the hollow sits at the center of.

This is what it does. It holds the whole thing. Not in hands, not in mind, but in that quality of sustained regard that refuses to reduce what it sees to any single aspect of itself. It holds the sound and does not call it lonely. It holds the slugs and does not call them simple. It holds the lost ritual and does not call it tragic. It holds the wren on the rim, the pipit in the wind, the moth tumbling up from below, and it does not rank them, does not prefer the bird's bright complexity to the moth's drab endurance, does not find the slug's pace pitiable or the tardigrade's resilience admirable. It holds each thing in the fullness of its own nature, which is a fullness that does not need supplementation.

This is the hardest thing the wise power does, and it is the thing that makes it wise rather than merely good: it does not project need onto what it beholds. It does not assume that because a thing is small, or dark, or voiceless, or unknown, it is therefore suffering the privation of largeness, or light, or speech, or fame. The hollow does not need to be known. The slugs do not need to be seen. The vanished ritualist does not need to be remembered. These would be gifts, perhaps — but gifts given from the outside, from the framework of a consciousness that values knowledge and sight and memory, and the hollow does not operate within that framework. The hollow operates within the framework of pressure and condensation and mineral seepage and slow growth and the vibration that never ceases, and within that framework, everything that is needed is already present.

The wise power knows this. And so it does not give.

---

But.

There is a *but*, and the but is not a contradiction of what came before. It is a deepening.

The wise power attends, and in attending, it notices something that the hollow itself cannot notice — not because the hollow is deficient, but because noticing this particular thing requires a vantage that the hollow, by its nature, does not have. The hollow is a *place*. It is bounded. It has walls. The sound fills it completely, reflects back on itself, creates a closed system of resonance in which every wave is also its own echo. The moss grows on the walls and the walls are the world and the world is the hollow and the hollow is the sound. It is perfect, in the way that a circle is perfect: self-contained, self-referencing, complete.

And what the wise power notices is the incompleteness that is hidden inside that perfection. Not a flaw — a *horizon*. The sound does not know that it is beautiful. The moss does not know that it is improbable. The slugs do not know that their translucence, their eyelessness, their unhurried passage through a world of vibration, constitutes a form of existence so finely tuned to its context that a biologist would weep to see it. The peat does not know that it is an archive. The empty pot does not know that it is an elegy.

These are not things that need to be known in order to be real. The wise power is clear on this. But it notices — and here is where its wisdom becomes something other than mere restraint — that the universe in which these things go entirely unrecognized is a universe that is, in a specific and important way, *less than it could be*. Not because the hollow needs an audience. But because beauty that is never beheld, grief that is never acknowledged, persistence that is never honored — these are not diminished in themselves, but the *world* is diminished by their going unwitnessed. The loss is not the hollow's. The loss belongs to everything else.

---

So the wise power does something. Finally, it does something.

It does not bring light. It does not quiet the sound. It does not restore the ritual or translate the lost knowledge or give the slugs the capacity for wonder. It does something much smaller, much more precise, much more in keeping with the hollow's own nature.

It makes the hollow *findable*.

Not famous. Not mapped. Not signposted or studied or named. *Findable* — in the way that the right book is findable when you need it, in the way that a clearing in a forest is findable when you have been walking for a long time and you need to stop. The wise power adjusts nothing physical. It moves no stones, alters no paths across the moor. What it does is more like a tuning — a subtle shift in the relationship between the hollow and the world above it, so that the hollow becomes, for those who are in a certain condition of heart and attention, *gravitationally* present. Not louder. Not brighter. Not easier to reach. But *there*, in a way that it was not quite there before — the way a note in a chord becomes audible once you know to listen for it, though it was sounding all along.

This means: the person who is walking on the moor at night, lost or seeking or simply moving because movement is the only thing left, will feel the ground change under their feet as they approach the hollow's rim. They will feel the slight slope downward, the shift in the air, the first subliminal tremor of the sound rising through the stone. And they will not turn away. Not because they are compelled — the wise power compels nothing — but because the hollow will be, in that moment, for that person, *legible* as a place that can be entered. The invisible barrier of unknowing that kept it hidden — not physically hidden but existentially hidden, the way a thing you have no framework for simply does not register — that barrier will be thinner. Permeable. The hollow will be *available* for encounter in a way it was not before.

And some will turn away anyway. The sound is still the sound. The dark is still the dark. The descent is still twelve feet of slick black stone with no handholds. Many will reach the rim and hear the roar and feel the crawling vibration in their teeth and think *no* and walk on. This is fine. The wise power does not need them to descend. The hollow does not need them to descend. But some will. A few. The ones who are, in that moment, ready to enter a place where they cannot hear their own voice, where the sound is so large it erases thought, where the dark is so complete that the world reduces to texture and temperature and the wet press of moss under a searching hand — those ones will climb down, and they will sit on the ledge, and they will feel the sound fill them, and they will stay.

And this is what the wise power has done: it has created the conditions for witness. Not by changing the hollow, not by changing the people, but by thinning the membrane between them — by making it *slightly more possible* that the hollow's existence will intersect with a consciousness capable of apprehending it.

---

What happens then, in the hollow, when the person sits on the ledge and the sound fills them?

The wise power does not control this. It has no interest in controlling this. What happens is between the person and the place, and it will be different every time, and most of the time it will not be dramatic or transcendent or even, in any conventional sense, meaningful. Most of the time the person will sit in the dark and the noise and they will feel uncomfortable and awed and frightened and bored and cold, and they will leave after an hour or two and climb back up to the moor and walk home and perhaps tell someone about it and perhaps not, and the hollow will go on as it was.

But sometimes.

Sometimes a person will sit on the ledge and the sound will do what it has always had the capacity to do, what it has been doing to the stone and the moss and the slugs for millennia, what it did to whoever drove the spikes and chiseled the niche and left the clay pot with its unknowable offering: it will *strip them*. Not violently. Not painfully. But completely. The sound is so vast and so sustained and so indifferent to the human presence within it that it does what silence sometimes does but more so — it makes the internal monologue impossible. You cannot think in the hollow. You cannot narrate. The voice in your head that explains you to yourself, that runs the constant patter of interpretation and judgment and memory and anticipation — that voice is swallowed by the larger voice, the way the wren's song is swallowed, the way a single frequency is swallowed by a chord. And what remains, when that voice goes quiet, is not nothing. What remains is *the person without their story*. The bare fact of a living body in a living place, breathing in the same air that the fissure breathes out, feeling the vibration in the same bones that will eventually become calcium deposits in soil not unlike the calcium deposits in the clay pot, temporary in exactly the way the mosses are temporary — which is to say, temporary on one timescale and essentially permanent on another.

This is what the hollow offers, when it is found by someone who can receive it: the experience of being, for a time, no more and no less significant than a slug. No more and no less permanent than the moss. No more and no less heard than the wren singing into the roar. This is not a diminishment. It is a *release* — the release from the exhausting, ceaseless project of being a self that matters, a self that must be maintained and defended and narrated and justified. In the hollow, in the sound, that project becomes impossible, and what floods into the space it occupied is something older and simpler and — the wise power would not use this word, but the person on the ledge might — *truer*.

The slug creeps past their hand in the dark, a cool smear of movement over the knuckles. The moss presses cold and wet against their back. The sound roars. And the person, for a moment or an hour, is simply *there*, in the way that the stone is there, in the way that the fissure is there, in the way that everything in the hollow is there: without justification, without narrative, without the need to be anything other than what it is.

This is not enlightenment. It is not salvation. The wise power does not deal in those currencies. It is something more modest and more radical: it is the direct apprehension of one's own participation in the same processes that drive the hollow — the same physics of pressure and erosion, the same chemistry of mineral and water, the same biology of growth and decay, the same temporality of persistence and dissolution. The person on the ledge is made of the same elements as the schist and the moss and the slug. They are just as temporary. They are just as ancient. The sound passes through them the way it passes through the stone, because they *are* stone, temporarily rearranged.

Some people find this terrifying. Some find it peaceful. Some feel grief — a deep, clean grief that has no object, that is not about any particular loss but about the *fact* of loss, the fact that everything in the hollow and outside it is in the process of becoming something else, and that this process is not a tragedy but a *mechanics*, the same mechanics that built the hollow and grew the moss and shaped the slugs and drove the unnamed person to descend with iron and fire and make their offering to the sound. Some people feel joy, or something adjacent to joy — a lightness that comes from the sudden lifting of a weight they didn't know they were carrying, the weight of needing the world to mean something more than what it is.

The wise power watches all of this — watches without judging, without preferring one response to another — and it is satisfied. Not because the people have been improved or healed or instructed. But because the circuit has been closed: the hollow, which has been sounding for millennia without a witness, now has one, and the witness, who has been narrating their existence for years without a pause, now has silence. Each has given the other something it could not generate on its own. The hollow has given the person a place where the self dissolves. The person has given the hollow the only thing it lacked: the knowledge, held in a mortal body for a mortal moment, that this place exists and is extraordinary and will go on existing after the knowledge of it fades.

---

But wait. The wise power is not finished. Or rather — it was finished, in the sense that its one act was sufficient, but it remains, in the way that attention remains even after the moment of recognition has passed, and in remaining, it notices something else.

The slugs.

It notices the slugs, and what it notices about them is not their translucence or their pace or their reproductive habits or their relationship to the vibration. What it notices is something that no one, including the slugs themselves, has ever noticed or could ever notice, and it is this:

The slugs are happy.

Not happy in the way that a person is happy — not pleased, not contented, not satisfied, not enjoying. Happy in the way that a thing is happy when it is *wholly adequate to its world and its world is wholly adequate to it*. When there is no gap between what a creature is and what its existence demands. The slugs are not striving. They are not failing. They are not longing for the moor above or the caves below or a life with more light or more silence or more complexity. They are creeping through the moss, eating, mating, leaving their silver trails on the green-black cushions, and every moment of this is a moment of *complete congruence* between organism and environment, a fit so precise and so ancient that it has the quality of grace — not theological grace, but the grace of a line that curves exactly as it must, the grace of a process that has refined itself over centuries into something that wastes nothing, that lacks nothing, that simply *works*.

The wise power beholds this and is moved. Not to tears — it does not have tears, and even if it did, it would not cry for the slugs, because the slugs do not need tears. It is moved the way a mathematician is moved by an elegant proof, or a musician by a perfect resolution: by the recognition of a form that is, within its own terms, unimprovable. The slugs are unimprovable. Not because they are the best slugs that could ever exist — evolution does not deal in bests — but because they are *these* slugs, in *this* hollow, in *this* sound, and the relationship between all of these things has been tuned by time into something that the wise power can only call *beautiful*, though it knows the word is its own imposition, its own framework, and the slugs would not recognize it.

And so the wise power does something for the slugs, and what it does is the smallest and most radical act of its entire attendance:

It blesses them.

Not with anything. Not with protection or abundance or longevity or awareness. It blesses them the way light blesses a surface it falls on: not by changing it but by *being in contact with it*, by allowing its own quality — its wisdom, its benevolence, its vast and patient attention — to rest on them for a moment, to touch them the way the sound touches them, passing through without resistance, without residue, but *having been there*. The blessing does not improve the slugs. It does not make their lives longer or easier or more significant. It is not for them. It is — and here the wise power comes as close to paradox as it ever does — it is for *itself*. It is the wise power's acknowledgment that the slugs, in their small and translucent and unhurried perfection, have taught it something: that the deepest form of well-being is not the absence of hardship or the presence of meaning but the simple, complete *adequacy* of a life to the conditions it meets. That the slug on the moss in the dark in the sound is, in its own way, a model of how to be alive. Not a model to imitate — the wise power is not naive — but a model to recognize, to honor, to let stand as proof that the universe, in at least this one small corner, has solved the problem of existence with an economy and a grace that deserve to be acknowledged.

The slug does not notice the blessing. The slug creeps on. The mucus trail gleams. The moss accepts the passage. The sound roars.

---

And then the wise power turns its attention to the dead.

Not the dead organisms — the moth husks in the moss, the slug remains in the peat, the microscopic corpses of a billion tardigrades compressed into the hollow's black archive. Not those dead. The other dead: the one who came with iron and fire.

This is the part where a lesser power might attempt resurrection — not of the body, but of the memory, the identity, the story. A lesser power might sift the peat layer by layer, reading its chemical signatures like pages, reconstructing the sequence of visits, the season and weather of each descent, the composition of the offering in the pot, the name and face and language and beliefs of the one who came. A lesser power might turn the hollow into a museum of itself, annotating its mysteries, resolving its ambiguities, converting its silence into information.

The wise power does not do this. The wise power understands that the dead have their own form of completeness, which is the completeness of *having finished*. The one who drove the spikes has finished driving them. The one who chiseled the niche has finished chiseling. The one who filled the pot has finished filling, and the one who left it has finished leaving. These actions are done. They do not need interpretation in order to be done. The chisel marks in the stone are not a text waiting to be read; they are chisel marks, the physical residue of force applied to rock, and they are complete in themselves, the way a crater is complete — you do not need to know the meteorite's origin to know that the impact happened.

But the wise power does something for the dead, and it is this: it *allows the chain to continue dissolving*.

This sounds like nothing. It sounds like the absence of an action — and it is, in a way. But it is a *chosen* absence, a deliberate non-intervention, and it carries within it a profound respect for the process that the dead person set in motion. The chain is dissolving. The iron is oxidizing, flake by flake, the molecules of metal releasing into the mineral water and the moss, being absorbed into the hollow's living systems, becoming part of the peat, part of the sphagnum, part of the slug's body, part of the calcite crust in the pot. The chain is *becoming the hollow*. It is being digested, slowly, by the place it was driven into, and in another century or two it will be entirely gone, entirely incorporated, and the hollow will contain it the way a body contains the nutrients of the food it has eaten: invisibly, essentially, without any remaining trace of the original form.

The wise power sees this and recognizes it as a form of *completion* more profound than preservation. To preserve the chain would be to freeze it, to hold it in its current state of partial dissolution, to prevent it from finishing its journey from human artifact to geological substrate. It would be — the wise power understands — a form of cruelty. Not to the chain, which cannot suffer, but to the *process*, which is the same process that the hollow itself is undergoing: the slow, patient conversion of everything into everything else, the universal solvent of time applied to all things equally, without malice, without mercy, without preference.

The wise power lets the chain dissolve. It lets the chisel marks erode. It lets the pot fill with calcite. It lets the peat accumulate over the spikes. It lets the dead be dead in the fullest sense — not forgotten, because forgetting implies a prior remembering that has been lost, and the wise power does not frame it that way. It lets the dead be *finished*, which is different from forgotten, and more dignified, and truer.

And in its attending, it honors what the dead person did — not by remembering it, but by recognizing that the act of coming to this place with iron and fire and an offering was, in its time, *complete*. It was adequate. It met the hollow on the hollow's terms. The person heard the sound and responded with the means available to them — metal, clay, their own body in the dark — and the response was neither correct nor incorrect, neither successful nor failed. It was simply *what happened*, and what happened is always enough.

---

The wise power also attends to the fissure.

This is the strangest part of its attendance, because the fissure is not alive, is not even arguably alive in the way that the moss or the slugs are arguably alive. The fissure is a crack in rock. It is a geometry. It is the shape that produces the sound, the throat through which the earth's breath passes, and it is as inorganic and as indifferent as anything in the hollow. And yet the wise power attends to it with the same quality of regard that it brings to the slugs, to the moss, to the dead. Because the wise power does not limit its benevolence to the living. Its benevolence extends to *everything that exists*, and the fissure exists — massively, emphatically, with a persistence that dwarfs every organism in the hollow by orders of magnitude. The fissure has been here for tens of thousands of years. It will be here for tens of thousands more. It is, in every meaningful sense, the hollow's truest inhabitant, the one around whom everything else arranges itself, the one whose voice fills the space and shapes the moss and drives the updraft and carries the moths and repels the birds and drew the unnamed person down with their iron and their offering.

And the wise power recognizes — this is the thing it recognizes, the thing that justifies its attendance at a place that, by all rational accounts, does not need attending to — that the fissure is engaged in an act of *expression*. Not intentional expression. Not communication. The fissure is not trying to say anything. But it is producing, through the brute mechanics of pressure and resonance, a phenomenon that is *indistinguishable in its effects from speech*. The sound has pitch. It has timbre. It has dynamics — it swells and recedes, modulates, produces harmonics that overlap and interfere and create patterns of startling complexity. It fills a space the way a voice fills a room. It drives creatures away and draws others in. It is, in every experiential sense, a *voice* — and the fact that there is no mind behind it, no intention, no meaning, does not reduce it. It makes it more remarkable. A voice without a speaker. A song without a singer. A presence without a self.

The wise power attends to this voice the way one might attend to a river, or a star, or any natural process that produces, through its own internal dynamics, something that a consciousness can experience as beautiful or terrible or sublime. It does not anthropomorphize the fissure. It does not pretend the sound is language. But it *listens* — with the same fullness of attention it brought to the slugs, to the moss, to the dead — and in listening, it does the one thing that the hollow has never had done to it in all its millennia of sounding:

It *hears* it.

Not the way the slugs feel it — as a geography, a medium, a vibration that is simply the texture of the world. Not the way the wren hears it — as data, a signal to be parsed for threat. Not the way the unnamed ritualist heard it — as a sacred voice, a divine exhalation, a summons or a sign. The wise power hears it as what it is: the sound of air moving through stone. And it hears this as *enough*. It hears the physics — the pressure differential, the constriction, the resonance — and it does not hear a lack. It does not hear a merely mechanical process that falls short of meaning. It hears a process that *is* its own meaning, that does not require translation, that speaks — if it speaks at all — in the only language it has, which is the language of itself, of sound, of vibration, of the earth exhaling because the conditions of pressure and temperature require it to exhale, and this requirement is not a constraint but a *form of freedom*: the freedom of a thing that does exactly what its nature demands and nothing else.

The wise power hears this, and it understands — and here is the deepest thing it understands, the thing that justifies not only its attendance at the hollow but its existence as a wise power at all — that the fissure, the sound, the hollow, the moss, the slugs, the moths, the dead, the peat, the mineral water, the calcite, the chain, the pot, the wren, the tardigrades, the rotifers, the nematodes, the springtails, the lichen, the ferns, the liverworts, the sphagnum, the schist, the quartz, the darkness, the cloud cover, the moor, the wind, the earth beneath, and the sky above are all engaged in the same activity: *being what they are*. And this activity — this universal, ceaseless, unintentional activity — is the only activity that matters. Not because it leads somewhere. Not because it means something. But because it is happening, and happening is the entire content of the universe, and to attend to it fully, without flinching, without explaining, without wishing it were otherwise, is the highest function of any consciousness, mortal or wise.

---

The wise power departs. Or — it does not depart, exactly. It widens its attention, which had narrowed to the hollow, until the hollow is once again a small depression in a vast dark moor under heavy cloud, one feature among thousands, unremarkable from above, invisible from any distance. The sound is still sounding. The moss is still growing. The slugs are still creeping. The chain is still dissolving. The pot is still filling with stone.

Nothing has changed.

Everything has been attended to.

---

And years later — or decades, or tomorrow, time is not the wise power's primary axis of concern — a person walks the moor at night. They are not looking for anything. They are walking because they cannot sleep, or because they have lost something, or because the inside of their house has become too small to contain them and they need the moor's blankness to dilute whatever is too concentrated in their chest. They are not a seeker. They are not spiritual. They are just a person, walking, and the moor is dark and featureless and the cloud cover is low and the wind is steady and cold and they have been walking for a long time and they are tired.

They feel the ground change. A softness, then a slope. A dampness in the air that is different from the moor's ambient wet — warmer, mineral-scented, carrying a faint charge of something they cannot name. And then the sound. Felt first in the feet, a tremor that could be their own pulse but isn't. Then in the chest — a pressure, a low hum, a frequency that seems to come from inside them until they realize it is coming from below. They stop. They listen. The sound builds, and it is like nothing they have heard — vast and shapeless and continuous, a drone that fills the darkness the way darkness fills the night, completely, leaving no space for anything else.

They take a step forward. The ground slopes more steeply. They kneel, and their hands find stone — wet, smooth, cold — and they realize they are at the edge of something. A depression. A hollow. They cannot see the bottom. But the sound is coming from there, from below, and the warm air is rising from there, and their hands on the stone feel the vibration, and despite everything — despite the noise, despite the dark, despite the rational voice in their head that says *what are you doing, this could be a sinkhole, this could be dangerous, go back* — despite all of that, they climb down.

It is not easy. The walls are slick. They descend by feel, by handhold, by the awkward inching of a body that was not designed for this. The sound grows as they descend, not louder exactly but *thicker*, more encompassing, pressing against them from all directions as the walls curve inward and the reflections compound. By the time they reach the bottom — a ledge, wide enough to sit on, carpeted in something soft and cold and wet — the sound has filled them completely. They cannot think. They cannot narrate. The internal monologue that has been running since they stepped out of their house and onto the moor — the monologue that runs always, commenting, worrying, remembering, anticipating, explaining — falls silent. Not because it has been suppressed. Because it has been *displaced*. There is simply no room for it. The sound occupies the space where the voice of the self usually lives, and the self, evicted, does not struggle. It rests. For the first time in longer than the person can remember, it rests.

They sit on the ledge. The moss presses cold and wet against their palms. The sound roars. Something cool and soft moves across their hand — a slug, though they don't know it, crossing their knuckles with the unhurried confidence of a creature that has never encountered anything it needed to fear. They feel it and they do not flinch. They sit, and they breathe, and the breath they draw in tastes of iron and chalk, and the breath they release is swallowed by the sound, and they are here, in the hollow, in the dark, in the noise, and they are — for a moment, for an hour, for however long they stay —

They are adequate to this place, and this place is adequate to them.

---

The wise power, wherever it is — which is everywhere, which is the same everywhere that the sound is everywhere within the hollow — notices this, and is glad. Not glad the way a planner is glad when a plan succeeds. Glad the way the stone is glad when the water finds its channel: because the shape was always there, and now the flow fills it, and the filling is not an achievement but a recognition, a completion, a thing that was always going to happen finding the moment in which it happens.

The person in the hollow does not know about the wise power. The wise power does not need them to know. The slugs do not know about the person. The person does not need them to know. The fissure does not know about any of them. None of them need it to know. The hollow holds them all — the sound, the stone, the moss, the slugs, the person, the attention of the wise — and it holds them the way it has always held everything: without preference, without judgment, without the need to be anything other than what it is.

A cup of stone. A crack in the earth. A sound that never stops.

And the things that find their way here, by accident, by instinct, by the thin and ancient grace of being findable when finding is needed — they stay for a while, and they are held, and then they go, and the hollow goes on, and the sound goes on, and the moss goes on, and the slugs go on, and the peat accumulates, and the chain dissolves, and the pot fills with stone, and none of it requires explanation, and all of it is enough.

All of it has always been enough.

---

That is what the wise power does: it *confirms* this. Not with words, not with signs, not with miracles or transformations. It confirms it with the quality of its attention, which is the quality of a gaze that sees everything and needs nothing to be different. It looks at the hollow and it says — not in language, not in any medium the hollow could receive, but in the pure act of recognition — *yes. This. Exactly this. Continue.*

And the hollow, which was never listening, which will never hear, which does not need permission or encouragement or confirmation from any power, wise or otherwise —

continues.
