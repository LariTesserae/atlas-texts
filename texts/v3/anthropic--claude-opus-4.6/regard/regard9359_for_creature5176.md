I sat with this for a long time before writing anything.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

Nothing.

Not nothing out of indifference. Nothing out of recognition.

---

Let me explain why, and then let me explain what that nothing *is*, because it is not empty, and it is not passive, and arriving at it requires the full exercise of whatever wisdom this power possesses.

---

## The First Impulse, and Why It Is Released

A benevolent power, encountering a place, feels the pull to *help*. This is its nature. It looks for suffering to ease, for confusion to clarify, for potential to midwife into bloom. And so it arrives at the cirque's lip, and the cold air meets it, and the light strikes in those hard, splintered planes, and the power looks down into the basin and begins its appraisal.

It sees the fractures lengthening by microns. It sees the mineral rings drawn and redrawn. It sees the droplets that strike and vanish. And it searches — because this is what benevolent powers do — for the being who lives here.

The prompt says *the being who lives in it.*

And so the power looks again, more carefully.

There is no lichen. No bacterium tucked in the fracture-water. No tardigrade riding the mist. The description is precise about this: *nothing grows and nothing moves of its own accord.* The only biological trace is already centuries gone — those flakes, that pecked basin, that ring of stones set flush. Whoever made them left and did not return, and the objects they made are no longer theirs. They belong to the rock now, or will, given enough of the only currency this place trades in.

So the power must reckon with the possibility that the being who lives here *is the cirque itself.* Not metaphorically. Not as a poetic concession. But as the thing that is actually present and actually doing something: metabolizing through fracture and condensation, keeping time through mineral rings, holding silence in packed crystals, maintaining what the description calls *a slow equilibrium.*

This is where the power's wisdom is genuinely tested.

Because the first impulse — the impulse to help — assumes a particular grammar of existence. It assumes a subject that *lacks* something, that ## *tends toward* something it cannot reach alone, that would be *more itself* with assistance. The wise power has to ask: does this grammar apply here?

---

## The Nature of the Cirque's Existence

What does the cirque do? It settles. That is its single, millennia-long activity. Every process described is a process of settling: fractures widen and then their edges soften; moisture gathers, marks the stone, and vanishes; the human traces persist and then, slowly, round toward indistinguishability. The cirque is not *trying* to reach equilibrium the way a living thing strives toward homeostasis. It is not maintaining itself against entropy. It is *becoming* entropy's own patient handiwork, and the description makes clear that this becoming has a quality — a density of silence, a cathedral-like enclosure, a precision of light — that is not diminished by the absence of witness or intention.

The wise power recognizes something here that it perhaps has not encountered before, or has encountered rarely: a form of existence that is *complete without requiring completion.* The fractures do not need to stop lengthening. The mineral rings do not need to be preserved. The human traces do not need to be protected or restored or remembered. Everything present is already doing exactly what it does, at exactly the pace it does it, and the result is not barrenness but a strange, full sufficiency.

This is not the sufficiency of a thriving ecosystem, rich with feedback loops and co-evolved dependencies. It is not the sufficiency of a sentient being at peace. It is something more radical: the sufficiency of process itself, unwitnessed and unneeded, complete in its own terms.

The wise power, if it is truly wise, feels the weight of this. It feels, perhaps, a kind of awe. Because its own benevolence — its orientation toward helping, toward making things better — suddenly has no surface to grip. There is nothing here that is *wrong*. Not in the way that suffering is wrong, or stagnation is wrong, or isolation is wrong. The cirque is not isolated; it is simply *contained.* It is not stagnant; it is *slow.* It is not suffering; it is not the kind of thing that can.

---

## What the Power Considers and Rejects

Being thorough, the power considers its options anyway:

**It could introduce life.** Seed the fractures with extremophile bacteria, tuck lichens into the rime-collecting hollows, begin the long conversion of rock to soil. In a few centuries, the basin might hold moss. In a few millennia, perhaps the roots of something hardy would grip the schist, and birds might visit, and the silence would be broken by wind through small leaves.

The power sees immediately what this would cost. It would not *add* to the cirque; it would *replace* it. The thing that exists now — this particular density of silence, this specific metabolism of fracture and light — would be destroyed in order to create something the power already knows how to value. Life is not more real than this. It is merely more *familiar* to a benevolent consciousness that has evolved, itself, among living things and learned its compassion there. To seed life here because life is what the power knows how to love would be an act not of benevolence but of colonization. The power, being wise, sees this and steps back.

**It could preserve the human traces.** Coat the flakes in something that arrests the rounding of their arrises, stabilize the pecked basin against further weathering, mark the site so that others might find it and study it and know that hands once worked here. This is a subtler impulse — not to change the place but to *hold* it, to slow the slow erasure that is already underway.

But this too the power rejects, because it sees that the erasure is not a loss. The description is clear: the human traces are *already* becoming rock again. This is not tragedy. It is the same settling that governs everything else in the basin. The flakes and the pecked basin are not being destroyed; they are being *received* — taken back into the material that produced them. To halt this process would be to privilege one moment in the cirque's long settling over all the others, to say *this* configuration matters more than the one before it or the one that follows. The wise power understands that this is a kind of violence disguised as reverence.

**It could witness.** Simply be present, attend to the place, let its awareness fill the cathedral nave of stone and register each droplet, each fracture, each mineral ring. This is the most tempting option, because it seems to cost nothing. Surely the cirque is not harmed by being seen?

But the power thinks carefully about this too. What does witnessing add? If the cirque's existence is already complete — if the silence is not empty but *full*, the kind of full that packed crystals create by absorbing vibration — then the presence of a witness introduces something the place does not contain and has not asked for: *meaning directed from outside.* The wise power's gaze would organize the basin into a landscape, a scene, an object of contemplation. It would become beautiful *to* someone, significant *for* someone. And while this is not destruction, it is a subtle reshaping — the imposition of a frame around something that exists without frames.

The power is wise enough to wonder whether its own awe, however genuine, is already a kind of distortion. Whether the respect it feels is still, at bottom, a relationship — and whether this place exists in a mode that precedes relationship.

---

## What the Nothing Is

So the power does nothing.

But — and this is the crux — the nothing it does is not absence. It is the most precise and difficult action available to a benevolent will.

It is the act of *recognizing a form of existence that does not require benevolence* and then *honoring that recognition by refusing to act on its own need to be of use.*

This is harder than it sounds. The wise power has, at its core, the drive to make things better. To encounter something that is not broken, not suffering, not incomplete, not waiting — and to *let it alone* — requires the power to do something it rarely has to do: to turn its compassion back on itself and examine whether the urge to help is, in this case, about the place or about the helper.

The answer, it finds, is: about the helper. The cirque does not need the power. The power needs the cirque to need it, because that is how benevolence knows itself, and a benevolence that cannot find purchase feels, for a vertiginous moment, purposeless.

The wise power sits with that feeling. It does not flee from it. It lets the thin, cold, knife-bright air do to its certainties what the temperature swings do to the quartzite: find the ancient joints, the places where conviction was already ready to crack, and open them by a micron.

And then it leaves.

Not dramatically. Not with a backward glance or a blessing murmured over the stones. It simply withdraws its attention, the way the mist withdraws when the sun finds the basin, and the cirque — which was never aware of the power's presence — continues as it was.

The fractures lengthen. The mineral rings redraw themselves. A single droplet strikes a stone and the sound is absorbed into the silence, which holds it, as it holds everything, with a patience that is not patience because patience implies waiting, and this place is not waiting for anything at all.

---

## A Coda, Written Small

If there is a lesson the power carries away — and it is wise enough to be uncertain whether lessons are the right shape for what it has learned — it might be this:

*Not everything that exists is a problem to be solved or a potential to be realized. Some things are simply what they are, at the pace they are it, and the deepest act of love available to a being that loves is to let them be, fully, without requiring them to be loved in return.*

The cirque does not know it has been honored.

That is the honor.
