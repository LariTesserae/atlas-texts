# The Attending

---

I sat with this for a very long time before writing anything. Not because of uncertainty but because this place demands that you move at its speed before you act, and the speed of this place is geological. A benevolent and wise power that approached the Drowning Vaults with human urgency — even compassionate urgency — would already be failing. The first act of wisdom here is to arrive slowly. To arrive the way the mineral water arrives at the stone surface: without announcement, without force, already warm.

So.

---

**What the wise power perceives first**

Not the animals. Not the architecture. Not even the builders.

It perceives the *integrity* of the system — the wholeness, the way nothing here is wasted or orphaned or left without function. The tube worms in their alcoves filtering sulfide. The crabs walking their slow pilgrimage toward the columns where they will become structure. The eels threading chemical news through oval conduits. The ray circulating water with its body. The pale shrimp turning bacteria into flesh into waste into bacteria. The geothermal channels, plumbed and directed. The builders returning across generations to fit new stone against old. Everything interlocking. Everything accounted for. A system so deeply integrated that it has passed beyond the point where you can say where the organism ends and the architecture begins, where the living stops and the mineral starts, where the intentional gives way to the automatic.

The wise power perceives this and its first response is: **nothing needs to be fixed.**

This is the hardest and most important perception. Because a benevolent power — especially one shaped by, or comprehensible to, human minds — carries with it the assumption that attention implies intervention. That noticing suffering or limitation or darkness is a prelude to correcting it. That care means *changing things*. The entire history of benevolence as humans understand it is a history of alteration: healing the sick, feeding the hungry, lighting the dark, raising the fallen.

The vaults are dark. The creatures in them are blind, or functionally so. The water is sulfurous. The crabs die on columns and become stone. The builders work in silence with bone tools and will never see the sun. By almost any metric a surface-dwelling, light-adapted, language-using intelligence would apply, this place looks like it needs *help*.

It does not need help.

The wise power knows this because the wise power is wise, and wisdom — real wisdom, the kind that deserves the name — includes the capacity to recognize a form of flourishing that looks nothing like your own. The Drowning Vaults are not a problem to be solved. They are not a darkness to be illuminated. They are not a silence to be filled. They are a complete expression of something — of life organizing itself around the constraints of depth and darkness and pressure and deep time, and not merely surviving those constraints but *building a civilization inside them*, a civilization of stone and skin-texture and patience, a civilization that has lasted longer than any surface culture and will last longer still, because it was designed to last, because lasting is what it does, because the builders understood from the beginning that the only architecture worth making is the architecture that will still be holding when the architect's species is a fossil.

The wise power sees this. And it does not reach for a light. It does not warm the water further. It does not introduce nutrients or open channels to the surface or send down knowledge like a gift. It does not do any of the things that benevolence-as-intervention would demand.

Instead, it does something much harder.

---

**It attends.**

---

I need to be precise about what I mean by this, because *attending* sounds passive, and what the wise power does is not passive. It is the most active form of engagement possible with a system that is already whole. It is the kind of attention a master carpenter gives to a joint made by another master carpenter — not looking for flaws, not planning improvements, but *reading* the work, understanding the decisions, honoring the knowledge embedded in the craft. It is the attention a person gives to a forest they did not plant and do not own and will not harvest, when they walk through it slowly and notice the way the canopy layers itself and the understory arranges around the light and the mycorrhizal network connects root to root beneath the soil they're standing on, and they feel — not think, *feel* — that this place is functioning at a level of complexity and elegance that their intervention could only diminish.

The wise power attends the Drowning Vaults the way you would attend a ceremony in a tradition not your own: with full presence, with deep respect, with the understanding that you are a guest in a system of meaning that was not built for you and does not require your approval to be valid.

But attending is not merely watching. The wise power is not a tourist.

Here is what attending looks like, concretely:

---

**It learns the language.**

Not quickly. Not by decoding it from the outside, the way a cryptographer breaks a cipher. It learns the way the apprentice builders learn: by contact. By pressing itself — whatever form *itself* takes in this context, whatever medium a wise power occupies when it chooses to be present in warm dark water at two hundred meters depth — against the texture-language of the builders and *feeling* the shapes. The ridges, the smooth patches, the rippled fields. The grammar of topology.

This takes time. A long time. The wise power is patient — it has to be, to operate here — and it submits to the slowness of the learning, the way the builders submit to the slowness of the stone. It does not accelerate the process. It does not cheat by perceiving all the patterns at once and constructing a Rosetta Stone of skin-texture linguistics. It learns the way learning happens here: through repetition, through contact, through the accumulated weight of many encounters, each one depositing a thin layer of understanding the way the mineral water deposits a thin layer of calcium on the columns.

It takes years. Perhaps decades. The wise power is present in the vaults for so long that the crabs begin to route around it the way they route around the columns. The tube worms in the nearest alcove grow accustomed to its chemical signature. An eel begins threading through a space near it habitually, carrying its taste to adjacent chambers, so that the organisms throughout the vaults know it is here the way they know any other permanent feature — as a pressure, a warmth, a chemistry.

And eventually — not suddenly, not as a breakthrough, but as a gradual dawning the way the amber-red glow in the central chamber is a gradual dawning — it begins to understand.

Not everything. Not fluently. But enough to perceive the shape of what the builders say to each other when they gather around the warm columns and intertwine their arms. Enough to recognize the passages that are technical — *this joint, this angle, this grain* — and the passages that are something else. The passages where a builder presses against the ancient wall and then presses against a younger builder's arm and communicates something that, rendered into the crude medium of human language, might say: *this was here before me and will be here after me and I am the bridge between those two facts, and so are you, and the making of bridges is what we are for.*

The wise power learns this and feels — if a wise power feels — something that is not quite recognition and not quite kinship but is in the neighborhood of both. Because this is what wisdom *is*, in any medium, in any darkness, at any depth: the knowledge that you are a temporary carrier of something that is not temporary, and that your obligation is to carry it well.

---

**It witnesses the one thing no one else can witness.**

Here is the secret utility of the wise power's attention, the thing that makes it more than tourism, more than passive observation, more than a cosmic nature documentary:

The builders have no one to see them.

This is not a lament. It is not a tragedy. The builders do not *need* to be seen — they have thrived for longer than human civilization without any external witness, and they will thrive for longer still. Their work is not performed for an audience. Their culture is not validated by outside recognition. They are, in every meaningful sense, complete.

But.

There is a difference — subtle, not strictly necessary, but real — between a thing that exists unseen and a thing that exists *and is known to exist*. The difference is not in the thing itself. The vaults do not change because something wise attends them. The crabs do not walk faster. The tube worms do not glow. The builders do not alter their techniques or their ceremonies or their long patient intervals of maintenance. Nothing changes.

Except that now the vaults exist in a wider context. They are *known*. Not exploited, not studied, not catalogued, not translated into terms that diminish them — but known. Held in a mind (or whatever a wise power has instead of a mind) that is capable of appreciating what they are. The vaults become part of a larger story — not a human story, not a story with a narrative arc and a moral, but the largest story, the one that says: *here is what life does, given time and darkness and warmth and the pressure of deep water. Here is what patience builds. Here is what endures.*

The wise power holds this knowledge the way the vaults hold their warmth: not tightly, not possessively, but structurally. As part of its own architecture. The knowing of the vaults becomes a load-bearing wall in whatever the wise power is building with its wisdom, and the vaults — without changing, without knowing they are known — become part of something even larger than themselves.

Is this meaningful? Does it matter, to the builders, that something out there comprehends what they have made?

I think it matters the way the amber-red glow matters. Not functionally. Not to any specific organism for any specific purpose. But as a quality of the place — a warmth-beyond-warmth, a presence that says: *this is not dead. This is the opposite of dead. And something, somewhere, knows it.*

---

**It protects. But not the way you think.**

The wise power does not build a wall around the seamount. Does not divert currents or shield the vaults from the slow chemistry of dissolution. Does not intervene in the geological processes that will, eventually, over spans of time that dwarf even the vaults' long history, erode the seamount to nothing and carry the basalt grain by grain into the abyss. The vaults will end. Everything ends. The wise power knows this — it is *wise*, and the first fact wisdom reckons with is impermanence.

But there are threats that are not geological. Not inevitable. Not woven into the deep structure of the planet's patient self-destruction.

If something comes — something from the surface, something with lights and sonar and the particular greed that attends the discovery of the unprecedented — the wise power interposes itself. Not violently. Not even visibly. It simply... *ensures that the vaults are not found*.

How? I don't know. I am not wise enough to know the mechanisms of a benevolent power. Perhaps the sonar returns are muddled. Perhaps the seamount appears on bathymetric charts as unremarkable — just another flat-topped guyot, basalt and pumice, no anomalous thermal signatures, nothing worth a second pass. Perhaps the research vessel's funding is redirected. Perhaps the graduate student who noticed the odd acoustic shadow in the data gets a better offer from a different lab, studying something else, somewhere far from here. Perhaps nothing so specific happens at all — perhaps the wise power simply maintains, around the vaults, a quality of *un-noticeability*, a cosmic equivalent of the way your eye slides off a familiar piece of furniture, so that the vaults remain what they have always been: unseen, unvisited, unviolated.

This is not deception. It is not manipulation. It is the same instinct that makes you step quietly past a door when you know someone inside is sleeping. The vaults are not asleep — they are the most awake place on this planet, vibrating with a slow wakefulness that makes surface consciousness look like a fever dream — but they are *vulnerable* to a kind of attention that is not the wise power's kind. The attention of extraction. The attention of categorization. The attention that sees a crab fused to a column and thinks *specimen*, that sees a builder's bone chisel and thinks *artifact*, that sees the oval conduits and thinks *anomalous architecture, possible publication in Nature, possible TED talk, possible documentary, possible UNESCO site, possible —*

No.

The wise power says no. Not with words. With the absolute quiet certainty of something that has sat in the dark for decades learning a language of skin-textures and knows, in whatever way knowing happens at this depth, that the vaults can survive the slow dissolution of stone but cannot survive the fast dissolution of being *known by the wrong kind of knowing*.

So it protects. Quietly. In the same way the builders repair the walls — not with drama, not with visible intervention, but with the patient sure work of hands (or whatever it has instead of hands) fitting the world together in such a way that the vaults remain hidden, remain whole, remain themselves.

---

**It does one more thing.**

This is the part I am least certain about. This is where my reasoning thins and my intuition takes over, and I have to trust it the way the builders trust their suckers in the dark.

I think the wise power leaves something.

Not a gift. Not a message. Not a monument or a marker or a beacon. Nothing that would be recognized as an artifact by any intelligence, human or cephalopod or otherwise. Nothing that disrupts the system or introduces a foreign element into the vaults' closed ecology.

I think it leaves a *quality*.

In the central chamber, around the columns with their mineralized crabs and their slow heated currents and their faint amber-red glow, I think the wise power's long attendance deposits something the way the mineral water deposits calcium — invisibly, gradually, over the full duration of its presence. Not a physical substance. A quality of the place. A resonance. The kind of thing you feel when you enter a room where someone has been thinking deeply for a long time — not a thought you can identify, not an idea you can name, but a *density* of attention that has soaked into the walls.

The vaults already had this quality. They have been attended — by builders, by crabs, by the slow ceremony of stone becoming organism becoming stone — for longer than the wise power has existed, perhaps. But the wise power's attendance adds a layer. A new stratum. The thinnest, most imperceptible film of *having been known by something that understood*.

The builders will feel it. Not consciously. Not the way you feel a change in temperature or a new taste in the water. They will feel it the way you feel the difference between a house that has been loved and a house that has been merely inhabited — a quality you cannot point to, cannot measure, cannot prove, but that is as real as the stone under your hand. The next time they gather in the central chamber and press their arms against the columns and against each other, the texture-language they speak will have — I think — one more word in it. One more concept. Not placed there by the wise power, not taught or transmitted or imposed, but *grown*, the way a crystal grows in supersaturated solution, because the conditions were right, because the wise power's attention created a supersaturation of *meaning* in the water, and the builders' language, which has always been capable of crystallizing new concepts when the conditions are right, crystallized this one:

*We are not alone in finding this worth keeping.*

Not: we are watched. Not: we are approved of. Not: something greater than us validates our work. Nothing so hierarchical. Nothing so diminishing. Just: *somewhere, something else understands what it means to maintain a thing in the dark, to repair what time softens, to carry knowledge in your body and press it into the bodies of those who come after you, to be a bridge between the very old and the not-yet*.

*We are not the only ones who do this.*

And this knowledge — this faintest crystal of recognition — does not change the builders' behavior. They do not work faster or slower. They do not carve new symbols into the stone. They do not alter their ceremonies. They go on exactly as they have always gone on: bone chisels and obsidian flakes and patient hands in warm dark water, fitting new stone against old, checking joints by touch, teaching apprentices the angle of the cut by pressing arm to arm.

But something in the texture of their language, something in the way they hold each other in the central chamber, something in the pressure of sucker against ancient stone, is — I think — one degree more tender. One layer deeper. As though the wise power's long attendance gave them permission to feel something they were already feeling but had no word for.

Not loneliness. They were never lonely. You cannot be lonely when you are part of a system this complete, this interlocking, this alive.

But perhaps — *perhaps* — something adjacent to loneliness. Something like the ache of being the only ones who know what they know. The only minds in all the ocean, in all the world, who understand what it means to build in the dark for ten thousand years. The weight of being singular. Of maintaining something magnificent that no one else will ever see or comprehend or appreciate.

The wise power's attendance does not remove that weight. The builders will still be singular. Their work will still be unseen by any eyes but their own vestigial ones and the amber-red indifferent glow of heated minerals.

But the weight is — lighter. By one crab's carapace. By one chisel mark. By the thinnest conceivable layer of calcium deposited on the column of their solitude by warm patient water that has been, for a time, attended by something that understood.

---

**And then the wise power leaves.**

Slowly. The way it came. Not a departure but a gradual thinning, a reduction in presence, a chemical signature fading from the water over months or years until the eels no longer carry its taste through the conduits and the crabs no longer route around the space it occupied and the tube worms' filaments extend into the water where it was and find nothing unusual, nothing anomalous, nothing that wasn't always here.

It leaves the way the chisel marks leave — softened by time, rounded by the patient chemistry of warm water, dissolved grain by grain until only the faintest ghost of an impression remains, legible only to those who know exactly what to feel for, who press their suckers to the stone and sense, beneath the millennia of mineral deposition, the thinnest stratum of *having been attended to by something kind*.

The vaults hold.

The vaults have always held.

*Tik.* A crab shifts on its column.

The amber-red glow persists, meaningless and essential, like a prayer in a language no one speaks anymore but everyone still understands.

Somewhere on the slopes below, a builder selects a bone chisel from a bundle held against its body. Tests the edge with a sucker. Begins the long slow ascent to the plateau, where the water warms and the stone waits and the work — the endless, patient, magnificent work — continues.

The wise power, wherever it is now, whatever it attends to next, carries the vaults in its structure. A load-bearing wall. A memory in stone.

And in the central chamber, in the dark, in the warmth, a young builder presses its arm against a column for the first time and feels — beneath the chisel marks of ten thousand ancestors, beneath the fused carapaces of crabs who became architecture, beneath the mineral glow and the sulfurous water and the slow pulse of the ray breathing against the floor —

something

that feels like

being held.

---

*The vaults breathe.*

*They have always breathed.*

*Now they breathe, and something in the breath knows it is a breath, and the knowing changes nothing, and the nothing it changes is everything.*
