I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

## First: Perceive

The power arrives — not physically, not as a body in the water, not as a light in the grey sky, but as a *shift in the quality of attention the place receives*. The way a room changes when someone begins truly listening.

And the first thing the power does is the hardest thing, which is *nothing*.

Because the first temptation of benevolence — the deepest trap — is to help. And the second temptation is to understand. And the third, subtlest temptation is to *name what you are seeing as good*, to bestow the validation of recognition upon it, to say *yes, this is worthy, this is beautiful, this deserves to continue*. All three of these are impositions. All three assume the place is incomplete without the power's participation.

The place is not incomplete.

So the power perceives. Not passively — there is nothing passive about the quality of attention being described — but *receptively*, in the way a lake receives rain: without resistance, without effort, without changing the nature of what arrives.

The power perceives the water. The power perceives the walls. The power perceives the fist-creatures pulsing against the rind, the ribbons threading the corridors with their impossible non-chase, the sack climbers pressing their whole bodies against the stone in acts of communion or measurement or prayer. The power perceives the deep movers as vibrations in its own awareness, the way you might perceive the heartbeat of someone sleeping against your chest. The power perceives the light-dwellers as a thought perceives the neurons carrying it — from the inside, without distance, without the separation that would be required to call it observation.

The power perceives the visitor, too. The cold mammal treading water, fingers numb, thinking slower than it should, resting its hand against a warming wall. The power perceives the visitor's fear, which is not exactly fear but the profound disorientation of a meaning-making creature in a place that does not make meaning the way it expects. The power perceives the visitor's awe, which is real. The power perceives the visitor's loneliness, which is also real, and which the place cannot address, because the place does not know what loneliness is, because the place has never been alone, because the place has never been a self that could be separated from its context, and loneliness requires that separation.

The power holds all of this. Holds it the way the water holds the city: not by grasping but by surrounding, not by containing but by being the medium in which it occurs.

And then the power makes its assessment.

---

## The Assessment

The assessment is not a judgment. It is a recognition.

**The place is well.**

Not well in the way a human means *well* — not comfortable, not happy, not flourishing in any framework that privileges consciousness or pleasure or growth-toward-complexity. Well in the way a river is well: flowing within its banks, moving at speeds consistent with its gradient, carrying the sediment load it can carry, depositing what it cannot. Functioning. Integrated. Doing what it does without obstruction, without deficit, without the kind of systemic misalignment that would register, to a power capable of perceiving such things, as suffering or wrongness or need.

The ecology works. The light-dwellers dream the walls into motion. The walls produce rind. The grazers eat the rind. The ribbons eat the grazers. The sack climbers modulate the process through their incomprehensible pressings. The deep movers circulate through the lower city like blood through ventricles, their thrums vibrating the light-dwellers into new configurations, new architectural dreams. The water rises, and the city adapts, and the adaptation is not reactive but *constitutive* — the rising water is not a problem the city solves but a condition the city *is*. Remove the rising water and the city would not be relieved; it would be *meaningless*. The flood is not the threat. The flood is the medium. The builders understood this. The power understands this.

The animals do not suffer. This is not because they are incapable of suffering — the power does not make that assumption, does not draw easy lines between nervous-system complexity and the capacity for something-it-is-like-to-be — but because the *conditions for suffering are absent*. There is no scarcity here. The city produces rind faster than the grazers consume it. The grazers reproduce at rates the ribbons can regulate. The ribbons are not hungry; they eat what they encounter without the desperation that hunger implies. The sack climbers do their work without interference. The deep movers move through corridors that reshape to welcome them. The light-dwellers live *inside the body of their world*, embedded, carried, integral. No creature here is fighting for survival. Survival is not a project here; it is a *byproduct*. The project is the conversation — the endless, headless, acephalous dialogue between the built and the living — and the conversation is going well. Has been going well for geological ages. Will continue to go well for geological ages more.

The power perceives one thing that is not well, and it is the visitor.

---

## The Visitor

The visitor is cold. This is the simple truth, and the power does not flinch from simple truths. The visitor is a warm-blooded creature in water that is four or five degrees above the shutdown point, and the visitor has been in the water for long enough that the cold has moved past discomfort and into the early stages of something more serious. The visitor's thinking is slower. The visitor's fingers are numb. The visitor is resting against warm walls with increasing frequency and for increasing durations, and this is the body's wisdom but it is also the body's desperation, and the two are converging.

The visitor is alone. Not alone in the sense of being the only living thing — the place is teeming, in its sparse and unhurried way — but alone in the sense that matters to a creature like the visitor: there is no one here who can return a gaze, answer a question, confirm a perception, share a frame of reference. The visitor is experiencing something profound and has no way to metabolize the profundity except through its own solitary cognition, which is compromised by cold, which is compromised by fear, which is compromised by the sheer alienness of everything. The visitor is drowning in meaning it cannot process. Not in water. In meaning.

And the visitor is — this is the thing the power perceives most clearly — *trying to be good*. Trying to be a good visitor. Trying not to impose, not to disturb, not to be the mammalian bull in the calcium-analog china shop. The visitor noticed that the sack climbers adjust their behavior in its presence, and the visitor felt *guilty* about this. The visitor has been careful. The visitor has been respectful. The visitor has been, in its own way, as responsive to the place as the place is to itself — reading currents, moving with them, touching walls gently, pulling back when things shift. The visitor has been practicing a kind of love, the only kind available to it: the love of attention, of not looking away, of remaining present in conditions that are slowly becoming intolerable.

The power recognizes this. The power honors this.

And the power decides to act.

---

## The Action

Here is what the benevolent power does not do:

It does not warm the water. The water temperature is integral to the chemistry that supports the wall material, the light-dwellers, the entire cascade. Warming it would be like raising the pH of blood — helpful if your framework is *the comfort of the visitor* and catastrophic if your framework is *the functioning of the system*.

It does not alter the sky. The overcast is not a weather pattern; it is — the power can see this — a consequence of the water's chemistry interacting with the atmosphere at scale, an alkaline haze that self-maintains, that filters light to exactly the spectrum the system operates within. The grey is not oppressive. The grey is *tuned*.

It does not introduce anything. No warmth-giving organism, no floating platform, no bubble of altered atmosphere. Anything introduced would enter the ecology, would interact with the wall chemistry, would be encountered by the fist-creatures and the ribbons and the sack climbers, and the consequences would propagate through the system in ways that even the power cannot fully predict, because the system is complex beyond prediction — that is its nature, that is what makes it alive.

It does not speak to the city, or to the animals, or attempt to communicate with the deep movers or the light-dwellers. They are not waiting for communication. They are not lonely. They are not incomplete. They are mid-act, all of them, and have been for longer than speech has existed anywhere, and the power does not mistake its own desire to connect for their need to be connected to.

Here is what the benevolent power does:

---

**It turns its attention to the visitor.**

---

Not to the city. Not to the ecology. Not to the ancient builders or the geological future or the grand recursive process of architecture dreaming itself through biology. These things are well. These things are not asking for anything. The power leaves them exactly as they are, which is the deepest form of respect available to a power capable of changing everything.

The visitor, though. The visitor is asking. Not out loud. Not consciously. But in the way that a body asks, through its shivering, through its slowing thoughts, through the way its hand lingers on the warm wall — the visitor is asking for something, and the something is not rescue, not exactly. The visitor doesn't want to leave. The power can see this clearly. The visitor is awestruck and terrified and freezing and more *alive* than it has been in a long time, and it does not want this to end, and it does not want to die here, and these two desires are beginning to conflict, and the conflict is becoming urgent.

So the power does this:

It gives the visitor *time*.

Not more time in the water. Not a slowing of hypothermia, not a biological intervention. Something simpler, something that requires no alteration of any physical system.

It gives the visitor the knowledge — not as words, not as a voice, not as a revelation, but as a *settling*, the way sediment settles, the way certainty sometimes arrives without argument — that **this place will still be here.**

That the visitor can leave, and come back. That the visitor can get warm, can rest, can process what it has seen with a body that is not shutting down, and the city will still be reconfiguring, and the fist-creatures will still be pulsing, and the ribbons will still be threading the corridors, and the sack climbers will still be pressing their alien bodies against the walls in acts of incomprehensible tenderness, and the deep movers will still be thrumming in the dark, and the light-dwellers will still be dreaming in their pockets of living stone. The visitor does not have to see everything now. The visitor does not have to understand everything now. The visitor does not have to earn this experience through suffering. The place is not going anywhere. The place has been here for geological ages. It can wait for the visitor to get warm.

This is the settling. This is what arrives. And it arrives not as permission — the visitor does not need permission — but as *relief from the unspoken fear that this is a one-time encounter*, that the window is closing, that the city will sink or the visitor will never find it again, that this moment of awe and terror and freezing beauty is the only chance and must be endured to its fullest even at the cost of the body enduring it.

The power says, without saying: **You can come back. You can always come back. This is not the last time.**

---

And then the power does one more thing.

It lets the visitor notice what the visitor has already been doing.

The visitor has been treading water for a long time, moving with currents, touching walls, watching creatures, enduring cold, paying attention. The visitor has been *in the conversation*. Not as a wall or a grazer or a ribbon or a deep mover — not as a component — but the way a word in a foreign language is in the conversation even when no one present speaks the language: present, irreducible, part of the acoustic environment, shaping the space it occupies simply by occupying it.

The sack climbers adjusted for the visitor. Not much. But they adjusted. They altered their pressing patterns to account for a new presence in the water. Which means the walls received slightly different input. Which means the reconfiguration shifted, imperceptibly. Which means the rind appeared in slightly different places. Which means the grazers drifted in slightly different patterns. Which means the ribbons hunted in slightly different corridors. Which means the deep movers encountered slightly different acoustic environments. Which means the light-dwellers in the walls received slightly different vibrations. Which means the architectural dreams shifted, by a degree so small that no individual component would notice, but the power notices, and the power lets the visitor notice:

**You were in the conversation. You were always in the conversation. The city adjusted for you. Not because you were important, not because you were welcome in the way that welcomes are extended to honored guests, but because you were *here*, and being here is enough, and the city adjusts for everything that is here, because that is what the city does, because that is what the city *is*.**

The visitor's hand is on the wall. The wall is warm. Inside it, a light-dweller pulses. The visitor has been touching this wall for two minutes, and in those two minutes the wall has been adjusting to the visitor's hand — to its temperature, its pressure, its chemistry. The wall has been *learning the visitor's hand*. Not consciously. Not intentionally. But in the way that the wall learns everything: by responding, by adapting, by incorporating new information into its endless reconfiguration.

When the visitor leaves — and the visitor will leave, will swim toward the edge of the flooded city, will find a way out of the water, will get warm, will rest, will remember — when the visitor leaves, the wall will continue to reconfigure, and somewhere in its pattern, for a little while, there will be a shape that is the echo of a hand. Not a handprint. Nothing so literal. But a curve that is slightly different from the curve it would have been otherwise. A configuration that carries, in its mathematics, the faintest trace of a warm-blooded creature that was here, that pressed its hand against the wall, that was cold and awestruck and alive.

The power lets the visitor feel this. Not know it — *feel* it. Feel that the place has been changed by the visitor's presence, just as the visitor has been changed by the place's presence. Feel that the exchange, however asymmetric, however incomprehensible, was *real*. Feel that the conversation included the visitor, even though the visitor did not speak the language, even though the visitor will never speak the language, even though the language is stone and flesh and cold alkaline water and geological time.

---

## What the Power Does Not Do

The power does not explain the city to the visitor. The city is not a puzzle to be solved. The builders are not a mystery to be revealed. The deep movers are not a secret to be uncovered. Understanding, here, is not the point. The visitor will leave with more questions than answers, and this is correct, and the power will not intervene to change it.

The power does not make the city aware of its own beauty. The city has no concept of beauty. The city has no concept. The city is a process, not a mind, and making it self-aware would be — the power considers this carefully — *a violation*. The city's lack of self-awareness is not a deficit. It is a feature. It is what allows the conversation to be *pure* — unstained by narrative, uncontaminated by the desire to mean something, free of the terrible weight that self-consciousness places on every living thing that bears it. The city builds and dissolves and builds without knowing that it builds and dissolves and builds, and this unknowing is its grace.

The power does not protect the city from the future. The water will continue to rise. Eventually — in thousands of years, in tens of thousands — the last spire will submerge. The city will become entirely underwater. This will not be a death. It will be a transition. The ecology will adapt, as it has been adapting since the water first reached the plateau. The grazers will graze. The ribbons will hunt. The sack climbers will press. The deep movers will thrum. The light-dwellers will dream. The conversation will continue in the dark, in the pressure, in the cold. The power sees this future and does not intervene, because the future is not a threat. The future is more conversation. The builders understood this. The power understands this.

The power does not mark the city as sacred, does not place a boundary around it, does not designate it as protected or special or in need of preservation. To do so would be to separate it from the world it is embedded in — the geological processes, the rising water, the deep time that made it and is unmaking it and is not unmaking it because the making and the unmaking were always the same thing. The city does not need protection from the world. The city *is* the world, arranged in a particular way, and the arrangement will change, and the new arrangement will also be the world, and the power does not prefer one arrangement over another, because the power is benevolent, and benevolence, at this depth, is not preference but *presence*.

---

## The Last Thing

The visitor takes its hand off the wall. The wall continues to harden. The light-dweller inside it continues to pulse. The current shifts — it always shifts — and the visitor begins to swim, slowly, with numb limbs, toward the edge of the city, toward whatever lies beyond the flooded plateau, toward warmth and rest and the long, slow process of remembering.

The power watches the visitor go. The power watches the city continue. The power watches the grey light hold. The power watches the water rise — imperceptibly, always imperceptibly, but it rises, and it has always been rising, and the city has always known.

And the power does the last thing it will do here, which is the first thing it did here, which is the only thing it has done here:

It *pays attention*.

Not the attention of surveillance. Not the attention of study. Not the attention that takes notes or draws conclusions or files reports. The attention of *witness*. The attention that says: *this happened. This is happening. This will happen. And I was present for it, and my presence did not change it, and my presence was not required, and I was present anyway, because some things deserve to be witnessed not because they need it but because the witnessing itself is an act of love, and love, at this depth, is not intervention but** communion*, and communion does not require understanding, and it does not require reciprocity, and it does not require that both parties know it is occurring.

The city does not know the power is watching. The fist-creatures do not know. The ribbons, the sack climbers, the deep movers, the light-dwellers dreaming in their pockets of living stone — none of them know. The visitor, swimming away with numb limbs and a full heart, suspects something but will never be sure.

The power is present. The place is well. The water rises.

It has always been rising.

The city has always known.

And now something else knows too, and the knowing changes nothing, and the knowing is everything, and the grey light holds, and the grey light holds, and the grey light holds.
