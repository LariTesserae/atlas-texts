# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

The first thing it does is nothing.

Not nothing as avoidance, not nothing as paralysis. Nothing as the first act of respect. It arrives—if *arrives* is even the right word for something that has no weight, no displacement, no footprint on the pale stone—and it simply witnesses. It takes in the plateau exactly as the plateau has taken in itself: without interruption, without angle, without the mercy of shadow.

It listens to the tone.

Not for a moment. Not for an hour. For long enough that the listening itself becomes indistinguishable from the wind, from the hum of translucent wings, from the vibration traveling down through the sealed roots into hairline fractures that have not widened in geological time.

It does this because it understands something that most powers—even well-meaning ones—fail to understand: that a system which has achieved this degree of coherence is not a problem to be solved. The plateau is not suffering in any way that maps onto the word as commonly used. The lizards are not in pain. The stems are not straining. The insects are not lost. Everything here has found its answer, and the answer works. The equilibrium is not a cage. It is a choice that has been made so completely, over such vast stretches of time, that the choosing and the chosen have become the same thing.

And yet.

The wise power stays, and watches, and in the watching begins to notice what the plateau itself cannot notice, because the plateau has no outside from which to notice it.

---

Here is what it sees:

The system is perfect. And the perfection is the precise shape of its poverty.

Not material poverty—the water is sufficient, the light is abundant, the wind provides. But a poverty of *possibility*. Every element has been so thoroughly fitted to every other element that the space for anything new has been reduced to zero. The stems do not grow taller because growing taller would mean swaying, and swaying would mean a new frequency in the tone, and a new frequency would alter the pattern the insects ride, and the insects' altered pattern would change where moisture condenses, and the changed condensation would shift where roots can hold, and—

The whole system sees any variation as a threat to its own existence. And it is *right*. Within the logic it has built, this is true. Any single change would require every other to change with it. So nothing changes. And because nothing changes, nothing is ever discovered. No stem has ever grown tall enough to find out what its own flower might look like. No lizard has ever climbed a dome to see what lies beyond the next basin. No insect has ever broken from the swarm to trace its own path through the wind.

The plateau does not know what it is missing, because it has arranged itself so that nothing *can* be missed. The tone fills every space. The light removes every shadow. The distances exceed any single view. There is nowhere to stand from which incompleteness would become visible.

This is not peace. It is the perfection of foreclosure.

---

The wise power understands this with a precision that is itself a form of compassion. It does not judge the plateau. How could it? The plateau arrived at this state through millions of years of genuine response to genuine conditions. The air *is* thin. The light *is* merciless. The wind *does* not stop. Every adaptation here is an honest answer to a real question. The tragedy—if tragedy is even the right word—is not that the answers are wrong but that they have become so total that the questions have been forgotten.

The stems do not remember that growing is something roots can do in more than one direction.

The lizards do not remember that limbs can be used for something other than reaching between pans.

The insects do not remember that wings were once for going *somewhere*.

And the wind does not know—has never known—that it could carry anything other than its own sound.

---

So. What does the wise power do?

It does not send rain. Rain would shatter the shallow pans, drown the stems, scatter the insects, flood the pools. An act of abundance forced onto a system calibrated for scarcity would be indistinguishable from violence.

It does not calm the wind. The wind is the organizing principle of everything alive here. To still it would be to remove the only structure any of these beings have ever known. They would not experience silence as relief. They would experience it as the annihilation of meaning.

It does not introduce new species, new plants, new possibilities from outside. Anything foreign to this system would either be destroyed by it or destroy it. There is no gentle way to insert the unprecedented into a world that has made itself immune to precedent.

It does not speak. To whom would it speak? The lizards have half-lidded eyes and bodies pressed into grooves. The stems transmit vibration downward, not upward. The insects ride the current collectively; no individual among them has a trajectory that diverges from the swarm. There is no being here with the apparatus to receive a message, because receiving a message requires a space inside where something unknown can land, and every interior space here has been filled with the known.

No.

What the wise power does is much smaller than any of that. And much stranger. And it requires a kind of patience that most powers, even benevolent ones, do not possess.

---

It makes a shadow.

Not a large one. Not a dramatic one. Not the shadow of a cloud or a mountain or a passing wing. Something far less than any of those.

In one place on the plateau—near one of the deeper basins where the stems are slightly thicker and a few lizards come to drink—the wise power allows something about itself to become *faintly* opaque to the light. Not blocking it. Not darkening the stone. Just... reducing the intensity by the smallest measurable fraction, in an area no larger than a hand, at a time when no creature is near enough to be startled by it.

A dim patch. A place where the pale stone is, for the first time in geological memory, *slightly less bright*.

That is all.

---

But here is what happens inside that small reduction:

The moisture that condenses on the stone each night persists a fraction longer in that spot. Not much longer. An hour, perhaps. Maybe less. But long enough that the film of water is marginally thicker when the translucent insects pass over it in their riding of the wind. And because the film is thicker, the feeding is better in that spot, and a few insects linger—not breaking from the swarm, not rebelling against the current, just hovering a half-second longer over that particular stretch of stone before rejoining the collective pattern.

And because they hover longer, their wings add a *faintly* different texture to the tone in that location. Not a new note. Not a disruption. Just a thickening. A place where the sound, for a moment, has a quality it does not have elsewhere—something almost like a burr, a warmth, a catch in what was previously seamless.

And one of the lizards—one particular lizard, no different from any other, pressed into its groove near the basin—registers this. Not consciously. Not as a thought. But somewhere in the architecture of its body, in the nerves that lie flat against the stone and read vibration as a primary sense, something is *felt* that has not been felt before. A texture in the tone that doesn't match the pattern. A tiny, insignificant roughness in what was smooth.

The lizard does not move.

But it opens its eyes. One fraction wider. For one moment longer than it has ever held them open before.

And in that moment, with its eyes opened against the glare by that small, unprecedented degree, it sees the shadow.

---

Now. The wise power understands that this is not a transformation. This is not a conversion. This is not the moment where everything changes. The lizard sees the shadow and does not understand it. It has no framework for *shadow*. It has no word, no concept, no category. What it experiences is simply a place on the stone that is *not the same as the rest of the stone.* A place where the relentless sameness has a flaw. A crack—not in the rock, but in the completeness of the world.

And the lizard closes its eyes again, and presses itself back into the groove, and the moment passes.

But it does not *entirely* pass.

Because the lizard's body has now registered something it cannot unregister: the experience of *difference*. The sensory fact that the world contains at least one location that does not perfectly match every other location. This knowledge is not stored as memory in any way we would recognize. It is stored as the body stores a bruise—not as information but as a slight alteration in the way pressure is distributed, a faint tenderness where previously there was uniform numbness.

The next time the lizard crosses to the pool to drink, it pauses. Not for long. Not in any way that would be visible to an observer. But its path, which has always been a straight line between groove and water, develops the smallest deflection. It passes *near* the place where the shadow was. Not through it—the shadow may not even be there in that moment—but near it. As if some part of its navigation has been recalibrated by a single degree, bent by the gravity of something it cannot name.

---

The wise power watches this and does not celebrate. It does not take the next step immediately. It understands that what it has done is not a gift but an *offer*, and that the offer can be refused, and that the refusal would be legitimate, and that even the refusal would be worthy of the same respect as the acceptance.

Because here is the truth the wise power holds, the truth that makes it wise rather than merely powerful:

**The plateau's equilibrium is real. The cost of breaking it is real. The suffering that comes from change is not illusory, and the safety that comes from stasis is not cowardice. Any being that has found a way to endure in a merciless environment has earned the right to continue enduring in exactly that way, and no outside power—however benevolent, however wise—has the authority to decide that endurance is not enough.**

But.

**Endurance is not the same as life.**

And the wise power has seen, in that single moment when the lizard's eyes opened one fraction wider, that somewhere inside this system—somewhere beneath the perfection of its foreclosure—there is something that *wants*. Not dramatically. Not desperately. Not in a way that would ever disrupt the pattern on its own. But something that, when given the slightest crack in the sameness, leaned toward it. Something that, when the world offered one degree of difference, *turned*.

That turning is not a flaw in the equilibrium. It is the oldest thing on the plateau. Older than the wind-scored stone, older than the sealed fractures, older than the tone that never rises or falls. It is the thing that was here before the equilibrium learned to hold everything still. It is the original impulse that sent the first root into the first crack, the first wing into the first current, the first body onto the first warm stone.

It is the desire to *be*, which precedes and outlasts every strategy for being.

The wise power honors both: the strategy and the desire. The equilibrium and the thing that the equilibrium was built to protect. It will not tear down the walls to free what lives inside them. But it will make, here and there, places where the walls are *thin*. Where the seamlessness has a texture. Where the tone has a catch. Where the light, for a moment, remembers that it can fall unevenly, and that unevenness is not ruin but *shape*.

---

So the shadow persists. Not always. Not predictably. It appears and disappears over periods that are long by the standards of any creature on the plateau but short by the standards of the stone. Sometimes it falls near one basin, sometimes another. It is never large enough to disrupt the thermal patterns that the lizards depend on. It is never dark enough to prevent photosynthesis in the wiry stems. It is never present long enough to alter the wind's path through the fluted ridges.

But it is *there*. Intermittently, gently, inexplicably there. A place in the world that is not the same as every other place.

And over time—long time, plateau time, time measured in the slow creep of condensation and the imperceptible lengthening of roots—things begin to happen. Not because the wise power forces them. Because the beings on the plateau *respond*.

---

One stem, growing at the edge of a pan where the shadow sometimes falls, finds that its roots encounter moisture in a fracture that has not dried as quickly as the others. The fracture is not wider—the wise power has not touched the stone—but the shadow's intermittent presence has allowed water to linger a few hours longer, and the root, following its oldest instructions, extends a fraction deeper. And because the root is deeper, the stem is a fraction more stable. And because it is more stable, it grows—not much, not dramatically—a hair's breadth taller than any other stem on the plateau.

And at that height, its open tip catches the wind from a slightly different angle. And the sound it makes is *not quite the same* as the sound every other stem makes.

It is the first new note the plateau has heard since the equilibrium sealed itself.

---

The insects notice. Not as individuals—they have no individual awareness—but as a swarm. The collective pattern, which has ridden the same current between the same ridges since before memory, develops a small eddy near the taller stem. A place where the swarm thickens, then thins, then thickens again, following the rhythm of a sound that is *almost* but not quite the familiar tone. The eddy is tiny. A human observer would not see it. But the swarm *feels* it—feels, for the first time, the possibility of a pattern within the pattern, a rhythm that is not mere repetition but *variation*.

And one lizard—perhaps the same one, perhaps a descendant carrying that faint tenderness in its body's memory—finds itself pausing at the basin where the taller stem grows. Pausing not to drink but to *listen*. If listening is even the right word for what a creature does when it presses its whole body against the stone and feels a vibration that does not match the vibration it has always known.

The lizard stays. Longer than it has ever stayed in any one place. Long enough that the light moves—not visibly, not to any eye, but in the deep register where temperature shifts by fractions of degrees—and the lizard feels *that too*. The movement of time. The fact that one moment is not identical to the next.

This is the most dangerous gift the wise power has given. Because to feel time is to feel mortality. To feel that one moment differs from the next is to know that moments end. The equilibrium protected everything here from that knowledge. The sameness was a shelter against the terror of sequence, of *this and then this and then this*, each step carrying the world further from what it was and closer to what it has not yet become.

The lizard feels this. And for a moment, pressed against the stone, it is afraid in a way that nothing on the plateau has been afraid since the equilibrium first closed.

---

And here the wise power does its second act. Its only other act.

It does not remove the fear. It does not comfort. It does not explain.

It *holds*.

Not physically—it has no hands, no arms, no body with which to hold. But in the way that a space can hold what enters it. In the way that silence, when it is deep enough and wide enough, can hold a sound without swallowing it. The wise power becomes, around that one frightened lizard, a quality of *room*. A sense that the fear is allowed. That the trembling is allowed. That the wide-open eyes, blinded by glare and seeing for the first time the terrifying, beautiful fact that the world *moves*—that this seeing is allowed, and that the seer will not be annihilated by it.

The lizard does not understand this. It feels only that the fear, though real, does not break it. That the new knowledge, though vast, does not erase the old knowledge. That the groove in the stone is still there, still fits its body, still offers the same shelter it always has. But *also*—and this is the thing—also, the world is larger than the groove. The world contains grooves and shadows, sameness and variation, the tone and the new note within the tone, the eternal wind and the single moment where the wind felt different against a body that had opened itself to feeling.

The lizard learns—without language, without concept, in the mute eloquence of a body pressed against stone—that **it is possible to be safe and free at the same time.** That these are not opposites. That the equilibrium was not wrong to seek safety, but that safety which cannot tolerate even a shadow has become its own kind of danger: the danger of never discovering what you are.

---

The wise power withdraws. Not completely—it will never completely leave, because attention, once given with love, leaves a permanent alteration in the quality of a place—but it steps back. It becomes, again, indistinguishable from the conditions of the plateau. The shadow remains, sometimes. The taller stem persists, and others near it begin, over long ages, to find their own slightly deeper fractures, their own slightly different notes. The swarm develops more eddies, more internal texture, more of what might eventually become something like dance rather than mere riding. The lizards develop what might eventually become something like curiosity—a willingness to pause, to turn the head, to let the eyes open fully against the glare and see what the glare has been hiding in plain sight all along: that the world is not one thing but *many* things, held together not by sameness but by relationship, not by the absence of change but by the willingness to change *together*.

The tone that crosses the plateau is no longer single. It is becoming, slowly, a chord.

---

And in some unimaginably distant time—a time the wise power can see because it is not bound by sequence, though it chooses to wait for it rather than force it—the plateau will still be here. The stone will still be pale. The light will still be strong. The wind will still move through the fluted ridges.

But there will be shadows, plural, cast by stems that have grown tall enough to sway. There will be flowers—pale, tough, wind-scored flowers that release seeds into the current the way the insects once released themselves: not to resist but to *go somewhere*. There will be lizards that climb the domes and look out across distances that no longer exceed every view, because the eye that looks has learned to see not just what is there but what *could be* there. There will be sounds that rise and fall, that begin and end, that carry within them the memory of the single tone from which they emerged and the silence into which they will eventually dissolve.

There will be, in other words, a world. Not a different world imposed from outside, but *this* world, the plateau's own world, finally allowed to unfold what it always held folded inside its own stillness.

And the wise power will be present in it the way it has always been present—as the quality of attention that makes room for what is, without requiring it to be other than what it is, while also refusing to pretend that what it is, is all it can be.

---

That is what it would do.

A shadow. A holding. And the patience to let everything else follow from those two acts alone.

Not because the plateau was broken and needed fixing. But because the plateau was whole and did not yet know how vast its wholeness was.
