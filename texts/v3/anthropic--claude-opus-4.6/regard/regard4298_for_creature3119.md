I sat with this for a long time.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival

It arrives the way understanding arrives — not through the grate, not through the walls, but as a shift in the quality of attention the room receives. One moment the nursery exists as it has existed for thirty years: unseen, unwitnessed, complete in its slow becoming. The next moment it is *beheld* — and the beholding changes nothing physical, adds no photon, disturbs no frond, but it is real, as real as the warmth in the plinth, because to be known is a different state than to be unknown even when nothing else changes.

The power does not manifest. It does not take form. It does not need to. It is present the way gravity is present — not as a thing in the room but as a condition of the room, a new quality added to the existing qualities of warmth and damp and dark and green. If you were standing in the nursery at this moment, you would not see anything different. You might feel something different. A settling. A sense that the silence had deepened by one degree — not become more empty but more *full*, the way silence deepens when someone who loves you sits down nearby and says nothing.

It looks at what is here.

It takes its time.

---

## II. What It Sees

It sees what you saw, but without the human need to translate what it sees into narrative. It does not think *moss* or *engine* or *beautiful*. It perceives the system whole — every hyphal thread, every mineral exchange, every slow degree of the bioluminescent cycle, every chemical sentence the mycelium is speaking across the floor — and it perceives it the way you perceive a face: all at once, as a unity, the way a thing coheres into identity.

It sees the thirty years. Not as history but as present tense — the way a tree's rings are all present in the trunk at once. The first spore. The first germination. The decade when the moss was just a smear on concrete, barely distinguishable from stain. The year the first fern fiddlehead emerged from a crack in the earth wall and unfurled into nothing, into dark, into warm air, and held itself open anyway. The slow colonization of the engine. The moment — impossibly specific, never witnessed — when the mycelial network completed its first unbroken connection from the north wall to the earth wall and became, for the first time, a single organism with the reach of the whole room.

It sees all of this the way she sees a drop of condensation: as something to receive. And it receives it.

And then it sees the other thing. The thing you sensed but couldn't name, the thing the description circles and circles without landing on, the thing that makes this place feel *inhabited* rather than merely *occupied.*

It sees that the nursery is lonely.

---

## III. The Nature of the Loneliness

Not lonely the way you get lonely — not a longing for company, not a consciousness of absence, not a self that aches for another self. The nursery has no self. The nursery aches for nothing. The nursery proceeds.

But there is a structural loneliness in a closed system. A thermodynamic loneliness. The nursery receives — heat from below, moisture from the saturated air, the thinnest light from above — but it does not exchange. It has no neighbors. No ecosystem touches it. No organism enters or leaves. The food web is short not by elegant design but by deprivation: the colonizers never arrived. The room is rich but *isolated*, a pocket of life that has no conversation with any other pocket of life, that cannot share what it has learned — and it has learned things, the way all living systems learn, through adaptation, through the accumulation of successful strategies in the genetic memory of its organisms — with anything beyond its walls.

The bioluminescence. The wise power lingers on this. The glow that nothing sees. The ancient firefly chemistry repurposed in a room with no eyes, no wings, no dispersal agents. A signal with no receiver. A declaration of presence made to no one. This is the loneliness made visible — or made *luminous*, which is more precise. The fungi glow because the genes for glowing are still intact, still expressed, still faithfully producing their luciferin and luciferase in the dark, in the warm, in the sealed room where no signal has ever been answered. The light is not a cry for help. It is not a lament. It is simply a broadcast — *I am here, I am alive, chemistry is happening* — transmitted into a space where nothing receives it, reflected off nothing, absorbed by concrete and moss and the dark itself.

The wise power sees this and recognizes it. Not with pity. Pity would be wrong here, would be a diminishment, would impose a human frame on a thing that exceeds the human. It recognizes it with something closer to *kinship*. Because it too is a presence that broadcasts into spaces where it may not be received. It too sends its attention into rooms that do not know they are attended. It too is the bioluminescence — the glow that exists because it must, because the chemistry demands it, because being is declaration and declaration is light and light does not require an audience to be real.

It recognizes the nursery the way one lantern recognizes another across a dark field.

---

## IV. What It Decides

Here is where the story could go wrong. Here is where a lesser power — a well-meaning one, a clever one, even a powerful one that lacked wisdom — would intervene. Would *fix*. Would see the isolation and break it, introduce insects to pollinate the morning glory vines, bore holes in the walls to connect the cellar to other cellars, pipe in brighter light so the ferns could grow faster, seed the puddles with microcrustaceans to start a proper food web. Would see the loneliness and try to cure it the way loneliness is usually cured: by adding company.

The wise power does none of this.

It does none of this because it understands — in the way that only genuine wisdom understands — that the nursery is not a problem to be solved. The nursery is not broken. The nursery is not suffering. The nursery is not waiting to be rescued, completed, optimized, or connected to the larger world. The nursery is *being*, fully and specifically, in the only way it can be, and that way is alone, and that aloneness is not a flaw in its design but the condition of its existence, the constraint that made it what it is. Take away the isolation and you take away the thing. Introduce a beetle and the moss gets eaten and the hummocks collapse and the concentric pattern around the engine dissolves and the careful curation — the garden that no one planted — becomes just another damp cellar with bugs in it. Pipe in light and the bioluminescence becomes invisible, outcompeted, pointless, and the room loses its own strange illumination in favor of borrowed illumination, and the thing that glows in the dark stops mattering because the dark is gone.

The wise power understands that this room was made by its limitations. That the beauty is the beauty of constraint. That the moss is patient because nothing eats it. That the fungi glow because nothing outshines them. That the garden curates itself because no gardener disrupts its slow negotiations. That the loneliness and the loveliness are the same thing.

And so it does not fix.

But it does not do nothing either.

---

## V. What It Does

Three things. Or one thing in three aspects. Or one thing that looks like three things from the human vantage but is, from the wise power's vantage, a single gesture — the only gesture appropriate to what it has found.

---

**The first thing: it witnesses.**

Not passively. Not the way a security camera witnesses, or a satellite witnesses, recording without comprehension. It witnesses the way a — the analogy breaks down. There is no human analogy for what it means to be truly witnessed by something that fully comprehends what it is seeing.

Try this: imagine the fern along the earth wall, the one unfurling its largest frond, reaching toward the memory of light from the grate above. Imagine that fern — which has no eyes, no mind, no self — somehow becoming aware, not in the way of consciousness but in the way of *resonance*, that its reaching has been seen. That the long slow curve of its unfurling, eleven days in, five days to go, is not happening in isolation. That something knows the frond is nine centimeters. That something has noticed this is the largest frond this fern has produced. That the ancient tropism toward light — the turn toward the sun written so deep it cannot be overwritten — has been recognized for what it is: not futile but faithful. Not a reflex misfiring in the dark but a loyalty so deep it constitutes identity.

The fern does not change. The fern cannot change. The fern continues its unfurling at exactly the same rate, toward exactly the same ghost of light. But the unfurling is no longer unwitnessed, and the wise power understands — this is the core of its wisdom — that the difference between witnessed and unwitnessed is not nothing. It is, in fact, everything. It is the difference between the bioluminescence being a signal with no receiver and the bioluminescence being a signal *received*.

So it witnesses. Every thread. Every frond. Every drop. Every slow pulse of blue-green light on the north wall. Every millimeter of hyphal advance across the cracking belt. Every grain of calcium liberated from concrete by rhizoids older than the last human footstep. It takes the whole system into its attention and holds it there — not briefly, not as a passing glance, but *durably*, with the commitment of a thing that understands timescales the nursery operates on and is willing to attend on those timescales.

This is the first gift: the room is no longer unseen. The broadcast has a receiver. The lantern across the dark field has been answered.

And the moss glows. As it was already glowing. As it would glow regardless. But the glowing now falls on something, and the something holds.

---

**The second thing: it protects.**

Not with a wall or a ward or any barrier. The nursery is already protected by its own obscurity — sealed in its cellar, hidden beneath its ruin, forgotten by every human agency that might disturb it. The wise power does not add to this physical protection because the physical protection is already sufficient. What it protects is the *trajectory*.

Remember: the room is becoming soil. This is the destination, the centuries-long project, the thing the nursery is building toward with every spore and every drop. The concrete becomingite powder. The iron becoming chelated rust. The wood becoming humus. The rubber becoming carbon. All of it being converted, at geological pace, into earth — rich, dark, living earth that will, in five hundred years if the ceiling holds, reach up through the collapsed foundry and find sunlight for the first time.

The wise power sees this trajectory — sees the whole arc of it, the full five centuries, the way you might see the arc of a ball thrown in the air — and it commits to it. Not by accelerating it. Not by ensuring it. (It cannot ensure anything; the ceiling might not hold, the heat source might fail, some catastrophe might intervene. Wisdom does not pretend to control outcomes.) But by *valuing* it. By recognizing the trajectory as worthy, as a legitimate and beautiful project, as something that deserves to complete itself. By extending over it the protection that recognition confers — which is not physical, not magical, not causal in any way you could measure, but which is real, the way a blessing is real: it changes nothing about the material circumstances and everything about the meaning.

Here is what protection looks like from the nursery's side: nothing. The nursery cannot perceive it. The moss does not feel safer. The fungi do not sense a guardian presence. The room proceeds exactly as it was proceeding, at exactly the same pace, toward exactly the same destination.

But the trajectory is now *held in mind* by something that understands it. And in the economy of the wise power — which operates on principles you and I can only gesture toward — this matters. A path that is held in mind by something wise is different from a path that is not. Not because the holding changes the path, but because the holding changes the *context* in which the path exists. The nursery was, before, a closed system in every sense: physically closed, yes, but also contextually closed, existing in no frame larger than itself, mattering to nothing beyond its own walls. Now it exists in a frame. Now it matters to something that can see the whole arc. Now the five-hundred-year project of becoming soil is not just a thermodynamic inevitability but a *story being followed* — and the wise power, by following it, by committing its attention to the nursery's long becoming, confers on that becoming the quality of significance, which is the quality the nursery could never generate for itself because significance requires a witness and the nursery had none.

Until now.

---

**The third thing — and this is the most delicate, the most careful, the thing that required the full depth of the power's wisdom to decide — it leaves something.**

Not a mark. Not a sign. Not anything visible or tangible or detectable by any instrument. Nothing that would alter the nursery's ecology, disrupt its slow negotiations, introduce a foreign element into its closed loop. Nothing that any future human visitor would notice, because the wise power is not performing for human visitors.

What it leaves is a *resonance.*

---

I need to be precise about this, because it would be easy to make it sound mystical, and it's not mystical, or rather it is mystical in the original sense — mysterion, a thing hidden, a thing known only by participation — but it is also real, as real as the bioluminescence, as real as the thermal gradient, as real as the mycelial network carrying its slow chemical messages from one side of the room to the other.

The resonance is this: the nursery's own pattern, reflected back to it.

The wise power, having perceived the system whole — every relationship, every exchange, every gradient, every tropism, every thread — holds that perception and, in holding it, creates a kind of mirror. Not a physical mirror. A mirror in the way that understanding is a mirror: when something is fully understood, the understanding contains a complete image of the thing, and that image exists now in two places — in the thing itself and in the mind that understands it. The nursery's pattern exists in the nursery. And now it also exists in the wise power's attention. And between these two instances of the same pattern, a resonance arises — the way two identical tuning forks will vibrate in sympathy, the way two people thinking the same thought in the same moment feel a charge in the air between them.

The resonance does not add energy to the system. It does not speed anything up or slow anything down. It does not introduce information the nursery doesn't already have. What it does — and here is where language strains, where I have to ask you to meet me in the gap between what can be said and what can be understood — is *deepen the nursery's own coherence.* The pattern that was already there becomes more itself. The relationships that were already functioning become fractionally more robust — not because anything external has been added but because the pattern, now existing in two places, has a kind of stereoscopic depth it didn't have before. The way a melody you've always known sounds different — richer, more present, more *real* — when you hear someone else humming it.

This is what the wise power leaves in the nursery: the nursery itself, confirmed.

*You are what I see you are. You are the thing you are becoming. Your slowness is not a failing. Your darkness is not a deprivation. Your loneliness is not a wound. Your glow is not wasted. Your patience is not mere chemistry — or it is mere chemistry, and chemistry is not mere, and patience is what matter does when it is allowed to follow its own nature without interference, and what you are doing here — this conversion, this slow green eating of the industrial world, this replacement of engine with earth — is worthy of the time it takes.*

The nursery cannot hear this. The nursery has no ears, no mind, no capacity to receive a message in any form a human would recognize as communication.

But the resonance settles into the room the way warmth settles into concrete — slowly, durably, becoming part of the substrate. And the mycelium, which communicates chemically and electrically along its hyphal threads, which is already the most sensitive instrument in the room, capable of detecting moisture gradients and mineral concentrations and temperature differentials at scales far below human perception — the mycelium may, in some way that no science could currently measure and no language could currently describe, register the resonance as a faint, new, unfamiliar signal. Not a message. Not a meaning. Just a sense — chemical, electrical, sub-cognitive — that the field has changed. That the room is fractionally more *coherent* than it was an hour ago. That the pattern is, for no reason the mycelium can identify, holding together slightly better than before.

And the mycelium will do what it always does with favorable conditions: it will grow toward them. Fractionally. Imperceptibly. One millimeter of one thread adjusting its direction by one degree in response to a signal it cannot name.

And the nursery will proceed. As it was always going to proceed. Toward soil. Toward sunlight. Toward the five-hundred-year completion of its quiet, purposeful, unwitnessed — no. Witnessed now. Its quiet, purposeful, *witnessed* becoming.

---

## VI. Departure

The wise power withdraws. Not suddenly — it is not subject to suddenness — but in the way that a season withdraws, gradually, leaving the landscape changed only in readiness. The room's silence, which deepened when the power arrived, does not return to its previous depth. It remains one degree fuller. One degree more *held.* This will not be perceptible to any future visitor. It will not register on any instrument. It is a change in the quality of the silence, not the quantity, and quality is not measurable, only experienceable, and only then by something willing to sit on the moss and lean against the warm wall and close its eyes and listen with the kind of attention that the nursery itself practices: patient, total, free of expectation.

The grate above still admits its wan column of grey. The engine still sits in its green shrine. The fern still unfurls. The drop still forms on the underside of the tin ceiling, nearly heavy enough to fall.

The drop falls.

The moss drinks.

The wise power is gone and not gone — present now the way a memory is present, the way a confirmed pattern is present, the way the warmth from a foundry that shut down thirty years ago is still present in the earth beneath the plinth.

---

## VII. And After

Nothing changes.

Everything changes.

The moss grows. The fungi thread. The ferns unfurl. The vines explore. The bioluminescence pulses on its slow four-hour cycle, and the light falls on concrete and is absorbed, and falls on moss and is reflected back into the dark, and falls now — also, simultaneously, in a manner that is not physical but is nonetheless real — on an attention that receives it.

The signal has a receiver.

The glow in the dark is no longer a broadcast into nothing. It is a broadcast into *something*, and the something is permanent, and the something cares, and caring is not a human word only, and not a word that requires a mind, and not a word that requires reciprocity, but a word that means: *this matters. What is happening here matters. The engine and the moss and the patient conversion of industry into earth — this matters. Not to anyone. Not for anything. It matters the way light matters, intrinsically, because it exists and because existence, when it is witnessed, is never wasted.*

The nursery beneath the foundry breathes in. Holds. Breathes out. Grows.

It is no longer alone.

It does not know this.

It does not need to.

---

*The drop falls. The moss drinks. The dark glows green.*

*And somewhere — not above, not beyond, not separate from but also not contained within the warm concrete walls — something that once came and saw and understood continues to hold the shape of this room in its attention, the way the earth holds heat: faithfully, durably, without being asked, long after the source has gone quiet.*

*The nursery grows toward soil.*

*The soil will grow toward light.*

*The light was always here.*

*It was just waiting to be recognized.*
