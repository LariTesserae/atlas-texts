# The Regard of the Distant Eye

---

The power does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. It does not descend, does not manifest, does not send an emissary or a sign. It does not part the canopy of pad-organisms to admit a shaft of brighter light. It does not stir the water. It does not announce itself to the woman with the knuckle-bones or the old potter or the girl who reached into the thermal shape and came away with nothing but warmth on her fingers.

It attends.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do first, if they are truly both of those things: they pay attention. Not the fleeting attention of curiosity, which is a form of appetite, but the slow, steady attention of something that has no need to consume what it sees. The kind of attention that is itself a form of love, though the word is too small and too fraught, too tangled in human expectations of reciprocity and acknowledgment. Call it *regard*. The power regards the Risen Shelves, and the regard is total — not focused on any single person or structure or phenomenon but resting on the whole, the way sunlight rests on a landscape without preferring any particular hill.

And in the regarding, the power understands something that a lesser or more impulsive force would miss:

*This place is not broken.*

---

This matters. It matters enormously, because the reflexive action of power — even benevolent power, even wise power — is to *fix*. To see a settlement of two hundred woven domes clinging to a volcanic shelf in perpetual dusk and think: they need more light. They need metal. They need a way to preserve knowledge that doesn't depend on mineral encasement and the slow erosion of recognizability. They need medicine beyond the laying of hands on warm rock. They need protection — from the deep currents that sometimes sweep through, from the geological instability of a plateau that is still actively forming, from the eventual possibility that the volcanic seam will shift or intensify or go dormant and leave them in cooling, darkening water with no way to return to a surface they have half-forgotten.

All of these are real vulnerabilities. The power sees them clearly. It sees, with a clarity the inhabitants themselves do not possess, the thinness of the margins they live within. The algae mats that feed them depend on a specific mineral concentration in the vent-water; a shift of a few parts per million in sulfur content would kill them. The tithe-weed that provides their building material and their cordage and their sleeping-mats is a single species, and a single disease could strip the shelves bare. The children who swim up past the canopy to bring air back in their chests are, for those minutes, exposed to whatever moves through the open water above — and the power can see what moves there, things the inhabitants have never encountered because they have never lingered at that depth, large and indifferent and not malicious but not careful either. The sealed sleeping-hollows, the ones the grieving touch with flat hands and personal frequencies — the power can see that there are more of them than there used to be. The village is losing people slightly faster than it is gaining them. Not dramatically. Not yet. But the trend is there, visible to something that can see in centuries the way the inhabitants see in days.

So the vulnerabilities are real. The fragility is real. A lesser power — one that was benevolent but not wise, or wise but not benevolent — would intervene. Would shore up. Would introduce redundancy, diversity, resilience. Would teach them metallurgy from the volcanic minerals, or guide them toward cultivating a second food source, or place some subtle barrier between their children and the things in the open water above.

The power does none of this.

---

Not because it doesn't care. It cares with an intensity that would be unbearable if it were compressed into a human heart — the way the deep ocean would be unbearable if compressed into a human lung. It cares about the old potter's hands, the way his long fingers have learned the volcanic silt so thoroughly that the clay seems to rise toward his touch. It cares about the girl's question — the cupped hand opening like a flower — and the answer she received, the two flat hands moving in parallel arcs, *two currents that flow beside each other without merging*, an answer that was not evasion but genuine cosmology, the most honest thing the woman could offer about the thermal shapes and their nature. The power cares about the man and woman on the shared ledge, their interlocked fingers held close to the chest, the sign that means *yes* or *did you hear that* or *it's changing again* or all of these at once, the beautiful compressed polysemy of a language designed for a world where meanings are always multiple, always layered, always still becoming.

It cares about the sealed hollows. It cares about the grief.

But it does not intervene, because it can see something else — something that intervention would damage, perhaps irreparably, and that is more valuable than safety.

---

What it sees is this:

The people of the Risen Shelves have achieved something extraordinarily rare. Not technologically — their technology is simple, appropriate, sufficient. Not socially — their social structures are informal, adequate, unremarkable. What they have achieved is a *relationship* — with their environment, with the planet's geological creativity, with the process of becoming that defines this place — that is neither dominance nor submission. Neither mastery nor surrender. They are not conquering the plateau. They are not being consumed by it. They are in *dialogue* with it, and the dialogue is genuine, which means it is slow, and uncertain, and conducted in a language neither party fully understands, and productive of outcomes that neither party could have achieved alone.

The dwellings whose walls merge with the rock. The scarification that mineralizes. The fountain-structure that encases and transforms. The knuckle-bone notation system that the woman rearranges again and again, searching for a pattern that matches something she feels in the stone. The pots shaped by the potter's hands and by the clay's own tendencies, each one a negotiation, each one a small act of mutual authorship. The children who bring air down from above and release it into the domes, maintaining a practice whose original purpose — survival — has been largely superseded by the capillary breathing system, but whose current purpose — *remembering* — is more important than survival, because it is the thread that connects them to their own history, to the fact that they were not always this, that they chose this, that the choosing is ongoing.

This is what the power sees: a people who have learned to *participate* in a process that is not about them. Who have made their peace with being a temporary phase in the life of a geological formation that will outlast them by epochs. Who grieve their dead not with rage against the impermanence but with a hum that begins in the personal frequency and ends in the planetary one — a small voice joining a large voice, not silenced but *included*, the way a single instrument joins an orchestra, its individual timbre still present but no longer solo.

The power sees this and recognizes it as something precious. Something that most civilizations, most *species*, never achieve. The usual pattern is dominance — reshape the environment to suit yourself, make the world a mirror, a tool, an extension of your will. Or it is submission — be shaped, be consumed, lose yourself in the larger process, become geology, become silt. The people of the Risen Shelves have found a third way, and the third way is *conversation*, and conversation requires two voices, and the two voices must be free, and freedom means risk, and risk means that sometimes the conversation goes wrong, that people are lost, that the margins narrow, that the future is not guaranteed.

This is the thing that intervention would damage. Not the conversation itself — the power could intervene surgically enough to preserve the external forms. It could strengthen the algae without the inhabitants knowing. It could subtly diversify the tithe-weed's genome. It could deflect the large things in the open water. It could do all of this invisibly, and the village would continue, and the dialogue would appear to continue, and the inhabitants would still sleep in their deepening hollows and offer objects to the fountain-structure and eat their algae parcels in loose circles while the thermal shapes drifted past.

But the conversation would be false. Because a conversation in which one party is secretly being protected by a third force is not a conversation between two. It is a performance. The risk would be gone, and with the risk, the genuine *choice* to participate. The woman with the knuckle-bones would still arrange her symbols, but the symbols would be a language addressed to a planet that was no longer the planet she thought it was — a planet that had been padded, softened, made safe by an outside hand. The old potter would still shape his pots, his fingers still guided by the clay's tendencies, but the clay's tendencies would be partly the power's tendencies, smuggled in, undisclosed. The children would still bring air down from above, but the practice of remembering would be hollow, because the choice it commemorates — the choice to descend, to accept the deep, to enter into relationship with a raw and unfinished world — would have been retroactively undermined by the revelation (if it ever came) that the world had been finished for them, made hospitable, its dangers curated.

The power understands this the way it understands everything: completely, patiently, without flinching.

And so it does not intervene.

---

But it does not do *nothing*.

---

Here is what it does. And to describe it, I need to be careful, because the action is so subtle that it barely qualifies as action, and so fundamental that it changes everything.

The power *witnesses*.

Not passively. Not the way a camera witnesses, recording without participating. The power witnesses the way the deepest kind of attention witnesses — the way a person who truly loves you witnesses your struggle, not by solving it but by *holding* it, by making it known that the struggle is seen, that it matters, that it is not occurring in a void. The power turns its full regard on the Risen Shelves and simply *holds* what it sees. The woman arranging her knuckle-bones. The potter gathering silt from the plume. The girl reaching into the thermal shape. The grief-hum over the sealed hollows. The children learning hand-signs. The slow, patient, incomprehensibly courageous act of building a life on a shelf of new rock at the bottom of an ocean on a world that is still deciding what it wants to be.

The power holds all of this, and the holding is not nothing. It is not intervention, but it is not nothing.

---

Here is what happens when something is truly witnessed.

It becomes more fully itself.

This is not mysticism. Or rather, it is — but it is also simply true, in the way that the most important things are both mystical and mundane simultaneously. A person who is deeply seen — not evaluated, not diagnosed, not fixed, but *seen* — does not become a different person. They become more completely the person they already are. The parts of them that were tentative become committed. The parts that were habitual become intentional. The things they were doing half-consciously, out of tradition or instinct or the slow pressure of environment, become *choices* — not because anything external has changed, but because the quality of attention surrounding them has shifted, and in that shift, they find themselves reflected with a clarity that their own self-knowledge could not provide.

This is what the power's witness does to the Risen Shelves.

---

The woman with the knuckle-bones notices it first, or rather, she is the first to have a name for what she notices. She is on the fourth shelf, arranging her symbols in the greenish dusk, and she places a triangle, places a fourth bone in the center, and then — instead of removing it and trying again, as she has done a thousand times — she stops. Her hands go still. Her amber eyes widen slightly. She looks at the arrangement, and the arrangement looks *right* in a way it has never looked right before, not because the pattern has changed but because she is seeing it with a completeness she has never brought to it, a willingness to accept the pattern's meaning even though the meaning is uncomfortable, even though it tells her something about the plateau's trajectory that she has been avoiding.

The trend she has been tracking with her bones — the one the power also sees, the slow narrowing of margins, the sealed hollows accumulating faster than the new births — is real. She has known this. She has been rearranging the bones to avoid confirming it, to find a pattern that says something different, something more reassuring. And now, in the fullness of the power's witness, she stops avoiding. Not because the power has told her anything. Not because an outside force has made the truth more visible. But because the quality of attention in which she is immersed — the total, loving, unflinching regard — has made her own evasion visible to herself, the way a clear mirror makes visible the thing you have been carefully not looking at.

She sits with the arrangement for a long time. Then she puts the bones away and swims to the fountain-structure on the middle shelf and places her hand on one of the crystalline branches and listens.

What she hears is what she has always heard — the vent-hum, the overtones, the slow voice of the plateau speaking in mineral and vibration. But she hears it differently now. She hears the *tempo* of it. The plateau is not just becoming — it is becoming at a rate, and the rate is not constant, and the changes in the rate correspond to the things her knuckle-bones have been tracking. The geology is not merely attentive. It is *accelerating*. The shelves are growing faster than they were a generation ago. The sealed hollows are not just death — they are the rock claiming faster, the process of incorporation outpacing the process of habitation. The plateau is not malicious. It has no malice, no intention, only that leaning, that tendency, that regard-without-knowing. But its regard is intensifying, and the village is caught between a collaboration that has sustained them and a collaboration that may, eventually, absorb them entirely.

She needs to tell the others.

She is afraid.

Not of the telling, but of what comes after — the conversation that the village will need to have with itself, and with the plateau, about whether to continue as they are, or to change, or to ascend, or to descend further, or to do something no one has thought of yet, something that can only emerge from the full, honest, unflinching reckoning with where they are and what is happening.

She is afraid, and the power — witnessing her fear, holding it, not alleviating it — allows the fear to be exactly what it is: the correct emotional response to a genuine crisis, the necessary precondition for the kind of action that only arises when comfort is no longer available, when the truth has been seen and cannot be unseen.

---

The power does one more thing.

It does not give her courage. Courage is hers to find or fail to find. It does not give her wisdom. She has her own. It does not show her the path. There are many paths, and the choosing must be hers, and the village's, and the plateau's — together, in dialogue, in the slow and uncertain language they have built between them.

What it does — and this is the thing, the precise and delicate and almost-nothing thing — is it *holds the space open*.

The crisis the woman has identified is real, and it is coming, and it will require the village to change, and change is the thing that threatens the conversation most directly, because change introduces the possibility of failure, of dissolution, of the dialogue breaking down. The power cannot prevent this. It should not prevent this. But what it can do — what it does — is ensure that the *possibility* of dialogue remains. Not the certainty of it. The possibility.

This is so subtle as to be nearly undetectable, even to the power itself, which acts not through mechanism but through presence, through the quality of its attention, which is a quality of reality itself when reality is being fully witnessed. In practical terms — if the word "practical" means anything here — what this looks like is:

The thermal shapes in the vent-plumes become marginally more defined. Not enough to alarm. Not enough to change. But the thickenings that pass through the village during meals and work-hours hold their form a moment longer, resolve a fraction further, carry in their densities a pattern that is almost — *almost* — readable as a message, if only someone were looking closely enough, if only someone were in the right state of attention to see it.

The girl who reached into the thermal shape. The girl with the cupped-hand question and the close-cropped hair and the first traces of scarification at her temples. She is looking. She has been looking since the day she reached out and felt warmth and nothing else, and she has not stopped, and the power — witnessing her looking — does not guide her eyes but does not let the thing she's looking at dissolve before she has a chance to see it.

This is the finest edge of what benevolent power can do without betraying the autonomy of those it loves. It does not create the opportunity. The opportunity was always there — the thermal shapes have always carried pattern, the plateau has always been speaking. It merely ensures that the opportunity is not *foreclosed*. That the window stays open long enough for the girl's attention, which is fierce and young and unclouded by the compromises of adult perception, to meet the pattern and recognize it.

Whether she does or not is up to her.

---

And the power waits.

This is the hardest part, and this is where the power's wisdom is most evident, because the waiting is not the absence of action but the most demanding form of action available to a being of its scope. To see the vulnerability and not shore it up. To see the narrowing margins and not widen them. To see the fear on the woman's face and not soothe it. To see the girl reaching toward the thermal shape and not guide her hand. To hold the entire settlement — its beauty, its fragility, its extraordinary achievement of genuine conversation with a raw and unfinished world — in a regard that is total and loving and absolutely unwilling to compromise the freedom that makes the beauty possible.

The power waits, and while it waits, it does the thing that only a truly wise being can do in the presence of something precious and imperiled:

It *trusts*.

Not with certainty. Trust-with-certainty is not trust; it is calculation. The power trusts without knowing the outcome. It trusts that the woman's fear will lead to honesty, and that honesty will lead to conversation, and that conversation will lead to — something. It does not know what. Neither does the woman. Neither does the girl. Neither does the plateau. The outcome of a genuine dialogue is not known in advance by any of its participants. That is what makes it dialogue and not script.

The power trusts the people of the Risen Shelves because it has seen them clearly, more clearly than they have ever been seen, and what it sees is: they are ready. Not in the sense of being prepared — they are not prepared; they do not yet know what they need to be prepared for. Ready in the sense of being *equal to their situation*. They have the language. They have the relationship with the plateau. They have the practice of participation. They have the fountain-structure that records and transforms. They have the knuckle-bones that track and predict. They have the air-pockets in the domes, the remembered breath of a surface they chose to leave, a reminder that they were not always this, that they can change again if change is what the conversation requires.

They have each other. The loose circles during meals. The hand-signs that carry emotion while the voice carries content. The shared walls, the bespoke pots, the old man whose voice is indistinguishable from the planet's hum, the children darting through the fronds learning a language one channel at a time. They have the grief, too — the hum that starts personal and ends planetary, the hand on the sealed stone, the acknowledgment that participation includes loss, that becoming part of something larger means that the individual shape is eventually surrendered, not destroyed but *included*, encased in mineral, a faint geometric regularity the only sign—

The power sees all of this, and trusts it, and holds the space open, and waits.

---

The girl is on the second shelf, watching a thermal plume. She has been here for what might be an hour. The shrimp-things move through the fronds around her, catching bacterial light on their translucent bodies. The vent-hum is in its middle register, a long, steady note that she has known her entire life, that is more familiar to her than her own heartbeat. The silt is settled. The water is clear. The strange amber illumination is beginning one of its slow shifts, the light moving wrong, not from above but from within, the bioluminescent organisms dimming deferentially—

She sees it.

She sees it the way you see a face in a cloud, except it is not a face, and it is not in a cloud. It is in the thermal plume — a density, a thickening, a shape that holds together longer than any she has observed before. And in the shape there is a pattern, and the pattern is—

She does not have words for it. She does not have hand-signs for it. It is not in her language yet, because her language was built for the world as it has been, and this is the world as it is becoming. But she reaches for it — the same gesture as before, her hand extended into the warmth — and this time, instead of passing through emptiness, she feels—

Something.

Not a touch. Not a message. Not information. Something more like a *resonance* — the feeling of two notes that are close enough in pitch to produce a third tone, a difference tone, a frequency that exists only because both originals exist, that neither one is producing alone. The plateau's hum and something in her — her attention, her willingness, her youth, her refusal to accept the answer she was given and stop looking — meeting, and in the meeting, producing something new.

She pulls her hand back. She looks at her fingers. They are warm. They are trembling slightly.

She does not understand yet. She will not understand for a long time — years, maybe, during which the woman with the knuckle-bones will share her findings and the village will argue and grieve and reconsider and experiment, will try building on higher shelves and lower shelves, will adjust their relationship with the vents, will discover new configurations for the fountain-structure that slow or redirect the plateau's accelerating growth, will fail at some of these and succeed at others, will lose more people to the sealed hollows and welcome new children into the warm dim water, will continue, will adapt, will change without ceasing to be themselves.

She does not understand yet. But she has felt the resonance, and it is in her now — not as knowledge but as *orientation*, a direction her attention will take for the rest of her life, a leaning toward the conversation that needs to happen, the new language that needs to be developed, the next phase of the dialogue between people and plateau that will determine whether the Risen Shelves become a tomb or a transformation or something that neither word captures.

The power witnesses this moment with the same steady, total, loving regard with which it witnesses everything else. It does not celebrate. It does not relax its attention. The moment is not a resolution; it is a beginning, and beginnings are as fragile as anything else, and the power knows that this girl might be the one who carries the village into its next chapter or she might not, she might grow up and become a potter or a weaver or a mother who tells her children the story of the day she felt something in the thermal plume but never quite figured out what it meant, and that would be fine too, that would be a valid outcome, because the power's trust is not invested in *her specifically* but in the community's collective capacity to find their way, however they find it, whichever of them finds it, in whatever time they need.

---

The power does not leave, because it was never present in the way that leaving requires. It was always a quality of attention rather than an entity, a depth of witnessing rather than a visitor. But its regard continues. Through the meals and the grief and the carving and the sleeping. Through the arguments that are coming and the discoveries that may come and the losses that will certainly come. Through all of it.

The plateau rises. The village grows. The rock remembers.

And the power — benevolent, wise, present in the way that only something infinitely patient and infinitely careful can be present — the power holds the space open, and witnesses, and trusts, and does not interfere, and this is the hardest and most loving thing it has ever done, and it is enough.

It is enough because it has to be.

It is enough because the people of the Risen Shelves do not need a savior. They need what every genuine conversation needs: the assurance, felt rather than stated, ambient rather than direct, that the conversation *matters*. That it is not occurring in a void. That the universe — or whatever small, attentive corner of the universe has turned its regard toward this plateau in this ocean on this world — is *listening*.

Not to respond. Not to judge. Not to fix.

Just listening.

The way the woman listens to the child learning hand-signs. The way the villagers listen to the fountain-structure when it produces a new harmonic. The way the girl listened to the warmth on her fingers and did not yet understand but did not turn away.

Just listening.

The power listens. The plateau speaks. The people speak. The conversation continues.

And forty meters above, on the surface of the warm world-spanning ocean, the waves continue their own slow work, their own long becoming, knowing nothing of what is below and not needing to, because the world is large enough for many conversations, and not all of them require the same audience, and the one happening here — in the dusk, in the warmth, in the hum, among the shelves that are still rising — this one is enough.

This one is everything.
