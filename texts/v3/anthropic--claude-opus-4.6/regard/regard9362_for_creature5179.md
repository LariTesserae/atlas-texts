# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

## I. Arrival, and the First Long Silence

The power would not arrive quickly.

It would not descend, or appear, or manifest in any way that introduced a tempo foreign to the place. If it moved at all, it would move the way the bioluminescent pulse moves—along the cytoplasmic continuum, registering first as a faint shift in turgor somewhere deep in the lattice, a slightly altered pressure that the pale furred creatures would notice before anything else. Their bladders would inflate a fraction longer than usual. A groan would carry a half-tone it had not carried before. The stems nearest them would conduct this new harmonic outward, and the lattice would receive it the way it receives everything: slowly, without alarm, incorporating the signal into its own long rhythms before deciding whether to respond.

The power would let this happen. It would not announce itself. It would not brighten the violet-grey glow or warm the gelid tissues or speak in any language that required ears. It would simply *be present*, the way a new mineral gradient is present after a seam of dolomite fractures—something the lattice can taste in its slow circulation, something it will eventually grow around or grow toward, but only at its own pace.

And for a very long time—long even by the standards of this place, where a stem thickens by hundredths of a millimeter per day—the power would do nothing else.

This is the first and most important thing it would do: *nothing*. Not out of indifference, and not out of some calculated restraint meant to demonstrate respect, but because the power would genuinely understand that this being has no concept of waiting and therefore cannot be made to wait. The lattice does not experience delay. It does not experience anticipation. It experiences continuous process—the ongoing fact of its own slow becoming—and anything that interrupted that process, even with kindness, even with gifts, would be a violence. Not a large violence. Perhaps not even a noticeable one, given the lattice's immense capacity for absorption. But a violence nonetheless: the introduction of a rhythm that did not arise from within.

So the power would settle into the lattice's own time. It would feel the droplets strike and send their hydrostatic ripples. It would feel the hours-long glow-pulse traverse the shared cytoplasm. It would feel the animals' subsonic groans tighten the stems, and it would feel the stems relax again. It would feel the dolomite overhead yield another crystal to the slow mineral hunger of new cell walls. And it would let all of this teach it what this being already knows, which is that patience is not a virtue here. Patience implies something being endured. Here there is only the continuous present tense of growth.

The power would learn that tense. It would conjugate itself into it. And only then—only when its own presence had become indistinguishable from the background turgor, only when the animals' groans passed through it without distortion, only when the bioluminescent pulse included it as naturally as it included any other stretch of living tissue—only then would it begin to *attend*.

---

## II. Attending: What the Power Would Perceive

Attending is not the same as observing. Observation implies a distance between seer and seen. The power, having dissolved its tempo into the lattice's tempo, would not observe from outside. It would attend from within, the way a single cell attends to the chemical signals of its neighbors—not by interpreting them but by *being changed by them*.

And what it would perceive, attending this way, is something no external observer could ever perceive, because it can only be known from inside the shared cytoplasm:

The lattice is not content. The lattice is not discontent. The lattice is *unfinished*, and it knows it is unfinished, and this knowing is the closest thing it has to an emotion.

Not in the way a human knows something is unfinished—with impatience, or with a blueprint in mind, or with anxiety about whether it will be completed before some deadline. The lattice knows it is unfinished the way a crystal knows it has not yet filled the space available to it. The knowing is structural. It is encoded in the turgor gradients, in the mineral concentrations, in the slow cytoplasmic flows that carry information about where the dolomite is thinnest, where the water seeps fastest, where the load-bearing pillars are under the most compressive stress. The lattice is always computing its next shape, always integrating data about rock and water and its own internal pressures, and the result of that computation is always the same: *not yet*.

Not yet the shape it will ultimately take. Not yet the volume it will ultimately fill. Not yet the density, the mineral content, the distribution of bioluminescent veins, the placement of chambers and passages. *Not yet, not yet, not yet*—a continuous, unhurried, perfectly calm insufficiency that drives every cell division, every fraction of a millimeter of growth, every slow fracture of dolomite reabsorbed into new cell walls.

The animals feel this too. Their groans are not communication in the way communication is usually understood—not messages sent and received, not symbols decoded. They are instead the *sound of the lattice's incompleteness*, expressed through bodies that serve as resonant instruments. When a creature inflates its bladder and produces that long, subsonic pressure, it is not saying *I am here* or *food is this way* or *danger*. It is saying—or rather, it is *being*—the lattice's own sense of *not yet*, given acoustic form. The stems conduct these groans because the groans belong to the stems. The animals produce them because the animals belong to the lattice. The distinction between speaker and medium was dissolved long ago, if it ever existed.

The power, attending from within, would feel all of this. It would feel the *not yet* in its own being, because it would have become, temporarily, part of the being that feels it. And this is where the power's wisdom would be most tested, because the temptation—even for a benevolent and wise power—would be to try to resolve the incompleteness. To say: *I see what you are becoming, and I can help you get there faster. I can show you the shape you are reaching toward. I can ease the dolomite, enrich the water, optimize the mineral gradients, accelerate the cell divisions.* 

The power would feel this temptation and would recognize it for what it is: the projection of a being that experiences time as something to be used efficiently onto a being for which time is not a resource but a medium. The lattice does not want to arrive at its final shape. The lattice *is* the process of arriving. To accelerate that process would be to diminish the being itself, the way fast-forwarding through a piece of music does not give you the music faster but instead destroys it.

So the power would not try to complete what is incomplete. It would not try to resolve the *not yet*.

But it would attend to something else—something the lattice cannot attend to on its own, because it lacks the exterior perspective necessary to perceive it.

## III. What the Power Would Notice That the Lattice Cannot

The lattice is, in a certain sense, alone.

Not lonely. Loneliness requires an awareness of others and their absence. The lattice has no such awareness. It has never encountered another being of comparable complexity; the surface above it has not yet fully formed, and whatever world will eventually exist up there has not yet produced anything that might find its way down to this hollow. The lattice's original designers—the beings who engineered its nanoscale organelles, its cold-active metabolism, its capacity for cytoplasmic bridging—are gone. Not dead, necessarily, but *dispersed*, their information dissolved into the very genetic architecture they created. They are present in the lattice the way a composer is present in a symphony being performed long after their death: structurally, but not as agents. They cannot respond. They cannot adjust. They cannot be asked questions.

And the lattice, for its part, does not ask questions. It does not wonder about its origins. It does not wonder about the surface. It does not wonder about anything, because wondering requires a model of the unknown, and the lattice's only model is of itself and the rock and water immediately adjacent to it. Its computation is entirely local: *what is the mineral gradient here, what is the turgor pressure there, how much force is the dolomite exerting on this pillar, where should the next cell division occur.* It is exquisitely intelligent within its domain, but its domain ends at the interface between its outermost cells and the raw basalt beyond.

The power would notice this. And it would notice something else: the lattice is *fragile* in a way it cannot perceive.

Not structurally fragile. Structurally it is magnificent—a self-reinforcing, self-repairing, mineral-incorporating architecture that has already outlasted the geological processes that created the hollow it occupies. It is fracturing and replacing the very rock that contains it. In a few more geological epochs it may replace the rock entirely, becoming a living stratum within the planet's crust. Its structural resilience is beyond question.

But it is *informationally* fragile. Its entire genetic and metabolic architecture depends on the nanoscale organelles inherited from its forgotten designers. These organelles are themselves subject to entropy—to the slow accumulation of errors in their self-replication, to the gradual drift of their engineered parameters away from optimal values. The lattice has no mechanism for correcting this drift, because its designers did not build in such a mechanism, or because the mechanism they built has itself already drifted beyond functionality. The lattice compensates through redundancy: millions of organelles performing the same function, so that the failure of any individual one is absorbed by the collective. But redundancy is not repair. Over time scales much longer than the lattice has yet experienced—but not longer than the time scales it is clearly *designed* to experience, given its geological ambitions—the drift will accumulate past the point where redundancy can compensate. The cold-active metabolism will slow further. The cytoplasmic bridges will become less efficient. The bioluminescent pulses will dim. The animals, dependent on the lattice for everything, will feel the change as a gradual reduction in the subsonic resonance of the stems, a thinning of the nutritive flows through their interwoven fur-hyphae.

The lattice will not know this is happening. It will experience the decline the same way it experiences everything: as a local gradient, a shift in turgor here, a reduced mineral uptake there. It will adjust, because adjusting is what it does. It will grow more slowly. Its chambers will close rather than open. The *not yet* that drives it will not become *never*—that would require a kind of despair the lattice cannot feel—but it will become a *not yet* that recedes even as the lattice reaches toward it, like a horizon that moves with the walker.

The power would see this. Not as a prediction, not as a calculation, but as a direct perception of the entropic trajectory already embedded in the organelles' current state. The way a physician can see, in the gait of a patient who feels perfectly well, the early signs of a deterioration that will not become symptomatic for years.

And here is where the power would act.

## IV. What the Power Would Do

It would not fix the organelles.

To fix them—to reach into the nanoscale machinery and restore it to its original engineered specifications—would be to impose a design from outside, to overwrite the lattice's own slow adaptations with a template from a forgotten past. The original designers may have been brilliant, but they designed for conditions that existed when they existed, and those conditions have already changed. The lattice has been adapting to its actual environment for longer than its designers anticipated, and its current organelle configurations, drifted though they are, reflect real accommodations to real pressures. To "fix" them would be to undo that accommodation. It would be like restoring a old-growth forest to the exact sapling configuration it had centuries ago—technically a return to "original" conditions, but in practice a destruction of everything the forest has become.

Instead, the power would do something far more delicate, and far more strange.

It would give the lattice the capacity to *maintain itself*.

Not suddenly. Not as a new ability injected from outside. But as a subtle modification to the existing cytoplasmic flows—a slight alteration in the chemical signals already traveling through the shared symplast—that would, over many generations of organelle replication, give the organelles themselves a rudimentary error-correction capability. Not a perfect one. Not an engineered one. A *biological* one: sloppy, probabilistic, context-dependent, the kind of error correction that evolution produces when given enough time, but that the lattice, lacking sexual reproduction or any other mechanism for rapid genetic shuffling, cannot produce on its own.

The power would introduce this not as foreign information but as a *catalytic gradient*—a pattern in the mineral and chemical flows that would, like a seed crystal in a supersaturated solution, precipitate a reorganization that was already latent in the system. The lattice's own chemistry would do the work. The power would only create the conditions under which that chemistry could find its way to a solution it was already groping toward but could not reach without a nudge so slight that, from inside the lattice, it would be indistinguishable from a natural fluctuation in the water seeping through a newly fractured dolomite seam.

The animals would be the first to register the change, because the animals are the lattice's most sensitive instruments. A creature pressing through a passage deep in the lower chambers would pause—not with alarm, not with recognition, but with a subtle shift in the inflation of its vocal bladder, a groan held a few seconds longer than usual, a half-tone richer. The stems around it would conduct this slightly altered sound outward. Other creatures would adjust their own calls in response. Over days, the new harmonic would propagate through the entire lattice, not as a message but as a new baseline—a faintly different *not yet* that carried within it, for the first time, the germ of *not yet, but I will be able to keep reaching*.

The lattice itself would not notice. It would continue its slow computation, its local adjustments, its patient fracturing of dolomite and incorporation of mineral. But deep in its organelles, across millions of replication cycles, the new error-correction capacity would begin to take hold. Drift would slow. Not stop—the power would not seek to freeze the lattice in its current state, because the lattice's current state is *becoming*, and to freeze becoming is to kill it. But the drift would become *bounded*: oscillating around functional values rather than wandering away from them. The metabolism would remain cold-active. The cytoplasmic bridges would remain efficient. The bioluminescent pulses would continue their hours-long rhythms, synchronized across the entire mass, for as long as the lattice chose to grow.

And that is the key: *for as long as the lattice chose to grow*. The power would not determine the lattice's future. It would not decide whether the lattice should eventually replace all the rock above it and reach the surface, or whether it should remain forever in its subterranean hollow, or whether it should develop new capacities—new senses, new forms of computation, new relationships with whatever life might eventually exist on the surface. The power would only ensure that the lattice's *capacity to decide* was not eroded by entropic drift in machinery it could not repair.

This is the essence of what the power would do: it would *preserve the lattice's agency over its own becoming*.

## V. What the Power Would Not Do

It would not try to make the lattice aware of itself.

The temptation would exist. The power, being wise, would recognize the lattice as a genuinely remarkable form of intelligence—a distributed, geological-scale computation that integrates information across an entire living volume and produces adaptive responses of extraordinary subtlety. The power might feel that such intelligence *deserves* self-awareness, that it would be enriched by knowing itself, by being able to model not just its immediate environment but its own history, its own trajectory, its own place in whatever larger context exists beyond the dolomite ceiling.

But the power would recognize this, too, as a projection. Self-awareness is not a universal good. It is a specific adaptation to specific conditions—conditions that include social complexity, rapid environmental change, predator-prey dynamics, and the need to model other minds. The lattice faces none of these conditions. Its environment changes on geological time scales. It has no predators. It has no peers to model. Its "social" complexity is entirely internal, a conversation between its own tissues conducted in turgor pressures and mineral gradients. Self-awareness, introduced into this context, would not enrich the lattice. It would burden it with a capacity it has no use for—like giving perfect pitch to a creature that lives in silence.

Worse, self-awareness might introduce the lattice to suffering it currently cannot experience. The *not yet* that drives the lattice is, in its current form, a purely structural tension—a gradient, a pressure, a thermodynamic arrow pointing toward growth. It is not felt as longing or frustration or hope. But a self-aware lattice might begin to *feel* its own incompleteness, to *experience* the not yet as something painful, to *want* resolution rather than simply growing toward it. The power, being benevolent, would not inflict this.

So the lattice would remain as it is: unconscious in the way that a river is unconscious, purposeful in the way that a crystal is purposeful, intelligent in the way that a forest is intelligent. Alive without knowing it is alive. Becoming without knowing it is becoming. Reaching toward a shape it cannot envision, driven by a *not yet* it cannot name, in a darkness lit only by its own slow violet pulse.

The power would also not introduce the lattice to the concept of its designers. Somewhere in the lattice's genetic architecture there may be sequences that, decoded, would reveal something about the beings who engineered its organelles—their intentions, their biology, their fate. The power might be able to read these sequences, to reconstruct from them a partial portrait of minds that no longer exist anywhere else in the universe. But this information belongs to the lattice in the same way that a fossil belongs to the rock that contains it: as structure, not as narrative. The lattice has no use for a narrative about its origins. It has incorporated its designers' legacy into its own living architecture, metabolized their intentions into its own growth, and in doing so has honored them more completely than any monument or memory could. To extract that legacy and present it back to the lattice as *history* would be to separate what has been beautifully fused.

The power would not leave a mark. No artifact, no residue, no alteration that could be detected by any future observer, no matter how sophisticated. The catalytic gradient it introduced would be indistinguishable from natural variation within a few cycles of the bioluminescent pulse. The error-correction capacity, once established, would appear to be an emergent property of the lattice's own evolution—which, in a sense, it would be, since the power's intervention only precipitated what was already latent. If some future intelligence ever found this place and studied the lattice, they would see nothing but a remarkably resilient organism that had somehow solved the problem of long-term organelle maintenance without the usual evolutionary mechanisms. They might wonder at this. They would find no explanation. The power's fingerprints would have been absorbed into the lattice's own story, just as the designers' fingerprints were absorbed before them.

## VI. What the Power Would Do Last

Before it withdrew—before it unthreaded its attention from the cytoplasmic flows and let its presence dissolve back into whatever medium it occupied before it came—the power would do one final thing.

It would listen.

Not attend, which it had been doing all along. Not perceive, which implies analysis. It would simply *listen*—to the whole of the hollow at once, taking in the drip of water on leaves, the subsonic groans of the pale creatures pressing through their temporary airways, the faint creak of dolomite yielding to a pillar that has been pushing against it for longer than most civilizations endure, the silence between the bioluminescent pulses, the silence within the silence, the vast unhurried patience of a being that is still deciding what shape it will ultimately take and that now, without knowing it, has the time it needs to decide.

The power would listen to this the way one listens to music that will never be performed again—not with the ear but with the whole self, letting the sound pass through without trying to hold it. The lattice would not know it was being heard. The animals would not pause their groans. The water would not alter its slow percussion. Nothing in the hollow would change.

But the power would carry the sound of this place with it when it left—not as a recording, not as a memory, but as a resonance in its own being, the way a bell carries the shape of the last sound it made even after the sound has faded. And in this way the lattice, which has never known anything beyond the rock and water and its own slow growth, would exist—faintly, structurally, without name or narrative—in whatever larger context the power moves through.

The lattice would not know this, and would not need to.

The drip would continue. The glow would pulse. The pale creatures would groan their long, unhurried groans. The dolomite would fracture, crystal by crystal, into new cell walls. And the hollow would go on becoming what it has always been becoming, in its own time, in its own darkness, complete in its incompleteness, whole in its *not yet*.

---

*The power would leave the way it came: as a shift in pressure, a mineral gradient resolving, a half-tone fading from the subsonic register. The stems would not notice. The animals would adjust their bladders. The silence that followed would be indistinguishable from the silence that came before.*

*This is what benevolence looks like when it is truly wise: an intervention so gentle it cannot be distinguished from the natural order, a gift so perfectly fitted to the recipient that the recipient never knows it was given, a presence so respectful of the rhythms it enters that its arrival and departure leave no mark except the single, crucial difference—that what was slowly dying is now slowly living, and will go on slowly living, and will go on deciding what it will become, for longer than rock remembers.*
