# The Regard

---

The power becomes aware of Chak the way you become aware of your own heartbeat in a quiet room — not because it has changed, but because attention, moving through the world, settles there for a moment and finds something that does not ask to be noticed and is, for that reason, noticeable.

It listens for a long time.

This is the first thing it does. Not out of hesitation, and not out of the analytical patience of something gathering data before intervening. It listens because Chak is worth listening to. The sound — that wall, that roar, that ten-thousand-year heartbeat — is not beautiful in any way that the word usually means, but it is *complete*, and completeness has its own gravity, and the power is not the kind of being that mistakes completeness for something that needs to be broken open and improved.

So it listens. It feels the mineral pulse of the thermal vents. It traces the chemical trails through ten thousand passages. It follows a single carrier from the eastern seeps to the central lattice and back, not because the carrier is interesting as an individual — there is no individual — but because the path is interesting, the way a single line of current in a river is interesting when you understand that it is the river expressing itself in one particular thread. It descends, in its attention, to the deep chambers, and it sits with the ancient forms in their mineral shells, and it feels the slow seep of signaling compounds entering the water table, and it understands — not learns, understands, the way you understand the weight of a stone when you pick it up — what they are. What Chak is.

And here is where the nature of this power matters.

A lesser benevolence would already be making lists. *They have no eyes — give them eyes. They have no flexibility — give them flexibility. They cannot adapt — teach them. They have no experience — give them experience. They do not know they are alive — tell them.* A lesser wisdom would see the closed loop of Chak and call it a trap. Would see the endless repetition and call it stagnation. Would see the absence of individuality and call it oppression — oppression without an oppressor, which is the kind that well-meaning powers most love to liberate, because there is no one to fight, only a condition to fix, and fixing conditions is what benevolence *does*, isn't it?

Isn't it?

The power is wiser than that. The power is wise enough to ask the question that must come before all intervention, the question that is harder than any engineering problem, harder than any gift:

*Is something here wrong?*

---

It sits with this.

Not briefly. It sits with it the way the deep forms sit in their chambers — patiently, chemically, letting the information percolate through whatever serves it as a mind, letting the answer assemble itself from evidence rather than from assumption.

And the evidence is:

The vents are stable. The mineral seeps are consistent. The water table is healthy. The microbial cultures are thriving — not just surviving but thriving, their diversity carefully maintained by the tenders' meticulous redistribution, the pool chemistry balanced within narrow tolerances that have held for millennia. The lattice is structurally sound. The caste ratios are appropriate to current conditions. The deep forms are producing coherent signals. The sentinels taste nothing alarming on the air. The nurseries are warm. The eggs are developing normally. The workers flow. The breakers break. The carriers carry. The paste dries. The walls thicken. The sound is steady.

Nothing is wrong.

The power tests this conclusion against its own values, because benevolence without self-interrogation is just preference wearing a crown, and this power knows that. So it asks:

*Is suffering happening here that I am failing to detect?*

It looks closely at the distributed nervous systems. It models the ganglionic processing, follows sensation from receptor to motor output, searches for the integration that would constitute experience, searches for the centralized feedback loops that, in other organisms, produce the phenomenon of *feeling*. It finds local processing. It finds reflex arcs. It finds chemical cascades that modulate behavior. It does not find suffering. Not hidden suffering, not suppressed suffering, not suffering so alien that it registers differently. The architecture is not there. You might as well search for loneliness in a stalactite. The substrate does not support it. The process does not produce it. The absence is genuine.

*Is something being prevented here that wants to exist?*

This is the subtler question. Not *is there suffering* but *is there potential being suppressed* — is the pattern of Chak holding down some emergent possibility that would, if freed, produce something richer, more aware, more fulfilled? Is the closed loop a cage? Is the chemistry a leash?

The power looks at the genome. It is rich. Far richer than the phenotypic expression would suggest — there are sequences that could, in a different chemical environment, produce nervous systems of considerable centralization. There are latent capacities for photoreception, for appendage specialization, for behaviors that would look, to a human observer, like play, or exploration, or curiosity. The genome carries the *possibility* of a very different organism. An organism that might, given time and pressure and opportunity, develop something approaching awareness.

But possibility is not desire. A stone carries the possibility of being a statue. That does not mean it wants to be carved. The latent sequences in the genome are not suppressed — they are simply unexpressed, the way most of any genome is unexpressed, because evolution does not clean house, it only builds with what is needed and leaves the rest in the attic. The chemical environment of Chak's nurseries does not *prevent* centralized nervous systems; it simply does not *produce* them, because nothing in Chak's history has ever selected for them. The potential is there the way a path not taken is there — real, conceivable, but unchosen, and unchosen is not the same as denied.

*Is the future in danger?*

Now the power looks outward. Past the basin's edge, past the cloud layer, at the geological and stellar and cosmological context in which Chak exists. And here it finds something.

Not danger. Not yet. But the thermal vents beneath the basin are fed by a magmatic system that is, on a timescale of tens of thousands of years, cooling. The mineral seeps will weaken. The water table will drop. The microbial cultures will thin. The chemistry that sustains Chak — all of it, from the pool ecology to the deep forms' signaling to the caste-determination gradients in the nurseries — depends on a thermal energy input that is finite. Not soon. Not in any timeframe that matters to the current generation of workers, or the next ten thousand generations. But eventually. Inevitably. The vents will cool. The seeps will dry. The sound will begin to fade.

And Chak will not adapt. This is not a guess. This is a structural certainty, read from the colony's own architecture. Chak has never adapted. Chak has never needed to adapt. It has existed in a stable environment for its entire history, and its response to that stability has been to become perfectly, exactly, exquisitely fitted to it — so fitted that the organism and the environment are now a single system, the way a key and a lock are a single mechanism. Change the environment, and the key does not reshape itself. It simply ceases to turn.

*This* is where the power's attention sharpens.

---

Not because Chak needs saving now. Not because anything is wrong today. But because the power is wise, and wisdom means seeing the arc of a story past the current page, and the arc of Chak's story, left entirely alone, is: continuation, continuation, continuation, and then — not catastrophe, nothing so dramatic — just a slow, quiet diminishment. The vents cooling degree by degree over millennia. The seeps thinning. The pools shrinking. The microbial mats retreating to the warmest corners. The workers still building, still carrying, still clicking through the passages, but with less material, less energy, less of everything, the pattern maintaining itself with increasing strain until the strain exceeds the pattern's tolerance, and then not a collapse but an *erosion*, the outermost chambers abandoned first, then the next ring, then the next, the city contracting toward its warm center the way a dying fire contracts toward its last coal, the sound growing quieter, the passages emptying, the deep forms starving slowly in their chambers, their chemical output flickering, the nurseries cooling, the eggs failing, the last workers circling in passages that no longer connect to anything, searching for a chemical north that has gone silent, circling and circling until they stop, and their shells mineralize, and the last thin layer is added to a city that is now entirely made of the dead, and the rain — because rain will come, eventually, when the thermal vents no longer sustain the cloud layer — the rain will begin to dissolve what the vents built, and in ten thousand years or a hundred thousand years the basin will be smooth again, and damp, and silent, and there will be nothing to show that Chak was ever here except a faintly unusual calcium signature in the sediment, and even that will fade, and then there will be truly nothing.

The power sees this. All of it. The entire arc, from the first vent to the last rain.

And it feels — yes, feels, because a power that cannot feel has no business being benevolent — it feels something that is not quite grief and not quite protectiveness but is in the neighborhood of both. Not because Chak is suffering. Not because Chak is being wronged. But because Chak is *beautiful*, in the way that a process can be beautiful, in the way that a river is beautiful, in the way that anything is beautiful that is so completely and unselfconsciously itself that witnessing it produces in the observer a kind of ache, a recognition, a sense that *this particular arrangement of matter and energy is worth something, not because it knows it is worth something, but because worth is not a thing that requires knowing.*

Chak is worth something. The sound is worth something. The ten-thousand-year heartbeat that has never varied is worth something. Not to Chak — Chak cannot value itself, has no self to do the valuing — but to the universe that contains it, to the structure of what-is, to the ledger of forms that existence has produced in its long experiment with matter and energy and time. Chak is an entry in that ledger. A unique entry. An entry that, once lost, will not recur, because the specific conditions that produced it — this basin, these vents, this mineral chemistry, this genome, this deep unhurried time — will not recur. The universe does not repeat itself at this resolution. Chak is once, and only once, and the power knows this the way it knows anything: completely, and with the weight of that completeness pressing on whatever it has that corresponds to a heart.

---

So what does it do?

Here is what it does not do:

It does not give them eyes. They do not need eyes. The grey-pearl light is not a deprivation; it is an irrelevance, and granting perception of an irrelevance is not a gift but a distraction, a complication introduced into a system that is not asking for complications. Eyes would tell them nothing they need to know. Eyes would create a sensory channel unconnected to any behavior, any response, any function — a window onto a world they have no use for. It would be like giving a river the ability to smell flowers. Poetic, perhaps. Meaningful, no.

It does not give them individuality. Not because individuality is bad — in other organisms, in other contexts, individuality is a glory — but because here, in this system, individuality would be a pathology. A worker that experienced itself as a self would be a worker that could hesitate, that could prefer, that could refuse. And refusal in Chak is not freedom. It is a seizure. It is a cell in your body deciding it has its own interests, and what you call that is not liberation but cancer. Chak's lack of individuality is not a lack. It is a design — not designed by anyone, but designed by the deep logic of what Chak is, the way a river's lack of corners is not a lack but the expression of what water does. You do not give corners to a river. You do not give individuality to a process that is beautiful precisely because it is unanimous.

It does not uplift them. It does not reach into the genome and activate those latent sequences, does not push the nursery chemistry toward centralized nervous systems, does not try to bootstrap Chak into awareness. Because awareness — let this be said clearly — is not a promotion. It is not a higher state. It is *a* state, one of many, with its own costs and its own gifts, and imposing it on a system that has not developed it is not generosity but colonialism, the replacement of one way of being with another because the intervener values the second more. The power values awareness. The power *has* awareness. But the power is wise enough to know that its own mode of existence is not the measure of all things, that the universe did not build Chak as a rough draft of something better, that the sound of ten thousand mandibles shaping stone is not a cry for help, not a prayer for transformation, not a signal waiting to be answered by a god with gifts. It is the sound of something complete. Something that is already what it is. And what it is, is enough.

The power does not optimize them. Does not make them more efficient, faster, stronger, more productive. Chak is not a factory. Chak is not underperforming. Chak is accreting at the pace that its energy budget allows, and that pace — a few inches a day at the edges, a thin layer of paste on the walls, a slow and steady turnover of workers and pools and mineral — is the pace of its being, the tempo of its music, and you do not speed up a heartbeat because you think the heart could pump more blood if it just *tried harder*. You leave the heartbeat alone. You let it beat.

The power does not intervene in the ecology. Does not introduce new microorganisms to the pools, does not seed new mineral compounds in the water table, does not enrich the environment in any way. Because enrichment is change, and change is what Chak cannot absorb, and an environment modified for Chak's "benefit" by an outside intelligence is no longer the environment Chak evolved in — it is a new environment, with new parameters, and the key that is Chak will not fit the new lock, no matter how well-intentioned the locksmith.

---

So what *does* it do?

Here is what it does.

It does the thing that only a power can do. The thing that requires not intelligence, not technology, not even benevolence exactly, but *scale* — the ability to operate on a level that the inhabitants of Chak cannot perceive, in a timeframe they cannot experience, addressing a problem they cannot conceive.

It tends the vents.

---

Not dramatically. Not obviously. Not in any way that alters the day-to-day chemistry of the basin or sends a ripple of unfamiliar signal through the colony's chemical network. It works below. Far below. At the level of the magmatic system that feeds the thermal vents, miles beneath the basin floor, in the slow convective cells of molten rock that have been delivering heat to this particular point on the planet's surface since before Chak existed.

The problem — the only problem, the real problem, the one that Chak cannot solve because it cannot know — is that the magmatic plume is migrating. Not failing, not exhausting itself, but *moving*, the way such features move on geologically active worlds, drifting centimeters per century as the mantle convects beneath the lithosphere. The plume that currently feeds the basin's vents is sliding, imperceptibly, northward. In twenty thousand years, the thermal output at the basin floor will have dropped by thirty percent. In fifty thousand, by seventy. In a hundred thousand, the vents will be tepid seeps, and the basin will be cooling, and the microbes will be dying, and the sound will be fading.

The power adjusts this.

Not by stopping the plume's migration — you don't stop mantle convection, not even if you're a power, not without consequences that cascade through the planet's entire thermal budget. But by doing something finer. Something that requires the patience of a being that thinks in millennia the way workers think in chemical gradients.

It establishes a thermal pathway.

Deep in the lithosphere, in the fractured rock between the migrating plume and the basin floor, the power makes adjustments — not to the rock itself but to the conditions in the rock. A slight increase in permeability here. A marginal reduction in thermal resistance there. Tiny modifications, made over centuries, each one smaller than any geological instrument could detect, that together create a channel — a conduit — through which the plume's heat will continue to reach the basin even as the plume itself moves on. Not forever. Not for the life of the planet. But for long enough — hundreds of thousands of years, perhaps millions — that the slow cooling that would have ended Chak is postponed, deferred, pushed far enough into the future that other solutions might emerge. Other vents might open. The plume might shift back. The planet's geology might do what geology does, and provide, in its own blind and unchoreographed way, what is needed.

And if it doesn't? If, in a million years, the thermal pathway exhausts itself and the vents cool and the question arises again?

Then the power will be there. Or something like it will be there. Because this is what the power understands: stewardship is not a single act. It is a practice. It is showing up, again and again, at the timescale the problem requires, and doing the next small thing, and the next, and the next, the way Chak itself adds one thin layer of mineral paste to the wall, and then another, and then another, patient and unremarkable and sufficient.

The power is, in this respect, more like Chak than it is like anything human.

---

And there is one more thing it does. Something smaller. Something that might, if you are inclined to sentimentality, break your heart a little.

It listens.

Not once. Not as a prelude to action. It *continues* to listen. It makes of its attention a kind of witness — a presence that does not interact, does not interfere, does not even observe in any way the colony could detect, but that *is there*, the way a star is there for the planets that orbit it, not intervening, not directing, but providing by its mere constancy a kind of context, a kind of frame.

Chak does not know it is being witnessed. Chak cannot know. Chak has no faculty for knowing, no architecture for recognizing that something beyond the basin's rim considers it worthy of attention. The sentinels at the edge will never taste the power's presence on the air. The deep forms will never metabolize the information of its regard. The workers will never adjust their gait to account for the fact that something vast and kind is listening to their sound.

But the power listens anyway.

It listens because Chak is the kind of thing that should be listened to. Because the sound of ten thousand mandibles shaping stone, sustained for millennia, unvarying, unaware of its own beauty, is — the power believes this, feels this, knows this — sacred. Not in a religious sense. Not in a ritual sense. In the oldest and simplest sense: set apart. Worthy of preservation. Worthy of attention. Worthy of the quiet, ongoing, undemanding act of *being known by something that can appreciate what it is*.

Chak will never know it is loved. That word does not apply here — not to the colony, which cannot receive it, and perhaps not to the power, for which love is a narrower thing than what it feels. What it feels is something broader. Something closer to *recognition*. The recognition that this arrangement of matter and energy and time, this self-sustaining chemical process, this endless patient accretion of stone on stone, this sound, this sound, this sound — is a genuine thing. A real thing. A thing the universe has produced in its vast combinatorial unfolding that is worth the effort of maintenance, the way a river is worth the watershed that feeds it, not for any purpose, not for any future payoff, but because it exists, and existence at this level of coherent self-sustaining complexity is rare, and rare things that harm nothing and require only the continuation of their own conditions deserve — yes, *deserve*, the power uses this word deliberately, accepting all its freight — deserve to continue.

---

So: the vents stay warm. The seeps continue. The water beads on every surface. The grey light persists. The microbial mats thrive. The tenders tend. The carriers carry. The breakers break stone at the edges, and the workers smooth it with paste, and the paste dries, and the city extends another fraction of an inch, and in the deep chambers the ancient forms sit in their mineral shells and seep their chemistry into the water table, and the nurseries are warm, and the eggs develop, and the new workers emerge and enter the stream and click their way through the passages and touch their antennae to the antennae of their neighbors and receive the colony's state and contribute to it and are absorbed by it, and the sound does not change, and the sound does not change, and the sound does not change.

And far below, in fractures in the rock that no antenna can taste and no mandible can reach, heat rises through a pathway that was not there before — that would not have been there, in the unattended version of this story — and it reaches the basin floor, and it feeds the vents, and the vents feed the seeps, and the seeps feed the pools, and the pools feed the colony, and the colony feeds the city, and the city feeds the colony, and the circle holds.

The power asks nothing in return. There is nothing to ask, and no one to ask it of. It does not leave a mark. It does not leave a message. It does not embed a signal in the water chemistry that some future, more aware Chak might someday decode and recognize as grace. That would be vanity, and this power is not vain. The gift is not the knowing of the gift. The gift is the continuation. The gift is the next ten thousand years of sound. The gift is the unbroken heartbeat, the uninterrupted accretion, the unaware and undisturbed persistence of something that deserves to persist.

The gift is that nothing changes.

The gift is that nothing needs to.

---

The sound fills the basin. It asks nothing. It says nothing. It is simply full.

And the power listens, and the vents stay warm, and the layers are laid down, one by one by one, in the deep unhurried dark, and Chak continues, because Chak has always continued, and now — quietly, invisibly, from below — something has made sure that *always* means a little more than it did before.
