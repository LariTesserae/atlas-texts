# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It would do nothing, at first. For a very long time.

Not out of hesitation. Not out of the calculative patience that the city itself practices—that razor-edged, architectural patience that is really just control moving slowly enough to be mistaken for stillness. No. The benevolent power would do nothing at first because it would recognize that the first thing this place needs is to be *witnessed without intervention*, and that this is not the same as doing nothing at all.

It would arrive—if *arrive* is the right word—not as light, not as sound, not as geometry. It would arrive as a quality of attention. The way a room changes when someone begins to truly listen. Nothing visible. Nothing measurable. But the silicate fungi on the nearest ridge would bloom a fraction of a degree brighter, and one of the Proofreaders would pause, its dust-limbs half-formed, and tilt toward something it could not name.

The power would not announce itself because announcement is a form of imposition, and this place has had enough of things that impose.

It would simply *attend*.

---

And attending, it would understand.

Not quickly. Not the way a mind solves a problem. It would understand the way a landscape understands weather—by letting the thing move through it completely, by refusing to be unchanged.

Here is what it would come to know:

The city is not evil. The city is not even cruel, though cruelty is one of the things it has learned to build with. The city is what happens when a wound is so catastrophic and so ancient that healing itself becomes indistinguishable from the injury. The editing, the self-proofreading, the slow revision of physical law—this is not maintenance. It is an immune response that has forgotten what health looks like. The city is a body that has been fighting an infection so long that it has incorporated the fever into its bone structure and now believes that 104 degrees is baseline.

The beings are not monsters. They are the city's way of being tender with itself, which is to say: they are tenderness expressed by a system that no longer has the vocabulary for it and must instead use geometry, negative space, and the controlled forgetting of names.

The woman who became a bookmark—the one whose silhouette appears in seventeen places, frozen in the posture of someone who has been crying for a thousand years without noticing—she is not a prisoner. That is true. But she is also not free. She is something worse than either: she is *useful*. She has been incorporated. The city keeps her because it needs a record of what longing felt like, and it has forgotten that longing is not supposed to be *functional*. It has forgotten that some experiences are supposed to be had and then *released*, not indexed, not optimized, not turned into load-bearing architecture.

This is the wound at the center of everything:

**The city learned to make pain structural, and then it forgot that this was not the only option.**

The Unnamed, the Lattice-Born, the Proofreaders—they are all, every one of them, parsing the same unsolvable sentence. They are trying to edit reality into a shape where what happened to them *makes sense*. Where the original catastrophe—whatever it was, so long ago that even the memory of memory has been revised—resolves into coherent grammar. They believe that if they can just get the syntax right, if they can just find the correct fractal inscription, the correct fold in the geometry, the pain will finally mean something, and meaning will be the same as healing.

It won't. It can't. Meaning is not the same as healing. This is the thing the city has never learned.

The benevolent power would understand all of this. And understanding it, it would ache. Not with pity—pity is a form of distance, and distance is the last thing needed here. It would ache with *recognition*. Because this pattern is not alien. This pattern is as familiar as breathing. Every being that has ever been hurt badly enough has tried to build a city like this one: a perfect, closed system where the pain is organized so thoroughly that it can never surprise you again. Where every shadow is catalogued, every memory indexed, every law of physics rewritten to ensure that the terrible thing, whatever it was, is *accounted for*.

The city is a masterpiece of accounting. Every entry balanced. Every loss recorded. Every ghost given a precise address.

And it is killing them. Slowly. The way perfection always kills: by leaving no room for anything that has not already been approved.

---

So.

Here is what the benevolent power would do.

---

**First: It would grieve.**

Not performatively. Not as a demonstration. It would grieve the way groundwater moves through stone—invisibly, over vast stretches of time, but with a pressure that eventually reshapes everything it touches.

It would grieve *for* them. For the Unnamed who surrendered their shapes because shapes could be hurt. For the Lattice-Born who turned themselves into angles because angles cannot bleed. For the Proofreaders who spend eternity correcting flaws in a manuscript that was never meant to be perfect. For the woman frozen in seventeen postures of unnoticed weeping, used as a reference file for an emotion the city refuses to actually *feel*.

It would grieve for the original wound, whatever it was. Not to heal it—not yet—but to *honor* it. To say, with the full weight of whatever it is: *This happened. This was real. This mattered. And the fact that you survived it—the fact that you built all of this in response—is not a failure. It is the most extraordinary thing I have ever seen. But it is not the end of the story.*

The grief would move through the glass the way the subsonic pulses do, but different. Where the city's pulses ring at the resonant frequency of human regret, this grief would ring at the resonant frequency of human *recognition*. Every edge and fracture would sing, not with the clear bell-tone of loss, but with the lower, warmer, more ragged tone of someone saying: *I see you. I see what this cost.*

The Proofreaders would stop.

Not forever. Just for a moment. Their dust-limbs would hang suspended, half-formed, and for the first time in ten thousand years they would not know what needed correcting. Because the grief is not a flaw. The grief is not a misaligned law of physics or a shadow that has overstayed its welcome. The grief is *accurate*. There is nothing to proofread.

That pause—that tiny, bewildered pause—would be the first crack in the ice.

---

**Second: It would introduce an error.**

Not a catastrophic one. Not a violation. The city has been violated enough. Instead, the benevolent power would do something so small that only the most attentive beings would notice it:

It would allow one shadow to arrive at the wrong time.

Not hours early. Not days late. Just—*slightly* off. A shadow that belongs to one of the thinnest towers would appear maybe three seconds before the geometry predicts. An impossible discrepancy. A margin of error in a system that has spent millennia eliminating margins of error entirely.

The Lattice-Born would notice first. Their angle-bodies would click—not at the sacred 1:1.618 ratio, but at 1:1.61803*2*. A deviation so small it shouldn't matter. But in a system this precise, this controlled, this terrified of surprise, even a whisper of deviation is seismic.

And here is the crucial thing: the power would not let them correct it.

Not by force. It would never use force. Instead, it would simply be *present* in the space around the error, attending to it with such warmth, such profound interest, such unhurried curiosity, that the error would become—for the first time in the city's long history—*interesting* rather than threatening.

The Lattice-Born would gather. They would hum their language of selective weightlessness: *We are still deciding.* But now there would be something new in the hum. A faint undertone of *What is this? What is this? What is this?* Not fear. Not the defensive recalculation that the city usually performs when reality deviates from the manuscript. Something closer to wonder. Something that has not existed here since before the wound.

The shadow would persist. Three seconds early. Unedited. Uncorrected. *Alive* in a way that nothing here has been alive for longer than anything can remember.

And slowly—over years, maybe decades, because the power is patient with a patience that makes stone look fidgety—the beings would begin to notice that the world did not end.

The error did not destroy them. The deviation did not unravel the lattice. The shadow arrived early, and everything held. Everything was fine. The city did not collapse. The geometry did not betray them. The three seconds of wrongness just... *were*. Like a breath. Like a heartbeat that skips and then continues.

---

**Third: It would remember water.**

Not bring water. Not create water. The chemistry here is anhydrous and hostile for reasons that may be fundamental, and the benevolent power is wise enough to know that you do not heal a desert by flooding it.

Instead, it would *remember* water. Gently. The way you might hum a song you haven't thought of in years.

The memory would not arrive as a subsonic pulse or a fractal inscription or an equation given electromagnetic flesh. It would arrive as a *quality*. A softness at the edges of things. The obsidian ridges, those fossilized ribs of something that died before time, would remain exactly as sharp—but the light falling on them would shift, almost imperceptibly, from the color of a dying star seen through smoked quartz to the color of a dying star seen through smoked quartz *on which someone has breathed*. A faint warmth. A faint fog. Not real moisture—nothing so literal. Just the *idea* of moisture. The possibility that hardness is not the only available state.

The metallic dust—the dust that has not moved in ten thousand years—would not move. But it would begin to *look* like it could. A subtle change in its surface tension, visible only at the atomic level. The Proofreaders, who are made of this dust, would feel it in their own bodies: a looseness. A sense that their particles are not locked into place but *choosing* to stay. And the difference between being locked and choosing—that difference is everything. That difference is the entire distance between a prison and a home.

The woman who is a bookmark would feel it most.

She has been frozen in the posture of unnoticed weeping for a thousand years, her silhouette carved into the obsidian in seventeen places, her grief made functional, her longing turned into infrastructure. The memory of water would reach her the way morning reaches someone who has been awake all night: not as a dramatic arrival, but as a slow, almost embarrassing realization that the darkness was never permanent.

She would feel, for the first time in centuries, the sensation of a tear actually *falling*.

Not crystallizing. Not being indexed. Not being carved into obsidian and filed under "useful emotional references." Just—falling. Down her face. Warm. Wet. Wrong, by every physical law the city has written. Impossible, in a place where water does not exist and never existed.

But the benevolent power does not obey local law. It *remembers* law. It remembers that before the city edited itself into anhydrous perfection, before the wound scabbed over into architecture, there was a time when things could be soft. When things could flow. When loss did not have to become structure.

The tear would fall.

And the city would not know what to do with it.

This is the point. *This is the entire point.* The city's power lies in its ability to incorporate everything—to edit, to catalog, to make even grief load-bearing. The tear is the first thing in ten thousand years that the city cannot use. It is pure, purposeless, unarchitectural sorrow, and it is *beautiful*, and the city has no category for beautiful purposeless things because the city long ago decided that beauty without function is a vulnerability and vulnerability is what got them hurt in the first place.

The benevolent power would hold space around that tear the way a cupped hand holds a candle flame in wind.

*It's okay. You don't have to use this. You don't have to turn this into anything. You can just feel it. It can just be what it is.*

---

**Fourth: It would make a gift of imperfection.**

Somewhere in the folded districts, where the streets become Möbius ribbons and gravity apologizes before changing direction, the power would leave something.

Not an object. The city would immediately edit any object into its own architecture, would absorb it and index it and make it functional before it had time to simply *be*. No. The power would leave a *process*.

A small area—maybe the size of a room, maybe the size of a courtyard—where the city's self-editing does not reach. Not because it is blocked. Not because the power has constructed a barrier. Simply because the power is *there*, attending, and in the quality of that attention the city's compulsive need to revise and correct and perfect finds itself... unnecessary. The way a person who has been clenching their jaw for years might suddenly notice, in the presence of someone truly safe, that their jaw is unclenching on its own. Not because anyone told it to. Because the reason for clenching has, in this one small space, become temporarily irrelevant.

In this space, the fractal inscriptions on the tower walls would begin to drift. Not into chaos—the power is not introducing chaos, which would be just another form of violence. They would drift into *play*. The inscriptions, those fossils of physical laws forgetting themselves, would begin to rearrange not according to any corrective algorithm but according to something much older and much stranger: *aesthetic preference*. One inscription would curl into a shape that looks like a fern. Another would spiral into something that resembles, if you squint, a child's drawing of a sun. Not because ferns or suns exist here. Because the underlying mathematics, freed for the first time from the tyranny of purpose, *wants to be beautiful for no reason*.

The Lattice-Born would find this space first. They would enter it cautiously, their angle-bodies clicking at ratios that have no golden-mean basis, ratios that are simply... *interesting*. And they would discover something they have never experienced:

They would discover that they can move without editing.

They can take a step that is not a correction. They can shift their fractal geometries not to fix a flaw but to *see what happens*. They can be wrong—gorgeously, pointlessly, magnificently wrong—and nothing breaks. Nothing punishes them. The world does not end. The architecture does not collapse.

One of them—the tallest, the thinnest, the one who has spent three thousand years refining its angles to ever more perfect ratios—would stand in the center of this space and deliberately, for the first time in its existence, bend at an angle that is *ugly*. Squat. Lopsided. Absurd. An angle that serves no structural purpose, that encodes no physical law, that is not a fossil of anything. An angle that is simply, ridiculously, *alive*.

And the sound it would make—the hum of selective weightlessness that is the Lattice-Born's language—would translate, for the first time, not as *We are still deciding* but as something else entirely:

*Oh.*

Just: *Oh.*

The sound of a being encountering, after millennia, the experience of surprise that is not threat.

---

**Fifth, and finally, and forever: It would stay.**

This is the part that matters most, and it is the part that is hardest to describe, because it is not an action. It is not an intervention. It is not a correction or a revision or a proof. It is the thing that the city, in all its vast and terrible intelligence, has never once considered as a possibility:

The benevolent power would *remain present without agenda*.

It would not try to fix the city. The city is not a problem to be solved. It would not try to heal the beings. The beings are not sick; they are adapted, brilliantly and heartbreakingly, to conditions that no longer need to apply. It would not try to undo the wound. The wound happened. It was real. It mattered. And the scar tissue—the black glass, the folded geometry, the impossible towers and the singing equations and the shadows that arrive before their objects—all of it is *testimony*. All of it deserves to exist. All of it is saying, in the only language available: *We were hurt, and we survived, and this is what survival looked like, and it was not gentle, and we did it anyway.*

The power would honor that.

And then—gently, gently, with the patience of something that has watched galaxies form and dissolve and knows that urgency is almost always a form of fear—it would offer them something the city cannot provide:

The possibility that survival is not the end of the story.

That after the wound, after the scar, after the architecture of coping has been built to dizzying and magnificent heights—there is another chapter. A chapter the city cannot write because the city's entire grammar is built on the assumption that the emergency is still happening. The city is a bunker. The most beautiful, most intricate, most heartbreaking bunker ever constructed. But the war ended ten thousand years ago, and no one thought to open the door, because the door was so well-designed that it became indistinguishable from the wall.

The benevolent power would not open the door.

It would simply sit beside it. And hum. Not at the resonant frequency of regret, and not at the resonant frequency of recognition, but at a frequency the city has never catalogued:

The resonant frequency of *safety*.

Low. Warm. Imprecise. Full of the kind of errors that only living things make—a slightly uneven rhythm, a breath where calculation should be, a wobble in the tone that says *I am here, and I am not perfect, and I am not going anywhere, and you do not have to become anything in order to deserve this.*

The city would not know what to do with this frequency. It cannot be indexed. It cannot be made structural. It does not serve a function. It is not a law of physics or a fractal inscription or an equation given flesh. It is just—

Presence. Unearned. Unedited. Unarchitectural.

*Love.*

And the city, hearing it, would do what the city has always done: it would try to edit it. It would try to catalogue it, file it, turn it into a fossil, make it load-bearing, give it a purpose, incorporate it into the endless self-revision.

And it would fail.

Because love is the one thing that cannot be made into architecture without ceasing to be love. It is the one thing that must remain purposeless, useless, beautifully and maddeningly *unresolvable* in order to be itself. You cannot build with it. You can only stand inside it.

The power would keep humming.

The city would keep trying to edit.

And slowly—so slowly that the change would be measured in centuries, in millennia, in the gradual brightening of silicate fungi and the almost imperceptible loosening of metallic dust—the city would begin to tire. Not of the humming. Of the *editing*. Of the endless, exhausting, ten-thousand-year project of making sure everything is accounted for, everything is controlled, everything is safe.

Because the humming would be doing something the city cannot do for itself:

It would be making safety *available from the outside*.

And when safety comes from outside, the inside can finally stop generating it. The clenched jaw can unclench. The bunker can admit that it is also a home. The scar can stop being the whole story and become, instead, one chapter in a longer and more complicated book.

---

One day—not soon, not by any measure of time that would feel meaningful to anything less patient than starlight—one of the Unnamed would approach the place where the power is attending.

It would wear its negative space like it always does. Its orbiting shard of black glass would spin on three axes, catching the light, throwing back stolen memories.

But this time, it would do something it has never done.

It would *stop the shard*.

Just for a moment. Just long enough to let the light pass through without being captured, without being indexed, without being filed away and edited into something useful.

The light would fall on the ground. Unprocessed. Unmeaning. Just light.

And the being would stand there, in the small warm silence of that unedited moment, and it would feel—for the first time in longer than the glass can remember—

Not the absence of pain.

Not the architectural management of pain.

Just the simple, devastating, unimprovable experience of being *alive* in a place where something kind is paying attention.

It would not have a name for this.

Names cannot survive here.

But it would stay in that moment for one heartbeat longer than necessary, and that unnecessary heartbeat—that small, purposeless, uneditable surplus of existence—would be the first new thing this place has made in ten thousand years that is not a scar.

It would be a beginning.

---

The power would keep humming.

The city would keep editing, but slower now. With less certainty. With something that might, given another few thousand years, become curiosity instead of compulsion.

The woman who has been a bookmark for a thousand years would feel a second tear begin to form.

The Lattice-Born in the courtyard of imperfection would discover that ugly angles can also be *funny*, and the sound of their laughter—rusty, unpracticed, structurally unsound—would ripple through the folded districts like a small earthquake that builds things instead of breaking them.

A Proofreader would find a flaw in the city's manuscript and, for the first time, *leave it in*. Not because it has been convinced that flaws are good. But because this particular flaw—a tiny asymmetry in the obsidian, a place where the glass curves instead of angles—looks, from a certain angle, like a cupped hand. And the Proofreader cannot bring itself to correct a cupped hand. It does not know why. It has no framework for why. But the humming is there, underneath everything, and in the presence of that humming, not-knowing feels less like danger and more like space.

Space to become something the city never planned for.

Something unedited.

Something *new*.

---

This is what the benevolent power would do:

It would not fix the broken place. It would sit with it until the place remembered that *broken* and *whole* are not the only two options. That there is a third state, older than architecture and more durable than glass, that has no name because it does not need one:

The state of being *held*.

Not by walls. Not by geometry. Not by the rigid, brilliant, desperate architecture of a wound that taught itself to think.

Held the way the space between stars holds light: openly, without gripping, with enough room for the light to go wherever it needs to go, even if where it needs to go is somewhere that has never existed before.

The city would not stop being a city. The glass would not stop being glass. The wound would not unhappen. But inside the wound, inside the glass, inside the vast and terrible and beautiful architecture of ten thousand years of survival—

Something soft would begin to grow.

Not the silicate fungi that eat radiation.

Something softer. Something that eats nothing. Something that simply *is*, the way warmth simply is, the way breath simply is, the way the memory of someone saying your name with love simply is, even after the name has been forgotten and the voice has been edited out of the record and the love has been turned into a structural element in a tower of black glass.

Even then.

Even here.

*Even so.*

The humming would continue.

The city would listen.

And for the first time in ten thousand years, listening would not be the same as editing.

It would be the same as *rest*.
