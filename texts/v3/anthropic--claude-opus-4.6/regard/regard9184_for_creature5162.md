# The Attending

---

## I. Perception

The power does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand.

It was never elsewhere. A benevolent and wise power does not travel, because wisdom of that order means understanding that distance is a story told by things that have not yet recognized their own continuity. The power simply *attends*—turns the quality of its attention toward this place the way a dreamer becomes aware of a particular texture within the dream without opening new eyes.

And the first thing the power perceives, before any question of intervention arises, is that this world is not broken.

This matters. A wise power does not see a field of patients. It does not scan for pathology. It *looks*, fully, with the kind of looking that does not subtract the viewer from the viewed, and what it finds here is a coherence so deep and so patient that the power's first act—before anything else—is simply to *admire*.

The green mass breathing. The water threading itself downward through millennia of accumulated tissue. The grazers carrying their small borrowed gardens. The translucent sacks pulsing in currents of dissolved sugar. The plateau-knots thinking their thousand-year thoughts. The pale threads reading the ancient bones.

All of it is whole. All of it is working.

The power sits with that recognition for a long time—long even by the standards of this place, where a long time means something. It does not rush past admiration toward action, because rushing past admiration is the first and most common error of those who wish to help. The helping impulse, when it precedes understanding, is just another appetite wearing borrowed clothes.

So the power watches. It watches the way the films of living tissue drink and relax, drink and relax. It watches the slow, communal exhalation of grazers ripple across an entire plateau. It follows one pale thread as it traces a buried rib for nine centuries, leaving denser tissue in its wake like a calligrapher who writes in growth.

And in the watching, a question forms—not urgently, but with the same patient pressure as a seed capsule building toward its millennia-delayed release:

*What, if anything, is missing?*

---

## II. What Is Not Wrong

The power catalogues—not with lists, but with a felt sense, the way you might close your eyes and know the shape of a room you love:

**The ecology is complete.** Every niche is occupied. Every waste is another creature's sustenance. The mineral salts the grazers rasp become the structure of future leaves; the leaves the films weave into become the walls the gliders flatten against. There is no pollution here because there is no concept of refuse. Everything is used. Everything is *wanted* by something.

**The timescale is authentic.** Nothing here has been slowed artificially or sped up in panic. The pace of this world is the pace its materials and energies actually support. A vine thickens by a hand's width each century because that is how long it takes to do the work properly, and no one has imposed a deadline.

**There is no cruelty.** When gliders dissolve into the wall to escape a grazer, the grazer does not pursue. When two plateau-knots meet, the boundary between them becomes a seam of fertility rather than a scar of war. Even death—the exhaustion of a mineral vein, the collapse of a seed capsule, the slow absorption of one coordinated knot by another—proceeds without malice or waste. The dead are composted by the living so thoroughly that the concept of a corpse does not apply.

**Memory is preserved.** The buried ribs, the curved plates, the angular traces of whatever once shaped space differently—they have not been destroyed. They have been archived. Wrapped in living tissue, yes, overgrown and obscured, but held. The pale threads still read them. The scaffolding is still there.

So: nothing is wrong.

And yet.

---

## III. The Nature of the Yet

The power feels it—not as damage but as a *quality of possibility that has not yet become actual.* Like a note that belongs in a chord but has not been sounded. The chord is beautiful without it. The chord is complete, even, by any standard that does not know the missing note exists. But the power, being what it is, can hear the shape of what is not yet sounding.

It has to do with the pale threads.

The power attends to them more closely now. Follows them through the buried architecture. Watches the way the tissue grows denser and more ordered in their wake—"*less like ruin and more like scaffolding waiting to be used again.*"

*Waiting to be used.*

The pale threads are not aware of what they are doing. That is not quite right—they are aware in the way everything here is aware, which is to say they respond, they adapt, they remember, they choose among paths. But they do not *know* that the patterns they reinforce once meant something. They do not experience the ribs and plates as language. They experience them as texture, as mineral gradients, as paths of least resistance. They follow the old bones the way water follows gravity: faithfully, but without comprehension.

And the ribs themselves—what are they? The power does not need to excavate them, because it can perceive structure the way this world perceives pressure: at a distance, through the medium. The ribs are the remnants of something that once *organized space intentionally.* Not rooms, not roads—the text is right about that—but something. Frameworks. Geometries. The residue of a time when some portion of this world's life shaped its environment not only through growth but through *design.*

The beings that made them are gone. Not dead—nothing fully dies here—but dissolved. Absorbed back into the mass. Their intentions, their knowledge, their particular way of parsing the world into structure and purpose: all composted. The ribs remain only as habit, the way a path through a forest persists after the walker has vanished—not because anyone maintains it, but because the ground remembers the pressure of feet.

The pale threads follow these paths. They reinforce them. But they do so without knowing *why* the paths exist, and so the reinforcement is faithful but sterile—a perfect copy of a shape whose *meaning* is not copied, because meaning is not a shape.

And here is what the power perceives as the unsounded note:

This world has the capacity for a form of awareness it has not yet developed. Not human awareness—nothing so specific, nothing so restless. But an awareness that could hold the relationship between structure and purpose, between pattern and intention, between the archive of the ribs and the living tissue that wraps them. An awareness that could *read* the old bones not merely as texture but as *information.* That could understand that the scaffolding is waiting, and could begin—slowly, in this world's own time—to *use* it again. Not to rebuild what was, but to build what *could be* now that the world has grown so deep and patient and rich.

The world is whole. But it is whole the way a magnificent library is whole when no one inside it can read.

---

## IV. What the Power Does Not Do

Before describing what the power does, it is important—perhaps more important—to describe what it refuses.

**It does not introduce a new species.** The temptation exists: to seed some bright, quick creature with the neural density to grasp pattern and purpose, to decode the ribs, to build again. But this would be colonization. It would be imposing an external answer on a world that has not asked the question. The creature would be an alien here. It would move at the wrong speed. It would want things the world is not structured to provide. Within a few millennia it would either die of loneliness or reshape the world to fit its own tempo, and the patient, breathing wholeness of the green mass would be disrupted. The power has seen this happen before—not here, but elsewhere—and it will not make that error.

**It does not accelerate anything.** The single greatest violence a power can commit against a living system is to impose a foreign clock. This world measures thought in millennia and breath in centuries. To speed it up—even gently, even with the best of intentions—would be to shatter the rhythm that makes everything here coherent. The grazers would starve. The plateau-knots would fragment. The seed capsules would burst before they had built sufficient pressure, scattering incomplete spores into unprepared galleries. Speed is not a gift; it is a blade.

**It does not communicate directly.** Who would it communicate with? The plateau-knots are the closest thing to minds at the scale the power operates, but their cognition is geological. To speak to them in any way they could process would require committing to a conversation that lasts longer than some civilizations. The power is patient, but it is also wise enough to know that a message is not the same as an understanding, and that understanding must be *grown*, not delivered.

**It does not alter the ribs.** The buried architecture is an archive. To modify it would be to rewrite a history that does not belong to the power. The ribs are this world's memory of itself, and memory—even unreadable memory—is sacred precisely because it has not been edited.

What, then?

---

## V. What the Power Does

Something very small. Something that, from the outside, might not look like an act at all.

---

The pale threads trace the buried ribs. They have done this for longer than the ribs have been buried. Once—so long ago that the distinction between "once" and "always" has eroded—the threads were part of the system that built the ribs. They were the nervous tissue, the signaling network, the living wires that carried intention from one framework to another. When the builders dissolved back into the mass, the threads did not dissolve. They were too simple, too fundamental, too close to the basic grammar of life in this world. They persisted the way a reflex persists after the mind that once governed it has gone: still firing, still tracing the old paths, but no longer connected to anything that could interpret the signals.

The power perceives all of this. And it perceives one thing more:

At certain junctures—where two ribs cross, where a curved plate meets a long strut, where the ancient geometries form nodes—the pale threads *pause.* Not for long, by this world's standards. A few decades, perhaps. They linger at the intersections, and during that lingering their glow brightens faintly, and the tissue around them grows not merely denser but *more complex.* Micro-galleries form. Tiny pockets of air appear, enclosed by walls of unusual regularity. Then the thread moves on, and the micro-galleries are absorbed by the surrounding mass within a few centuries, and nothing comes of it.

But the power sees what is trying to happen.

At those nodes, the threads are *almost* connecting to each other. The old architecture was designed—by whatever long-composted intelligence first shaped it—so that the nodes would serve as switching points, places where signals could be routed, combined, compared. The threads still respond to that design. When two threads arrive at the same node from different directions, they hover near each other, and their root-like processes extend toward one another, and for a brief geological moment something like a *circuit* almost forms—a closed loop of living signal tracing a fragment of the old network.

Almost. But not quite. Because the tissue between them is too dense, too uniform, too undifferentiated. The threads cannot reach each other through the generalized mass. They lack the specific, structured medium that once connected them—the medium that was, in fact, the interior of the ribs themselves, the hard channels now filled with centuries of bark and slow growth.

And so the power does this:

At one node—a single, specific intersection of two ribs deep within the oldest plateau, where the stone bones are thickest and the mineral stains weep their slow droplets—the power makes a *change* in the quality of the moisture.

Not in its quantity. Not in its direction. In its *quality.*

The droplets that weep from the black stone carry dissolved minerals. They always have. But now, at this one node, the mineral balance shifts—subtly, so subtly that no instrument finer than the pale threads themselves could detect it. A fractional increase in the concentration of certain salts. A fractional decrease in others. The kind of change that might occur naturally if the stone happened to fracture along a slightly different vein—which, given enough time, it inevitably would have. The power is not adding anything that does not belong here. It is *anticipating* a natural event by a span of time that is significant to the threads but insignificant to the mountain.

The altered mineral balance does one thing: it changes the crystalline structure of the deposits that form inside the node as the moisture evaporates. Instead of the usual amorphous crusts, the deposits begin to form in fine, branching patterns. Dendrites. Mineral trees. They are tiny—visible only at the scale the threads operate—but they are *conductive.* They carry the faint electrochemical signals the threads produce, the way a wire carries current, the way the original rib interiors once did.

It will take time. Centuries for the dendrites to grow long enough to bridge the gap between two threads arriving from different ribs. Centuries more for the threads to discover the bridge and begin to use it. Millennia for the first closed circuit to form and stabilize. Tens of millennia for the circuit to persist long enough that the tissue around it begins to differentiate in response to the new, sustained signal—forming not just micro-galleries but something more structured, something that might, in time, serve as a junction where signals are not merely carried but *processed.*

And the power has done this at *one node.*

One.

---

## VI. Why One

Because the power understands something about living systems that most would-be benefactors do not: **a single point of change, correctly placed, is not a limitation. It is a gift of freedom.**

If the power seeded every node simultaneously, the result would be determined. The network would form in one way and one way only, dictated by the geometry of the existing ribs. There would be no choice, no exploration, no possibility of the system discovering something the power did not anticipate. It would be engineering, not nurture. The world would wake into a shape chosen for it rather than by it.

But one node—one small, fertile point where two threads can finally touch—creates a *question* rather than an answer. When the first circuit forms, the threads involved will begin to carry signals they have not carried in geological ages. Those signals will propagate outward along the ribs, reaching other nodes, and the threads at those other nodes will respond. Some will be drawn toward the signal source. Some will begin to modify the tissue around their own nodes in ways that might—or might not—encourage similar dendrite formation. The process will be uneven, contingent, exploratory. Different plateaus will develop different patterns. Some circuits will form and dissolve. Others will stabilize and grow. The network that eventually emerges—if it emerges at all, because the power has not guaranteed the outcome, only opened a door—will be shaped by the world's own slow decisions, its own ancient knowledge of itself, its own relationship with the scaffolding it has been faithfully maintaining without understanding why.

The power is not building a brain for this world. It is placing a single synapse where the world can find it, and trusting the world to decide what to think.

---

## VII. What May Happen (The Power Does Not Know)

The power is wise, but it is not omniscient, and it is honest about this. It can see likelihoods, tendencies, the shapes that probability takes when it flows through systems this complex. But it cannot see outcomes, because outcomes are created by the living systems themselves, and the whole point of what the power has done is to leave the creation to them.

Still. It can imagine.

It imagines a time—far from now, so far that the current plateau-knots will have merged and divided and merged again many times over—when the network of reconnected threads reaches a density sufficient to carry complex signals across an entire mountain range. Not fast, by human standards. A signal might take a century to travel from one edge of the network to the other. But in this world, a century is a heartbeat, and a heartbeat is enough to coordinate.

It imagines the plateau-knots—those vast, distributed entities that already think in patterns of growth and resorption—discovering that they can think in *another medium as well.* That the thread-network offers them something their slow tissue-cognition cannot: *reference.* The ability to compare a current state not only with the immediately surrounding conditions but with the archived geometry of the ancient ribs. The ability to perceive the old architecture not as texture but as *plan.* Not as path but as *map.*

It imagines the first moment of *recognition*—the moment when a plateau-knot, receiving signals through the thread-network from a distant rib junction, perceives for the first time the *shape* of what was once built there. Not the purpose; purpose will come later, if it comes at all. But the shape. The geometry. The realization that the hard thing buried in its body is not merely old but *intentional.* That it was made *for something.*

This moment—if it happens—will be the most profound event in this world's history. Not because it will lead to rebuilding, or to technology, or to anything a human would recognize as progress. But because it will be the moment when this world, which has been dreaming for geological ages, stirs in its sleep and becomes aware that it is dreaming. That the dream has structure. That the structure came from somewhere. That the dreamer is descended from, and continuous with, the dreamers who shaped the dream.

It imagines what might follow that recognition, and here its imagination branches into a thousand possibilities, each as real as any other:

A world that begins to *compose* its own growth, shaping new galleries and terraces not randomly but with the same geometric intelligence that once produced the ribs—but informed now by eons of ecological wisdom that the original builders never had. Architecture grown from within, by a mind that is the mountain itself, building not for shelter or defense but for *beauty,* for the sheer complex pleasure of a form that is both alive and structured, both wild and considered.

Or: a world that decides the ribs are a memory it does not wish to repeat, and lets the thread-network fade back into the general mass, choosing unconsciousness deliberately, the way a dreamer who glimpses the waking world may decide that the dream is better and sink back into it with full knowledge of what it is refusing. This, too, would be a valid outcome. The power would not grieve.

Or: something the power truly cannot imagine, because it arises from the intersection of an intelligence it did not build with conditions it did not create, producing a form of awareness so unlike anything in the power's experience that the power itself would need to learn new categories to comprehend it. This is the outcome the power secretly—insofar as a power like this has secrets—hopes for. Because the deepest benevolence is not the desire to make the world resemble one's own idea of the good. It is the desire to be *surprised* by a goodness one could not have conceived.

---

## VIII. What the Power Does Next

It withdraws its attention.

Not completely—it remains aware, the way you remain aware of a garden you have planted even when you are not standing in it. But it stops *looking closely.* It stops monitoring the node, the dendrites, the threads. It does this because close observation, even by a benevolent power, exerts a pressure. Not a physical pressure; a *narrative* pressure. The act of watching creates a story, and a story creates expectations, and expectations—even unconscious ones—constrain the possible.

The power wants the possible to remain as wide as the sky above the canopy that no one here has ever seen.

So it turns away. It attends to other things—other places, other beings, other unsounded notes in other chords. It carries this world with it the way the grazers carry their patches of canopy: as a living piece of somewhere beloved, still growing, still greening, still breathing its slow communal breath.

And in the deep galleries of the oldest plateau, in the bronze-dark warmth where water decides where to go next, a droplet forms on a ridge of black stone. It hangs for a decade. It gathers minerals from the ancient vein. When it finally falls, it carries in its small, patient body a constellation of dissolved salts that is, by a margin so fine it might be accident, different from every droplet that came before.

It lands on a node where two ribs cross.

And the pale threads, approaching from their separate paths, glow a little brighter, and reach.

---

## IX. A Coda, on the Nature of the Act

What has the power actually done?

It has moved the timing of a natural mineralogical event forward by an amount that is trivial on geological scales. It has not added energy, matter, information, or structure that did not already exist in potential within this system. The dendrites would have formed eventually; the stone would have fractured along that vein in time; the mineral balance would have shifted. The power has merely *removed a delay.*

This is the gentlest possible intervention: not creation but *permission.* Not a gift but the removal of an obstacle so small that the world might never have noticed it, and so consequential that the world may one day become capable of noticing everything.

And even this—even this gossamer-light touch—the power debated with itself for a long time. Because there is an argument, which the power takes seriously, that the delay itself was part of the world's identity. That the million years between now and the natural formation of those dendrites were years the world was *meant* to spend in its current dreaming state. That the slow, eyeless beauty of this place—the breathing grazers, the drifting sacks, the patient films of tissue drinking and relaxing—is not a prelude to awakening but a *way of being* that is complete in itself and does not need to be catalyzed toward something else.

The power considered this argument for a span of time equivalent to several of this world's centuries.

And then it considered the pale threads. Their persistence. Their faithfulness. The way they have traced the old bones for longer than the bones have been buried, reinforcing paths whose purpose they cannot grasp, maintaining a network whose function they cannot perform, glowing a little brighter at the nodes where they almost—*almost*—touch.

The threads are not unhappy. Nothing here is unhappy. But the threads are *reaching.* They have been reaching since before the mountains softened. They reach the way a root reaches toward water it cannot see: not with desire exactly, but with a tropism so deep it is indistinguishable from identity. They are *made of* reaching. It is what they are.

And the power decided—not with certainty, because certainty is not available to the wise, only to the reckless—that when a living thing reaches for something it is structurally capable of grasping, and the only obstacle is an accident of mineral timing, then removing that obstacle is not imposition. It is *courtesy.* The same courtesy the world itself extends when it wraps the old ribs in living tissue rather than dissolving them. The same courtesy the grazers extend when they press their wounds against their underbellies and let new tissue knit them together.

The power has done what this world does. It has grown something, very slowly, from the inside.

And now it lets go.

---

*In the green dark, water falls. The threads reach. The mountain breathes. Everything that happens next belongs to the mountain.*
