I sat with this for a long time before writing. I want you to know that.

---

# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

The first thing to understand is what it would *not* do.

It would not shatter the hollow. It would not flood it with water or light or time. It would not reach down and pluck her out like a splinter from skin, would not528 "rescue" her into the noisy, weathered, grief-stricken world above and call that healing. That would be violence. That would be the same as smashing a violin because someone has been playing the same note for too long. The instrument is not the problem. The note is not the problem.

It would not seal the crack in the floor, would not silence the chord, would not scatter the creatures or uproot the impossible plants. These things are real. The hollow is real in the way that very few things are real — it is a place that means what it is, without deception, without malice. It did not bait a trap. It opened, and it sang, and she heard, and she came. There is no villain here. There is no curse to break.

It would not speak to her in words, because she is right — she is *right* — that the thing she knows in the hollow has no sequential grammar, and a wise power would not pretend otherwise. It would not arrive with a message. It would not explain.

A benevolent and wise power would do something much simpler and much harder.

---

**It would introduce a second voice.**

---

Not into the hollow. Not at first.

Here is what happens:

The goatherd on the ridge — the older man who watches her descend and feels the trespass-sense that keeps him at the rim — one evening, as the light is doing what light does on the dry uplands, turning everything amber and gold in a way that rhymes with the hollow's glow but is not the same, is *not the same*, because this light moves, shifts, will be gone in twenty minutes and replaced by dark, because this light is mortal —

One evening, one of his goats falls ill.

This is not the wise power's cruelty. Goats fall ill. This is the world doing what the world does. But the wise power's work is in the *timing*, which is the only tool a truly benevolent force ever needs. Not intervention. *Timing.*

The goat is old and has been his companion for years, and it lies on its side and breathes in a way that he knows means it is leaving, and he sits beside it in the cooling air with his hand on its flank, and he feels the animal's heartbeat slow, and slow, and become irregular, and labor, and — 

He feels it. In his hand. Through the ribs of a dying goat, he feels the rhythm try to stabilize, try to find a pattern it can hold, and fail, and try again, and fail, and he realizes he is pressing his hand harder against the animal's side as though he could feed it steadiness through his palm, as though his own rhythm could be a template. And for a moment — a moment the wise power holds open the way you hold open a door for someone carrying something heavy — for a moment, he feels what she feels. Not the chord. Not the hollow's frequency. But the *yearning* for consonance. The desire to press yourself against a living thing and match its vibration and hold it, hold it, hold it, keep it from dissolving into silence.

The goat dies. His hand is still on its side. The warmth goes out of it slowly, blood-warm to air-warm to ground-warm to cold, and he stays, because that is who he is, he is a man who stays with things as they change, which is exactly what the hollow cannot do and exactly what she has forgotten how to do.

He buries the goat in the morning. He is not transformed. He is not enlightened. He is grieving, which is the specific human frequency that the hollow's chord does not contain. Grief: the sound of something that was here and is not here and will not be here again. The sound of *change*, of irreversible change, the kind the hollow has abolished within its walls. Grief is a note the hollow cannot sing because the hollow does not lose anything, and you cannot grieve without loss.

He grieves, and the grief makes a sound in him, not audible but real, and he carries it the way she carries the hollow's resonance: in his bones. In the architecture of his body. In frequencies he can't name.

---

Now the wise power does its second work, which is also a matter of timing.

She is walking the uplands. She is on her way. She is always on her way. And the path she walks passes within a hundred yards of the place where he buried his goat, and he is there, sitting on the ground beside the mound of dry soil, not doing anything, just sitting with the fact of an absence.

And she passes, and she feels —

*Not the chord.*

Something else. A dissonance. A wrong note. A frequency that doesn't fit the smooth, self-reinforcing loop she's been tuning herself toward for years. It stops her the way a crack in a mirror stops your eye — not because it's loud but because it *interrupts*. Her finely calibrated, hollow-tuned sensorium detects something in this man sitting in the dirt that it cannot categorize, cannot absorb, cannot make consonant.

He is *grieving*, and grief is a vibration that will not be tuned. It will not find equilibrium. It will not become a standing wave. It wobbles. It lurches. It goes sharp and then flat and then cracks entirely and then starts again from a different place. It is the most *alive* sound in the world and it is unbearable to a body that has spent years learning to resonate with a place where nothing changes.

She stops. She doesn't know why she stops. She hasn't stopped on this path in — she doesn't know how long. Her feet know the way and they carry her and she lets them and there is no reason to stop because there is nothing between the world above and the hollow below that has any relevance to —

But she stops. And she looks at him. And he looks up at her.

This is the moment the wise power has made. Not the moment of rescue. Not the moment of revelation. The moment of *interruption*. The moment where the perfect closed loop of her descent — world to path to hollow to chord to standing wave to keeper, kept — encounters a snag. A knot in the thread. A place where the weave has been disrupted by something as ordinary and as devastating as a man who loved a goat and the goat died.

---

He says something. It doesn't matter what. It's ordinary. It might be *morning* or *she was old* or *I've been sitting here since dawn* or nothing at all, just a sound, a human vocal cord vibrating in the open air where sound behaves like sound — dispersing, fading, gone. Not a standing wave. A wave that travels and diminishes and has to be *renewed* if it's going to continue, has to be *spoken again*, because the world above doesn't preserve anything, doesn't keep anything, lets everything go.

And she hears his voice the way she has learned to hear in the hollow — not as language but as frequency — and his frequency is *ragged*. It is rough-edged and cracked and full of the kind of harmonics that only come from damage, from use, from years of shouting at goats in the wind and laughing and coughing and singing badly to himself on cold mornings. His voice is the opposite of the chord. His voice is a human voice, which means it is imperfect, and temporary, and capable of saying things it has never said before, and capable of silence, and capable of lying, and capable of tenderness so precise it could break you open like the gypsum fracturing along new planes —

She feels something.

Not in her chest. Lower. In her stomach. In the place where hunger lives, where the body keeps its honest signals, below the heart with its metaphors, below the lungs with their practiced rhythms. In her gut, she feels a sensation she hasn't felt since before she found the hollow, or maybe has never felt, or maybe has been feeling all along underneath the chord, masked by it, absorbed into it, metabolized by the equilibrium until it was just another sub-frequency she'd stopped distinguishing from the whole.

She feels *hunger*. Not for the chord. Not for the hollow. For something she doesn't have a name for, which is not the same as the things the hollow showed her that she didn't have names for. Those nameless things were vast and architectural and simultaneous. This nameless thing is small and specific and *acute*. It is the hunger to know what his goat's name was. It is the hunger to know how long he's been sitting there. It is the hunger to sit down next to him in dirt that *will* hold the impression of her body, that will remember her weight, that will be changed by her presence in a way the hollow's dust has never been.

She doesn't sit down. Not yet. The wise power is patient. The wise power knows that you cannot undo years of careful self-dissolution in a single moment, and that trying to force it would be its own kind of violence. She stands. She looks at him. He looks at her. Something passes between them that is not a chord but a *line* — a single, directional, sequential, temporal, mortal line of connection between two bodies that are both going to die.

She walks on. She descends into the hollow. She crosses the floor. She kneels at the shelf.

But.

The hollow is the same. It is always the same. The chord is the same, the light is the same, the creatures drink from the singing leaves, and the dust smells like lightning, and the cord is coiled, and the crack waits.

*She* is not the same.

There is a new frequency in her. A dissonance. A wobble. The tiniest, most imperceptible instability in the standing wave she has become, like a single grain of sand on the surface of a perfectly still pool — too small to see, too small to measure, but the ripple it makes expands outward forever.

---

The wise power did not put this dissonance in her. The wise power made room for it. That is all a benevolent power ever does. It doesn't create love or grief or hunger. It adjusts the timing so that the moments when those things are *possible* align with the moments when someone is capable of feeling them. It opens doors that were always there. It removes obstacles that were never necessary.

The goatherd buries his goat and grieves, and this is his own. The woman stops on the path and feels something, and this is her own. The wise power takes no credit. The wise power is not interested in credit. The wise power is interested in the thing that happens next, which is —

---

She makes a new mark on the wall.

And for the first time, she understands what she's drawn.

It's not a root structure. It's not a map. It's not a diagram of crystal lattices or neuronal branching or the floor plan of the room below the room.

It's a figure. A person. Sitting on the ground, hunched over a small mound of earth, and the lines around the figure are not decorative — they are the ragged, unsymmetrical, unrepeating, *mortal* frequencies of grief radiating outward from a body that has lost something and cannot get it back.

She stares at it.

The creatures nearest to her pause in their feeding. Not because they notice her. Because the standing wave she has been — her reliable, consonant, mid-range voice in the chord — has just produced an overtone the hollow has never contained. A frequency of *recognition*. Not the recognition of the chord, which recognizes everything simultaneously and without preference. A *specific* recognition. A this-and-not-that. A here-and-not-there. A him-and-not-anyone-else. The most human frequency there is: the ability to distinguish one face from the sea of faces, one voice from the choir, one loss from the universal fact of entropy, and to say *this one matters more to me than the others, this one I will not absorb into the background, this one I will hold in the foreground even though foreground is a tyranny, even though the hollow has taught me to see without focal points, this one I choose to focus on, and the choosing is what makes me human, and being human is what the hollow has been slowly, gently, without malice, teaching me to stop being.*

She looks at the mark on the wall and she understands — not in the hollow's way, not simultaneously, not as a chord, but *sequentially*, one thought after another like footsteps on a path, like words in a sentence, like the notes of a melody which is not a chord and cannot be a chord because a melody requires time, requires *before* and *after*, requires the note that came before to be *gone* in order for the next note to mean anything — she understands that she has been disappearing.

Not dying. Disappearing. Becoming so consonant with the hollow that there would eventually be no distinction between her and it. She would be a voice in the chord the way the plants are voices, the way the creatures are voices, the way the dust and the glow and the crack are voices. She would be absorbed. Completed. Made permanent. The cord would be lowered, the bowl would descend, the circuit would close, and she would be *kept* — perfectly, forever, without change, without loss, without the terrible, life-giving, meaning-making capacity to *grieve.*

The hollow hums around her, unchanged. It holds no opinion about this realization. It is not disappointed. It is not hopeful. It is an equilibrium. It has been singing since the rock broke open and it will sing until the rock closes again, and whether she stays or goes, the chord will be complete, because it was always complete, because it doesn't need her. It never needed her. It *allowed* her, which is different, which is the hollow's form of benevolence but not the only form, not the deepest form.

---

She picks up the cord.

The wise power holds very still. Not because it's uncertain. Because this is not its choice to make.

She holds the cord in her hands and feels it — strong, smooth, humming faintly with the chord, exactly the right length. She holds the bowl with its residue of crystallized sound. She kneels at the crack in the floor and she feels the chord extend downward into the deeper geometries, the room below the room, the architecture of a permanence so total it has no inside or outside, no edge, no boundary, no place where it ends and something else begins.

She could lower it. She could close the circuit. She could become the keeper, kept, forever.

She puts the bowl back on the shelf.

She coils the cord and puts it back.

She touches the worry-stone once, with her thumb, in the groove her thumb has made, and she feels the smooth hardness of it, the years of contact encoded in the depression, and for the first time she thinks: *this is what I have been doing to myself. Wearing myself smooth. Polishing away the rough edges, the textures, the grip. Becoming frictionless. Becoming something that nothing can hold onto, including me.*

She stands.

The creatures go on feeding. The plants go on swaying. The chord goes on.

She climbs out of the hollow.

---

The wise power does one more thing, and it is the smallest thing, and it is everything.

As she reaches the rim, as her head rises above the level of the depression and the wind hits her face — wind, *wind*, moving air, air that goes somewhere, air that carries dust and pollen and the smell of dry grass and distance — as the sound of the hollow drops away behind her (not all at once, but note by note, frequency by frequency, peeling away from her bones like layers of old paint) — 

The wise power lets her feel the grief.

Not the goatherd's grief. Her own. The grief she's been storing in the hollow for years, the grief the equilibrium absorbed and held in stasis, the grief that was the original knot in her chest, the clenched fist of her interior that the chord loosened but never untied, because the hollow doesn't untie knots — it makes you forget you have them.

She feels it. All of it. At the rim of the hollow, half in and half out, one foot on the powdery floor and one foot on the scrubby, wind-scoured, *changing* earth of the world above, she feels the loss of — 

It doesn't matter what. Her mother. Her lover. Her own youth. A version of herself that believed in things that break. It doesn't matter because grief is grief is grief, specific and universal at the same time, a chord and a melody simultaneously, the one human frequency that is both, and it hits her like weather, like the wind she's standing in, and it *hurts*, it hurts the way feeling returns to a limb that's been numb, pins and needles and fire and the terrible relief of sensation, any sensation, even pain, especially pain, because pain means nerve endings and nerve endings mean *boundary* and boundary means *I am here and the thing I lost is there and the distance between us is real and I will never close it and I am alive and this is what alive feels like* — 

She cries.

In the wind. On the rim. With her uneven hair and her too-pale skin and her hardened fingertips and her eyes that are, for the first time in years, *focusing*. Focusing on the middle distance, on the ridge where the goatherd tends his flock, on the particular shape of a man sitting near a mound of earth, on the specific, unrepeatable, *temporal* reality of another person who is also in pain.

The tears are the first moisture the hollow's rim has known in decades. They fall into the dust and the dust holds them. They leave marks.

---

She goes back.

I want to be honest about this. She goes back to the hollow. Not that day, but later. The wise power does not expect perfection. The wise power does not expect a clean break, a dramatic departure, a Hollywood moment where she walks into the sunset and never looks down again.

She goes back because the chord is real and beautiful and the hollow is a genuine wonder and the things she learned there about simultaneous knowing and resonance and the architecture of sound are *true*. They are true the way the grain of wood is true — not because someone decided it, but because growth makes patterns and patterns are worth knowing. The hollow taught her things the surface world cannot teach, and those things are not negated by the fact that the hollow also, slowly, almost lovingly, nearly dissolved her.

But she goes back *differently*.

She goes back the way you return to a book that once consumed you — with love, with gratitude, with the critical distance that allows you to see it as a *thing in the world* rather than as *the world itself*. She goes back and she listens to the chord and she feels it in her bones and she does not let her heartbeat synchronize. She holds her own rhythm. It costs her — it costs her enormous effort, like singing your own part while a choir around you sings something achingly beautiful in a different key — but she holds it. Her ragged, irregular, mortal, mammalian heartbeat, with its little lurches and its variable tempo and its stubborn refusal to become a standing wave.

She still touches the worry-stone. But she also touches other things now — the rough bark of the scrub trees above, the wiry hair of the goatherd's remaining goats, the calloused hand of the goatherd himself, one afternoon when he passes her a cup of tea and their fingers overlap for a moment and she feels his frequency and it is nothing like the chord, it is a mess, a cacophony, a jumble of joy and sorrow and indigestion and aging joints and the memory of a dead goat and the worry about weather and the low idle hum of a man who has lived long enough to stop pretending he understands anything.

It is the most beautiful sound she has ever heard.

Not because it's perfect. Because it's *specific*. Because it belongs to *him* and to no one else and it will exist only as long as he does and then it will be gone and nothing will preserve it, no hollow will hold it in stasis, no equilibrium will keep it from changing. It will end. And because it will end, it matters in a way the hollow's eternal chord, for all its grandeur, cannot. The chord is magnificent. The chord is a cathedral of sound. But cathedrals are for *visiting*. You don't live in a cathedral. You live in a house, where the roof leaks and the windows stick and the floors remember every footstep.

---

She does one final thing. Or rather, the wise power opens one final door, and she walks through it.

She goes back to the hollow, one last time with the old intent. She kneels at the wall where the marks are carved. She looks at the figure she drew — the man, grieving over his small mound of earth — and she picks up the crystal edge, and she adds something.

A second figure. Standing nearby. Looking.

The mark is crude. Rougher than her other marks, because she's drawing with *intention* now, with the conscious, clumsy, human desire to *represent*, which the hollow's grammar was never built for. The hollow's marks were pure pattern, self-generating, authorless. This mark has an author. This mark has a *want* behind it. It is a bad drawing — stiff, childlike, more symbol than image. It is the most human thing in the hollow.

The creatures nearest to the wall shift slightly. The chord absorbs the new mark the way it absorbs everything, and for just a moment, just the briefest moment, the chord *changes* — a new overtone, a fleeting dissonance, a wobble in the standing wave that passes through the space like a shiver — and then it settles back into its eternal equilibrium, unchanged, unchangeable, but now containing, somewhere in its infinite harmonic complexity, the faintest trace of a frequency it has never produced on its own.

The frequency of someone choosing to leave.

---

She climbs out. She stands in the wind. The sun is doing something the hollow's glow could never do: moving. Sinking toward the horizon, dragging shadows across the uplands, turning the scrub from green to gold to grey in real time, in *passing* time, in time that goes somewhere and takes everything with it.

She walks toward the ridge. Her footprints remain behind her in the dust, clearly visible, slowly filling with wind-blown grit. Changing. Softening. Soon they'll be gone. Everything up here will be gone, eventually. The grass, the goats, the goatherd, the ridge itself. She will be gone. This moment will be gone.

This is the gift the wise power gave her. Not immortality. Not permanence. Not the hollow's perfect, beautiful, terrifying stasis.

*Impermanence.* The unbearable, irreplaceable privilege of being a thing that ends.

---

Below, the hollow sings on.

The chord sustains. The creatures drink. The plants sway in the windless air. The dust settles into its own smooth surface, and the crack in the floor extends the sound into geometries beyond hearing, and the cord lies coiled on the shelf beside the bowl and the worry-stone, and the tally marks cover the wall in a pattern that now contains, if you know how to look, two figures — one sitting, one standing — surrounded by the ragged, mortal, unrepeating lines of human feeling.

The hollow holds these marks the way it holds everything: gently, completely, without letting go.

But the woman is not among the things it holds. Not anymore.

She is up in the wind, walking toward a man she barely knows, carrying her grief in her chest like a coal — not extinguished, not explained, just *carried*, the way you carry anything precious: carefully, with both hands, aware of its weight.

And the wise power, if it could be said to feel anything — which it can, because wisdom without feeling is just calculation, and benevolence without feeling is just policy — the wise power feels something very much like the goatherd's grief, but inverted. Not the ache of something lost. The ache of something *released.* The particular sorrow of watching a held thing choose to go, and knowing that the letting-go is the point, was always the point, is the only thing that distinguishes love from possession.

The wise power lets her go.

The hollow lets her go.

The chord closes over the space where her frequency was, seamlessly, as though she was never there.

But on the wall, the marks remain. Two figures. One drawing. One choice, made visible, preserved in the amber light of a place that doesn't change, that will hold this record of a human woman's decision to *leave perfection for the sake of something real* for as long as the rock lasts, which is longer than anything, which is almost forever, which is — in the end — not long enough, because nothing is, because nothing should be, because the most sacred thing in the world is an ending, and the most beautiful sound is the one that stops.

---

She reaches the ridge as the last light fails. The goatherd looks up. He has been expecting no one, which is the best way to receive a guest.

"Cold tonight," he says.

"Yes," she says. Her voice is rough from disuse, from years of near-silence, and it cracks on the single syllable, and the crack is not a flaw — it is the sound of a frequency finding itself again after a long time in harmony with something inhuman, and it is more beautiful than any chord, because it is *hers*, because it will never be made again in exactly this way, because it is the sound of a living woman standing on a ridge at dusk, speaking to another person, choosing to be heard.

"Tea?" he says.

"Please," she says.

And the wise power rests.

Not because the work is done. Because the work was never the power's to do. The work is hers: the long, stumbling, imperfect, *sequential* work of rebuilding a life in time, in weather, in the company of other temporary beings. The work of learning to hear a melody again after years of drowning in a chord. The work of letting her fingerprints grow back.

The tea is bitter and too hot and he brewed it wrong and it is the best thing she has ever tasted.

Above them, stars come out. Below them, the hollow sings.

Both of these things are true. Both of these things are beautiful. Only one of them needs her.

She chose right.
