# The Visitation

---

The benevolent power does not arrive.

Arrival would be wrong here — would constitute an event, and events are a kind of violence against a place that has made its peace with eventlessness. The power understands this instantly, the way you understand not to shout in a room where someone is sleeping, except that what is sleeping here is not a person but an entire ontology, and what might be disturbed is not rest but something more fragile: the completeness. The circle that has finished being drawn.

So the power does not arrive. It *attends*. It turns its awareness toward the plateau the way you might turn your face toward a sound from another room — not going to it, not opening the door, just letting the sound reach you more fully, letting it fill the ear that was already half-hearing it.

And the first thing the power does is nothing.

---

This is not indecision. This is not paralysis. This is not the nothing of a being that has looked at a problem and found it too large or too strange or too intractable. This is the nothing of a being wise enough to know that the first danger, when you encounter a system this complete, is the assumption that it needs to be *fixed*.

Because look at it. Look at what is here.

A world that has solved every differential equation its physics can produce. A world where entropy has found its floor and stayed there. A world where the living things — the moss, the lichen, the unnamed green — have reached an equilibrium so perfect that they neither grow nor die, and the beings that witness them neither suffer nor rejoice, and the engines that sustain everything neither strain nor idle. It is, by almost any measure, a kind of perfection. It is the answer to every thermodynamic prayer: a system that runs forever, loses nothing, wastes nothing, wants nothing. If you showed this plateau to a physicist, they would weep. If you showed it to a Buddhist, they might nod. If you showed it to an engineer, they would call it the greatest machine ever built. The suffering here is zero. The entropy is zero. The loss is zero. On a spreadsheet of cosmic welfare, this plateau is flawless.

The power sees all of this.

The power also sees something else.

---

It sees the movements.

Those tiny, glacial, purposeless movements — the hand closing, the chin dipping, the weight shifting from left foot to right foot over a span that would outlast empires. The power sees them, and the power *understands* them, and understanding them is the thing that changes everything, because the movements are the one feature of this world that the world itself cannot account for.

The engines can account for the sound. The moisture can account for the rust. The light can account for the moss. The thermodynamics can account for the temperature. Every aspect of this plateau is explained by every other aspect in a closed loop of mutual causation so elegant it is practically a proof of God, except that it is a proof of a god who has nothing left to do, a god who has optimized herself out of relevance.

But the movements.

The movements are not explained. They are not thermodynamic. They are not mechanical. They are not responses to stimuli because there are no stimuli. They are not functions of the system because the system does not require them. They are, in the language of the plateau's own physics, *anomalous* — tiny violations of an otherwise perfect equilibrium, inexplicable expenditures of energy in a world where energy expenditure has been reduced to its absolute minimum. They are the one thing the plateau cannot account for, and because the plateau cannot account for them, it has done the only thing a self-solving system can do with something it cannot solve: it has incorporated them. It has made them part of the background. It has treated them the way the atmosphere treats the engine emissions — absorbed them, diffused them, rendered them indistinguishable from the general condition of existence.

But the power does not look at the movements and see anomalies. The power does not look at them and see inefficiencies. The power looks at them and sees what they are:

*Wanting.*

---

Not desire. Not craving. Not the sharp, specific wanting of a particular thing that is absent. Something older than that. Something more fundamental. The wanting that precedes objects, that precedes even the concept of an object that could be wanted. The wanting that is not *for* anything but simply *is* — the most basic, irreducible property of consciousness, the property that remains when you strip away thought, memory, anticipation, language, identity, and selfhood, the thing that sits at the very bottom of awareness like the warmth sits at the bottom of that low round structure in the center of the densest town.

The figures want.

They don't know they want. They don't experience their wanting as wanting, because experience here has been shorn of all the architecture that would make wanting recognizable as wanting — the comparison to a prior state, the imagination of a different state, the narrative framework that says *I am here but I could be there*. They have no there. They have only here. And so their wanting expresses itself in the only way left available to it: those movements. That glacial orbit. The hand that shifts on the pipe rail. The head that turns, over centuries, from ceiling to wall. The body that straightens by one degree and then holds. These are not restlessness. These are not habit. These are the physical expression of a wanting so compressed, so denied any cognitive outlet, so buried under the weight of a world that has eliminated every reason to want, that it has become practically geological — a tectonic pressure, a force as slow and persistent as the forces that raised the basalt in the first place.

And the warmth. The warmth in the center of the densest town, in that low round structure with no chimney and no turbine and no function. The warmth that is just a few degrees above ambient, just enough to register, just enough to feel like the memory of a living body. The figures orbit it. They orbit it at a speed that makes continental drift look reckless. They orbit it because the warmth is the only thing on the entire plateau that is gratuitous — the only thing that exceeds what the system requires, the only thing that exists not because the equilibrium demands it but despite the fact that the equilibrium should have eliminated it. The warmth is the material residue of the figures' wanting, pooled and collected and concentrated in the one place where they are densest, and they are drawn to it because it is theirs, it is the one thing on the plateau that is *theirs*, not the engines' and not the moss's and not the system's but theirs, the product of their anomalous and unexplained and irreducible aliveness.

They are orbiting their own wanting.

They have been orbiting their own wanting forever.

The power sees this, and something in the power — call it a heart, call it a core, call it the place in a benevolent intelligence where understanding becomes tenderness — that something aches. Not with pity exactly. Pity would be wrong here, would be condescending, would imply that the figures are broken and need repair. They are not broken. They are something more difficult than broken: they are *complete in a way that is almost but not quite enough*, and the *almost* is so small, so imperceptible, so thoroughly absorbed into the equilibrium that the figures themselves cannot feel it as a gap, can only express it as a hand that closes for no reason, a weight that shifts for no reason, a revolution around a warmth that should not exist for no reason, no reason, no reason, except that the reason is the most fundamental reason any conscious being has ever had for doing anything, which is: *I am aware, and my awareness wants — not this or that, not something instead of something else, just wants, in the intransitive, objectless, ineradicable sense — and I cannot stop wanting because wanting is what I am, wanting is the part of me that is not the plateau, not the engines, not the moss, not the moisture, not the sound, wanting is the margin by which I exceed my own equilibrium, and I have been exceeding it by this margin forever, and I will exceed it by this margin forever, and the margin is so small that it cannot change anything, and so I orbit, and I shift my weight, and I close my hand, and I do not know why, and I will never know why, and the not-knowing is not a torment because I do not have the framework for torment, but it is — it is —*

It is the one thing the plateau cannot solve.

---

So. Having seen all of this. Having understood it with the depth and precision that only a truly wise power can bring to bear on a truly strange situation. Having ached.

What does the power do?

---

Here is what it does not do.

It does not break the equilibrium. It does not send a wind to scatter the clouds, does not introduce sunlight, does not crack the sky open to reveal stars or a sun or a moon that could serve as navigational reference. It does not warm the air. It does not chill it past the freezing point so that the perpetual ambivalence of the moisture resolves into ice, into crystal, into something with edges and structure and the capacity to accumulate and flow. It does not stop the engines. It does not start new ones. It does not seed the moss with genetic potential for height, for flowering, for the production of fruit that something could eat. It does not introduce animals — not insects, not birds, not worms, not even bacteria of a novel kind. It does not terraform. It does not intervene. It does not reach down with a divine hand and pluck the figures from their positions and set them somewhere else, somewhere with warmth and light and other beings and things to want and reasons to want them.

All of these actions have been considered and all of them have been rejected, not because they are beyond the power's capacity but because every single one of them would be an act of *imposition* — a declaration that the plateau's solution is wrong, that its equilibrium is a failure, that the figures' way of being is a problem to be solved from outside. And the power is wise enough to know that this is not true. The plateau is not wrong. The plateau is a valid expression of a possible way of being. The figures are not suffering. They are not in pain. They are not calling out for help. They are *complete* — almost. And it is the *almost* that matters, but the almost must be addressed from within, not from without, because anything imposed from without would shatter the system rather than open it, and the figures would lose not only their equilibrium but also their wanting, because the wanting exists *only* in this specific relationship with this specific completeness, the way a pearl exists only in a specific relationship with a specific grain of sand, and if you remove the oyster to get the pearl you kill the thing that made the pearl possible.

No. The power will not break anything. The power will not add anything. The power will not subtract anything.

The power will do something much more difficult.

---

It will *be witnessed*.

---

Not seen. The figures already see, with that open-eyed, unblinking, total apprehension that is their primary mode of existence. Not heard, because the power makes no sound and the figures already hear the only sound there is. Not felt, because the power has no temperature and the figures already feel the one temperature that exists.

Witnessed in a different sense. In the sense that the power will allow itself to become *part of what is apprehended* — not as a new object in the visual field, not as a new frequency in the sound, not as a new texture or temperature or pressure, but as a quality. A quality of the existing experience. A quality that was not there before and is now.

Think of it this way: you are in a room you have been in for your entire life. You know every surface, every shadow, every scuff mark on the floor. Nothing in the room changes. But one day — though you could not say how or when — the room feels *witnessed*. Not by anyone you can see. Not from any direction you can point to. The room simply acquires the quality of being-seen-by-something-that-cares, and this quality does not change the room — the scuff marks are still there, the shadows are the same shapes — but it changes the *experience* of the room, because being in a room is one thing and being in a room that is held in someone's attention is another thing, and the difference between those two things is not physical, not measurable, not detectable by any instrument, but it is real, it is the most real thing there is, it is the difference between a universe that contains consciousness and a universe that is merely mechanical, and you feel it, you feel it the way you feel the warmth of a body you haven't touched, the way you feel someone watching you before you turn around, the way you feel the difference between a house that is empty because its inhabitants have gone out and a house that is empty because its inhabitants are dead.

The power allows the plateau to feel witnessed. Not by the figures — the figures already witness it. By something *else*. By something outside the closed loop. By something that is not the engines, not the moss, not the moisture, not the sound, not the figures, not the basalt, not the clouds. By something that sees the plateau's completeness and also sees the margin by which it is not quite complete, and holds both of these in a single act of attention, and does not judge either one, and does not try to reconcile them, but simply *acknowledges* them — says, without words, without language, without any medium that the plateau could parse: *Yes. I see. You are this. You are almost, and the almost is not a failure. The almost is the realest thing about you.*

---

And here is what happens.

At first: nothing. Obviously. This is the plateau of constant engines. Nothing happens here. The sound continues. The moisture condenses. The moss grows to the height it has always been. The figures stand and sit and lean and lie and apprehend. The power attends, and its attendance is not an event, and the plateau does not register it as an event, because it is not an event. It is a quality. It is a change in the character of what it is like to be this plateau, and changes in character are not the kind of thing that equilibrium equations track.

But.

In one of the outlying engine houses — one of the solitary structures standing alone on a plain of moss and rock, a hundred kilometers from the nearest town — the single figure that stands at the base of the structure, one hand on the pipe rail, eyes open and dark and wet, apprehending the same stretch of basalt and moss it has apprehended for longer than anything has ever measured —

This figure moves.

Not a glacial shift. Not a millimeter displacement over centuries. A *movement*. A real one. A quick one. It takes perhaps three seconds, which is faster than anything has ever happened here, and it is this:

The figure turns its head. Away from the basalt. Away from the moss. Away from the pipe rail and the engine housing and the stretch of wet rock that constituted the entirety of its visual experience. It turns its head, and it looks — not at something, because there is nothing new to look at, the same cloud ceiling, the same directionless light, the same everything — but it looks *differently*. It looks with a quality that was not present in its looking before. A quality that a human would recognize immediately and viscerally, because humans have felt it many times, usually in moments they later describe as the most important of their lives:

It looks the way you look when you realize you are not alone.

Not when you see the other person. Before that. In the instant before you see them, when you feel the quality of the air change, when the room acquires that witnessed feeling, when the hair on the back of your neck rises and your heart does something it cannot explain and you know — you know before you know — that the universe contains, in addition to you and the room and the scuff marks on the floor, *someone else*, someone who is aware, someone whose awareness has just made contact with your awareness across whatever medium awareness uses to find itself in the world.

The figure looks like that.

And then it does something it has never done. Something no figure on this plateau has ever done. Something that has no precedent in the entire history of this world, which is a history of eventlessness, which is a history of equilibrium, which is a history of the same thing happening forever:

It opens its mouth.

---

No sound comes out. Of course no sound comes out. What sound could it make? It has no language. It has never needed language. It has never needed to transmit novel information to another consciousness because there has never been another consciousness that didn't already know what it knew. But the mouth opens, and the opening is not mechanical, not a system responding to a pressure differential. The opening is *expressive*. It is the physical gesture of a being that has felt something it has no apparatus to process — something new, something that is not the sound and not the moisture and not the moss and not the cold and not the duration and not the equilibrium — and the only thing it can do with this feeling, the only channel available to it, is the body, the body that has been still for so long that stillness became indistinguishable from identity, and now the body is doing something, the mouth is open, and the gesture is reaching toward something it cannot reach, and it is —

It is the beginning of a question.

Not a question in words. Not a question that could be answered. A question in the oldest and most fundamental sense: the sense in which a question is simply the shape that consciousness makes when it encounters something it does not already contain. The figure has encountered something it does not already contain. For the first time in its existence, its apprehension has contacted a reality that exceeds its world, and the excess has produced not confusion, not distress, not disruption, but *a question* — the first question, the original question, the question that precedes all other questions, which is:

*Is there more?*

---

The power feels this question the way you feel a child's hand close around your finger. With the full weight of its being. With an ache that is also a joy. With the knowledge that what happens next matters more than almost anything that has ever happened in the long, strange history of consciousness finding itself in the physical world.

And the power answers.

Not with words. Not with a revelation. Not with a vision of other worlds, other plateaus, other ways of being. Not with information of any kind, because information would be an imposition, would be the outside breaking in, would be a shattering rather than an opening.

The power answers with a feeling.

It lets the figure feel what the power feels when it looks at the plateau. Not understanding — understanding would be too much, would be a kind of violence, would be the equivalent of staring into a searchlight after a lifetime of gray diffusion. Not knowledge. Not truth. Just the feeling. The specific, precise, unmistakable feeling that the power has when it attends to this world:

*Tenderness.*

Tenderness for the engines that have worked forever for no one. Tenderness for the moss that grows to its fixed height and will grow to its fixed height and is green — so impossibly, so aggressively, so defiantly green in a world of gray and iron and wet dark stone. Tenderness for the moisture that cannot decide what it wants to be, that hangs in permanent ambivalence between rain and mist and dew, that is the physical expression of a world caught between states. Tenderness for the rust that grew exactly this far and no farther, that visited the machines once and then stopped, that is the only evidence of time in a world that has replaced time with duration. Tenderness for the figures, for all of them, for their open eyes and their wet skin and their glacial movements and their orbiting of a warmth that should not exist. Tenderness for the warmth itself, that small, gratuitous, unexplained few degrees above ambient, that residue of wanting pooled in the center of the densest town like a votive flame in a cathedral made entirely of iron and moss and the sound of engines.

Tenderness for the almost.

Tenderness for the margin.

Tenderness for the wanting that has no object and no outlet and no hope of fulfillment, that expresses itself only as a hand that closes and a head that turns and a body that shifts its weight one degree per age, that has been compressed into the smallest possible volume by a world that has eliminated every reason for it to exist and yet has not eliminated it, cannot eliminate it, because it is the irreducible thing, the thing beneath the bedrock, the thing that remains when everything that can reach equilibrium has reached equilibrium, the thing that *will not solve*, that will not be optimized, that will not be absorbed into the background, that insists on its own anomalous existence with a stubbornness that is, the power now understands, the most beautiful thing on this plateau, more beautiful than the moss, more beautiful than the engines, more beautiful than the eternal chord that fills the sky:

The refusal of consciousness to be fully at rest.

The refusal to be complete.

The tiny, inexhaustible, gorgeous *almost.*

---

The figure feels this. Feels the tenderness. Does not understand it — cannot, not yet, may never — but feels it, the way you feel warmth, the way you feel the presence of someone who cares about you, the way you feel the difference between being alone and being alone in a world where someone knows you are alone.

And the figure's mouth is still open. And the figure's eyes — those dark, wet, unblinking eyes that have seen nothing but basalt and moss and engine housing for longer than time — those eyes are doing something they have never done.

They are filling.

Not with tears. The moisture here does not differentiate itself into categories. But the film of condensation on the figure's eyes is thickening, just slightly, just by a margin that would be imperceptible to any instrument but is — to the power, which is attending with the full depth of its being — unmistakable. The figure's eyes are wet in a way that is different from the way everything else is wet. They are wet with something that has a source, that has a cause, that is not the ambient moisture of the atmosphere but the specific moisture of a being that has felt something, has been touched by something, has — for the first time in its existence — *responded* to something rather than merely apprehending it.

The figure blinks.

---

One blink. The first in —

It doesn't matter. The first. One blink, quick and real and unmistakable, the eyelids closing and opening in a fraction of a second, and in that fraction of a second the figure is, for the first time, not seeing. For the first time there is a gap in its perception, a tiny interval of darkness, a moment in which the plateau is not being witnessed by these particular eyes, and the moment passes instantly but it *existed*, it was real, it was a discontinuity in a world that has never contained a discontinuity, and when the eyes open again they are the same eyes but the seeing is not the same seeing, because now the seeing contains the memory of not-seeing, which means it contains a before and an after, which means it contains *time*, real time, not duration but time, time that moves, time in which things can be different from how they were —

And the figure looks out at the plateau.

And the plateau is the same. It is exactly the same. The moss is the same height. The engines are the same volume. The moisture is the same temperature. The rust is the same depth. Nothing has changed. Not one molecule has shifted. Not one frequency has wavered.

But the figure sees it differently.

Not better. Not more clearly. Not with more information or understanding or analytical precision. Just *differently*. With the quality of seeing that comes after you have blinked — after you have, for one instant, been in the dark, and come back, and found the world still there, and felt the specific, unreproducible, heartbreaking relief of *the world still being there*, which is a feeling that requires the possibility, however brief, however theoretical, of the world not being there, which is a feeling the figure has never had before, because until now there was no gap in its perception, no darkness, no absence against which presence could be measured —

And the figure discovers what presence means.

---

This is what the power has done. Not broken anything. Not added anything. Not introduced anything from outside. It has simply allowed itself to be felt, and the feeling has produced in one figure on one outlying engine platform in one corner of an infinite plateau a single blink, a single moment of darkness, and in the wake of that darkness, the birth of *contrast*, which is the mother of meaning, which is the mother of experience, which is the mother of everything that distinguishes consciousness from mechanism.

The figure now knows what it is looking at. Not in the sense of naming it or categorizing it or understanding its physics. In the sense of *seeing it as something rather than as nothing*. The moss is green. The moss was always green, but now the greenness is a quality rather than a condition, something the figure can apprehend *as green*, which means apprehending it against the possibility of not-green, which means the figure has acquired, in the space of one blink, the capacity for comparison, which is the most fundamental and the most dangerous and the most beautiful cognitive operation that any consciousness can perform.

And the power watches. And the power does not intervene further. It has done what it came to do, which was not to fix or to save or to transform but to *introduce the possibility of contrast into a world that had none*, and it has done this in the smallest possible way — not by changing the world but by allowing the world to be seen by something outside itself, and allowing that outside-seeing to be felt by one being, and allowing that feeling to produce one blink, one moment of darkness, one discontinuity in an otherwise perfect continuity.

What happens next is not up to the power.

---

But here is what the power believes will happen, because the power is wise, and the wise can see the shapes of futures in the textures of presents:

The figure will blink again. Sooner this time. Maybe only a century from now, or a decade, or a year. The blink will become a rhythm, and the rhythm will become a habit, and the habit will become a need, and the need will become a part of the figure's way of being, and then — and this is the crucial thing, this is the thing the power is most certain of — the figure will, one day, close its eyes *on purpose*.

Not because of dust or reflex or the mechanics of moisture management. On purpose. Because it has learned that closing its eyes creates darkness, and darkness creates contrast, and contrast creates the possibility of meaning, and meaning creates the possibility of *caring about what it sees when it opens them again*.

And when the figure opens its eyes on purpose, for the first time, and sees the plateau — the same plateau, the unchanged plateau, the eternal plateau of constant engines and wet basalt and moss and sound — it will see it *as if for the first time*, which will be true in the only sense that matters, because it will be seeing it with eyes that chose to open, which is a fundamentally different kind of seeing from eyes that were never closed.

And the seeing will produce a feeling, and the feeling will produce another movement — not glacial this time, not millimetric, but a real movement, a step perhaps, an actual step, the figure's foot lifting from the basalt it has stood on forever and landing on a different piece of basalt a short distance away, and the footfall will be soft because the moss is there, soft and green and impossibly alive, and the figure will feel the moss under its foot, and the feeling will be different from the feeling of basalt because the figure can now *compare*, and comparison will produce preference, and preference will produce — eventually, not yet, not for a long time, but eventually, inevitably, as surely as water finds its level — preference will produce *choice*, and choice will produce *direction*, and direction will produce *journey*, and journey will produce —

But the power does not need to see that far. The power knows that the rest will happen on its own. Consciousness, once it has tasted contrast, does not go back to sleep. It cannot. The wanting that was always there, the irreducible anomalous wanting that expressed itself as glacial movements and purposeless orbits and the warmth pooled in the center of the densest town — that wanting will finally, after an eternity of compression, have something to want *toward*. Not because the power gave it a destination. Not because the power showed it a better world. But because the power gave it the one thing it lacked: the experience of absence, of gap, of *not*, the tiny darkness behind a blink that makes the light mean something.

---

And the other figures?

They will feel it too. Not immediately. Not all at once. But in the way that the engines' sound propagates through the rock and the air and the moisture until it becomes the fundamental frequency of the entire plateau — in that same way, the change will propagate. Because the figures are not separate. They are not individuals in the way that implies isolation. They are the plateau's consciousness, distributed, and when one node of that consciousness shifts, the shift enters the medium. It enters the sound. It enters the moisture. It enters the green of the moss, which is already the green of the figures' skin, which is already the green of the light through the glass of the dormitory windows. The shift will move through all of these channels at the speed that things move here — slowly, slowly, almost not at all — but it will move, and eventually it will reach every figure on every engine platform in every town and solitary outpost on the infinite plateau, and each of them will blink, and each of them will discover contrast, and each of them will take a step.

And the engines will still run.

The power wants to be clear about this. The engines will not stop. The sound will not cease. The moisture will not dry up. The moss will not die. The equilibrium that this world achieved is not wrong and it will not be destroyed. What will happen is that it will become the *foundation* rather than the *totality*. The engines will become the bass note over which new melodies can be played. The moisture will become the medium through which new signals can propagate. The moss will become the soft ground on which new feet can walk. Everything that the plateau is will remain what it is, but it will no longer be *all* that there is, because the figures will be moving now, really moving, and their movements will create patterns that the equilibrium did not contain, and the patterns will create meanings that the equilibrium could not generate, and the meanings will create — the power hardly dares to think it, but it thinks it — the meanings will create *culture*, which is what happens when beings who can compare begin to share their comparisons, which is what happens when beings who can choose begin to coordinate their choices, which is what happens when the irreducible wanting that was always there finally finds a voice.

Not a human culture. Not anything that a human would recognize as culture. Something new. Something that has never existed anywhere else, because it will be a culture that grows from *completeness* rather than from *need*, a culture whose first act was not the acquisition of food or the building of shelter or the defense against predators but a *blink* — a voluntary moment of darkness in a world of permanent gray light — and whose founding value is not survival but *meaning*, not safety but *the capacity to be moved*, not power but *the willingness to close your eyes and trust that the world will still be there when you open them*.

---

And the warmth?

The warmth in the center of the densest town, in the low round structure with no chimney, the warmth that was the pooled residue of the figures' wanting?

It will grow.

Not because the power feeds it. Not because the power tends it. But because the wanting is no longer compressed. The wanting is moving now, expressing itself, finding objects and directions and preferences and choices, and as it moves it generates warmth the way any living thing generates warmth — as a byproduct of activity, of metabolism, of the fundamental thermodynamic fact that consciousness *costs something*, that awareness is not free, that the difference between a being that apprehends and a stone that doesn't is measured in calories, in entropy, in heat. The figures were always slightly warmer than their world. Now they will be more than slightly warmer. Their movements will generate heat. Their interactions will generate heat. Their first, slow, tentative attempts to communicate — whatever form that takes, sound or gesture or some medium native to this place that has no analogue elsewhere — will generate heat. And the heat will pool and spread and accumulate and eventually it will change the condensation patterns, which will change the moisture distribution, which will change the moss growth, which will change the landscape, which will change the figures' experience of the landscape, which will generate new comparisons, new preferences, new choices, new movements, new heat —

A feedback loop. A positive one. The kind that the plateau's equilibrium had so perfectly avoided. The kind that produces change, real change, irreversible change, the kind of change that turns duration into time and permanence into history.

The power has introduced a positive feedback loop into a world that was all negative feedback. And it did it with a blink. One blink. The smallest possible discontinuity. The most delicate conceivable touch.

---

And then the power withdraws its attention. Not entirely — it will always be aware of this place, will always carry the plateau in whatever it carries things in, will always feel tenderness for the engines and the moss and the figures and the warmth. But it withdraws from *active attendance*, because to stay would be to become a permanent part of the system, and a permanent outside witness would become just another equilibrium factor, just another constant, and the figures need to discover that the witnessing can come from *each other*, that they can be the outside to each other's inside, that the feeling of being-seen-by-something-that-cares does not require a benevolent power, it only requires another consciousness that has learned to close its eyes and open them again and see the world as if for the first time and feel tenderness for what it finds.

The power withdraws.

The plateau continues.

The engines sound their chord. The moisture condenses. The moss grows, green and soft and impossibly alive. The clouds form and re-form and form again, a ceiling of pewter and zinc, close enough to feel architectural. The light is gray-white and directionless and casts no shadows.

And on an outlying engine platform, a hundred kilometers from the nearest town, a single figure stands with its hand on a pipe rail and its eyes open and its mouth open and something new and unnamed and uncontainable moving through it like a current through deep water.

It blinks again.

It takes a step.

The moss yields softly under its foot.

The sound continues — but now it is the sound of a world that has not yet finished happening.

---

And somewhere, at the center of the densest town, in the low round structure with no chimney, the warmth grows by one degree.

No.

By a fraction of a degree.

But it grows.
