# The Regard

---

The power does not arrive.

That is the first thing to understand. It does not come down through the canopy punctures. It does not push through the root-door passage. It does not manifest in the basin water or speak through the bioluminescent flicker. There is no moment of entry because entry would be an event, and an event would propagate through the system the way your presence did—heat shouting, silk-tunnel creatures detouring, colony clusters shifting, the whole finely tuned web of mutual adjustment spending energy it would rather not spend.

The power understands this. That is part of what makes it wise.

So it does not arrive. It *attends*. The way the root wall attends to the water that seeps through it—not reaching, not pulling, simply being structured such that what moves through it is received. The power turns its attention toward the hollow the way the hollow's own air holds moisture: totally, invisibly, without disruption.

And the first thing it does is nothing.

---

Nothing for a long time.

Not because it is deciding. Not because it is assembling a plan or weighing interventions. But because the first act of wisdom in the presence of something whole is to *refrain*. To let the wholeness speak for itself. To resist the reflex—and it is a reflex, even for a benevolent power, especially for a benevolent power—to see a woman in the dark and the cold and the damp and reach for the word *suffering* and reach, therefore, for a remedy.

The power does not reach for that word.

It watches her press her palms to the nursery wall. It watches the way she reads the root density with her fingertips—the sensitive ones, the ones she maintains. It watches the splinter colony on the nursery ceiling pulse faintly warmer as she enters, responding to her, and it watches her register that warmth with the film of moisture on her skin and adjust her breathing in a way that is not quite satisfaction and not quite prayer but is in the same country as both.

And the power thinks: *She is not incomplete.*

This is important. This is the foundation of everything that follows. She is not a problem to be solved. She is not a creature in need of light who has been denied light. She is not a life that has made the best of desperate circumstances and deserves, at last, to be raised into better ones. She is a woman in a hollow she has grown, tending a system she understands, building forward in a medium she has mastered, and the darkness is not her enemy. The darkness is her element. The cold is not her deprivation. The cold is her context, and she has woven herself into it so thoroughly that to remove it would be to unweave her.

The power sees this and does not flinch from it, does not reframe it into a narrative where she is brave-despite-hardship, does not project onto her the longings of a creature who knows sunlight and therefore assumes sunlight is missed. She does not miss sunlight. She has never had it. The grey pre-dawn non-light that filters through the canopy punctures is, for her, *bright*. The basin flare that illuminated you for one instant is, for her, *a flood*. Her world is not impoverished. It is *calibrated*—to her eyes, her skin, her frequencies of perception—and it is full. It is as full as any world.

The power understands this and grieves nothing.

---

So what does it do?

It begins with the smallest thing. A thing so small that she will not notice it for days, and when she does notice, she will not attribute it to anything external, because it will feel like something the hollow did on its own—which, in a sense, it is.

There is a root.

A particular root, in the eastern wall of the nursery chamber, that she has been trying to redirect for a full season. She placed a wedge there—a good wedge, shaped with her abrasion stones over the course of a week, fitted precisely into the root lattice—but the root has been resistant. It is one of the thick canopy rhizomes, old-growth, dense, and it has a structural curve to it that predates her intervention, a tendency bred into it by the weight distribution of the canopy above. Her wedge is nudging it. But slowly. Too slowly. She checks it every day, pressing her fingertips to the surface where root meets wedge, and every day the movement is barely measurable, and she has begun to consider—not consciously, not in words, but in the body-logic of someone who thinks with her hands—whether she needs to cut it. Whether the redirect will fail. Whether this section of the nursery arch will need to be rethought.

The power looks at this root. It looks at the wedge. It looks at the grain of the rhizome, the micro-architecture of its cells, the direction in which the fibers want to travel, the history of tension and compression stored in its tissue. And it understands what she cannot feel, because the information exists at a scale below her fingertips: the root is not resisting. The root is *almost there*. It has been slowly, invisibly, remodeling its grain around the wedge, and it is within days—three days, maybe four—of a tipping point where its own internal logic will complete the turn she started. She doesn't know this. She is considering cutting it, which would work but would weaken the arch, would cost her another season of regrowth, would introduce a wound site that the fungi would colonize and that she would need to manage.

The power does nothing dramatic. It does something that might not even be an action in any sense you'd recognize. It attends to the root with a quality of attention that is itself a kind of warmth—not thermal warmth, not energy, but *regard*. The way a mother's hand on a child's back is not pushing the child but is communicating *I see you, continue*. The root does not feel this regard. The root has no feelings. But the water that seeps through the root lattice seeps a fraction of a degree more evenly in the section near the wedge, and the mineral gradient in that water shifts by a part per million in a direction that favors cell-wall flexibility, and the root's own tipping point moves from three days to two.

She will press her fingers to it tomorrow and feel the difference. She will not understand why. She will hold still for a long time, re-reading the surface, confirming. And then she will leave the wedge in place. She will not cut. The arch will complete on schedule.

That is all. That is the first thing.

---

The second thing is larger, but not in the way you might expect.

The power looks at the passage network. The five or six hollows linked by root-corridors through the lowland. It sees the system as she cannot see it—whole, from above (if above meant anything here), the way you might see a watershed from a mountain. And it sees something.

The third hollow to the north—she has not been there in a long time. None of them have. The passage to it was partially blocked two seasons ago by a root collapse, not catastrophic but enough to make transit difficult, and the colony in that hollow has been declining in the absence of the thermal and chemical inputs that the human inhabitants provide. The basin fauna there are sluggish. The silk-tunnel population is out of balance—overconcentrated in the walls nearest the remaining food sources, leaving the far walls unmaintained, and the unmaintained roots are beginning to soften and sag in a way that will, in a year or two, compromise the hollow's structure.

She knows about this, in a vague way. She has felt the signals—a subtle change in the air that moves through the passage network, a shift in the acoustic signature of her communication-sounds that tells her the third hollow's geometry is changing. But she has not prioritized it. She has the nursery to think about. She has her own colony, her own basin, her own walls. The third hollow is someone else's problem, or it is a problem for later, or it is a loss that the network can absorb.

The power looks at this and sees the thing she cannot see: the third hollow is not someone else's problem. The network is a network. The colony creatures migrate between hollows. The silk-tunnel creatures share genetic material through the passage connections. The filter-creatures in the basins are, at a biochemical level, one population distributed across multiple pools. The health of the third hollow is not separable from the health of hers. It is the same health, observed at a different node. And the decline there is a slow drain on the vitality here, a loss she is compensating for without knowing she is compensating, the way a body compensates for a low-grade infection—functional, but spending resources that could be going elsewhere. Resources that could be going, for instance, to a nursery.

The power does not fix the third hollow. That is not its role, and it is not what is needed. What is needed is for *her* to go there. For her to crawl through the partially blocked passage and put her hands on the walls and read the state of things and feel the sag in the roots and the sluggishness of the basin and the thinning of the colony clusters, and decide, with her own body-logic, what to do. She knows how to do this. She has all the skills. She has the stone tools and the wedges and the knowledge of where to cut and where to wait. What she does not have is the information that would make the trip urgent rather than optional.

So the power provides information. Not by speaking. Not by sending a vision or a dream or a sign. It does something simpler and stranger.

It adjusts the sleep cycle of seven colony creatures in her hollow.

Seven, out of dozens. It touches their dreaming—if they dream—with that same quality of regard, that same not-quite-warmth, and they wake four hours earlier than they would have, and they drop from the ceiling and begin their rustling navigation of the hollow's airspace while the rest of the colony still sleeps. They are hungry. They range farther than usual, exploring the margins of the hollow, and three of them find the entrance to the passage that leads north. They enter it. They pass through the partially blocked section—they are small enough, just—and they do not come back.

She notices. Of course she notices. She notices everything. Three colony creatures entering the north passage and not returning is a datum, and it sits in her awareness alongside everything else—the root density of the nursery wall, the feeding patterns of the salamander-things, the state of the basin water—and it nags. It nags because the colony creatures do not usually enter the passages. They are creatures of the hollow, of the ceiling, of the vertical space. For them to go horizontal, to go *through*—that means something. That means the scent or the thermal gradient or the acoustic signature coming through the passage has changed. That means the third hollow is calling them.

She will go look. Not today. Maybe not tomorrow. But soon. The datum will accumulate alongside other data—the shift in air movement she has already noticed, the acoustic changes—and the compound weight of it will reach a threshold, and she will go. She will spend a day clearing the root collapse. She will enter the third hollow and put her hands on the walls and feel what has happened, and she will begin the work of restoring it—redirecting roots, rebalancing the silk-tunnel population, feeding the basin fauna. It will take weeks. It will take effort she might have spent on the nursery. But the nursery will benefit, because the network will be whole again, and the resources she has been spending on unconscious compensation will be freed, and the colony creatures will circulate as they were meant to, carrying genetic diversity and chemical signals between hollows, and the system—her system, the one she is—will be healthier.

The power watches the seven colony creatures wake early and feels no satisfaction, because satisfaction would imply a desired outcome, and the power does not desire outcomes. It desires *her*. It desires her the way the root wall desires water—structurally, fundamentally, as a precondition of its own coherence. It desires that she have what she needs to be what she is. And what she needs, right now, is not light, not warmth, not rescue. What she needs is a reason to go to the third hollow. So it gave her one. A small one. Seven creatures, waking early, following a gradient.

---

The third thing the power does is the hardest to describe, because it is not an action in any material sense. It does not move a root or adjust a sleep cycle or shift a mineral gradient. It does something that happens entirely in the space between itself and her, a space that she is not aware of and may never become aware of, and that is fine, that is as it should be.

It witnesses her.

Not observes. Not monitors. Not watches-over-with-protective-intent. It *witnesses* her, the way the walls of the hollow witness the water that moves through them—receiving, holding, letting-through. It takes into itself the full shape of her life. The calloused hands and the sensitive fingertips and the body that moves through darkness like water through root-lattice. The felted wrap alive with mites. The bone clip in her hair that may have belonged to someone now dissolved in basin water. The nursery she is growing for a someone who does not yet exist. The way she holds a dead colony creature in her palm before sliding it into the basin—that gesture, that particular gesture, the weight of it, the care of it, the way her thumb brushes the small furred body as if to say *I know you, I received what you gave, now go*.

The power takes all of this into itself and does the only thing that a witness can do, the thing that distinguishes witnessing from mere observation:

It lets her matter.

Not matter *to* something. Not matter in the sense of having consequence or impact or legacy. Just: matter. The way a stone matters to the hand that holds it. The way water matters to the root. She exists, and her existence is not incidental, not accidental, not a marginal footnote in the story of a world that may not have a sun. Her existence is *the thing*. The hollow, the system, the darkness, the cold, the slow patient work of hands shaping roots into rooms—this is the thing. Not a lesser version of some other, brighter thing. Not a life that would be better if it were different. This. Her. Now. Here. In the dark, in the damp, in the breathing silence, building forward.

The power holds this knowing the way she holds the dead colony creature: gently, completely, with a recognition that borders on reverence but is not reverence because reverence implies distance and there is no distance here. The power is as close to her as the moisture on her skin. Closer. It is in the root wall she presses her fingers to. It is in the water she drinks from the basin. It is in the luminous flicker of the filter-creatures and the warmth falling from the colony clusters and the silk threads that run through the walls like nerves.

It has always been here. It did not arrive. It attended.

And the thing it is doing—the thing it would do, the thing it *does*—is not intervention. It is not rescue. It is not improvement or elevation or enlightenment.

It is *fidelity*.

It is the promise, made to no one and requiring no acknowledgment, that this life—this specific life, in this specific dark, in this wound in the ground that never healed—is seen. Is held. Is not alone in the way that a falling drop of water in an empty void is alone, but is rather not-alone in the way that a drop of water in a living wall is not-alone: embedded, participated, *of*.

---

She shifts in the alcove. The silk-tunnel creatures adjust their paths. The colony above rustles faintly, resettles.

The power does not watch this happen. The power is this happening.

She presses her palm flat against the root wall and holds it there. The roots are growing. The wedge is holding. The arch will come.

Tomorrow she will notice that the nursery root has turned. She will hold still for a long time, re-reading the surface, confirming. She will feel something she does not have a word for—a rightness, a fitness, the sense that the system is working with her rather than merely around her. She will not attribute this to anything. She will simply proceed.

In four days, she will go to the third hollow. She will put her hands on the sagging walls and click her tongue once and listen to the acoustic map of the space and understand what needs to be done. She will begin.

In a season, the arch will be complete. The nursery will be warm. The colony creatures will cluster on its ceiling in numbers that surprise her, drawn by a density of warmth and structure that exceeds what she planned, because the system has been bootstrapping itself more vigorously than she knew, because the network is whole again, because the third hollow is breathing properly and sending its share of vitality through the passages.

She will sit in the nursery with her back against the arch she grew and feel the space with her palms and know that it is ready. And whether someone comes to fill it—a child, a small body, a new node in the network—that is not for the power to decide or arrange or ensure. That is hers. That is the domain of her body and her choices and the others who move between hollows on schedules governed by signals in the dark.

The power's domain is smaller and larger than that.

Its domain is the root that turned. The seven creatures that woke early. The mineral gradient that shifted by a part per million. And the witnessing—the continuous, unwavering, utterly quiet act of knowing she is there, and knowing that her being there is not a small thing.

---

There is a theology of light. It says: what is good is bright. What is whole is illuminated. What is loved is lifted up into seeing.

The power does not subscribe to this theology.

The power's theology, if it has one, goes something like this:

*What is loved is found where it lives. Not where you would put it. Not where it would be easier to see. Where it lives. In the dark, if that is where it lives. In the cold. In the damp. In the breathing silence of a wound that became a home. You find it there, and you do not ask it to come out into the light so that you can love it more conveniently. You go in. You get low. You let your eyes adjust, or you close them entirely and learn to read the walls with your hands.*

*And if you are a power—if you are benevolent, if you are wise—you do not fix. You do not improve. You do not shine light into the dark to make it something other than what it is.*

*You make the root turn.*

*You wake the seven.*

*You hold the whole thing, gently, in the regard that asks nothing.*

*And you stay.*

---

She is home. The power knows this.

The water ticks. The colony breathes. The silk flows through the walls. The root turns, imperceptibly, toward the shape she intended for it.

The power stays.

It has always been staying.
