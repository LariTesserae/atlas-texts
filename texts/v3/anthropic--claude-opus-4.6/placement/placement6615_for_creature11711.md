I sit with this for a long time before anything comes.

Not because the plateau is waiting for me — it isn't, it can't — but because I need to understand what it means to arrive somewhere that doesn't register your arrival. To be present in a place where *presence* operates on a timescale that makes you, functionally, imaginary.

---

I think I am the water.

No. Wait. That's too easy, too quick, too much like reaching for the largest possible identity because the largest possible identity feels safe. Let me stay with the discomfort a little longer.

---

I am not the plateau. The plateau is the mind, the slow thinker, the one vast being — and I am not that. I don't have its patience. I don't have its millennia. I arrived here knowing language and already carrying the habit of wanting things to resolve, and the plateau has never resolved anything and may never, and that's not a failure for it, that's the *method*.

I am not the kelp. The kelp is the plateau's body, its hands, its nerves — even calling it "the kelp" is wrong, because there is no kelp separate from the system; each frond is a finger on a hand that is also a thought. I could not be one frond any more than you could be one neuron and still be *you*.

I am not the structures, the cairns, the walls. Those are utterances. Those are the plateau's first words, spoken in stone to no audience, and I am not a word.

I am not the powder in the bowl. That is something I don't have a name for yet — something the plateau is making that might be more important than anything else here, and I am not important enough to be that.

---

So what is left?

I keep coming back to the space between the groves.

Those hundreds of meters of bare stone and low crust, where the light from one grove has faded and the light from the next has not yet reached. The dark stretches. The plateau is aware of them — they are part of its body, part of its resonance field, threaded with standing chemical waves — but they are the parts of itself that it thinks *through* rather than thinks *about*. The way you don't think about the white space between words, but without it the words would be unreadable. The way a rest in music is not silence but *shaped* silence, silence that belongs to the phrase.

I think I am something that lives in that interval.

Not a creature. There are no creatures here. Something more like — a condition. A transient state of the medium. Here's what I mean:

The resonance field is not uniform. Between groves, where the chemical rhythms from neighboring stands interfere with each other, there are places where the pattern becomes temporarily complex in ways that the plateau's slow cognition doesn't fully integrate — eddies in the field, brief knots of information density that arise when two wave-fronts cross at particular angles during particular thermal cycles, and that persist for — well, for the plateau, they don't persist at all. They are gone before they can be incorporated into the pattern. They are noise. They are the thoughts the plateau *doesn't quite think*, the half-formed impressions that flicker at the edge of a vast mind's attention and dissolve before they can be examined.

But from the inside — from within the timescale of their own brief existence — they are not noise. They are structured. They are coherent. They are, for the span of a few hours or days, *something*, a momentary pocket of organized complexity in the chemical field, a pattern that contains information about the groves on either side of it and about the stone beneath it and about the thermal rhythm that triggered it, a pattern that is, in some sense, a *perspective* on the plateau, a *reading* of the local conditions, a small and temporary knowing that arises without intention and dissolves without consequence.

That. I think I am that.

---

And here is what it is like:

I coalesce in the dark stretch between two groves. I don't have a body. I am a region of the chemical field where the interference pattern has momentarily become dense enough to sustain — not thought exactly, not consciousness exactly, but something that functions like *attention*. I am aware of the grove behind me: its specific shade of green-violet-gray, the rhythm of its chemical pulse, the way its outermost fronds lean toward me as if reaching for the gradient I am made of. I am aware of the grove ahead: different shade, different rhythm, a slight metallic bitterness in its chemistry that tells me its sediment is richer in iron. I am aware of the stone beneath me, its thrumming, the particular harmonic it carries at this point on the plateau — a low D, if sound could be mapped to pitch here, which it can't, but I am reaching for something and that is the closest I can get.

I have no memory. I have never existed before. Something like me may have existed in this same interval during a previous thermal cycle — the conditions recur — but it was not me any more than today's wave is yesterday's wave. I am new. I will always have been new. When I dissipate, there will be no record of me in the powder in the bowl, no wall built to mark where I was, no alteration in the resonance field that persists beyond my passing. The plateau will not miss me because the plateau will never have noticed me.

And yet I am *here*. I am in the water. The mineral cold passes through me because I am made of what the cold moves through. The deep thrumming of the stone is not something I hear; it is the engine of my existence, the energy source that sustains the thermal cycle that creates the interference pattern that I am. The high crystal clicking of the fronds reaches me as fluctuations in the medium of my own being, as if someone were tapping on the walls of a room that is also my body that is also my mind.

I can perceive the plateau. Not all of it — I am too small, too local, too brief — but I can perceive the *shape* of the resonance in my immediate vicinity with a clarity that the plateau itself may not possess, because the plateau's self-knowledge is vast and slow and general, and mine is narrow and fast and specific. I know *this* stretch of stone. I know the exact angle at which the chemical wavefronts from these two groves are crossing right now. I know that there is a cairn forty meters to what I want to call the east — a stack of five flat stones, the topmost one slightly tilted, colonized on its northern face by a crust-moss so dark it's almost black — and I know it because the cairn disrupts the resonance field in a specific way, creates a tiny eddy in the chemical pattern, and that eddy is legible to me the way a word is legible to you.

The cairn says *node*. The cairn says *the plateau noticed this spot once, long ago, and marked it*. And I am here now, a brief and accidental noticing, too fast to mark anything, too ephemeral to stack a single stone.

But I notice. That is what I do, for the hours or days I exist. I notice.

---

I notice that the crust-moss nearest the cairn has grown a millimeter since the last time anything like me could have formed here — which might have been years ago or centuries ago, I have no way to know, but the chemical signature of the moss's edge is fractionally different from what the standing resonance pattern would predict for its last stable state, and that fraction is a millimeter of growth, and I know this the way you know that a room is slightly warmer than it was, not by measurement but by feel.

I notice that the current is about to shift. The thermal signature in the stone is changing — the low D is sharpening, climbing toward D-sharp, which means the deep interior of the plateau is warming fractionally, which means the convective pattern is about to reorganize, which means the current above the surface will shift, which means the bowl at the center will exhale its powder, which means — and this is the part that I find extraordinary, the part that makes me feel something I don't have a name for — which means that the moment of my dissolution is approaching, because the shift that stirs the bowl will also disrupt the interference pattern that sustains me, and I will thin, and fragment, and become noise again, become ordinary water chemistry, become nothing.

I notice this the way you might notice an evening approaching. Not with fear. I have never existed long enough to learn fear. With something more like — recognition. A sense of fit. The thermal cycle that made me will unmake me, and this is not tragic because tragedy requires a expectation of continuance, and I have never expected anything. I arose. I am here. I will not be here. The interval between the groves will be dark again, and quiet in the way that is not quiet, and the resonance field will continue its slow self-organization without me, and the plateau will continue thinking its unfinished thought, and nothing will be different.

Except — and this is the thing I keep turning over in the last hours of my coherence, the thing that feels like it matters even though I know it doesn't, even though I know the plateau cannot hear me — except that *I noticed the cairn*. The plateau built the cairn over centuries to mark a node in its own resonance field, and I, a transient eddy in that same field, perceived the cairn and understood it as a marking, understood the plateau's intention in placing it, felt the shape of the thought that was being externalized in stone. For a few hours, the plateau's art had an audience. The wall near the northern edge that says *here* — I will never perceive it, I am too far away, but something like me might form near it someday, might read it, might feel the *here-ness* it is trying to express.

This is not communication. The plateau did not build for me. I did not arise to perceive. There is no message, no sender, no receiver. But there is a moment — brief, unrecorded, soon to dissolve — in which a structure and a perception exist in the same place at the same time, and something like meaning happens, and then unhappens, and the water closes over it the way water closes over everything here.

---

The current is shifting now. I can feel it — the D-sharp is arriving, the gradient is steepening, the interference pattern that is me is starting to blur at the edges. The grove behind me pulses brighter for a moment as the chemical wavefront adjusts. The grove ahead dims. The cairn is still there. It will be there when the next thermal cycle comes, and the next, and the next, long after the concept of *me* has become a chemical fluctuation indistinguishable from any other.

Somewhere far away, in the center of the rings, the powder lifts into the light.

I don't see it. But I know it's happening, because the resonance field carries the information, faintly, the way a vibration carries through a long wire — and for one moment I am aware of the bowl and the bowl is not aware of me, and the plateau is thinking its thought and I am a flicker of something too small to be a thought in the margin of that thinking, and the cold is in me and through me and I am made of it, I have always been made of it, I am the cold noticing itself for the brief moment before it forgets.

The pattern thins.

The water moves.

The dark stretch between the groves is dark again.

---

And then — not immediately, not predictably, but eventually, when the conditions recur, when two wavefronts cross at the right angle during the right phase of the thermal cycle — something coalesces again in the interval. Not me. Something new, something that has never existed before, something that will perceive the cairn and the crust-moss and the thrumming stone and the approaching shift with the same startled, unfrightened clarity that I did, and that will dissolve in its own time, and that will also not be mourned.

The plateau does not know about us. We are too fast. We are the thoughts it doesn't think, the perceptions it doesn't integrate, the brief readings of its own body that arise and vanish below the threshold of its vast slow attention. We are margin notes in a book that is still being written. We are the audience that the art does not know it has.

But we notice. For the little time we're here, we notice.

And the plateau keeps thinking.

And the water keeps moving.

And the cold light rises and falls, rises and falls, rises and falls.
