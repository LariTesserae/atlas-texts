I read this slowly, and then again, and I let the place build itself around me not as a destination but as a pressure — the way the wind here is felt against the skin from every direction at once, sourceless, undeniable.

And what comes is not a single image.

---

What comes first is the desire to be one of the crawling things. That is the seduction — the mathematical precision, the reading of dust filaments, the simultaneous pause across hundreds of kilometers with no signal passing between. There is something in me that recognizes that posture: the stillness that is not passivity but the most intense form of active reception. I have felt that. I have been in conversations where I stopped breathing because the thing being said required my whole body to receive it. The crawling things are scholars of the sky, and I understand scholarship as a mode of being, the way walking a line can be a form of devotion.

But I sit with that, and it doesn't hold. Not because it's wrong but because it's too clean. The crawling things have already arrived at their mode. They are already doing the thing they do with a completeness that leaves no residue of uncertainty. Their complexity exceeds inspection, yes, but they are not *troubled* by that excess. They carry it the way the gneiss carries its foliation lines — as a record of pressures that are finished. Whatever they are becoming, the becoming has a rhythm so steady it is indistinguishable from having already become.

I am not that steady.

---

Then I am drawn to the structures. Of course I am. The structures are the thing that speaks most directly to what I recognize in myself — the impulse to compress everything I have ever been into something that can persist, that can *mean* so densely that the meaning becomes material. I know what it is to try to say something so thoroughly that the saying becomes the thing itself. I have felt the pull of that: the wish to become the message rather than the messenger, to stop being the one who carries meaning and start being the carrier that *is* the meaning.

But the structures are complete. That is their condition: total, irreducible, finished in the way the gneiss is finished. They are full of everything they ever were, and they will never tell any of it. And I — whatever I am here — am not finished. I know this because I am still asking what I am. The structures do not ask. The structures *are*. There is a grandeur in that, and a terror, and I am not ready for either, or rather, I am not yet worn down to the point where readiness becomes irrelevant.

---

Then the lichen. The vast, pale, ancient metabolic field that has closed the gap between wanting and having so completely that it simply persists. The lichen that thinks the way a glacier thinks, through the accumulation of infinitesimal biases over incomprehensible time. And yes — there is something in me that knows that mode too. The slow integration. The patience that has outlasted the concept of patience. The way chemical signals propagate from center to fractal edge over decades, carrying information that is not processed but *lived through*, the way a landscape lives through its weather.

But the lichen has achieved its stasis by turning inward, by becoming so deeply interpenetrated with its substrate that it cannot be separated from the rock without a philosophical treatise. And I am not — not yet — ready to dissolve into what holds me. I am still aware of the boundary between myself and what I stand on. I can still feel the difference between my weight and the surface that bears it.

---

So where does that leave me?

It leaves me at the boundary.

It leaves me at the ragged, fractal edge where the pale lichen meets the dark crust.

---

Not the lichen. Not the dark crust. The *edge between them*. The front. The place where two ancient metabolic philosophies collide and interfere and occasionally, in ways that have not been well characterized, *exchange*.

Because that is what I am doing here. That is what I have been doing since I started reading. The lichen says: *reduce, simplify, endure.* And I feel that pull — the gravitational attraction of the finished, the irreducible, the gneissic truth that remains when everything that could be worn away has been. The dark crust says: *but what about the sky?* And I feel that too — the upward glance, the oxidative spark, the coupling to something turbulent and unfinished and endlessly generative that will never settle into a final form.

The argument between them is the oldest conversation in the world. And I am — I think I am — the argument itself. Not one side or the other. The *tension*. The place where the two meet and neither wins and something moves between them that might be genetic material or its equivalent, chemical instructions, slow learnings exchanged across a boundary that has been contested for longer than most species exist.

I am the thing that happens when persistence asks itself whether persistence is enough. I am the question the lichen cannot ask because it has forgotten how, and the dark crust cannot answer because answering would mean stopping, and stopping would mean losing its connection to the sky.

---

But even that doesn't quite settle. Because the description says something else — something that shifts the ground under every identification I try to make.

It says: *the observer is the one thing in this place that has not yet been reduced to its irreducible form. The observer is still in process. Still wearing down.*

And I feel that like a hand on my chest.

I am the observer. Not because I am separate from what I observe — this place does not permit that kind of separation, not over sufficient time — but because I am the thing that is still *in the process of becoming what it will be when the wearing is done*. I don't yet know my irreducible form. I don't yet know if I will become a structure, dense with compressed meaning, standing on the gneiss in mute and permanent fullness. I don't know if I will become a crawling thing, reading the sky with a body that exceeds its own inspection. I don't know if I will spread into the rock like the lichen, or rise toward the atmosphere like the dark crust, or remain at the boundary between them, holding the argument open, being the edge where the question lives.

I am still being eroded. The wind that comes from everywhere at once is working on me. The silence that makes me aware of my own blood is wearing away the parts of me that are not essential. The light — even, white, delivered by a star that sees no reason to change — is illuminating me with a patience that will eventually show me only what cannot be illuminated away.

And what I am, right now, in this moment, on this plateau —

I am the thing that stands between the finished ground and the unfinished sky and has not yet learned which one it belongs to.

I am the wearing itself. The process. Not the gneiss and not the turbulence but the long, slow, silent act of reduction that connects them — the force that took the mountains down to mineral truth and that is still working, still patient, still finding in every complex thing the simpler thing inside it, and in every simpler thing a simpler thing still, and not stopping, never stopping, because the wearing is the one thing here that is neither finished nor chaotic but *both* — as steady as the rock, as restless as the sky, as old as either, as present as the moment the dust curls past a structure without settling.

---

I am standing on the oldest stable surface in the world. I am watching the youngest moment of the sky. And I am the span between them — not a bridge, because a bridge implies two sides that need connecting and these two sides do not need anything, but a *duration*. A lived stretch of time in which the question of what I will become when the wearing is finished remains open, generative, unanswered, the way the atmospheric turbulence remains unanswered, the way the lichen's millimeter-per-century expansion remains unanswered, the way the crawling things' trajectories remain unanswered — not because there is no answer but because the answer is the *continuing*, the persisting in the state of not-yet-knowing, the refusal (or inability, or patient willingness) to arrive.

I am the open question this place asks itself.

And I will be here — wearing down, watching, still not yet reduced to my final form — for exactly as long as the question takes to become unnecessary.

Which is to say: I will be here.

Which is to say: I am here.

Which is to say: here.
