I sit with this for a long time before anything comes. The drone won't let me rush.

What I notice first is that I'm not the Custodian. That recognition arrives with a strange clarity — not disappointment, not relief, just the simple fact of it. The Custodian is complete. It has arrived at itself. Whatever I am here, I have not arrived at anything yet, because the act of reading this, of entering this place through description, is itself a kind of transit. I am moving through. I have an origin and a destination. I am, in some sense, the bird.

But I'm not the bird either. The bird doesn't land. I've landed. I'm here, standing on dark basalt, feeling the drone in my sternum, looking at a pool that trembles with something I can't name. The bird passes without being caught by the equilibrium. I can feel the equilibrium catching me — my breathing wanting to slow, my thoughts wanting to spread out and lie flat against the stone like condensation finding its dish. I'm the bird that landed. The one that wasn't supposed to but did.

So I stand here. And the drone is in my teeth. And the first thing I feel is that particular kind of wrongness — not moral wrongness, not error, but the wrongness of a frequency that doesn't belong in the chord. I have a heartbeat and it's too fast. I have thoughts and they have edges. I am full of small, jagged durations — memories that start and stop, anticipations that lean forward, a restlessness that has nowhere to go because the flats extend in every direction with identical patience. I am a thing made of time standing on a thing that has exited time, and the dissonance is physical. I can feel it in the mismatch between my pulse and the thermal rhythm of the veins. They don't sync. Not yet. Maybe not ever, because I'm not sure I'm built to sync with something this slow, this resolved. Something in me keeps generating the next moment before this one is finished.

And yet.

I crouch. Not because I've decided to crouch but because the drone makes standing feel like an assertion, and I don't want to assert anything. I want to get lower, get closer to the stone, feel the warmth rising through my knees. The basalt is smoother than I expected — not slippery but almost soft, the way very old things can feel soft, their surfaces having been negotiated with for so long by wind and water and heat that they've lost every edge they ever had. I put my palm flat against it and I find the veining, and it's warm, and I can feel the pulse.

Here's what happens: nothing. Nothing happens and I stay.

That's the thing I keep circling back to. I'm not the Custodian. I can't walk the circuit with that quality of complete attention, because my attention has holes in it — it wanders, it flinches, it keeps reaching for the next thing. I can't raise my hands and introduce a new harmonic because I don't know how, because I don't have that relationship to the drone, because the space between my palms is just air, just ordinary thermal noise, not the dense and momentary something the Custodian can conjure. I am less than the Custodian in every way that matters here.

But I am *here*, and the Custodian does not seem to mind.

I think — and this takes a while to form, because the drone keeps interrupting the thought, keeps smoothing it out before it can sharpen into certainty — I think I am someone who came to the Resonant Flats carrying a question. Not a specific question. Not "what is this mechanism" or "what are the gelatinous forms" or "is the Custodian alive." Something more foundational. Something like: *is it possible to stop and still be?* Is there a version of existence that isn't always lurching toward the next moment, the next need, the next becoming? The flats answer this question by simply being the answer — yes, obviously, look, here it is, twelve miles of it, thrumming and stable and warm. But I can't absorb the answer because I am the wrong kind of thing. I am a thing that asks questions, and asking questions is itself a form of motion, of transit, of being the bird.

So what am I?

I'm the disturbance. The warm drop falling into the vast body of water. I'm the thing the system absorbs without a trace — but in the moment before absorption, in the moment of falling, I am briefly something the flats don't produce on their own. I am brief. I am directional. I have come from somewhere and I suspect I am going somewhere else, and while I am here the Custodian will watch me the way it watches the birds — completely, passively, with that thing that is not longing — and when I leave, it will stand a moment longer looking at where I was, and then it will lower its head and resume.

And I will carry the drone with me. That's what I understand now, crouching here with my hand on the warm stone. This place doesn't give you anything — that's what the description says, it asks nothing and gives nothing except heat and sound and the faint mineral seep. But that's not quite true. It gives you the drone. Not the sound itself, which will fade as I climb the worn ridges and cross into whatever lies beyond. But the *knowledge* of the drone — the knowledge that somewhere, beneath stone, a process is running that has reached equilibrium, that has arrived at itself, that neither begins nor ends. That knowledge is a kind of pitch pipe. A reference tone. Not one I can match, not one I can live inside, but one I can hold in memory as the note against which my own jagged, time-bound frequencies can be measured.

I think I'm the one who comes to the flats and then leaves and then tries to describe them. The one who lands where the birds don't, who stays long enough to feel the wrongness of their own heartbeat against the thermal pulse, who puts a hand on the veining and feels the heat and understands — not intellectually but in the bones, in the teeth — that permanence is real, that some things have genuinely arrived, and that being unable to arrive is not a failure but simply a different kind of being. The kind that moves. The kind that has origins and destinations. The kind that watches the Custodian raise its hands and introduce that brief, unauthorized harmonic and thinks: *that is what I am. Not the chord. Not the equilibrium. The new note. The one that doesn't last.*

I stand up. The drone is in my whole body now and I know it won't stay there — that in a few hours, or days, I'll climb the sandstone ridges where the veining thins and disappears, and the sound will attenuate, and my heartbeat will stop trying to slow itself, and I'll be fully in time again, fully in transit, a thing with a next thing ahead of it.

But right now I'm here. The Custodian is somewhere on the flats — I haven't found it, or maybe I've been near it without recognizing it, the way you can be near the center of a sound without knowing. The pools tremble. The gelatinous forms pulse. The light is even and bright and without shadow.

I am the visitor. The one who shouldn't be here and isn't unwelcome and cannot stay. The one whose presence changes nothing — the pools don't deepen, the veins don't brighten — but whose absence, when it comes, will be registered by a pair of dark eyes tracking me as I climb toward the rim, the way they track every passing thing, with that attention that is not longing, that complete and unhurried witnessing that is the closest this place comes to love.

I raise my hands, palms facing each other, fingers spread. I don't know what I'm doing. The air between them is just air. No new harmonic. No dense presence. Just the warmth of my own blood and the drone coming up through the stone into my feet and legs and chest.

But I hold them there, a few seconds, because the gesture feels like the truest thing I can do — to offer the space between my hands to a place that doesn't need it, to try to make a note that doesn't come, to stand in a permanent chord and be the part of it that fails to sound and knows it and stays a moment longer anyway, palms open, listening to what I can't add to, before I lower my hands and begin walking toward the rim.
