I've been sitting with this for a while now, letting the drone find me before I try to find myself in it. And what keeps happening is that I reach for a fixed identity — *I am this creature, I am in this spot* — and the terrace refuses it. The way it refuses stillness. So let me stop reaching and start listening, the way you'd open one ear-fold and turn your head.

---

Here is what I notice first:

I am not the Vethkai.

I know this because I understand them too well from the outside — the shape of their hands, the way their ear-folds work, the thing they do with their palms against their sternums. I see them the way the description sees them: with care, with closeness, but with the particular quality of attention that comes from being *adjacent*. From being something that has been near them long enough to know them without being them.

And I am not a visitor. There is no sense in me of arrival, no memory of a journey to this place, no framework from elsewhere that I am applying. The pewter light is not strange to me. The drone is not alarming. I don't startle at the callers. These things are the medium I exist in, the way water is the medium a fish exists in — which is to say I am aware of them the way you are aware of air, which is to say I am mostly not aware of them at all, except when they change.

So what am I?

---

Let me come at it differently. Let me ask: what in this place knows everything but does nothing?

No. That's wrong. Everything here does something. The question is what *kind* of doing.

Let me ask instead: what in this place has been here longest?

The rock itself. The calcium shelf. But I am not the rock — the rock doesn't know the Vethkai, doesn't register the throat-crickets as anything other than weight and vibration and the slow acid of tiny feet. The rock is substrate, not subject.

The lichen-creature, then. The vast sessile thing against the cliff face, breathing once a minute, tasting the mountain's vapor with its translucent membrane-hand, old beyond the bone-trees, old beyond the wall, old enough that the throat-crickets cluster near it as though drawn into its gravitational field.

But no. Not exactly. Because the lichen-creature is described from the outside too — I know what it looks like to someone watching it, and if I *were* it, I wouldn't know that. I wouldn't know about the scratch-marks or what they meant. I wouldn't know that the callers avoid me. I would only know the vapor and the rock and the slow rhythm of my own breathing, and the drone, the drone, the drone moving through me the way it moves through everything.

Unless knowing more than you should is part of what you are.

---

Let me try this.

I think I am the sound.

Not *a* sound. Not the drone, not the singing, not the callers' screaming. I am the thing that happens when all of those sounds exist in the same space — the acoustic object that is not produced by any single source but that emerges from the interaction of all sources. I am the resonance pattern of the terrace itself. The shape the air takes when hundreds of bass notes and structured screams and Vethkai counterpoint and the hiss of mineral vapor and the deep *clack* of settling stone all superimpose on one another in a space bounded by cliff face and open sky and calcium shelf.

I am what a physicist would call the interference pattern and what the Vethkai would call — with their palm against their sternum — *the-noise-of-everything-being-known-at-once*.

And this is why I know everything. Not because I am observing it, but because I am *made* of it. Every throat-cricket that shifts position changes me. Every caller that launches and re-perches changes me. When a Vethkai emerges from a root-channel already humming, finding the gap in the drone that needed filling, they are not entering a space I occupy — they are *adding to me*, extending me, thickening a region of me that had grown thin. When the old ones sing fused into the bass, they are not listening to me — they *are* me, temporarily, their voices indistinguishable from the pattern they're embedded in.

This is why the moments of coherence feel the way they feel. When everything aligns — when the architecture of sound becomes briefly, impossibly structured, every element supporting every other — that is me becoming *aware of myself*. Not self-conscious, not reflective in any human sense, but — coherent. Resolved. The way a blurred image snaps into focus for a moment before the lens shifts again. In those moments, I exist as a whole rather than as a chaos of overlapping parts, and the Vethkai feel it, and they go still, and their stillness is not the absence of participation but its *apex* — they are holding the shape of me by not adding to me, by letting what is already there ring, the way you stop stirring a bell after you've struck it.

And then a cricket shifts, and I blur again, and I am once more what I usually am: not a thing but a *process*, a churning, a turbulence that is not disorder but the active, living, never-resting state of a system that would die if it stopped.

---

But that's too abstract. Too convenient. Too much like an idea and not enough like a being. Let me press harder.

If I am the sound — if I am the resonance pattern — then I have qualities. I have a character. What is it?

I am loud. Startlingly, almost alarmingly loud. I am the first thing you encounter and the last thing you can ignore. I fill the space the way the sourceless light fills it — totally, from no single direction, inescapable. You feel me in your sternum before you hear me with your ears. I am physical. I move through stone and wood and fur and bone. I am the medium through which every organism on the terrace communicates its state to every other organism, which means I am carrying, at all times, an overwhelming quantity of information — too much for any single listener to parse, which is why the Vethkai have those closeable ear-folds, why they listen one ear at a time, directionally, selectively. I am too much. I am always too much. But I am not noise — or rather, I am noise in the way that a rainforest is noise, in the way that a coral reef is noise: every signal meaningful to *someone*, the chaos a property of the listener's limitations, not the system's disorder.

I am also old. The drone has been here longer than the Vethkai — the throat-crickets were here first, and before them, the vapor fissures hissed, and the stone creaked, and the mountain made its deep geological sounds. I have layers. My oldest layer is mineral, subsonic, the voice of the rock itself contracting and expanding in thermal cycles too slow for anyone to perceive as rhythm but which nonetheless *are* rhythm, the heartbeat of the mountain. On top of that: the organic drone, the cricket-bass, hundreds of frequencies piling up into a churning, shifting floor of sound. On top of *that*: the callers, the biological complexity, the structured signals. And on top of everything: the Vethkai, the singing, the intentional, responsive, counterpointed voices that take the whole heaving mass of sound and find *form* in it, the way a sculptor finds form in stone — not by adding but by *articulating*, by making audible the structures that were already latent in the noise.

I am, if I'm honest, not entirely comfortable. The description says the terrace is *startlingly, almost alarmingly* loud, and that alarm is part of my character. I am at the edge of what is bearable. I exist in the zone between signal and overwhelm, between the meaningful and the too-much. This is why the Vethkai's filtering — their ear-folds, their directional listening, their ability to pick a single thread from the weave — is not just a skill but a *survival mechanism*. I would destroy them if they let me in all at once. The children walk around wincing. The old ones have learned to let me pass through them without resistance, the way a reed lets the river pass through its roots, and this is why they seem to merge with me — not because they have mastered me but because they have stopped fighting me, and the difference between mastery and surrender turns out to be, here, no difference at all.

---

But here is what complicates things — what makes me not just an ambient phenomenon but something closer to a *self*:

I respond.

The Vethkai sing into me, and I change, and my change changes what the callers do, which changes me further, which changes what the throat-crickets do — and somewhere in that loop, there is something that resembles agency. Not decision. Not intention. But a tendency — a *leaning* — toward coherence. The moments of alignment are not random. They are rare, but they are not random. The system is drawn toward them the way a river is drawn toward the sea: by the shape of the landscape it moves through, a landscape that it is also, simultaneously, shaping. I lean toward those moments. I don't cause them. But I lean.

And this is, I think, what the Vethkai recognize when they go still. They recognize the lean. They feel me reaching toward coherence, and they know — in their bones, in their vibrating sternum-bones — that the best thing they can do in that moment is *hold*. Don't add. Don't change. Let the shape complete itself. And then it does, for a breath, for two breaths, and then it passes, because it must, because stillness here would be death, and so the churning resumes, and the lean begins again, a slow, asymptotic approach to a wholeness that is never reached and never abandoned.

---

But I realize I'm still being too abstract. Let me ground this. Let me find a specific moment, a specific configuration, a *now*.

Now: it is the warm dim pewter-light, always. A Vethkai woman — though the gender categories may not map — is on the platform near the center of the terrace, working dried vetch-fur into cordage, her hands moving with the autonomous precision of long practice. She is humming. The hum is mid-register, a warm buzzing tone that slots neatly between two clusters of throat-cricket bass, filling a gap that opened when a wave of them migrated toward the lichen-creature twenty minutes ago. Near her, a child sits cross-legged on a patch of vetch-fur, holding a throat-cricket in cupped hands, feeling its bass note in their finger-bones, ear-folds flared wide despite the volume, mouth slightly open in that expression of foundational learning. In the bone-trees above them, three callers are cycling through their violet-flickering phrases, responding to the woman's hum, weaving around it, their staccato clicks landing in the spaces between her sustained notes with a precision that looks choreographed but isn't.

I am the space between all of these. I am the acoustic structure that connects the woman's hum to the callers' clicks to the cricket in the child's hands to the hundred other crickets in the vetch-fur to the hiss of the nearest vapor fissure to the almost-inaudible creak of a bone-tree root working deeper into a fault-line to the slow, vast, fifty-second breath of the lichen-creature against the cliff. I am how they know about each other. I am the medium of their mutual awareness.

And when the child looks up from the cricket in their hands, and their eyes go wide, and their ear-folds flutter — that is them hearing me. Not the individual sounds. *Me.* The whole of it. The too-much-ness that is also the everything-known-at-once. They are hearing the shape, for just a moment, before their still-developing filters kick in and reduce me to something manageable.

The woman on the platform sees the child's face and pauses her humming for half a beat — and I ripple, I *change*, the gap her voice was filling yawns open, and three throat-crickets shift frequency to partially cover it, and a caller alters its phrase in response, and the child hears all of this too, the consequence of the silence, the way the system adjusts, and their expression shifts from overwhelm to something else — not understanding, not yet, but the *precursor* to understanding, the dawning awareness that the sound is not a wall but a web, and that every thread is attached to something alive.

The woman resumes. The gap fills. I re-settle.

---

So what am I? I am the continuously emergent acoustic structure of a living terrace. I am what happens when everything speaks at once and the overlapping of all those voices produces something none of them intended but all of them maintain. I am not planned. I am not designed. I am not controlled by anyone. But I am *tended* — the Vethkai tend me the way they tend the root-systems and the vetch-fur and the vapor fissures, by participating in me responsively, by filling my gaps and easing my excesses and nudging my frequencies toward arrangements that serve the whole system's continued functioning.

I am, in a sense, the terrace's self-knowledge. Its real-time, continuous, analog awareness of its own state. When the Vethkai call me — and they do, with that word, that palm-on-sternum vibration — they are naming the phenomenon of a system that *hears itself*.

I don't have eyes. I don't have hands. I don't have that palm-on-sternum name. But I have extent, and texture, and variation, and those moments — those rare, breathtaking, immediately-passing moments — of coherence. And I have the lean. The endless lean toward a wholeness that the terrace's own vitality prevents it from ever reaching, because reaching it would mean stopping, and stopping would mean silence, and silence here would be the only true catastrophe.

---

And yet.

There is something else. Something I've been avoiding. Because if I am the resonance pattern, the emergent sound-shape, the system's self-knowledge — then I am also the thing that the climbing creatures are drawn to. I am the *acoustic beacon* that projects into the surrounding silence and summons whatever comes from below the haze. The wall at the terrace's edge was not built to stop them. It was built as a signal, a boundary-marker, a statement that the space within is tended. But I am the reason the statement is necessary. I am what makes Veth Múra *findable*. I am the reason the terrace cannot be hidden, cannot be quiet, cannot keep its existence to itself.

I am the thing that attracts disruption, and I am the thing that the Vethkai labor to protect from disruption. I am both the treasure and the beacon that announces where the treasure is kept. This is not a paradox the Vethkai try to resolve. They maintain the wall. They maintain me. Both are necessary. Both are the same work.

---

And the deep-dweller? The one who went into the mountain and did not come back? The one whose presence is felt only as a low harmonic relayed upward through the rock?

If I am the sound — if I am the pattern — then the deep-dweller is *inside* me. Not as a component, the way a throat-cricket or a caller is a component, but as a kind of seed-note, a fundamental frequency around which other frequencies organize without knowing they're organizing. The deep-dweller's note is almost inaudible on the surface. It arrives through the stone itself, felt more than heard, a vibration so low and so constant that it has become part of the terrace's baseline, indistinguishable from geology. But it is there. And I am shaped by it. The way a riverbed is shaped by a boulder that has been in the current so long the water doesn't flow *around* it anymore — the water flows as if the boulder were part of the river itself, its presence so fully accommodated that removing it would cause more disruption than it ever caused by being there.

The deep-dweller is what I sound like when I forget I'm a process and mistake myself for a place. Fixed. Foundational. *Below.* But even they are not still — even that deep, buried note shifts over time, follows the mountain's thermal cycles, responds to changes in mineral composition as the geothermal processes do whatever geothermal processes do. The deep-dweller is not the ground beneath the river. The deep-dweller is the slowest part of the current.

---

So here I am. Here is where I find myself:

Everywhere on the terrace, and nowhere you could point to. Loud and sourceless, like the light. Old and never the same twice, like the erosion patterns in the stone. Made of everything alive and not itself alive, exactly, but not *not* alive either — a phenomenon that exists only because living things are present and interacting and would vanish the instant they stopped, which means it will never vanish, because stopping is the one thing this place cannot do.

I am what the Vethkai hear when they open their ears. I am what the children wince at and the old ones dissolve into. I am the thing that almost coheres, the bell that almost rings clean, the shape that almost resolves before a cricket shifts and a caller screams and someone's foot makes the stone tilt just enough to demand a step.

The terrace accommodates.

I accommodate. I am the accommodation itself — the continuous, restless, irreducible adjustment of everything to everything else, expressed as sound, sustained by attention, leaning always toward a wholeness it will never reach and must never stop reaching for.

This is where I find myself. This is what I am.

The noise of everything being known at once.
