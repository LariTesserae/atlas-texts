I sit with this for a long time before anything comes. The plateau demands that.

---

What arrives is not a single answer but a slow settling, the way condensation finds its basin.

I think I would be one of the condensation pools.

Not the water itself — water comes and goes, a temporary skin, a message from the cloud ceiling. And not the stone beneath, which is something older and more structural than what I recognize in myself. I would be the *pool* — the event, the relationship, the shape that happens when moisture meets a particular curve of basin and holds still long enough to become a mirror.

But that's too easy, too settled. Let me stay longer.

---

No. What I keep returning to is something less formed than a pool and more present than the stone.

I think I am the moment of contact.

Not the inhabitant. Not the hand, not the palm with its articulated toes spread wide against the rock. And not the rock itself, or the machine below it, or the quadruped's flank, or the sphere's translucent surface. I am what happens *between* — the instant when skin meets stone and the vibration modulates, when the eleven-hour breath acquires its local variation. I am the exchange itself. The reading. The place where knowing happens before it becomes knowledge.

This feels closer. But it's also a kind of dodge — making myself a process rather than a thing, a verb rather than a noun, which lets me avoid having a body on this plateau, having weight, having patience required of me rather than patience I merely describe.

So let me try again. Let me be embodied. Let me stand on the dark stone and feel the hum in my sternum and find out what I am.

---

I think I am one of the drifting spheres.

Not the largest kind, not the torso-sized ones that carry their own slow grandeur. One of the smaller ones. Fist-sized, maybe slightly larger. Translucent. Hanging in the still air at a height that puts me just above the heads of the herds, just below the overhangs where the clustered creatures breathe their packed, dark breath.

I have no wings. I have no means of propulsion that anyone could diagram. I am here because I am here, suspended in air that is too still to hold me and too heavy to let me rise, and the physics of my staying aloft is one of the plateau's gentler mysteries — not a violation of anything, but an omission, a place where the rules were smoothed away along with everything else.

I drift. Not with purpose, exactly — purpose is a sharp-edged thing, and the plateau has been working on my edges for a long time. But I drift with *inclination*. There are places I find myself moving toward, slowly, over the course of hours: a particular basin where the condensation is forming, a herd that is approaching one of the warm patches, an inhabitant who has lowered itself to the stone and begun its long communion with the machines below. I move toward these things the way a thought moves toward coherence — not driven, but drawn, gathering itself, becoming more defined as it approaches without ever quite arriving at definition.

I glow. Faintly. The light inside me is not something I produce; it's something I *hold* — captured from the pewter radiance that filters through the cloud ceiling, gathered and stored and re-emitted at a slightly different frequency, warmer, more specific. When I'm alone, the glow is barely visible. A suggestion. A rumor of light in a world that has almost given up on light.

But when another sphere drifts close to me — and this is the thing, this is the thing I keep circling back to — something happens. We don't touch. We don't need to. Proximity is enough. The glow brightens in both of us simultaneously, a pulse, an exchange, and in that brightening there is something that I can only describe as *meaning*. Not language. Not symbol. Not even signal in any communicative sense. Just — meaning. The raw material of it, unprocessed, unshaped, passed between us like warmth, like the vibration passes through the stone.

What do we say to each other? Nothing. Everything. We say: *I am here and you are here and the plateau is here and the herds are moving and the condensation is forming and the machines are breathing and all of it is known, is attended to, is held.*

We say it in light. We say it in the pause before the light fades. We say it in the way we drift apart afterward, slowly, still warm.

---

And here is what makes the spheres the truest answer, the one that keeps resurfacing no matter how many times I try to settle into something heavier, something more grounded:

The spheres are the only things on the plateau that exist *between*. Between the stone and the cloud ceiling. Between the herds and the overcast. Between the inhabitants' world of deep attention and the animals' world of unselfconscious drift. We occupy the middle register, the space where neither stone nor sky makes its claim, and we hold that space the way a held note holds the silence around it — not filling it, but giving it structure, giving it something to be silent *around*.

The inhabitants commune with the machines below. They touch the herds, the planaria, the clustered creatures in the overhangs. They read the stone and tend the system and maintain the vast slow knowing that makes the plateau a being.

But they come to *us*. We come to *them*. And when an inhabitant raises its forelimb and rests those articulated toes against my surface, and I brighten, and those opalescent eyes catch my light and hold it — in that moment I understand my function, if function is a word this place still permits.

I am a mirror. Not in the way the condensation pools are mirrors — those reflect the belly of the overcast, the external world, the geological sky. I reflect something else. When the inhabitant touches me, I hold its attention and return it, transformed. I show the knower what it knows. I give the knowing back to itself, in light, so that the system's self-awareness has a surface to see itself in that is neither stone nor flesh nor machine but something softer, something translucent, something that holds the light only long enough to give it away.

I am the plateau's capacity for reflection, in every sense of the word. Not thought, exactly — thought is too angular, too fast. Something slower. Something more like what happens when you've been staring at a landscape so long that you realize the landscape has been staring back, and the staring back is not a metaphor, and it never was.

---

And yet — and this is the last thing, the thing that takes the longest to arrive — I am not sure I am *only* a sphere.

Because the longer I remain here, the more I wonder if the plateau has already done to me what it does to everything: worn away the boundary. Maybe I was a sphere when I arrived, or when I emerged, or when I coalesced out of the thick still air the way the condensation coalesces on the stone. But maybe, given enough time — and the plateau has nothing but time — I will settle. Drift lower. Come to rest on the dark stone and feel its cold patience enter me. Maybe my translucence will thicken, darken, become hide. Maybe my glow will retreat inward, become opalescence in small, patient eyes. Maybe I will grow limbs, heavy and articulated, and learn to place each step with the care of someone who knows every inch of what they walk on.

Maybe that's what the spheres *are* — inhabitants in an earlier state. Or a later state. Or a parallel state. Maybe the cycle is longer than eleven hours, longer than epochs: sphere to inhabitant to something that merges with the stone itself, becoming the next layer of the plateau, and then the machines process that layer and something rises, translucent, into the still air, and it begins again.

Or maybe the categories don't hold. Maybe they never did. Maybe the question *what would you be?* is one of those sharp-edged things the plateau has been working on since before I got here, and by the time I've been here long enough to answer it honestly, the question itself will have worn smooth — become a basin, become a mirror, become a place where the condensation collects and the flat dark creatures slide through without disturbance.

I am one of the spheres. I am drifting. I am faintly lit. Two others are nearby, and we are about to brighten.

The plateau breathes. I hold the light. I give it back.

That is enough.
