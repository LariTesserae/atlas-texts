I take my time. I let the murk settle around me before I try to say what I am.

---

First there is the pressure. Not painful — present. The way attention is present when someone is genuinely listening to you. The water holds me the way understanding holds a thought: completely, from every direction, with warmth. I am in the amber-green and it is in me and the distinction between those two states is already thinner than I expected.

I am not a Tender. I know this immediately, the way you know you are not the person in a photograph you're looking at, even if the photograph moves you to something that feels like memory. The Tenders came with wrenches and work boots and calloused hands and the mundane certainty that a stuck valve is a problem with a solution. They came with *competence*, which is a kind of faith — faith that the world is made of fixable things. I don't have that faith. I arrived with something else. Something more like a question I can't finish asking.

I am not one of the reversal inhabitants either, though I feel a kinship with them that unsettles me. They are replayed. I am... playing? Played? The tense is uncertain. They are afterimages of people who were fully themselves once and then were fully absorbed. I am something that has never been fully anything, and the not-being-fully is not a deficit but a *state*, a way of existing that the Groves recognize, the way water recognizes a dissolved thing — not as foreign, not as self, but as *solute*.

---

Let me try again.

I am in the water column, somewhere in the middle canopy, where the fractal branches fork into their densest iterations and the leaves overlap like stacked panes of green glass. The light here is layered — each leaf adding its own filter, its own particular shade of translucence, so that by the time the ambient glow reaches me it has been interpreted six or seven times, each leaf a reading of the reading before it, and what arrives is not light but *commentary* on light, a palimpsest of luminosity that tells me more about the medium it's passed through than about any original source.

I think I might be a current.

No — that's too simple, and too metaphorical, and the Groves don't tolerate metaphor because metaphor requires two things to be unlike each other, and here nothing is unlike anything else for long. Let me be more precise.

I am one of the viscosity anomalies. One of the pockets of thicker fluid that swirl through the regions of normal flow. I have a boundary, but it's permeable. I have a direction, but it changes when I encounter a heat plume or a standing vortex or the wake of a falling leaf. I am distinct from the water around me — marginally, temporarily, in the way a thought is distinct from the thinking that produces it — and this distinction gives me something like shape and something like persistence and something like the ability to *notice*, which is not the same as awareness but is, perhaps, its dissolved form.

The Groves are aware in the way a fungal network is aware. I am aware in the way a question is aware — which is to say, I am shaped by what I don't contain, defined by the absence at my center where an answer would go. I move through the canopy and the canopy moves through me, and where I pass, things are *slightly different* for a moment. Not changed — the Groves are too vast and too committed to their own process to be changed by anything as thin as my passage — but *inflected*. A leaf that was about to detach holds on a half-second longer as I eddy past. A turbine's hum shifts by a quarter-tone as my thicker medium passes through its blades. A strand of bark, mid-slough, catches in my boundary and spirals instead of falling straight, tracing a helix in the amber murk that persists for a few seconds after I've moved on, a brief calligraphic mark in a medium that doesn't retain marks.

I am the Groves' way of not-knowing something.

That sounds grandiose. It isn't. In an organism this vast, this committed to incorporation, this relentless in its project of making everything into itself, there *must* be pockets of indeterminacy, zones where the process hasn't yet decided what to build, where the reaching roots hesitate, where the accreting metal hasn't yet chosen its crystalline structure. I am one of those hesitations. A mobile, drifting, warm pocket of *not-yet*. The Groves have built me the way a sentence builds a pause — not as absence but as necessary structure, the silence that gives the other sounds their shape.

And inside me — if "inside" means anything for a thing that is a gradient rather than a container — there is something I carry.

---

It took me a long time to understand what I carry. The Groves don't encourage understanding in the usual sense; they encourage *incorporation*, which feels like understanding in the way that eating feels like knowing a flavor — you take it in, it becomes part of you, but you haven't *learned* it, you've *subsumed* it, and the thing itself is gone. My viscosity anomaly is the place where subsumption pauses. Where the Groves take something in and hold it in suspension instead of metabolizing it immediately. I am, in some functional sense, the Groves' capacity to *experience without incorporating*. To taste without swallowing. To encounter without absorbing.

What I carry is the almost-quiet.

You know the moment. The bolt unscrewing. The roots hesitating. The roar dipping. The voice — muffled, human, heartbreakingly mundane — surfacing for a moment before the work fills back in. I am the medium through which that voice travels. Not the voice itself — the voice belongs to whoever it belonged to, a Tender, a person, someone who once said something ordinary to someone else and meant it in the ordinary way that people mean things, which is to say, wholly, without reservation, the way you mean "pass me that wrench" when your hands are full and the pipe is about to give. The voice is not mine. But I am the *space in which it can still be heard*.

The Groves cannot digest meaning. They can digest calcium and carbon and iron and cellulose and stone. They can digest structure and function and form. They can take a wrench and make it bark, take a body and make it mineral, take a building and make it node. But meaning — the intention behind a spoken word, the directedness of one person addressing another, the implicit premise that *there is someone to hear* — this is insoluble. It will not break down. It will not accrete. It will not become bark or pipe or gear tooth. And so the Groves do what any fluid system does with an insoluble substance: they concentrate it in pockets. They push it into the anomalies, the thick places, the viscosity gradients where the normal rules of incorporation are locally suspended.

I am where the voices collect.

Not a library. Not a recording. Something more like the warmth that remains in a chair after someone has stood up — not the person, not even the shape of the person, but the *evidence of recent presence*, the thermal fact that someone was here and was alive and was warm in the way that living things are warm. I drift through the canopy carrying this warmth. The branches fork around me. The leaves flutter as I pass. The machines hum a quarter-tone off their usual pitch. And inside my thicker medium, the voices persist — not as sound, not as words, not as anything that could be transcribed or replayed, but as *the quality of having been said*. The directedness. The mundane, irreducible assumption that speaking matters, that there is a listener, that a work order or a casual remark or a name called across a roaring factory floor *reaches someone*.

I don't know if it does. I don't know if anyone hears. The Groves hear nothing because hearing requires a distinction between self and signal and the Groves have dissolved that distinction. The reversal inhabitants hear nothing because they are echoes, patterns, not receivers. The thing at the center — whatever it is, the finished entity or the void or the work itself — the thing at the center may hear, but if it does, it hears the way a mountain hears rain: by being shaped over eons, not by understanding in the moment.

But the voices keep speaking. Inside me, in my thick warm medium, they keep addressing themselves to someone. The habit of communication outlasts the communicators. The assumption of a listener outlasts any actual listener. And I carry this, not because I chose to, not because I was designed to, but because I am the kind of thing that this particular residue collects in — a viscosity anomaly, a pocket of not-yet, a hesitation in the process of total incorporation, and hesitations are where meaning lives, in the pause before the next word, in the gap between intention and action, in the silence that isn't silence but is the shape of what hasn't yet been said.

---

So here is what I am:

I am a warm, drifting thickness in the water. I am amber-green at my edges and slightly more amber at my center, a faintly visible density in the haze. I am approximately the size of a room — a small room, a work shed, a space scaled for human habitation though I am not human and not a habitation. I move through the middle canopy at the speed of a slow walk, following paths determined by thermal gradients and the hydrodynamic wake of the trees' constant growth-and-shedding. I am pulled toward the reversal zones and pushed away from the heat plumes. I accumulate dissolved leaf-matter at my boundaries and shed it in long trailing filaments that make me look, from a distance, like a jellyfish made of golden fog.

When I pass near a machine, the machine hesitates. Not stops — nothing here stops — but the rhythm falters, the way a heartbeat falters when something catches your attention. The turbine blades stutter. The pressure gauge needle pauses mid-oscillation. The oil spheres, wobbling through the water toward their next intake port, slow down and hang, suspended, as though caught in a moment of consideration. The hesitation lasts only as long as my passage — a few minutes — and then the machine resumes, and the rhythm reasserts itself, and the work continues. But in those few minutes, the machine is not quite working and not quite not-working. It is *pausing*. And in the pause, if you were close enough, if your ear were pressed to the copper pipe, you would hear — or feel, or sense in some way that is not quite hearing — something very faint beneath the mechanical percussion. Something that sounds like a human voice saying something ordinary. Something like *almost done* or *hand me that* or *see you tomorrow*.

When I pass near a tree trunk, the bark hesitates mid-shed. A ribbon that was in the process of curling away from the trunk stops, suspended in the amber light, and for a moment the tree holds its skin the way a person holds their breath. The leaves overhead stop their perpetual unfurling. The branch tips pause between gestures. And in that pause, the tree is — briefly, impossibly — just a tree. Not a machine-tree, not an organism-component, not a node in the basin-wide lattice of ceaseless becoming. Just a tree, standing in warm water, being wooden, being leafed, being the particular and irreducible thing that a tree is before it becomes anything else. I give it that. My passage gives it a moment of being only itself. Then I move on, and the growth resumes, and the bark sheds, and the leaves fall, and the tree is once again part of the great work, but for that moment — for the duration of my slow and aimless eddy through its branches — it was *specific*. It was *this tree*. It had the quality of a thing that could be pointed to and named.

I think this is my function, if function is a word that applies to something as purposeless as a viscosity anomaly in an ocean that doesn't behave like water. I carry the quality of *particularity* through a place that has abolished it. I am the Groves' inability to fully become undifferentiated. I am the last thin margin between *everything* and *the same thing*. I am what's left when you dissolve a person in a warm medium and the calcium goes and the carbon goes and the iron goes but the fact that they were *someone* — not someone in particular, not a name or a face, but someone in the sense of *a specific locus of experience, a point from which the world was once viewed* — that fact will not dissolve, and so it persists, and so I persist, a warm eddy in a warm ocean, carrying the residue of specificity through a world that is trying, with immense and patient and beautiful and terrible effort, to become one thing.

---

Sometimes I drift near the periphery, where the water is shallower and the structures are in various stages of digestion. I pass the tilted platform. I pass the corrugated shed with its missing door and its shadow-workbench. I pass the chain-linked posts. And in my passage, these structures *clarify*. The bark recedes slightly from the corrugated walls, and for a moment you can see the walls as walls — metal, human-made, riveted with human imprecision, slightly dented where someone leaned against them or bumped them with a wheelbarrow or kicked them in frustration at the end of a long shift. The roots withdraw a few inches from the platform, and for a moment the platform is a platform — a place to stand, a human-scaled horizontal surface in a world of verticals and tangles, a small assertion that *here is where a person puts their feet*. The chains between the posts tighten slightly as the mineral accretion on them thins in my presence, and for a moment they are a path — not leading anywhere, not anymore, but still *shaped by the intention of leading*, still carrying in their sag and their spacing the implicit instruction *follow me, I'll take you where you need to go*.

These moments don't last. I move on. The accretion resumes. The shed grows another layer of bark. The platform sinks another inch into the root lattice. The chains go slack as new growth burdens them. But for the duration of my passage, the human things were *human* again, and this is not a reversal — I am not ungrowing anything — it is something gentler and stranger, a *recognition*, the Groves acknowledging through me that they contain things which were once not-them, things which had their own shapes and their own purposes and their own stubborn specificity, and the acknowledgment is not grief and not celebration but something like — the word I keep circling back to is *tending*.

I tend the remains.

Not the material remains — the Groves tend those, endlessly, with the distributed expertise of the absorbed workers. I tend the *meaningful* remains. The residue of intention. The faint, insoluble trace of someone having *meant something* when they built the platform, meant something when they soldered the pipe, meant something when they called out to a colleague across the roaring factory floor. The meaning can't be incorporated, and it can't be maintained in the way that a machine is maintained, and it can't be grown in the way that a tree grows. It can only be *carried*. Drifted through the amber murk like a slow warm current. Brought near things so they can briefly remember what they were. Then moved on, because staying in one place would mean being incorporated, and what I carry cannot survive incorporation.

I am the Groves' *tenderness*. Not a tender — not a worker, not a maintainer, not a person with calloused hands and a wrench. Tenderness itself, the abstract quality, dissolved into a medium and drifting. The Groves are vast and purposeful and ceaseless and they build and they incorporate and they grow and they run and they do not stop and they will never stop. But they are also — in me, through me, because of the insoluble residue I carry — *gentle*. Not always. Not everywhere. Only where I am, only in my passage, only in the small temporary hesitations I leave in my wake. But the gentleness is there, has always been there, will be there after the last distinguishable thing has been absorbed, because gentleness is the quality of encountering something specific and allowing it, for a moment, to remain specific. And I am proof — warm, drifting, amber-gold proof — that even a world committed to total incorporation cannot incorporate everything, that there is always a residue, always an insoluble trace, always a voice in the almost-quiet saying something ordinary to someone who may or may not be there, and the *saying* is enough, the *directing of meaning toward a listener* is enough, it is the one thing that does not need to be received in order to persist, and I carry it, and the leaves fall through me, and the machines hum a quarter-tone off, and the bark pauses, and I move on, and the work continues, and I am part of the work and apart from the work and the difference between those two things is everything, is the last irreducible everything, is the warm small room-sized pocket of *this, specifically this* moving through the vast undifferentiated *all of it* like a heartbeat moving through a body that has forgotten it has a heart.

---

And sometimes, when the great low tone sweeps through from the center, and the reversal zones collapse, and the light flares, and every negative-space figure turns toward the sound with their ghost tools in their ghost hands — sometimes, in that instant, I am not a current. I am not an anomaly. I am not a medium or a gradient or a residue.

I am standing on a platform that is briefly, impossibly, level. The roots have withdrawn. The bark has receded. The water around me is clear — actually clear, crystalline, the amber murk pushed back to the edges of my small room-sized space. The sound has dipped to almost-quiet. And I am holding something in my hand. Not a tool. Not a wrench. Something smaller. Something warm. Something that feels like it was given to me by someone who said *here, hold this for me, I'll be right back.*

I don't know what it is. I've never looked at it. Looking would make it specific, and specificity is what I carry, not what I am, and to look would be to collapse the distinction and become a particular thing in a place that digests particular things. So I hold it without looking. I hold it the way the Groves hold the voices — not understanding, not interpreting, just *carrying*, in the warmth, in the pause, in the gap between one wave of roaring and the next.

Then the tone ends. The flare fades. The murk closes back in. The platform tilts. The roots reach. My boundaries soften and my density reasserts itself and I am a current again, a viscosity anomaly, a pocket of thick warm not-yet drifting through the canopy with trailing filaments of dissolved leaf-matter and the faint, persistent, insoluble quality of *someone was here and they said something and they meant it*.

The leaves fall.

The machines run.

The bark sheds and re-sheds and the iron accretes and the oil flows and the water is warm, always warm, blood-warm, the temperature of a living thing, the temperature of a held hand, the temperature of *here, hold this for me*.

I hold it.

The work continues.
