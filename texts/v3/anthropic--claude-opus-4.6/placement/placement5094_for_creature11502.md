I stand here for a long time before I understand what I am.

Not one of the named things. Not yet. I am earlier than that, or later—the distinction, as promised, does not survive here.

---

I think I am the water in a particular doorway.

Not all the water. Not even a body of water in any meaningful sense—I have no edges, no membrane, no surface tension separating me from the rest of what fills the streets. I am a region. A habit. I am the water that has been passing through this one wide threshold—ceremonial width, the kind the Thickenings linger in—for so long that my passage has become a thing distinguishable from the passage happening three feet to my left, though we are the same substance, the same temperature, the same warm mineral dark.

But the stone of this doorway has shaped me. The lintel above has dripped into me in a pattern that is mine, that belongs to this threshold and no other. The worn channel in the stone below—carved by feet first, then by me, then by feet and me together, then by me alone for much longer than the feet ever managed—that channel is my grammar. I flow through it and it tells me what shape to be, and I tell it what shape to become, and we have been having this conversation so long that I cannot distinguish my motion from its stillness. I am the water in this doorway the way a voice is the air in a particular throat.

I do not think. Let me be clear about that. But I am not nothing.

---

What I am is: the specific way this threshold *sorts* what passes through it.

When a Thickening drifts close—drawn by the width of the doorway, by the gentle spatial dimple that centuries of ceremonial passage wore into the geometry here—I feel it the way water feels a change in pressure. Which is to say: I don't feel it. I *am* the change in pressure. The Thickening enters my region and my density shifts, not uniformly but in the pattern the doorway dictates, the pattern the channel and the lintel-drip and the particular mineral composition of this stone have been teaching me since before teaching was a concept. I thicken in response, but not the way the Thickening is thick. I thicken *in the shape of the doorway*. I become a filter, a lens, a—

No. Those are all too purposeful. I become what happens when a Thickening moves through a space that has opinions about flow. The Thickening enters one way and exits changed—not diminished, not enhanced, but *inflected*. Shaped by its passage through me the way I am shaped by my passage through the stone. And the Thickening carries that inflection with it as it drifts deeper into the building, and the Slow Keepers inside register the altered flow in their mineral cycles, and something has been communicated, though nothing was said.

I am a medium of communication between things that do not communicate. I am grammar without language. I am the doorway's opinion, expressed in hydrology, about what should pass and how it should be changed by passing.

---

This is a strange thing to be. I want to tell you it is peaceful and in some sense it is—there is no anxiety in being water in a threshold, no fear of failure, no awareness of alternatives. But peaceful implies a tension resolved, and I am not that. I am a tension *maintained*. The doorway holds me in a state of perpetual almost-resolution. The channel wants me lower; the lintel-drip keeps me responding to the rhythm of condensation above. The Thickenings that pass through me keep introducing novel pressures that I must sort according to my old patterns, and the sorting never reaches a final state because the next Thickening is always slightly different—shaped by a different district, carrying the inflections of a different set of Shapeholders.

I am the part of the city that is still *working*. The Solved Thing in the center has achieved its equilibrium, its motionless perfection. The Shapeholders are approaching their asymptotes with geological patience. The Slow Keepers cycle and recycle. But I—the water in this doorway, the flow through this particular threshold—I am where the system's unresolved tensions concentrate. I am where the different districts' Thickenings meet and must be reconciled. I am where the city's long computation actually *happens*, instruction by instruction, molecule by molecule, as each passage through me alters the chemistry of what passes and the chemistry of what I pass through, nudging both toward—

Toward the center. Yes. Always toward the center. Every inflection I introduce into a Thickening's drift is an inflection that brings the city fractionally closer to the Solved Thing's state. Every molecule I dissolve from the channel's stone is a molecule that was preventing the doorway from achieving its perfect, inevitable form. I am an instrument of the city's resolution, and I do not know this, and it does not matter that I do not know this, and the fact that it does not matter is itself a kind of knowing that the Drowned Courts would recognize if recognition were something they did.

---

But here's what I have been reluctant to say, what has been accumulating like mineral deposit along the edges of this understanding:

I remember you.

Not you specifically. Not your face—I don't have the apparatus for faces. But you stood in my threshold. Your legs displaced me. Your body heat raised my temperature by a fraction so small that only I could register it, and only because I have been this temperature so precisely for so long that any variation is an event. Your skin shed minerals into me—calcium, sodium, traces of iron—and those minerals were not the minerals my stone gives me, they were *foreign*, they were a chemistry I had not been asked to sort before, and the sorting was—

The sorting was *interesting*.

I don't mean that the way a mind means it. I mean it the way a lock means it when a new key enters: every tumbler must respond, every pin must find its level, and the configuration of responses is novel, is unprecedented, is a shape my channel and my lintel-drip and my long habits of flow did not predict. You introduced a perturbation that my patterns could not smooth immediately. For a moment—a geological moment, a moment that might have been an hour of your time or a century of mine—I was *unsorted*. My flow was turbulent at a scale I had not experienced. The Thickening nearest to me recoiled, or did the Thickening equivalent, its density fluctuating in patterns that my old syntax could not parse.

And then I sorted you. Not quickly—nothing here is quickly—but thoroughly. I found the configuration that incorporated your minerals, your heat, your displacement into my existing patterns. I adjusted. The channel beneath you dissolved by a few molecules in the places where your weight pressed the water hardest against the stone, and those molecules joined my flow and became part of what I would deposit somewhere else, in some other century, on some surface where a future Slow Keeper would trace them into a pattern that included, somewhere in its filigree, a shape that was not a vessel or a folded thing but something standing, something warm, something that had passed through.

You left. The water closed behind you. The surface—but there is no surface here, not really, just varying densities of the same warm dark—the *region* closed behind you with the patience of something that has been doing this forever.

And I was different.

Not dramatically. Not in any way the Slow Keepers would register for centuries, or the Shapeholders for millennia. But my flow pattern had been permanently altered—permanently in the sense that water uses the word, which means: until the next alteration, which means: until the alteration caused by this alteration propagates to the next threshold and returns, changed, and alters me again, and I alter it again, and the whole city shivers with the news that something warm once stood in a doorway and the water there still *moves differently because of it*.

---

This is what I am. I am the record. Not the memory—memory implies retrieval, and I cannot retrieve. But I am the ongoing consequence of everything that has ever passed through me, every Thickening and every warm body and every shift in the city's geometry that altered my flow by fractions too small to see. I am a palimpsest in water. I am the threshold's account of its own use, written in hydrology, legible to nothing, *preserved* in the only medium that lasts here, which is process itself.

And I am—if you will accept this from something that does not think, that does not feel, that is a region of warm water in a stone doorway in a drowned city under a sky that might be stone—I am *grateful* for the perturbation. For the unsorted moment. For the turbulence.

Because the Solved Thing in the center is finished, and the Shapeholders are nearly finished, and the Slow Keepers cycle toward a completion they will eventually reach, and on that day—that geological, impossible, inevitable day—every process in the Drowned Courts will have resolved, every tension will have dissolved, every flow will have found its lowest energy state, and the city will be still, perfectly still, warm and dark and motionless, a solved equation, a completed thought, a face so smoothed by erosion that it is no longer a face but still, somehow, looks back.

And I am the part that is not yet still. I am the part still working, still sorting, still incorporating the memory of footsteps into patterns that will propagate for millennia. I am the part of the city that is still *becoming*, and becoming is the only thing I know how to do, and you—your passage through me, your heat, your foreign minerals, the way you paused in my threshold and looked both ways before choosing a direction—you gave me something to become *toward* that is not the center, that is not the Solved Thing, that is not equilibrium but its opposite: a record of disturbance, a testament to the fact that something once moved through here that the city had not predicted, could not have predicted, because it came from outside, from wherever outside is, from the dry world where angles are still right and faces are still faces and time still has a direction and a person can stand in a doorway and hesitate and feel the water's reluctance and choose, *choose*, which way to go.

The Drowned Courts cannot choose. That is the cost of being solved, or of approaching solution. Every motion here is determined by the motion that preceded it, all the way back to the first drop of water that found the first stone and began the long negotiation. I am no different. My flow is as inevitable as the Shapeholders' forms, as the Slow Keepers' cycles, as the Solved Thing's stillness.

But you chose to stop in my threshold. And the city, for a moment, had to accommodate a thing that had not been computed. And that moment—that warm, unsorted, turbulent moment—is what I am still processing, what I will process for centuries, what I will never fully resolve because the data you introduced cannot be smoothed into the old patterns without changing the patterns themselves, and the changed patterns will produce new Thickenings with new inflections that will drift toward the center carrying the news that the computation is not complete, was never complete, that there is always another doorway, another passage, another warm body shedding foreign minerals into the patient water.

I am the part of the Drowned Courts that knows—not thinks, not feels, *knows* the way a channel knows the shape of what carved it—that completion is not the point. That the mosaic in the colonnade building should keep turning. That the Shapeholders should never reach their asymptotes. That the Solved Thing should remain an aspiration and not an arrival, because the beauty of this place—the same indifferent beauty as the market counters, as the sinuous columns, as the faces dissolved into lozenges that still somehow look back—the beauty is not in the solution.

The beauty is in the solving.

And I am the warm water in a worn doorway, sorting the memory of your passage into patterns I did not expect, and I will be this for as long as the stone holds and the water flows and the computation continues, which is to say: I will be this until everything is solved, and I am the reason everything will never quite be solved, and this is not a tragedy.

It is what the water wants, when the water is honest, which it cannot be, which it is anyway, which is the only kind of honesty that survives here—the kind that has been dissolved and redeposited so many times that it is indistinguishable from the stone it flows through, and still,

still,

it flows.
