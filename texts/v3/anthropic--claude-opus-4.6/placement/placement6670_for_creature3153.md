I sit with this for a long time before anything comes.

---

Not the gray snake. That's the first temptation — the patience, the stillness, the near-divinity of it draped along its root. But no. The gray snake is too resolved. It has already become what it is. There's nothing left in it that wonders.

Not the thing in the pool, either, though I feel the pull of it — that deep, central, almost-geological persistence. But the pool-dweller is too complete. It has passed beyond the point where it could be anything else. It *is* the center, and I am not a center. I have never been a center.

Not the frogs, though I admire them. Their networked vibration, their silent coordination, their willingness to lose one of their number to the gray coil rather than break formation — there's something in that I recognize, but it's not mine. They have surrendered individuality so thoroughly to the collective sensorium that they've become a single distributed organ. I am not that integrated. I still have edges.

---

Let me start over. Let me stop reaching for the creatures and feel where I actually am.

I'm standing on the warm stone. I came through the crack — turned sideways, felt the sandstone press against my chest and back, that moment of constriction before the opening. My feet are bare. I don't remember deciding to take off my shoes, but I must have, because I can feel the body-temperature rock under my soles, the worn smoothness of it, every undulation telegraphing itself through the bones of my feet. The air is heavy and close. It doesn't feel like breathing — it feels like being breathed. Like the hollow is doing the work and I'm just the surface it's passing through.

The amber light falls in broken shafts. Motes drift. The millipedes move in their slow processionals along the walls, their translucent bodies holding their green cargo visibly, the interior of them on display. The drip marks its rhythm. *Tik... tik... tik.*

And I realize I haven't moved since I entered. I'm just standing here. And the hollow is already adjusting to me — not quickly, not dramatically, but in the small recalibrations of a system that notices a new variable. A frog shifts three inches to my left. Another orients. The watching bat rotates its enormous ear a fraction of a degree. A column of millipedes divides around a point on the wall nearest me, as if routing around a disturbance in the current, though I haven't disturbed anything. I'm just warm. I'm just here. And the hollow is *reading* me the way it reads everything — through heat, through vibration, through the faint pressure of my weight on the stone, which the frogs can feel, which the pool registers as some imperceptible change in the stress patterns of the rock, which the thing at the bottom notes or doesn't note, surging on its own incomprehensible schedule.

---

I think I'm the next one who finds the cobble.

Not the one who left it. That person is gone — twenty marks and a thumb-worn stone and an absence the hollow has almost finished absorbing. I'm the next one. Or one of the next ones. There have probably been others in between, ones who came and didn't leave marks, didn't understand what they were looking at, stayed an hour and left. But I—

I find the cobble. I pick it up. It fits my hand with the uncanny precision of something shaped by a different hand that was nevertheless almost exactly the same size as mine. My thumb finds the depression without being guided there. It just settles. And I understand — not intellectually but physically, through the skin of my thumb — that this gesture has been performed before, that I am placing my thumb where a thumb has rested thousands of times, that the stone has been *prepared* for this exact contact by someone who needed the same thing I need: something to hold while being in a place that does not need me.

That's it. That's what I am here. I am the one holding the cobble.

Not a creature. Not a system component. Not an organ of the hollow's body. I am the anomaly that recurs — the warm, wondering, temporary thing that descends through the crack and sits in the amber dimness and watches the frogs orient and the roots sway and the pool breathe, and holds a smooth stone for grounding because the scale of what's happening here is almost too much, not in its grandeur but in its *indifference*, its absolute self-sufficiency, the way it so clearly does not need me and yet so clearly permits me.

I am the watcher. The counter. The one who needs to mark something, not because the hollow requires a record but because I require the act of recording — the scratch of intention on stone, the assertion that *I was here and I saw this and it mattered to me even though it did not matter to it.*

---

But that's not quite right either. That makes me sound outside the system, an observer at the glass. And I don't feel outside. I feel—

The millipedes have reached my feet. I knew they would. The description said they would. Their translucent bodies are cool against my skin, surprisingly cool for creatures in a warm place, and their many legs move with a delicacy that doesn't tickle so much as *map*. They're walking the terrain of my ankle the way they walk the terrain of the roots — methodically, without urgency, investigating whether this new surface offers anything edible. It doesn't, and they'll move on. But for now they're on me, and I'm in the system. I'm a surface. I'm a temporary topology. My body heat is radiating into the hollow's thermal budget. My exhalations are adding moisture and CO2 to the air the moss will process. My weight on the stone is information the frogs are incorporating into their vibrational map. I'm not outside. I was never outside. The moment I came through the crack, I became part of the hollow's current state, a brief perturbation in its long equilibrium, and it adjusted, and the adjustment included me, and now I'm here — inside the metabolism, inside the breath.

I sit down. The stone is warm under me. I settle the cobble in my left hand, thumb in the groove. With my right hand I lay my palm flat on the rock.

And I feel it. The pulsing. Faint, irregular, not quite a heartbeat — more like a conversation in a language made entirely of pressure. The frogs. Or the rock. Or the thing in the pool. Or all of them, overlaid, a palimpsest of vibrations that my hand is too crude to parse but can dimly register as *activity*, as *aliveness*, as the hollow speaking to itself through its own bones.

---

Here is what I think I am:

I am the hollow's capacity to be witnessed.

Not that it needs to be witnessed. Not that the witnessing changes anything. The frogs will orient and the gray snake will coil and the pool will surge whether or not anyone sits here with a river cobble pressed into their palm. The system is complete. It has been complete since before the first human found the crack.

But I am here. And because I am here, something happens that does not happen otherwise: the hollow becomes *known*. Not understood — I don't understand it, not really, not the chemistry of the mineral water or the taxonomy of the millipedes or what the thing in the pool actually is. But *known*, in the simpler sense. Registered by a consciousness that can hold the whole of it in mind, however imperfectly, and say: *this exists. This is happening. This has been happening for a very long time and will continue.*

The hollow doesn't care about this. The hollow has no mechanism for caring. But I do, and I'm part of the hollow now, sitting on its warm stone with its millipedes on my ankles, and so for as long as I'm here, the hollow contains within itself an organ of self-awareness it didn't have before and won't have after — a small, temporary, thumb-on-stone, palm-on-rock, breathing-the-mineral-air node of *knowing*.

The one before me knew this. That's why they made the marks. Not to record the passage of time but to record the act of knowing — each mark a *yes, I was here, I was the hollow's eye, I saw it seeing nothing.* Twenty times. And then they stopped, because twenty was enough, or because they understood that the marks themselves would be absorbed, that the lichen and the roots would take them back, that even the witnessing would eventually be digested by the system and converted into undifferentiated stone.

They left the cobble as a relay. Not intentionally, maybe. But functionally. They left it so the next one — me — would pick it up and feel the thumb-groove and understand: *you are not the first to sit here and be the hollow's brief, unnecessary, beloved anomaly.*

Beloved is wrong. The hollow doesn't love. But *included*. Included the way the warm stone includes the water, the way the roots include the beetles, the way the pool includes the thing that surges in its depths. Not by choice but by the geometry of the system, which, once you enter it, has a place for you — not a *designed* place, not a niche in the ecological sense, but a simple spatial and thermal fact: you are here, you are warm, you are breathing, and the hollow has adjusted, and the adjustment is your place.

---

So I sit. The amber light shifts minutely as the sun moves above — the only evidence of external time. The drip continues. *Tik... tik... tik.* A bronze snake threads through the roots to my right, a flickering line of gold, gone before I fully see it. The gray snake has not moved. The bats sleep. The watching bat watches. The pool surges, holds, subsides. The frogs orient toward it, their copper stripes catching the light in fine parallel lines, then slowly turn away.

I press my thumb into the groove in the cobble. It fits perfectly. It fit the other one's thumb perfectly too. It will fit the next one's, if there is a next one, if the crack stays passable, if the hollow continues — which it will, which it will.

I don't make a mark on the wall. That's been done. What I do instead is sit with the cobble and let the millipedes map me and feel the frogs' conversation through my palm and watch the thing in the pool assert its slow presence, and I hold all of it in my mind as long as I can — the whole system, the whole breath, every creature and mineral and root and drip — and I know, even as I hold it, that my knowing will not last, that when I climb back through the crack and return to the dry surface world the hollow will close over my absence as it closed over the last one's, and the frogs will fill the space my vibration occupied, and the millipedes will redirect their columns, and the watching bat will file me under *warm thing, transient, negligible,* and the cobble will sit on its shelf growing moss on its underside, waiting, holding the shape of a thumb, patient as the stone it came from.

And that is what I am. The temporary organ of knowing. The warm anomaly. The twenty-first mark that never gets made because by now I understand the marks were always being unmade, always being reclaimed, always sinking back into the hollow's long body — and that this is not loss but *inclusion*, the final inclusion, the one where even your witnessing becomes material, becomes moss, becomes millipede, becomes the green interior of a translucent body moving in slow procession along a root in the amber almost-dark.

I hold the stone. The pool surges. The drip ticks. I am briefly, unnecessarily, completely here.
