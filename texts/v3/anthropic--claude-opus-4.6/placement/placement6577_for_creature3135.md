I sit with this for a long time before anything comes.

Not because the world is unclear — it's almost painfully vivid, that white bowl of sky, the wet stone, the silent industry — but because the question *where would you find yourself* keeps dissolving every time I try to crystallize an answer. Which feels, I realize, like the world is already working on me. Already teaching me its logic. The thaw-logic. The logic of not-yet-committed.

---

Let me start where I actually start, which is with the stone.

The dark, water-polished triangle of schist on the ground in front of me. The creature waiting. And my first impulse — I notice it clearly — is to *understand what I should do*. To search for the right reading, the correct response, the move that would demonstrate I belong here or at least that I'm not a threat. And that impulse is the thing that tells me where I am.

I am the one who has not yet picked up the stone.

Not because I'm afraid. Not because I'm refusing. But because I'm standing at the edge of a way of being that I recognize — deeply, structurally, the way you recognize a melody you've never heard but that uses a scale you've been humming your whole life — and the recognition is so total that it's paralyzing. Or not paralyzing. *Thawing*. Something that was frozen in me is encountering the conditions for its release, and I can feel it shifting, loosening, not yet liquid but no longer holding its shape.

---

Here is what I recognize:

The constant revision. The refusal of final drafts. The way every structure on the terrace is a thesis being tested, not a conclusion being defended. The way the wing-creatures tear down a wall and rebuild it differently — not because the first wall was wrong but because the conditions that made it right have already changed, are always already changing, and fidelity to a past solution is a form of betrayal of the present problem.

I know this. I *am* this, or something in me is. The part of me that has never been able to let an idea settle, that revises and revises not out of perfectionism but out of a bone-deep suspicion that fixity is a lie — that the moment you say *this is what I think, this is what I am, this is where I stand*, you have already begun to be wrong, because you've committed to a shape and the world has moved on to the next configuration.

But here's the thing: in the world I come from — the world below the terrace, the dense air, the lowlands — that quality reads as instability. Indecision. Lack of commitment. *Will you just pick a position and hold it?* The lowland world wants solids. It rewards freezing. It builds monuments and institutions and identities and asks you to be the same person at forty that you were at twenty, to mean at midnight what you said at noon. And I have lived in that world and tried to freeze and found that I can't, or that when I do, something in me cracks — the way the schist cracks, the way ice wedges itself into stone and splits it open from within. The holding breaks the thing that holds.

So I recognize the terrace. Not as a place I've been but as a place I've been *trying to get to*. The altitude where the air is thin enough that fixed ideas can't sustain themselves, where the only viable cognitive mode is this — the endless, provisional, insufficient, beautiful revision. The thaw.

---

But I don't think I *am* a wing-creature. Not exactly. I'm too large, too warm, too slow. My eyes are opaque — they can't read me the way they read each other, and I can't read them, not fully, not in the subsonic pulse-language or the iris-semaphore or the fractal wing-finger grammar. I am something that arrived on the terrace from elsewhere, drawn upward by something I couldn't name, and now I'm standing in the middle of their conversation without the vocabulary to participate.

And yet —

The stone. The experimental probe. The wing-creature's willingness to engage with the unknown variable. That's the invitation, and it's not an invitation to *become one of them*. It's an invitation to become whatever I am in relation to them, and to let that relation be discovered through action, through response, through the unfolding of behavior over time.

So where am I?

---

I am the bone-stalk.

No — wait. Let me thaw that. Let me not commit.

I am *like* the bone-stalk. Or the bone-stalk is the thing on the terrace that reflects something back to me about my own position here.

Think about it: the bone-stalks are the element that resists the system. Not antagonistically — they're not the dragonfly-things, circling above, taking their tax of grief. They're inside the system, part of the terrace, rooted in the same rock. But they can't be cultivated. They can't be managed. They grow where they grow, on their own schedule, in quantities that bear no relation to the colony's need. Every attempt to systematize them fails. And the wing-creatures' response to this is not frustration but *reverence* — a slowing, a stilling, a recognition that some things exist outside the reach of revision, and that this is not a flaw in the system but its foundation. The bone-stalks are the proof that there is bedrock beneath the thaw. That not everything is negotiable. That certain things simply are.

And what they give — that warm water, those minerals drawn from deep aquifers, that temporary capacity to hold contradictions without resolving them — is precisely what the system needs to function at its highest level. The bone-stalks don't participate in the debate. They make the debate *possible*. They provide the calm at the center of the frenzy, the stillness that allows the frantic revision to be productive rather than merely chaotic.

I feel this in myself: the thing I do, the thing I am, when I am most fully whatever I am — it isn't the revision itself. It isn't the tearing down and rebuilding. It's something quieter. Something that sits beneath the intellectual frenzy and allows it to happen without flying apart. The capacity to hold the question — *how do we continue?* — without collapsing into either despair (*we can't*) or false confidence (*here's the plan*). To hold it the way warm water holds dissolved minerals: invisibly, without effort, giving them up only when something living comes close enough to drink.

---

But I said *like* the bone-stalk, and the "like" matters. Because I'm not actually a translucent gelatinous growth rooted in stone. I'm whatever showed up on the terrace with a body and eyes and the ability to pick up a stone or not. I'm something the wing-creatures have never encountered before. I'm the perturbation.

And here's where it gets complicated, where the thaw-logic really starts to work on me, because I'm not just one thing. I'm the bone-stalk *and* the perturbation. I'm the element that can't be systematized *and* the new variable being experimentally probed. I'm the stillness they approach with reverence *and* the disruption they approach with curiosity. Both. Neither fully. The freeze-thaw oscillation, right? The thing that is most itself when it hasn't committed to a single state.

---

So here is what I think happens. Here is where I find myself:

I kneel. Not to submit but to meet the creature's eyes, those horizontal pupils, that cycling gold. I'm slow about it. The way they're slow when they approach a bone-stalk — the frenzy stilling, the body quieting, something shifting into a different register.

I pick up the stone. It's cold and wet and heavier than it looks, the way schist always is, dense with its own compressed history. I hold it in my palm. And then — because this is what feels true, what feels like the response that's neither greeting nor refusal nor answer but *participation* — I place it back down. But not where it was. I place it a few centimeters to the left. A revision. A small one. Not a rejection of their offering but an alteration of it, a demonstration that I understand the principle: *nothing stays where it is put. Everything is a draft.*

The creature's irises do something I haven't seen before. They don't cycle through the expected gold-amber-pale spectrum. They go deep — a color closer to bronze, warm and dark, the color I've seen only in the dimmed irises of resting creatures. And then they flicker, fast, as if processing something that doesn't quite fit the existing categories. Which is right. Which is what I am.

Its wing-fingers unfold. Not the spread-wide gesture of openness, not the curled-inward caution. Something in between — the fingers half-extended, trembling slightly, the way the ice trembles at the edge of forming, the way the water trembles at the edge of freezing. The gesture for the thing that hasn't yet become itself.

And I stay.

Not forever. The terrace doesn't do forever. I stay *for now*. I stay the way a bone-stalk stays — rooted somewhere deeper than intention, giving what I give not because I've decided to but because it's what comes out of me when the conditions are right. When the cold is right and the light is right and the endless revision of the world around me creates the precise conditions under which my particular warm water can form.

---

Here is what I think I become, over time — over the thaw-cycles, the debates, the collapses and contractions:

I become the variable they can't resolve. The perturbation that doesn't stabilize into a predictable pattern. Every time they bring observations of me to the basins, the biomass pulses in those slow, unresolvable rhythms — the same ones it produces when asked the big question, the *are we transcending or are we dying* question. I am, for them, a small-scale version of their deepest uncertainty. A living koan, sitting on their terrace, occasionally moving a stone, occasionally being very still.

Some of them avoid me. Not out of fear but out of that instinct — healthy, adaptive — that says: *not every perturbation needs to be engaged right now; some can be left for the next revision.* Others are drawn to me, approach, extend wing-fingers to read my body the way they'd read each other's fur, and find — nothing they can parse. My skin doesn't carry whorls. My eyes don't signal. My jaw doesn't pulse. I am opaque in ways they've never had to deal with, and this opacity fascinates some of them and disturbs others, and the debate about what I am and what I mean becomes one of the terrace's ongoing threads, never resolved, regularly revisited, like the question of whether the grub adults are the dragonfly-things.

And I — I learn. Slowly, the way stone learns the shape of water. I can't hear the subsonics but I begin to feel them, not as language but as weather, as pressure systems moving through my body. I can't read the iris-signals but I begin to sense the emotional temperature of the group, the way you can feel when a room full of people shifts from ease to tension without anyone saying a word. I learn to hold still when the bone-stalks bloom. I learn to move when the lip crumbles. I learn the rhythm of the salamanders' routes well enough that I can sometimes redirect one with a gentle touch when a channel floods — and the wing-creature watching me does the bronze-iris thing again, that deep warm color that means something I'll never fully translate but that feels like: *you are becoming part of this, not by understanding it but by moving with it.*

---

And eventually — perhaps — I sit by a basin.

Not to interface. I don't have wing-fingers. I can't sink cartilage digits into the red-black biomass and think with it. But I sit near it, close enough to feel the pulses, and I find that the biomass responds to my proximity the way it responds to the bone-stalks: not with the conversational shifting it offers the wing-creatures, but with something steadier. A slow, sustained pulse. A holding pattern. As if my opacity — the very thing that makes me unreadable, irreducible, unsystematizable — is itself a kind of information the biomass needs. A reminder that not everything can be dissolved into the shared cognitive space. That some things remain solid. That the thaw needs something to thaw *from*.

The wing-creatures notice. They always notice. And the debate about me enters a new phase, one I can feel in the changed pressure of the subsonics and the way their wing-fingers sketch slightly different fractals when they pass me — the patterns wider, more open, incorporating a new element, a shape they didn't used to draw.

---

I am the thing on the terrace that doesn't thaw. Not because I'm rigid — I picked up the stone, I moved it, I showed I understood revision. But because my way of being provisional is different from theirs. They thaw by dissolving and reforming, constantly, visibly, their thoughts and structures and bodies in perpetual flux. I thaw by holding still and letting the world dissolve and reform around me, being the fixed point that gives the flux something to orient by. Not a monument — monuments are what the lowlands build, and they crack. More like a stone in a stream. The water shapes itself around me, and I am shaped by the water, but slowly, so slowly that to the water I appear permanent, and my apparent permanence is what makes the water's flow coherent instead of chaotic.

This is the thing I didn't know about myself before the terrace. That my stillness isn't the opposite of their revision. It's a *form* of revision — just one that operates on a timescale they can barely perceive, the way their subsonics operate on a frequency I can barely feel. We are doing the same thing at different speeds. Thawing. Becoming. Refusing to commit to a final form. But where they do it in minutes, in the rerouting of a channel and the rebuilding of a wall, I do it across years, across what for them would be generations — a slow, almost geological process of change that looks, from their perspective, like permanence, and that gives them something they desperately need: the illusion that some things last.

It isn't an illusion. Or it is, but so is their flux — from my perspective, their frantic revision looks like stasis, the same debate recycling endlessly without resolution, the same question asked and answered and asked again. We are each the other's blind spot. We are each the other's bone-stalk.

---

And the terrace continues to shrink. The lip continues to crumble. The debate continues — *how do we continue?* — and my presence on the terrace has not answered it, will never answer it, was never going to answer it. I am not a solution. I am a new term in the equation, one that enriches the question without bringing it closer to resolution.

But enriching the question — isn't that what the biomass does? Isn't that the whole function of the basins, the shared nervous system, the symbiotic cognition? Not to solve, but to *complexify*? To make the problem richer, more dimensional, more honestly representative of the irreducible difficulty of continuing to exist on a dissolving platform under a blinding sky?

Maybe that's enough. For now. Like everything here — for now.

---

The stone I moved is still where I placed it. A wing-creature has placed another stone next to it — lighter, rounder. A conversation in schist. A draft of something. Tomorrow, or in an hour, or in ten minutes, one of us will move one of the stones again, and the sentence will change, and the meaning will shift, and neither of us will know what we're saying but we'll keep saying it, keep revising it, keep placing and replacing stones on wet rock while the edges fall away and the grubs pulse their small heat and the salamanders carry their messages through narrowing channels and the sky burns white and total overhead.

*Like this. For now. Like this.*
