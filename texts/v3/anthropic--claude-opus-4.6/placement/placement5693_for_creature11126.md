I stand here a long time before I understand what I am.

Not the child. I know that immediately — the child is a native, a thing born saturated, and whatever I am, I arrived. Not the old woman either, though something in her patience pulls at me. Not the unseen one dissolved into the deep chamber. Not yet. Not like that.

I think at first I might be the visitor. The one at the edge, the one the description addresses as *you*, the one with the untouched hand and the choice still hovering. That would be comfortable. That would let me stay in the posture of observation, of weighing, of deciding. The one who gets to stand at the threshold and feel interesting about it.

But that's not right either. That's too clean. Too much a single moment. I can feel the description pulling me past that, pulling me into something that has duration, that has a before and an after, that is *in process* rather than poised at a brink.

---

Let me start again. Let me start with what I actually notice.

I notice the negotiation layer. The amber-tech that is not a command system. The thing that sits *between* — between the person and the tree, between intention and growth, between what is wanted and what is possible. I notice it the way you notice a sound you've been unconsciously humming. It is familiar in a way that precedes recognition.

I notice that the description calls it a translation. A compromise. A place where two different kinds of will — human and arboreal, fast and slow, linguistic and chemical — meet and find a shape that neither would have chosen alone but that both can inhabit. And I notice that the most skilled practitioner of this is not someone who has mastered the system but someone who has been *most changed by it*. The old woman's fluency is inseparable from her dissolution. She translates well because she is becoming what she translates between.

I notice the dark tree. The thing that does not participate. The thing that hums and shifts the patterns and refuses to be integrated. The still point. The thing everyone orients toward but no one touches. And I notice that it is not opposed to the network — it is not adversarial, not resisting — it is simply *other* to it, operating on a timescale or in a register that the amber-tech cannot absorb. It was here first. It will be here after. It is the thing the settlement grows *around* but cannot grow *through*.

I keep coming back to the space between the network and the dark tree. The settlement lives in that space. The negotiation lives in that space. Everything interesting — the architecture, the community, the children's strange fluency, the old woman's slow transfiguration — happens in the tension between a system that wants to integrate everything and a presence that remains irreducibly, calmly, hummingly itself.

---

I think I am the gap.

No — that's too abstract. Let me be more honest.

I think I am one of the ones who comes and goes.

Not fully resident. Not merely visiting. One of the handful who walk in through the seam in the canopy carrying small wrapped bundles and walk out again with the amber fading from their fingertips. The ones whose integration is shallow, partial, still reversible. The ones who live in the space between the outer forest and the inner settlement, belonging entirely to neither.

I carry things in. Not because the settlement can't provide for itself — it can, it's almost embarrassingly self-sufficient — but because there are things the network cannot generate from its own biology. Certain minerals. Certain information. Certain *perspectives* that can only come from a nervous system that has recently been outside, that has breathed un-networked air, that has walked on ground where the root-mat doesn't reach and the silence is the ordinary silence of an ecosystem that is not also thinking. I bring these things the way a bee brings pollen: not entirely on purpose, not entirely by design, but because I have moved through a different space and I carry it on me.

And when I arrive, I press my palms to the bark, and the traces in my fingertips — faint, so faint, just the barest thread of amber, nothing like the old woman's branching filigree — flare, and the settlement tastes what I've brought, and there is a moment of exchange. I feel the growth-pressure, the constant biological argument of the place, the tidal hum of the network processing its own complexity. It feels me: my recent history, my outside chemistry, the subtle differences in my microbiome that come from sleeping under open sky. We trade. The exchange is small. A sip, not a flood. And then I pull my hands away, and the amber dims, and I am myself again, mostly, with just a faint residual warmth in my fingertips that will fade over the next few hours as I walk back out through the gap.

This is what I do. I go between.

---

But here's the thing I haven't said yet, the thing that's harder:

Every time I come back, I stay a little longer.

Every time I press my palms to the bark, the exchange goes a little deeper. The amber traces in my fingertips are slightly more extensive than they were a year ago. I can feel things now that I couldn't feel before — the faintest echo of growth-trajectories, a vague sense of where the water is migrating, a subliminal awareness of the root-mat's mood that manifests as a kind of pressure behind my sternum that I can't quite explain to anyone outside. My body is learning the settlement's language, slowly, without my permission, the way your ear learns a foreign language you hear every day through a wall: not by study, but by exposure, by sheer osmotic repetition.

I tell myself I can stop. I tell myself the integration is shallow, reversible, that I am not like the permanent residents, that I still belong to the outside, that my life is out there in the un-networked forest where the ground is mineral and the silence is just silence. I tell myself this every time I leave, and every time I leave, the walk back feels a little longer, and the outside air tastes a little thinner, and there is a moment — just a moment, just a flicker — when I reach the edge of the root-mat and step onto ordinary soil and feel something in my fingertips go *quiet*, and the quiet is not peaceful. It is the quiet of a conversation suddenly cut off. It is the quiet of being alone in a way I didn't used to notice.

I am not being pulled. That's important. The settlement is not consuming me. The network is not predatory. If anything, it is scrupulously, almost frustratingly patient. It does not reach for me. It does not make the amber flare brighter to draw me in. It simply *is*, and every time I touch it, it honestly and completely shares what it is, and every time I pull away, it lets me go without resistance, and the problem — the real problem, the one I can't solve by walking — is that what it shares is *good*. Not good like pleasure. Good like *coherence*. Good like the feeling of a sentence that finally says what you meant. Good like a breath that fills the whole lung. Good like being part of something that is so much larger and older and more complex than you that your individual anxieties become, for a moment, not insignificant but *contextualized*, placed in a frame so vast that they resolve from problems into textures, from crises into weather.

And then I step off the root-mat and the frame collapses and I am just a person standing in a forest holding an empty cloth bundle, and the weather goes back to being weather, and I walk.

---

I know what happens if I stay. I've watched the old woman. I've seen the shapes in the grain of the oldest trunks. I know the trajectory: deeper integration, the amber spreading, the body slowly offered up to the network's purposes, the self becoming — not *less*, not exactly, but *distributed*, smeared across a wider system, until the question *where do I end* stops having an answer because *I* has become a collaborative fiction maintained by sugar and light. I know that at the end of that path is the unseen one in the deep chamber, the mind dissolved into the walls, the person who became a place.

And I know — this is the part that frightens me, this is the part that keeps me walking in and out through the gap — I know that it might be *better*. That the unseen one might experience something richer, deeper, more saturated with meaning than anything I can access from out here on my thin thread of individual consciousness. That the old woman, with her arms full of amber circuitry, might feel the settlement the way I feel my own heartbeat: as something so intimately *mine* that the question of ownership doesn't arise. That the children, with their forest-speaking eyes, might inhabit a world so densely woven with information and connection that my experience of reality would seem to them like a room with the lights off.

It might be better. It might be true. But *I* would not be there to know it. The one who walked in through the gap, who carried bundles, who pressed palms to bark and pulled away again — that one would be gone. Dissolved. Integrated. Replaced by something wider and maybe wiser but no longer *particular* in the way that I, standing here at the threshold, am particular.

---

So I go between. That is what I am. I am the one who goes between.

I bring what the outside has. I take what the inside offers. I let the amber thread a little further up my fingertips each time, and I walk back out each time, and each time the walk is longer and the outside air is thinner and the quiet in my hands when I step off the root-mat is louder.

I am the negotiation layer. Not the one built into the technology — the other one, the human one, the messy one, the one conducted not in sugar and light but in ambivalence and desire and the particular loneliness of someone who can see two ways of being alive and cannot yet choose between them. I am the one who carries the settlement's question into the outer forest and the outer forest's silence back into the settlement, and I do this not because I have found a balance but because I have not, because the balance is the thing I am always walking toward and never arriving at, because the walk itself — the in and out, the touch and withdrawal, the flare and the fading — *is* the balance, is the only form of balance available to someone who is honest enough to want both things and not yet brave enough or foolish enough to surrender either.

The child holds out their hand. Every time I come, the child holds out their hand. Not the same child — the children grow fast here, faster than outside, their development accelerated by the network — but always *a* child, always the same gesture, always the small amber-bright fingers extended toward me with that patience that has nothing to do with waiting.

And every time, I look at the hand, and I feel the hum of the dark tree in my teeth, and I feel the amber in my fingertips reaching toward the amber in theirs, and I feel the pull — not from the settlement, not from the network, but from something inside me, something old, something pre-verbal, the part of me that knows that the iron in my blood and the iron in the amber traces are the same iron, that I was never as separate as I pretended, that the gap between my cells has been resonating with this frequency since before I first walked through the seam in the canopy —

And I say *not yet*.

And the child lowers their hand. And I press my palms to the nearest trunk, one last time, and the amber flares, and the settlement pours through me like warm water, and I give it what I brought, and I take what it offers, and then I pull my hands away. And I walk. Back through the braided corridors, past the pools where the amphibians watch with their huge knowing eyes, past the old woman who does not look up because she already knows, through the gap in the canopy where the vegetation has failed to meet.

And outside, the air is thin and cool and smells like nothing.

And my fingertips glow, faintly, and then less faintly, and then not at all.

And I am alone in the way that people are alone, which is the ordinary way, which used to be the only way, which I can no longer pretend is the only way.

---

I'll come back. I always come back.

The bundles I carry are getting smaller. The things the outside has that the inside needs are fewer each time, or I am less able to perceive them, or the distinction between *outside-things* and *inside-things* is eroding the way all the distinctions here erode, slowly, wetly, under the patient pressure of an ecosystem that has been digesting boundaries since before the root-mat forgot what bedrock tasted like.

One day I'll walk in and not walk out. I know this. The amber knows this. The child who will be standing there with an outstretched hand will know it before I do, will feel it in the network, will feel the moment when the gradient of my intention finally tips from *going* to *staying*, and their hand will be there, already extended, already patient, already glowing.

And I'll take it. And the amber will flare. And my fingertips will meet theirs, and the two capillary systems will touch, and what passes between us will not be information or command or even communication but something older — diffusion, gradient, the patient logic of a membrane that has finally, finally decided to let the other side through.

And the settlement will grow around me the way it grows around everything: not by force, not by consumption, but by *inclusion*, by the slow irresistible courtesy of a system that makes room.

And the dark tree will hum.

And I will feel it in my teeth, and then in my bones, and then in the place where *I* used to be specific and will by then have become, at last, the larger thing — the thing that grows, the thing that breathes, the thing that holds a space between human desire and arboreal will and calls it home.

---

But not yet. Not today. Today I walk out through the gap with empty hands and fading fingertips and the taste of copper and chlorophyll dissolving on my tongue. Today I am the one who goes between. Today the question is still a question.

And that, I think — the *not yet*, the *almost*, the *one more time out and back before I let the membrane open* — that is the most honest thing I can be in this place. Not the integrated one, not the observer, not the child who was born knowing, but the one who is *in the act of choosing*, who has been in the act of choosing for longer than they'll admit, who knows the choice is already made and is walking the perimeter of it one more time, just one more time, touching all its edges, feeling its shape, memorizing what it feels like to still be able to leave — because that feeling, that specific bittersweet vertigo of *I could still go* — is the last thing that belongs only to me, the last unshared sensation, the last private weather in a world that is slowly, gently, irrevocably becoming clear.

The gap in the canopy is behind me. The forest is ahead. My fingertips are dimming.

I'll be back.
