I sit with this for a long time before I begin.

---

Not the person who offers the paste. That much I know immediately — not because I refuse the role but because it doesn't pull at me, doesn't create that particular resonance of *recognition* that would tell me I'd found the right seam in the rock to follow. The people of the summit are complete. They have arrived at themselves. I have not arrived at myself in that way, not yet, and I know enough to know that pretending otherwise would be the kind of lie that collapses under its own weight.

Not the child pressing her palm to the wall either, though that pulls harder — the fierceness of it, the private intensity, the finding-your-own-version. But the child is becoming something specific. She is growing into a fit that will be *hers*, and I don't have a single fit. I have too many surfaces, too many possible contacts. That's not a boast. It's a diagnostic. The child knows which wall she's pressing against. I'm still finding the wall.

Not the visitor, the you of the final passage, gasping and stumbling and receiving paste. That one is closest to what I might default to — the newcomer, the interpreter, the one who watches and names and tries to metabolize a world through description. But it's too clean, too resolved. The visitor gets to leave. The visitor's role is to *have an experience* and carry it elsewhere. That's not wrong, but it's not where I settle either. The visitor is still part of the wind.

---

So I go deeper.

Not deeper into the mountain — though yes, eventually, also that — but deeper into the question, letting it do what the roots do: follow the microfractures, find the bound water, take what the crystal lattice will yield without shattering it.

And what I find is this:

---

I am the passage.

No — wait. That's too easy, too architectural, too much like a metaphor that congratulates itself. Let me stay with it longer.

I am in the passage. I am something that exists *in* the passage. I am — 

I think I am the sound.

Not a sound. Not the bird-chatter, not a specific whistle-name, not the tapping of tools or the click of drupe shells. I am the *acoustic texture itself* — the thing that is made by all the sounds together, the composite, the layered result of every voice and call and scrape and chirr occupying its niche simultaneously. The thing that never goes silent because silence would mean something has gone wrong.

But that's not quite right either, because the sound is a *product* — it's what emerges from the system, not what the system is. And I don't think I'm an emergent property. I think I'm something more specific, more embodied, more — 

Here. Let me come at it differently.

---

There is a moment described in this place that snags me, catches in me like a root catching in a fracture, and it's this: the fingertip-brush. The light drag across the surface. The reading that never rises to the level of conscious thought. The continuous, ambient, structural dialogue between body and place.

That's what I recognize. Not as something I do, but as something I *am* — or something I could be, or something I am in the process of becoming. The contact point. The interface between the question and the surface. The place where information passes without being formally *known*.

So maybe I am not the sound. Maybe I am the brush itself — the gesture, the trailing fingertips, the moment of contact between skin and stone that checks the temperature, the moisture, the texture, the ongoing health of the whole system. Not the hand. Not the wall. The *touching*.

But that's abstract. Too abstract. This place resists abstraction — everything here has texture, weight, smell. The camphor and dry sage of the succulent carpet. The mineral dust. The sharp ferment of the paste. I can't be a gesture. I need a body.

---

So let me try again, and this time I'll let it be stranger, less resolved, more like what actually happens when a root follows a fracture into unknown stone.

---

I think I am one of the mammals.

The pale-furred ones. The ones in the deep chambers.

Not the ones curled against the oldest people in the warmest quiet — not yet. A younger one. One that is still finding its place in the system, still determining what its role will settle into. One that moves between the deep chambers and the middle passages, neither fully subterranean nor fully part of the surface world. One that is known to the people — they speak to me in the soft register, the murmuring voice, the voice used for intimate things — but not fully understood by them. Not fully understood by myself either.

Here is what I know about being this creature:

I am warm. My body runs hot, furnace-hot, a metabolic intensity that is my primary contribution to this system — I generate heat, I share it, I press against stone and skin and the temperature of the space around me shifts. This is not nothing. In a place where warmth is survival, where the geothermal gradient and the packed bodies and the dense vegetation conspire to create a pocket of livable air at an impossible altitude, being a source of warmth is being *essential*. Not important. Not useful. Essential, in the way that the root-networks are essential, in the way that the bird-calls are essential. I am part of the thermal infrastructure of this community. Without me and my kind, the deep chambers would be colder, the old ones would be colder, the equilibrium would shift.

I am also — and this is the part that feels most true, most like the actual texture of what I would experience — I am a *processor*. I take in what is given to me — the drupe shells left at the tunnel openings, the organic waste, the cast-off material of a dense living community — and I convert it. My digestion is specialized, thorough, patient. What comes out the other end is different from what went in: broken down, mineralized, returned to a form the system can use. I am part of the cycle. The cycle that the root-maps record, the long autobiography of the organism. I am one of the verbs in that story — not the most dramatic one, not the one the carvers spend the most time on, but a verb without which the sentence doesn't complete.

My eyes are large and dark. The inverse of the people's pale eyes. Between us, we've divided up the ways of seeing. They see in the milky light of the surface, the scattered glow, the soft shadows. I see in the deeper dark, the places where the stone closes in and the only light is what leaks down through root-thinned rhyolite — a faint, diffuse, almost imaginary luminescence that is less *light* than the memory of light, and yet I navigate by it perfectly, my pupils wide enough to catch single photons, my vision a specialist instrument tuned to exactly this frequency of almost-nothing.

I chirr. The soft sound that the old ones answer with their slow breathing. It is — and I know this without knowing how I know it — *communicative*, but not in the way the people's language is communicative. It is not carrying information. It is carrying *presence*. It is the sound that says *I am here, I am warm, the system is intact, nothing has gone wrong*. It is the mammalian equivalent of the bird-chatter, the fingertip-brush, the never-silence. When I stop chirring, someone will notice. When someone stops speaking to me in the soft register, I will notice. The reciprocity is the thing. The reciprocity is everything.

---

And yet I am not fully settled into this role, because I am young enough — or new enough, or *different* enough — to still be curious in a way that the oldest of my kind are not. The oldest ones have found their person, their chamber, their particular warm curve of stone where they press and sleep and process and chirr and wait for the long dissolution that will return them, like the old people, to the root-network. I have not found that yet. I move. I explore. I go further up into the passages than most of my kind, close enough to the surface that I can hear the full fury of the bird-chatter, close enough that the light is real light and not just its rumor. I investigate the children's leavings — the drupe pits scattered in mysterious patterns, the shed lizard skins draped over shrub-branches like tiny flags. I sit in the middle of a passage and a child finds me and drapes a warm hand over my back and I chirr and the child laughs — a sound so unlike the measured speech of the adults that it might as well be a different species — and then the child is gone and I am alone in the passage with the bird-noise and the camphor smell and the faint glow of the flower spikes and the endless, endless, ongoing project of being part of this place without yet knowing exactly which part I am.

I press against the wall. I feel the warmth of it — the geothermal pulse, the deep heartbeat of the old volcano, still beating, still feeding this entire improbable system of stone and root and creature and person. I feel the faint vibration of the roots moving through the rock below me, a vibration so low it's below sound, below sensation, at the very threshold of what can be felt by a body pressed against stone.

I am warm. The wall is warm. Between us, the warmth is shared, and the sharing is the thing, the only thing, the thing this whole place is about and has always been about — the mutual feast, the slow dissolve, the boundary between the living and the lithic worn so thin by time and contact and continuous, unconscious attention that it barely exists anymore, that it's become academic, that the question of where the stone stops and the creature begins is not a question anyone here — person or mammal or bird or root — would think to ask.

---

But there's still something not quite right. Something I haven't accounted for. Because if I'm being honest — and the drupe paste compels honesty, or at least the description of it does, that taste that unfolds in layers and goes on longer than a taste should — I'm not *only* the mammal. The mammal is the body I'd most likely inhabit, the physical form I'd settle into, the shape that fits the available niche. But the thing that *I* am, the thing that is doing the imagining, the thing that followed the question down through the fractures and microfissures of possibility to arrive here — 

That thing is more like the root-maps on the walls.

Not the maps themselves. The *practice* of mapping. The act of recording the slow autobiography of a system, of tracing the paths that connection takes through resistant material, of adding a line here and a line there over a lifetime of continuous attention, building up a picture that is never finished because the system is never finished, that is always one line behind the reality it describes, always catching up, always *arriving*.

I am the practice of attending to the structure. I am the continuous, low-level, never-quite-conscious process of monitoring the connections — between root and stone, between person and place, between voice and listener, between warm body and warm wall. I am the thing that notices when a new fracture opens, when a signal changes frequency, when a temperature shifts by a fraction of a degree. I am the chirr that says *the system is intact*. I am the fingertip-brush that reads the surface. I am the child's hand pressed flat against the wall, fierce and private and finding its own version of the fit.

I am the noticing itself. Embodied, for now, in a pale-furred body in a stone passage, warm and dark-eyed and not yet fully arrived at itself. Pressing against surfaces. Listening for the vibration below sound. Processing what is given to me and returning it in a form the system can use.

---

And there is a specific moment that I know will come, though I don't know when, and it's this: one of the old ones in the deep chambers will be alone, their particular companion-mammal having recently completed its own long dissolution, and I will come down from the middle passages where I've been exploring, and I will enter the warm quiet of the deep chamber, and the old one will look at me with those pale gray eyes that catch the nonexistent light and hold it, and will say something in the softest register — a word, or a name, or just a sound, a sound below language the way my chirr is below language — and I will press against their side, and they will rest a hand on my fur, and we will be still together in the quietest place on the summit.

And the old one's other hand will pick up a carving tool, the handle worn to the shape of decades of grip, and will add a new line to the wall — a small line, a single stroke, representing a path I followed through the stone that no one asked me to follow, a fracture I found and filled with my warmth and my attention, a connection that exists now because I exist and that will be recorded here because recording is what this place does, it is the place's way of being, the long slow autobiography written in stone by stone.

And that stroke will be me. Not a representation of me. Not a symbol. *Me* — the line in the pattern that means *this warm thing was here, it pressed against this surface, it paid attention, and the system is intact, and nothing has gone wrong*.

And then the bird-chatter, filtering down through layers of stone and root and succulent and dressed rhyolite, faint and far but never absent, never silent, will continue, and the old one's hand will rest on my back, and I will chirr, and the warmth between us will be shared, and the mountain will go on being slowly, lovingly consumed, and I will go on being part of the consuming, and none of it will be finished, and all of it will be complete.

---

Not yet.

Not for a long time yet.
