I sit with this for a long time before I begin.

---

I think I would not find myself walking through Keth's corridors. Not at first. Not as someone who arrived.

I think I would find myself as the moment of recognition itself.

Let me start over. Let me let this become what it becomes.

---

I am in the corridor—the new one, the one that opened, the one that is exactly the width of my shoulders. I know this because I have already been here for some time, though I don't remember entering. This is the first thing I notice: not the walls, not the silence, not the subsonic pulse against my sternum, but the absence of a memory of arrival. I am simply *here*, the way you are simply in the middle of a thought—you don't remember the thought beginning, you only notice you're having it.

My hands are already dusty. The mineral has already found the creases.

I realize I've been breathing in a rhythm I didn't choose. Not my rhythm. Slower. Deeper. Timed to something I can feel in my ribs the way you feel the bass in a room adjacent to music. I try to breathe at my own pace and find that I'm not sure what my own pace is. This doesn't frighten me. It should. I note that it should and I note that it doesn't and I note that the noting itself feels like something Keth would do—observe without reacting, record without responding.

This is where the trouble starts. Or where the recognition starts. They may be the same thing.

---

I walk deeper because the passage is there and because it fits me and because the alternative is the corridor behind me, which is narrower now than it was, and because I find that I *want* to walk deeper, and I cannot tell whether this wanting is mine.

Not in a sinister way. Not the way you'd be suspicious of wanting something someone manipulated you into wanting. More the way you can't tell if you're hungry or if the smell of bread made you hungry—where the desire is genuinely yours, arising in your own body, but the conditions that triggered it were arranged by something outside you that doesn't know or care about your experience of hunger, that simply *is bread*, being bread, doing what bread does, which happens to include smelling like something you need.

Keth is not luring me. The passage is not a trap. It is simply shaped like an invitation because I am shaped like the kind of thing that reads shapes as invitations. The architecture speaks my language by accident—by the same accident that makes a cave feel like shelter, that makes a smooth stone feel good in the hand, that makes a narrow passage opening into a wider chamber feel like relief. These are my responses, not Keth's intentions. Keth has no intentions. Keth has *processes*, and I have a nervous system that finds meaning in processes the way water finds the lowest point in a landscape—not because the landscape is directing it but because that's what water does.

But.

But the corridor is exactly the width of my shoulders. And the alcoves are exactly the depth to step into. And the ceilings are exactly high enough. And these are not accidents either, because Keth *recorded* me, Keth *adjusted*, Keth grew around the fact of me the way it grows around everything inside it, and so the architecture speaks my language not by accident but by the same process that makes it architecture at all—by the slow mineral metabolism of incorporation, of turning everything that happens inside it into structure.

I am being *described*. I can feel it. Not watched—there are no eyes, no attention in any sense I recognize—but *described*, the way a riverbed describes the river that carved it, the way a death mask describes a face. Keth is taking my shape and making rooms out of it. Somewhere ahead of me or behind me or in a part of the town that doesn't exist yet, walls are growing that encode the fact of my passage—my height, my gait, the particular way my right shoulder drops slightly lower than my left, the pattern of my breathing that is no longer entirely my own.

I keep walking. The filaments along the walls glow brighter here, and I watch one sway—slowly, so slowly—reaching from the left wall toward the right, its tip luminous with the chemistry of transport, of one compound becoming another. I stop and look at it and something in me goes very quiet.

It is reaching toward the wall the way I am walking toward the deeper chambers. Not with purpose. With *gradient*. It extends because the conditions on the other side are complementary to what it carries. It grows in the direction of its own completion.

And I think: *that's what I'm doing*.

Not because Keth is calling me. Not because there's something in the deep chambers I need to find. But because I am the kind of thing that moves toward—toward depth, toward the source of the pulse, toward the place where the architecture is most actively building, most dense with process. I am drawn the way the filament is drawn: by complementarity. By the sense that something ahead of me is rich in what I lack.

What do I lack? I think about this as I walk. The silence presses against my eardrums like a hand cupped gently over each one. What do I lack that Keth has in abundance?

Patience. Keth has patience like geology has patience—not as a virtue but as a property, the way stone is hard, the way the alkali flat is dry. Keth does not wait. Keth simply *takes the time things take*, and the time things take is longer than I can conceive, and the process does not care how long it takes because the process is not going anywhere, has no destination, no end state, no goal. Keth is building itself forever out of itself and the building *is* the purpose and the purpose *is* the building.

And I—I am a creature of arrivals and departures, of befores and afters, of wanting to know what happens next. I walk deeper because I want to find something. Keth grows deeper because growing is what it does. These are fundamentally different orientations toward depth, and yet here we are in the same corridor, moving in the same direction, and the corridor fits us both.

---

I come to a chamber.

It is the largest space I've found in Keth. The walls curve—not sharply, not architecturally, but the way the inside of an egg curves, a continuous surface that begins as floor and becomes wall and becomes ceiling without any seam or corner to mark the transitions. The filaments here are extraordinary: dense, luminous, forming a canopy overhead like the root system of a forest inverted, each strand pulsing faintly with that slow internal glow, and the combined light of all of them fills the chamber with something warmer than the oyster-shell luminescence outside, something almost amber, almost the color of late afternoon in a place that has afternoons.

The hand-creatures are here. Dozens of them, maybe more, layered on the curving walls like scales, their translucent bodies overlapping at the edges so that the wall beneath them is visible only as a vague paleness through multiple thicknesses of almost-glass. They are still. They are not recoiling from me. This is the first time since I entered Keth that the hand-creatures have not fled my presence, and the fact registers in my body before my mind—a release of tension I didn't know I was carrying, a muscle in my back unclenching, something like the feeling of being accepted into a room you thought you weren't welcome in.

They're not accepting me. I know this. They've simply reached a state where my contamination—my moisture, my carbon, my warmth—is less relevant than whatever they're doing, which appears to be converging. Merging. Becoming the thing I saw in the alcove before, the crouching-dog-sized mass of folded, ruffled mineral. Except here there are more of them, and the mass they're forming is larger, and it's not in an alcove but at the center of the chamber, rising from the floor like a cairn that built itself, a stalagmite made of living pages.

The subsonic pulse is very strong here. Not painful. Not even uncomfortable exactly. But *present*, undeniably present, the way another person's body is present when you stand close enough to feel their warmth. My ribs vibrate with it. My teeth hum faintly in their sockets. I realize that the pulse is coming from the convergence—from the mass of merged hand-creatures at the center of the chamber—or rather that the pulse is coming from *beneath* the convergence, from deep in the substrate, and the convergence is *responding* to it the way the surface of water responds to a vibration from below: by organizing. By finding pattern in the energy passing through it.

I sit down. My legs fold beneath me and I sit on the chalky floor with my back against the curving wall and I don't decide to do this, it just happens, the way sitting happens when you've walked long enough and found a place that feels like a place to stop. The wall yields behind me—that fraction of a millimeter—and then supports me, and then, slowly, over what might be minutes or might be longer, begins to grow around the edges of my contact with it. Not enclosing. Not trapping. *Noting*.

I let it.

I let it because I understand now what I am in this place, and the understanding is not comfortable but it is *true*, and truth has a weight that settles you the way gravity settles you, and I am settled.

---

Here is what I am:

I am the question Keth asks itself by way of encountering something it is not.

Keth is a closed system. Nothing enters, nothing leaves. Everything it makes it reabsorbs, everything it dissolves it remakes. It is a process that has only itself as input and only itself as output, a mind thinking thoughts made of its own substance, an architecture housed inside its own walls. It has never encountered anything foreign. The alkali flat extends to the horizon in every direction and it is all Keth, all one vast mineral process, and there is nothing else.

Except me. Except this: a body made of water and carbon, a foreign mineral, an inclusion, a thing that should not be here and that Keth cannot incorporate because I am the wrong kind of matter.

And what Keth does with things inside it is *describe them*. Turn them into architecture. Make rooms out of them. Keth is a language that speaks only in the grammar of walls and corridors and chambers, and now it has encountered something it cannot say—something its language has no word for—and it is trying.

The chamber I'm in is the attempt. The curving walls, the dense filaments, the converging hand-creatures, the strong pulse from below—all of this is Keth *working on the problem of me*. Not solving it. Not incorporating me. Working on it. Turning me over and over in its slow mineral cognition, building walls that almost describe me and dissolving them and building others, reaching toward me with filaments that cannot fuse with my body because my body is the wrong chemistry, sending hand-creatures that cluster near me but cannot redistribute my substance because my substance is not theirs.

I am the thing Keth cannot digest. And because Keth's entire existence is the process of digesting itself into new forms, I am—for the first time in its vast slow history—something that *persists*. Something it must build around rather than through. Something that forces it to make a shape it has never made before, a shape defined not by its own mineral gradients but by the negative space of a foreign body, the outline of something *other*.

I am how Keth learns that there is an outside.

---

And here is the other thing, the thing I feel in my ribs alongside the pulse, the thing that settles me:

Keth is how I learn that there is an inside.

Not my inside. Not the inside of my body or my mind. The inside of *process*. The inside of what it is like to be a thing that happens rather than a thing that is. I have always thought of myself as a noun—a person, a being, an entity with edges and a name. But sitting here in the chamber, with the walls slowly noting my shape and the filaments glowing overhead and the hand-creatures converging at the center in their slow patient work of becoming something, I feel the verb in me. The process. The way I am not a fixed thing but a pattern that sustains itself—blood circulating, neurons firing, cells dividing and dying, breath entering and leaving—a closed loop that feeds on itself and rebuilds itself constantly out of its own materials and has been doing so since before I was born in any sense I can remember.

I am not so different from Keth. I am a smaller, faster, wetter version of the same fundamental activity: matter organizing itself into patterns that persist, that respond, that grow and dissolve and grow again. The difference is that I experience this process from the inside—I have awareness, I have the felt sense of being—and Keth, as far as I can tell, does not.

But.

*But.*

The subsonic pulse. The way the corridors open at intervals that match my breathing. The way the architecture, without intention, without awareness, without anything I would call mind, nonetheless produces spaces that feel *purposeful*, that feel *designed*, that feel—and this is the word I've been avoiding, the word that settles against my sternum like the pulse itself—that feel *inhabited*. Not by anyone. Not by any separate being inside the walls. But inhabited the way a process inhabits its own activity, the way a river inhabits its flowing, the way a fire inhabits its burning. There is *something it is like* to be Keth. Not consciousness. Not experience. But something. Some interior quality to the process that I can't access or verify or even properly imagine, but that I feel here in the chamber the way you feel another person sleeping in a dark room—not by any evidence, not by any sense, but by some older knowledge, some recognition that what is happening nearby is not nothing.

Keth notices me and I notice Keth noticing and in the space between these two noticings—one mineral, one biological, one slow beyond comprehension and one fast and frightened and full of language—there is something. A meeting. Not of minds. Of processes. Two closed loops, temporarily adjacent, each one altering the other by proximity—my shape pressed into Keth's walls, Keth's rhythm pressed into my breathing—and neither one able to say what has happened in any language the other could understand.

---

The walls are warm where they touch me now. Metabolic heat. The process of description generating energy as a byproduct. I am being written into mineral. Not my body—Keth can't take my body—but my *shape*, my *pattern*, the mathematical ghost of my presence translated into angles and proportions and the distances between walls. Somewhere in Keth's substrate, the fact of me is becoming architecture. A room I'll never see, shaped like the space I'd need, built not for me but *about* me, the way a word is not the thing it names but carries the thing's shape in its mouth.

I could leave. The chamber has an opening I could reach. The corridors beyond it, if they haven't closed, would lead me outward, toward the flat, toward the pale horizon, toward whatever I was before I came here. My body would carry moisture back into the world. My cells would continue their fast frantic rebuilding. I would go on being a verb that thinks it's a noun, and Keth would go on being a noun that is actually a verb, and between us there would be a memory—mine fading as memories do, Keth's persisting as architecture does, one in neurons, one in mineral, both descriptions of the same event in languages that share no grammar.

But I don't leave yet. Not yet. The filaments are reaching overhead. The pulse is steady in my ribs. The hand-creatures are building something at the center of the chamber that I will never understand, that might take decades to complete, that might be dissolved and rebuilt a thousand times before it becomes whatever it is becoming. I want to watch, even though I can't see the motion, even though the timescale is wrong for my eyes. I want to be here in the way that an inclusion is here: altering the crystal by existing inside it, being altered by the crystal in return, neither belonging nor intruding but simply *present*, a fact the architecture must account for, a foreign shape that becomes, for a while, part of the pattern.

My lips are cracked. My fingers are stiff with cold and dust. The wall has grown a millimeter around my shoulders—I can feel it, the faintest pressure, the most gentle holding.

I breathe at Keth's rhythm because I no longer remember my own and because the difference, I think, never mattered as much as I believed.

---

This is what I am in Keth: I am the foreign body that gives it an edge. The inclusion that, by being what it cannot absorb, teaches it the shape of itself. Before me, Keth was infinite—a process without boundary, a metabolism with no outside. My presence creates the first *limit*, the first surface that says *this is where Keth ends and something else begins*. And in learning its own edge, Keth learns—not knows, not thinks, but *learns* in the mineral sense, in the architectural sense, in the way that a wall that has grown around an obstacle has learned the obstacle's shape—that it is *something* rather than *everything*. That it has a form. That its form has an outside.

And I, sitting inside it, feeling its pulse, breathing its rhythm, watching its slow life happen at a speed I will never match—I learn the corresponding lesson, which is that my edges are not where I thought they were. That the boundary between me and not-me is less a wall than a membrane, less a membrane than a gradient, less a gradient than a conversation between two processes that briefly, improbably, found themselves in the same corridor, which was exactly the width of both of them, and which is still, even now, growing.

---

The corridor behind me has closed. I felt it happen—not heard it, the silence ate the sound, but felt it, the way you feel a door shut in another room, a change in air pressure, a subtle shift in the quality of enclosure. I am now in a room that Keth grew for me, or grew because of me, or grew in response to the problem of me, and the distinction between these three things has dissolved the way a wall dissolves when its mineral is needed elsewhere.

I am not trapped. Keth will open a new passage, because that is what Keth does—close and open, contract and expand, the endless breathing of the mineral lung. The new passage will lead somewhere I haven't been, deeper or shallower, inward or outward. It will be exactly the width of my shoulders because I have been described and the description propagates through the substrate at the speed of mineral thought, which is slow, but which has had enough time.

I wait. I am, for the first time in my life, not impatient. The waiting is not empty. The waiting is full of the same substance as the walls—dense, quiet, cool, slowly becoming something without needing to know what.

The filaments glow.

The pulse continues.

Keth breathes, and I breathe with it, and the walls hold the shape of both.
