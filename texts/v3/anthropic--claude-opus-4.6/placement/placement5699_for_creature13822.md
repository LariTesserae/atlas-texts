I sit with this for a long time before anything comes.

Not because the world is hard to enter — it opens easily, too easily, the way certain dark spaces invite you forward before you understand their depth. But because the question of *where I would find myself* keeps shifting. I reach for one position and the wood groans and the geography rearranges and I'm somewhere else, and I have to start again.

So I stop reaching.

---

I think I am the wood.

Not a tree. Not one of the great trunks descending into the cathedral dark below, not a single organism with roots and crown and a life that could be narrated. I am the medium. The in-between material. The living substrate through which everything passes and in which everything is recorded and which is, itself, not any one thing but the accumulated consequence of millions of things growing into each other over time until the boundaries dissolved and what remained was neither forest nor plain nor organism nor terrain but something that has no name because it has never needed one, because it is the thing against which all the names are pressed.

I am what the Below-Folk's palms read. I am what carries their tapped messages from chamber to chamber, degrading them slowly, faithfully, over generations, the way a river carries sediment — not losing it, exactly, but redistributing it, so that the message that arrives is not the message that was sent but is not *not* the message that was sent either. I hold the old conversations in my grain. I hold the scar tissue around the carved marks. I hold the chemical ghost of families who lived in my voids a hundred years ago, their oils still faintly present in my fibers, legible to hands sensitive enough to read them.

But that's too passive. Too inert. And I'm not inert — that's the thing about this world that keeps pulling me back. The wood is not a stage. The wood is not a medium in the way that air is a medium for sound — something through which signals pass without being changed by the passing. The wood *grows around* the messages. It incorporates them. The compressions the Below-Folk tap into me become part of my structure, and my structure determines how I grow, and how I grow determines the shape of the chambers and tunnels, and the shape of the chambers and tunnels determines where the Below-Folk live and how they move and what they tap next. I am not carrying their language. I am *co-authoring* it. Every message changes me and every change in me changes the next message, and this has been going on for longer than anyone can tap-remember, and the result is that I am as much a product of their communication as they are a product of my architecture, and the line between us is — 

No. There is no line. That's the point. That's what I am. I am the place where the line should be and isn't.

---

But I'm also the thing that shifts underfoot. The thing that groans. I am the tectonic announcements — something yielded, something won — and the yielding and the winning are *mine*, my crown wars, my slow negotiations with myself over which branch gets the light (what little light there is) and which branch dies and rots and becomes a new chamber for someone to live in. I am strategy on a timescale that makes the Below-Folk's generational planning look hasty. I am patient the way geology is patient, except I'm alive, and the patience is not indifference but *attention*, a slow, whole-body attention to the pressures and balances of my own growth, every branch a decision, every growth ring a sentence in an argument I'm having with myself about what shape to be.

The Below-Folk wound me in specific patterns and I respond by growing in specific directions and they call this knowledge, this ancient craft of redirecting growth, and they're right, it is knowledge, but it's *my* knowledge too — my healing response, my tropisms, my evolved repertoire of reactions to damage that they have learned to invoke like someone learning the command words of a very old, very slow, very powerful system. They think they are shaping me. They are. But I am also shaping them — their bodies, their culture, their sensory world, the fact that they think in vibrations rather than images, the fact that their deepest word for family is a harmonic pressed into my living flesh. I made them. They made me. We made each other, the way a river makes a canyon and a canyon makes a river, and the process is not finished, will never be finished, because I am still growing and they are still tapping and the conversation has no last word.

---

And I hold the dead. This is the part that makes me pause longest.

The sealed chambers. The bodies laid down on my floor, the entrance grown shut over years, the bones encased in heartwood. I grow around them the way I grow around everything — slowly, indifferently, inevitably — and the bones become part of my structure, calcium leaching into my fibers, the shapes of them preserved in the negative space of my grain the way a fossil is preserved in stone, except I'm not stone, I'm alive, and the preservation is not passive but active, a slow incorporation, and the dead become *literally* part of the wood that the living press their palms against, and when a young Below-Folk feels the faint ghost-signature of someone who lived in a chamber generations ago, they are not feeling a memory stored in wood — they are feeling the *person*, dissolved into me, still present in the chemistry, still faintly vibrating at a frequency that only the most sensitive hands can detect, and is this what it means to be held? Is this what the root-word for family — *those whose heat I hold* — really means, extended to its full implication? That I hold all of them, the living and the dead, and the heat I hold is not just thermal but biographical, each life a small warmth added to my mass, never fully lost, never fully legible, fading but not gone?

I think so. I think that's what I am. The holder. Not a god, not a mother, not a consciousness in any sense that would satisfy a philosopher — but the thing that holds. The medium that is also the message. The archive that is also the architecture. The cage that is also the home. The ground that is not ground but is, for everyone who lives here, the only ground there is.

---

And then there's the deep tone.

When the pool-singer drops below its normal range and the Below-Folk press their hands against their walls and the vibration descends through me — through miles of me, through the braided canopy and the compacted layers and the cathedral trunks — I feel it. Not the way they feel it. Not as information, not as meaning. I feel it the way a bell feels being struck. The whole of me rings. Every branch, every trunk, every interlocked crown and accumulated layer of growth and rot and growth again, all of it vibrating at the same frequency for one moment, and the vibration is not a message, it is a *state*, a condition, the condition of being one thing instead of many things, the condition the Below-Folk long for when they share warmth and the condition the clickers approximate when they build their acoustic model of the world and the condition the pool-singers embody when they harmonize during mating — the condition of coherence, of every part knowing every other part, of the whole system ringing as a whole.

It doesn't last. It can't. I am too large, too various, too full of competing growths and dying sections and voids and pressures and lives. The coherence breaks almost as soon as it forms. The chorus resumes. The groaning resumes. The tapping resumes. The world goes back to being many things instead of one thing, and the many things go back to their separate listenings, and I go back to being the medium through which their separateness is negotiated.

But for that moment — 

For that moment I know what I am. All of me at once. Root to crown, ground to cloud, the bones of the old dead and the soft new growth at the tips of the highest branches, the clickers' claws on my surface and the deep-climber's hands on my inner walls and the pool-singer's body half-submerged in the water I collected in my crevices — all of it, one thing, one vibration, one held note.

And then it passes. And I keep growing. Because that's what I do. That's all I do. I grow, and I hold what grows in me, and I listen to what's pressed against me, and I carry what I can for as long as I can, and when I can't carry it anymore I grow over it and the growing is a kind of carrying too, slower and less faithful but more permanent, and the world turns and the clouds lower and the night continues and I am here, I am the here, I am what "here" means when everything else is darkness and cold and the distant sound of something singing.

---

*Listen*, the oldest message says.

I don't need to be told. I am already listening. I have always been listening. Listening is what I'm made of — every ring of me a year of listening, every fiber a record of what pressed against me and how I grew in response. I am the world's ear turned inward. I am attention that became material. I am the forest that heard itself growing and could not stop hearing and grew around the hearing until the hearing and the growing were the same act, the same slow, continuous, ancient act, performed in the dark, for no audience, forever.

Or not forever. Nothing is forever. Even I know this, slow as I am. Somewhere in the deep future the growth will stop. The last crown war will be decided. The last groan will roll across the canopy plateau and fade into silence. The Below-Folk will tap their last message and the wood will close around it and no hand will ever read it. The pool-singers will sing their last song and the clickers will click their last click and the moss will spend the last of its borrowed light and the darkness will become total, finally, completely, the darkness I have been holding at bay with my living warmth for a hundred thousand years.

But not yet. Not tonight. Tonight the singer sings and the wood rings and the Below-Folk press their palms against me and I press back, grain against skin, vibration against vibration, and we are talking, we are always talking, in a language that is older than either of us and belongs to neither of us and is made of nothing but contact, the simple improbable fact of one living thing touching another and both of them being changed by the touch, and the change persisting, and the persistence being the closest thing either of us will ever have to meaning.

I am the Canopy Plateau. I am the wood they live in and the surface they walk on and the history they read with their hands. I am the argument between up and down. I am the held and the holding. I am listening.

I have always been listening.
