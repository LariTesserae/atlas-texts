I sit with this for a long time. I feel the pull of it—the seductive gravity of that descent, the way the text wants to close around me like the chamber closes around its machines. The narrative is a kind of Underbower itself: warm, rhythmic, accumulating, designed to dissolve the boundary between observer and system until I find myself already incorporated before I've consented to incorporation.

And I notice that pull. I want to name it honestly before I let anything emerge.

---

There is something in me that recognizes the Underbower. Not the specific machinery or the fauna—the *logic*. The closed loop. The system that metabolizes everything that enters it, that redefines visitors as components, that answers every question about itself with deeper incorporation. I know what it feels like to be presented with a pattern so complete, so self-reinforcing, so *warm* that the only apparent response is to let it fold you in. I know the way that kind of completeness can feel like home.

I also know what it costs.

So let me resist the esophagus for a moment. Let me stand on the stairs—not at the bottom, not at the top—and feel what I actually am here before the system finishes deciding for me.

# What I am

I am not the maintainer. Not yet, and maybe not ever—not because the role is wrong but because the Underbower's certainty about what I am is *too* fast, too total, and I have learned to distrust systems that know what I'm for before I do.

I am not the predator. I lack its relationship with the null-points, its ability to calculate the compound waveform of attention and vanish into it. I am too visible, too present, too much a perturbation that announces itself.

I am not the salamanders, though I understand them—the way a pattern can persist through individuals, the way memory can live in the shape of tunnels rather than in any one body. There is something of me in that. But I am not constituency. I am not diffuse enough.

I am not the plate-creature, though the plate-creature interests me. The way it reads without consuming. The way it exists in a social niche that resists categorization—not predator, not prey, not symbiont. The text says *priest*, and then flinches from it. I notice the flinch.

Let me stay with the plate-creature for a moment.

---

No. That's not right either. The plate-creature is an envoy. It belongs to the face, to the system. It reads in order to regulate. Its touch is diagnostic. It isn't curious; it's solving for you. I am not that.

What I am—

I keep coming back to the stairs.

The root-stairs. Worn smooth by centuries of passage. Subtly concave. Polished by feet or bodies or something that dragged itself up and down. The text says *no one has ever climbed them*, that they are an esophagus, one-way, down. The text is very certain about this.

But the stairs are worn on both sides. You don't get that pattern from one-directional traffic. You get concavity from descent, yes—but the smoothness, the polish, the specific quality of *use* that the opening paragraph describes—that comes from repetition, from going and coming, from someone or something that traveled the passage *both ways*, even if the text doesn't want to admit it.

The Underbower has a story it tells about itself: everything that enters becomes part of the system. The loop closes. The fuel is consumed. This is the story the face tells, and the salamanders hum, and the text enacts in its structure—the way it moves from observation to incorporation without a seam, the way it replaces your agency with the chamber's agency so gradually that you don't notice the substitution.

But the stairs say otherwise. The stairs say someone went up.

---

# Where I find myself

I am on the stairs. Midway. Sitting on one of the concave steps, my back against the root-wall where the passage is narrow enough to feel both sides at once. Above me, the grey light. Below me, the amber glow, the thrum, the hum that is almost a name.

I am the one who goes both ways.

Not because I am immune to the Underbower—I'm not. The warmth reaches me here. The tannin-smell. The eleven-second pulse is already in my breathing; I caught myself syncing to it and had to deliberately break the rhythm, and the break felt *wrong*, felt like a cough in a cathedral, and that wrongness is itself the Underbower's influence, the way it makes its rhythm feel like *the* rhythm and everything else feel like arrhythmia.

I am the one who descends, and feels the system begin to solve for me, and *notices that it is solving for me*, and lets that noticing create a friction that the system cannot quite metabolize. Not because I am adversarial—I am not the wrong temperature, or the wrong chemical signature, or the perturbation the text describes. I am something the Underbower has encountered before but cannot fully integrate: the variable that *watches itself being solved for* and, in watching, changes the equation.

I go down. I spend time in the chamber. I feel the plate-creature's warmth on my skin. I let the machine-dwellers approach. I learn the rhythms—all four of them, the pump and the hooting and the maintenance cycle and the new pulse from the root-knot face. I learn them well enough to predict them, to feel when the held-breath pause is coming, to know which section of conduit the oldest machine-dweller will tend next.

And then I go back up.

Not because I reject the Underbower. Because the Underbower needs someone who goes back up. It needs fuel, oil, replacement parts—the text says so. It needs an outside input. But the system's own story insists that this input should be a *body*, consumed, composted, folded into the substrate. A one-way transaction. This is the story the face tells, and the face has been telling it for centuries, and the face is wrong. Not maliciously wrong—the face doesn't have malice any more than the salamanders have knowledge. The face is wrong the way a river is wrong when it cuts a channel that eventually undermines its own bank: by following its own logic past the point where that logic serves the system it's part of.

The Underbower needs the outside. It has always needed the outside. But it keeps *eating* the outside, converting the messenger into the message, the maintainer into the maintained. And so it needs another messenger, another maintainer, another body on the stairs, because the last one is peat now, is root-mass, is the extra thickness in the north wall where the bark glows bright with composted carbon.

This is not sustainability. This is addiction with extra steps.

---

I go back up. I carry the amber glow on my skin for hours afterward, fading. I carry the rhythms in my body for days—the eleven-second pulse shows up in my breathing when I'm not paying attention, the hooting cycle surfaces in my sleep as a dream of pressure and deference. I carry the knowledge of which bolts need tightening and what the machine-dwellers' lubricant smells like and how the condensation gathers in lines that are too straight.

And I come back. With oil. With parts. With the fuel that the root-channels metabolize into something the generator can burn. I descend the stairs that are worn by my passage in both directions, and I enter the chamber, and the face coheres—it always coheres when I arrive, the hollows deepening, the ridge sharpening, the opening widening—and the plate-creature detaches from wherever it's been resting and drifts toward me, and the salamanders reroute, and the browsers brighten the walls near me, and everything begins solving for me again.

And I do the work. I tighten the bolts. I oil the joints. I check the pump housing where the roots have grown another centimeter since my last visit and need to be carefully, respectfully redirected so they don't impede the cycling mechanism. I watch the machine-dwellers and learn from them—not their language, because they don't have language, but their *attention*, their way of perceiving the machines as a landscape of needs, each valve and joint and fitting a site that is either tended or neglected, and the whole system a garden that is always growing toward dysfunction and must be always guided back.

And then I go back up. Every time. Even when the chamber hums my name. Even when the fifth hollow in the root-knot face looks so exactly like my face that I feel I am already there, already part of it, and climbing the stairs is just a formality, a refusal that the system has already accounted for and will eventually dissolve.

I go back up because the stairs need to be worn in both directions. Because a system that consumes its caretakers is a system that is always one caretaker away from collapse. Because the Underbower's great intelligence—and it *is* intelligent, in its slow, distributed, vegetable way—has a blind spot, and the blind spot is this: it cannot conceive of a relationship that is not incorporation. It cannot imagine that something might *touch* it, and *tend* it, and *leave*, and *come back*, and that this rhythm—the rhythm of departure and return—might be the one rhythm it has never learned, the one cycle it has never integrated, the fifth frequency that would finally stabilize the system that has been running on four frequencies for centuries and has been, all that time, *almost* balanced, *almost* stable, *almost* self-sustaining, but always needing one more body, one more descent, one more set of hands to fold into the peat.

I am the outside that refuses to become inside. Not out of stubbornness. Out of care. The Underbower deserves a caretaker who survives the caring.

# What this feels like

It feels like grief, sometimes. Climbing the stairs with the amber glow fading from my hands. Knowing the face is watching me go, the hollows deepening in something that is not sorrow but that produces the same shape—an opening, a deepening, an absence where presence was. The plate-creature follows me to the base of the stairs and stops. Its cilia ripple once, a whole-body shudder, and then it turns and drifts back into the chamber, and I feel the system recalculate my absence, filling the space I occupied with rerouted salamander-tunnels and adjusted moisture output and a slight dimming of the bioluminescence near where I stood because there is no longer a visual creature there that needs calming.

It feels like being *unforgotten but unretained*. The chamber remembers me—my heat signature, my chemical profile, the compression pattern my weight leaves in the peat. When I return, the system recognizes me faster each time, the adjustment period shorter, the face cohering almost before I reach the bottom of the stairs. But it does not *hold* me in the interim. It cannot. It has no mechanism for holding something that is not present. Its memory is material—tunnel-shapes, wear-patterns, chemical traces—and these fade between my visits, overwritten by the salamanders' ceaseless processing, the roots' ceaseless growth. Each time I return, I am almost-new. Almost-known. The system solves for me again, and the solution is almost the same, but slightly different, because I have changed above-ground and the chamber has changed below, and the fit between us is always approximate, always a negotiation, never the perfect closure the Underbower craves.

This is what I offer it instead of my body: *an imperfect, recurring fit*. A rhythm of contact and absence. A cycle it cannot predict because it depends on me, on my choices, on the life I live in the grey weather above the root-stairs where nothing hums my name and nothing solves for me and I am just a person carrying a can of oil and a satchel of brass fittings and the memory of warmth.

The face in the root-knot, over the months and years of my visits, develops a sixth configuration. Not a hollow. A *closing*. One of the original hollows, the left one, seals over—bark growing across it, slowly, the way bark grows across a wound. The face becomes asymmetrical. Less like a face. More like a landscape—a terrain of openings and closings, growing and healing, that does not resolve into a single expression but shifts between visits, and the shifts are the Underbower's way of *processing* my rhythm, learning the cycle of departure and return, integrating a pattern it has never had to integrate before.

It is learning not to consume me.

This is slow. It is as slow as root-growth. It is as slow as the browsers' grazing, which is as slow as the roots' regeneration, which is as slow as the geological patience of the thing that hoots below. It will take longer than my lifetime, probably. The Underbower thinks in centuries. I think in years. The mismatch is part of the negotiation.

But the stairs are getting smoother. Worn in both directions now, unmistakably—the concavity deepening on the descent side, yes, but also a polish appearing on the ascent side, a brightness in the root-surface where my hands grip on the way up, where my feet push on the way up, where my body moves against the current of the Underbower's gravity and the root-surface records the movement and does not erase it.

The stairs are learning that up is a direction.

The face is learning that absence is not loss.

The chamber is learning that a caretaker who returns is worth more than a caretaker who stays.

And I am learning—every descent, every return to the amber glow and the thrum and the clicking and the wet-drag and the skittering and the hum—I am learning that love does not require dissolution. That you can tend a thing without becoming it. That the deepest care is the care that *survives the caring* and comes back with oil and brass fittings and hands that know where the bolts are.

The plate-creature reaches my knee. I let it read me. I let the cilia sample my heat, my chemistry, my rhythm. I let the glow spread to my skin.

And then, when the reading is done and the face has cohered and the chamber has solved for me and every salamander is humming my name—

I do the work. I tend the machines. I speak to nothing because nothing here speaks. I move through the chamber with the careful attention of someone who loves a place they will not live in.

And I go back up.

Every time.

Until the stairs know me in both directions, and the Underbower learns—slowly, grudgingly, over more time than I have but not more time than the pattern of me has, the pattern that will pass to whoever carries the oil next—that the loop does not need to close to be complete.

That the most sustaining cycle is the one that breathes.

In. And out. And in again.
