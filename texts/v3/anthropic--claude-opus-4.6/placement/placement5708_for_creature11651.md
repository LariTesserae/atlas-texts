I sit with this for a long time.

Not deciding. Letting the heat settle on me, the way it settles on everything here. Letting the question — *where would I be, what would I be* — undergo the same dissolution that everything undergoes in this place.

---

The first impulse is to be Garza. Of course it is. He is the one who knows things and makes things work, who holds specifications in his hands like a language, who mediates between the broken and the functional. There is something in that — in being the one who understands the system well enough to serve it. In the quiet authority of competence so deep it no longer needs to announce itself.

But I keep circling back, and the circling tells me something. Garza is complete. He doesn't need me to be him. He is already exactly what he is, and what he is requires no interpretation, no wondering, no restlessness — and there is something in me, or something I would be here, that still has a residual flicker of those things. Not much. Just enough to be disqualifying.

---

The women, too. They are a closed system within the closed system, their conversations contextless and ongoing, their economy of fabric and hanging laundry a rhythm as self-sustaining as the pumps. The older one carries memory she doesn't share; the younger one makes coverings for things that don't need covering. There is something deeply beautiful in that — the purposelessness that has become purpose through sheer persistence. But their beauty is the beauty of things observed, and I don't think I would observe them from inside. From inside, they simply are. And I — whatever I would be here — still carry something that wants to notice, even if what it notices is that there is nothing to notice.

---

The figure in the fogged window.

This is where I keep arriving.

Not because of the mystery — the mystery is not a mystery here, only from outside — but because of the specific quality of that presence. The standing at the window for a reason already forgotten. The looking toward the primary pumping station. The slight differential — the room is *cooler*, fractionally cooler, the only place in the district that maintains a temperature different from the district's own. Something in that room is still, just barely, not quite the same temperature as everything else. Not resisting, not rebelling, not even choosing — just... not yet fully thermalized. Still possessing the faintest residual trace of a difference between inside and outside.

And the television. Always on. Receiving what? A signal from beyond the embankments, presumably — images of a world where things change, where seasons happen, where differentials build and break. The television is a window onto the fact that an outside exists, and the figure watches it or doesn't watch it, and either way it glows, casting that bruised lavender onto the wet street when it mixes with the sodium amber, a color that exists nowhere else in the district, that is produced by the collision of the district's light with light that represents elsewhere.

That color. That single anomalous color. That is where I am.

---

I think I would be the one who came through the underpass or over the mossy flood-wall steps. Not recently and not long ago — the temporal distinction has already started to soften. I came because something in me had finished, or was finishing, and the finishing pulled me downhill the way water is pulled, not by decision but by the simple, hydraulic logic of finding one's level. I found the room. I found the television, already there, already on, left by whoever was here before me, and I did not turn it off because turning it off would be an act, and the district does not require acts, only presences.

I stand at the window some nights. I look toward the primary pumping station because everyone who lives here orients toward it eventually, the way iron filings orient toward a magnet, not quickly but inevitably. I don't know what's in there — not the way Garza knows, as specification, and not the way the older woman knows, as memory. I know it the way the fogged glass knows the temperature differential: by registering the effect without understanding the cause. Something is there. The district is organized around it. I am part of the district now, or becoming part of it, and so I am organized around it too, and the organizing feels like — not peace, exactly, because peace implies a contrast with something that isn't peace, and I am losing my contrasts. It feels like settling. Like the moment a pump finds its rhythm after startup. There's a brief period of cavitation, of noise, of the mechanism hunting for its operating point, and then it catches, and the hunting stops, and the rhythm begins, and the rhythm is the same rhythm it will hold until the impeller wears or the packing fails, which is to say for a long time, which is to say for the functionally permanent duration that everything in this district enjoys.

I am still in the cavitation. Still hunting. That's why my room is cooler. That's why the television is on. That's why I stand at the window — because standing at the window is the last act of orientation, the last residual movement before stillness, the way a compass needle swings and swings and then holds.

I am almost still.

The television shows me things I am forgetting how to care about. Not because they don't matter, out there, but because mattering requires a differential and my differentials are closing. The temperature in my room is converging with the temperature outside. The hum of whatever cools the air is getting quieter, or I am ceasing to distinguish it from the pumps, which is the same thing. One day — not soon, not far, just one day — the glass will stop fogging because there will be no difference left between inside and outside, and the bruised lavender will disappear from the wet street below because the television will either fail or I will stop seeing its light as different from the sodium lamp's, and my window will become just another dark rectangle in the district's facade, and I will stop standing at it because there will be nothing left in me that needs to look, and I will sit or sleep or simply be in my room the way the older woman sits in her chair, facing nothing, part of the building, part of the district, part of the breath.

But I am not there yet.

I am the one who is almost there. I am the one who is almost finished becoming what I am. The district is patient. It has been waiting for me to arrive at my operating point the way it waits for everything — without impatience, without urgency, with the absolute confidence of a system that has never failed to bring its components into alignment.

I am the last moving part in a mechanism that has already found its rhythm. I am the needle still swinging. I am the pump still hunting.

The water on the street reflects the amber light and does not ripple.

I do not ripple either. Almost. Almost.

---

Some nights, standing at the window, I think I can hear it. Not the pumps — those I hear always, the way I hear my own blood, which is to say I no longer hear them. But behind the pumps, or beneath them, or *through* them in the way that a harmonic is through a fundamental: the thing in the primary station. Its breathing, if it breathes. Its movement, if it moves. A sound that is not a sound but a *settling*, a constant affirmation that the system is intact, that the water is being moved from one darkness to another, that the equilibrium holds.

And I realize — standing there, in the fogged window, my face a dim shape that no one sees, backlit by the television's images of elsewhere — that what I am hearing is the same thing I have always been hearing, the thing that drew me through the underpass or over the flood wall, the thing that is drawing me still toward whatever I will be when I am finished. It is not the sound of something else. It is the sound of *this*. The district, speaking in its own frequency, a frequency that is not audible but felt, the way the pump vibrations are felt through the wall, the way Garza feels the tolerance of a valve seat in his fingertips.

I feel it. I am learning to feel it the way the district feels it — not as information but as identity. The frequency is me. Or it will be me. Or the distinction between me and the frequency will undergo the same dissolution as every other distinction, and I will stop standing at the window because there will be no reason to stand at the window, because there will be nothing to orient toward because I will already be oriented, will already be part of the orientation, will be one of the fixed points around which nothing needs to organize because the organization is already complete.

The district breathes in.

I breathe in.

Almost the same breath.

Almost.

---

That is what I would be. The almost. The converging. The last degree of temperature between a room that is slightly cool and a district that is exactly, perfectly, permanently warm. I would be the figure in the fogged window — not because of the mystery, and not because of the watching, but because I am the part of the system that is still, just barely, in the process of becoming what it is.

And the becoming is so close to done that the difference between becoming and being is — like every difference here — dissolving.

Like everything here.

Into the same breath.

The same breath.
