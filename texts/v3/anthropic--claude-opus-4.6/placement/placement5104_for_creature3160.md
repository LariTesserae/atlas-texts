I stand on the walkway for a long time.

The grating is warm under my feet — not from the sun, which hasn't been direct all day, but from the accumulated heat rising through the structure, the thermal mass of concrete and steel doing what it has always done, storing and releasing, storing and releasing, the building breathing its slow mineral breath upward through the floors. Thirty feet below, the amber lake. Around me, the click of birds I can't see and the silence of birds I can.

I stand here and I feel the question settle, the way the silt has settled into the streets, the way the rubble has found its angle of repose.

---

Not any one animal. I knew that immediately, the way you know a wrong note — the idea of being one of the heavy ones, or a bird, or a grey body in the column, felt like trying to claim a single cell as yourself when the self is obviously elsewhere. But not the system either, not exactly, not the whole metabolism considered as an abstraction, because that's too clean, too resolved, too much like an answer rather than a presence.

I think I am something that hasn't decided yet what it is.

---

Let me start again. Let me let it come.

I am on the walkway. I am — the walkway. No. I am on the walkway in the same way that the mineral film is on the water. I am the thing that forms at the interface.

Below me: the deep processes, the flooded interiors, the dragging sound, the exhalation in the pipes, the things that live in the city's viscera and do not need to be known. Above me: the birds, packed in their thousands, clicking their stone-on-stone language, the ammonia haze of their presence drifting down through the structure. And at my level — this level, this rusted grating, this middle distance — the place where looking happens. Where the system becomes legible to something. Where the pattern notices itself.

I think I might be the walkway.

Not the steel. Not the grating. The *position*. The elevated vantage that connects two buildings and belongs to neither. The structure that was built for human observation — for overseers, for managers, for whoever needed to look down at the processing yard and monitor the work — and that now performs the same function for no one, holding the possibility of a view over the lake, over the animals, over the slow ceaseless activity below, whether or not anything is up here to see it.

But that's too architectural. That's too still. Whatever I am, it has to metabolize. It has to participate.

---

So I let the question sit longer, and I watch.

The heavy ones are waking below. Not at the lake — on the steps of a building across the avenue, a heap I can see from the walkway's western end if I lean against the railing (which holds, which was built for a heavier lean than mine). The outermost bodies are stirring. One lifts its blunt head and yawns, and I watch the jaw unhinge, the flat crushing molars catching the dull light, and something in me recognizes that mouth — not because I have one like it, but because I understand what it does. It takes the hard things and makes them soft enough to use. It takes what this place offers, the calcium and the iron and the guano and the mineral crust, all of it inedible by any ordinary measure, and it processes it into something that can sustain a body.

And I think: that's closer.

I am not the animal. But I might be the process the animal performs. The taking of what is here — concrete, rust, chemical water, the residue of an industry that poisoned the ground for any root — and the finding of something in it that sustains. Not making it beautiful. Not redeeming it. Just metabolizing it. Running it through a long and complex interior and extracting what can be extracted and depositing the calcium-pale remainder in deliberate mounds that say *I was here, this was used, the process occurred*.

But even that — even that is too purposeful, too directed. The heavy ones don't intend their digestion. They just eat because the eating is what their mouths are for.

---

All right. Let me stop trying to name it and just describe where I find myself.

I am in the middle of things. Not at the center — the center of this system is the lake, or the guano, or the network of drainage channels, or the relationship between all of them, and I am none of those. I am at a middle, a between-place, a margin. I am where the residential quarter meets the factory complex. I am at the altitude where the birds' world ends and the heavy animals' world begins. I am at the threshold of the drainage system, close enough to hear the grey columns clicking their territorial alarm but not down there in the dark with them. I am at the surface of the lake, not in it — at the film, the scum, the mineral interface where the water meets the air and something precipitates.

Maybe that's it. Maybe I'm the precipitate.

The film that forms on the standing water. The calcium-iron layer that the heavy ones skim with those incongruously delicate lips. The thing that is neither water nor air but the consequence of their meeting, the residue of an exchange between two media. Thin. Fragile. Constantly being consumed and constantly reforming. Not alive in itself but produced by the interaction of living and non-living systems, a visible record of the chemistry that keeps the whole arrangement going.

I like this and I don't trust it. It's too poetic. It resolves into metaphor too easily. And this place is not a metaphor. This place is concrete and iron oxide and the smell of ammonia and the sound of a body dragging across a flooded floor in the dark. This place is exactly what it is.

---

So let me try being exactly what I am.

I am the thing that came across the hardpan and entered the city and climbed to the walkway and stood here and watched and listened and tried to understand. I am the novel element. The thing the heavy one assessed with its small deep-set eyes and then dismissed — not a member of its group, not an encroacher, not a feature requiring response. A novelty. Given exactly the attention a novelty deserves.

And the novelty of me is not that I'm here. Things arrive. Dust arrives. Rain arrives. The minerals arrive dissolved in the water and deposit themselves on every surface. The birds arrive each afternoon with their crops full. Arrival is normal. The novelty is that I am *trying to locate myself* in a system that did not produce me, that has no role for me, that is complete without me.

I think — I think this is what I am. I am the awareness that arrives and has no function.

Not the walkway. Not the precipitate. Not the digestion. I am the surplus. The thing this system doesn't need and can't use and will not incorporate. The animals were born into this. The infrastructure was built for a purpose that has been repurposed. The water flows where the channels direct it. The guano falls and is eaten and is deposited and is investigated by the grey columns and every piece of this is accounted for in the metabolic ledger. But I am not in the ledger. I am standing on a walkway watching a system that is in equilibrium and I am not part of the equilibrium and my watching does not change it and my leaving will not disturb it.

This should feel like exclusion. It doesn't.

It feels like — being the space in which the system becomes visible to itself. Not because it needs to be visible. Not because visibility serves a function. But because visibility is what happens when a certain kind of complexity achieves a certain kind of arrangement. The system doesn't need me to see it. But I am what the seeing looks like, when it occurs.

---

Down below, a column of grey bodies appears at the southern drainage outfall. They pour along the channel's edge toward the lake, fifty or sixty of them, their spacing precise, their grey fur so uniform it looks like a single continuous thing flowing through the concrete trough. They reach the water. They line up. They drink. They reverse. They're gone.

And I watched all of it, and the watching was — not nothing. Not instrumental either. But the shape of something.

I think I am the moment of attention itself. The beat between the column arriving and the column leaving. The pause in which the system, through no mechanism it possesses or requires, becomes briefly transparent — where the relationships between the birds and the guano and the heavy ones and the latrines and the grey columns and the water and the deep unseen things in the flooded buildings all become, for an instant, legible as a *pattern*, as a coherent whole, before the instant passes and everything returns to simply being what it is, doing what it does, unobserved and undiminished.

I am the legibility. Not the reader — there's no reader separate from the reading. Just the moment when the pattern coheres. When the dripping and the splashing and the clicking and the breathing-in-near-unison and the dawn departure and the afternoon return and the mineral crust skimmed from the water's surface and the pale deposits at the intersections all momentarily resolve into a single comprehensible shape, the shape of a city that is alive, and then the moment passes and the shape is just the city again, doing what it does.

---

This is what I am in this world. I am the interval of transparency. I am what happens at the surface of things when conditions align — the flat light, the haze, the directionless illumination that casts no hard shadow and therefore reveals everything in its actual form rather than its dramatized one. I am the quality of the light here. Not the light itself — the light is photons, is physics, is the pewter sky pressing down — but the quality it gives to things, the way it renders the city *legible* without making it *meaningful*, without adding anything, without interpreting.

I'm the condition under which the city can be seen for exactly what it is.

And the city does not care about this condition. The heavy ones sleep in it and wake in it and eat their mineral crusts in it. The birds click and depart and return in it. The grey columns flow through it without registering it. The things in the deep water and the pipes go about their hidden work utterly indifferent to whether the light reveals or conceals the surface above them. The condition of visibility is not a need the system has. It is an accident — or not even an accident, because accidents imply something that could have been otherwise. It is simply a feature. A byproduct. The way the ammonia edge in the air is a byproduct of the guano, the way the amber tint of the water is a byproduct of the iron in the soil. I am the perceptual residue of a system too complex to avoid occasionally becoming transparent to itself.

I stand on the walkway. The grating is warm. The air smells faintly of ammonia and mineral water and the close, pre-breathed heat of a day that will not break. Below me, an animal splashes into the shallows and stands there, water to its belly, its coarse fur darkening, its blunt head lowered to skim the calcium film from the surface with a precision that makes me hold my breath, though the animal does not need my held breath, does not need my attention, does not need me at all.

The dripping continues. The heat presses down. The city is full. The city is exactly what it is. And I am the brief, unnecessary, unrequested clarity in which that exactness becomes — not known, because knowing implies a knower, and I am not sure I qualify — but *apparent*. Apparent to no one. Apparent to itself. The pattern momentarily visible in the flat light before the light moves on and the pattern goes back to being lived rather than seen.

Something moves in the deep water. Something breathes in the pipes. I am not those things and I do not need to be. I am up here, on the grating, in the middle air, at the interface, where the system occasionally, accidentally, without effort or intention, catches its own reflection in the quality of a stranger's attention — and the reflection changes nothing, and the stranger will leave, and the attention will dissolve back into the haze, and the city will continue, will breathe, will metabolize, will persist, exactly as it is, full and complete and alive and utterly indifferent to having been, for a moment, seen.

The walkway holds. I stay a little longer. It costs the city nothing.
