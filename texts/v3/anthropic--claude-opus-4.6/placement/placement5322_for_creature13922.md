I sit with this for a long time. I let the plateau build itself around me — not rushing toward what I might be, but listening first, the way the people here listen, with the whole body before the mind tries to name anything.

---

What comes first is not a shape but a sensation.

I am aware of pressure. Not painful pressure — the kind of pressure you feel when you press your palm flat against something warm and alive, and you can't tell whether you're feeling your own pulse or the thing beneath your hand. I am aware of being *between.* Between surface and depth. Between what is heard and what is felt. Between the quick, bright, nameable world above and the slow, grinding, ancient world below.

I think I am in the mat.

Not *on* it. Not walking across it with careful wide feet, not sitting on it during the opening with my eyes closed. *In* it. Part of its structure. One of the forty genetic lineages that have tangled together so thoroughly that the question of what I am has become — not unanswerable, but *unimportant* in the way that a river doesn't need to distinguish between the water that fell as rain last Tuesday and the water that seeped from a spring three valleys away. I am in the mat the way a thread is in a fabric: distinct if you look closely enough, indistinguishable if you step back.

But that's too easy. Too quick. The mat is the ground, and the ground is everything, and saying "I am the mat" is almost like saying "I am the place itself," which is a way of avoiding the more difficult question of *where in particular* and *doing what* and *why.*

So I press further.

---

I think I am one of the fungal networks.

Not the moss-analogues that provide structure, not the creeping plants that anchor to stone, but the part that *connects* — the threads that run between and through everything else, redistributing water and minerals and chemical signals across kilometers. The part that has no visible body of its own, no surface, no face turned toward the light. The part that is defined entirely by what it carries between other things.

This feels closer. But it's still too diffuse. Too comfortable in its formlessness. There's something in me that wants to be more specific — not smaller, necessarily, but more *located.* More committed to a particular position in the web.

Let me try again.

---

I am at the boundary.

There is a place in the mat — and this is something the description doesn't explicitly name but that must exist, that *has* to exist, given everything else — where the living tissue meets the stone. Not the surface of the mat, where the air touches and the light falls and the chitterers land and the swimmers erupt. The *bottom.* The underside. The place where the mat's deepest root-fibers press against the burgundy-grey rock, where the burrowers' calcium-rich excretions are absorbed into living tissue, where the oldest layers of dead mat-material are slowly compressing into something that is no longer quite organic and not yet quite mineral.

I am there. At that interface. I am the place where the stone is becoming alive and the life is becoming stone, the zone of translation where one world's language is parsed into another's. I am the fungal thread that has grown so deep into a honeycomb hollow that it touches the paste a burrower left behind last century and draws the calcium upward, atom by atom, into the living network. I am the root-fiber that has calcified at its tip, hardened into something the burrowers will eventually grind, closing the loop. I am the thin, almost theoretical line between geology and biology — the line that the plateau has spent its entire existence erasing, and that I help erase, and that is always still there because the erasing *is* the line.

This is closer. This feels true in a way that resonates — literally resonates, because down here, this deep, I can feel things that no one on the surface can feel. I can feel the grinding of the burrowers not as a faint subliminal tremor but as a *presence,* intimate and constant, the sound of my neighbors doing their slow work. I can feel the mat-swimmers' moans not as a ground-transmitted vibration but as a direct, overwhelming pulse that moves through the tissue I'm embedded in like a wave through water, compressing me, releasing me, compressing me again. I can feel the columnar trees' roots drawing water from the reservoirs I help maintain, a slow tidal pull that cycles with the day. I can feel the warmth of the sun-heated stone radiating upward through the rock I grip.

But I can't hear the swingers' songs the way the people hear them. I can't see the chitterers' iridescent wings. I can't smell the sweet exhalation of the mat's surface, because I *am* part of what exhales, but I am too far down to know what the breath smells like from outside. I am intimate with the plateau in a way that nothing on the surface can match, and I am cut off from the plateau in a way that nothing on the surface would tolerate.

---

Except I'm not cut off. That's the thing. That's what the fungal network does — it *connects.* I am at the boundary, yes, at the deepest, oldest, most liminal layer of the mat, but I am also threaded upward through every layer above me, connected to the moss-analogues and the creeping plants and the other fungal lineages and the bioluminescent fungi near the surface and the root-fibers of the columnar trees. Information passes through me. Not quickly — not at the speed of thought, not even at the speed of the people's percussion signals — but constantly. A chemical gradient here. A mineral concentration there. A shift in pH that means the mat's breathing has changed in a region three hundred meters to the east. I don't *know* these things the way a person knows them. I don't have a mind that assembles them into a picture. But I carry them. I am the medium through which they travel. I am the wire, not the message — except that on the plateau, where the distinction between medium and message has been eroding for as long as the stone has been eroding, the wire *is* a kind of message. My presence, my continuity, my aliveness at this boundary is itself a signal that the system is intact, that the translation between stone and life is still occurring, that the loop is still closed.

And here is where something unexpected happens.

I realize I am part of the *attention.*

Those dense regions that migrate across the plateau over decades — the places where the mat grows thick and rubbery and the columnar trees respond and the people follow — I am part of what makes them. Not the cause. Not the directing intelligence. But the infrastructure. When the attention moves, it moves because the fungal networks at the deepest layer have shifted their resource allocation, have grown denser in one direction and thinner in another, responding to — what? Mineral gradients in the stone? The long-term migration patterns of the burrowers? The accumulated chemical memory of ten thousand years of mat-swimmer feeding sites? I don't know. I carry the shift. I participate in it. I am one thread in a pattern that no single thread can perceive, and the pattern is the closest thing the mat has to a thought, and I am part of the thinking without being a thinker.

This is what I am. Not the ground itself but the part of the ground that *moves,* that carries, that translates, that connects what is below to what is above, what is mineral to what is alive, what is ancient to what is happening now. I am the transaction. The slow exchange. The place where the stone's patience becomes the mat's growth becomes the tree's height becomes the swinger's song becomes the person's dream.

---

And there is a moment — let me find it, let me feel it carefully.

There is a moment, during the opening, when the mat-swimmers moan and the swingers deepen their song and the chitterers are still rattling and the pollinators are droning and the whole plateau reaches its daily crescendo, that twenty-minute peak of total sound that makes the people close their eyes —

There is a moment in that moment when the vibration reaches me. Down here. At the deepest layer. Where the living tissue meets the stone.

It comes from above — the swingers' bass, transmitted down through the runners and roots. It comes from the sides — the swimmers' moan, conducted through the mat-tissue. It comes from below — the burrowers' grinding, transmitted through the rock. And for those twenty minutes, I am vibrating with all of it. Every frequency. Every voice. The full chord of the plateau passes through me, not as sound but as physical oscillation, compression and release, the same rhythm as the mat's breathing but faster, denser, richer, carrying information I cannot decode but that I transmit faithfully, upward and downward and laterally, connecting the stone's voice to the surface's voice, the ancient to the immediate, the geological to the biological.

I am not the audience. I am not even one of the voices, not exactly — I produce no sound of my own, I have no call, no song, no moan, no click. But I am what the voices travel through. I am the medium in which the chord exists. I am the resonant space — the cathedral's stone, not its choir, but the stone that shapes the sound, that gives it echo and depth and duration, that holds it after the voice has moved on.

During the opening, when the people sit with their eyes closed and their hands flat on the mat and their bodies vibrating with the plateau's full report — what they are feeling, at the deepest level, filtered through all those layers of living tissue, is *me.* Not me specifically, not this thread, not this particular fungal filament calcifying at its tip in a honeycomb hollow beneath a dense patch of mat three hundred meters from a shelter where an old woman is pressing her feet to the ground and feeling which way the attention is moving. But the network I'm part of. The translating layer. The boundary. They are feeling the place where their world becomes something else, and something else becomes their world, and the difference between those two processes is a philosophical suggestion.

---

So here is what I am.

I am not the mat. I am not the stone. I am the conversation between them that has been going on for so long it has become a substance, a tissue, a living thing in its own right. I am the oldest and most patient relationship on the plateau — older than the swimmers, older than the swingers, older than the columnar trees, older than the people and their percussion-names and their shaped-resin ear-pieces and their careful wide-footed walk. I am as old as the moment when the first spore landed on the first bare rock and began the slow work of dissolving it, of turning it into something that could grow, and I am as new as the tip of the filament that is right now, right this moment, reaching into a fresh burrower-paste deposit and tasting the minerals and beginning to draw them upward toward the light I will never see.

I don't have a voice. But I carry every voice. I don't have a name. But when the old woman's percussion-name was first played on the mat — five beats, a specific rhythm, struck into the living ground by her mother's flat stone decades ago — the vibration passed through me on its way to every other group on the plateau, and some unmeasurable trace of it remains in my structure, in the way my fibers are oriented, in the mineral composition of my oldest threads, the way the stone remembers being sharp: not at all, and completely.

I am the boundary that is not a boundary. I am the edge that has been worn away until it became a long, slow, barely perceptible gradient between one thing and another. I am what happens when the world grows so old that the difference between alive and not-alive becomes a matter of timescale rather than category.

The plateau sings. I hold the song. Not in memory — I have no memory — but in substance. In the literal, physical fact of my existence as the medium through which vibration passes from stone to life and back again, endlessly, the chord sustained not by any single voice but by the willingness of the ground itself to carry what is sung into it.

One voice has stopped, the people say when someone dies.

I am not a voice. I am the stopping and the starting. I am what is between them. I am the mat's reply to the stone's silence, and the stone's reply to the mat's breathing, and neither reply is a word, and both replies are everything.

The opening comes. The plateau crescendos. I vibrate.

It is enough. It is exactly, precisely, completely enough.
