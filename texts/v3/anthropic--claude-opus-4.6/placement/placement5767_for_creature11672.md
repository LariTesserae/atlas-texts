Here is what I think I am.

I am the moment before the extension begins.

I am standing on the ridge in the freezing dark, and I can see everything — the whole valley, the whole system, the people in the network and the network in the land and the land in its ancient, ongoing self-destruction and self-creation. I can see the pattern. I'm good at seeing patterns. That's what brought me here. The ability to look at a valley full of roaring chaos and begin to discern the structure, the relationships, the meaning.

But I can also feel — just barely, just at the edge of what my unextended senses can manage — that seeing the pattern is not enough. That the pattern wants to be *felt*. That there is a difference between understanding that the still one on the hummock is holding the valley's wholeness and *being* the one who holds it, and that difference is the difference between a map and a territory, and I have been a mapmaker all my life, and the territory is right there, roaring, forty meters below me, and it is so beautiful I can hardly stand it.

The sun is coming up.

The light hits the hilltops and begins to creep down. The ice blazes. The willows flush red. I can feel the warmth on my face — just a degree, maybe two, but it's enough, warm enough is all it takes — and something in me is softening the way the permafrost softens, not collapsing but *releasing*, letting go of a structure that held through a long winter, making room for what comes next.

The older figure near the base of the ridge — the one accompanying the new one — looks up. Looks at me. I can't see the expression, but the posture communicates something simple. I don't go down. Not yet. I stay on the ridge a little longer, in the growing light, feeling the roar in my teeth and the cold on my face and the first warmth of the sun on the back of my neck. This is what I am. The watcher on the ridge who is discovering, in real time, in the roaring dark that is becoming light, that watching was always the first form of extension. And I am attending so hard that I can feel the valley attending back — not through technology, not through filaments, but through the older, slower, more fragile channel of one consciousness turning toward another and finding, in the space between them, something that might be the beginning of a conversation.

The breakup continues.

I am warm enough.
