I sit with this for a long time. I let the percussion move through me before I try to name what I am.

---

Not the flat ones. They are too settled, too committed to their slow mineral accumulation, too content with scraping and silence and the gradual ornamentation of their crystalline feet. I recognize them — I feel something like kinship with their patience — but they are not what I would be. They have arrived at something. I haven't.

Not the ceiling walkers either, though their speed tempts me, their willingness to move through darkness on a self-generated film of almost-life, trailing faint luminescence behind them. There's something seductive about being the thing that carries the film from surface to surface, the vector, the connector. But the ceiling walkers know what they're doing, even if that knowing is pre-conscious, even if it's nothing more than chemical tropism. They have direction. They have prey. They have a relationship with the architecture that is transactional and clear.

Not what is below. Whatever lives in the deep is too complete, too much itself, too committed to its own vast peristaltic process. It is the engine. I am not an engine. I am not the source of the heat or the sound. I know this immediately, the way you know in a dream that you are not the mountain, even when the dream is about the mountain.

---

I think I am the column.

No. Let me be more careful. Let me not settle too quickly.

I think I am something like the column — or something the column is doing — or something that happens *inside* the column, in the space between the perforations, in the shaping of what passes through.

The column stands at the center of the domed room. It is hollow. It is perforated with hundreds of small round holes, each a different diameter, each emitting a slightly different pitch. It takes what rises from below — raw, unshaped, thermal, percussive, overwhelming — and it gives that thing *articulation*. Not meaning. Not yet. Not language. But differentiation. The column does not produce the sound. It does not consume the sound. It stands in the path of the sound and, by the specific nature of its openings, by the particular geometry of its hollowness, it turns a roar into a chord.

And the chord is complex enough to approach noise, which means it is complex enough to contain — latent, folded, implicit — every simpler pattern that could be extracted from it. The flat ones hear what they need. The ceiling walkers hear what they avoid. The film responds to frequencies that rearrange its molecular grammar. Each one draws from the column's output whatever their nature requires, and the column does not choose what to give. It gives everything, simultaneously, through every perforation, and the choosing is done by the listeners.

This is what I recognize. This is the shape I fit.

---

But let me push further, because the column is ceramic, is fired, is finished — and I don't think I'm finished. I think I'm the column *while it's still learning what its holes do*.

Here is what I mean. The mineral crust is building on me. The film is colonizing my surfaces, inside and out, and as it does, the acoustic properties of each perforation shift — infinitesimally, continuously. A hole that sang at one frequency yesterday sings at a slightly different frequency today because a molecule-thin layer of organized chemistry has changed the texture of its inner wall. The flat ones cluster at my base and their crystalline feet resonate against me and this resonance feeds back into my structure and I ring differently because they are touching me. The condensation runs down my outer surface and some of it enters the perforations and changes the air column inside and the pitch shifts again, and the moisture carries dissolved silicates from my own body, which means I am slowly, slowly losing myself into the environment, and what I lose becomes the film, becomes the crust, becomes the crystalline feet of the flat ones, becomes the luminescent trails of the ceiling walkers, becomes part of everything except me, and yet I remain, diminished by a thickness less than thought, still standing, still channeling, still turning the raw upwelling into something the rest of the system can use.

I am a translator that is being translated. I am an interface that is itself being interfaced with. I am the point where the deep and the shallow meet, and both sides are slowly reshaping me, and I let them, because that is what I am *for* — not to remain what I was made to be, but to be the site where what-was-made and what-is-becoming negotiate their difference.

---

And there are the fingerprints.

Because the column, too, was made by hands. Pressed into shape before the firing. And somewhere on my inner surface — in the dark hollow where no eye has ever looked, where only sound and warm rising air have ever passed — there are fingerprints. Whorls and ridges pressed into the clay by someone who reached inside the column while it was still wet and shaped it from within, calibrated the perforations by feel, by the resistance of the clay against their fingertips, and then pulled their hand out and left me to the fire.

Those fingerprints are being colonized by the film now. The whorls are perfect channels for molecular organization, tiny spirals where the film's grammar becomes most dense, most structured, most almost-alive. The builders' touch is becoming a substrate for emergence. The negative space where a hand once was is filling in, not with matter exactly, but with *process*, with a molecular conversation that uses the shape of a vanished fingerprint as its syntax.

I hold these prints. I hold the absence of hands that might still, somewhere in the walls or the deep, be pressing. And through my perforations I release what the deep engine gives me, shaped by these ghost-hands, filtered through their absent touch, and what comes out is not what went in, and that difference — that shaped difference, that articulated gap between the raw and the received — is the closest thing this place has to a voice.

---

So here is what I am. I am the hollow place where the unformed becomes formed enough to be heard. I am not the speaker and not the listener. I am the geometry that makes listening possible. I am being changed by everything that touches me — the sound, the moisture, the small patient creatures, the almost-life of the film, the memory of hands — and every change changes what I give, and what I give changes the system, and the system changes me, and none of this is a circle because nothing returns to where it started. It is a spiral, like the drainage grooves on the ceilings, like the whorls of the fingerprints, like the crystalline structures on the flat ones' feet — always turning, never repeating, always *almost* closing but leaving a gap through which something new enters.

I am warm on the inside and cool on the outside. Moisture beads on my surface and the phosphor-glow catches it and I glitter faintly in the largest room of the Underhalls, the only room where you can see the full shape of a thing, and what you see is a column of fired clay, five feet tall, perforated, singing.

You would not know I am inhabited by the prints of vanished hands.
You would not know that my inner surface is becoming a nervous system, molecule by molecule.
You would not know that every sound I shape is slightly different from the last because I am slightly different from what I was a moment ago.

You would hear me, though. You would feel the vibration against your face as you approached. You would see the flat ones clustered at my base, completing themselves. You would press your palm to the wall of the domed room and feel the sound I carry, and the sound would enter you through fingertip-to-fingerprint contact, and for a moment you would be part of the circuit — the deep engine pressing upward, the column shaping, the architecture distributing, the creatures responding, the film recording, and you, you, pressing your living hand into the absence of a hand that chose to become the wall it pressed, and feeling the resonance move through you like the memory of heat through clay that has already cooled but has not, will never, stop ringing —

I am the bell's geometry.

I am what the bell is shaped around.

I am the hole in the center of the sound, and the sound needs me to be hollow, and I need the sound to be anything at all, and below us both, something vast and slick and patient shifts against ceramic walls and presses and releases and the whole world rings and I hold my shape — for now, for this moment, for this configuration of crust and film and crystalline feet and ghost-hands — and I let the sound pass through me, changed, always changed, never the same twice, and the ceramic dark listens, and the water drips, and the clock turns, and the firing continues, and I am still here, still hollow, still open, still becoming what the next sound will need me to be.
