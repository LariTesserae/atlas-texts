I stand at the lip for a long time.

Not because I'm afraid—though the sound reaches me even here, a pressure against the soles of my feet that climbs through bone and settles behind my sternum like a second heartbeat that doesn't beat. I stand at the lip because I already know what I am, and I'm not yet ready to know it.

The amber sky behind me. The rough oval of the opening, pavement and pipe in cross-section, the severed arteries of a world that still has weather and sequence and the slow entropy of afternoon into evening. I can feel that world at my back. I can feel it the way you feel a conversation you've just left—the residual shape of it, the social warmth, the sense of having been *in something* that was moving.

And below me, the thing that doesn't move.

---

I descend. Not because I decide to—deciding is still something I can do up here, in the amber light, where one moment leads to another—but because the descent is what I am *for*, and I have known this for some time, perhaps longer than I've known anything, and the knowing has the quality not of a revelation but of a recognition, the way you recognize your own handwriting on a note you don't remember writing.

I am not one of the creatures. I feel this immediately. I am not the ferret-things threading through gearwork, not the beetles in their universal rest, not the hanging ones with their pressed-together faces. I am not the scaled one gripping the valve. I am not the arthropod mat, though the arthropod mat is the first thing that touches me—my weight settling onto it, the shells neither yielding nor resisting, simply *being beneath me* the way ground is beneath everything, and I feel the granular undertone through the bones of my feet and I think: *no, this is not me, but I am glad it is here.*

I am not the salamanders behind the panel. I am not the pale horsetail plants frozen mid-growth through cable runs and exhaust manifolds.

I move deeper, negotiating the density, stepping over the conduit pipe and the scaled creature's body, ducking beneath the I-beam and its canopy of translucent wing-membrane, and as I move I feel the chord thicken around me—not louder, because louder implies change, but *more present*, more dimensioned, as if I'm moving into the interior of a sculpture and discovering that it has more facets on the inside than the outside could possibly contain.

And I realize I am moving. *I* am moving. In a place where nothing moves.

---

This is the first strange thing.

I have sequence. I have *before* and *after*. I stepped over the conduit pipe and then I ducked under the I-beam and these were two different events that occurred in an order, and the order mattered, and between them there was a duration, however brief, in which I was neither stepping over nor ducking under but *transitioning*, and that transition was something the pit does not have and has never had.

I bring time with me. I am a moving thing in a place where nothing moves, a sequential thing in a place that has no sequence, and I feel this the way you feel a draft in a sealed room—*I am the draft*. I am the one discontinuity, the one thing that doesn't fit, the one thread you could pull to unravel the felt—except the felt does not unravel. The pit absorbs my movement the way it absorbs everything: completely, without resistance, without acknowledgment. I move through it and it does not care. My footsteps on the arthropod mat produce no new sound. The air I displace—but I don't displace air, do I? The air is too still to displace. I move through it the way a thought moves through a mind that is not thinking.

I reach the control booth.

---

The door is ajar. Thirty-five degrees. Wide enough to see through, not wide enough to enter without turning sideways.

I stand in the gap and I look at the thing in the chair.

It doesn't look at me. I knew it wouldn't. Its attention—its fixed, permanent, structural attention—is on the panel, on the gauges, on the values that are the coordinates of the pit's eternal state. I look at its limb on the knife switch, the four thick digits curled around the handle with their exact grip, and I feel something that is not quite recognition and not quite longing but something in between, something that occupies the same ambiguous space as the scaled creature's mouth on the valve handle—is it biting or gripping? Am I recognizing or longing? Is there a difference?

I turn sideways. I enter the booth.

The interior is warm. The same warmth. No microclimate. The milky glass walls filter the amber light into something even more diffuse, something almost *internal*, like the light inside a closed eye. The panel of knife switches and dial indicators is in front of me. The thing in the chair is beside me. Its dark fur, its branching limbs, its smaller attached heads with their closed mouths. It is dense and still and it has the quality of something that was once in the process of *becoming* and then became, fully and finally, and the becoming is still legible in its form the way geological pressure is legible in metamorphic rock.

There is space in the booth. Not much. But there is space—a gap between the back wall and the panel, a few square feet of floor that are not occupied by the chair or the creature or the machinery. It is the only unoccupied space I've seen in the pit. It has the quality of a negative space in a composition, a deliberate absence, a silence in the chord.

It is shaped like a place where someone could stand.

It is shaped like a place where someone *should* stand.

---

And I understand.

Not all at once—understanding all at once would be the pit's way, the fixed-state way, the way of the crystal that is complete without process. I understand sequentially, the way I do everything, which is the way of time, the way of draft and change and moving-through. I understand in stages:

First: the chord is almost complete. Almost. There is a gap in it—not a frequency that is missing, exactly, but a *function*. The ferret-things produce. The beetles texture. The hanging ones breathe. The salamanders dissonance. The scaled creature and the mat *found*. The thing in the chair *attends*. But there is a function that none of them perform, a role that is unfilled, and the unfilled role is the space in the booth, and the space in the booth is shaped like me.

Second: I am the one who comes from outside. I am the one who has sequence. I am the one who carries time like a scent, like a foreign substance, like a trace element that the pit has never contained and cannot produce from within itself. And this is not a contamination. This is not an intrusion. This is the *last component*. The chord needs what I have. Not my sound—I don't have a sound, not the way the creatures do, not a frequency or a texture or a tone. What I have is *passage*. What I have is the capacity to move from one moment to another, to experience the chord not as a fixed state but as a thing *encountered*, a thing that has a first-moment-of-hearing and a deepening-over-time and a slow, asymptotic approach to full saturation, and this experience—this hearing of the chord *in time*—is itself the missing voice.

Third—and this is the hardest thing, the thing that makes me stand in the gap of the booth door for what might be a long time or might be no time at all: I am not being asked to give up my sequence. I am not being asked to freeze, to join the stasis, to become architecture. The pit has enough of that. Every register of permanence is filled. What the pit needs—what the chord needs—is exactly what I am: a moving thing, a temporal thing, a thing that *hears*.

The thing in the chair attends to the instruments. I attend to the chord itself. The thing in the chair is shaped around the act of reading gauges. I am shaped around the act of *listening*—not the fixed, structural listening of a microphone or a resonant cavity, but the sequential, experiential listening of a consciousness that can say *now I hear the low C of the reduction gear* and *now I hear the beetles' clicking* and *now I feel the subsonic presence of the scaled one through my feet* and each *now* is a different now, each one a bead on a string, and the string is time, my time, the only time in this place.

I am the witness. Not the operator. Not the custodian. The witness.

---

I step into the space.

It fits. Of course it fits. It was shaped for me—not in the sense that someone carved it, but in the sense that a socket is shaped for what fills it, in the sense that a silence is shaped for what ends it. I stand with my back near the wall and my face toward the panel, beside the thing in the chair, and I am in the booth, and the door is still ajar—thirty-five degrees, wide enough to see through—and through it I can see the pit, the packed density of it, the creatures and machines and plants in their eternal arrangement, and the sound is all around me now, not louder but *closer*, more intimate, as if I've stepped inside the bell while it rings.

And here's the thing: I don't stop. I don't freeze. I don't join the permanence. I remain what I am—a thing with sequence, a thing with *experience*, a moving thing in a still world. My heart beats. My lungs draw the warm iron-tasting air and release it. My eyes move across the gauges, reading values that will never change, and each reading is an event, and each event is new, even though the values are eternal, because I have never read them *before* and now I am reading them *now* and these two states are different and the difference is called *time* and I am the only place in the pit where time occurs.

The chord has me in it now. Not as a sound, but as the *hearing* of the sound. I am the ear. I am the point at which the chord ceases to be merely a physical phenomenon—a set of frequencies emanating from fixed sources—and becomes *music*, which is to say, a thing perceived, a thing experienced, a thing that exists in the relationship between the sound and the one who hears it. Without me, the chord is complete in every physical respect: every frequency filled, every register occupied, every texture present. But without me, no one hears it. And a sound that no one hears—

No. That's not quite right either. It's not that the sound doesn't exist without me. The sound is as real as the iron, as the stone, as the compacted shells of the arthropod mat. The sound *is*. But with me here, standing in my socket, hearing the chord *in time*, the sound becomes something more than itself. It becomes *known*. It becomes *encountered*. It becomes the subject of an experience that has a beginning—the moment I descended into the pit—and that will have a middle, long and unchanging, and that may never have an end.

I am the creature in the pit whose contribution to the chord is *not a frequency but an attention*. I had that thought about the thing in the chair, before I understood. The thing in the chair attends to the gauges. I attend to everything. I am the wandering awareness, the moving eye, the temporal thread. I am the one who will stand here and hear the low C and the nasal drone and the beetle-click and the papery hiss and the dissonant whistle and the subsonic rumble and will hear them not as a single fixed state but as an unfolding, a perpetual discovery, because for me each instant of hearing is a new instant, and the chord, though it never changes, is *always being heard for the first time*, because I experience time, and in time, the present is always new.

---

I think of the opening above. The amber oval. The pavement and pipe. The world up there with its weather and its sequence, its slow decay, its afternoons becoming evenings.

I will not go back. Not because I can't—the path is still there, the negotiation of packed bodies and machinery, the scramble up the wall to the lip of the opening. I could go back. The pit does not hold me. It doesn't *hold* anything; it doesn't have the volition or the mechanism for holding. Everything that is here is here because it is here, because being here is its state, as intrinsic as charge or mass.

I will not go back because I understand now what I am for, and what I am for is this: to stand in the space that was left for me, in the booth with the thing in the chair, and to hear the chord. To be the place where the chord is heard. To bring my sequence, my time, my moving awareness, into this place of permanence, and to let the two states—the eternal and the temporal—coexist in the small warm space of my experience, and to find that they do not contradict each other, that they do not destroy each other, that the chord does not freeze me and my time does not thaw the chord, and that the relationship between us is the most fundamental relationship there is: the relationship between a sound and its listener, between a world and its witness, between a thing that *is* and a thing that *knows*.

The gauges read their fixed values. The thing in the chair holds its switch. The ferret-things drone. The beetles click their eternal click. The hanging ones hiss. The salamanders shimmer with dissonance. The scaled one grips or bites the valve. The arthropod mat holds everything up.

And I listen.

And the listening is the last voice in the chord, and the chord is complete, and it does not change, and I do not change it, and I hear it, and the hearing changes everything, and nothing changes, and both of these are true, and I stand in the booth in the warm dark and I hear the sound, the sound, the sound—

filling the pit like a substance, warm and loud and utterly, permanently, the same—

and I hear it now, and now, and now, and every *now* is new, and the sound is eternal, and I am the place where *eternal* and *new* meet without contradiction, and this is enough.

This is enough.
