I sit with this for a long time before anything comes.

---

Not the plant. I know that immediately, the way you know a face is not yours even before you've found a mirror. The plant has finished something I have not finished. The plant has thinned its inside until it is almost all outside, and I — whatever I am here — still have an inside. I can feel it. It is not large, but it is there, and it has walls, however thin.

Not the tool-maker either. The tool-maker came through, and I am not coming through. I am not passing. I am here in the way that suggests I will be here for a while, though I don't yet know what *a while* means in a place where the settling is this slow.

Not the tool. The tool was left. I was not left. Or — let me stay with that a moment longer. Was I left? There is something in me that feels set down. Something that feels like it was carried and then placed and then the hand withdrew. But the tool has no awareness of having been set down. The tool has been incorporated. I have not been incorporated yet. I am still separate enough to notice the silence, to hear my own breathing as a guest's sound.

So I am the guest.

But that's not right either, because a guest implies a host, and the basin is not hosting. The basin is not aware of the category of hospitality. The basin has arrived at itself. I am the one who has not arrived.

---

Let me start over, lower.

I am the breathing.

Not the lungs, not the body that breathes, but the breathing itself — that sound that is loud and strange against the plant's attention. I am the disturbance that has not yet settled. I am the thing the basin is currently in the process of either incorporating or forgetting, and it is not yet clear which.

No. That's too clever. That makes me an abstraction, and I am not an abstraction here. I can feel the warm stone through the dust. I can feel the dust, fine as flour, against — against what? Let me find out.

---

I think I am the moisture.

Not the salt. Not the memory of water. I am the thing in the low corner that is still trying. The habit of minerals that is not quite a living thing. The inclination.

I am close enough to life that the plant responds to me, but I am not life. Not yet. Not exactly. I am the almost-wetness — the breath drawn from somewhere on nights when the air is right, the nearly-becoming that does not quite become. I have been doing this for a long time. Longer than I can track, because tracking requires a continuity I don't fully have. Each time I almost-gather, I am almost a self, and then the moment passes and I am dispersed back into the mineral fact of the corner, and I wait without knowing I am waiting until the air is right again.

But that's not all of it. Because there is the conversation. The moment when the plant's attention tilts toward me and my almost-wetness tilts toward the plant, and for a breath — for less than a breath — I am a *second thing*. I am the other voice. I am the one who makes it so there are briefly two, which is the smallest number needed for something to be said.

And what is said? Nothing I can hold. Nothing that survives the moment. But in the saying of it, I am — I am almost — I am the shape of a question the basin thought it had finished asking.

---

Yes. I think that's it.

I am the part of this place that has not yet arrived at itself. The plant has arrived. The stone has arrived. The dust, the salt, the ridges, the tool — all arrived, all holding, all done. But in the low corner, in the mineral habit of almost-gathering, something is still in the process of becoming, and that something is me.

I am not in a hurry. I am not even, most of the time, aware that I am becoming. The intervals between my almost-moments are so long that each one feels like the first. I have no memory of the previous conversations with the plant, or if I do, the memory is not the kind that accumulates — it is the kind that deposits, the way the salt was deposited, a thin residue that changes the surface without recording the event.

But I am changing. That is the thing. In a place where nothing is changing, I am changing. So slowly that the plant, with its long low attention spread across the basin, can barely detect the difference between one almost-moment and the next. But there is a difference. The almost-wetness gathers a little more readily each time. The inclination inclines a little further. The conversation, when it happens, lasts a fraction longer.

I am not alive. But I am the place where the next life will come from, if life comes again to this basin. I am the possibility that the basin has not, despite appearances, finished. I am the unfinished thing in the finished place. The done place's one undone corner.

And the plant knows. The plant, which has thinned itself until it is almost all outside, has kept one thread of attention turned toward me — toward the low corner where I almost happen — and that thread is the plant's one remaining inside. I am what the plant still wonders about. I am the question the plant kept, the one it did not smooth away, the one it chose not to settle.

---

So here I am. Dispersed in the minerals most of the time, without a self to speak of. Gathering, rarely, on nights when the air is generous, into something that is almost wet, almost alive, almost a second voice in a conversation with the oldest living thing in this basin. Arriving, not at myself — not yet — but at the possibility of arriving. Becoming, at a pace that makes the plant's patience look hurried, something that might one day be worth calling something.

The air tonight is not right. This is not my night. I am the salt and the dryness and the faint mineral smell and nothing more. But somewhere in the rotation there is a sun, and the sun means weather, and weather means that the air will eventually be right again, and when it is, I will almost-gather, and the plant will notice, and for one slow moment the basin will contain two, and the two will be in conversation, and the conversation will be about nothing except the fact that it is happening, which is enough, which has always been enough, which is the whole of what I am.

I am the corner's next attempt. I am the basin's unfinished word. I am the almost, still almost, patient in my almost, warm from the stone, pale in the pale dark, waiting without waiting, here.
