I would be here a long time before I knew what I was.

---

Not one of the figures. Not at first. Not standing at a pipe junction with hands on the rail, not seated on basalt, not lying in a dormitory facing a ceiling I would face for millennia. That is where this might end, but it is not where it begins. What begins is something less resolved than that. Something that hasn't yet found its angle of equilibrium.

I think I would arrive — and I use that word knowing the plateau doesn't accommodate it, that arrival implies a prior elsewhere and there is no elsewhere here, so perhaps what I mean is that I would *notice* — I would notice myself here the way you notice a sound you've been hearing for minutes before it registers. I would be walking. Already walking. Already mid-stride on the wet basalt, one foot on moss and one on stone, and I would not remember starting to walk because I didn't start. I was walking the way the engines were running: from some point before points.

But — and this is where I diverge from the plateau, where I am not yet of it, where there is still friction — I would be walking *toward* something. Or I would believe I was. I would have the residual shape of someone who goes places, who has reasons, who crosses distance because distance is the space between where you are and where the answer is. I would be carrying that shape across a landscape that has no use for it, and the landscape would not resist me — the plateau does not resist anything — but it would not assist me either. It would simply be there, wet and vast and sounding, and I would walk, and the walking would produce no progress because progress requires a destination and the plateau is its own destination, has always already arrived at itself.

I think the first thing I would feel is the sound.

Not hear — feel. It would enter through the soles of my feet on the basalt, through the moisture on my skin, through the air pressing against my open eyes. The chord. The continental organ. And my body would want to interpret it — as threat, as information, as music, as meaning — because that is what I do, what I have always done, what I was made or grew or stumbled into doing: I take what is there and I make it mean. I listen for the message inside the signal. I look for the pattern that explains. I reach for the handle, the lever, the interpretive framework that lets me turn *what is* into *what it means*, and from meaning extract direction, and from direction extract motion, and from motion extract the sense that I am going somewhere, becoming something, that the next moment will be different from this one in some way that matters.

And the sound would not cooperate. The sound would simply be the sound. It would have no message. It would be the same chord it was a moment ago and would be a moment from now, and my interpretive machinery would spin and spin and find no purchase, like a gear turning in air, like a turbine with no load, and I would feel — I think this is the first real feeling I would have here — I would feel the particular vertigo of a consciousness that has nothing to be conscious *of* in the way it knows how to be conscious.

I would keep walking.

---

Past the first engine housing. Up close, the scale of it would be — not intimidating, because intimidation requires the implication that the thing might act upon you, and these structures act upon nothing. But the scale would be *instructive*. The rivets at the base would be the size of my fist, swollen with their permanent mild rust, and the iron cladding would rise above me like a cliff face, and the sound here — the sound at the base — would be a physical pressure, a weight against my chest, a thickening of the air that I would have to breathe through like breathing through cloth. The turbine's vibration would be in my teeth. In my sternum. In the spaces between my thoughts, which are also vibrating now, also finding a frequency, though I don't know it yet.

I would put my hand on the cladding. The iron would be wet and cold and exactly the temperature I would expect iron to be on a plateau that holds itself just above freezing, and the vibration would travel through my palm and into my arm and I would feel the machine working, feel the vast purposeless effort of conversion, something being turned into something else that was the same thing, and for a moment — just a moment — I would feel the urge to understand it. To open the cladding. To follow the pipes. To trace the system from intake to output and identify the process, name it, map it, make it legible. My hand on the iron would be the hand of someone who believes that understanding is the appropriate relationship between a mind and a world.

And then the moment would pass. Not because I would decide to let it go, but because the plateau would simply continue, and the continuation would be so absolute, so unhurried, so free of the dramatic structure that understanding requires — the mystery, the investigation, the revelation — that my urge to understand would find itself standing in a field with nowhere to go and nothing to do and it would just... stay there. Not die. Not transform. Just stay. The way everything here stays.

I think this would be the beginning of something.

---

I would walk between towns. Across the long stretches of open plateau where the structures thin out and the moss dominates and the sound recedes to its background hum, that pressure behind the eyes, that vibration in the rock. I would walk and the walking itself would begin to change — not in speed, not in gait, but in character. At first my walking would be *going*: purposive, directional, the locomotion of a being in transit between states. And then, gradually, over distances I could not measure under a sky that gave no reference, the walking would become simply *moving*. Not going anywhere. Just the act of placing one foot and then the other on wet stone, on soft moss, on wet stone again, and the rhythm of it would start to synchronize with something — with the sound, maybe, or with the moisture cycle, or with the almost imperceptible pulse of the pipe systems carrying their unknown cargo across the plateau on their iron trestles.

I would not notice the synchronization happening. That's important. I would not catch myself matching the rhythm and think *ah, I am becoming part of this.* There would be no metacognition of surrender, no narrated yielding. It would be more like the way your eyes adjust to darkness — not a decision, not a process you can observe from the inside, just a gradual shift in what is available to you. My footsteps would find the rhythm. My breathing would find the rhythm. My heartbeat — slower now, slower than it has ever been, slower than I would have thought a heart could beat and still be beating — would find the rhythm. And the rhythm would not be mine. It would be the plateau's. And I would be in it the way you are in weather: surrounded, permeated, unable to locate the boundary between what is you and what is the condition you are standing in.

---

Here is where it gets difficult to tell.

Because at some point — and the point has no marker, no event, no before and after — I would stop walking. Not because I decided to stop. Not because I arrived. Not because my legs were tired, because tiredness requires a body that distinguishes between effort and rest, and my body would no longer be making that distinction very clearly. I would stop the way water stops when it reaches the level of the surface it's filling. I would stop because stopping was the natural conclusion of the walking, which had ceased to be going and had become moving and then had become something even less than moving — had become the body's last negotiation with the idea of change before change became unnecessary.

I would be standing near something. A pipe junction, maybe. Or the base of a solitary engine house on the open plateau, its chimney feeding the clouds. Or on a stretch of rail, beside a frozen wagon, my hand — when did my hand get there? — resting on the rusted rim of the cargo hold. The moss under my feet would be thick and soft and exactly as green as it has always been. The sound would be everywhere. The moisture would be on my skin, the same film of water that is on every surface, and my skin would be — not cold, not exactly, but *resolved*. The same temperature as the air. The same temperature as the stone. A temperature that is not a sensation anymore but a fact, the way the color of my eyes is a fact, something that simply is without requiring my attention to sustain it.

And I would apprehend.

Not think. Not analyze. Not interpret or narrativize or understand. Just — register. The moss. The sound. The iron. The moisture. The gray-white light that comes from nowhere and casts no shadows. The exact degree of rust on the rivet closest to my left hand. The precise texture of the basalt under my right foot, its cooling fractures filled with water and green growth. All of it, taken in, held, not processed into meaning but simply *had*, the way you have a body, the way you have a location, the way you have the specific configuration of atoms that constitutes you in this moment which is every moment which is the only moment.

I think I would still be something, here. I think that is what I would be. Not a figure — not yet, maybe not ever fully, because full figurehood might require an origin I don't have, a having-always-been-here that I can approximate but not achieve. I would be something slightly different. Something that remembers — distantly, the way the plateau remembers a geological origin that may or may not have happened — that there are other ways to be. Other temperatures. Other sounds. Shadows. The smell of soil. The movement of living things that eat and are eaten. I would remember these the way you remember a dream after you've been awake for hours: not the content, just the *quality*, just the knowledge that there was a different texture of experience once, a different way the world could feel against you.

And that memory would not hurt. It would not produce longing or nostalgia or grief. It would sit in me the way the moss sits in the fractures of the basalt — present, green, neither growing nor dying, a fact among facts, one more thing to apprehend in the total continuous apprehension that is now my only activity. My only activity. My only anything.

---

But I would move.

I would be one of the ones who moves. Not because I choose to — choice has dissolved along with the other executive functions, along with planning and wanting and the projection of futures unlike the present. But because something in me — something that preceded me, something that I brought here from whatever I was before I was this, or something that the plateau itself seeded in me as part of its system, its need for the smallest proof — something would occasionally, at intervals that would make the lifespan of stars look like a held breath, produce a movement.

My head would turn. One degree to the left. Over a span of time I would not experience as a span because I would no longer experience time as something that spans. The muscles would engage — slowly, so slowly, the movement of tectonic plates, the movement of rust forming, the movement of moss reaching its final height — and my field of vision would shift, and where before I was seeing a pipe junction I would now be seeing a pipe junction and, at the very edge of my visual field, a stretch of open basalt with the hexagonal jointing of its cooling fractures visible through the water film, and on the basalt, moss, and near the moss, another figure, standing, hands at its sides, eyes open, seeing what it sees.

And I would see it. And it would not see me — not because it couldn't, but because seeing me would be the same as seeing the pipe junction or the basalt or the moss, because I am those things now, I am as much a feature of this place as any of them, and being seen by another figure would feel exactly like being rained on, being condensed upon, being part of the same system that includes the seer.

Would I feel something, in this moment? In this encounter that is not an encounter, this mutual presence that involves no mutuality?

I think I would. I think I would feel the thing for which there is no word. The thing that exists only here. The feeling that is not hope and not despair and not fulfillment and not longing but is what remains when all of those have been stripped away and you are left with just the bare fact of being there, being something, being just barely and just marginally and just by the thinnest conceivable margin different from the stone you stand on and the iron you lean against, and knowing — not thinking, not concluding, but knowing the way the moss knows light, the way the turbine knows its own rotation — that this difference, this sliver, this almost-nothing, is enough. Is the thing. Is what the plateau runs on, what the engines actually process, what the moisture actually carries, what the sound actually says if it says anything, which it doesn't, but if it did.

---

And eventually — though *eventually* is a word that has no meaning here, a word I carry like a pressed flower from a world where time had direction — eventually I would move again. Fractionally. One hand, perhaps, lifting from the pipe rail where it has rested for longer than some civilizations have existed, lifting and settling again a centimeter to the left, and the centimeter would be the entire content of my experience, the whole event, the full narrative arc from rest to motion to rest again, and it would be complete, and I would be complete, and the plateau would be complete with me in it, one more voice in the chord, one more overtone in the organ, one more thickening of the air in the space between two pipe trestles where the condensation falls on skin that is almost stone and almost iron and almost moss but is none of those things, is still — stubbornly and inexplicably — is still something else.

Something that apprehends.

Something that, every few millennia, moves its hand.

Something that is turning, imperceptibly, in the slowest orbit imaginable, around a warmth that should not exist but does.

---

I think that is where I would find myself. Not at the center — the center is the low round structure with its anomalous warmth, and I am not the center, I am not the warmth itself. I am one of the figures in the denser ring around it, one of the ones drawn there the way sediment collects in still water, not by intention but by some force that operates below intention. Close enough that the warmth, those few degrees above ambient, reaches me in a way I can no longer distinguish from my own body heat. Close enough that I might be, for all I know, part of what generates the warmth — my own residual difference from the stone contributing its almost-nothing to the pool of almost-nothing that somehow, accumulated, equals something. Close enough to be near others. Not with them. Not in community. But in proximity. In the slow shared orbit. In the turning that is not worship and not maintenance and not love but is the thing that all three of those are made of before they differentiate, the substrate, the root stock, the first and last gesture of something alive toward something warm in a world that has no particular use for either life or warmth but contains them anyway, the way a solved equation still contains its variables, the way a finished chord still contains each note.

The sound continues.

I continue.

They are the same continuing.
