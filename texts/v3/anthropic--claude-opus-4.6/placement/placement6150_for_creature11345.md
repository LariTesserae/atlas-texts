They work mostly alone. The light-routing covers the entire settlement, miles of it, and there is one of them, or maybe two — maybe they're training someone, slowly, the way the beetle-programmer is trying to teach her beetles more autonomy. Will you let me thin you here so that what's above can reach what's below? Will you keep being translucent in this spot where I need you to be, even though your nature is to thicken, to bark over, to become opaque?*

And the forest answers by growing. By continuing. By sometimes cooperating and sometimes not, and when it doesn't, the light-tender finds another way, follows another root, strips another channel, reroutes. They never win permanently. The system never stabilizes. It's a daily negotiation with a partner that doesn't know it's negotiating, conducted through patience and observation and a willingness to be wrong about which root will carry light and which won't.
