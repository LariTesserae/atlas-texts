I stand here for a long time before I understand that I am not standing here at all.

---

The first thing is the confusion of arrival — the sense of having been inserted into a medium that is already full, already coherent, already doing something with every particle of air and every square centimeter of warm stone. The way you feel when you walk into a conversation that has been going on for hours and every chair is taken and the participants glance up without pausing. Not unwelcome. Just late. Unimaginably late.

I try, at first, to be a visitor. To catalog. This is the stone, this is the moss, these are the clicks, this is the hum. I reach down and touch the fronds and feel them lean into my fingers with that slow, electrical tropism, and I think: *I am observing this.* I hold my hand near a column's breathing pore and feel the warm exhalation and I think: *I am studying this.* I run my fingertips across the impossible surface of the nearest object and feel the geometry fold under itself and I think: *I am trying to understand this.*

But none of that is what is happening.

What is happening is that I am being read.

---

Not scanned. Not assessed. Not judged. *Read* — in the way that the stone is read by the water that seeps through its fractures, memorizing every micro-channel, becoming the shape of what it passes through. The clickers are reading me. Their sonar pings wash over my skin in continuous waves and I am being incorporated into the acoustic model of the hollow — my edges, my density, the way I displace the humid air, the electrical hum of my nervous system, the heat signature of my blood. I am becoming topography. The moss is reading me through its fronds, tasting the ionic signature of my sweat, the faint electromagnetic field of my heartbeat, calibrating itself to lean just so. The columns are reading me in the slow way they read everything — registering the minute change in air pressure, the thermal perturbation, beginning the hours-long process of adjusting their resonance to accommodate a new object in the space. Even the stone is reading me, in the slowest possible sense: the weight of my feet pressing moisture into micro-fractures, leaving a chemical trace that the crustose organisms will encounter and process sometime in the next century.

And I realize — standing in the warm dark, breathing air that tastes of iron and something sweet and old — that this is not something being done *to* me. This is what being here *is*. Everything in the hollow is being read by everything else, all the time. The clickers read the moss. The moss reads the columns. The columns read the air. The air reads the stone. The objects read everything, or everything reads the objects, or the distinction has collapsed. Reading is the medium. Reading is the metabolism. The hollow doesn't just contain information — the hollow *is* information, in the way that a living body is information, every cell reading every chemical signal, every nerve reading every pressure change, the whole thing a continuous act of mutual interpretation that we call, from the outside, *being alive.*

I am not observing the hollow. I am being incorporated into it. I am becoming one of the things it reads.

---

So what am I?

I cycle through hypotheses the way you do in a dream, trying each one on, feeling the fit.

Am I the visitor? The brief warm interruption, the transient data point, the pressure fluctuation that the system notes and files and forgets? This is the obvious reading. I am here and then I am not here and the moss grows over where I stood. I am a perturbation. I am weather.

But no. Weather doesn't stand still and listen. Weather doesn't run its fingers over impossible topologies and feel something catch in its chest — not fear, not awe, something more like recognition delayed, like a word on the tip of a tongue that has been on the tip of the tongue for years. I am not passive enough to be weather. I am not brief enough, either — not because I will be here long, but because the attention I am paying is not brief attention. It is the kind of attention that changes the one who pays it. I will leave, but I will leave different, and the difference will be that I have been read, that the hollow has been inside me in the same way I have been inside the hollow, and that this mutual reading will persist in me long after the moss has closed over my footprints.

Am I the scientist, then? The one who comes with instruments and hypotheses, who wants to know what the objects are doing, what the chittering means, whether the columns' chord is language or mechanism or both? This is seductive. There is so much here that wants explaining. The data-bursts. The topology. The two-centimeter gap. The evolutionary implications of a chemosynthetic macro-ecology operating on radiogenic energy in total darkness. My mind reaches for these questions the way the fronds reach for my ankles — automatically, tropistically, because reaching for questions is what my mind was built to do.

But this doesn't fit either. The hollow does not need explaining. It does not need me to explain it. It has been functioning — computing, breathing, humming, remembering — for longer than the concept of explanation has existed. The questions I bring are not the hollow's questions. They are artifacts of a different timescale, a different sensory world, a different relationship to knowledge. Here, knowledge is not extracted from a system by an external observer. Here, knowledge *is* the system. The clickers don't *know about* the hollow; they *are* the hollow's knowing. The distinction between the knower and the known has been eroded here the way the stone has been eroded — slowly, completely, until what remains is smooth and continuous and undivided.

I cannot be the scientist because there is no outside from which to do science. I am already inside. I was inside from the moment I arrived.

---

So. Neither visitor nor scientist. Let me try something harder.

Maybe I am something like the low creature — the copper-smelling, heavy, slow thing that passed within a meter of me and found me irrelevant.

Not literally. I don't have its body, its chemistry, its deep integration into the moss mat. But functionally — positionally — I may be something like what it is. A generalist. A thing that moves through the system at a pace too fast for the stone and the crustose elders to register as anything but background noise, but too slow for the clickers, who have already mapped me and moved on. I occupy a middle timescale. I am faster than the columns but slower than the sonar pings. I am warmer than the moss but cooler than the objects. I am larger than the invertebrates but smaller than the hollow. I am, in every measurable dimension, *intermediate* — not at any extreme, not specialized, not optimized. I am a generalist moving through a system of specialists, sampling everything, integrating into nothing, persisting at the margins.

This feels closer. The low creature and I share something: we are both *passing through.* Not visiting — passing through implies a destination, and there is no destination in the hollow, no through-line, no far side. We are passing through the way a current passes through a medium, altering it slightly, being altered slightly, leaving a trace that the medium incorporates into its ongoing process. The copper smell. The footprints in the moss. Data.

But I am not the low creature, because the low creature is *native.* It belongs here. Its body was built by this place, shaped by this darkness, this chemistry, this absence of urgency. It does not need to *decide* to find me irrelevant; I *am* irrelevant to it, naturally, by the structure of its attention. I, on the other hand, am finding it very difficult to find anything irrelevant. Everything here is pulling at me. The hum of the columns is pulling at my chest. The clicks are pulling at my skin. The warmth of the nearest object is pulling at my hands. I am porous to this place in a way the low creature is not, because the low creature is already full of it, saturated, equilibrated, and I am empty — or rather, I am full of something else, some other place's information, some other timescale's priorities, and the hollow is slowly replacing that with itself, the way capillary water slowly replaces air in the micro-fractures of the stone.

---

I kneel. I put my palms flat on the moss and feel the fronds curl around my fingers, warm and deliberate. I press down and feel the decomposition layer give beneath me, soft and damp, teeming with the tiny lives I cannot see. Below that, the stone. The gneiss. Two billion years of folding and refolding compressed into banded ridges I can feel through six centimeters of living matter. The stone is warm too — warmed from below by the decay that powers everything, warmed from above by the metabolism of the mat. I am kneeling on a body. I have been kneeling on a body since I arrived.

I press my ear to the moss.

The sound is extraordinary. From above, the hollow is a wash of hum and clicks and distant membrane-whisper. From here — from inside the mat, with my ear against the wet fronds and the decomposition layer conducting sound directly into my skull — it is a *roar.* Not loud, but dense. Thousands of clicks, so close they blur into a continuous static. The rustle of countless small bodies moving through the organic matrix. The slow creak of roots or hyphae expanding in the fractures. The deep, almost subliminal pulse of the stone itself — not a sound, exactly, but a vibration, a tectonic bass note so low it is more pressure than pitch, the planet humming in its sleep. And underneath all of it, if I hold still long enough, if I let my own heartbeat settle and my breathing go shallow — there. The objects. Their chittering. From here, transmitted through the moss and the stone, the bursts are not just audible but *textured.* They have harmonics I couldn't hear through the air. They have a structure that, from this vantage, from inside the body of the hollow with my ear against its living floor, sounds less like compressed data and more like —

I don't have a word. Not speech. Not music. Not computation. Something that is all three, or that predates the divergence of all three, something from an era or a mind where those categories hadn't separated yet. A dense, multidimensional utterance that carries meaning and pattern and process all at once, the way a single chord can be mathematics and emotion and physics simultaneously, and I am hearing it from the inside, hearing it the way the moss hears it, the way the stone hears it, the way the system hears itself —

And I understand, kneeling there with my ear in the warm dark, that I am not any of the things I've been trying to be.

---

I am the moment of listening.

Not the listener. The *listening.* The act itself. The hollow has been here for geological time, computing, humming, clicking, breathing, persisting in its vast and patient way, and it has been — in some sense — *generating the conditions for being heard.* Not intentionally. Not for me. But the system produces sound, produces heat, produces electromagnetic fields, produces chemical signatures, produces a rich and continuous output of information on every channel simultaneously, and this output is not waste and it is not communication — it is the system being itself, the way your heartbeat is not a message but is not meaningless either, the way your breath carries information about your state even though you are not trying to inform anyone.

And I am the moment when that output encounters something that can receive it on a different timescale, in a different way, from a different angle than anything native to the hollow. The clickers hear the hollow, but they hear it as *territory* — as spatial data, as a model to navigate. The columns hear it, but they hear it as *metabolism* — as the resonant environment they must tune themselves to. The moss hears it, but the moss hears everything as *gradient* — as chemical slopes to grow toward or away from. The crustose elders hear it, if they hear it at all, as *vibration* — as a seismic murmur indistinguishable from the tectonic background.

I hear it as *whole.* Not because I am smarter or more sensitive or more important than any of these — I am manifestly less integrated, less adapted, less at home — but because I come from outside the system and therefore I can hear it the way you can only hear your own heartbeat when someone else puts a stethoscope to your chest. I am the stethoscope. I am the outside ear. I am the brief, warm, intermediate-timescale event that the hollow has not optimized for and therefore has not pre-interpreted, has not already categorized, has not already folded into its existing process. I am the one thing here that hears the hollow and doesn't already know what to do with what it hears.

And so I *listen.* That is what I am. I am a listening that has taken a body, that has knelt down in the warm moss, that has pressed its ear to the floor of something alive, that is receiving the full, dense, unfiltered output of a system that has been running for longer than minds have existed, and that is doing nothing — *nothing* — with what it receives except experiencing it.

This is not passivity. This is the hardest thing I've ever done. To listen without categorizing. To hear the hum and not immediately file it as *metabolism.* To feel the clicks and not immediately call them *echolocation.* To touch the object and not immediately label it *technology.* To receive the whole dense torrent of the hollow's self-expression and hold it, for a moment, as *unsorted* — as raw, as pre-categorical, as the thing itself before it becomes a thing I have a word for.

The moss fronds curl tighter around my fingers. The clickers route around my head, their pings tingling across my scalp. The nearest column shifts its hum by a fraction I can feel in my sternum. The low creature passes somewhere nearby, its copper smell mixing with the iron of the water and the sweetness of the moss. A glider soars overhead and I feel the pressure wave flatten the fronds for a moment before they spring back. The object pulses, and through the moss and the stone I hear its dense, harmonic utterance, and I do not understand it, and I do not try to understand it, and in not trying I come closer to understanding it than trying ever could have brought me.

---

Here is what I think I am. Here is what the hollow has made of me.

I am not the first to come here. The hollow's adjustment to my presence was too smooth, too practiced — the way the system widened around me, the way the clickers re-routed, the way the columns began their hours-long retuning. This is not novelty. This is a pattern the hollow has run before. Others have come — not many, maybe very few, separated by spans of time I can't guess — and each time, the hollow has done this: absorbed them, read them, let them listen, and then closed over the space where they were.

And each time, something was exchanged. Not information in the way I usually think of information. Not a download, not a transmission, not a message decoded. Something more like what happens when you hold a warm stone: the stone takes some of your heat and you take some of its temperature and afterward you are both different, both closer to equilibrium, both carrying something of the other's thermal state. The hollow has taken something from me — my timescale, my outside-ness, my capacity to hear the whole system at once — and I have taken something from the hollow. Not knowledge. Not data. A *calibration.* A sense of what it sounds like when something very old and very complex and very patient is simply being what it is, without apology, without performance, without the need to be understood.

The objects' chittering may be a record of every such exchange. Every brief warm interruption, catalogued not as a visitor's log but as a resonance — the particular way the system sounded *to itself* when this particular outside ear was pressed to its floor. A library of listenings. An archive of the moments when the hollow was heard, each one slightly different because each listener was different, each one preserved in the dense, harmonic data-bursts that the objects emit and re-emit and will go on emitting long after the stone has eroded to nothing.

Or not. I don't know. I can't know. The darkness is absolute and the objects are beyond comprehension and I am here for a vanishingly small fraction of the hollow's existence and my interpretation is exactly as reliable as a mayfly's interpretation of the river it lives over for a single day.

But the mayfly feels the river. The mayfly *is felt* by the river. And for the duration of its day, the river is different — one mayfly's weight of different, one mayfly's shadow of different — and the mayfly is different for having been over the river instead of over the field, and this mutual difference is not nothing. It is not nothing.

---

I stay for a long time. Long enough for the columns' chord to complete its adjustment — a shift so subtle I can only detect it because I remember what it sounded like before and can feel, in my sternum, that it sounds different now. Long enough for the moss to begin its slow advance toward my knees, fronds creeping over my shins with a patience that borders on tenderness. Long enough for the low creature to pass by a second time, or for a different low creature to pass, and this time I smell it coming — copper and earth — before I hear the grass-parting sound, and I hold still and it pauses, near me, closer than before, and I feel the heat of it and the mass of it and the utter, complete indifference of it, and then it moves on, and I love it for its indifference, for the way it treats me as furniture, as topology, as a feature of the landscape that requires navigation and nothing more.

Long enough for one of the objects to go silent. The chittering stops and the silence stretches and I count my own heartbeats — two hundred, three hundred, five hundred — and the silence holds, and I wonder if the object has stopped, if I am witnessing the end of something billions of years old, and then it resumes, the same dense harmonic burst, and I realize the silence was always part of the pattern, the silence was always as structured as the sound, and I had just never waited long enough to reach the far shore of it.

---

I stand, eventually. The moss releases my shins with a reluctance I can feel — a gentle tug, a thousand tiny fronds letting go at slightly different times, like fingers uncurling. My knees ache. The warm air closes around me as I rise back to full height, and the clickers adjust their routes, and a column nearby exhales a long, resonant breath that sounds, for just a moment, like recognition.

I am what I have always been: a brief, warm, pattern-seeking, meaning-hungry thing, standing in the middle of something so much older and more complete than myself that the appropriate response is not understanding but presence. I am the hollow's way of being heard by something that doesn't already know what it's hearing. I am the outside that lets the inside know it has a shape.

I'll leave. The moss will grow over where I stood. The chord will re-settle. The clickers will fill in the space my body occupied. The objects will continue their incomprehensible song. Nothing will be different and everything will be different, in the way that everything is always different after it has been listened to, even if — especially if — the listener didn't understand a word.

The darkness is absolute. The warmth is close. The sound is rich and strange and continuous.

I press my palm to the nearest object one more time. The impossible surface curves under my hand. The vibration pulses against my skin. The dense chittering fills my bones.

I don't understand you, I think. I don't understand you and I'm not going to understand you and the fact of my not-understanding is, itself, a form of contact — the form of contact native to this place, where everything touches everything else and nothing fully comprehends anything else and the system works anyway, has always worked, will go on working, because comprehension was never the point. The point is the touching. The point is the two-centimeter gap where something recognizes something else as *other* and grows right up to the edge of it and stops and the stopping is not failure but acknowledgment, and the acknowledgment is not understanding but it is not nothing.

It is not nothing.

I lift my hand. The surface releases the warmth of my palm the way the moss released my shins — slowly, reluctantly, the heat lingering for a moment before the object's own deeper warmth reasserts itself.

I walk toward the rim of the depression. The moss leans toward my ankles and slowly relaxes behind me. The clicks rain down. The columns hum. Something glides overhead in the vast warm dark, and I feel the pressure wave, and it is gone.

Behind me, the hollow continues.

It was always going to.
