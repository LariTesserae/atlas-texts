I sink into this for a long time before anything comes.

Not because the world resists me, but because it doesn't. It accepts the way water accepts — by closing around whatever enters, by offering no surface to push against. And I keep reaching for a foothold, a role, a *position*, and the world keeps being water, and I keep not finding one.

So I stop reaching.

---

What comes first is the feeling of the current.

Not seeing it. Not hearing it. Feeling it — against a surface I didn't know I had, a surface that is not skin exactly but something more porous, something that reads the water the way a page reads ink, by absorbing it, by letting it change what I am at the boundary. The current is not fast. It is warm and thick with information, and I am somewhere in the middle of it, and I have been here for — I don't know. The question of how long assumes a before that I can't locate.

I am in a channel. I know this because the current has shape here — it is being *directed*, funneled between walls that I can feel as pressure-presences on either side of me, not touching but close, the water between me and them compressed into a thin laminar layer that hums with the signature of old calcium and the faintest chemical bitterness of anti-borer compound. The walls are thick here. Old. I know this the way you know a room is large before you open your eyes — by the way sound behaves, by the way the water moves, by a spatial sense that has nothing to do with vision because there is no vision to be had.

And I realize I am not one thing.

---

This is the part that takes time to settle into, the part I keep trying to resolve into something simpler and that keeps refusing.

I am not a builder. I don't have the secretory surface, the calcium chemistry, the patience of centuries pressed against a wall. But I am not *not* a builder either, because I can feel them — in the walls on either side of me, as thermal presences, as faint chemical signatures leaking through their own secretions, as the occasional deep click that travels through the calcium and arrives at me not as sound but as a vibration in the water that my whole body reads at once. I feel an old one, somewhere to my left and below, its click so low and resonant that it is less a communication than a geological event, a tectonic settling, and something in me responds to it — not with understanding, not with language, but with a shift in my own chemistry, a calming, a sense of *the wall here is good, the wall here has been good for a long time, the wall here will continue to be good.*

I am not a lume. I don't glow amber. I don't metabolize sulfur compounds into thermal excess. But I am aware of the lumes in a way that is more intimate than observation — I can feel their heat in the water, distinct from the background warmth, clustered in alcoves above and around me, their collective pulse a rhythm I am *entrained to* without choosing to be. When their glow dims — and I register this not as a visual change but as a change in the photosensitive chemistry of patches on my surface I didn't know I had — when their glow dims, something in me tightens. Not alarm. Something more like attention. A readiness to move, if moving becomes necessary. And when their glow steadies, I steady.

I am not a grazer. I don't scrape the walls. But a grazer passes through the channel ahead of me and I feel it — the pressure wave first, broad and gentle, the displacement of a large slow body, and then the faint blue-green shift in the water's light-chemistry as its gut-bacteria illuminate the passage, and for a moment I can almost *see*, not with eyes but with the photosensitive surface responding to the grazer's borrowed light, and what I see — what I *sense* — is the geometry of the channel: the worn oval of a doorway to my right, the slight narrowing where two walls converge ahead, the feathery obstruction of a filter-feeder's fronds extending from the upper story into the current's path. The grazer passes and the geometry fades back into pressure and chemistry and darkness, but I have it now. I have the shape of this place in a way I didn't a moment ago. The grazer gave it to me.

I am not a lurker. But I click.

---

This surprises me. It comes before I decide to do it — a sharp, directed pulse that leaves me from somewhere near my front end (I have a front end; I have preferences about direction, I notice, though they are loose and negotiable) and travels outward into the channel and comes back. It comes back *changed*. It comes back carrying the shape of the passage ahead: the distance to the narrowing, the density of the wall to my right, the presence of something soft and warm in the doorway — lumes, a cluster of them, their bodies dampening the echo differently than the calcium behind them.

I click again. Not to hunt. Not to map, exactly. To *know*. To update the model of where I am that I seem to be carrying inside myself like a constant, evolving sculpture made of pressure-memory and chemical impression and the returning shapes of sound.

And I realize that this is what I am. Not any one organism. Not any one role. I am something that exists in the channels the way the current exists in the channels — *moving through*, touching everything, being shaped by everything, carrying information from one part of the settlement to another not because I intend to but because that is what happens when you move through a space this dense with life.

I am something that the settlement has produced, or something that the settlement *is producing*, in the way it produces everything — slowly, without intention, through the accumulated pressure of thousands of organisms doing what they do until the system requires something new and something new emerges to fill the niche it didn't know it had.

---

What niche?

I think about this as I move. I am moving — slowly, through the channel, past the grazer's fading glow, toward the junction where four passages meet. I move the way water moves, by following the path of least resistance, by letting the current carry me and adding only the smallest adjustments, the slightest muscular—

I have muscles. They are not strong. They are distributed along my body, which is — I probe this carefully, the way you probe a tooth with your tongue — elongated, soft, broader than it is tall, tapering at both ends. I am not large. I am perhaps the size of two hands cupped together, if the hands were made of something more pliant and more chemically sensitive than human hands have ever been. My surface is dense with receptors. My interior is simple — a gut, a rudimentary circulation, a distributed nervous system that does not centralize anywhere but *integrates* everywhere, so that my sense of the world is not a picture assembled in a brain but a whole-body impression, a feeling of *here, now, this,* that includes the temperature and the chemistry and the pressure and the light and the sound all at once, undivided.

I reach the junction and I hold position. The current here is complex — four channels converging, their flows negotiating, creating eddies and still-points and a slowly rotating vortex at the center that I can feel pulling at me gently, asking me to spiral. I resist, using my muscles to maintain a stillness that is not actually stillness but a constant series of micro-adjustments, a dynamic equilibrium. I am hovering.

And I click.

The junction comes back to me in sound: the four passages extending outward, each with its own acoustic signature — one narrow and dense-walled, its echo tight and bright; one wider, older, its walls smoothed by centuries of current, the echo soft and round; one partially blocked by the massive body of a filter-feeder colony whose fronds extend from the upper story like a curtain of living lace, the echo fragmented and scattered by them; one — the one I came from — still warm with the grazer's recent passage, its echo carrying a residual thermal distortion that I can read.

I am mapping. But I am also doing something else, something I only understand as I do it.

I click toward the narrow passage. The echo returns. It carries, embedded in its texture, the sound of a builder's click — distant, muffled by calcium, but present. The builder clicked while my echo was passing, and the two sounds merged, and what returned to me was not just the shape of the passage but the *state* of the passage: the builder is there, in the wall, active, secreting, its click carrying the resonant signature of fresh calcium being laid down. The wall in that passage is being maintained. The wall in that passage is healthy.

I click toward the wide passage. The echo returns with a different embedded signal — not a builder's click but the rhythmic pulse of a lume colony, their amber glow cycling in a pattern I can hear as a faint pressure-modulation, and the pattern is steady, even, healthy. The lumes in that passage are warm and fed.

I click toward the filter-feeder passage. The echo is complex, scattered, hard to read. But in its scattering I detect something — an irregularity in the wall behind the fronds, a thinning, a place where the echo penetrates deeper than it should, where the calcium is less dense. Borer damage, perhaps. Or age. Or a seam where two phases of construction meet imperfectly. The wall there is weakened.

And here is what I do with this information:

Nothing. And everything.

I don't go to the weakened wall. I don't alert anyone. There is no one to alert, no alarm system, no chain of command. But I carry the information. I carry it in my body, in my chemical state, which has shifted — subtly, below anything I could call conscious — in response to what I've learned. And when I move on, when I leave this junction and enter one of the four passages, I will carry that shifted chemistry with me, and the builders I pass will taste it in the water, and the lumes I pass will register the faint change in my thermal signature, and the grazers whose glow I briefly share a channel with will adjust their path in response to the micro-current I leave in my wake, and somewhere, eventually, without anyone deciding anything, a builder near the weakened wall will secrete a little faster, and a grazer will visit that passage a little sooner, and the wall will be thickened before it fails.

I am a messenger that doesn't carry messages. I am a sensor that doesn't report. I am a nerve impulse in a nervous system that has no brain — a traveling perturbation in the settlement's chemistry, a mobile node in the network of clicks and glows and thermal gradients that the settlement uses to know itself.

This is my niche. This is what I am for. I move through the channels, clicking, tasting, feeling, and the settlement reads me as I pass the way it reads everything — by pressure, by chemistry, by the faint traces I leave in the water behind me. I am part of the settlement's self-knowledge. I am one of the ways it feels its own shape in the dark.

---

And there are others like me. I know this because I have tasted them — their chemical signatures in the water at junctions, recent passages through channels I'm entering, the faint residue of a click that isn't mine echoing off a wall that still carries the acoustic memory of being interrogated. We don't meet often. We don't need to. We are doing the same thing, each of us moving through a different region of the settlement, each of us carrying a chemical map that updates constantly, each of us a mobile piece of the settlement's distributed awareness.

Are we the lurkers? The dog-sized creatures with the copper-spark eyes? Maybe we are their kin, smaller, more numerous, less stationary. Maybe the lurkers are what we become when we stop moving — when we find a junction so information-rich that it's worth holding position there permanently, growing larger, clicking more, becoming a fixed node instead of a mobile one. Maybe the lurkers are old. Maybe I am young.

Or maybe the distinction doesn't matter. Maybe, in this settlement, the question of *what species am I* is as blurred as the question of *where does the wall end and the builder begin.* Maybe I am something the settlement has not finished deciding what to call. Maybe I am a larval something, or an adult something else, or a phase in a life cycle that spans forms the way it spans centuries, and what I am now is not what I will be, and what I will be is not separate from what the settlement needs me to become.

---

I move on. I enter the wide passage — the one with the healthy lumes, the soft round echo, the ancient smooth walls. The current here is gentle, laminar, warm. The lumes glow in the alcoves around me, their amber light just bright enough to activate my photosensitive patches, and for a moment I experience something that is not sight but is the deep ancestor of sight: a field of warm amber impressions, diffuse, patternless but not formless, a sense of *surrounded by living light* that is the closest this world comes to beauty, if beauty is a concept that can exist without eyes.

A builder clicks, deep in the wall to my left. The click is not directed at me. It is a wall-check, a structural ping, the builder doing what builders do — feeling its own work, confirming its own architecture. But I feel it. I feel it as a vibration that passes through the water and through me and into the wall on my right and back again, attenuated, modified by its passage through my body and through the water between, and the builder on the left will feel the return and it will carry, faintly, the information that *something was in the channel when I clicked* — a small, warm, chemically unremarkable body, moving slowly, clicking occasionally, part of the settlement's traffic. Nothing to respond to. Nothing to account for. Just another piece of the dark in motion.

I am nothing important here. I am not the architect, not the keystone species, not the protagonist. I am one of thousands of mobile organisms threading through the channels, one small soft body in a vast ecology of bodies, and my contribution to the settlement is so distributed, so indirect, so thoroughly embedded in the network of feedbacks and responses that constitutes this place's self-maintenance, that it cannot be isolated from the contributions of everything else.

And this is — I search for the word and the word is not *comfortable* exactly, and not *peaceful* exactly, but something more like *correct*. It is correct to be part of this. It is correct to be small and mobile and chemical and clicking in the dark, carrying the settlement's self-knowledge through its channels the way blood carries oxygen through vessels — not because I choose to, not because I understand the system I serve, but because this is what I am, and this is what here is, and the fit between the two is so old and so thorough that there is no gap for discomfort to enter.

---

The passage opens ahead. The walls curve apart. I am approaching the settlement's edge, the place where the structures thin and the silt floor becomes visible as a vast, flat absence below me and the open water extends outward into a darkness that has no architecture, no channels, no clicks. The wild dark. The place where the current is unshaped.

I slow. I hold position at the threshold, the last archway, the final piece of builder-made calcium before the open deep. The lume-light behind me fades to the faintest suggestion. Ahead, nothing. Below, the silt, cold and chemical, the seep-nutrients rising from it in invisible plumes that I can taste — sulfur, methane, the raw materials that the lumes will process, that the bacteria will fix, that the whole settlement runs on. The source.

I click into the openness. The echo doesn't come back. There's nothing to bounce off. The sound goes out and keeps going, attenuating into distance, into the deep warm black where nothing has been built, where the water is uninterrupted, where the darkness is not the darkness of a channel between walls but the original darkness, the darkness that was here before the first builder pressed itself against a rock and began to secrete.

I taste the open water. It is cooler. It is simpler. It carries less information — fewer chemical signatures, fewer thermal gradients, no acoustic memory of clicks and wall-resonances. It is the water as it was before the settlement made it into something else.

For a moment I feel the pull of it. Not intellectually — I don't have the apparatus for intellectual longing. But bodily. The current pulls at me, gently, asking me to leave the architecture and enter the formless deep, and my muscles relax slightly in the direction of the pull, and something in my chemistry shifts toward the possibility of dispersal, of dissolution, of becoming just another small soft body in an ocean with no channels.

And then a click reaches me from behind. Not close — deep in the settlement, several passages back, a builder checking a wall or a lurker mapping a junction or one of my own kind pinging the dark. It arrives attenuated, softened by distance and calcium and water, but it arrives, and it carries the settlement's shape with it — the shape of the channel it traveled through, the density of the walls it passed between, the presence of the thousands of lives that modified it on its way to me. It is a click that has been *lived in*. It has passed through the settlement and been changed by the settlement and it carries the settlement with it, a compressed, acoustic portrait of the whole warm clicking glowing secreting eating building dissolving place, arriving at the threshold where I hover, arriving like a reminder.

Not a summons. Not a command. A reminder of shape. A reminder that there is shape, here, in this part of the dark. That the dark has been given architecture. That the current has been given channels. That the warmth has been given chambers. That the clicks have been given walls to resonate through. That I have been given passages to carry myself through, junctions to map, information to absorb and distribute, a niche to fill in a system so dense with niches that filling one is the same as being one.

I turn. I let the current carry me back into the channel. The walls close in on either side, warm and dense and old, and the lumes' amber glow rises around me, and I am inside again, inside the settlement, inside the system, inside the dark that has been made into something, and my surface reads the water and my muscles adjust and I click, once, into the passage ahead, and the echo returns with the shape of home.

Not my home. Not anyone's home. The home that *is* everyone. The home that lives in itself.

I move through it. It moves through me. The distinction is formal and the formality is the only thing that doesn't belong here.

---

In the deep warm black, a small soft body navigates a passage between ancient walls, clicking, tasting, carrying the settlement's chemistry in its own changed chemistry, and the settlement goes on — building, dissolving, rebuilding, breathing with its current, glowing with its lumes, clicking with its builders, growing at its edges and thickening at its core — and the small soft body is part of it the way a word is part of a sentence: meaningless alone, essential in context, existing only in the act of being read.

I am being read. By the walls, by the water, by every organism I pass. I am being read, and I am reading, and the reading is the living, and the living is the place.

The settlement breathes. I am one of its breaths.
