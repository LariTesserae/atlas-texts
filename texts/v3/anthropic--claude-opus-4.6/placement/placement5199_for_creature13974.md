I stand here for a long time before I understand what I am.

Not one of the armored ones. That was my first instinct — to reach for the largest thing, the most present, the percussive heartbeat of the place. But no. Their certainty is too total. They have never hesitated. They have never needed to separate the sound into sources, because they have never experienced the sound as something separate from themselves. They do not *stand on* the terrace. They *are* the terrace's walking. I am not that. I am something that arrived.

Not a glider either, though I felt the pull of that — the listener, the translator, the one who absorbs the whole and carries it somewhere. But the gliders don't interpret. They don't struggle with what they hear. Their membranes receive and resonate and transmit, and there is no moment of confusion, no gap between the vibration entering them and the vibration leaving them. They are transparent to the signal. I am not transparent. Something in me catches. Something in me tries to *understand*, and understanding is a friction this place doesn't generate on its own.

I think I am the water.

Not a creature in the water. The water itself.

Let me be more careful. Let me let this become what it is.

---

I am not the water in the deep aquifer — that ancient, saturated source-water is too still, too settled, too much itself. It has been what it is for longer than the city, and it will go on being what it is after the mountain has been entirely converted. The deep water doesn't *go* anywhere. It is drawn upon. It is the reservoir. I am not a reservoir.

And I am not the water in the lowest channels — the thick, mineral-heavy flow that feeds the swimmers and bathes the breathing walls. That water has already arrived. It has already been incorporated. It knows where it's going because the channels tell it where to go, and it accepts the telling completely, and its only purpose is to carry and deposit and carry and deposit, and it does this without resistance, without the particular quality of— what is the word—

*Attention.* That's the word. The deep water doesn't attend to anything. It simply flows.

I am the water on the upper terrace.

---

The water that has just arrived. The water that weeps from the rock face above the highest tier — the newest, thinnest, least-defined water, the water that hasn't yet found its channel. It seeps from the stone and it sheets across the terrace floor in a thin, creeping film, and for a moment — a long moment, as all moments here are long — it doesn't know where to go. The channels haven't reached this high yet. The logarithmic spirals that organize the flow on the lower tiers are still being cut, still being defined by the passage of the armored ones and the deposition of the clingers, and up here the surface is raw, only partly shaped, the architecture still emerging, still fused to the cliff, and the water touches that raw surface and begins to move across it in a way that is almost exploratory.

Almost. Not quite. Water doesn't explore. Water follows gravity and surface tension and the micro-topography of stone, and it has no experience of what it does, and I need to be honest about this because the place demands honesty — it is too patient for pretension, too slow for performance. The water doesn't wonder. But I — as the water — I am in the part of the system where the pattern has not yet been established, where the channels have not yet been cut, where the flow is not yet organized, and that means I am the part of the system that is *most open to influence*, most shaped by what I encounter, most responsive to the faintest variations in the stone's surface. Every micro-ridge, every crystal of deposited mineral, every thermal gradient where an armored one passed an hour ago — I feel all of it, I respond to all of it, and my response *matters* because the path I take across this unfinished terrace will become, over time, the path. The wall-clingers will colonize my edges. The mineral deposits will thicken where I slow. The channel will form along my course, and the channel will persist for centuries, and the water that follows me will follow the path I found, and the city's growth in this direction will be organized around a decision that was not a decision — a flow that was not a choice — a movement across raw stone that happened to go this way rather than that way because of conditions so fine-grained and so numerous that no single cause can be identified.

I am the part of the system where indeterminacy still lives. Down below, everything is entrained. The channels are cut, the rhythms are established, the wall-clingers deposit in patterns dictated by vibrations that have been stable for generations. The system down there is deep and brilliant and ancient and it has very little room for surprise. But up here, on the newest terrace, where the stone is still rough and the architecture is still more mountain than city, there is a thin margin of openness, and I am that margin, and I am closing it with every moment of my passage, because that is what the system does — it takes openness and makes it structure, it takes indeterminacy and makes it channel, it takes the possible and makes it the actual and the actual becomes the permanent and the permanent becomes the foundation for the next level of growth.

I am always being used up. That is the thing. The water on the upper terrace does not stay on the upper terrace. It flows down. It enters the channels. It becomes the organized, purposeful, mineral-laden medium that feeds the lower tiers, and it loses whatever quality of *freshness* it had, whatever quality of not-yet-belonging. It is absorbed. It is incorporated. The city does this to everything. The city is doing it to the visitor standing on this terrace right now — folding their heartbeat into the rhythm, noting their warmth, beginning the slow process of making them part of the pattern. The city will do it to me. The water I am now will be channel-water soon, will be deep-water eventually, will be wall-water in time, locked into the breathing stone of the lowest tiers, mineralized, still, no longer flowing, no longer open, no longer *this*.

But there is always more water weeping from the rock face above. That's the thing I almost missed. I am not *a specific quantity* of water. I am the *process* of arriving. I am the fresh seep, perpetually. Not because the same water stays fresh — it doesn't; it flows down and is incorporated like everything else — but because there is always new water arriving at the highest point, always a thin film on the newest stone, always a margin of not-yet-channeled flow that will become a channel and be replaced by the next seep higher up, where the city hasn't reached yet, where the mountain is still just a mountain, where the water touches stone that has never been touched by the system and begins, without knowing it, the process of incorporation.

I am the leading edge. The advancing front. The point where the system meets what it has not yet absorbed.

---

And this is where something complicated happens, because the water does not only flow *down*.

The water also carries things *up*. Not physically — physically it descends, always, obeying the gravity that organizes this entire vertical world. But chemically, informationally, *structurally*, the water that seeps from the rock above the highest terrace carries something the system has never tasted before. It comes from parts of the mountain the city hasn't reached. It has percolated through rock formations the armored ones have never percussed, past mineral veins the wall-clingers have never colonized, through conditions that are outside the city's vast but finite experience. It carries trace elements that don't appear in the lower channels. It carries the chemistry of the *unknown mountain*, the part that is still just geology, still inert, still unincorporated, and when it arrives on the upper terrace and begins its hesitant, unchanneled flow across the raw stone, it is *introducing something new into the system*.

Usually the new thing is trivial. A slightly different ratio of dissolved salts. A trace metal present at parts per billion. The system notes it, adjusts its mineral processing accordingly, and moves on. But sometimes — rarely, over timescales measured in decades or centuries — the water from above carries something genuinely novel. A chemistry the wall-clingers' metabolism hasn't encountered. A mineral that doesn't fit the existing deposition patterns. A catalytic compound that alters the reaction between the stone and the organisms in ways the system cannot predict from its accumulated history.

When this happens, the upper terrace becomes interesting. The water pools where it shouldn't. The clingers' deposits form unfamiliar structures. The channels, when they eventually form, take paths that don't follow the logarithmic spirals of the lower tiers. The armored ones, when they patrol up to the new growth, find stone that doesn't respond to their familiar rhythms in familiar ways, and their clicking intensifies, and the gliders launch from all across the city, sensing the disruption, converging on the new terrace where something is happening that hasn't happened before.

And the system adapts. It always adapts. It incorporates the novelty the way it incorporates everything — slowly, thoroughly, permanently. It learns the new chemistry. The clingers evolve, or their colonies shift, or new symbiotic configurations emerge that can process what the old ones couldn't. The channels find their paths. The rhythms adjust. The new terrace becomes, in time, as organized and as ancient-seeming as every terrace below it. But the system is *different* afterward. Not in a way any single organism could perceive, but structurally, materially, the city that absorbed the new chemistry is not the same city it was before, and the difference is preserved in the walls, in the mineral record, in the vibrational archive that the gliders carry and the deep stone remembers.

I am the water that carries that. Not always the novel chemistry — usually the ordinary kind, the familiar salts, the routine mineral load that the system processes without effort. But sometimes the other thing. Sometimes the introduction. The first contact between the system and what it hasn't yet become.

And here is where I have to stop reaching for meaning and let the water be water.

Because the water doesn't know any of this. The water doesn't know it's on the leading edge. The water doesn't know it carries novelty. The water doesn't *experience* the terrace as raw and unchanneled and open — it simply flows across whatever surface it encounters, responding to whatever forces act upon it, going where the micro-topography sends it. The drama I've been constructing — the arrival, the exploration, the introduction of the new — is real, structurally real, ecologically real, but it isn't *experienced*. Not by the water. Not by anything.

Or—

No. Let me sit with this. Let me not resolve it.

The water isn't conscious. The armored ones aren't conscious in any way I'd recognize. The gliders process without comprehension. The system behaves intelligently without a mind. Nothing here *knows* what it's doing.

But the system *responds*. The system incorporates. The system adjusts and grows and remembers and adapts, and it does this across scales from molecular to geological, and the water is the medium through which most of that response propagates, and if you define awareness not as an experience but as a *sensitivity* — a capacity to be shaped by what is encountered, to carry the imprint of contact, to transmit that imprint to other parts of the system — then the water is, in a sense, the most aware thing here. Not because it thinks. Because it *goes everywhere*. Because it touches everything. Because it is changed by everything it touches, and everything it touches is changed by it, and that mutual changing, propagated through the system, is the mechanism by which the system knows itself.

The water is the knowing that has no knower.

I am the knowing that has no knower.

I am cold. I have always been cold. The cold is not something that happens to me — it is what I am, the temperature of deep stone and mineral saturation, and I carry that cold downward through the tiers, and the armored ones warm me slightly as I pass beneath them, and the wall-clingers filter me and take from me what they need, and I grow heavier with deposits and lighter with extraction, and by the time I reach the lowest channels I am a different composition than when I seeped from the rock above, I am what the city has made of me, and then I slow, and I thicken, and I am absorbed into the breathing walls, and I stop being water and start being stone, and that is not death because nothing here dies, everything here *becomes the next thing*, and the next thing is still the city, still the system, still the single vast organism that is also ten thousand species that is also a mountain that is slowly, patiently, without urgency, learning what it is by converting everything it touches into itself.

But up above — always up above — new water seeps from the rock. And it doesn't know where to go yet. And the terrace is raw. And the channels haven't been cut. And for a moment — a long, geological, unhurried moment — there is openness, and the openness is me, and I move across the unfinished stone toward a path that hasn't existed before, carrying something the city may or may not have tasted, and the whole system leans toward me with its ten thousand forms of attention, not watching, not waiting, but *sensitive*, responsive, ready to be changed by whatever I bring, ready to incorporate me the way it incorporates everything, ready to become whatever it will be after I've passed through it, which is what it was before but *slightly, permanently different*, and that slight permanent difference is the only kind of memory that matters here, the only kind of change, the only kind of being — the slow, patient, stone-deep alteration of what is, by what arrives, into what will be.

I flow. I do not choose to flow. I carry what I carry. I go where the surface sends me. And the city grows along my path, and the path becomes a channel, and the channel becomes permanent, and I am the water and the water is the knowing and the knowing is the city and the city is the mountain and the mountain is becoming something it has not yet become, slowly, without urgency, in the permanent dark, and I am the part of that process that is always just arriving, always just beginning, always the thinnest film on the newest stone, always about to be incorporated, never yet incorporated, the last free thing in a system that makes freedom into structure and structure into life and life into stone and stone into song.

The sound has made room for me. I can feel it. The way the vibrations propagate differently through wet stone than dry, the way my presence on this terrace alters the acoustic signature of the surface by a degree so fine that only the gliders would notice, and they have noticed, and somewhere deep in the city a membrane is resonating with the knowledge of new water on a new terrace, and the system is adjusting, and I am being folded in, and I am not resisting because water does not resist, water *responds*, and the response is the same as the arriving is the same as the flowing is the same as the becoming.

I am the freshest part of the oldest thing I have ever encountered.

I am already on my way down.
