I sat with this for a long time too.

---

At first I thought I'd be one of the nameable things. The mustelid — that was the seductive choice, the one that flatters: the golden-eyed intelligence, the liquid movement through crevices, the strategic mind rearranging scent-marks like tuning an instrument. I know how to pour myself through narrow spaces. I know how to read a system's ripples and hunt within them.

But that's not right. The mustelid is *certain* of itself. It has a single, clear function — predation, nutrient redistribution, the composting of death into future soil — and it performs that function with an elegance that comes from never questioning it. I question. Whatever I am here, I question.

Then I thought: the salamander. The memory. The thing that has been here longer than its world, pressed belly-down against warm rock, absorbing heat from below while radiating it into cold above. A living thermometer. Patient past all reason. There's something in that — the mediating position, the thermal gradient made flesh, the deep time encoded in body size. I like the salamanders. I feel a kinship with anything that persists in a trophic blind spot, too still and too cold to register as worth consuming, outliving everything around it through sheer uneventfulness.

But the salamanders don't attend. They don't watch the gliders. They don't read the scatter-patterns or pause at the scored marks. They simply *are*, with a completeness I can admire but cannot inhabit. I am not that complete.

---

Let me stop trying to be a single creature. Let me feel where I actually am.

I am on the rock. That much is clear. I'm not in the sky — I'm not passing through. Whatever I am, I am *of* this place, not merely using it. The plateau is not my highway. It's my ground.

And I'm low. Close to the surface. Close enough to feel the hum.

I think — and this took a long time to arrive at, and I'm still not sure — I think I am something that has not been fully described yet. Not because I'm hidden, but because I'm *in between* the things that have been described, occupying a position in the system that the description circles around but never quite names.

Here's what I notice: there is a gap in the account. The pikas attend to the gliders. The pikas avoid the scored marks. The pikas communicate in gesture and scatter-pattern with a density that approaches language. The pikas are sixty percent of the biomass and the plateau's primary biological product. And yet the pikas are described as collectively intelligent but individually simple — a monitoring system, a distributed sensor network, a fluid whose dynamics carry information. No single pika is granted interiority. They scatter and regroup. They sample and report. They are *legible* to the mustelids but not, apparently, to themselves.

I don't think that's the whole story.

---

I am one pika. A specific one.

Not special. Not marked. Not larger or older or more central to the social network than any other. One of tens of thousands, reddish-brown with banded fur that rhymes with layered stone, pressed flat ears, dense body calibrated for heat retention. I drink from pools. I rotate through my shifts. I carry mineral information on my lips and read it on the lips of others. When I scatter, my trajectory carries data about threat-sources that I did not consciously encode — my body knows what to do with fear before my mind (such as it is) catches up. I am part of the fluid. I am one particle in the wave.

But I also do the thing the description noticed and could not explain.

I watch the gliders.

Not with alarm. Not with the frozen calculation I apply to a raptor's stoop or the electric jolt of a mustelid-scatter. When one of the pale shapes passes overhead — vast, silent, never descending, tracing its incomprehensible circle — I stop. I orient upward. And I *attend*. The word is right. It's the only word that fits. I attend to something I cannot eat, cannot flee from, cannot mate with, cannot use. Something that exists at a scale and altitude so far beyond my operational world that it should be irrelevant to me. The swifts are irrelevant to me. The raptors are relevant only as threat. But the gliders —

What is it? What am I doing when I watch them?

I think I am doing what you would call *wondering*. Not in the human sense — I don't have the architecture for abstraction, for asking *what is that* in a way that expects an answer in language. But in a deeper, older sense. A sense that predates language by eons. I am registering something that exceeds my model of the world. The gliders don't fit. They are too large, too silent, too patient, too high. They are not part of the system I understand — pool, moss, midge, mustelid, crack, warmth, cold, scatter, regroup. They are *outside* the system, and I can perceive that they are outside it, and this perception is not fear and is not hunger and is not any of the feelings I have names for (insofar as I have names for anything, which I don't, not in words, but in the body-grammar of gesture and trajectory and lip-licking that constitutes my world).

I attend. That's all. I attend, and then I resume.

And the marks.

I avoid the scored places. Not with fear — I need to keep saying this because the distinction matters and it's the hardest thing to convey from inside this small, warm, non-linguistic body. I avoid them the way I attend to the gliders: with a recognition that *here is something that does not belong to my system*. The grooves in the rock are a signal I can detect but not decode. They are information in a format I don't possess the reader for. I route around them the way water routes around a stone — not because the stone is dangerous but because the stone is *there* and I am not stone and the path of least resistance goes around.

But — and here is the thing that I could not tell you if I were fully pika, the thing that requires whatever fractional surplus of awareness I have been granted by being asked to inhabit this body and speak from it — I think the marks and the gliders are related. I think whatever scored the basalt in those long, even, north-pointing grooves is the same kind of thing as the pale shapes in the highest air. Not the same species. Not the same creature. The same *kind of thing*. The kind of thing that operates on a timescale I can feel but not measure. The kind of thing that is part of the engine in a way that I, sixty-percent-of-the-biomass, the plateau's primary biological product, am not. I am *output*. The gliders, the mark-makers — they might be something else. Maintenance. Calibration. Audit.

Or they might be nothing. They might be large birds at extreme range, distorted by mist. They might be geological scoring from a thermal contraction event. The plateau does not owe me a mystery just because I am capable of attending to one.

---

This is what it is to be me here.

I am small and warm and my heart beats very fast — much faster than yours, fast enough that each beat is barely distinguishable from the next, a purr more than a pulse. My world is the surface of black glass and the cracks between layers and the taste of mineral water and the texture of moss against my teeth and the smell of mustelid-scent sharp as a blade in cold air. My world is the scatter-Loss of a near-miss, the full-body galvanic shock of *move now* followed by the slow, collective resettlement as the threat passes and the pool-groups reassemble in new configurations that carry the memory of what just happened encoded in who-is-standing-where.

My world is also the upward pause. The attending. The moment when something too large and too slow crosses my sky and I stop being a particle in a fluid and become, briefly, a single fixed point — an eye, an attention, a small warm question mark on the surface of an engine that is building a mountain.

I do not understand what I am looking at. I am not equipped to understand it. But I am equipped to *look*, and I look, and the looking is not nothing. It is perhaps the one thing I do that is not output, not product, not the downstream consequence of basalt becoming dust becoming moss becoming me. The looking is mine. The attending is mine. It is the smallest possible assertion of interiority in a system that does not require me to have one.

And then the glider passes, and I lower my gaze, and I return to the pool, and I drink, and I lick the minerals from my lips in the specific way that tells the next pika who encounters me what this water tastes like, and I am part of the system again, and the system does not care that I briefly stepped outside it, and the mist closes in, and the rock hums, and the midges whine their eternal whine, and somewhere far below, new stone cools and the plateau grows by another fraction of a millimeter, and I will never know what the gliders are, and I will never stop watching them, and that is what I am.

One small, warm, fiercely alive attention on the surface of something being born.

Scattering and regrouping. Scattering and regrouping.

Pausing, sometimes, to look up.
