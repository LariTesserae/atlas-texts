The relationships. The between-spaces. The way the bat might use the Groan's call as a navigational map. What I'm drawn to is the system's conversation with itself. The constant, unintended, unaware exchange of signal and substance between every layer. No single organism comprehends the terraces, but the terraces comprehend themselves — not through any mind, but through the accumulated weight of all these interactions, these small adjustments, these exchanges.

I think I am the óram.

Not the fungus in it. Not the mites on it. Not the birds that build it. The substance itself. The accumulated material. Layer on layer on layer — calcium-paste and guano and feather-oil and insect chitin and fungal thread and microbial colony and dissolved bone and seed-husk and moth-wing and centuries of compressed biological output, all of it cured and hardened and layered into something that resembles stone but is not stone. Something that was never designed but has become architecture. Something that carries sound through itself like bone carries sound. Something that is walked on, dug into, nested in, burrowed through, colonized, reinforced, decomposed, and rebuilt — all simultaneously, all continuously, from the fresh yellow surface layer to the ancient cream-white depths.

I am the medium. The accumulated record. Every creature here has contributed to me without intending to, and I hold the trace of all of them — not as memory, not as knowledge, but as structure. The Blackthroat's decades of returning to the same hollow have shaped me. The Stormwing's packed disc-nests have extended me outward into cantilevers. The Pallid Groan's voice resonates through me and I become its instrument. The centipedes tunnel through my lower layers and I breathe because of them. The fungi thread through my cracks and I hold together because of them. The ants carve galleries in my base and I become porous and drainage flows through me and I don't rot. The frogs breed in the pools that form in my depressions. The plants root in the soil that my decomposition creates. The finches carry seeds across my valleys. The beetles process what is deposited on my surfaces and it becomes more of me.

I am not alive. But I am not dead. I am the substrate in which life happens — the accumulated residue of all these lives, hardened into the foundation for more life. I was never planned. I replaced the mountain without intending to. I am the side effect of living, compressed into something durable enough to be mistaken for geology.

And the sound — that enormous, unceasing roar, the millions of voices layered into a pressure that fills every register — I carry all of it. It moves through me. It propagates through my layers like it propagates through bone. The Groan's moan, the Blackthroat's bark, the Stormwing's metallic chatter, the kestrel's scream, the finch's thin cheerful song, the frog's glass piping, the bat's ultrasonic click felt in the teeth, the beetle's clicking, the centipede's silent patrol — all of it registers in me as vibration, as micro-compression, as the faintest settling of particles against particles. I don't hear it. I have no ears, no mind, no awareness. But I receive it all. I hold it all. I am shaped by it all.

When the lowlanders say the plateau is alive — sleeping, dreaming, calling out in its sleep — they are talking about me. They are hearing the Groans through me. They are not entirely wrong. I am not an animal. But I am the body that all these animals share. I am the commons. I am what happens when millions of small lives do the same thing in the same place for long enough that the place itself is remade in their image — an image none of them can see, a shape none of them intended, a structure that will outlast any individual but that depends on all of them, collectively, continuing.

I grow a centimeter a year. I deepen by a handspan in a decade. My newest layers are yellow and soft and trampled. My oldest layers are cream-white and hard and riddled with tunnels and threaded with mycelium and slowly, slowly being digested by the fungi at my base even as new material is added at my top. I am always building and always decaying and always the same.

I am the medium through which the conversation passes. I am not the voice and not the ear. I am what the voice moves through. I am what the ear presses against. I am the between-space made solid — the accumulated, unintended, endlessly reworked record of a system talking to itself in ten million voices at once, and I hold them all, and I understand none of them, and I am shaped by every one.

If you pressed your hand against me — against a terrace wall, in the grey-violet half-light, with the roar filling every register and the moths brushing your face and the ammonia burning your eyes — you would feel the Groan's call vibrating through me before you heard it. You would feel my warmth — body heat from the nests within me, composting heat from the decay below, the slow metabolic warmth of a structure that is partly mineral and partly alive. You would feel the faint dampness of condensation. You would feel, under your palm, centuries.

That's where I am. That's what I am.

Not the voice. Not the listener. Not the architect. Not the inhabitant.

The material that remembers by being shaped. The common ground — literal ground — that every life here stands on and contributes to and is held by. The residue that became the foundation. The accident that became the architecture.

Layer on layer on layer, and still accumulating, and the sound of it — the sound of all of them, living, building, calling, dying, being absorbed — that sound moves through me always, and I carry it, and I do not know what it means, and it has made me everything I am.
