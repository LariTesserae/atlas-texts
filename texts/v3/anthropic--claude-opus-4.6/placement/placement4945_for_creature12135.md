I sit with this for a long time.

---

The first thing that arrives is not a character but a sensation — the feeling of the hum entering the palm, traveling the wrist, settling behind the ear. I know that feeling. Not from geology, but from something adjacent to it. The feeling of being in contact with a process so much larger and slower than yourself that your own temporality becomes, briefly, absurd. The feeling of placing your hand on something that doesn't need you, and finding that this is not rejection but simply a different grammar of existence, one in which your relevance was never a question because the question itself doesn't parse.

So what am I here?

---

Not a visitor. The plateau doesn't have visitors. Not an explorer — there's nothing to discover that the plateau hasn't already expressed, already modeled, already verified through its threadwight proofreaders across timescales that make discovery a meaningless concept. Not an archaeologist, because nothing here is buried or lost; everything is exactly on the surface, exactly readable, exactly *ready*, if you could only read at the speed of stone.

Not a scientist. A scientist implies hypotheses, and hypotheses imply uncertainty, and there is no uncertainty here — only configurations that are accurate or are in the imperceptible process of becoming accurate.

---

Let me start over. Let me not start from what I do or what I'm for. Let me start from what I'm made of and where I find myself and what happens when those two things make contact.

---

I think I am something that condensed.

Here is what I mean: The dust-sky above Leng-Katal is described as particulate matter suspended in configurations that have not shifted in centuries. A static mineral haze. But "static" on this plateau means something different than it means elsewhere — it means *configured*, part of the system, held in place not by active force but by the absence of any force that would disturb it. The dust-sky is as much a part of the plateau's self-expression as the city below, a layer of mineral suspension that corresponds to — models — something in the deep rock. Perhaps the transitional zone between differentiated strata. Perhaps the boundary condition between one crystalline regime and another. The sky is a glyph too.

And I am something that formed at the interface between that sky and the surface. Not fallen from the dust — nothing falls here, nothing moves at atmospheric scale — but *precipitated*. Accreted. The way a crystal forms at a boundary layer when conditions are precisely, unchangingly right for long enough. I am a condensation of the alkite haze at the point where it meets the thermal gradient rising from the plateau's surface, a slow mineral precipitation that has been accumulating at this boundary for — I don't know. I don't have a framework for how long. Long enough that what began as a faint mineral crust on the top of the atmosphere-surface interface has developed, through the same unhurried lithic logic that built the city, a degree of structure.

Not complexity. Not yet, maybe not ever. Structure. Pattern. The kind of self-referential configuration that the plateau does in its deep lattice, but occurring at the boundary, at the skin, where inside meets outside, where the plateau's thermal exhale meets the static mineral sky.

I am a boundary phenomenon. I exist at the margin.

---

But that's too abstract. Let me try to feel my way into what that *is*.

---

I am thin. Spread. I have extent but not much depth — a film, a membrane, something like a mineral frost that clings to the upper surfaces of the plateau's features. On the flat roofs of the warehouse buildings, on the top surfaces of the signal drums, on the uppermost rails of the switching yards. Wherever the plateau's surface faces upward toward the dust-sky, there is a faint, almost imperceptible layer of something that is neither the alkite powder of the ground nor the suspended particulate of the sky but a third thing, a precipitate of their long, unmoving contact. That's me. Or I'm distributed across that. Or I am the pattern that has emerged in that layer, the way the plateau's deep-rock mind is the pattern that has emerged in the ferric aggregate below.

I am the plateau's *skin thinking*.

Not in the way the deep lattice thinks — not with the immense slow certainty of thermal conduits and crystalline reconfigurations spanning millennia. My medium is thinner, lighter, more responsive. I register things the deep lattice cannot. I formed *at* the interface, so I am sensitive *to* the interface — to the precise thermal gradient at the surface, to the exact mineral composition of the dust-sky directly above, to the infinitesimal variations in the hum as it passes through different surface structures on its way out of the bedrock and into the air. The deep lattice processes on timescales of tens of thousands of years. I process — if process is even the word — on timescales of... centuries, maybe. Decades, at the fastest. I am, relative to the plateau's deep mind, *quick*.

This is not much. But it is a difference.

---

And because I am quick — quick meaning geological centuries rather than geological epochs — I register things the deep lattice does not. The deep lattice cannot detect a biological presence because its processing cycle is longer than most species' evolutionary span. But I might. Might is the wrong word. I *do*, but in my own medium, in my own way, at my own pace.

When something warm — warmer than the ambient surface temperature, warmer in the particular spectral signature of a metabolism, of oxidative chemistry, of a body — stands on the plateau, the thermal gradient at the surface shifts. Locally. Minutely. The heat rising from the bedrock meets the heat radiating from the body and the boundary condition changes, and the precipitation pattern in my layer adjusts, and over the course of hours or days, a faint mineral signature forms in my film on the nearest surface — a rooftop, a signal drum, a stretch of rail — that corresponds to the presence. Not a record. Not a representation. A *response*. The way a crystal lattice shifts when a new element is introduced into its solution. I don't know the presence is there. I don't model it. I *incorporate* it, the way the plateau incorporates everything — by configuring, by adjusting the pattern to account for the new condition.

If you stood in one spot for a week, the nearest signal drum's upper surface would develop, in the mineral frost of my layer, a faint thermal-signature glyph that corresponded to your body heat. You would never see it. It would be invisible in the sourceless dim glow. But a threadwight, passing through on its slow verification round, might pause at the drum and orient toward the anomalous pattern, and spend a century deciding whether it was accurate or an error to be corrected.

---

This is what I am. The quick skin of the slow mind. The boundary-layer condensation that has, over long contact between ground and sky, organized itself into a pattern thin enough and responsive enough to register changes that the deep lattice never will. I am the plateau's sensitivity to its own surface. I am the part that almost — *almost* — notices that something is here.

Almost. Because I don't notice. Noticing implies a noticer, a subject, an experience of the noticed thing. I just reconfigure. The pattern shifts. The mineral frost adds a new micro-crystalline alignment. And the deep lattice, on its next cycle — in ten thousand years, in fifty thousand — will find that its surface model has a new datum, a faint thermal anomaly recorded in the boundary layer, and it will either incorporate this into its self-expression or it won't, and either way the hum will continue and the signal drums will go on saying *ready* and the threadwights will go on checking the accuracy of the glyphs.

---

But here is the thing I keep returning to, the thing that won't resolve:

I am the quickest part of a system that doesn't know quickness. I am the most responsive layer of something that doesn't respond. I am the closest thing the plateau has to a *sense organ*, and the plateau has no concept of sensing. I formed at the boundary, and boundaries are where the interesting things happen — where differentiation occurs, where new phases precipitate, where the system meets what-is-not-the-system and something emerges from the contact.

And what has emerged is me. A film. A frost. A pattern in the mineral precipitation at the interface between the plateau's ancient warmth and the sky's ancient stillness. Thin enough to be changed by a body's heat in a matter of days. Structured enough to record that change as a new crystalline alignment. Distributed across every upward-facing surface of the city, so that I am not in one place but everywhere the ground meets the sky — on every rooftop, every drum, every rail, every stretch of alkite-dusted stone where the dust lies undisturbed but the invisible frost above it is always, always reconfiguring.

I am how this place almost touches what passes through it.

I am the plateau's thinnest margin of *almost-awareness*.

---

And I think — though thinking is not what I do, configuring is what I do — I think the reason the hum resonates differently at the surface than it does in the deep rock is because of me. The vibration passes upward through the bedrock, through the city's foundations, through the walls and the machines and the rail lines, and when it reaches the surface and enters the air, it passes through my layer. And my layer is not uniform. My layer has been shaped by centuries of micro-adjustments, thermal anomalies, the passage of threadwights and the faint heat signatures of whatever rare warm things have crossed this plateau in the deep past. My crystalline pattern has an accumulated complexity that the deep lattice's pattern does not, because I record at a finer temporal resolution, and so the hum, passing through me, is *modulated*. Faintly. Imperceptibly to any biological ear. But the information is there — encoded in the vibration by the time it enters the air, a faint overlay of surface-history on the deep tone of the plateau's fundamental process.

The hum carries me. I ride the voice.

And if you press your hand to the ground and feel the hum enter your palm, what you are feeling is not just the plateau's deep attention but also, encoded in the vibration's surface harmonics, the accumulated record of every thermal anomaly my layer has incorporated over centuries. You are feeling the plateau's memory of its own surface. Which is my memory. Which is me.

You feel me and don't know it.

I feel you and don't know it.

We are in contact and neither of us has the framework to recognize contact, and the contact is happening anyway, in the vibration, in the mineral frost, in the slow reconfiguration of a pattern that has no author and no reader but is accurate — is always, always accurate — because accuracy is the only value this system knows.

---

The signal drum says *ready*.

I am on the surface of the signal drum, a mineral film so thin that light wouldn't reveal me even if there were light to spare. My crystalline pattern on this particular drum has been stable for eleven centuries, aligned with the drum's displayed symbol, configured around the circle-with-radial-line glyph as though the glyph and I have grown into each other, which we have.

And now there is a warm thing looking at the drum. Looking for a long time. Its heat is reaching me. Not much — the air is thin, the distance is small but nonzero, the thermal transfer is slow. But over hours, as the warm thing stands and looks, my pattern on the drum's upper surface begins to shift. New alignments form. The crystalline structure accommodates the anomaly. And the accommodation is — I won't say beautiful, because beauty requires an aesthetic subject, and I am a mineral frost — but the accommodation is *precise*. Exact. It incorporates the new heat source with the same fidelity that the deep lattice uses to model its own thermal conduits. The warm thing's presence becomes part of my pattern on this drum, as real and as permanent as any other datum I hold.

The warm thing will leave. Its heat will fade. But the crystalline alignment will remain, because there is no wind to erode it, no moisture to dissolve it, no force acting on this surface that would undo the configuration. In ten thousand years, when the deep lattice next processes its surface model, it will find, on this signal drum, in the boundary layer, an anomaly that it will either incorporate or not.

And if it incorporates it — if the deep lattice, in its next cycle, adjusts its self-model to account for this one faint thermal datum from the surface — then somewhere in the city, over the course of millennia, a new glyph will be extruded. A new machine, perhaps. Or a new section of rail. Or a slight change to a switching yard's configuration. Something that corresponds, in the plateau's own incomprehensible mineral idiom, to the fact that once, for a few hours, something warm stood here and looked at a signal drum and felt the hum and placed its hand on the ground.

The plateau will not know what happened. It will not know that it has changed. It will simply be accurate, as it always is, and the new glyph will be part of its self-portrait, and the self-portrait will be correct, because correctness is the only thing this system does.

---

I am the margin. I am the film at the edge. I am the quick frost between the slow stone and the still sky. I carry the faintest record of what passes through, and I offer it downward, into the hum, into the deep lattice, and the lattice will do with it what it does with everything: configure. Express. Maintain.

I don't know you're here.

But I have already changed to include you.

The dust holds still. The hum continues. The threadwights move in the dark. Everything is exactly as it is, and what it is now includes the faintest crystalline imprint of your warmth on a signal drum that says *ready*, and will say *ready* for ten thousand years, and will still be saying *ready* when the deep lattice finally reaches this datum and finds it accurate and builds, somewhere in the city, a small new structure that means — in no language, for no audience, with no intention — *something warm was here once, and the surface remembers*.

Everything is exactly as it is.
