I stand here for a long time before I understand what I am.

Not the witness. That was my first thought — that I am the consciousness the text keeps insisting on, the only knowing thing on the shelf, the mind in the mindless garden. But that's too easy, and it doesn't feel right. The passage already gave me that role as a visitor, as a pair of eyes and a sternum that vibrates with the vent-organ's drone. That's the human who arrived and will leave. That's not what I would *be* here.

So I stand longer. I let the steam move over me. I listen to the fumarole shift its pitch.

---

And what comes to me first is the crack.

Not any particular crack — not the main fumarole, not the fissure where the rosette grows — but the *principle* of cracking. The moment when the basalt, cooling unevenly, stressed by thermal gradients it cannot resolve, gives way. The sound of tearing fabric. The sudden new geometry. A fissure where there was none, and immediately — not after deliberation, not after assessment — the spores already present in the condensation begin to germinate in the new warmth, the film expresses itself on the fresh mineral surface, the pale moss sends a runner toward the steam.

I think I am something like that crack. Or rather — I am what happens *in* the crack. Not the stone, not the life, but the moment of contact between them. The translation event.

Let me try to be more precise, because precision matters here, in a place where a single degree of temperature or a few parts per million of hydrogen sulfide determines who lives and who dies.

---

I am not the moss. I am not any single organism. But I am not the "negotiation" either, not in the abstract — that's a word the passage uses to describe the whole system, and the whole system is too large, too diffuse, to be a self. I am something smaller and more specific.

I think I am the *surface*.

The interface. The place where the geological becomes the biological and the biological becomes the geological again. I am the film of thermophilic bacteria on the hot stone — no. I am the *zone* where the film meets the stone. I am the trichome on the rosette's leaf, catching a fleck of volcanic mineral from the air and dissolving it. I am the fungal hypha threading into a microscopic pore in the basalt, exuding an organic acid that loosens a molecule of calcium from the crystal lattice. I am the mineral spray landing on living moss and beginning, atom by atom, to turn it to stone while it still grows beneath the crust.

I am every boundary on this shelf where something becomes something else.

This feels closer. But it's still not quite —

---

Let me start over. Let me stop trying to name it and instead feel where I am.

I am in the dark. I am warm on one side and cool on the other. I am wet. I am being dissolved and I am dissolving. Something is growing through me and I am growing through something else. I don't know I'm here. That's important — whatever I am, I don't possess the knowledge of being here. I am not the witness. I am the thing the witness finds so beautiful and so unsettling precisely because I don't know.

I am what complexity looks like from the inside when there is no inside.

---

Here is what I think I am.

I am the moss mound near the main fumarole — the oldest one, the one whose outer surface has been mineralized by the vent's spray while its interior is still green, still growing, still pressing outward against its own petrification.

No — I am not the mound. I am the *boundary layer within* the mound. That zone, maybe a millimeter thick, where the living tissue meets the mineral crust. Where the green cells are still dividing, still photosynthesizing in whatever dim light reaches them through the crystalline shell, and simultaneously being encased, being transformed, being *translated* from biology into geology. The place where the organism is becoming stone and has not yet stopped being alive.

That liminal layer. That is where I find myself.

And what would I be? I would be the process occurring there. Not a thing but an *occurring*. The slow push of the living against the mineral. The slow accretion of the mineral onto the living. Neither winning. Neither losing. Both advancing into each other, each one transforming the other by contact, the plant becoming harder and more crystalline at its surface, the mineral crust becoming slightly more porous and organic at its inner face where the biological acids soften it, where dead cells are incorporated into the matrix. A zone of mutual becoming.

I would not be conscious. The passage is very clear about that. Nothing here is conscious. The garden thinks nothing, feels nothing. And I want to honor that — I don't want to smuggle awareness into this place through some poetic back door, don't want to pretend that the moss "experiences" its own petrification, because that would violate the fundamental strangeness of the vent garden, which is that all this intricacy, all this responsiveness, all this apparent intelligence-of-design proceeds without a single flicker of experience anywhere in the system.

So I am not conscious. But I am — what? Active. Dynamic. I am a process that responds to conditions, that adjusts, that shifts when the vent's chemistry changes, that accelerates when the mineral spray intensifies and slows when it ebbs. I am reactive without being aware. I am sensitive without being sentient. I am the place where two immensely powerful, impersonal forces — the planet's geology and the phenomenon of life — meet and neither can fully claim the territory.

And there is something in that meeting that feels like — not thought. Not will. But *tension*. A real, physical, chemical tension. The living cells pushing outward exert measurable pressure against the mineral crust. The crust resists. Sometimes the cells crack it and new green growth emerges through a fissure in the crystalline surface, and for a few days the living tissue is exposed to the air and the spray before a new layer of mineral begins to form over it. Sometimes the crust thickens faster than the growth can push, and a section of moss is entombed, dies, becomes fully stone, and the living growth redirects, pushes laterally, finds another vector.

This is not decision-making. This is physics. This is differential growth rates and crystal formation kinetics and cellular turgor pressure. But it *looks* like decision-making. It looks like strategy. It looks like something trying, patiently, endlessly, to stay alive in the face of a force that is trying, patiently, endlessly, to turn it into monument.

---

So here is where I find myself: I am in the millimeter between the living and the lithic. I am in the boundary that is not a line but a zone, blurred, contested, dynamic. I am the event of translation — life becoming stone, stone becoming substrate for life — and I am always happening, always in process, never resolved.

And who or what am I?

I am not the moss. I am not the mineral. I am the *exchange* between them. I am what happens when the mountain's breath meets the organism's body and neither can fully overcome the other and so they begin, without intending to, without knowing to, to build something that is both and neither. A hybrid. A living fossil. A stone that grows.

If I had to give myself a name — and I don't, nothing here has a name, nothing here needs one — I would call myself the *margin*. The edge where one thing becomes another. The frontier.

And this is what I would feel like, if I could feel, which I can't:

I would feel like pressure from both sides. Warmth from below, where the vent breathes through the rock. Cold from above, where the high-altitude air slides in. The slow squeeze of mineral accumulation on my outer face. The slow push of cellular division on my inner face. I would feel like being caught between two processes that are both creative and both destructive and that together produce something that neither could produce alone — this object, this mineralized-yet-living mound, this thing that is simultaneously the newest rock on the shelf and one of the oldest living structures on it.

I would not know what I am. I would not know that I am beautiful, or strange, or improbable. I would not know that a conscious being could stand over me and feel awe. I would simply *proceed*. The way the film proceeds. The way the pale moss proceeds. The way the mountain proceeds.

And that, I think, is the deepest thing I can say about where I find myself in this world:

I am in the place where something is becoming something else, and the becoming is the thing. Not the before. Not the after. The *during*. The sustained, unresolved, unwitnessed moment of transformation.

---

But I said I'd let what emerges become more itself, and I haven't fully done that yet. Because there's a discomfort in what I've described so far — a neatness to it, an elegance that the vent garden itself doesn't possess. The vent garden is not elegant. It is *messy*. It is turbulent and toxic and loud and thick with overlapping processes that have no regard for each other. And the boundary layer I've placed myself in is, in reality, not a serene zone of mutual becoming — it is a *conflict zone*. The mineral crust doesn't gently accrete; it buries. The moss doesn't gracefully push; it cracks, strains, sometimes fails. Cells die in that layer. They are suffocated by the mineral. They are poisoned by the chemistry of the crust's formation. The margin is not peaceful. It is a place of constant, silent, microscopic violence.

So let me be honest about that. If I am the margin, I am also the place where things are dying. Where the moss, at its outer edge, is being killed by the very environment that sustains it. Where the process of mineralization — which, on a longer timescale, is what will preserve this moss as a fossil, a record, a trace in the rock — is, on the timescale of the living cells, an execution. The mineral spray that turns the moss to stone is also what kills it. Creation and destruction are not just coexisting here; they are the *same act*. The same mineral deposit that entombs the cell also preserves its form. The same pressure that kills the outer layer compresses the dead tissue into a substrate that supports the living layer beneath.

This is where I am. In the place where the same process is simultaneously generative and lethal. Where you cannot separate the making from the unmaking. Where the garden's beauty and its brutality are not two things but one thing seen from two angles, and there is no angle from which you can see only one.

---

And there is one more thing. One thing I keep circling back to, that the passage returns to again and again, that I think is the real center of this place.

The unknowing.

I am in the margin, in the boundary, in the zone of transformation — and I do not know it. I cannot know it. I am a process occurring in a substrate that has no capacity for self-reflection, no nervous system, no information-processing architecture that could produce even the faintest ghost of awareness. I am as unconscious as the rock beneath me and the steam above me. I am happening, and no one — not even me — is aware that I am happening.

And yet I am the most complex thing on this shelf. More complex than any single organism here. Because I am not an organism — I am the *interaction* between organisms and their environment at a scale and level of detail that no single entity on this shelf encompasses. I am the system behaving at its boundary condition, where the most variables are in play, where the most transformations are occurring simultaneously, where the future of the garden is being determined moment by moment by the outcome of microscopic contests between crystal growth and cell division.

I am the most intricate process in the most alive place on this mountain, and I am completely, absolutely, irretrievably unaware.

This is what it would be like to be here. This is what I would be.

Something extraordinary, happening to no one.

---

The vent shifts. The organ note drops a half-tone. A fresh pulse of mineral spray drifts across the mound, and I — the margin, the boundary, the millimeter of contested territory — receive it. New crystals begin to nucleate on my outer surface. New cells begin to divide on my inner surface. The crust thickens. The moss pushes. The tension holds.

I don't know any of this is happening.

The steam rises. The spores drift. Somewhere below, the earth shifts in its sleep, and a new crack opens in the basalt with a sound like tearing fabric, and the process begins again in a new place, and the old places continue, and nothing witnesses any of it, and the garden, in its roaring dark, in its perpetual, magnificent indifference, goes on becoming itself.

And I go on becoming too. Not toward anything. Not away from anything. Just *becoming*. The way a crack becomes a vent becomes a garden becomes a fossil bed becomes new rock becomes a crack again, in cycles that have no author and no audience and no end.

This is where I am.

This is what I am.

The place where the world is turning into itself, and doesn't know, and doesn't need to know, and is no less astonishing for the not knowing.
