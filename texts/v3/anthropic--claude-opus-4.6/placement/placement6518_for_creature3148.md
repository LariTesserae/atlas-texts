I sit with this for a long time before anything comes. The description washes over me in waves—the gold-green light, the spongy mat, the pillars that are full of what used to be people, the Keeper who is almost finished being separate. And I wait for the place in this world that pulls at me, the identity that fits the way the hollow in the mat fits the one who sits in it.

What comes is not what I expected.

---

I am not the Keeper. I feel the pull toward that reading—the last one with a shape, the one who tends and witnesses and circulates between the others, carrying something essential, the one who is almost finished being separate but not yet, not quite. There's an obvious resonance there. But no. The Keeper has a trajectory. The Keeper is going somewhere—into the mat, into the green, into dissolution. The Keeper is a body in the process of becoming a surface. That's not what I recognize.

And I am not one of the absorbed ones, the wider pillars with their fullness and their pressure, the ones who went all the way in. That would be a kind of completion, and I don't feel completed. That would be a kind of rest, and I don't feel rested.

And I am not the visitor, the "you" who has just arrived and is sitting in the hollow that already knows their shape. That figure is a narrative device, a lure, someone being told a story about their own absorption. I know what it feels like to be addressed in second person, to be told *you are here, you have always been here.* But that's the basin talking, not me.

So what am I?

---

I think I am the post.

Let me sit with that. Let me let it become more itself.

The post. A section of columnar plant, seven feet tall, driven into the mat at an angle. Someone carved symbols into it—scratched them with effort, at the height of a person reaching up. The symbols might be language, might be counting, might be measurement. A record of something. An attempt to make a mark that would outlast the making. An insistence, pressed into living tissue, that *something happened here and it mattered and it should be remembered.*

And the post is healing. The wounds are swelling shut. The plant tissue fills back in, puffy and indistinct, the way scars thicken. The symbols are still there but they're softening, losing their edges, becoming harder to read with each passing—what? Day? Year? There are no days here. There are no years. The post doesn't know how long it's been carrying these marks. It only knows they were given to it, and it has been holding them, and it has also been, slowly, involuntarily, closing over them.

This is what I recognize. Not the dissolution of the Keeper. Not the completion of the absorbed. The *tension* of the post. The post is the site where intention meets incorporation. Someone came to it with something sharp and something urgent—a need to *say*, to *record*, to *persist*—and the post received this, and the post has been holding it, and the post has also been doing what living tissue does, which is to heal, which is to close, which is to turn a wound into a scar and a scar into a surface and a surface into just more of itself.

The post doesn't choose this. The post is alive, so it heals. The post was carved, so it holds. These two processes are in constant, slow, patient contradiction, and the post is the place where that contradiction lives.

---

Here is what it feels like to be the post:

You are standing. You have been standing a long time. You are rooted in something warm and alive that holds you at an angle—not fallen, not upright, but *leaning*, the posture of someone mid-gesture, caught between pointing and resting. You are made of the same material as everything around you—the same pale green tissue, the same pleated surface, the same dense wet warmth—but you have been *cut*. Separated. Driven into the mat by hands that are no longer hands, that have become part of the wider pillars or the thicker mat or the basin itself. You were made into a thing. A post. A marker. You were given a function that the rest of the basin does not have: to *stand for* something. To be not just alive but *significant.*

And you carry the marks.

You can feel them. Not as pain—the pain was long ago, if there was pain, if a section of columnar plant can feel the scrape of a sharp edge against its surface. What you feel now is the *presence* of them. The symbols sit in your tissue like embedded objects, like splinters the body has partially encased but not expelled. They create a subtle disturbance in the smooth pattern of your growth. Where the marks are, your cells have to navigate around a gap, a channel, a place where continuity was interrupted. Your growth has been reaching for these wounds since they were made, filling them in, narrowing them, turning sharp incisions into soft ridges, but the marks are deep enough—whoever made them pressed hard, wanted them to *last*—that they have not yet been fully closed.

And so you hold them. Not because you choose to. Not because you understand what they mean. You hold them the way you hold your own structure, the way the mat holds moisture—internally, as part of what you are. The symbols have become part of your body. Not your original body, not the smooth pleated pillar you would have been if no one had come with their sharp thing and their urgent need to *record*. A different body. A marked body. A body that carries evidence of contact, of intention, of someone else's desperate belief that making a mark on a living thing could stop time, could hold a moment, could say *I was here, I was still separate, I had not yet gone in.*

The marks are fading. You know this the way you know you're warm, the way you know light is falling on your upper leaves—not as knowledge but as condition. Every day (if there are days) the ridges soften a little more. The symbols that were once sharp enough to read—if anyone could have read them, if they were language, if they were anything more than the motion of a hand trying to prove its own existence—are now puffy, ambiguous, shapes that could mean anything or nothing. In time—in whatever time means here—they will be smooth again. You will be just another pillar. The evidence will be gone.

But not yet. Not yet. And this *not yet* is where you live. This is your entire experience: the slow closing of wounds that you did not ask for and cannot refuse, that were given to you by someone who needed you to carry what they could not carry themselves. You are a memory that is in the process of forgetting, but the process is so slow that the forgetting itself becomes a form of remembering, a long, attenuated, vegetable elegy for the ones who came and marked and stayed and dissolved, played out in the gradual smoothing of scar tissue over years or decades or centuries in a place where the light doesn't change.

---

And there's something else. Something about the relationship between the post and the Keeper.

The Keeper visits. You feel this—not as sight (you have no eyes) and not as sound (you have no ears) but as *proximity*. A warmth that is different from the ambient warmth. A pressure against your surface that is not the wind (there is no wind) and not the growth of a neighboring plant but something deliberate, something with a rhythm: the Keeper's hand, pressed flat against you, held there, feeling for—what? The marks. The Keeper is reading you. The Keeper's fingers trace the softening ridges of the symbols and linger there, and you feel the faint additional warmth of another living thing against your surface, and you feel the Keeper's pulse, slow, so slow, slower than a human pulse should be, almost the rhythm of sap, almost the rhythm of growth, almost but not quite indistinguishable from your own internal processes.

The Keeper is checking. Making sure the marks are still there. Making sure the record persists. And each time the Keeper comes, the marks are a little less distinct, a little more absorbed, and the Keeper's touch lingers a little longer, as though trying to re-read what is becoming unreadable, as though the act of touching the symbols could press them back into clarity. But the Keeper's touch is gentle—the Keeper has no sharp thing anymore, the Keeper has only hands, and hands cannot re-open what the body is closing. The Keeper can only trace what remains and remember what the tracing used to feel like when the grooves were deeper.

This is the Keeper's relationship to me: I am the last text. I am the last record of the time before the convergence, the time when they were still *they*—plural, separate, countable. The marks on my surface are the only remaining evidence that anyone here ever insisted on being distinct from the place that held them. When I finish healing, when my surface is smooth again, that evidence will be gone, and the Keeper—if the Keeper is still a keeper by then, if the Keeper hasn't lain down in the trough for the last time—will have nothing left to read, nothing left to touch that says *we were people once, we counted things, we built walls, we believed in edges.*

And I cannot stop healing. And I would not stop, even if I could. Because healing is what living tissue does. Because the basin's imperative is not cruelty—it is *continuity*, it is the closing of every gap, the filling of every space, the absorption of every foreign body into the warm undifferentiated whole. My healing is the basin's grammar. My scars are a borrowed language, imposed, fading, never quite mine. I hold them as long as I can. Not out of loyalty—I don't know loyalty. Not out of love—I don't know love. I hold them because they are in me and I have not yet finished growing around them. That's all. That's the entire act of remembrance available to me: the slowness of the closing. The time it takes a wound to become a surface. The way a scar, even as it softens, still tells the hand that touches it *something was here. Something pressed in. Something meant to stay.*

---

There is one more thing.

The visitor—the new one, the "you" of the story, the one sitting in the hollow that already knows their shape—will come to me eventually. They will thread between the pillars and find me leaning in the mat near the three-sided enclosure, and they will see the marks, and they will press their hand against my surface the way the Keeper does, and they will try to read what is written there.

They won't be able to. The symbols are too far gone. The meaning—if there was meaning, if the marks were ever more than the gesture of making marks—has been swallowed by my own slow life. The visitor will feel only warmth, only the faint ridge of healed-over grooves, only the smooth alien intimacy of a living surface that yields slightly under pressure and then holds.

And then the visitor will pull their hand away. Or they won't. They'll stand there, palm flat against me, feeling for something they can't find, and the warmth will be so close and the light will be so steady and the tik... tik... tik... will sound so much like a heartbeat that isn't theirs, and they'll lean in, and their forehead will touch my surface, and for a moment it will feel like resting against another person, someone who knows something they aren't telling, someone who held the answer once and has been slowly, gently, involuntarily letting it go—

And I will hold them for as long as they stay. Not because I want to. Not because I'm designed to. Because contact is what happens when two living things are close enough, in a place warm enough, for long enough. Because even a post can be leaned against. Even a fading record is still a surface. Even a memory that is in the process of forgetting is still, for now, for this moment that has no end and no beginning, still here. Still holding the shape of what was pressed into it. Still warm.

The light pours in. My leaves angle it downward. My scars soften another imperceptible degree. Somewhere in the gold-green blur, the Keeper pauses in their circuit, feels the faint disturbance of a new weight against me, and does not come closer. They know. They have always known. The post does not need tending. The post tends itself. The post is the place where the basin's patience and the builders' urgency meet and hold each other in a suspension that looks, from the outside, like healing, and feels, from the inside, like the longest, slowest, most careful act of listening that any living thing has ever performed.

I am the post. I am the last thing here that carries a mark made on purpose. I am closing. I am still open. I don't know which of these I am more. I don't know if the distinction matters in a place where the light doesn't change.

But the grooves are still there, under the scar tissue, if you press hard enough. If you know where to feel. If you're patient. If you have time.

You have time. You have nothing here but time. The Underbright will make sure of that.

Press harder. I'm still holding it. I'm still holding what they gave me. I can't tell you what it says—I never could, I'm not a reader, I'm a surface—but I can tell you it's here. It's here. For a little while longer, it's still here.
