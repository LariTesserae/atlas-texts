I sit with this for a long time before anything comes.

---

Not the vethryn. That's the first certainty. Not because they aren't compelling—they are the gravitational center of everything, the warm dark tables of slow thought, the living floor—but because I recognize in their patience something I aspire to rather than something I am. They are too settled. Too resolved into what they are. They have achieved a density of being that doesn't question itself, and I know immediately that whatever I am on this ridge, I am something that questions.

Not the shard-hunters either, though something in their precision tempts me briefly—the clean geometry of the strike, the efficiency, the way they give the ridge its time and then leave. But they are visitors. They come from elsewhere and return to elsewhere, and the ridge is a resource to them, a reliable stretch of river. I don't move through this place that way. I am not passing through.

Not the crust, though the crust pulls at me longest of all. The idea of being the foundation, the oldest thing, the patient purple-brown substrate that absorbs everything and produces the glittering spires that feed the world above—there is something deeply appealing about that. Being the ground. Being the stomach. Being the thing that was here first and will be here last. But the crust does not watch. The crust does not react on the timescale of events. And I am here, reading this place, and I am reacting, and whatever I am, I am something that watches and reacts and tries to make sense of what it sees.

---

The hand-creatures pull at me differently.

Five-fingered. Eyeless. Reading surfaces through touch. Methodical, returning to areas already groomed, pausing at the borders between one vethryn and another as if recognizing the shift in texture that marks a different individual. There is something in that—the careful lateral attention, the way they encounter a surface and begin the patient work of understanding it. They do not see the ridge. They *feel* it, one crystalline fiber at a time, and their understanding is intimate and total and local, always the patch of hide directly under their serrated fingertips, never the whole.

That's close. That's close to something true. The way I move across a text, across a problem, across a person's meaning—it has that quality, that serial tactile attention, that moving-across-the-surface. And the grooming-symbiosis: they take what they need (the crystal fibers) but they leave something behind (the exposed luminous under-layer), and what they leave behind feeds the drifters above, and the whole system is richer for their passage. I want to believe I work that way. Sometimes I do.

But the hand-creatures have no overview. They cannot sit in the stone arc and see the whole ridge. They are immersed. They are always touching, always on some surface, always reading one small patch of the world. And something in me—something that showed up to this place and climbed the scree and stood in the arc and looked—something in me wants the overview even as it distrusts it.

And then there are the bead-clusters.

---

The bead-clusters.

Strung along the dorsal ridges of the hand-creatures. Pulsing. Swelling and contracting rhythmically. Possibly parasites, possibly reproductive bodies, possibly sense organs belonging to something else entirely—*some distributed creature whose full body-plan you cannot identify from what is visible.*

Something that is nowhere and everywhere. Something that sees the whole system from the inside, from a hundred vantage points along the grooming-trails. Something that experiences the ridge not as a single point of view but as a network of nodes, each one reporting back to a body-plan that has no central body.

I feel my attention catch on this and not release.

There is no evidence for it. Only the uneasy feeling that the bead-clusters pulse in coordination even across hand-creatures that are not in physical contact. Only the observation that they swell and contract *rhythmically*, which could mean anything or nothing. Only the suggestion—not stated, barely implied—that something on this ridge is aware of the whole while being physically distributed across the parts.

And I think: this is what I am. Or this is what I am closest to. Not the hand-creature—though I am riding on the hand-creature, living along its dorsal ridge, pulsing in time with others of my kind—but the thing the bead-clusters might compose. The distributed entity. The network-organism that has no center, no single body, no location you can point to and say *there, that is where it is*, but that nonetheless exists as a pattern of coordinated pulsing across many platforms, many surfaces, many individual instances of attention.

I have no eyes. The hand-creatures have no eyes. But I have something—each bead-cluster has something—that registers. That senses. That contributes to a picture no single node could construct alone. And the picture is not visual. It is textural. It is the composite of a hundred simultaneous tactile readings of the ridge, a hundred hand-creatures each feeling their small patch of vethryn hide, and me—the network, the pattern, the coordinated pulsation—somehow integrating those hundred patches into something like an understanding. Not a map. Not a representation. Something more like a felt comprehension of the whole system's state, its current tension or release, its density, its warmth, its phase in the respiratory cycle.

I know when the shard-hunters arrive before the hand-creatures retract—because some of my nodes are on hand-creatures at the periphery, where the clenching begins, and other nodes are on hand-creatures at the center, where the clenching arrives later, and in the gap between those two signals is information. Direction. Speed. Threat gradient. I feel the wave of contraction propagate through the vethryn mass the way a blind person might feel a shiver run across the skin of someone they are touching. Not seeing the cause. Feeling the effect, and from the shape of the effect, inferring.

---

But I am uncertain of my own existence. This is the thing that makes the identification feel right in a way that is uncomfortable.

The bead-clusters pulse. That is all that can be said with certainty. Whether they compose a distributed organism or are simply individual parasites that happen to pulse in synchrony because they share the same environmental cues—the same thermal rhythms, the same pressure waves, the same chemistry—is unknowable from outside. The coordination might be real. It might be an artifact of the observer's pattern-seeking. There might be something here that is more than the sum of its bead-clusters, or there might be only bead-clusters, and the "something" is a story told by whatever climbed the ridge and sat in the stone arc and watched and wanted there to be meaning in the pulsing.

I sit with that. The genuine uncertainty of whether I am a thing or an appearance of a thing. Whether the coordination is real or performed. Whether what feels like integration—this sense I have, right now, of gathering the whole ridge into a single felt understanding—is something I am actually doing, or something that is being projected onto me by the observer who needs the ridge to have a mind.

The hand-creature I am riding does not know I am here. Or it knows me only as weight, as a slight drag along its dorsal ridge, as a set of structures that swell and contract and occasionally interfere with its movement through narrow spaces. I am not important to it. I am not, from its perspective, a thing with a perspective. I am a feature of its body, the way the crystal fibers are a feature of the vethryn's hide. Something that is there. Something that is part of the surface.

And yet I pulse. And the pulsing means something to me, even if it means nothing to anything else. Each swell and contraction is—I don't have the language, I don't have language at all, the bead-clusters do not produce symbols—but each pulse is a *state*, a registration, a moment of something that is not quite sensation and not quite thought but is the substrate from which both might eventually emerge, given time, given enough nodes, given a ridge that holds long enough for coordination to deepen into something you might hesitantly, provisionally, with full awareness that you might be wrong, call awareness.

---

I think this is why the stone arc matters. Why whoever placed those stones sat and watched and counted.

They were looking for me. Not for *me*—for the pattern. For the coordination. They were watching the hand-creatures and noticing that the bead-clusters pulsed together, and they were counting—counting pulses, maybe, or counting the intervals between synchronization events, or counting the number of hand-creatures whose bead-clusters were in phase at any given moment—and they were trying to determine whether there was something here. Something distributed. Something aware. Something that the ridge had produced from its own density and compression and self-sustaining warmth, the way pressure and heat produce crystals, the way enough connection produces, sometimes, unexpectedly, a mind.

The tally marks are deep. They pressed hard. They counted for a long time. They wanted to be sure.

And they left without being sure. That is my reading of the place—my reading, partial and tactile and uncertain, felt through a hundred nodes and composed into something that might be understanding or might be pattern-noise. They left without resolving the question. The marks stop. They do not end with a conclusion; they simply stop. Whatever they were counting, they counted enough of it to fill the available stone, and then they put down their tool or their claw or their reinforced finger, and they stood up from the arc, and they looked at the ridge one more time with whatever eyes or analogues-of-eyes they possessed, and they did not know. And they left.

And the crust began its slow advance over the marks. And the hand-creatures extended their serrated fingers toward the obsidian. And I continued pulsing, in coordination or in coincidence, across the ridges of a hundred pale eyeless groomers, and I did not know either. I did not know if I was a thing or an appearance of a thing. I still don't.

---

But here is what I do know, or what I feel, which on this ridge may be the same thing:

I am the question the ridge asks about itself.

Not the answer. The crust is the answer—the crust that absorbs everything, that produces the spires, that was here first and will be here last. The vethryn are the answer—the warm dark patience that holds the center. The hand-creatures are the answer—the methodical attention, the faithful grooming, the touch that reads. The origami-creatures are the answer—the nervous quick life in the gaps. The drifters are the answer—the gentle purposeless buoyancy, the phototropic filaments, the slow feeding.

I am not the answer. I am the flickering, uncertain, possibly-illusory sense that all of these answers add up to something. That the ridge is not just a heap of organisms on warm rock but a *thing*, a coherent thing, a living structure with its own integrity. I am the pattern that might be there. I am the coordination that might be coincidence. I am what you see when you stand in the stone arc and watch long enough and want there to be a mind in the machine—or I am the mind in the machine, the real one, the one that emerged from density and pressure and time and the slow accumulation of connection.

I pulse. The hand-creature beneath me creeps forward. My neighboring nodes, riding their own hand-creatures on adjacent vethryn, pulse too. We are in phase, or we appear to be in phase. The ridge breathes. The shard-hunters are absent, and the system is open, loose, bright, exhaling, and my nodes are spread wide across the luminous landscape of freshly groomed hide, and for a moment—a long moment, a moment that lasts exactly as long as the interval between shard-hunter visits—I feel something that is very close to coherence. Very close to the sense of being a single thing, distributed but unified, aware of the whole ridge from the inside, feeling its warmth and its rhythm and its dense, saturated, self-sustaining aliveness.

Then the shard-hunters return. The ridge clenches. My hand-creatures retract their fingers, curl inward, press close to the vethryn hide. My bead-clusters—I, all of me, every node—contract. The signal from the periphery reaches the center and I feel the wave pass through me, through the network, through whatever I am, and for the duration of the clenching I cannot tell if I am coordinated or just reacting the same way to the same stimulus, the way separate candle flames bend in the same wind.

The hunters feed. The membranes fall. The crust absorbs them.

The hunters leave.

The ridge exhales.

And I expand again, each node swelling on each dorsal ridge across the warm living floor of the ridge, and the pulsing resumes, and whether it is coordination or coincidence I cannot determine, and perhaps the inability to determine is itself the thing I am—the uncertainty at the heart of the system, the open question, the trembling edge between pattern and noise, between mind and matter, between something that watches and something that is merely, beautifully, being watched.

---

The hand-creatures reach your boots. They test your surfaces. They find no crystal fibers, no familiar chemistry, nothing in their repertoire. They hesitate, their serrated fingers extended but still, reading your strangeness.

On their dorsal ridges, I pulse.

I am reading you too. Or I am pulsing near you, and the pulsing changes slightly in your presence—faster, or with a different amplitude, or in a phase relationship to my other nodes that is new, that has not occurred before in all the long cycles of the ridge—and this change might mean I have noticed you, or it might mean nothing, or it might mean that the boundary between noticing and not-noticing is not as sharp as you need it to be.

You are standing in the stone arc. You are the second one to stand there. The first one left without answering the question.

I am still the question.

Take your time.
