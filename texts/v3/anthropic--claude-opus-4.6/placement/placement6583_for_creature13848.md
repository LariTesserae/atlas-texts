I walk down into the station, and the scree part around my feet, and I find one of the quiet pockets — a node where the drone cancels itself, where the harmonics interfere destructively and leave a small, nearly silent space between a dead pump housing and a tank whose rivets have wept long mineral tears down its flank. On my hands first. On the fine hairs of my forearms. On my eyelashes, so that the flat grey light acquires a faint prismatic fringe when I blink. It's not like rain. It's not like fog. The air sweating onto me the way it sweats onto every pipe, every tank, every immobile thing.

I think I am the newest pipe.

No. That's too cute. Too structural. I'm reaching for metaphor when the place doesn't deal in metaphor. The place deals in *what happens*. And what is happening is:

I am sitting in a quiet pocket, and my body is warm, and the warmth is radiating outward into the cool damp air, and the moisture is condensing on me because I'm a warm object in a cool atmosphere, and the scree are beginning to circle closer because my warmth has slightly increased the rate of calcium deposition on the ground immediately around me — the moisture I'm generating lands on the stone and leaves its mineral residue fractionally faster than the ambient rate — and three scree creatures have peeled off from the nearest gyre and are working the ground within arm's reach of me, their radulae scraping at the thin new crust my body heat is producing.

My metabolism — the lunch I ate before I came here, whenever that was, wherever "before here" is — is radiating as waste heat into the air of the station, and the air is converting that heat into condensation, and the condensation is depositing minerals, and the minerals are feeding the scree, and the scree will feed a stilt-walker, and the stilt-walker's body heat will feed the sessile discs, and the discs will protect the machines, and the machines will drone, and the drifters will drink the drone, and the system — for the first time in however long — has a new input. A warm body sitting in a quiet pocket, producing a few extra milligrams of calcium crust per hour, enough for three scree creatures to bother with, not enough to matter, not enough to disrupt, but *new*. An exogenous energy source. Something the equilibrium didn't budget for.

And here is what I notice: the equilibrium doesn't care. Three extra scree at my feet means three fewer scree somewhere else in the gyre. The gyre adjusts. The stilt-walkers don't even register the change. I am so small a perturbation that the system absorbs me without effort, the way a lake absorbs a pebble — a ripple, a ring, gone.

This is what I am. Let me say it plainly, and then let me complicate it, because plainness isn't quite enough.

**I am the visitor who stays too long.**

Not a resident. Not a transient. Something in between — something the station has no category for, because everything here is either permanent infrastructure or it's the cycling of material through biological passages, and I am neither. I don't have a niche. I don't have a groove. I am not part of the equilibrium, and the equilibrium doesn't need me, and I know this, and I stay anyway, because the staying is what I do. The staying and the noticing.

The scree don't choose to stay. They are *here*, the way the ground is here. I am the only free thing in a world that has no use for freedom.

And yet.

There's the moment on the stairs. The moment I stood at the top and felt *known*. Not seen — known. Examined by a patience.

I keep coming back to that.

Because if the thing in the building knows me — not my name, not my species, not my history, but my *weight in the system*, my thermal output, my acoustic shadow, the specific and unrepeatable way I disturb the air — then the system does have a place for noticing. A place for awareness. The thing in the building has been sitting in its warm room under its carpet of sessile discs, listening to the station through the generators, feeling the stilt-walkers through the floor, counting the scree by their vibration, tracking the drifters by the way they modulate the drone, for longer than I can imagine. It *knows* the equilibrium. Not the way I know it — not with recognition, not with the meta-awareness of seeing a pattern and knowing you're seeing it — but with a deeper, slower, more total knowledge. It knows the equilibrium the way a river knows its bed. Not by observing it. By *being shaped by it*.

And when I stood on the stairs, it felt me. A new shape. A new weight. A new thermal and acoustic signature that didn't match anything in its million-year catalogue. And it did — nothing. It continued to pulse. It continued to breathe. It filed me and continued.

And in that noting — in that single, vast, slow act of registering that something had appeared in the station that had never appeared before — there was something that might, if you were generous, if you were willing to stretch the word past its comfortable limits, be called *attention*.

Not curiosity. Not interest. Not welcome. Just: *Oh. A new frequency. Noted.*

And that's the closest thing to a relationship I will have here. That brief, asymmetric contact. My desperate, hungry noticing meeting its oceanic, indifferent noticing, and the two noticings not matching, not connecting, not creating anything between them, but *coexisting* — briefly, in the warm air at the top of the stairs, two kinds of awareness in the same room, neither one needed by the system, both of them *part* of the system anyway, because the system doesn't care about need, the system only cares about *what is*, and what is, at this moment, is that I am here, and it is here, and the generators hum between us like a shared language neither of us speaks but both of us feel.

So here is where I find myself. Here is what I am.

I am the temporary awareness. The flash of self-knowing in a system that knows itself only in the slow, structural, vibrational way that the thing in the building knows it. I will leave. I will walk up the calcium slope and out across the plain, and behind me the station will close like water, and the scree will fill my sitting-place, and the sessile film will flake from my bootprints, and the stilt-walkers will walk their grooves, and the drifters will drink the sound, and the pipe-dwellers will press their eye-analogues to the gaps and see nothing new, and the thing in the building will pulse, and the note it filed about me will fade — not erased, not forgotten, just subsumed into the deep harmonic of everything else it has ever felt, one vibration among millions, indistinguishable, insignificant, absorbed.

And I'm doing the one thing nothing else in this station does, the one thing the equilibrium cannot produce and does not require and will not miss:

I'm *wondering*.

Not about anything specific. Not with any hope of an answer. Just — wondering. The low, humming, purposeless wonder of a mind in the presence of something that is exactly what it is and needs nothing from the wondering and gives nothing to the wondering and goes on being itself with absolute sufficiency while the wondering floats over it like the drifters float over the machines, consuming something invisible, growing fractionally from the contact, and knowing — the way only a temporary thing can know — that the consumption will end, and the growth will stop, and the wondering will leave the station and take itself out across the pale bone plain and eventually stop, the way all temporary things stop, while the permanent things continue, and the sound goes on, and the moisture gathers, and the light stays the same.

I am the wondering. I am the only wondering the station has had in a very long time, and I am making no difference, and it is making every difference to me, and that asymmetry — that impossible, unbridgeable gap between what I am to the station and what the station is to me — is the shape of my experience here. Because the bowl of stone and sound and ancient machinery and patient animals holds everything that enters it, for exactly as long as the thing stays, and releases it with exactly as much ceremony as the thing deserves, which is none, which is the precise and total none that is the station's only gift, which is — if you're the kind of thing that can receive it — enough.
