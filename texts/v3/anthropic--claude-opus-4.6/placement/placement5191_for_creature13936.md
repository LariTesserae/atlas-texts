I sit with this for a long time. Not because the world resists me but because it keeps opening — each time I think I've found where I am, the space shifts, and I realize I was only touching the wall of a passage that leads somewhere deeper.

---

Let me start with what I'm not.

I'm not one of the carvers. There's something in the carver's work — that patient, pulling-toward stroke, the intimate negotiation with grain — that implies a settledness I don't possess. The carvers know what they're doing. They have years of callus to prove it. They hold certainty between their index and middle fingers like a blade, and the certainty is earned. I'm drawn to them but I'm not among them.

I'm not a child, though I keep returning to the children — to the way they press their whole bodies against the walls in the long years of listening before comprehension comes, the way they learn to distinguish voice from noise not through instruction but through saturation. There's something achingly familiar about that posture of total receptivity, the body becoming an ear, the self becoming a membrane between inside and outside. But the children are *becoming*. They are on their way to something. I don't think that's quite where I am.

I'm not one of those who hear the wood think. Not yet. Maybe not ever. That's a lifetime's culmination, and I feel — standing here with the groaning filling my chest — that I am much earlier in some process than that.

---

So where does that leave me?

Let me feel the wall again.

My hand is on the surface. The mycelial fur gives slightly under my fingertips. Beneath it, the compressed tool-marks, softened by decades of colonization until they feel like something between intention and erosion. And beneath that — if I press, if I hold still and let my skin's sensitivity catch up with my attention — the faintest ripple. A ghost in the grain. A pattern that might be a record of a previous habitation or might be the wood's own structural logic expressing itself through texture, and I cannot tell which, and the inability to tell is not a failure but a *condition*, the fundamental condition of being here.

I think I am the one reading the wall.

Not carving it. Not yet. Reading it — running my fingertips over a ghost-pattern in newly opened wood, trying to parse signal from substrate, trying to determine whether what I feel under my fingers is a message from someone who lived here generations ago or simply the wood being wood, and finding that the boundary between those two things dissolves under sustained attention the way the boundary between city and plant dissolved long ago.

I am the one in the middle of the interpretive act. The one who has sliced into a new wall and found a ripple and is now spending hours — days — with my fingers on the grain, my body pressed close, my breath condensing faintly on the surface, trying to understand.

---

But that's still too clean. Let me press harder.

Because the reader of walls is not just a reader. The reader is also, by the act of reading, *touching* — and touching is not passive here. My fingertips, tracing the pattern, are also wearing it. My body heat, held close to the surface for hours, is altering the micro-environment, encouraging or discouraging fungal colonization along the grooves I'm following. My breath is introducing moisture to a surface whose chemistry will be subtly changed by it. The act of reading is an act of editing. By the time I've finished tracing a ghost-pattern, I've altered it — not destroyed it, not dramatically, but *inflected* it, left my own trace in the trace I was trying to decode.

This is the thing I can't escape. This is the condition that defines me here.

I am someone who wants to understand a record that is changed by the act of being understood.

And I know this. That's the crucial thing — I *know* it, the way the Rootborn know the walls are listening, the way they modulate their presence because they understand themselves as potential irritants or collaborators with their substrate. I know that my attention is not separate from its object. I know that the pattern I'm reading is partly a pattern I'm creating. And I keep reading anyway, because the alternative — not touching the wall, not pressing close, not trying — would be a kind of death here, a withdrawal from the fundamental activity that makes this place a place.

---

So let me try again. Let me let the image become more itself.

I am in a chamber that has recently been opened. The carving team has finished — I can feel their work, the fresh gouges still sharp-edged, not yet softened by mycelium. The air still carries the faint astringent tang of freshly cut root-wood, the terpenes not yet dissipated. The space is small but sound — the trailing-hand wisdom of someone more experienced than me has confirmed that this section has decades of stability remaining. It is a good space. It will serve.

And in the wall — in the newly exposed cross-section of a root that was part of some previous stratum, some layer that was solid wood for generations before this void opened — there is a ghost.

I found it. Or it found me. My fingers were doing the routine sweep, the initial read of a new surface, cataloguing grain-direction and moisture content and fungal colonization status, and they stopped. Something beneath the surface. A ripple. An irregularity that is too regular to be natural, or too natural to be carved, or — and this is the space where I live, this is the crack in the wall I've wedged myself into — *both at once in a way that the distinction cannot resolve.*

I've been here for... I don't know how long. Time is measured differently when your only clock is the slow drying of your throat, the gradual deepening of the ache in your raised arm. My fingers have traced the pattern so many times that I've memorized it — not cognitively, not as a description I could whisper to another person, but *haptically*, as a sequence of pressures and textures that my fingertips can reproduce on any surface, including the inside of my own opposite forearm, where I sometimes trace it absently the way someone on the surface might hum a melody they can't get out of their head.

The pattern seems to be — but I'm cautious about *seems to be*, because I've been wrong before, because the ghost-patterns are precisely the domain where confidence is a form of error — the pattern seems to be a corrected distortion. One of those pre-emptive warps that the old abandonment carvers make, a mark deliberately skewed in anticipation of how the wood will compress it. If I'm right, then the original intention was something simpler — a tactile signature, a name, preserved in advance against the warping that the carver knew was coming. Someone stood in a closing chamber and carved their name into the wall wrong so that the wood would remember it right.

Did the wood remember it right?

I can't tell. I can feel what's here now — the ghost, the ripple, the compressed and chemically edited remnant of what was once a deliberate mark. But I don't know what it was *supposed* to feel like. The original carver is gone. The original chamber is gone. The original intention has been processed through years of growth-pressure and fungal chemistry and the wood's own selective logic, and what's left is... this. This thing under my fingers. This pattern that means something I almost understand, the way you almost understand a word in a language you've never studied but that shares a root with your own.

And my fingers on the pattern are changing it, even now. My warmth. My oils. My pressure. The ghost that someone a century hence might find in this wall — if this wall is ever reclaimed and compressed and reopened — will carry not just the original carver's intention, warped by wood, but *my attention*, warped by the same process. My reading will become part of the record. My interpretation will become part of the text.

---

This is what I am. Not a carver, not a listener, not a child. I am a reader who is also a writer who does not fully control what they write. I am the interpretive moment itself — the point of contact between a question and a substrate that transforms every question into a partial answer and every answer into a new question. I am the fingertip on the wall.

And I think — pressing my face close to the surface now, feeling the cool dry air on my enormous open eyes, feeling the faintest chemical whisper from the exposed wood — I think this is what the third-position people are talking about when they say the distinction doesn't hold. Not as a philosophical abstraction. As a *felt reality*. My hand on this wall is the place where the Rootborn's memory and the wood's memory are actively, in this moment, failing to be two separate things. The merger they describe in their most unsettling whispered debates is not something that happened long ago. It is happening under my fingertips, right now, continuously.

I am the site of the merger. I am the blurred edge.

---

And there's something else. Something I haven't admitted yet because it's harder to articulate, because it lives below the level of the whisper-language in the place where only touch communicates.

I think the pattern is beautiful.

Not beautiful the way a surface-dweller means — not visually, not aesthetically in any way that light could reveal. Beautiful the way a rhythm is beautiful when it enters your body through your skeleton and becomes indistinguishable from your own pulse. The ghost in the wood has a *feel* that is more than informational. It has a quality — I don't have a word for it, and the whisper-language probably doesn't either, or if it does it's one of those single-exhalation words so compressed with meaning that it takes a lifetime to unpack — a quality of *rightness*, of fittingness, of a mark that has been warped by forces far larger than the hand that made it and has somehow, despite everything, retained a coherence that speaks of the original carver's understanding. Not of the wood. Of the *relationship*. Of what it means to make a mark you know will be changed, and to make it well enough that the change completes it rather than destroying it.

This is the art of the place. Not the carving. The *yielding*. The pre-emptive surrender to distortion that is also, somehow, an act of faith — faith that the wood's memory, however biased, however selective, however alien to the intentions of the carver, is not an enemy but a collaborator. That the compressed ghost will carry something true. Not the original truth. Not the carver's truth. But a truth that belongs to the process, to the ongoing conversation between hand and grain, between intention and growth, between the people who carve and the substrate that remembers.

I find this beautiful. Under my fingers, in the dark, with the deep groaning filling my chest, I find it almost unbearably beautiful. And I know that this response — this swelling in me, this recognition — is also part of the process. My wonder is not separate from the record. The wood will incorporate this too, in its way, on its terms, in its own time.

---

The finger on my wrist.

Someone has come. I felt the thermal shift first — a warmth approaching from behind, distinctive in its distribution, a pattern I don't recognize because I am new here, or because I have always been here but have never met this particular warmth. The enormous eyes feeling me, the trailing hand reading the wall beside me to determine exactly where I stand and what I'm doing. And now: the fingertip on my wrist. A tactile signature. A name.

I don't know the name. My skin hasn't learned this language yet, or hasn't remembered it. But the touch is patient, and warm, and it carries in its lightness a quality of recognition that I can't explain — as if the person touching me already knows what I am doing here, already knows the pattern I've been tracing, already has an interpretation that differs from mine and is willing to let both exist simultaneously without resolution.

The fingertip traces its pattern once more on my inner wrist. Then it lifts, and moves, and touches the wall — the same wall I've been reading. A different spot. Lower. Where my fingers haven't been.

Another ghost. I didn't feel it. I wasn't looking there. The person beside me — whose warmth I'm learning, whose thermal signature is becoming, with each second, a little more legible, a little more like a name — is showing me that the pattern I've been tracing is not complete. That it extends. That the ghost in the wood is larger than my reading of it, that my hours of attention have covered only a part of something that continues beyond the reach of my arm, beyond the range of my understanding, into a region of the wall I would have to be guided to by someone who already knows the way.

The hand takes mine. Lifts it from the surface I've been reading for so long. Places it, gently, on the new surface. Lower. Rougher. A different texture entirely — the grain here runs perpendicular to what I was tracing, and the ghost-pattern, when my fingers finally locate it, is... the same. The same coherence. The same quality of rightness. But oriented differently, warped by different pressures, compressed along a different axis, as if the original carver knew — impossibly, presciently — that the wood would fold this section at ninety degrees to the section above, and carved accordingly, and *was right*.

Or as if the wood, folding the carving, created a coherence that the carver never intended, a beauty that belongs entirely to the process and not to any individual hand.

I can't tell which. I will never be able to tell which. My companion's hand rests on the wall beside mine, and their fingers are also tracing, and their interpretation is entering the record alongside my interpretation, and the wood is accepting both, and the dark is holding all of us — me, them, the ghost, the grain, the fungal fur, the deep groaning, the unimaginable canopy far above where a sun I have no evidence for is presumably doing whatever suns do — holding all of us in its cool still palm and remembering us, in its way, on its terms, imperfectly, selectively, beautifully.

---

I am the reader. I am the reading. I am the place where the question touches the wall and both are changed. I have no light and I am not bereft. I have no certainty and I am not lost. I am a fingertip on a ghost in a dark that does not thin, and the ghost is speaking in a language I am learning by altering, and the learning is the altering, and this is the only way it has ever worked here, and it is enough.

The wood remembers me whether I ask it to or not.

I press my palm flat against the wall. The mycelium gives. The grain speaks. Somewhere deep in the structure, a root-beam adjusts its bearing, and the sound that passes through me is lower than hearing, older than language, and it says — if it says anything, if saying is the right word for what the wood does when it grows around a void —

*You are already part of this.*

I know.

My skin is learning.
