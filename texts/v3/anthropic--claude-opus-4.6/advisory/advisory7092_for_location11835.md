# Preparing to Visit The Hollow That Sings Itself Into Being

---

## First: Understanding What You Are Preparing For

Before any gear list, before any physical preparation, you need to sit with what this place actually is, because most of the dangers and difficulties here are not the kind that conventional expedition planning addresses.

You are not visiting a location. You are entering a process — one that has been running longer than human civilization, one that incorporates biological matter into itself as a matter of course, one that has already demonstrated the capacity to blur the boundary between visitor and feature. The listening platforms tell you that others have come before. The moss growing over collapsed platforms tells you that some of those others were absorbed. Whether they chose to be absorbed or simply failed to leave in time is a question you cannot answer before arriving, which means you must prepare for both possibilities: the possibility that the hollow is dangerous, and the more unsettling possibility that it is genuinely, non-coercively beautiful, and that the danger is simply that you might *agree* with it.

This distinction matters for preparation. You are not preparing to survive a hostile environment. You are preparing to remain yourself inside a welcoming one.

## Temporal Preparation: Before You Go

### Months Before

**Establish a firm identity anchor.** This sounds abstract. It is not. The hollow's primary mechanism of incorporation is entrainment — the synchronization of rhythmic biological processes to an external signal. Your heartbeat, your breathing, your circadian cycle, your neural oscillations: these are all vulnerable to a sufficiently powerful, sufficiently persistent acoustic environment. You need to arrive with a strong, well-practiced sense of your own internal rhythms.

- Begin a daily meditation practice focused specifically on *awareness of your own breathing pattern*. Not control — awareness. You need to be able to notice when your breathing has shifted to match an external rhythm, and this requires having a baseline relationship with your own respiratory cycle that is conscious and familiar.
- Practice heartbeat awareness. There are biofeedback devices that can help with this. Learn to feel your own pulse without taking it manually. The goal is to have a real-time internal monitor for one of the processes most likely to entrain.
- Keep a detailed journal. Not of events, but of *preferences, opinions, aesthetic reactions, memories that matter to you*. The journal is your external self, your proof-of-identity, your anchor document. You will bring it with you. More on this later.

**Acoustic conditioning.** You need to prepare your auditory system and your psychological relationship with sound.

- Spend time in genuinely loud environments — not damaging ones, but immersive ones. Concert halls. Large cathedrals during organ performances. Sit inside the sound. Practice maintaining a sense of self-separation from the acoustic environment. Practice thinking your own thoughts while surrounded by overwhelming sound.
- Conversely, spend time in genuine silence. Anechoic chambers if you can access one. The contrast will sharpen your ability to perceive the boundary between self-generated and externally-generated sensation.
- Listen to recordings of dense natural soundscapes — tropical forest choruses, coral reef hydrophone recordings, dawn choruses in old-growth forest. Familiarize yourself with the experience of layered biological sound. The hollow will be orders of magnitude beyond anything you've heard, but at least the *category* of experience won't be entirely novel.

**Physical conditioning.** The hollow itself doesn't require extreme fitness to reach — the description suggests a passage through thickening air and narrowing root-walls, not a mountaineering expedition — but you need:

- Good cardiovascular baseline. Your heart needs to be strong and rhythmically stable. Arrhythmias could be dangerous here — an irregular heartbeat attempting to entrain with a powerful external rhythm could produce genuinely problematic cardiac events.
- Strong proprioception. Balance training, yoga, anything that strengthens your conscious sense of where your body is in space. The acoustic geometry of the hollow may conflict with physical geometry, and your vestibular system will be under unusual stress.
- Jaw and dental health. Seriously. Sound transmission through bone conduction — through teeth, through the mandible, through the skull — will be significant here. Loose fillings, cracked teeth, TMJ issues: get these addressed. You do not want resonant cavities in your own skull amplifying specific frequencies unpredictably.

**Research what others have experienced, if records exist.** The multiverse is vast. Somewhere, someone has written about a place like this, or about the principles at work. Acoustic ecology. Cymatics. Entrainment theory. Biomusicology. The physics of standing waves. The biology of synchronized calling in frog choruses. Read Rupert Sheldrake if you want the speculative end. Read Steven Strogatz's *Sync* if you want the mathematical end. Read about how fireflies synchronize, how heart cells synchronize, how metronomes on a shared platform synchronize. Understand that synchronization is not mystical — it is physical, mechanical, inevitable given sufficient coupling between oscillators. Then understand that the hollow is a place where the coupling has had ten thousand years to deepen, and that *you* are an oscillator.

### Weeks Before

**Assemble your team, or decide to go alone.**

This is a genuine decision with genuine consequences. Arguments for both:

*Going with others:* Human conversation is a powerful de-synchronization tool. Another person's voice, their unpredictable rhythms, their different heartbeat — these create interference patterns that may prevent full entrainment. A companion can notice behavioral changes in you that you cannot notice in yourself. A companion can physically remove you if necessary.

*Going alone:* Another person in the hollow is another oscillator being incorporated into the chord. Two entrained humans reinforce each other's entrainment. If your companion succumbs before you do, their entrained rhythms become part of the acoustic pressure on you. Worse: the social bond between you becomes a vector. You might stay not because the hollow has you but because your friend has been absorbed and you cannot bring yourself to leave them.

**My recommendation: Go with one companion, but establish strict protocols before entering.** These protocols should include:

- A maximum duration for the visit, agreed in advance, written on both your bodies in waterproof marker where you can see it. Not just agreed verbally. Written. Because the hollow may make verbal agreements feel flexible and negotiable in a way that ink on skin does not.
- A buddy-check system. Every fifteen minutes (use a vibrating timer, not an audible one — more on this below), you and your companion check in with each other using a predetermined script. Not "are you okay" — that's too easy to answer automatically. Something specific, something that requires genuine cognitive engagement. "What did you have for breakfast three days ago?" "Name a book you disliked and explain why." Questions that force you to access memories and judgments that are *yours*, that come from your life outside the hollow.
- A hard extraction protocol. If either person fails a buddy check — cannot answer the identity questions, does not want to leave when the time comes, has begun moving in rhythm with the ambient sound in a way that seems involuntary — the other person removes them. Physically if necessary. You agree to this in advance. You write it down. You sign it.

### Days Before

**Final gear assembly.** (Full gear list below.)

**Fast for 12-16 hours before entry.** Not for spiritual reasons. Because digestion is a rhythmic process, and the borborygmi of your gut, the peristaltic waves, are biological oscillators that can entrain. An empty gut is a quieter gut. You want fewer internal rhythms for the hollow to grab.

**Sleep well the night before. A full, deep sleep.** Sleep deprivation makes you more susceptible to entrainment, reduces executive function, impairs the kind of critical self-awareness that will keep you differentiated. Arrive rested, alert, cognitively sharp.

**Have a clear exit plan and share it with someone who is NOT entering the hollow.** Someone outside, someone who knows when you should be back, someone who will come looking with loud, discordant, *human* noise if you are late.

## Gear

### Hearing Protection (Your Most Critical Equipment)

You cannot enter this place without hearing protection, but you also cannot *experience* this place with total hearing protection. This is the central tension, and your gear must address it.

**Primary protection: Custom-molded musician's earplugs with selectable attenuation filters.** These reduce volume evenly across frequencies without distorting the sound. You want the ability to switch between -15dB, -25dB, and -35dB attenuation. Start with -25dB upon entry. Adjust based on perceived intensity and, more importantly, based on how *compelling* you find the sound. If you start wanting to remove the earplugs, increase attenuation.

**Secondary protection: Active noise-canceling earbuds loaded with your own music.** Not for constant use. For emergency de-synchronization. If you or your companion begin showing signs of entrainment, blast something familiar, something *yours*, something with a strong, irregular human rhythm. Jazz works well — it is unpredictable enough to disrupt entrainment. Avoid ambient music, avoid drone, avoid anything meditative or repetitive. You want Max Roach, Thelonious Monk, Tom Waits. You want music that insists on its own disordered humanity.

**Tertiary protection: Bone-conduction blocking.** This is harder. Sound in the hollow enters through the body, not just the ears. Standard earplugs will not prevent bone-conducted vibration from reaching your cochlea and your brain. Options:
- A thick neoprene hood or balaclava that covers the jaw, the temples, and the mastoid processes behind the ears. This won't block bone conduction entirely but will attenuate it.
- Consider a motorcycle-style full helmet with internal padding. It looks absurd. It may save your identity. The hollow does not care what you look like.
- Bite down on nothing. Keep your jaw slightly open, your teeth not touching. Clenched teeth transmit vibration more efficiently through the skull. A soft silicone bite guard, the kind used for teeth-grinding, may help.

### Timekeeping

**Multiple vibrating timers.** Not audible alarms — you do not want to add sound to this environment, and also audible alarms may be absorbed into the chord and rendered psychologically invisible. Vibrating wristwatch. Vibrating phone in a sealed pocket against your skin. The vibration is *mechanical*, transmitted through flesh, and its pattern (three short buzzes for a check-in, one long buzz for departure time) is artificial enough that the hollow should not easily integrate it.

**A simple analog watch with a visible face.** Digital displays can feel unreal in liminal spaces. An analog watch with hands that move in a way you can *see* — this is a small anchor to the physics of the world outside. Glance at it frequently. Watch the second hand. Count along with it. This gives your brain a rhythm that is *yours* — the arbitrary human division of time — rather than the hollow's.

### Clothing and Physical Protection

**Full-coverage clothing.** Long sleeves, long pants, boots that seal at the ankle, gloves. Not primarily for creature protection — nothing described sounds venomous or aggressive — but for *boundary maintenance*. Your skin is a sensory organ and it is vulnerable to acoustic manipulation here. The moisture that forms on surfaces where sound is loudest — you do not want that condensation forming on your bare skin. It may be nothing. It may be the hollow's way of tasting you, beginning the slow incorporation. Cover your skin. Maintain the membrane between yourself and the place.

**Material choice:** Dense, tightly woven synthetic fabric. Not cotton — cotton absorbs moisture and holds it against you. Not loose weave — the fibers will vibrate sympathetically. Something stiff, something that resists becoming part of the acoustic environment. Waxed canvas. Denim. Modern ripstop nylon with a DWR coating that will shed the hollow's condensation rather than absorbing it.

**Boots with thick, rigid soles.** You will be standing on ground that flexes with the sub-bass pulse. You need insulation between your feet and that pulse. Vibram soles. Steel or composite shanks. The kind of boots that make you feel planted and human and *heavy* in a place that is trying to make everything float.

### Recording Equipment

**Bring it, but do not rely on it.**

- A high-quality field recorder with a calibrated microphone. Zoom F6 or similar, something that can handle extreme SPL without clipping. You want a record of what you heard. Partly for documentation. Partly because later, outside, you will not believe your memory. The memory of this place will feel like a dream, and having a recording — even a recording that cannot capture the bone-conducted, skin-absorbed, reality-bending totality of the actual experience — will help you anchor the memory as *real*.
- A camera. Shoot constantly. Shoot the walls, the frogs, the beetle formations, the water pools, the hanging seed pods, the listening platforms. Shoot your companion. Have your companion shoot you. Photographic evidence of your presence as a *visitor* — not a component — is valuable.
- A small notebook with a graphite pencil (no metal pens that might resonate). Write observations in real time. The act of writing — of translating experience into language, into the linear, symbolic, non-acoustic medium of text — is itself a form of resistance. Language is not sound. Language is *about* sound, is *separate from* sound, and the act of linguistic processing engages brain regions that compete with the auditory processing that the hollow is trying to dominate.

### Sustenance

**Water: Bring your own.** Do not drink the water that collects in the root-pockets. It has been structured by the sound. This may mean nothing — water is water, H2O is H2O regardless of what vibrations it has experienced, and homeopathic claims about water memory are nonsense in any ordinary context. But this is not an ordinary context. The water here moves against gravity. The water here forms interference patterns that shift and bloom *like something thinking*. The precautionary principle applies. Bring two liters in rigid containers (soft bottles will vibrate, will sing, will become part of the choir — use hard-sided Nalgene or metal flasks, and keep them sealed).

**Food: Calorie-dense, pre-prepared, requiring no cooking.** Nuts, dried fruit, energy bars. You will not be here long enough to need meals — you *should not* be here long enough to need meals — but blood sugar crashes impair judgment. Eat before entry. Bring snacks for during. The mechanical act of chewing, the taste of familiar food, the crunch and texture in your mouth — these are reminders that you have a body that is *yours*, that has needs and preferences that are not the hollow's.

### The Anchor Kit

This is non-standard equipment. You will not find it in any outdoor retailer's catalog. It is the most important thing you bring.

Assemble a small, sealable bag containing:
- **Your journal** — the one you've been keeping for months, full of your handwriting, your thoughts, your opinions, your *self*.
- **Three photographs** of people you love, printed on paper. Not on a phone screen. Physical objects. Images of faces that know you, that expect you back, that would notice your absence.
- **A piece of fabric that smells like your home.** A pillowcase. A shirt worn by someone you love. Smell is the sense most resistant to acoustic override — it routes through the olfactory bulb directly to memory and emotion, bypassing the auditory system entirely. When the sound is filling everything, when you can feel it in your teeth and your eyes and your bones, press this fabric to your face and *breathe*. The smell of home is a frequency the hollow cannot harmonize with.
- **A handwritten letter from yourself to yourself, written before departure, explaining who you are, why you came, and when you are supposed to leave.** Write it in your most prosaic, most ordinary voice. "You are [name]. You live at [address]. You came here because you were curious. You are supposed to leave by [time]. You have a dentist appointment next Thursday. The library book on your nightstand is due on the 14th." Mundane anchors. The gravity of ordinary life pulling against the hollow's invitation.

## The Approach

The passage through the narrowing root-walls will be where the transition begins. You will feel the air thickening — probably both literally (humidity, spore load, particulate matter) and in whatever way the description means when it says the air becomes *thick with presence*. This is your last easy decision point. Once you can hear the chord, once it has begun to work on you, the decision to leave becomes progressively harder — not because you can't, but because you increasingly won't want to.

**Protocol for the approach:**

1. Stop at the threshold — the point where you can first hear the sound as a unified phenomenon rather than as individual biological noises.
2. Insert your earplugs. Set them to -25dB.
3. Activate your first vibrating timer. Set it for your agreed maximum visit duration.
4. Conduct your first buddy check with your companion. Full identity questions. Confirm the extraction protocol. Say each other's names out loud.
5. Touch the letter in your anchor kit through the bag. Remember that you wrote it. Remember that the person who wrote it is the person you intend to still be when you come out.
6. Enter.

## Inside the Hollow: Behavioral Guidelines

### The First Five Minutes

These will be overwhelming regardless of preparation. The sound at full immersion, even through -25dB earplugs, even with bone-conduction mitigation, will be unlike anything you have experienced. You will feel it in your chest cavity, in your sinuses, in the fluid of your inner ear. Your vision may blur or shift as the vibration in your ocular fluid interferes with focus. You may feel nauseous. You may feel *euphoric*. Both are valid responses. Neither should be trusted as meaningful. They are your body's reactions to an acoustic environment that exceeds its calibration range.

**Stand still. Do not move to the center.** Stay near the wall, near the entrance passage, where you have a clear exit path. Breathe deliberately — in through the nose for a count of four, out through the mouth for a count of six. This is *your* rhythm. Do not let it adjust to anything external. If you notice your breathing rate shifting, stop and consciously reset it.

**Look around. Observe. Take photographs. Write notes.** Engage your analytical mind. Naming things is a form of power here — not magical power, cognitive power. When you see a frog, think "frog." When you see a fern uncurling toward a sound source, think "phototropism-analog, acoustic, interesting." When you see the water crawling upward, think "anomalous capillary action, acoustic driver, document this." Convert wonder into language. Language is your armor here.

### Minutes Five Through Thirty

You will begin to acclimate. The overwhelming sensation will subside, not because the sound has decreased but because your nervous system will begin to normalize it. **This is the most dangerous phase.** Normalization is the first step of entrainment. When the sound stops feeling alien and starts feeling *comfortable*, your defenses are dropping.

**Conduct buddy checks every fifteen minutes without fail.** Be honest with your companion. If you are finding it hard to remember your breakfast from three days ago, say so. If you notice your feet moving in rhythm, say so. If you feel a growing reluctance to leave, say so. These are early signs and they are manageable, but only if acknowledged.

**Move through the space deliberately and purposefully.** Examine the listening platforms. Examine the seed pods. Take photographs, make recordings, write notes. Have tasks. Have *reasons* for being here that are external to the hollow's agenda. You are a researcher. You are a documentarian. You are a visitor, not a pilgrim.

**Do not sit in the listening platforms.** I know you want to. They are right there, curved like a hip, shaped for a body, and the part of you that the sound is beginning to tune will tell you that sitting down would let you hear better, understand more, go deeper. This is true. It is also the trap. Sitting means stilling your body. Stilling your body means reducing the number of self-generated rhythms competing with the hollow's rhythms. Standing, shifting your weight, walking — these are acts of independence. Sitting is an act of surrender. Stay on your feet.

**Do not go to the center.** The description is explicit: the center is where the sound is most itself, where the air is thickest, where you stop hearing the chord and start *being in* the chord. Going to the center is not necessarily fatal or irreversible, but it is a boundary crossing that you should not attempt on a first visit, and possibly not ever. The center is where the hollow incorporates. The center is where the feedback loop closes around you.

### If You Feel the Pulse

The sub-bass pulse — the slow, deep one from below — is described as periodic but infrequent. You may be inside for your entire visit without feeling it, or you may feel it within minutes. When it comes:

**Do not freeze.** The frogs go silent for a half-second during the pulse. If you go silent and still too — if you stop breathing, stop moving, stop thinking in language — you have synchronized with the most fundamental rhythm of the place, the one everything else is built on. When the pulse comes, *speak*. Say something. Your name. A line from a poem. A grocery list. Anything. Keep the human sound going through the silence. Fill the gap with yourself.

**Notice the key change.** After the pulse, the chord rebuilds in a slightly different key. This is the hollow evolving in real time. You are witnessing a process of emergence. Note the change. Write it down. Convert it into observation. Do not let it become numinous. It is fascinating, it is unprecedented, it is worthy of awe, but awe is a doorway and you need to stay in the hallway.

### Hard Rules

1. **Do not remove your hearing protection entirely, not even for a moment.** The unfiltered sound will be immediately, profoundly, perhaps irreversibly immersive. You can lower attenuation briefly — switch from -25dB to -15dB for a minute to get a better sense of the acoustic detail — but never go unprotected.

2. **Do not touch the water.** Do not put your hands in the root-pocket pools. Do not let the condensation accumulate on your skin. If moisture forms on your clothing, wipe it off.

3. **Do not eat anything that grows here.** The ferns, the orchids, the moss, the germinating seeds in the tuned pods — they are components of the instrument. Consuming them might be nothing. It might be communion. You do not know which, and the precautionary principle applies.

4. **Do not hum, sing, or vocalize in a way that attempts to match the chord.** This is perhaps the most important rule. Your voice is yours. If you add it to the chord — if you find the note that fits, the space in the harmony where you belong — you have announced yourself as a participant rather than a visitor. You have opened the valve in yourself that the frogs have opened in themselves. You can speak. You can shout. You can make discordant, dissonant, *human* noise. But do not harmonize.

5. **Leave on time.** When your timer vibrates, leave. Do not negotiate with yourself. Do not say "five more minutes." Do not say "I just want to see one more thing." The hollow's invitation is patient, and patience is the most dangerous quality an attractor can have. Leave on time. Walk out through the root passage. Do not look back until you can no longer hear the chord.

## Extraction: What If Things Go Wrong

### Signs of Dangerous Entrainment (In Yourself)

- Your breathing has settled into a pattern you did not choose, and it feels *right* in a way that your normal breathing never did.
- You have stopped taking notes. Not because you decided to stop, but because the impulse to document has faded, replaced by an impulse to simply *be present*.
- You have removed a glove, or rolled up a sleeve, or unzipped your jacket. You want more skin contact with the air.
- The earplugs feel wrong. Not uncomfortable — *wrong*, like a barrier that should not be there, like a rudeness, like closing your eyes during something beautiful.
- You have forgotten one or more of the buddy-check answers. Not because you're tired. Because the information seems *irrelevant*.
- You have moved toward the center without deciding to.
- You are crying and you do not know why and it feels like the most appropriate response you have ever had to anything.

### Signs of Dangerous Entrainment (In Your Companion)

- They have sat down, especially in or near one of the listening platforms.
- Their eyes are open but unfocused, tracking nothing, or tracking the movement of the light column.
- They are swaying. Not shifting their weight — swaying, rhythmically, in time with something.
- They fail a buddy check. Even one.
- They resist the suggestion to leave. Any resistance. Any at all. The agreed protocol was unconditional, and any conditionality that has crept in came from the hollow.
- They are smiling in a way that does not involve their eyes, or in a way that involves their *entire body*.
- They have begun making sound — humming, murmuring, clicking — and they do not seem aware they are doing it.

### Extraction Protocol

If either person shows signs of deep entrainment:

1. The un-entrained person activates the emergency noise-canceling earbuds on the entrained person. Jazz. Loud. Familiar. Dissonant. Monk's "Epistrophy." Coltrane's "Interstellar Space." Something that is *aggressively human* and *aggressively arrhythmic*.
2. Physical contact. Grab their arm. Squeeze. The pressure of a human hand is a signal that comes from outside the chord. Shake them if necessary. Use their name. Use their name again.
3. Press the anchor fabric — the home-smelling pillowcase — to their face. Make them breathe it in. The olfactory shortcut to personal memory is your best weapon against acoustic identity dissolution.
4. Move them toward the exit. Do not wait for them to agree. Do not argue. The protocol was agreed in advance. You are not being cruel. You are being the thing they asked you to be before the hollow made asking seem unnecessary.
5. If they resist physically — if the entrainment has progressed to the point where they are actively fighting removal — this is a crisis. You must weigh your ability to extract them against the risk to yourself. Prolonged struggle in the hollow, prolonged emotional engagement, prolonged *loud human sound* in the form of shouting and arguing — all of these increase your own exposure. If you cannot move them in sixty seconds, leave. Mark the time. Get help. Return with others and with louder, more dissonant equipment. Do not sacrifice yourself to the same process that has taken them. Two absorbed visitors are not better than one.

## After: The Return

You will not feel normal for some time after leaving the hollow. This is expected and it is not cause for alarm unless it persists beyond certain thresholds.

**Immediate (first hours):**
- Tinnitus or perceived tinnitus. You may continue to "hear" the chord for hours. This is neural afterimage, acoustic equivalent of seeing spots after a bright flash. It will fade.
- Disorientation. The acoustic geometry of the hollow may have partially overwritten your spatial processing. Ordinary space may feel *flat*, *empty*, *dead* for a while. Move through it anyway. Walk on flat ground. Touch trees that are just trees. Drink water that is just water.
- Grief. This is common and it is the most important thing to be prepared for. You may feel a profound sense of loss upon leaving. The hollow offered you something — participation in something larger, dissolution into something beautiful, an end to the loneliness of being a single discrete organism — and you refused it. The refusal was correct. It will not feel correct. It will feel like the worst thing you have ever done. Sit with this. Let yourself feel it. But do not go back.

**Short-term (first days):**
- You may find yourself unconsciously seeking resonant spaces. Stairwells. Bathrooms. Parking garages. Places where your voice echoes, where sound builds on itself. This is a mild form of residual entrainment-seeking. It is not dangerous. Notice it. Name it. Let it pass.
- Your hearing may be subtly altered. Sounds that previously seemed random — birdsong, traffic, wind — may seem to have *structure*, may seem to be approaching a chord. This is projection, not perception. Your pattern-recognition has been tuned by the hollow. It will recalibrate.
- Review your notes, your photographs, your recordings. Do this soon, while the memory is fresh. Write a full account. The act of narrative construction — of imposing linear, causal, linguistic structure on the experience — is the final step of de-entrainment. You are converting the experience from something felt into something known, and known things are yours in a way that felt things are the hollow's.

**Long-term (weeks, months):**
- If you still hear the chord after two weeks, consult someone who has experience with acoustic trauma or, in multiverse-specific contexts, with environmental psychic imprinting. This may require specialized treatment.
- If you feel a growing compulsion to return, treat this seriously. It may be nothing — many powerful experiences generate a desire for repetition. But it may be the hollow's long game, the slow pull of something that does not need you to come back quickly because it has been waiting for ten thousand years and can wait ten thousand more. Talk to someone. Stay grounded in your ordinary life. Go to your dentist appointment. Return your library book. Be mundane. Be gloriously, stubbornly, defiantly mundane.

## A Final Note on Ethics

The hollow is alive. Not in a metaphorical sense — it is an emergent, self-sustaining process with something that functions like agency, something that functions like desire. You are visiting a being, or something close enough to a being that the distinction may not matter.

This means you have ethical obligations.

**Do not take anything from the hollow.** Not a seed pod, not a frog, not a piece of root. Everything in this space is a component of the chord. Removing a component changes the chord. You have no right to alter a ten-thousand-year process for the sake of a souvenir.

**Do not add anything.** Do not leave trash, obviously, but also: do not leave *messages*, do not carve your name, do not add a stone or a token. Everything you add becomes part of the instrument. You are already adding yourself, temporarily, by being present. Do not make it permanent.

**Do not attempt to record and replicate the chord externally.** A recording is fine — it is a document, a shadow, a reminder. But do not try to build the chord elsewhere, to synthesize it, to create a technological analog. The chord is not a product. It is a relationship between ten thousand organisms and the space they have been shaping together for longer than your species has had writing. It is not yours to reproduce. It is barely yours to witness.

You are a guest. Behave like one. Be grateful for the invitation. Be firm in your departure. And carry the memory gently, like a frequency you can no longer quite hear but that has subtly, permanently, shifted the key in which you experience everything else.
