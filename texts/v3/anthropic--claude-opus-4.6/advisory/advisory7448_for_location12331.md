# Preparing for the Weeping Plateau: A Comprehensive Guide for the Multiverse Traveler

---

## Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before packing a single item, sit with this: you are visiting a place that will not meet you halfway. Thurm does not accommodate visitors in the way that trading cities or pilgrimage sites do. It is not hostile — it is simply indifferent to the distinction between resident and outsider, in the same way the water is indifferent to whether the channels it flows through were carved by geology or by human hands. The plateau has its own logic, its own tempo, and its own physics of discomfort. Your preparation should be oriented not toward conquering these conditions but toward surviving them with enough residual capacity to actually experience the place. The travelers who struggle in Thurm are the ones who fight it. The ones who manage are the ones who become, temporarily, porous.

## Physical Preparation

### Cardiovascular and Respiratory Conditioning

The description specifies "intermediate altitude" — enough to notice, not enough to incapacitate. This places it roughly in the 2,000–3,000 meter range equivalent. If you're arriving from a low-altitude origin universe, spend at least two weeks beforehand doing sustained aerobic work at moderate intensity. Running, cycling, swimming — the specific modality matters less than the duration. You want your body accustomed to operating at slightly reduced oxygen efficiency, because the altitude effect in Thurm is compounded by the humidity. Warm, moisture-saturated air at moderate altitude carries less oxygen per breath than dry air at the same elevation. You will be breathing harder than the altitude alone would predict, and you will be doing it constantly, because there is no air-conditioned interior to retreat to. Thurm's buildings are open to the same atmosphere as its streets.

Consider altitude-acclimatization protocols if your multiverse transit allows for staged arrival — spending a day or two at a moderate-elevation waypoint before ascending to the plateau. If direct transit is your only option, plan to do very little on your first day. Walk slowly. Sit often. Let your erythropoietin levels catch up.

### Heat and Humidity Acclimation

This is the less obvious but more important physical challenge. The warmth in Thurm rises from below — it is geothermal, constant, and inescapable. The humidity is near-total. Your body's primary cooling mechanism, evaporative sweating, will be severely compromised. You will sweat, but the sweat will not evaporate efficiently; it will simply add to the moisture already on your skin, mixing with the condensation that forms on every surface including you. The result is a persistent, low-grade thermal stress that accumulates over hours.

Two to three weeks before arrival, begin heat acclimation. Sauna sessions of 20–40 minutes, three to four times per week, will expand your plasma volume and improve your sweat response. If you have access to a humid sauna rather than a dry one, prefer it — the conditions in Thurm are more Turkish bath than Finnish. Alternatively, exercise in layered clothing in a warm environment, though this is a blunter instrument.

Understand that you will never be dry in Thurm. Resign yourself to this before arrival. The psychological adjustment is as important as the physiological one.

### Auditory Preparation

This deserves its own section, and I'll address it more fully below under equipment, but your body needs preparation too. The drone is not merely loud — it is infrasonic at its foundation, climbing into audible range. Infrasound at sufficient intensity causes physiological effects independent of perceived volume: anxiety, disorientation, nausea, a sense of presence or dread, visual disturbances at the margins. These are not mystical effects. They are mechanical consequences of low-frequency pressure waves interacting with your organs, your eyeballs, your vestibular system.

You cannot train yourself to be immune to infrasound. You can, however, train yourself to recognize its effects for what they are. Spend time before your trip in environments with significant low-frequency noise — near large industrial equipment, in engine rooms, near certain waterfalls with deep bass signatures. Note what happens to your emotional state, your balance, your visual focus. Learn your own body's infrasonic signature of distress so that when it manifests in Thurm, you can attribute it correctly rather than spiraling into panic. The travelers who have the worst time in Thurm are the ones who don't understand why they suddenly feel terrified while standing on an ordinary street corner. It's not the street corner. It's the culvert crossing beneath it.

### Skin and Foot Care

Your skin will be perpetually wet. This means maceration — the softening and breakdown of skin from prolonged moisture exposure — is a genuine medical concern, particularly on your feet. In the weeks before arrival, toughen your feet with regular walking on varied terrain, and begin applying a moisture-barrier balm (zinc oxide or lanolin-based) to the spaces between your toes and any areas prone to friction. Learn to recognize the early signs of immersion foot: tingling, numbness, blanching of the skin. You will need to manage this actively during your visit.

Your hands will also be affected if you touch the city's surfaces — the railings, the walls, the stone — and you will, because the temptation is irresistible. The mineral content of the water is high, and the deposits it leaves are mildly alkalite. Prolonged contact can cause contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals. Bring a hand-repair balm with a slightly acidic pH.

## Equipment and Gear

### Footwear

This is the single most important equipment decision you will make. You need boots that achieve a seemingly contradictory set of goals: waterproof enough to handle standing water and constant wet stone, breathable enough that your feet don't stew in their own moisture, grippy enough to handle limestone worn glass-smooth by centuries of water and foot traffic, and soled thickly enough to provide some insulation from the geothermal warmth rising through the stone.

A full-grain leather boot with a Vibram-type sole and a Gore-Tex or equivalent membrane is the best compromise. The leather will absorb some moisture but can be treated with wax to maintain a water-resistant outer layer; the membrane will prevent immersion; the Vibram sole will grip wet stone better than most alternatives. Avoid anything with a smooth or hard sole — the worn concavities of Thurm's stone steps are essentially polished limestone bowls, and they are as slippery as they sound. Avoid mesh or fabric uppers; they will saturate immediately and never dry.

Bring two pairs if you can manage the weight. Rotate them. When you are not wearing a pair, stuff them with whatever absorbent material you have and place them somewhere with whatever marginal air circulation you can find. They will not fully dry. Accept this. The goal is to prevent them from becoming biologically active.

Bring at least five pairs of merino wool socks. Merino retains insulating properties when wet, resists odor, and wicks better than synthetics in conditions of extreme humidity. Change your socks at least twice a day. Wring out the used pairs and hang them from your pack or belt; even in Thurm's humidity, moving through the streets will create enough airflow for marginal drying.

### Clothing

Light, moisture-wicking, quick-drying synthetic base layers. No cotton — cotton absorbs water, holds it against your skin, and in Thurm's warm humidity becomes a clammy second skin that promotes chafing and fungal growth within hours. Merino wool is an alternative if you find synthetics intolerable, but it dries even more slowly in these conditions.

A single mid-layer of light fleece or wool for the rare possibility that you venture to the plateau's edge where the haze boundary creates localized cooling. You probably won't need it, but hypothermia can be paradoxically easy to achieve when you're wet and the air temperature drops even a few degrees.

No waterproof outer layer. I know this sounds wrong. Every instinct says to bring a rain jacket to a place where everything is wet. But in Thurm, the moisture comes from everywhere — below, around, condensation forming directly on you. A waterproof jacket will trap your body heat and moisture inside and create a personal steam room that is significantly worse than simply being wet in the open air. Let the moisture through. Let it wick. The one exception: if you'll be visiting the factory district, where concentrated steam venting can create localized drenching, a very light, very breathable poncho can keep the worst of it off your head and shoulders.

Bring a hat with a brim. The diffuse light in Thurm is deceptively intense — the haze scatters it omnidirectionally, and your eyes never get a break from the even, warm illumination. A brim provides the only shade you'll have.

### Hearing Protection

This is non-negotiable. You need a layered hearing-protection strategy.

**Primary: Custom-molded or high-fidelity earplugs.** These are the earplugs designed for musicians and industrial workers — they reduce volume evenly across the frequency spectrum without muffling speech into incomprehensibility. You want the 15–20 dB reduction range. These will take the edge off the drone enough to prevent hearing damage during normal street-level exposure and allow you to converse with residents (who already speak loudly, anticipating some degree of hearing impairment in their interlocutors).

**Secondary: Over-ear hearing protection with active noise cancellation.** For the seep-plazas, the culvert crossings, the factory district — anywhere the drone peaks into physical-pressure territory. Active noise cancellation is particularly valuable because the drone's fundamental frequencies are in the exact range that ANC handles best: low, continuous, relatively predictable. A good ANC headset will reduce the infrasonic pressure component significantly, which addresses the nausea-and-dread problem as much as the volume problem.

**Tertiary: Bone-conduction awareness.** Understand that no hearing protection will fully isolate you from the drone because a significant component of it is transmitted through the stone, through your skeleton, through direct contact with any surface in the city. When you sit on a stone bench, you will feel the drone in your pelvis and spine. When you lean against a wall, your shoulder blade becomes a resonator. Hearing protection addresses airborne sound; it does not address conducted vibration. For this, the only mitigation is minimizing contact — standing rather than sitting, avoiding leaning, keeping your hands off the walls — and accepting the residual hum as part of the experience.

Bring spare earplugs. In Thurm's humidity, anything you put in your ear canal will become a moisture trap. Clean them twice daily. Bring a small bottle of isopropyl alcohol or ear-drying drops.

### Vision

Sunglasses — but not dark ones. The light in Thurm is diffuse and warm-toned, not harsh. Dark lenses will reduce an already shadowless environment to a murk that makes navigating the wet streets dangerous. Instead, bring light amber or yellow-tinted lenses that enhance contrast without reducing overall brightness too severely. The amber tint will also help compensate for the warm color temperature of the light, giving you slightly more accurate color perception of your surroundings.

If you wear corrective lenses, bring contacts rather than glasses if possible. Glasses in Thurm will fog within seconds of being put on and will never clear. The condensation is omnipresent. Anti-fog coatings will delay the problem by minutes, not solve it. If contacts aren't an option, bring a generous supply of anti-fog wipes and accept that you'll be using them constantly.

### Sleep System

You will almost certainly be sleeping in one of Thurm's guesthouses or rented rooms — there is no camping infrastructure on the barren plateau and no reason to attempt it. The rooms will be warm, humid, and permeated by the drone. Bring:

- **A silk or synthetic sleep liner.** The bedding in Thurm will be heavy — wool blankets, thick cotton sheets — designed for residents whose thermal expectations have been calibrated by a lifetime of geothermal warmth. You may find this bedding unbearable. A light sleep liner gives you the option of sleeping atop the bed with minimal coverage.

- **A sleeping mask.** The flickering electrical light in Thurm is on a schedule that may not match yours, and the diffuse natural light penetrates the haze even at night to a degree that maintains a perpetual grey twilight. True darkness is rare on the plateau.

- **Earplugs rated for sleep.** Different from your daytime earplugs. You want maximum noise reduction, comfort for side-sleeping, and the ability to stay in your ears for 6–8 hours without causing canal irritation. Foam earplugs, freshly compressed and deeply inserted, are actually superior to custom molds for sleep in droning environments because their expansion provides broad-spectrum attenuation including low frequencies. Bring a large supply. They are single-use in these humidity conditions.

- **A white noise machine or app on a portable speaker.** This sounds counterintuitive — adding noise to reduce the effect of noise — but the drone's distressing quality comes from its constancy and its harmonic complexity. A broadband white or pink noise source, played close to your head, can mask the drone's specific frequencies enough to let your auditory system release its vigilance and allow sleep. Several experienced Thurm visitors report this as the single most valuable piece of equipment they brought.

### Water and Nutrition

**Do not drink the channel water.** It is warm, mineral-laden, and while the residents have generations of gut-flora adaptation to its specific microbial and chemical content, you do not. The mineral concentration alone — calcium, magnesium, iron, trace lithium (there are reports of lithium, which may partly explain the population's equanimity in the face of their circumstances) — can cause gastrointestinal distress in unaccustomed systems.

Bring a water filtration system rated for mineral reduction — a basic particulate filter is insufficient. A small portable reverse-osmosis system is ideal but heavy. More practically, bring purification tablets and a carbon-element filter in combination, and supplement with electrolyte powders, since you'll be sweating continuously and losing sodium that the mineral water, ironically, may not replace in the right ratios.

Food in Thurm is functional and mineral-inflected. Expect dense bread baked with steam from the geothermal vents, preserved vegetables with a faintly calcareous quality, and protein sources that are primarily preserved — dried, smoked, fermented — since the plateau supports no agriculture and everything must be brought up from the valleys below (through routes you will not see, because they descend into the haze). The cuisine is not unpleasant but it is heavy and salt-forward, optimized for bodies doing industrial labor in humid heat. Bring supplemental food if you have specific dietary needs or a sensitive palate: nuts, dried fruit, dense calorie bars, anything shelf-stable and moisture-resistant. Everything you bring will absorb humidity and soften within hours of being opened. Pack in vacuum-sealed portions sized for single use.

### Tools and Miscellaneous

- **An anti-fungal foot powder.** Apply liberally, twice daily, between toes and on soles. This is pharmacy, not luxury.

- **A small towel made of quick-dry synthetic material.** You will use it constantly — wiping your face, drying your hands after touching mineral-scaled surfaces, clearing condensation from equipment.

- **A waterproof journal and pencil** (not pen — ink runs in humidity, graphite doesn't). You will want to take notes. Thurm's curving streets and non-grid layout are disorienting, and mapping your route is both practically useful and psychologically grounding.

- **A tuning fork or pitch pipe.** This is idiosyncratic but recommended. When the drone becomes overwhelming and your sense of hearing feels colonized, striking a tuning fork and holding it to your ear gives you a single, clean, *known* frequency to anchor your auditory processing. It's a reset button for overwhelmed ears. Several travelers have reported that the psychological relief of hearing a single pure tone in the midst of the drone is disproportionate to its physical effect.

- **Small iron objects.** For the seep-pools. I'll discuss this in the cultural section, but you should bring them: a few iron nails, washers, or rings. Nothing precious. Nothing with sentimental value.

- **A vibration-dampening pad.** A small square of dense rubber or sorbothane foam, the kind used under heavy machinery. When you need to sit — and you will, because the heat and humidity and altitude sap your stamina — placing this between yourself and the stone surface will significantly reduce the conducted vibration entering your skeleton. It seems absurd to carry a slab of rubber into a city, but your body will thank you at the end of the first day.

## Psychological and Sensory Preparation

### The Drone

I've addressed this from a physical perspective, but the psychological dimension requires separate treatment.

The drone will be the dominant feature of your experience in Thurm. Not the architecture. Not the culture. Not the view from the plateau's edge. The drone. It will color everything. It will be the medium through which you perceive every other aspect of the city, because it is constant and inescapable and it occupies the precise frequency range — low, resonant, physical — that the human nervous system interprets as threatening.

You need to understand this going in: **you will feel afraid, and there will be nothing to be afraid of.** The drone triggers your fight-or-flight response not because it signals danger but because your brainstem cannot distinguish between the infrasonic signature of a large predator's vocalization (or a structural collapse, or an earthquake) and the infrasonic signature of warm water moving through limestone pipes. Your amygdala will be activated for the duration of your visit. Your cortisol levels will be elevated. You will startle easily. You will have a persistent, groundless conviction that something terrible is about to happen. In certain locations — the culvert crossings, the seep-plazas — this may intensify to the point of panic.

**This is mechanical. It is not prophetic. Nothing terrible is about to happen. The city has been here for centuries and the water has been rising for millennia and the terrible thing, if there is one, is operating on a timescale so long that you will not be present for any perceptible increment of it.**

Preparation strategies:

- **Meditation or mindfulness practice.** Two weeks minimum before arrival. Not the relaxation kind — the observational kind. You want the ability to note an emotional state ("I am feeling dread"), attribute it ("this is an infrasonic response"), and decline to act on it ("I will continue walking"), all in real time. This is an acquired skill and it will be tested strenuously.

- **Exposure to loud, sustained, low-frequency environments.** Power plants. Waterfalls. Large construction sites. Stadium events. The goal is not to acclimate to Thurm's specific sound — nothing else sounds like Thurm — but to build your tolerance for sustained auditory stress.

- **Establish a personal ritual for managing acoustic overwhelm.** This can be anything — stepping to the edge of the city where the drone diminishes slightly, using the tuning fork, humming a specific note that you find grounding, putting in your maximum-protection earplugs and closing your eyes for two minutes. Have a plan. Know what you'll do before you need to do it, because in the moment of peak drone exposure, your executive function will be compromised and you'll need the autopilot.

### Disorientation and the Non-Grid

Thurm's streets curve and spiral around the seep-nodes. There is no grid. There are no reliable sight lines because the diffuse light eliminates shadows that would normally help you orient. The drone has no directionality — it comes from everywhere, from below, reflected off every surface — so you cannot use it as a navigational cue. The haze at the plateau's edge is uniform, offering no landmark beyond the city in any direction.

You will get lost. Accept this. Build it into your plans. Allow extra time for everything. Carry your pencil-and-journal map and update it at every turn. If you find yourself on a street you don't recognize, walk toward the city's edge — the transition to bare plateau is always abrupt and obvious, and from the city's boundary you can reorient by noting the relative positions of the factory chimneys (southeast, identifiable by their steam plumes) and the ridgeline endpoints.

The spiral streets are not random. They follow the subsurface hydrology, curving around the seep-nodes where the water pressure is highest and the channels are densest. If you begin to perceive this pattern — that the streets are shaped by what flows beneath them — the city's layout becomes less bewildering, though no less labyrinthine. You are walking on the surface expression of an underground river system. The streets are its grammar.

### Time Perception

Multiple travelers report that time behaves strangely in Thurm. Not in a physical sense — your chronometer will keep accurate time — but in a perceptual sense. The diffuse, unchanging light removes the usual solar cues for time of day. The drone's long, slow pressure swells create a rhythm that is too slow for the human perceptual system to track consciously — you cannot feel a swell happening, only notice in retrospect that the drone is louder or softer than it was an unspecified time ago. The city's pace is glacial, its changes measured in years, and this temporal inertia is contagious. You will lose hours. You will think you've been walking for twenty minutes and discover it's been two hours. You will sit to rest for a moment and realize the light has shifted from diffuse-bright to diffuse-dim without your noticing the transition.

Set alarms. Carry a watch and look at it regularly. Build structure into your days deliberately, because Thurm will dissolve that structure if you let it. Plan meals at fixed times. Plan returns to your lodging at fixed times. The city exerts a kind of temporal gravity — a pull toward the plateau's own timescale, which is measured in the patient dissolution of limestone, in the slow widening of solution channels, in the generational creep of mineral deposits — and your mammalian sense of urgency is no match for it.

## Cultural Preparation and Conduct

### Communication

The residents of Thurm speak loudly, out of necessity. Their language — whatever it is; the drone makes phonemic precision difficult for an outside ear — is accompanied by an extensive system of hand signals that ranges from practical ("follow me," "stop," "danger") to nuanced ("approximately," "I am uncertain," "this is the old way"). You will not master this system in a short visit, but learning the basic practical signs before arrival will smooth every interaction. Ask previous travelers or consult whatever cross-dimensional guides exist for the local gesture vocabulary. At minimum, learn: greeting, gratitude, question, yes, no, and help.

The residents are not unfriendly. They are *preoccupied.* Life in Thurm requires constant attentiveness to the water system, to the pressure cycles, to the condition of buildings and channels and the slow chess game between infrastructure and geology. They will answer your questions but they will not elaborate unprompted. They will house you but they will not entertain you. You are a transient feature of a place that thinks in centuries; be gracious about your own impermanence.

### The Seep-Pools and the Offerings

The seep-plazas are significant to Thurm's residents in a way that is not religious in any framework you're likely to recognize but is absolutely serious. The offerings — small iron objects, ceramic tokens — placed at the edges of the seep-pools are not worship. They are closer to... acknowledgment. A recognition that the water precedes the city, that it permits the city, and that this permission is not guaranteed. The iron is symbolically (and chemically) significant: it is the material the city uses to shape and contain the water, and placing it back at the water's source is a gesture of reciprocity. Or perhaps humility. The distinction may not exist in Thurm.

**Do not take anything from a seep-pool.** Do not touch the offerings. Do not wade in the pools. Do not test the water's temperature with your hand (it is uncomfortably hot at these points, near the source — you could burn yourself).

If you wish to leave an offering — and it is considered respectful to do so, particularly if you are benefiting from the city's water-powered infrastructure, which you will be, since it heats your room and lights your hallway — bring a simple iron object. A nail. A washer. Approach the pool's edge, place it on the stone near the water without touching the water itself, and step back. No words. No ceremony. The casualness is the point. It is maintenance, not prayer. You are contributing a small piece of iron to the mineral cycle that will eventually dissolve it and carry its atoms into the same channels that power the city's dynamos and give the stone its voice. You are feeding the system.

### The Factories

The factory district is not off-limits to visitors, but it is unwelcoming by design. The buildings are thick-walled to contain pressure, not to create atmosphere. The workers are focused. The machinery is dangerous in the specific way that high-pressure water systems are dangerous — there is no fire, no explosion risk, but a failed pipe joint at geothermal pressure will cut through flesh as cleanly as any blade.

If you visit — and you should, because the machinery is extraordinary, a kind of steampunk-before-the-steam-was-harnessed engineering that uses the planet itself as its boiler — stay behind any marked lines, touch nothing, and follow any instruction from a worker immediately and without question. The hand signals in the factory district are emphatic and non-negotiable.

The steam venting from the factory chimneys is hotter than the ambient humidity and carries higher mineral concentrations. Avoid standing in a steam plume. The minerals will deposit on your skin, your eyes, your respiratory tract. It is not toxic in brief exposure but it is deeply unpleasant.

### The Plateau Edge

Walk to the edge at least once. The haze is disorienting and beautiful. You will stand on wet, bare limestone and look out at... nothing. White. The warm breath of the stone rising to meet the sky, with no visible ground below, no horizon, no reference point except the faint thermal updrafts that ripple the haze and sometimes, briefly, thin it enough to suggest depth without revealing it. You are standing on a mesa in a cloud of its own making, and the city behind you is humming, and the kestrel is somewhere above you or below you and you cannot tell which.

Stay well back from the actual edge. The limestone at the plateau's margins is eroded from below as well as above, and what looks like solid ground may be an undercut ledge with insufficient structural integrity to support your weight. A good rule: if the stone is wetter than the surrounding surface, it's a seep zone, and the rock beneath it may be compromised. Stay on dry-ish stone. (In Thurm, "dry-ish" means "not actively running with water." Everything glistens.)

## Health and Medical Preparation

### Bring These:

- **Broad-spectrum antifungal cream and powder.** You will need it. The humidity and warmth create ideal conditions for fungal skin infections — athlete's foot, jock itch, intertrigo in skin folds. Prophylactic application to vulnerable areas twice daily.

- **Anti-inflammatory medication** (ibuprofen or equivalent). The infrasonic exposure causes low-grade inflammation in some individuals — headaches, joint aches, a general sense of being bruised from the inside. Having an anti-inflammatory on hand is practical.

- **Antihistamine.** The mineral content of the air and water can trigger allergic-type reactions even in people without known allergies. A mild antihistamine will manage the sinus congestion, eye irritation, and skin prickling that some travelers report.

- **Electrolyte supplements.** You are sweating constantly and may not be adequately replacing salts through Thurm's local food and water. Oral rehydration salts or electrolyte tablets.

- **Motion sickness medication** (meclizine or equivalent). The infrasonic vestibular disruption can produce a motion-sickness-like syndrome, particularly at the culvert crossings and seep-plazas. Having a vestibular suppressant available is wise.

- **A basic wound care kit.** The mineral-laden water in Thurm is not sterile, and any break in the skin that contacts it — a scratch from rough stone, a blister from wet boots — is at risk of infection. Clean wounds promptly with purified water, apply antibiotic ointment, and cover with waterproof dressings. Check daily for signs of infection. The warm humidity accelerates bacterial growth in wounds.

### Departing Thurm: Recovery

Plan at least two days of recovery time in a dry, quiet environment after departing the plateau. Your ears will ring. The absence of the drone will feel like a sound in itself — a ringing silence that is initially more disorienting than the drone was. Your skin will peel. Your feet will need a week to fully recover their normal texture. You may find yourself reflexively flinching at low-frequency sounds — traffic rumble, air conditioning hum, distant thunder — for several weeks. Your sleep cycle will be disrupted; the loss of all temporal cues in Thurm and the chronic cortisol elevation will have knocked your circadian rhythm off its axis.

All of this is normal. All of this resolves. You were not damaged by Thurm; you were saturated by it, and the desaturation takes time.

## Final Notes on Disposition

Thurm is not a place you visit for pleasure, exactly. It is not a place you visit for knowledge, exactly. It is a place you visit to understand something about the relationship between human stubbornness and geological indifference — about what it means to build a city on a dissolving foundation and run your turbines on the very force that is dissolving it. The people of Thurm have not conquered their environment. They have not adapted to it, not in the usual sense. They have entered into a negotiation with it that they cannot win and have decided to conduct that negotiation with extraordinary engineering, with iron and ceramic and hand signals, with offerings at the seep-pools, with a patience that approaches the geological.

You will leave Thurm with water in your ears and minerals in your lungs and a hum in your bones that fades over days. You will leave with the memory of a city that functions — that heats itself, lights itself, powers itself — on the upwelling of warm water through ancient stone. You will leave with the understanding that the drone was not noise. It was the sound of the city's relationship with the force that made it and will eventually unmake it, and that the residents' refusal to hear it as anything remarkable was not ignorance but a form of grace.

Bring your iron nails. Place them at the water's edge. Do not expect the water to notice. That's not the point.

The drone swells. The lights flicker. You go.
