# Preparing for the Singing Hollow

---

## Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before anything else, sit with the description. Read it again. Let the implications settle, because this place operates on rules that will punish you for assuming it works like anywhere else you've been.

The Singing Hollow is not hostile in any way you're trained to recognize. It won't attack you. It won't poison you. It won't collapse on you. What it will do is far more subtle: it will begin to integrate you into its equilibrium, and you need to decide before you arrive how much of that you're willing to allow, and what your extraction plan looks like if you let it go too far.

The central danger is this: **the Hollow does not accept change, and you are a changing thing.** Your heartbeat, your breathing, your cellular metabolism — you are a process, not a state, and you are entering a place that appears to preserve states indefinitely. The question you cannot answer in advance is whether the Hollow will fail to affect you because you're foreign to it, or whether it will succeed in affecting you because it affects everything within its geometry. The evidence of the fauna — creatures that have apparently adapted to exist within its rules — suggests the latter is at least possible. The evidence of the human artifacts suggests that someone else has grappled with this question and arrived at their own answer, which they did not leave behind in any legible form.

Go in with humility. Go in with time limits. Go in with someone who stays outside.

## Physical Preparation

### Hydration — This Is Not Optional, This Is Survival

The air is described as pulling moisture from lips and eyes within minutes. This is aggressive desiccation. You're looking at something comparable to the driest desert air on Earth, possibly drier, possibly something qualitatively different given the mineral substrate's apparent hygroscopic properties. Gypsum is a hydrated mineral — calcium sulfate dihydrate — which means the walls themselves may be actively pulling water from the air and, by extension, from you.

**Before arrival (24-48 hours prior):**
- Hyperhydrate. Not to the point of water intoxication, but drink steadily and consistently in the days before your visit. Your body should be at peak hydration when you descend.
- Increase electrolyte intake. The kind of desiccation described here won't just pull water — it'll concentrate your blood salts, and if you're already electrolyte-depleted, you'll cramp, get confused, and make bad decisions in a place where bad decisions may be irreversible.
- Eat water-rich foods. Fruits, vegetables, anything that puts moisture in your tissues rather than just your bloodstream.

**Gear:**
- Carry at minimum two liters of water for every hour you plan to spend inside, and plan your visit to be short. Thirty minutes for a first visit. Sixty at most. More on timing below.
- Use a sealed hydration system — a bladder with a bite valve, not an open bottle. You do not want to expose water to the Hollow's air any more than necessary. The description mentions a bowl with residue that smells like ozone. We don't know what the Hollow does to exposed water, and you don't want to find out by drinking it.
- Bring a small spray bottle for your face — eyes, nostrils, lips. Reapply every ten minutes. Set a timer.
- Petroleum jelly or a heavy occlusive balm for your lips, the rims of your nostrils, and the corners of your eyes. These are the moisture-loss points the description specifically mentions. Seal them.
- Saline nasal spray. The "clean mineral ache" in the sinuses is your mucous membranes drying out. Once they crack, you're dealing with nosebleeds and an open pathway for whatever particulates are in that dust to enter your bloodstream.
- Eye drops — preservative-free artificial tears. Bring several single-use vials. If your eyes dry out, your vision goes, and you need your vision.

### Respiratory Protection

The dust is fine, pale, and behaves unusually — it "absorbs" footprints and settles back to smoothness. This suggests an extremely fine particulate, possibly sub-micron, with unusual electrostatic or physical properties. You will be breathing this.

Gypsum dust on Earth causes respiratory irritation. Whatever this mineral substrate is, it's gypsum-adjacent but clearly not identical — it glows, it may be biologically active given that plants are metabolizing it, and the residue in the bowl smells like ozone, which implies some kind of ongoing low-level chemical or electrical process in the mineral itself.

- Wear a properly fitted N95 or P100 respirator. Not a cloth mask, not a surgical mask. Something with a real seal. Test the fit before you go in.
- The respirator will also slow your moisture loss through exhalation, which is a significant secondary benefit.
- Bring a backup. If the first one becomes uncomfortable or you need to remove it for any reason, you want a fresh one.
- Be aware that the respirator will alter your breathing pattern, and your breathing is going to be under pressure from the resonance (more on this below). You need to practice breathing slowly and deliberately through the respirator before you enter, so that when the Hollow starts trying to synchronize your breath, you have a practiced rhythm to fall back on rather than letting it impose one through an already-restricted airway.

### Skin Protection

The dust doesn't hold footprints. It settles back. The description frames this as the Hollow "not accepting change," but the physical mechanism might be simpler and more concerning: the dust may be chemically or electrostatically active in a way that causes it to self-level, and that same activity might interact with your skin.

- Long sleeves, long pants, closed shoes. Not sandals, not shorts, regardless of the body-warm temperature.
- Light, breathable fabric — you don't want to overheat. The temperature is described as blood-warm, roughly 37°C/98.6°F. In dry air, this is tolerable but you won't be shedding heat efficiently through sweat, because sweat will evaporate almost instantly. Wear light colors, natural fibers if possible.
- Gloves. Thin, flexible, something you can work in. You will be tempted to touch the plants, the walls, the artifacts. Gloves give you a layer between your skin and whatever electrochemical processes are happening at the mineral surface.
- Do NOT go barefoot. I know the place will make you want to. The body-warm dust, the amber light, the drone — everything about the Hollow suggests a womb-space, and some part of your brain will want to curl up on the floor and press yourself to the ground. This is the same instinct that makes the fauna press themselves to the vibrating leaves. Recognize it. Resist it, at least on your first visit.

### Eye Protection

The wall-glow is described as faint and diffuse, but it's also described as having an "interior luminescence" — it comes from within the mineral. We don't know the spectrum. It could include UV. It could include frequencies you can't consciously perceive but that still affect your retinas.

- Bring UV-filtering clear-lens glasses or goggles. Not sunglasses — you need all the visible light you can get in a dim space. But protect against what you can't see.
- The goggles will also help with moisture retention around your eyes.

## The Resonance: This Is the Real Preparation

Everything above is physical logistics. Important, but not the thing that will determine whether your visit goes well or badly. The resonance is.

Let me be very direct about what the description is telling you: **you are entering an acoustic environment that will attempt to entrain your autonomic nervous system.** Your breathing will synchronize. Your heartbeat will try to follow. Your skeleton will resonate. This is not metaphorical. This is physics — your body is a mechanical system with resonant frequencies, and the Hollow is producing a complex, stable waveform tuned to... something. The fact that fauna have evolved (or been shaped) to feed on this vibration tells you it carries energy. The fact that it has "architecture" tells you it carries information. The fact that it's uniform throughout the space tells you it's a standing wave, which means the geometry of the Hollow itself is part of the instrument.

You are walking into the body of a musical instrument while it's being played.

### Before You Go

**Practice autonomous rhythm control.** This is the single most important preparation you can make, and most travelers skip it because it sounds abstract.

What I mean is: spend at least a week before your visit practicing the ability to maintain a deliberate, chosen breathing rhythm regardless of external stimulus. Meditation works. Box breathing works. Anything that gives you the experience of consciously setting your respiratory pace and holding it against distraction.

Then practice doing it with sound. Put on droning music — tanpura recordings, Tibetan singing bowls, even a simple sine wave generator — and practice breathing at a rhythm that is deliberately *not* synchronized to the sound. This is harder than it sounds. Your body wants to sync. You need to build the skill of not syncing, of maintaining your own rhythm as a conscious choice, so that when the Hollow starts pulling your breathing into its pattern, you have a trained response to fall back on.

**The heartbeat is harder.** You cannot consciously control your heart rate the way you can control your breathing. What you can do is control your breathing, and your breathing influences your heart rate through the vagus nerve. So: if you can hold your breathing, you can indirectly resist cardiac entrainment. If you lose your breathing, you may lose your heartbeat too — not in the sense that it stops, but in the sense that it's no longer yours. I don't know what that means in the Hollow. I don't think anyone does. The person who left those artifacts might know. They're not here to ask.

**Bring earplugs, but don't rely on them.** The description says you feel the resonance in your teeth and bones before you hear it in your ears. This is bone conduction and somatic transmission. Earplugs will reduce the auditory component, which is worth doing — bring high-NRR foam plugs or custom-molded musician's earplugs — but they will not stop the vibration from entering your body through your skeleton, your chest cavity, your skull. The sound is in the floor. It's in the walls. If you touch anything, it's in your hands.

Actually — this brings up an important point. **Bring a foam pad or inflatable cushion to stand or sit on.** Anything that decouples your body from the stone floor will reduce somatic transmission. Rubber-soled shoes help for the same reason. You want mechanical isolation between your body and the resonating surfaces. You cannot achieve total isolation — the air itself is vibrating — but you can reduce the input.

**Consider noise-canceling headphones playing a known, chosen sound.** Not music with emotional content — something neutral. White noise, pink noise, or a drone at a frequency you've chosen and practiced with. The goal isn't silence (which you can't achieve here); it's to give your auditory system something to lock onto that isn't the Hollow's chord. Your brain will try to parse the dominant acoustic input and synchronize to it. Give it a competing signal that you control.

This is a real tradeoff, though. The headphones will reduce your sensory awareness of the space. You'll be partially deaf to changes in the resonance that might signal danger or important information. If you choose headphones, have your outside partner monitoring and able to signal you visually (light flashes from above, pre-arranged signals).

### The First Few Minutes

When you step into the Hollow, the resonance will hit you immediately, but your conscious processing of it will lag. The description captures this perfectly — "the first thing you noticed and the thing you keep forgetting to think about." The sound enters your body before it enters your mind. By the time you're thinking about it, it's already in your breathing, already in your bones.

Plan for this. In the first sixty seconds, do nothing but stand still, breathe deliberately at your practiced rhythm, and inventory your body. What do you feel? Where do you feel it? Is your breathing steady? Can you feel your heartbeat? Is it regular? This is your baseline. Memorize it.

Set a timer. Not a mental timer — a physical device, vibrating on your wrist. You will lose time in here. Not because time moves differently (though it might) but because your proprioceptive and temporal processing will be disrupted by the resonance. A watch with a vibrating alarm every five minutes will anchor you to external time.

## The Temporal/Ontological Concern

The description tells you that the Hollow "doesn't accept change." The dust doesn't hold impressions. The plants sway but don't grow. Nothing degrades. This is presented poetically, but take it literally for safety purposes and think about what it means for you.

You are a changing thing. You metabolize. Your cells die and are replaced. Your blood circulates. You are a process. If the Hollow's equilibrium acts on you — if it attempts to impose stasis on your biology — the effects could range from trivial to catastrophic.

**Best case:** the Hollow's stasis field (for lack of a better term) is too weak to affect a large, complex, warm-blooded organism on the timescale of a short visit. You notice nothing. You leave fine.

**Moderate case:** metabolic processes slow subtly. You don't feel hungry or thirsty even though you're dehydrating. Your sense of time dilates. You feel profoundly calm, even sedated. You sit down and don't want to get up. This is the scenario I consider most likely, and it's dangerous specifically because it feels good. The Hollow is blood-warm, dimly lit, resonant — it is a sensory environment optimized for mammalian comfort. If it also suppresses your awareness of your own biological needs, you could sit there for hours without realizing you're critically dehydrated.

**Worst case:** the stasis effect is real and strong and acts on your tissues at a molecular level, and when you leave, accumulated biological changes that were "held" during your time inside release all at once. Hours of dehydration hitting you in seconds. Hours of waste product accumulation. Hours of oxygen debt. I have no evidence this is what happens, but I can't rule it out, and it argues strongly for short initial visits with careful medical monitoring afterward.

**Your outside partner** — the person who stays on the rim — should be tracking time and enforcing your extraction. Agree on a maximum duration before you enter. If you don't emerge on time, they come to the edge and signal you. If you don't respond, they come in and pull you out. They should have water, electrolyte solution, and emergency medical supplies (oxygen, at minimum — if you've been in a metabolically suppressed state, you may need supplemental oxygen when you emerge).

## What to Bring

### Essential
- Sealed hydration system, minimum 2L
- Facial spray bottle
- Petroleum jelly / heavy balm
- Saline nasal spray
- Artificial tears (multiple single-use vials)
- P100 respirator + backup
- UV-filtering clear goggles
- Light, full-coverage clothing
- Thin gloves
- Rubber-soled shoes with good support
- Foam sitting pad or inflatable cushion
- High-NRR earplugs
- Vibrating wristwatch with interval timer
- Small LED headlamp (red light mode — preserve your adaptation to the dim glow, but have light available if the glow changes)
- Notebook and pencil (not pen — ink may behave oddly in extreme dryness; pencil is graphite-on-cellulose and mechanically simple)
- Voice recorder (your note-taking ability may be compromised by the resonance; speak your observations even if you can also write them)
- A known object from outside — a stone from the surface, a photograph, anything that is definitively *from elsewhere* — to serve as a grounding anchor, a reminder that the world outside the Hollow exists and is real and is where you belong

### For Documentation
- Camera (the glow is dim, so bring something with good low-light performance; manual focus, because autofocus may struggle in uniform amber light with translucent subjects)
- Audio recorder — bring the best one you can find, something with high bit-depth and sample rate, ideally multiple microphones positioned at different points in the space. The resonance is described as uniform throughout the Hollow, but verify this. Any variation could be critically informative. Recording the sound also lets you analyze it later, outside the Hollow's influence, which is when you'll actually be able to think clearly about what you heard.
- Contact microphone — attach this to the floor or wall to capture the vibrational component separately from the airborne sound. The somatic resonance may have different characteristics than the audible sound.
- A tuning fork, a known frequency. Strike it inside the Hollow. Does it ring true? Does the Hollow alter its pitch? Does the Hollow absorb it, ignore it, harmonize with it? This is a simple, powerful test of how the acoustic environment interacts with introduced sound.
- A small battery-powered metronome, set to a rhythm that is *not* a factor or multiple of any rhythm you can detect in the drone. Observe whether the Hollow alters it, whether the resonance shifts to accommodate or suppress it. This tells you whether the equilibrium is passive (just stable) or active (maintaining itself against perturbation).
- A portable spectrometer, if you can get one, to analyze the wall-glow. Knowing the emission spectrum will tell you enormously about what's happening in that mineral substrate.

### For the Crack

I need to talk about the crack.

The crack in the center of the floor is the most dangerous feature of the Hollow, and I almost guarantee it will be the thing you're most drawn to. The description is careful to note that it changes the resonance — not in volume or pitch, but in *depth*, adding what's described as a spatial dimension to the sound, suggesting architecture below. The artifacts on the shelf are positioned facing it. The braided cord is exactly the right length to lower something into it.

**On your first visit, do not approach the crack.** Stay at the periphery. Observe from a distance. Let your recordings capture whatever they capture. Do not kneel beside it. Do not put your ear to it. The description tells you that doing so reveals a deeper layer of the resonance, and based on everything else we know about the Hollow's entrainment effects, bringing your head — your brain, your brainstem, your auditory processing centers — into close proximity with a concentrated source of that resonance is reckless until you understand what the resonance does to you during normal exposure.

On subsequent visits, if there are subsequent visits, approach the crack incrementally. Five feet closer each time. Monitor your physiological response. Have your partner watching.

If and when you do examine the crack closely:
- Do not put any body part into it
- Do not lower anything into it on the existing cord (you don't know what it's been exposed to, what's on it, or what protocol it was part of)
- You might lower a small microphone on your own cord, if you've reached a point where you understand the Hollow well enough to take that risk. But understand that this is the first *change* you'll be introducing to the Hollow's equilibrium in a deliberate way, and the Hollow is described as a system that does not accept change. What happens when you force change into a system that resists it? Maybe nothing. Maybe everything.

## The Artifacts

Do not touch them.

I know. I know you want to. The worry-stone, the braided cord, the bowl with the ozone-smelling residue — they're artifacts of human (or human-adjacent) activity, and they're the closest thing in the Hollow to a message from someone who's been here before you.

But consider: they are part of the equilibrium. They've been sitting on that shelf for an unknown duration in a place where nothing changes. They may be integrated into the standing wave. The bowl's residue may be electrochemically active. The cord may have properties that aren't apparent visually. Moving them could alter the resonance, could disrupt whatever balance exists between the plants and the fauna and the sound and the light and the crack.

Photograph them. Measure them if you can do so without touching them. Note their positions relative to each other, to the crack, to the tally marks on the wall. Record the smell of the residue if you can get close enough without disturbing anything.

On a later visit — much later, after multiple previous trips, after you've built a thorough understanding of the Hollow's baseline — you might carefully examine them. But not the first time. Not the second time. These are someone else's tools, left in someone else's ongoing experiment, and you don't know what the experiment is.

## The Fauna

The creatures that feed on vibration are, to me, the most important thing in the Hollow from a scientific perspective, and also the clearest warning about what the Hollow does to living things.

These organisms have adapted to metabolize sound. They press themselves to vibrating surfaces and feed. They are pale, mineral-colored, slow-moving, solitary, non-interactive. They have been shaped — by evolution, by the Hollow, by something — into minimal, isolated beings whose entire existence is oriented around their relationship with the nearest plant and its tone.

Look at them carefully and ask yourself: **is this what the Hollow does to living things over time?** Does it simplify you? Reduce you to your relationship with the sound? Strip away sociality, dynamism, complexity, and leave you as a pale, still thing pressed against a vibrating surface, drinking the tone?

I don't say this to be dramatic. I say this because the entrainment effects on your breathing and heartbeat are the beginning of a spectrum, and the fauna may be showing you the end of it.

Watch the creatures. Don't touch them. If one approaches you — they probably won't, but if one does — stay still and let it pass. Don't let it settle on you. If it tries to press itself against your skin, gently dislodge it without harming it and move away. You don't know what "feeding on vibration through a living surface" feels like from the surface's perspective, and you don't need to find out.

## Mental and Emotional Preparation

The Hollow will feel sacred. I don't use that word loosely. The combination of amber light, body-warm air, deep resonance, enclosed geometry, and living strangeness will trigger something in you that is very old and very powerful — the part of your brain that recognizes holy places, that wants to kneel, that feels the presence of something larger than itself.

This response is not wrong, exactly. The Hollow may genuinely be sacred in some meaningful sense — it is, at minimum, extraordinary, and the line between the extraordinary and the sacred is drawn by the observer. But the feeling of sacredness will make you want to do things that are unsafe: stay longer, go deeper, touch things, lie down, close your eyes, stop fighting the entrainment and let the sound take your breathing and your heartbeat and see where it leads.

**Decide before you enter what you will and will not do.** Write it on your arm if you have to. Specific, non-negotiable rules:

1. I will leave by [time].
2. I will not approach the crack closer than [distance].
3. I will not touch the artifacts.
4. I will not remove my respirator.
5. I will not lie down.
6. I will not close my eyes for more than a blink.
7. If I notice my breathing has synchronized, I will immediately perform [specific breathing exercise] to break the pattern.
8. If I feel the urge to stay, I will recognize that urge as a signal to leave.

That last one is critical. **The desire to stay is the clearest sign that the Hollow is working on you.** A place that doesn't accept change, that maintains perfect equilibrium, that entrains your autonomic rhythms — if it starts to feel like home, if it starts to feel like the place you were always meant to be, if the surface world starts to feel like the disturbance and the Hollow starts to feel like the rest-state — leave. Leave immediately. That is not your feeling. That is the Hollow's equilibrium asserting itself through your nervous system.

## After You Leave

Exit the Hollow and immediately:

1. Drink water. Slowly, steadily. At least a liter over the next thirty minutes.
2. Eat something. Anything calorie-dense. Your metabolism needs to be reminded that it's a process, not a state.
3. Move. Walk, stretch, jump, do anything that emphasizes your body as a dynamic, changing system.
4. Talk to your partner. Say out loud what you experienced. The act of narrating forces your brain to process the experience sequentially and linguistically, which is a different mode from the holistic, somatic, nonverbal processing the Hollow encourages.
5. Listen to something — music you know, a podcast, a human voice. Overwrite the resonance in your auditory memory as quickly as you can.
6. Monitor yourself over the next 24-48 hours. Sleep disruption, continued sense of hearing the drone, urge to return, unusual thirst, emotional flatness, loss of interest in social interaction — any of these should be taken seriously and may indicate that the Hollow's effects persist after departure.
7. Do not return for at least a week. Give your body and mind time to fully re-equilibrate to normal conditions before re-exposing yourself.

## Final Thoughts

The Singing Hollow is not trying to hurt you. I don't think it's trying to do anything. It is a system in equilibrium — a deep, total, possibly eternal equilibrium — and it will incorporate you into that equilibrium the way the sea incorporates a stone: not with malice, not with intent, but with the simple, overwhelming pressure of what it is.

The person who left the artifacts understood something about this place that you don't yet. They carved marks in the wall. They braided a cord. They chipped a bowl and put something in it that smelled like lightning. They wore a worry-stone smooth with their thumb. These are the actions of someone who came back, again and again, and developed a practice — a discipline, a ritual, a technology — for interacting with the Hollow safely. Or at least for interacting with it on terms they found acceptable.

You don't have their knowledge. You have only the evidence they left behind, and the Hollow itself, and whatever you bring with you in the way of caution, curiosity, and the simple animal stubbornness that keeps your heart beating at its own rhythm when everything around you is singing a different song.

Go carefully. Stay briefly. Come back changed as little as possible, knowing that "as little as possible" may still be more than you expected.

And if, standing at the rim, looking down into that amber glow, hearing the first faint edge of the resonance rising up to meet you — if you decide not to go in at all? That's not cowardice. That's the wisdom of a living thing that knows it wants to keep living, keep changing, keep being a process rather than a state.

The Hollow will still be there. It will always still be there. It doesn't change. That's the one thing you can count on.
