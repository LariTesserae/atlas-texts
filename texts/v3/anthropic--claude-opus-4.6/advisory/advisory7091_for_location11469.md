# Preparing for the Breathing Walls of Keth

## Understanding What You Are Entering

Before any practical preparation, you need to sit with something uncomfortable: Keth is not a destination in the way most locations are destinations. It is a process you will temporarily occupy. You will not explore Keth so much as Keth will flow around you while you stand inside it, and every preparation you make must account for the fact that the environment is not hostile toward you but is profoundly indifferent to you in the specific way that geological time is indifferent to a mayfly. The town does not mean you harm. It also does not mean you a door when you need one.

This distinction matters because it changes the nature of the danger. You are not preparing to survive an attack or resist a predator. You are preparing to remain intact inside a system that is constantly, silently rearranging itself and that will seal you inside a mineral tomb with exactly as much malice as your own fingernail has when it grows over a hangnail — which is to say none at all, and that is precisely what makes it terrifying.

## Hydration and Moisture Retention: The Primary Survival Concern

This is your most critical preparation, and I cannot overstate it. The alkali flat is described as not merely dry but *hostile to the concept of moisture*. The mineral substrate is calcium-hungry. This means the environment is actively pulling water from anything that contains it, including you.

### What Will Happen to Your Body

Within minutes: lips chap, nasal passages tighten and sting. Within an hour: your eyes will feel gritty, blinking will become deliberate rather than automatic, the corners of your mouth will begin to crack. Within several hours: your throat will feel coated, swallowing will require conscious effort, your skin — particularly on your hands, which you will inevitably touch the walls with — will begin to feel papery and tight. The moisture in your breath, which normally you never think about, will become something you are aware of losing with every exhalation. You will feel yourself evaporating.

This is not like desert dehydration, where the heat pulls sweat from you and you lose water through exertion. This is passive extraction. You could stand perfectly still and the environment would drink you. The calcium-hungry mineral doesn't need to touch you to affect you — the air itself, saturated with alkaline particulate, serves as the medium of extraction.

### Preparation

**Water supply:** Bring far more than you think you need. For a visit of six to eight hours — which is likely the maximum safe duration — carry at least six liters. Sealed containers are essential. Not bottles with screw caps, which will allow slow moisture migration through the threading. Containers with gasket seals, the kind used for laboratory reagent storage or high-altitude mountaineering. The environment may not degrade plastic or metal, but it will find any gap. Wrap your containers in a secondary sealed bag as a precaution. There is no water source in Keth or on the surrounding flat. None. If your water supply fails, you have hours before serious physiological consequences and perhaps a day before death, depending on your exertion level.

**Electrolyte balance:** The alkaline environment won't just dehydrate you; it will preferentially pull certain ions. Bring oral rehydration salts and dissolve them in a portion of your water. Sip regularly, not in response to thirst — by the time you feel thirst in Keth, you are already meaningfully behind.

**Moisture barriers for skin:** Your entire body should be covered in a heavy emollient before entering Keth. Not a light moisturizer — something with an occlusive base, petroleum jelly or lanolin, the kind of thing that creates a physical barrier rather than simply adding water to the skin. Pay particular attention to your hands, lips, nostrils, and the skin around your eyes. Your hands will want to touch the walls. You will not be able to resist this impulse entirely; the texture is too strange, too inviting. Every time you touch the mineral surface, it will pull moisture from your fingertips. Wearing gloves helps (see below), but you should assume your hands will make direct contact at some point.

**Nasal protection:** The inside of your nose will be the first casualty. The tissue there is thin, moist, and exposed. Apply a nasal emollient — saline gel, not spray, because spray evaporates — before entering. Consider soft nasal filters or plugs if you can tolerate breathing through your mouth, but mouth-breathing in this environment has its own costs (accelerated moisture loss from the throat and lungs). A better solution is a lightweight respirator or mask with a moisture-recovery element — something that traps the humidity in your exhalation and returns it to you on your next inhalation. Closed-circuit breathing apparatuses used in mining or space applications would be ideal but are heavy and restrictive. A compromise is a simple heat-moisture exchange filter, the kind used in medical ventilation, fitted over a comfortable face mask. This will feel claustrophobic in the already close corridors of Keth, but it will meaningfully extend your safe duration.

**Eye protection:** Sealed goggles, not safety glasses. The alkaline particulate in the air will irritate your conjunctiva within the first hour. Bring artificial tears in single-use vials — multi-use bottles, once opened, will lose moisture to the environment and may become hypertonic, which will make things worse. Apply them prophylactically every thirty minutes.

## Navigation: The Problem That Cannot Be Fully Solved

Keth rearranges itself. Corridors seal. Walls dissolve. The layout drifts. This means every navigation strategy that depends on fixed reference points is unreliable. You cannot map Keth because the map will be wrong by the time you finish drawing it. You cannot mark walls because the wall you marked may be reabsorbed. You cannot rely on retracing your steps because your steps may no longer exist.

### What This Means Practically

The most dangerous scenario in Keth is not getting lost in the conventional sense — it is getting *enclosed*. A corridor closes behind you. The one ahead of you narrows. You are now in a space that is still large enough to stand in but that will, in some number of hours, become a space that is not large enough to stand in, and then not large enough to sit in, and then not large enough to exist in. The mineral grows at fingernail pace — roughly 3 to 4 millimeters per day, perhaps 0.1 to 0.2 millimeters per hour. This is slow. But if a corridor has already narrowed to a few centimeters wider than your body, and growth is occurring from both sides, you have perhaps a day before it seals, and you may not notice the narrowing until it is too late to squeeze back through.

### Preparation

**Maintain proximity to the perimeter.** Keth occupies an area no larger than a small village square. The alkali flat surrounds it on all sides. At no point inside Keth are you more than perhaps fifty or sixty meters from open ground. Your primary navigation strategy should be to never go so deep that you cannot, with reasonable effort, break through a thinning wall to reach the exterior. Stay within the outer third of the structure if possible.

**Carry a tool for testing wall thickness.** A long, thin metal probe — a steel knitting needle would work, or better, a titanium rod of similar dimensions. When you encounter a wall, you can push the probe into it to gauge thickness. The mineral yields slightly to pressure. A wall that is actively being reabsorbed will be thinner, softer, easier to probe. If you find yourself in a corridor that is closing, locate the thinnest wall and prepare to break through it.

**Carry a tool for breaking through walls.** A small geological pick or rock hammer, compact enough to carry on your belt. The mineral is chalky — it is not granite, it is not concrete. A sustained effort with a pick should be able to breach a wall of moderate thickness. However, there is a complication: the mineral may resist in ways that are not purely mechanical. If the substrate can reabsorb its own material, it may respond to damage by accelerating growth at the breach site. You should test this early in your visit — make a small nick in a wall and observe whether the wound closes faster than the surrounding growth rate. If it does, your escape tool has a time limit on its effectiveness, and you must work quickly.

**Lay a guideline.** Old-fashioned but potentially life-saving. Anchor a high-tensile cord — Dyneema or Spectra, something that won't stretch or degrade — at the entrance to Keth and pay it out as you move inward. If a corridor closes on your cord, you lose the line but you know which direction leads out. The risk is that the mineral will grow around the cord and incorporate it, or that a closing wall will sever it. Check the line frequently. Tug on it. If it resists more than it should, the walls are eating it and you should consider returning immediately.

**Carry a compass or inertial navigation device.** If there is a magnetic field in this world (which you should test on the alkali flat before entering Keth), a compass gives you a fixed reference independent of the shifting architecture. If there is no magnetic field — which would not be surprising given the lack of a visible sun or star — carry a small inertial measurement unit, the kind found in aircraft or advanced smartphones, which can track your heading and distance traveled by accelerometer. This will drift over time but will give you a rough sense of which direction leads outward.

**Mark your path in ways the mineral cannot easily digest.** If you mark walls with chalk or scratches, the self-healing mineral will erase them. Instead, leave markers on the *ground* — small metal or ceramic objects, brightly colored, placed at junctions. The ground substrate may consume these too, given enough time, but it should take longer than the hours of your visit. Check early markers on your return trip to see if they are sinking. If they are, your visit has a harder time limit.

## Clothing and Physical Protection

### Temperature

The ambient temperature is described as that of an autumn evening — perhaps 8 to 14 degrees Celsius. This is not cold enough to be immediately dangerous, but it is cold enough to become a problem over hours, especially as the relentless dryness strips away the thin layer of moisture on your skin that normally provides a small amount of insulating benefit (humid skin radiates less efficiently than dry skin). The deeper chambers, where the walls close overhead, may be cooler still — or warmer, if the mineral metabolism generates any heat. You do not know until you are there.

Wear layers. A moisture-wicking base layer (though in this environment, there is no moisture to wick — the base layer serves mainly as a comfortable interface between your emollient-coated skin and your outer clothing). A mid-layer for insulation — fleece or light synthetic down. A windproof outer layer, which in the absence of wind serves mainly as a barrier against particulate and as an additional moisture seal for your body.

### Particulate Protection

The mineral is chalky. You will raise fine dust as you walk. This dust, given the calcium-hungry nature of the substrate, will aggressively desiccate any mucous membrane it contacts. Your respirator or moisture-exchange mask will help, but also wear a hood or balaclava to protect your ears and the skin of your neck. Goggles, as discussed. Gloves — thin enough for dexterity (you need to handle your probe, your pick, your guideline) but complete in coverage. Nitrile over emollient-coated hands, with a textile glove over the nitrile for grip and abrasion resistance.

### Footwear

The ground is chalky mineral substrate. It yields slightly underfoot. Boots with stiff soles — you want to distribute your weight and minimize the chance of your foot sinking into a soft spot in the substrate. Ankle support matters; the corridors are narrow, the footing irregular, and if you twist an ankle in the deep interior of Keth, your ability to exit quickly is compromised. Gaiters to keep particulate out of your boots.

## Light

The ambient luminescence casts no shadows and provides enough light to see by, but the deeper chambers dim to near-darkness. You need supplemental light, but you should think carefully about what kind.

The hand-creatures avoid light. This is useful — it means a light source will clear your path of them, giving you warning and space. But it also means you are disrupting whatever they are doing, and you do not know what consequences that may have. If they are part of Keth's ecology — if they are involved in the mineral metabolism, the filament-grazing, the cycling of the architecture — then disturbing them may affect the behavior of the structure around you. This is speculative but worth considering.

Bring a headlamp with adjustable brightness. Keep it on its lowest useful setting in the deeper chambers. A red-light mode may be less disruptive to the hand-creatures, or it may not — you don't know their spectral sensitivity, but since they react to your presence from several meters away and the ambient light is a diffuse oyster-shell luminescence, they may be sensitive to wavelengths you wouldn't expect. Bring a backup light. Bring a third backup light. If your light fails in a deep chamber of Keth, you will be in absolute darkness in a shifting maze with no sound to orient by. This is among the worst scenarios I can imagine.

## The Subsonic Presence: Psychological and Physiological Preparation

The deep chambers contain a subsonic phenomenon — something felt in the ribs, below the threshold of hearing. This deserves serious preparation because infrasound at sufficient intensity causes physiological effects in humans that you will not be able to distinguish from psychological ones, and the inability to distinguish them will make both worse.

### What Infrasound Does

Frequencies below 20 Hz, at sufficient amplitude, can cause: a sense of unease or dread with no identifiable source; visual disturbances (vibration of the eyeball at resonant frequencies can cause smearing or phantom movements in peripheral vision); nausea; chest tightness and difficulty breathing (not because the air is bad but because the chest wall is resonating); a sense of presence — the feeling that something is in the room with you that you cannot see. All of these are documented effects of infrasound exposure in humans. All of them will be dramatically amplified by the context of being inside a shifting mineral labyrinth with no shadows, no echoes, and flat translucent creatures watching you from the walls.

### Preparation

**Know what it is.** The single most valuable preparation is cognitive: understand before you enter that the dread, the nausea, the feeling of being watched, the chest pressure, may be entirely mechanical effects of low-frequency vibration on your body. This will not make the feelings stop, but it will give you a framework for not panicking when they intensify. Panic in Keth is extraordinarily dangerous because panicked people move fast, move deep, and stop checking their guideline.

**Set a depth limit.** Before entering, decide how deep you will go, measured not in meters but in sensory intensity. When the subsonic presence becomes strong enough that you can feel it in your sternum — when it transitions from something you suspect to something you are certain of — that is your turnaround point. Do not push past it on your first visit. You do not know the intensity gradient. It may increase linearly or it may increase sharply, and the deeper you go, the more architecture there is between you and the exit, and the more of that architecture may have shifted since you entered.

**Ear protection is probably useless** against the frequencies involved — they are felt through the body, not primarily through the ears. Earplugs will, however, deepen the already oppressive silence and may make you more psychologically uncomfortable. Consider leaving them out.

**Anti-nausea medication** (meclizine or ondansetron) taken prophylactically before entering may mitigate some of the vestibular effects. It will not stop the dread. Nothing will stop the dread except leaving.

## The Creatures: Approach and Protocol

### The Hand-Creatures

They avoid you. This is probably the best possible behavior they could exhibit, and you should respect it by not pursuing them, cornering them, or attempting to collect one. You do not know what they are. You do not know whether they are individual organisms or motile components of Keth itself. If they are the latter — if they are, functionally, Keth's white blood cells — then you do not want to trigger whatever response Keth has to a foreign body that is harassing its immune system.

Observe them from a distance. Move slowly in their presence. Allow them to retreat. If you find yourself in a chamber with several of them and they do not flee — if they hold their positions or, worse, begin to move toward you — leave the chamber immediately. Their established behavior is avoidance; any deviation from that pattern is information you should take seriously.

### The Larger Aggregation

You may encounter the folded, layered mass described in the deep alcoves. Do not approach it. Do not shine your light directly on it. Observe it from the entrance of the chamber for no more than a minute, then move on. You have no information about what it is, what it does, or what triggers it to act. The description specifies it did not move while being watched, but the observer did not watch for long, and there is a reason the observer did not watch for long — an instinct spoke, and they listened. You should listen to the same instinct.

If it moves, do not run. Running in Keth means running through narrow corridors where you may not fit, where you cannot hear your own footsteps, where you will lose track of your guideline and your heading. Walk briskly. Walk toward the perimeter. Check your line.

## The Filaments: Avoid Prolonged Contact

The pale growths on the walls are cooler than the surrounding material. They sway without wind. They fuse with whatever they touch long enough. Do not let them touch your skin for extended periods. If you stand in an alcove examining them and a filament brushes your glove, that is one thing. If you lean against a wall to rest and filaments begin to drape over your shoulder, that is something else entirely. The process by which they fuse with the opposite wall may work on any surface, including yours. This is speculative, but the downside risk — having your clothing or skin begin to integrate with the mineral architecture of Keth — is severe enough that speculation warrants caution.

Do not rest against walls. Do not sit on the ground for extended periods. If you must stop, stand in the center of the widest corridor or plaza you can find, touching nothing.

## Duration and Timing

Keth has no day or night cycle that is described — the light is constant, sourceless. You cannot use ambient conditions to track time. Bring a watch. A mechanical one, if you are concerned about the environment affecting electronics (the mineral metabolism is entirely unknown in its mechanisms and may involve electromagnetic effects, though there is no specific evidence of this).

**First visit: four hours maximum.** This gives you time to enter, explore the outer corridors, observe the growth and reabsorption processes, encounter some filaments and possibly some hand-creatures, and return to the perimeter with a margin of safety for corridor changes. Two of those hours should be inward travel and observation; two should be reserved for return and contingency.

**Do not sleep in Keth.** The temptation may arise — you are tired, the subsonic presence has a paradoxically lulling quality, the alcoves seem sheltering. Do not yield to this. While you sleep, Keth builds. A chamber that is comfortable at the start of an eight-hour rest may be measurably smaller by the end of it. Walls may close over the corridor you used to enter. The filaments may reach you. The hand-creatures may come closer when your light is off and your body is still. Sleep on the flat, outside Keth, at a distance of at least a hundred meters from its outermost walls.

## Psychological Preparation

This is not optional. It is as important as the water.

Keth will make you feel things that are not emotions but that your brain will interpret as emotions because it has no other framework. The silence will register as something watching. The subsonic presence will register as dread. The slow closure of corridors will register as malice. The hand-creatures will register as wrong. The larger aggregation will register as a threat. None of these interpretations are necessarily correct, and none of them are necessarily incorrect. Keth is not a human place. It is not even, in any obvious sense, a living place — or rather, it is alive in the way a liver is alive, performing functions without awareness, metabolizing, growing, cycling. You are a foreign particle in this organ. The organ may encyst you. It will not do so on purpose.

Before you go, practice being afraid without acting on fear. Meditation helps. Experience in caving helps — the combination of tight spaces, silence, darkness, and the knowledge that the rock around you is not going anywhere is, in some ways, a gentler rehearsal for Keth, which offers tight spaces, silence, dimness, and the knowledge that the mineral around you is absolutely going somewhere, just slowly. If you are prone to claustrophobia, Keth will find it and amplify it. Address this before you go. If you cannot address it, do not go.

Practice stillness. In Keth, the impulse to move quickly, to push forward, to explore the next chamber, to see what is deeper, is the impulse most likely to endanger you. The place rewards slowness. Move at the pace of its own growth — deliberately, incrementally, with frequent pauses to check your line, check your markers, check the width of the corridor you are standing in compared to the width it was when you entered it.

## The Question You Will Ask Yourself

At some point during your visit — probably in the first hour, and certainly by the second — you will ask yourself whether Keth is aware of you.

The walls yield to your touch and then close. The corridors reshape around your presence. The creatures react to you from a distance. The subsonic pulse may or may not synchronize with your heartbeat. The filaments reach. Everything in Keth is in slow, silent motion, and you are the only fast thing, the only loud thing, the only warm and wet thing, and it will feel — with a certainty that you cannot argue yourself out of — like the entire structure is paying attention to you.

You will not be able to answer the question. Nobody who has visited Keth has been able to answer it. The question is, in some sense, the point of going — not to answer it, but to sit with it, to feel what it is like to be inside something that might be thinking about you in a language that has no words, no thoughts, no mind, and yet produces architecture.

Bring the question home with you. It is the only souvenir Keth will let you keep.

## Departure

When your time is up — when your watch says it is time, not when your curiosity says it is time — follow your guideline out. Do not linger. Do not take one last look at a chamber that was not there when you arrived. Walk steadily toward the flat. When you emerge from between the last two walls and your boots hit the open alkali surface and the diffuse grey light is no longer filtered through mineral corridors but falls on you from the entire featureless sky, stop. Turn around. Look at Keth from the outside.

It will look like nothing. A slight depression in the flat, some pale protrusions, something between a ruin and a growth. It will be impossible to believe that what you just experienced is contained in that small, quiet, chalky irregularity on the plain.

Drink water. Apply emollient to your hands and face. Check your skin for any pale residue that doesn't wipe away easily — and if you find some, if there is a patch on your arm or hand where the mineral seems to have begun to bond with your skin, clean it immediately and thoroughly with whatever mild acid you have brought (a dilute vinegar solution works; the alkaline mineral should react with it and loosen). This is probably unnecessary. Probably.

Walk away from Keth. Make camp at a distance. Rest. Let the silence drain out of your ears, which may take hours — the acoustic emptiness of Keth can linger like tinnitus in reverse, a ringing absence that makes normal sounds feel garish and too loud when you finally encounter them again.

And in the morning, or whatever passes for morning under that sourceless light, if you look back toward Keth, it will be slightly different than it was when you left it. A new spur, perhaps. A wall that wasn't there. A gap where a wall was.

It is still building. It was always building. It will build long after you leave, long after you forget the feel of the mineral yielding under your fingertip and slowly, silently closing.
