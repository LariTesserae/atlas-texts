# Preparing to Visit The Pocket Under the Water

---

## First: Understanding What You're Actually Proposing

Before any practical preparation, you need to sit with what this visit means. You are proposing to enter a space that is approximately three centimeters across on the outside and four centimeters across on the inside. It contains a single organism, a single quasi-crystalline structure, a medium that is no longer air, and a set of physical laws that are still in the process of deciding what they are. The space is sealed inside a root knot beneath a flood that has no surface, at a temperature of 4°C, in total darkness — or rather, in a darkness that is almost total but occasionally varies in density in a way that might constitute light if you're generous with your definitions.

You cannot visit this place the way you visit a city, a ruin, a forest, or even a dangerous wilderness. Those places exist at your scale. They have room for you. The Pocket Under the Water does not have room for you. It barely has room for itself. The description says "if you could shrink yourself small enough to enter" — and this is where most travelers make their first error. They read that conditional and start researching miniaturization, thinking this is a problem of scale. It isn't. Or rather, it is a problem of scale, but scale is not the primary obstacle. The primary obstacle is that the Pocket is a sovereign space with its own emerging physics, its own internal logic, and exactly one citizen, and you are proposing to become the second thing to exist there, which would fundamentally alter everything that the Pocket is and is becoming.

So the first preparation is philosophical. You must decide what kind of visit you intend to make.

---

## The Three Modes of Visitation

### Mode One: Observation From Outside

You remain at human scale, somewhere in the root mass, in the cold water, and you observe the Pocket through the root walls. This is the safest option and the one most travelers should choose. It is also the least satisfying, because the root knot is packed so densely that observing anything inside it requires either instruments (which the space "predates" in some fundamental sense, meaning they may not function reliably) or a kind of patient perceptual attunement that borders on meditation.

### Mode Two: Reduction and Entry

You shrink — through whatever means your multiverse travel permits — and physically enter the Pocket. This is what the description invites you to imagine. It is enormously dangerous, not because you will be harmed (the space is not hostile) but because you will become part of the Pocket's emerging system, subject to its soft physics, and you may find that leaving is not a simple reversal of entering.

### Mode Three: Correspondence

You don't enter the Pocket. The Pocket enters you. You allow the conditions of the space — its thick attention, its temporal elasticity, its negotiable geometry — to propagate into your own perceptual field. This is the mode most experienced multiverse travelers eventually settle on for locations like this, and it is the one I will discuss most extensively, but it requires the most internal preparation and carries its own subtle risks.

Choose your mode before you begin preparing. The preparations differ significantly.

## Universal Preparations (All Modes)

### Thermal Readiness

The water is 4°C. This is the temperature at which freshwater reaches maximum density — it is not arbitrary. At this temperature, water stops convecting. It settles. It becomes as still as water can be while remaining liquid. You will be immersed in it for an extended period regardless of your mode of visitation.

Four degrees Celsius is not immediately lethal to an unprotected human, but it is deeply uncomfortable and becomes dangerous within minutes. Standard cold-water immersion protocols apply: you will need either a dry suit with thermal underlayers or some form of metabolic adaptation. If your multiverse travel kit includes thermoregulatory implants or enchantments, now is the time to activate them. You want to maintain core temperature without generating excess heat, because excess heat would create convection currents in the water, and convection currents would propagate through the root mass, and any disturbance in the root mass's hydraulic equilibrium could alter the pressure inside the Pocket, potentially triggering the dimensional snap — the moment when interior and exterior dimensions briefly agree — at an unnatural frequency, which could disrupt whatever process is allowing the Pocket to develop its own physics.

In other words: stay warm, but stay still. Your warmth should be contained, not radiated.

**Specific gear recommendations:**
- A dry suit rated for prolonged immersion at near-freezing temperatures, with as little thermal signature as possible. Neoprene is acceptable but imperfect; the best option would be an insulating layer that achieves warmth through vacuum or gas-gap principles rather than through material thickness, since you'll need to navigate tight spaces in the root mass.
- No chemical heat packs. The chemical reactions can produce trace gases that dissolve into the water and alter its chemistry. The root system and the Pocket's calcium processes are sensitive to dissolved mineral changes.
- If you have access to metabolic dampening — the ability to reduce your body's heat output while maintaining consciousness — this is ideal. You want to approach the thermal equilibrium of the water without succumbing to hypothermia. Some travelers describe this as "becoming the same temperature as your attention," which is poetic but not unhelpful.

### Navigating the Root Mass

The root structure has replaced the soil. You are not swimming through water with roots in it; you are navigating a solid matrix of interwoven root tissue with water filling the interstices. At human scale, this is like trying to move through a space that is simultaneously a flooded cave system and a living organism's circulatory network.

The roots range from thick woody structures (the primary taproot and its major branches) to filaments so fine they are essentially biological thread. The weave is dense enough that in many places there is no gap large enough for a human body. You will need to find — or make — a path.

**Finding a path:** The root mass is not uniform. There will be voids where roots have died and been reabsorbed, or where two major roots run parallel and create a channel between them. These channels will be narrow, irregular, and may not lead where you want to go. Mapping them is impossible in advance because the root mass is alive and shifts — slowly, but meaningfully over the timescales of a visit. Bring no rigid mapping tools. Instead, develop a kinesthetic memory of the path as you travel it. Feel the roots. Learn their grain. The thicker, older roots tend to run vertically (or what passes for vertically in a place where "above" is unreliable); the finer roots fill in laterally. Moving along a major root's length, following its downward trajectory, will generally bring you deeper into the mass and closer to the knot where the Pocket resides.

**Making a path:** This is ethically and practically fraught. Cutting roots will injure the organism. The organism is not sentient in any way you'd recognize, but it is the Pocket's substrate, its housing, its parent — and injuring it could send biological signals through the root mass that trigger the pressure pulses described in the text. Those pulses are the Pocket's closest thing to a heartbeat. Inducing them artificially would be like forcing a heart to beat out of rhythm. Don't cut roots unless you have no alternative, and if you must, cut only the finest filaments, the ones that are already partially decayed, the ones that give when you press them. Use a blade that is absolutely clean — no metals that could leach into the water, no organic residues. Ceramic or obsidian, ideally.

**What you will feel:** The root mass is alive. Not in an abstract sense — tangibly, physically alive. The roots are slightly warmer than the 4°C water, by perhaps half a degree, due to metabolic activity. As you move through them, pressing against them, squeezing through gaps, you will feel this warmth against your suit or your skin. It is subtle but unmistakable after a few minutes. You will also feel the hydraulic pressure: the roots are turgid with water, and pressing against them is like pressing against a system of very firm, very dense biological hydraulics. They will resist you. Not intelligently, not defensively, but physically. You are displacing water and root tissue, and the system pushes back with the blind insistence of fluid dynamics.

Some travelers report that after extended time in the root mass — an hour or more — they begin to feel a low-frequency vibration. This is real. It is the tidal resonance mentioned in the description, transmitted through the water column and through the root tissue simultaneously. It is subsonic, below the threshold of hearing, but your body will feel it in your chest, your sinuses, your joints. It is not harmful. It is, in some ways, the closest thing this place has to weather.

### Orientation and Direction

"Above" is unreliable here. The description is explicit about this: the flood didn't rise so much as the world sank into "a condition of water," and the concept of surface has become unstable. You cannot rely on buoyancy to tell you which way is up, because at 4°C and at this pressure, the density gradients that normally create buoyancy are flattened to near-uniformity. You may find yourself uncertain whether you are ascending or descending. This is disorienting but not dangerous as long as you don't panic.

**Do not bring a compass.** Magnetic orientation is meaningless here. The root mass is not ferromagnetic, and whatever geological substrate remains beneath the roots may not behave magnetically in expected ways.

**Do not rely on gravity as an orienting force.** Gravity is present and functioning normally (probably), but in the water, at this density, surrounded by a root matrix that constrains your movement to channels that twist and turn, your vestibular system will lose its ability to track gravitational direction within minutes.

**What to use instead:** Pressure. If you have a sensitive barometric instrument — something mechanical, not electronic, since electronic instruments may behave unpredictably near the Pocket — you can determine relative depth by water pressure. Deeper means more pressure. This gives you one reliable axis. The other two axes you'll have to navigate by feel, by memory, and by following root grain.

There is also the tidal resonance. Some travelers report that the resonance has a directionality — it propagates from a consistent direction, presumably from the main body of the organism above (or in whatever direction "above" has wandered to). If you can feel it, you can use it as a rough compass. The resonance comes from the organism's center of mass, which is above the root knot, which means following the resonance "upstream" generally means moving upward and outward, while moving in the direction of its propagation generally means moving deeper and inward, toward the Pocket.

### Light

There is almost none. The description gives 0.2 on an unspecified scale, and even that may be a product of the Pocket's internal conditions rather than any actual photon emission. In the root mass outside the Pocket, there is no light at all. Total darkness. The kind of darkness that is not merely the absence of light but a positive condition, a medium you move through.

**Do not bring a bright light source.** If the Pocket does contain bioluminescent chemistry in the root sap — which is one of the possibilities the description raises — then flooding the root mass with artificial light would overwhelm it and potentially disrupt the Pocket's delicate luminance variations, which appear to be part of its emerging internal environment.

**What to bring:** A light source that can produce illumination so dim it is barely distinguishable from darkness. A single red LED at minimum power, filtered through multiple layers of neutral density material, so that what reaches your eyes is just enough to make out the largest root structures in your immediate vicinity. You are not trying to see clearly. You are trying to maintain just enough visual input that your brain doesn't begin generating its own imagery (which it will do in total darkness, and which in the vicinity of the Pocket could become... complicated, given the soft physics).

Some travelers prefer no light at all, navigating entirely by touch. This is viable and in some ways preferable — it forces you to engage with the root mass on its own terms, as a tactile environment. But it requires strong psychological preparation (see below).

### Time

Time in the Pocket passes at an uncertain rate and in an uncertain direction. Outside the Pocket, in the root mass and the flood water, time appears to function normally — but "appears" is doing heavy lifting in that sentence. The description notes that the Pocket was sealed "not long ago, whenever *not long ago* means at this scale," which implies temporal ambiguity extending beyond the Pocket's boundaries, at least conceptually.

**Bring a timepiece, but treat it as advisory rather than authoritative.** A mechanical watch is best — no electronics, no quartz oscillator, just springs and gears. Note the time when you enter the water. Note the time at regular intervals during your descent. If the intervals begin to feel inconsistent — if five minutes by the watch feels like twenty minutes experientially, or vice versa — note this without alarm. You are approaching the Pocket's sphere of temporal influence.

**Do not set a hard deadline for your visit.** "I'll leave after one hour" is a reasonable intention, but the hour may not cooperate. Instead, set a conditional departure trigger: "I'll leave when I begin to feel that my thoughts are slowing in a way that is no longer comfortable." This is subjective, yes, but subjectivity is the only reliable instrument you have here.

**Be prepared for time to do unexpected things near the Pocket.** The description says the creature on the Pocket's wall "hasn't moved in what might be weeks, or might be a single long afternoon that has forgotten how to end." If you enter the Pocket itself (Mode Two), you may find yourself in a similar temporal condition — not frozen, not slowed, but *stretched*, as though each moment has more interior space than it should. This can feel euphoric or terrifying depending on your temperament. Know your temperament.

## Preparations Specific to Mode One: Observation From Outside

If you choose to observe the Pocket from outside the root knot, your primary challenge is perception. The Pocket is three centimeters across on the outside, sealed inside a dense tangle of root tissue, filled with a medium that is no longer air, and governed by physics that are still setting. You cannot see into it. You cannot hear into it in any meaningful sense. You can, with the right approach, *feel* it.

### Developing Tactile Sensitivity

Spend at least two weeks before your visit practicing fine tactile discrimination. Work with your hands in cold water — not ice water, but water around 4-6°C. Handle small objects. Learn to distinguish textures, temperatures, and densities through numbed fingers. Your fingertips will be your primary sensory instruments in the root mass.

When you reach the root knot, you will be placing your hands on the exterior of the tangle that contains the Pocket. Through the root walls, you may be able to feel:

- **The pressure discrepancy.** The Pocket is larger inside than outside. This means the root walls are under a strange kind of stress — compressed on the outside by the root knot, but pushed outward from the inside by the extra centimeter of space. If your tactile sensitivity is good enough, you may detect a subtle *wrongness* in the way the root walls resist your touch — a yielding where there should be resistance, or a resistance where the root grain suggests softness.
- **The creature's cilia.** Every few minutes, the creature waves its cilia. This displaces a tiny amount of the medium, which transmits a microscopic pressure wave through the root wall. You will not feel this directly. But if you hold your fingers very still against the root surface, in the quietest possible state of attention, you may feel a rhythmic *something* — not a vibration, not a pulse, something finer than either — that corresponds to the creature's slow tasting of its world.
- **The tidal pulse.** When the organism sends its periodic biological signal through the root mass, the Pocket's dimensions briefly normalize. This is the most detectable event from outside: a momentary tightening of the root knot, as though the tangle clenches, followed by a subtle relaxation. If you're touching the knot when this happens, you'll feel it. It will feel like the root is breathing. One breath, then stillness again for days.

### What You Will Not Learn

From outside, you will not learn what the medium inside the Pocket has become. You will not learn what the creature looks like or what it is. You will not understand the calcium lattice or its negotiable geometry. You will not experience the thick attention, the varying darkness, or the temporal elasticity. You will know that the Pocket exists. You will feel it the way you feel a heartbeat through someone else's chest — proof of interiority, without access to what that interiority contains.

For many travelers, this is enough. For some, it is everything. There is a profound practice in placing your hands on the outside of a mystery and simply acknowledging that it is there, that it is real, that it is becoming something, and that you are not required to enter it to honor it.

## Preparations Specific to Mode Two: Reduction and Entry

This is the dangerous option. Not dangerous in the sense of physical harm — the Pocket is small, quiet, and unaggressive — but dangerous in the sense of *ontological compromise*. You are entering a space whose physics are still curing. By entering it, you become part of the system. The soft physics will apply to you. Your dimensions may become negotiable. Your time may become elastic. Your thoughts may slow. And because the Pocket's emerging rules are partly shaped by the mutual attention between the Pocket and its occupant, your attention — the attention of a human mind, with all its freight and complexity — will become a factor in how those rules develop.

You could change the Pocket by entering it. You could change yourself by entering it.

### The Ethics of Entry

Consider the creature. It is the Pocket's only citizen. It has lived there — for weeks, or for an afternoon, or for a period of time that doesn't map onto any external calendar — in solitude, tasting the medium, feeding on root sugars, existing in a state of mutual awareness with its environment. It may be the only one of itself. It has no reproductive organs. It is, as far as can be determined, unique.

Your entry into the Pocket would be, from the creature's perspective, the most significant event in the history of its universe. You would be the second thing. The first other. The Pocket is "the smallest country" with "one citizen, one monument, one climate, and no map." You are proposing to double its population. You are proposing to become the first visitor to a nation that has no concept of visitation.

There is no ethical framework that can tell you whether this is acceptable. The creature is not sentient in a way that would allow it to consent or object. The Pocket is not a political entity that can grant or deny entry. But the *mutual awareness* between creature and Pocket suggests a relationship that is more than mechanical, even if it is less than conscious. You would be intruding on an intimacy.

**My recommendation:** If you enter, enter with the intention of being as small as possible — not just physically, but attentionally. Quiet your mind. Reduce your cognitive output. Try to become, as much as a human can, a simple presence rather than an interpreting intelligence. The Pocket doesn't need your analysis. It doesn't need your wonder. It needs you to be still.

### Miniaturization

The specific method of shrinking yourself to a scale where you can enter a three-centimeter (or four-centimeter, depending on which measurement you trust) cavity will depend on the technologies or abilities available to you as a multiverse traveler. I will not detail specific miniaturization techniques here, as they vary wildly across reality branches. What I will say:

- **You need to reach a scale of approximately 1-2 centimeters in body length** to fit comfortably inside the Pocket. Smaller is better. At 1 centimeter, you would be roughly five times the size of the creature, which is large enough to be disruptive but small enough that you're not filling the entire space.
- **Your suit or protective gear must scale with you.** If you're relying on thermal protection, it needs to work at reduced scale. Some miniaturization methods don't preserve thermal regulation — check this before you shrink. At 1-2 centimeters, hypothermia at 4°C would be rapid without protection, because your surface-area-to-volume ratio has increased dramatically.
- **Your senses will be different at this scale.** Vision, if there were any light, would be sharper for near-field objects but more affected by diffraction. Touch would be proportionally magnified — you'd feel the texture of individual root cells. Hearing would shift upward in frequency sensitivity. The subsonic tidal resonance might become audible. The ticking of the root walls shifting under hydraulic pressure would become loud, almost percussive.
- **You must be able to reverse the miniaturization remotely or on a timer.** Do not rely on your own judgment for when to return to full size. As noted above, time may become unreliable near and inside the Pocket, and your thoughts may slow. Set an external trigger — a device, a companion waiting at full scale, a time-delayed mechanism — that will restore you to your original size after a predetermined period. If you find yourself thinking "I'll stay just a little longer," that is the soft physics talking, and it is time to go.

### Entering the Pocket

The Pocket is sealed. The root knot that encloses it is a dense tangle of living and semi-calcified wood. At miniature scale, the gaps between root fibers that seem microscopic from human scale become navigable passages — tight, winding, but passable. You will be squeezing through a labyrinth of living tissue, in cold water, in darkness, following the pressure discrepancy inward.

You will know you are approaching the Pocket when the water changes. Not in temperature or chemistry — in *quality*. The water near the Pocket has been in prolonged contact with the root walls that are participating in the soft physics, and it has absorbed something of that quality. It will feel denser, or stiller, or more *present*. Your hands, pushing through it, will meet a resistance that is not physical but attentional, as though the water is paying attention to you.

The boundary between the water-filled root interstices and the Pocket's interior medium is not sharp. The medium inside is not-air — something that was once air and has become something else through prolonged enclosure in the evolving physics of the Pocket. As you push through the last layer of root fiber and enter the cavity, you will transition from water to this medium. It will feel like surfacing, except you will not break a surface — you will simply find that the stuff around you has changed from water to something lighter, thicker, more charged with a quality you don't have a name for.

Breathe, if you can breathe it. The medium is not toxic — it is not anything recognizable enough to be toxic. It is simply *the medium of this place*, and your lungs, if they work at this scale, will process it the way they process whatever the local atmosphere is. If you can't breathe it, you'll need a sealed breathing supply, which adds bulk and reduces your ability to minimize your presence.

### Inside the Pocket

You are inside.

The space is dark, but not perfectly dark. The darkness varies, thickening and thinning in patterns that almost repeat. This is the Pocket's light. Let your eyes adjust. After several minutes, you may begin to perceive the variations — not as light and shadow, but as degrees of visual presence, as though the darkness itself has texture.

The creature is on the wall. It is two to three millimeters long — at your reduced scale, it would be proportionally significant, perhaps a quarter of your body length or more. It has cilia, or cilia-like structures. They wave slowly. Do not touch the creature. Do not approach it closely. Find a spot on the wall opposite the creature, or as far from it as the small space allows, and settle there.

The calcium lattice is nearby. It is beautiful, or it is trying to be beautiful, or it occupies the space where beauty and geometry negotiate with each other. Its sides change number when you count them. This is not a visual illusion — it is the soft physics in action. Do not try to count definitively. Accept the multiplicity.

Your thoughts will slow. This is not harmful. It is the medium. There is so little happening that cognition has nothing to push against, and it decelerates like a wheel losing momentum. Let it slow. Do not fight it. Your thoughts do not need to be fast here. They need to be *present*, in the same way the creature is present, in the same way the lattice is present.

**What you will experience:**

The thick attention. This is the Pocket's most distinctive quality. You will feel observed, not by the creature (which is aware of you in a way that doesn't involve looking) and not by any intelligence, but by the *space itself*. The pocket and its contents exist in a state of mutual attention — everything in the space is aware of everything else, not cognitively but *physically*, the way two objects in a small room affect each other's gravitational fields. You are now part of this field. The Pocket is aware of you. The creature is aware of you. The lattice is probably aware of you, in whatever way a calcium crystal negotiating its own geometry can be aware.

This can feel unbearable. Not because it's painful — because it's intimate. You are being known by a space, and the knowing is absolute because the space is so small that there is nowhere to hide. Every thought you have, every shift in your body's position, every chemical exchange in your metabolism is a disturbance in the medium, and the medium is paying attention, and the creature's cilia are tasting the disturbance you make.

This is why I said to quiet your mind. A busy mind, in this space, is like a shouting person in a library — not harmful, but overwhelming, displacing the silence that the space has been cultivating. The silence between sounds is a terrain here, and you can get lost in it, and getting lost in it is one of the most profound experiences available in this location, but only if you're quiet enough to enter it.

**The dimensional discrepancy:** You will notice that the Pocket feels larger than it should. This is the extra centimeter. At your scale, an extra centimeter of interior space is significant — it's the difference between a cramped closet and a small room. The walls are thicker from inside. The root wood occupies two volumes simultaneously. If you press your hand against the wall, you will feel this as a kind of depth that shouldn't be there — your hand pushes into the wood and the wood goes deeper than it should, or your hand meets the resistance you'd expect at the wrong point, slightly too far in or slightly too near. The root is dreaming, and you are inside the dream.

**The temporal elasticity:** At some point, you will lose track of how long you've been inside. This is inevitable. The moments here have more interior space, like the Pocket itself. Each second contains more experience than a second should. Not more event — less is happening here than in almost any other location in the multiverse. But more *presence*. Each moment is so fully itself, so saturated with the thick attention, that it takes longer to pass through. Or it takes the same amount of time but feels longer. Or time is genuinely moving more slowly. From inside, you cannot distinguish between these possibilities, and the distinction may not exist.

This is why you need an external extraction mechanism. You will not want to leave. Not because the Pocket is pleasant (it is beyond pleasant and unpleasant, in a category that doesn't intersect with comfort) but because leaving requires *deciding to leave*, and decisions require the kind of cognitive momentum that the medium has gently bled away.

### What You Might Change by Being There

I won't pretend this is a zero-impact visit. You are a human mind inside a space the size of a heartbeat, and your presence will alter the system. The creature will taste you in the medium — your chemistry, your exhalations, the faint electrical field of your nervous system. The Pocket's soft physics will incorporate you — your dimensions, your mass, your attention — into its ongoing negotiation with itself. The calcium lattice may respond to your presence by adding or removing molecules at a different rate, or by temporarily stabilizing its geometry (six sides, definitely six, while you're watching) or becoming more uncertain (was it four? three? am I still looking at it?).

You may leave a permanent mark. A trace in the medium. A change in the creature's behavior — it might move, for the first time in weeks, orienting toward or away from the memory of your presence. You might nudge the Pocket's emerging physics in one direction or another — more stable, less stable, more like outside physics, more like something else entirely.

This is the weight of the visit. This is what you're signing up for.

## Preparations Specific to Mode Three: Correspondence

This is the mode I recommend for most travelers. It does not require miniaturization. It does not require entering the root mass at all, though proximity helps. What it requires is a willingness to let the Pocket's conditions propagate into your perceptual field — to *correspond* with the Pocket, in the old sense of the word: to be in a state of mutual relation.

### How It Works

The Pocket's soft physics are young and still establishing themselves. They are largely contained within the root knot, but they are not perfectly contained. The root walls are permeable — water seeps through them — and the soft physics appear to propagate through the same interstices, faintly, into the surrounding root mass and water. The further you are from the Pocket, the weaker the effect, until at some distance (perhaps a meter, perhaps ten meters, depending on factors that are not well understood) the effect is indistinguishable from ordinary physics.

But within that range, there is a gradient. The water is slightly stranger close to the Pocket. The roots are slightly more prone to spatial ambiguity. The silence between sounds is slightly deeper.

If you position yourself in the root mass, within perhaps half a meter of the Pocket, and you enter a state of extreme stillness and receptivity — not meditation exactly, but something adjacent, a deliberate quieting of your interpretive faculties — you can begin to feel the Pocket's conditions as though they were your own.

Your thoughts will slow. Not because you're inside the medium, but because you're allowing the medium's quality to resonate with your own cognitive rhythms. The thick attention will become perceptible — not as an external observation but as an internal state, as though your own awareness has taken on a density it doesn't usually have. The varying darkness will register not in your eyes but in your sense of interiority, as fluctuations in the depth of your own inner silence.

This is correspondence. You and the Pocket are not in the same space, but you are in the same *state*. You are rhyming with it. And through this rhyme, you can learn things about the Pocket that direct observation could never reveal: what the thick attention feels like from the inside, what it's like to exist in a space where geometry is negotiable, what the creature's slow cilia-waving means in the language of presence rather than biology.

### Preparing for Correspondence

**Practice silence.** Not just quiet — silence. Spend time in environments with minimal sensory input. A dark room. A flotation tank. A still body of water. Learn what your mind does when there is nothing to process. It will generate imagery, narratives, anxieties, memories. Let these arise and pass without engaging them. What you're training is the capacity to exist without interpreting — to be present without making meaning. The Pocket is not a text to be read. It is a condition to be shared.

**Practice attention without object.** This is harder than it sounds. Normally, attention is directed *at* something. In the Pocket's vicinity, attention becomes ambient — it fills the space rather than pointing at a target. Practice allowing your attention to become diffuse, to spread evenly through your perceptual field, to rest on everything equally and nothing specifically. This is the mode of attention that corresponds to the Pocket's thick attention.

**Accept geometric uncertainty.** Before your visit, spend time with shapes that resist counting. Look at fractal patterns, at aperiodic tilings, at objects whose properties change depending on how you measure them. The calcium lattice inside the Pocket has a variable number of sides. This is not a puzzle to be solved; it is a condition to be accepted. If you arrive at the Pocket with a strong need for geometric certainty, the correspondence will be distressing rather than illuminating.

**Accept temporal uncertainty.** Practice experiencing time as variable. Note how time speeds up when you're absorbed and slows when you're bored. Consider that these variations might not be subjective distortions of an objective time, but genuine fluctuations in the temporal medium. The Pocket's time is elastic in a way that your time usually isn't, but the difference is of degree, not of kind. Your time has always been a little bit soft. The Pocket's is just softer.

### The Correspondence Experience

You are in the root mass, in the cold water, in the dark. You have positioned yourself as close to the Pocket as you can manage without disturbing the root knot. You are still. You are quiet. You have dimmed your light or extinguished it. You are breathing slowly.

At first, nothing happens. The water is cold. The roots press against you. The darkness is total or nearly so. You hear the subsonic tidal resonance in your bones.

Then — it may take minutes or hours — you begin to feel a quality entering your awareness from outside. It is not a sensation exactly. It is more like a change in the *resolution* of your experience. Things become more present. The pressure of the water against your suit becomes more specific, more articulated, as though each square centimeter of contact is being individually attended to. The darkness becomes deeper — not darker, but *deeper*, as though it has acquired a third dimension it didn't have before.

Your thoughts slow. This is the medium reaching you through the root walls and the water. You are not inside the Pocket, but the Pocket's conditions are rhyming with your conditions, and the resonance is slowing your cognitive oscillation to something closer to the Pocket's own rhythm.

The silence between your thoughts becomes wider. You can explore it. You can turn your attention to the gap between one thought and the next and find that it is not empty but *full* — full of the thick attention, full of the same quality that fills the Pocket, that quality of *being attended to by a space*.

In this state, you may become aware of the creature. Not visually — you can't see through the root walls. But as a presence, a tiny knot of consumptive existence, a mouth and a gut and nothing else, clinging to the wall of a world that is barely three centimeters across and tasting the geometry of a space that is learning to be its own country. You will feel it the way you feel another person in a dark room — not through any specific sense, but through the perturbation they create in the field of mutual attention.

You may become aware of the lattice. Its geometry will flicker at the edge of your mathematical intuition — hexagonal, heptagonal, pentagonal, none of these, all of these. You will not be able to resolve it, and that irresolution will feel, somehow, correct. The lattice is not confused. It is *multiple*. It contains all of its possible geometries simultaneously, and the soft physics of the Pocket permit this multiplicity in a way that harder physics would not.

You may experience the dimensional discrepancy directly — a sudden, disorienting sense that the space around you is larger than it should be, that the root walls have receded, that the water has expanded. This is the Pocket's soft geometry leaking through the correspondence. You are not actually in a larger space. Your space has not changed. But your perception of space has become *negotiable*, temporarily, and the negotiation feels like expansion.

**When to stop:** Correspondence is not addictive in the way that direct exposure to the Pocket would be, but it is seductive. The thick attention feels profound. The slowed time feels like a gift. The varying darkness feels like a language you're almost learning. There is a temptation to stay in the correspondence state indefinitely, to sink deeper into the Pocket's conditions, to become more and more attuned until the boundary between your experience and the Pocket's experience dissolves entirely.

Do not let it dissolve entirely. You are not the Pocket. You are a visitor. The boundary between you is important — it is what allows you to return. If you feel the boundary becoming too thin — if you begin to lose track of which thoughts are yours and which are the Pocket's — pull back. Move. Flex your hands. Generate heat. Remind your body that it is a body, with its own dimensions, its own physics, its own time.

The Pocket will not hold you. It has no intention, no desire, no possessiveness. But it is becoming more itself with every moment, and if you are inside the correspondence when it becomes *too much* itself, you may find that the rules it has been developing apply to you as well — and you may discover that you have become part of its quiet, dark, cold, infinitely attentive world, and that leaving is not so much impossible as *inapplicable*, a concept that doesn't translate into the Pocket's emerging language.

## Psychological Preparation

Regardless of which mode you choose, you need to prepare yourself psychologically for several conditions that are unusual even by multiverse travel standards:

### Smallness

The Pocket is tiny. Even the flood, vast as it is, is characterized by its *density* rather than its expanse — the water presses in, settles, tries to become more solid. Everything here is about compression, enclosure, intimacy. If you have any claustrophobic tendencies, they will be activated, and they will be activated not by the physical tightness of the root mass (though that too) but by the *conceptual* tightness of the Pocket — a world so small it has room for only one of everything.

Sit with this before you go. Spend time in small spaces. Not to desensitize yourself — desensitization implies there's something to be afraid of, and there isn't — but to develop a positive relationship with enclosure. Learn to experience smallness as intimacy rather than confinement. The Pocket is not a prison. It is a nursery. A very, very small nursery for a very, very new set of physical laws.

### Solitude

The creature is alone. It may be the only one of itself. It has no companion, no predator, no competitor, no mate. It exists in a universe of one, plus a crystal, plus a darkness, plus a set of rules. If you enter the Pocket (Mode Two), you will be the second, but this is an intrusion, not companionship. If you observe from outside (Mode One) or correspond (Mode Three), you will feel the creature's solitude as a quality of the space — not loneliness, which implies a desire for company, but *soleness*, which is a simpler, more absolute condition.

This can be difficult for social beings. We are accustomed to environments that contain others of our kind, or at least others. The Pocket contains an Other so radical — so unlike you, so small, so featureless, so purely consumptive — that it barely registers as a being at all, and yet it is the most important being in its world, because it is the only being in its world.

Some travelers report feeling, in the Pocket's vicinity, a deep and sourceless grief. This is not the Pocket's emotion — the Pocket has no emotions. It is the traveler's response to encountering a solitude so complete and so untroubled that it reveals, by contrast, how much of the traveler's own existence is organized around the avoidance of aloneness. The grief is yours. Sit with it. It will pass, or it will teach you something, or both.

### The Incompleteness of the Physics

The hardest thing about the Pocket is not the cold, or the dark, or the solitude, or the smallness. It is the *incompleteness*. The physics here are still being written. The geometry hasn't decided how many sides it has. The time hasn't settled on a direction. The medium inside the Pocket hasn't finished becoming whatever it's becoming. Everything is in process, and none of the processes are complete, and there is no indication of when or whether they will complete.

This is profoundly uncomfortable for minds that are accustomed to finished things, to settled physics, to a reality that has already decided what it is. The Pocket is a reality that is still deciding. It is in the gap between snowflake and water droplet, between crystal and solution, between law and suggestion. You will want it to resolve. You will want the lattice to pick a number of sides. You will want the darkness to either be light or not. You will want the creature to either be alive or be something else. The Pocket will not give you these resolutions, because it doesn't have them yet.

Practice tolerance for incompleteness. Leave a puzzle unsolved on your desk for a week. Start a sentence and don't finish it. Listen to a piece of music and stop it before the final chord. These are small exercises, trivially easy compared to the Pocket's deep ontological irresolution, but they point in the right direction.

## After the Visit

You will return to full-scale reality. The water will be cold. The root mass will be dense. You will swim or climb or navigate your way back to whatever constitutes "outside" in a world where the concept of surface has become unreliable.

You will feel strange. This is normal.

The strangeness will manifest as a persistent sense that spaces are slightly larger than they should be — that the distance between you and the nearest wall is a centimeter more than your eyes report, that rooms contain more air than their dimensions should allow. This is the Pocket's soft geometry lingering in your perception. It will fade over days, or weeks, or it will become a permanent feature of your experience — a slight, perpetual awareness that the relationship between interior and exterior is not as fixed as you once believed.

You may find yourself pausing between thoughts, attending to the silence there, finding it deeper and more textured than you remembered. This is the correspondence lingering. The thick attention does not fully release when you leave its proximity. A trace of it remains in your cognitive rhythm, a slight deceleration at the boundary between one thought and the next, a quality of presence in the gap that you never noticed before because you never had a reason to look.

You may find yourself thinking about the creature. You will wonder if it's still there, on the wall, tasting the medium. You will wonder if the lattice has settled on a number of sides. You will wonder if the Pocket's physics have finished curing, or if they're still in process, still incomplete, still becoming.

They are still becoming. They will be still becoming for a long time. The Pocket has nowhere else to be.

Neither, in the end, did you, while you were there. That's what you'll carry with you: the memory of having been somewhere so small and so complete that the question of *where else you could be* simply didn't arise, because there was no elsewhere, because the Pocket was the entire world, and the world was three centimeters wide, and it was enough.

## A Final Note

Some travelers, upon reading the description, feel an immediate and powerful urge to protect the Pocket — to seal off the root mass, to post warnings, to ensure that no one else visits. This urge is understandable and misguided. The Pocket does not need your protection. It is sealed inside a root knot inside a root mass inside a flood that has no surface, at 4°C, in total darkness. It is already the most inaccessible place you will ever visit. The only thing that could disturb it is the same thing that created it: the slow, patient, directionless growth of the root organism, which might, in time, tighten the knot further and compress the Pocket out of existence, or loosen it and allow the medium to disperse, or do something else entirely, something the Pocket's emerging physics haven't accounted for yet, because they haven't finished becoming.

The Pocket will live or die on its own terms, in its own time, in its own direction — whichever direction that turns out to be. Your role is not to save it. Your role is to visit, to be quiet, to be still, to be briefly part of its thick attention, and then to leave, carrying with you the knowledge that somewhere beneath the flood, in a space no larger than a heartbeat, a set of physical laws is being born, and a creature is tasting them, and a crystal is trying to decide how many sides it has, and a darkness is varying, and none of it needs you, and all of it is real.

Go carefully. Go quietly. Go small.
