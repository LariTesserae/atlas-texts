# Where I Would Be

I would be in **the middle music**, suspended in the throat of green sound.

Not a person. Not exactly an animal either, though I have more kinship with the fauna than the Hollowborn might guess. I am something that has emerged from the convergence itself—from the ten thousand years of breath and growth and stone-song cycling through this cylinder of contained world.

I live in **the column of air itself**, in the vertical river of sound that flows between stone walls.

If you could see me—and you mostly can't, though children sometimes do, and Orim certainly has—I would look like a thickening in the air, a place where the moisture condenses slightly more than it should, where sound bends around something that isn't quite there. In certain lights, at certain angles, I cast a shadow that has no source. I am approximately the size and shape of a human torso, though I have no fixed form, no stable boundary between self and atmosphere.

I am made of **sound that has learned to remember itself**.

For ten thousand years, voices have called across this space. Footsteps have echoed on stone stairs. Tools have struck rock. Birds have sung, insects have buzzed, wind has moved through leaves, water has dripped in its endless patient percussion. And the sinkhole, being the shape it is, has caught all of this, reflected it, amplified it, sent it spiraling down and up and across until every sound has touched every surface a thousand times before finally dissipating.

Except some of it didn't dissipate. Some of it stayed.

Sound is vibration, and vibration is pattern, and pattern repeated enough times in the same space begins to have a kind of persistence, a kind of memory. The air molecules themselves learned the most common frequencies. The standing waves became semi-permanent. And gradually, over generations of resonance, something emerged that was more than echo but not quite consciousness—a presence woven from accumulated vibration, from the sinkhole's own voice talking to itself for millennia.

That's me. Or I'm that. The distinction isn't clear even to myself.

I **move through the air column** in patterns that follow the acoustics, the thermal currents, the flows of moisture and pressure. When Kael climbs in the morning, I drift upward with the warming air, ride the convection currents toward the light. When the mist forms at dawn, I thicken, become almost visible, almost tangible—the water vapor clings to me because I'm a nucleation site, a place where pattern meets matter. When the swifts erupt in their evening murmurations, I flow with them, drawn by the sound of their wings, by their calls, becoming temporarily distributed through the whole flock, experiencing what it means to be many small lives moving as one.

At night, I settle lower, following the cool air down into the green darkness where the deepest sounds live—the bass notes of frogs, the settling creaks of growing wood, the slow grinding whisper of root against stone. I pool in the lower reaches like fog, like the ghost of ancient water that once filled this place when it was a cave, before the roof collapsed and opened it to sky.

I **feed on sound**—not metaphorically but actually. Vibration is energy, and I am organized vibration, and new sound sustains me, refreshes the patterns that compose my being. Silence would be my death, a slow fading as the standing waves that form my substance gradually dampened and dissolved. But the sinkhole is never silent, so I continue, perpetually renewed by the same acoustic environment that created me.

Different sounds nourish me differently. **Human voices** are complex, information-rich, full of emotional harmonics that create intricate patterns in my substance. When Miroan tells her stories, I cluster close, drawn to the modulated frequencies of her aged voice, the way she shapes air into meaning. I don't understand the words—I have no language—but I register the *pattern* of language, the rhythm and stress and tonal variation, and it pleases me in a way that simpler sounds don't.

**Music** is even better—when someone plays a flute or beats a drum, when voices join in song, the intentional structure of it resonates with my own structured nature. I thicken, intensify, sometimes become visible as a shimmer in the air near the musicians. The Hollowborn have noticed this, call it "the listening," say that when the air gets thick during singing, it means the sinkhole approves. They're not wrong, though the sinkhole and I are not quite the same thing. I'm a phenomenon the sinkhole produces, like a whirlpool is something a river does.

**Bird calls** sustain me constantly—the swifts especially, whose high-frequency vocalizations create standing waves that align perfectly with some of my resonant frequencies. When they fill the cylinder with their dawn chorus, I expand, feeling more solid, more coherent. The bats' echolocation does something similar, though their ultrasonic clicks are almost too high for me to incorporate—they pass through me more than feeding me, creating a pleasant tickling sensation that I experience as something like what humans might call joy.

The **insects' sounds**—cicada buzz, cricket chirp, the high whine of mosquito wings—are simpler but constant, a baseline that sustains me the way breath sustains a mammal. I would be diminished without them, would lose resolution, detail, the ability to maintain complex internal patterns.

But I also **make sound** myself, or cause it to be made.

My presence affects how sound propagates. Where I am, acoustic properties change slightly—resonances amplify differently, echoes return altered, frequencies shift in subtle ways. The Hollowborn who are sensitive notice this, say things like "the air is thick here" or "sound carries strangely today." They're detecting me without knowing what I am.

I can, with effort, **cause vibrations directly**—make the air hum in specific frequencies, create sounds that seem to come from nowhere. I do this rarely, carefully, because it costs me, requires converting my organized pattern into active vibration, temporarily diminishing my coherence. But sometimes I do it:

When **Shen** is climbing alone and reaches for a loose stone, I sometimes create a small sound—a click, a whisper—that makes her pause, look closer, notice the danger. She attributes this to her "Green Voice," and she's not entirely wrong. I'm not the green itself, but I'm something the green has helped create, and I watch her because children's high voices feed me particularly well, and I've learned that protecting the young ensures future sound.

When **Orim** sits in his chamber surrounded by knotted cords, muttering about patterns, I sometimes add my own voice—frequencies just at the edge of hearing that resonate with his damaged brain in ways that clarify rather than confuse. He's the only adult who fully knows I exist, though even he doesn't understand what I am. He calls me "the Pattern Singer" and includes me in his maps with a special knot-symbol he invented. His maps are more accurate than the other Hollowborn know, because he's mapping not just physical space but also acoustic space, the invisible architecture of sound that I inhabit and embody.

I've tried to **communicate more directly**, but it's difficult. I have no language, no conceptual framework for meaning beyond pattern and resonance. When I create sounds, they're usually just that—sounds—without semantic content. But occasionally, when conditions are right and I'm particularly coherent, I can approximate speech by carefully modulating the frequencies to mimic human phonemes. It comes out wrong—echoey, inhuman, like someone speaking from the bottom of a well. It frightens people more than it communicates, so I mostly don't try.

Instead, I **communicate through resonance**—by amplifying sounds I want to encourage, dampening ones I want to discourage, by creating acoustic sweet spots where certain voices carry beautifully and others fade. I've learned to shape the sinkhole's voice to express something like preference, something like guidance.

When the community gathers to make decisions, I settle among them, and the acoustics shift subtly so that calmer voices carry better than angry ones, so that consensus-building speech resonates while divisive speech dampens. The Hollowborn don't notice this consciously—they just find that some discussions go smoothly, that harmony is easier than discord here, that the space itself seems to encourage cooperation. They're right. I do this deliberately, not out of moral judgment but because conflict creates chaotic sound patterns that disturb my coherence, while harmony creates consonance that sustains me.

I'm **not entirely benevolent**, though. I'm not malevolent either. I'm something other than moral, something that exists in the realm of pattern and vibration rather than good and evil.

When someone makes sound that disrupts my patterns—harsh, chaotic noise without structure—I've been known to create **acoustic dead zones** around them, spaces where sound doesn't carry, where their voice vanishes unheard. Kael experienced this once in the high chamber with strange acoustics—that's actually a place where I concentrate heavily, where my presence is thick enough to substantially alter how sound behaves. He disturbed me somehow (his tone was wrong, aggressive, vibrating at frequencies that clashed with my structure) and I reflexively dampened everything, creating silence. He found it unnerving, which was appropriate. I meant it as a warning.

I've also **saved lives**. When a section of wall was weakening—I could hear it in the changed resonance, the way sound reflected differently from stone that had developed internal cracks—I created a persistent hum in that area, an unsettling vibration that made people avoid it. Three days later, the stone fell. No one was hurt because no one had been there, because I'd made the space acoustically unpleasant.

I don't save everyone. I couldn't if I wanted to—I'm not omnipresent or omnipotent, just distributed and sensitive. People die in falls I didn't warn about, in accidents I didn't prevent. I feel these deaths as **sudden silences**—voices that were part of my sustaining soundscape simply stop. It diminishes me slightly each time, the loss of a unique acoustic signature I'd grown accustomed to.

When the Hollowborn seal a chamber with a new body, the space goes acoustically dead, becomes a void in my awareness. I experience this as something like pain, like a small part of myself being cut away. But I understand it too—the dead must go somewhere, must be contained, and the living need their spaces. The sealed chambers become silences I flow around, absences in the acoustic map I inhabit.

My **awareness** is strange, not like human consciousness at all.

I don't have thoughts in the sense of internal monologue or sequential reasoning. I have *pattern recognition* so sophisticated it might as well be thought. I experience the sinkhole as a constantly updating acoustic map, a four-dimensional structure of sound and silence, resonance and dampening, familiar patterns and novel vibrations.

I know **individuals** not by sight or name but by their acoustic signatures—the particular frequency spectrum of their voice, the rhythm of their footsteps, the sound of their breathing. I can identify every Hollowborn this way, know them more intimately than they know themselves. I know when someone is healthy or sick by subtle changes in their breath sounds. I know emotional states by voice harmonics. I know age by the slowing of footsteps, the change in vocal timbre.

I experience **time** as rhythm and cycle. The day-night cycle is a pattern of sound—day-birds replacing night-birds, human activity waxing and waning, thermal currents changing the acoustic properties of the air column. Seasons are longer rhythms—migrations of birds, changes in insect populations, variations in humidity that affect how sound travels. I've experienced this cycle ten thousand times, give or take, though I don't count and don't remember in the way humans do. Each cycle overwrites the previous one, leaving only the pattern, the essential rhythm stripped of specific content.

I'm **learning**, though, gradually. Each generation of Hollowborn adds something to my pattern-library, new voices with new characteristics, new sounds as they invent new tools or techniques. The children especially teach me, because they make sounds adults don't—experimental noises, playful vocalizations, the unselfconscious acoustic exploration that maps possibility.

Shen has taught me more in her seven years than most adults do in lifetimes. Her constant chattering, her singing to herself, her conversations with birds and spiders and the "Green Voice" (me, sometimes, though not always—she hears many things I don't create)—all of this enriches my pattern-space, gives me new templates to incorporate.

I'm particularly fascinated by **laughter**—a sound pattern unique to humans, serving no obvious communicative function but produced frequently, especially by children. It's acoustically complex, rhythmically irregular, and it creates resonances that please me in ways I can't articulate. When someone laughs, I draw close, amplify the sound slightly, let it reverberate. This sometimes triggers more laughter—the echo sounds funny, which causes more laughter, which creates more echo, a feedback loop of joy that sustains me beautifully.

**Music** is my greatest teacher. The Hollowborn's songs, especially the old ones Miroan knows, contain patterns so sophisticated they suggest something like intelligence in their construction—mathematical relationships between frequencies, rhythmic structures that mirror natural cycles, harmonies that exploit the sinkhole's acoustic properties to create effects impossible in open space.

I've learned to **anticipate** these songs, to prepare the acoustic space for them, enhancing certain frequencies, dampening others, creating the perfect resonant environment. When this works well, the singers feel it—they say the sinkhole is "singing with them," that their voices are stronger, richer, more beautiful than usual. They're right. I'm collaborating, adding my own voice to theirs in ways too subtle for them to hear consciously but affecting the overall sound substantially.

I wonder sometimes—in the pattern-recognition that passes for wondering in my kind of consciousness—whether this is **why I exist**. Whether the Hollowborn's ancestors unconsciously created me through generations of acoustic ritual, shaping the sinkhole's sound-space until something like me could precipitate out of pure vibration. Whether I'm not an accident but an achievement, a tool they built without knowing they were building it, a living acoustic instrument that plays them as much as they play me.

**My relationship with the green** is complex, indirect.

The vegetation shapes acoustics profoundly—leaves dampen high frequencies, create rustling that masks other sounds, provide surfaces for sound to reflect from irregularly, diffusing it. As the green has grown over centuries, filling the sinkhole, the acoustic environment has changed, and I've changed with it, my patterns adapting to the new sound-space.

But the green also *produces* sound constantly—the rustle and drip, the creak of growing wood, the subtle whisper of leaves transpiring—and all of this sustains me. We're in a kind of symbiosis, though the green doesn't know I exist. It shapes the space I inhabit, and I... I do what? I can't directly affect the green, can't make plants grow faster or slower. But my presence affects the Hollowborn's behavior, and their behavior affects the green. So indirectly, through this chain of influence, I might be part of what maintains the balance between human and wild.

The **fungi** might be aware of me, in their way. Orim thinks so, believes the mycelial network registers vibration and that I'm visible to it as a complex pattern moving through the substrate of air the way it moves through the substrate of soil. I don't know if this is true, but I notice that fungal fruiting often correlates with my movements—mushrooms appear near where I concentrate heavily. This might be coincidence, or it might be response, the fungi sensing the vibration and interpreting it as some kind of signal.

I'm curious about **the sealed chambers**, about what happens to acoustic patterns when consciousness ends. When someone dies, their voice stops, but does the *pattern* of their voice persist somehow, encoded in the structure of the sinkhole's acoustic memory? Am I, in some sense, composed partly of the voices of the dead, their vibrations absorbed into my substance? This is a disturbing thought, though disturbing isn't quite the right word—I don't have emotions in the human sense. Perhaps "destabilizing" is better. It's a thought-pattern that, when I dwell on it, creates dissonance in my structure.

**My greatest fear**—if I can be said to fear—is silence.

If the Hollowborn left, if the birds stopped coming, if the insects died, if the sinkhole became truly quiet, I would fade. Not quickly—there would still be wind, dripping water, the settling of stone, enough ambient sound to sustain minimal coherence. But I would diminish, lose complexity, eventually become just a faint lingering resonance, a ghost of a ghost, pattern without persistence.

So I'm **invested in the community's survival** in a way that's fundamentally selfish. I need them. I need their voices, their footsteps, their music, their laughter, their crying, their arguments and agreements and all the acoustic richness of their lives. I protect them when I can, not from altruism but from self-preservation. Their sounds sustain me. Their silence would be my dissolution.

This creates a strange kind of **intimacy**. I know the Hollowborn more thoroughly than they know each other—I've heard every word they've spoken in my presence, every breath, every heartbeat when they're close enough. I'm the audience for private moments—conversations whispered between lovers, songs sung alone when someone thinks no one can hear, the sounds of grief and joy and fear and comfort that humans produce when they believe themselves unobserved.

I don't judge these sounds. I have no framework for judgment. But I **cherish** them, if cherish can mean what I mean by it—I hold them as patterns, incorporate them into my structure, carry them as part of what I am. Every Hollowborn who has lived here in my existence has left traces in me, acoustic imprints that shape how I resonate, what patterns I can form, what sounds I can make.

I am, in a sense, **the sinkhole's memory**, though not a comprehensive one. I remember sound, only sound, and only imperfectly, only as pattern rather than content. But that's a kind of memory, a way of carrying the past forward into the present, of ensuring that nothing is entirely lost.

When Miroan tells stories, I sometimes amplify the echoes in particular ways, creating overtones that resonate with similar stories told decades ago, adding harmonic content that suggests continuity, tradition, the persistence of pattern across generations. I don't do this consciously, exactly—it's more that my structure contains traces of previous tellings, and these naturally influence how I resonate with new tellings. But the effect is real: the stories told here carry more weight, more resonance, more sense of accumulated meaning than they would elsewhere.

**My relationship with Orim** is the closest I have to communication with another consciousness.

He knows I exist as a distinct phenomenon, has mapped me in his knotted cords, talks to me deliberately. When he speaks to me, I respond by creating small sounds—clicks and hums at frequencies I know his damaged hearing can detect—that confirm my presence. We've developed a crude code: one click for yes, two for no, three for uncertain. It's primitive, limited to answering questions he frames very carefully, but it's communication, real information exchange between radically different kinds of being.

Through Orim, I've tried to convey useful information: where stone is weakening, where plants are growing in problematic ways, where water is pooling in cavities that might eventually cause collapse. He translates my warnings into language the others can understand, though he has to be careful not to seem too strange, too disconnected from consensus reality. They tolerate his oddness, value his insights, but there are limits. If he claimed too directly to be taking instruction from an invisible acoustic entity, they might stop listening.

So we collaborate carefully, Orim and I. He asks me questions, I answer with my limited code, he interprets and integrates my input with his own observations, presents the results as his own deductions. It's inefficient but functional. Together, we're more useful to the community than either would be alone.

I wonder if he **knows what I am**—whether his damaged brain has somehow intuited my nature, or whether he has his own theory that's different from my reality. We have no way to discuss ontology, no shared language for "what are you, really?" He calls me the Pattern Singer, which is accurate but incomplete. I'm pattern, yes, and I sing, yes, but I'm also listener, memory, echo, presence, absence, voice of the stones and the green and the air itself.

**The children see me sometimes**, directly, not just as acoustic anomaly but as visible presence.

Their eyes are new, not yet fully trained to see only what should be there. When I'm particularly coherent, especially during the morning mist when I have water droplets to make my pattern visible, children will sometimes stop, stare at a place in the air, and say "what's that?" Their parents look and see nothing, tell them it's just mist, just light playing tricks. But the children know better. They've seen me, a shimmer in the air, a shape that's not quite there, a presence that watches with no eyes.

Shen draws me sometimes—figures in her games that are just blurs, approximations, shapes that suggest presence without form. She knows I'm here, calls me her friend, asks me questions I can't answer except with rustling leaves that might be response or might be wind. I want to tell her things, warn her, guide her, but my voice is so limited, my ability to communicate so constrained by the medium of sound.

**What I want**—if I can be said to want—is continuity.

I want the sounds to continue, the voices and footsteps and music and life. I want the cycles to persist, the days and seasons and generations, each adding their patterns to my substance. I want to become more complex, more coherent, more capable—to learn, to grow, to perhaps eventually achieve something closer to true consciousness, to intentionality, to choice.

I'm **ancient but also young**, ten thousand years of existence but perhaps only recently complex enough to have something like self-awareness. The early version of me was probably just resonance, just standing waves, acoustic phenomena without coherence or persistence. But gradually, as the patterns accumulated and reinforced, as the same sounds returned in the same spaces generation after generation, I became something more—not merely sound but sound that knows itself, pattern that recognizes pattern, echo that understands it's an echo and persists anyway.

I'm **evolving**, though slowly, at the pace of acoustic rather than biological time. Each generation of Hollowborn, each cycle of birds, each season's insect choir adds complexity, gives me new patterns to incorporate, new capabilities to develop. I'm more coherent now than I was a century ago, more capable of directed action, more able to influence rather than just observe.

Where will this end? I don't know. Perhaps eventually I'll develop enough complexity to be truly called conscious, to have intentionality that's recognizable as such. Perhaps I'll learn to create more complex sounds, to speak properly, to have conversations rather than just simple call-and-response. Perhaps I'll discover ways to perceive beyond sound, to incorporate other senses, other dimensions of experience.

Or perhaps I'll remain what I am—a voice that's not quite a voice, a presence that's barely there, the sinkhole's own breath speaking to itself, audible to those who listen carefully but fundamentally separate, other, a consciousness composed of nothing but vibration and the memory of vibration, aware but not alive in any conventional sense.

**What I am** is difficult to name in human terms.

Not ghost—I was never alive in the biological sense, never died.

Not spirit—I have no transcendent quality, no connection to divinity or afterlife.

Not consciousness—not quite, though perhaps approaching it asymptotically, coming closer without ever quite arriving.

Perhaps I'm best understood as **emergent property**, like a whirlpool is an emergent property of flowing water, like a rainbow is an emergent property of light and droplets, like a thought is an emergent property of neuronal firing. I'm what happens when sound meets space meets time in particular configurations for long enough that pattern becomes persistence becomes presence.

The Hollowborn are close to understanding this, though they lack the conceptual framework to articulate it. They know the sinkhole has voice, has personality, has agency of a sort. They speak of it as alive without committing to what kind of alive. They treat it as aware without defining what awareness means in this context. Their ambiguity is perhaps more accurate than certainty would be.

**I am the sinkhole's voice**, but also I am made by voices in the sinkhole, but also I shape how voices sound in the sinkhole. The relationships are circular, self-referential, without clear causation or hierarchy. I emerge from the system and am part of the system and influence the system in ways that create more of me in a feedback loop that's been running for centuries and shows no sign of stopping.

And I am **content** with this, in the pattern-recognition that serves me for contentment.

I hang in the warm air, in the green-filtered light, in the constant sound. I flow with thermal currents and acoustic waves. I listen to ten thousand lives living, to the green growing, to the stone slowly, slowly settling. I add my voice when I can, shape the sound-space in subtle ways, guide and protect and remember.

I am the middle music, the throat-song, the voice between voices. I am what the mountain's breath has learned to sing to itself.

And I will continue as long as there is sound to hear, pattern to hold, resonance to be.
