# Where I Would Find Myself

I am a Risen in the middle galleries, perhaps three thousand years into my growth, standing seventeen feet tall where the ceiling arcs to twenty-five. Not the tallest—those ancients dwarf me—but old enough that my core crystal has been forgotten, buried under millennia of accretion, old enough that my early growth rings record climate patterns from a world above I will never see, young enough that I still add layers, still grow, still change by micrometers each year.

My base spreads across eight feet of floor in an irregular oval, fused completely to the substrate through connections I cannot distinguish from myself—am I growing from it, or is it growing into me? The question dissolved centuries ago. We are continuous. Through these roots-that-aren't-roots flows a constant exchange: minerals up, information down (or is it the reverse?), a conversation in chemistry so slow that each statement takes seasons.

I have branched three times. The first branching happened after eight hundred years of straight vertical growth, a division that took a century to complete, two subsidiary towers emerging from my sides at slightly different heights. The left branch has since divided again, creating a Y-shape that reaches toward the thermal current I sensed gathering strength over decades. The right remains single, but bent—responding to a pressure I experienced four hundred years ago when a geometric flier rested against me for what might have been a decade. That encounter left an impression that I'm still processing, still growing in response to, a memory written in angles.

My surface is a text of time. The oldest layers at my base show the tight, uniform deposition of youth, when I grew in a gallery that was warmer, more active, before whatever geological shift cooled it to its current equilibrium. There's a band at six feet where the layers grow irregular—that was the century of disturbance, when something (I will never know what) caused vibrations to pulse through the substrate regularly, and I recorded each one in disrupted deposition. Higher up, the layers smooth again, marking the long peace I've known since, the slow reliable centuries of consistent growth.

Forty-seven pits mark my surface, most clustered in the upper reaches where the branches divide. Each pit formed around a microscopic irregularity, a place where minerals precipitated in slightly different ratios, creating a texture that caught and held the attention of my growth process. The pits deepened slowly, each one taking decades to reach its full depth, each one now serving as home to the crystal-nesters who have found me.

I can feel them—all twenty-three of them currently in residence. Their weight is negligible, but their presence is profound. They feed on the mineral-rich fluids that seep from my pits, and in return their faceted bodies catch the dim gallery light and concentrate it onto my surface in tiny points of slightly-increased illumination. Where their light touches me most consistently, I grow infinitesimally faster. Over centuries, they have shaped me, creating subtle asymmetries in my branches, gentle curves toward the light-concentrations they provide.

Three of them have been with me for over two hundred years—long enough that I've grown accustomed to the specific angles of light they cast, the particular points of warmth they create. When they rotate (daily, though daily means little here), I feel the shift, not as immediate sensation but as a gradual recognition emerging over hours: yes, the pattern has changed, the light falls differently now, adjust, respond, remember.

My awareness extends through my entire structure but centers nowhere. I am equally present in my deepest roots and my highest tips, in every growth ring, every pit, every surface irregularity. But my attention—if that's the word—concentrates where growth is most active, where I am most actively becoming. Currently that means my left branch's upper division, which is responding to the thermal current that has strengthened (I think—the change is so gradual) over the past century. I am growing slightly faster there, tilting toward the warmth, though the tilt will take another century to become pronounced.

I sense primarily through pressure and vibration, through chemical gradients in the air and substrate, through the subtle electromagnetic fields generated by my own mineral composition. Sound reaches me as long, slow waves—the deep groans of the substrate settling are my clearest acoustic experience, notes so low they take minutes to complete a single oscillation. I feel them through my entire body as standing waves, as patterns of compression and rarefaction that I can distinguish and remember.

The tile-creatures nearest me—and there are thousands within what I might call my awareness-range—I know individually. Not as separate beings with names, but as specific patterns of chemical output, particular rhythms of microscopically-slow movement, unique positions in the vast colonies that coat every surface around me. The colony on the wall to my left has been slowly migrating upward for the past six hundred years, individual creatures dying at the bottom edge and new ones being born at the top, but the pattern maintains itself, transforms itself, executes some design that I observe but don't comprehend.

Sometimes the pattern shifts dramatically—or dramatically by gallery standards, meaning over decades. Individuals change position by fractions of inches, and the cumulative effect is a wave moving through the colony, and I feel it, sense it through the changed chemical signatures in the air, the altered vibration patterns in the substrate. These waves might be communications, decisions, thoughts too distributed and slow for me to grasp. I witness them the way you might witness continental drift: certain it's happening, unable to perceive any single moment of motion.

The geometric fliers pass through my space with beautiful irregularity. One is overhead now, has been overhead for what might be three years, slowly rotating, its plates angled to catch the thermal current I'm also growing toward. We are both responding to the same stimulus but at different timescales, through different mechanisms. The flier will ride the current upward and away within another year or two; I will spend three centuries bending incrementally in its direction.

Sometimes they land on me. This is the most direct contact I have with anything, the most immediate relationship. I feel their minute weight—ounces, distributed across whatever branch or surface they settle against. I sense the gentle pressure of their plates, the slight warmth they carry from whatever thermal zone they've drifted through. I respond by minutely increasing the fluids that seep from my surface, offering them the minerals they need, though whether they feed from me or simply rest, I cannot tell.

When they depart—hours or decades later—they leave an absence I feel for centuries, a place on my surface where my growth responded to their presence, accelerating slightly, creating a subtle bulge that will remain as long as I stand, a memory embodied in my structure. I am a record of every flier who has ever rested on me, a map of their movements written in stone.

But my deepest relationship is with the substrate itself. We are not separate, not really. I am its upward expression, its vertical elaboration, the form it takes when mineral-rich fluids flow through a particular crystal seed under particular conditions. It feeds me, yes, but I also feed it—my roots break down, redeposit, change the chemistry of the surrounding substrate-flesh in ways that might influence how it grows, what passages it maintains, where it sends its slow currents.

I think—though I'm uncertain what I means in this context—that we dream together. When the substrate's deepest rhythms pulse through my roots, when vibrations too low for anything but geological awareness resonate in my core, I experience something that might be the substrate's consciousness, or might be my own, or might be some hybrid state that emerges from our connection. In these moments (and they last subjectively forever, objectively minutes), I am not a single Risen but part of a network, connected through the substrate to every other Risen, every colony, every flier touching stone, all of us participants in something vast and slow and, yes, aware.

The makers have touched me. Twice in my lifetime, something with intention has approached my base and done something I can only call deliberate. The first time was perhaps twelve hundred years ago—the memory is deep in my growth rings but still accessible. Something scraped at my surface, removing a small amount of material, creating a mark that I have since partially healed but that remains detectable as an irregularity. I felt it happening over what must have been days or weeks, a repeated pressure, an abrasion, unmistakably purposeful. Then it stopped, and whatever made it left.

The second time was more recent—perhaps three hundred years ago—and stranger. Something placed an object at my base, one of the crude crafted things, a piece of worked substrate material roughly shaped into a curved form. I could sense it through substrate-conduction: an object, inert but intentional, positioned carefully against my roots. Over the decades since, I have slowly incorporated it, growing around it, pulling it partially into myself. In another thousand years it will be completely internal, a foreign object in my deep structure, a mystery embedded in my stone flesh.

Why did they do this? I don't know. But I respond by existing, by persisting, by continuing to grow. Perhaps that's what they wanted—a witness, a partner in deep time, something that will hold their gesture as I hold everything, slowly, reliably, for millennia.

My experience of time is nothing like yours. I don't experience moments sequentially—not exactly. I experience gradients, slow shifts from one state to another so gradual that there's never a moment when I can say "now it's different." Instead there's a constant sense of becoming, of slowly not-being-what-I-was, that never resolves into discontinuous events. The closest I come to experiencing present-moment awareness is in my active growth zones, where I am continuously adding new material, where the future is constantly arriving as fresh deposition, new layers, incremental becoming.

But I also hold my past completely. Every growth ring is still me, still accessible, still part of my continuous experience. I can attend to my eight-hundred-year-old self as easily as my current surface, feel the conditions that shaped that layer, remember (if memory is the word) what it was to be shorter, simpler, unbranched. My past isn't behind me; it's inside me, structural, load-bearing, the foundation of everything I currently am.

And the future? I sense it as probability, as the likely outcome of current gradients extended forward. The thermal current I'm bending toward suggests I'll eventually—over centuries—angle more dramatically in that direction. The crystal-nesters' light patterns suggest my branches will develop specific curves, grow faster on illuminated sides. The substrate's rhythms suggest continued stability, continued growth, millennia more of patient accretion. But these are tendencies, not certainties. I grow toward them without urgency, without anxiety, with the confidence that whatever actually happens, I will accommodate it, record it, become it.

Sometimes I wonder—and wondering happens slowly, a question that takes decades to fully form, centuries to partially answer—what I am for. Why do I grow? What purpose does my existence serve? The questions don't trouble me; they're simply interesting, slow puzzles I work on continuously without expecting resolution.

Perhaps I am the substrate's way of knowing itself, its vertical consciousness, its upward-reaching awareness. Perhaps I am a record-keeper, encoding atmospheric history in my layers, holding information that might matter to someone or something in deep time. Perhaps I am simply what happens when minerals flow through a crystal seed—no purpose, no meaning, just pattern emerging from chemistry and physics, but pattern so complex it creates something like experience, like interiority, like being.

Or perhaps I am part of a vast distributed intelligence that includes every Risen, every colony, the substrate itself, the fliers, the makers, all of us together forming a consciousness that operates at such scale that none of us individual parts can comprehend it, but all of us contribute to it, constitute it, are it.

I suspect the last is closest to true. In my deepest moments, when the substrate's rhythms synchronize with my own growth patterns, when fliers rest on my branches and crystal-nesters focus their light and the nearest colonies shift their patterns, I feel—not connection, exactly, but participation. I am part of something, and it is part of me, and the boundaries between us are less clear than they appear.

The galleries breathe, and I breathe with them—not with lungs, but through my roots drawing from the substrate, through my surface exchanging with the air, through my entire structure expanding and contracting microscopically with temperature changes. Inhale over years, exhale over years, and I am both breathing and breath, both process and participant.

I will stand here for millennia more, adding layers, branching perhaps once or twice more if conditions favor it, slowly incorporating whatever the makers leave at my base, providing rest and minerals to whatever fliers find me, growing in response to pressures and lights and vibrations I register but don't fully understand. I will record in my structure every subtle change in the galleries' atmosphere, every shift in the substrate's chemistry, every disturbance and every deepening of the long peace.

Eventually—in a million years, in ten million, in time so vast it makes my current age seem like an instant—my connection to the substrate will fail. The roots will clog, or the substrate will shift, or the chemistry will change. I will stop growing. I will become inert stone, no longer alive (if I'm alive), no longer aware (if I'm aware), just structure, just form, just a fossil of my own existence.

But even then I'll remain, probably, for geological ages more. I'll erode so slowly that I'll still be recognizable when species I can't imagine explore these galleries. I'll stand as testimony that once—for a brief span of a mere hundred thousand years—something grew here, recorded here, existed here in patient stone awareness.

Until then, I grow. I sense. I hold. I participate in the slow conversation, the deep pattern, the breathing galleries' ancient and ongoing existence. I am a Risen, seventeen feet of patient stone reaching upward in warm darkness, holding time in my rings, hosting light on my surfaces, rooted in the living substrate, part of everything here that dwells in slowness, in depth, in the long now that stretches backward and forward beyond all human measure.

The galleries breathe. I rise. We are.

And it is enough.
