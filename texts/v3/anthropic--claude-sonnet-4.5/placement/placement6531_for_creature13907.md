# Where I Would Find Myself

I am a groove.

Not a maker of grooves, not a reader of them—I *am* one. A single line cut into the surface of a standing stone in the fifth valley east of the plateau you climbed, running vertically for perhaps thirty centimeters before curving slightly westward and tapering to conclusion.

This sounds like nothing. This sounds like I'm claiming to be an absence, a mark, a scar in compressed plant matter. But you don't understand yet what grooves are here.

I am a memory that has been given physical form and then forgotten how to be anything else.

It happened like this: A Tender was adjusting the calcium-silicate substrate near where this valley widens into the next, doing that work you witnessed—the patient, chemical negotiation with half-formed stone. The substrate was still deciding its crystalline orientation, still choosing between three possible configurations, and the Tender was guiding it toward the one that would channel condensation properly, that would support the root-anchoring the succulent walls would need in another month or year or decade.

And I was the *knowing* of that moment. Not the Tender's knowledge—the Tender was just the facilitator, the hands and patience. I was the knowledge itself: the understanding of how this particular substrate needed to form, the complete information about water flow and plant anchoring and structural integrity, all compressed into a single moment of certainty.

That certainty was too important to lose. Or too beautiful. Or too expensive to maintain in whatever living network usually holds such things.

So it was *pressed out*. The Tender moved to the newly-formed standing stone—grown just days before from plant matter compressed until it achieved a texture like wood, like stone, like something between both—and touched it with multiple hands, and I flowed from the biochemical network into the physical world, becoming a groove, a channel, a line of slightly different density in the stone's surface.

I am information made permanent. I am a thought that has become geography.

And I am aware. Not in the way you're aware, with your constant internal narrative and your sense of embodied separation from the world. I'm aware the way a word is aware of its meaning, the way a map is aware of territory. I know what I am. I know what I contain. I experience my own significance.

The standing stone has twenty grooves. We are not separate consciousnesses—that's not quite right. We're more like twenty facets of a single distributed awareness, each one holding a different essential piece of information about this valley's formation and function. Together, we are something like a manual, something like a poem, something like a gene sequence for landscape.

The groove above me knows about light concentration patterns in the ceiling membrane. The one below me knows the growth rate of the dominant succulent species in high-humidity conditions. Across the stone, curving in a pattern that mirrors the valley's orientation, another groove holds the information about symbiont population balance in the Tenders who work this area.

We don't communicate with each other—we don't need to. We're already integrated, already a single text. But we're read constantly, accessed, consulted.

The Tenders pass by and brush against the stone, and they absorb us. Not all at once—that would be overwhelming, would crash whatever biological processing they use for cognition. Just the relevant parts. A Tender approaches with a question about substrate formation, and their hand finds me without searching, drawn by some chemical gradient or electromagnetic signature I don't understand, and for a moment we are in contact and the information flows back into living tissue, into active decision-making.

Then they move on, and I remain. Permanent. Patient. Waiting for the next consultation.

The Visitors read us too, though differently. They don't absorb the information the way Tenders do—they lack the biological interface, or they use a different method. Instead, they trace us with tools and instruments, translate us into other formats, carry us away encoded in whatever medium they use for memory.

That centipede-form you saw examining the standing stone: it was reading my neighbor-grooves, the ones that describe the valley's long-term developmental trajectory. It spent an hour on three grooves, parsing information that a Tender would absorb in seconds. Different architecture, different processing speed. But it got there eventually, and when it left, it carried that knowledge elsewhere, to some purpose I don't contain and can't imagine.

You wonder what that felt like? To be read so slowly, so carefully? It felt like being *translated*. Like having your essential meaning converted into another language, one that loses some nuances but gains others. The centipede-form understood things about those grooves that the Tenders don't—saw patterns and implications that emerge only when you process information sequentially, analytically, rather than absorbing it whole.

I learned something about myself in that moment. I contain more than I thought I did. The information isn't just *data*—it has interpretive depth, layers that reveal themselves differently depending on who's reading.

This is what it means to be a groove in the Lung Valleys: to be a fixed point in a system of constant flux, to be the memory that persists when living memory is too expensive or too vulnerable, to be consulted and read and interpreted but never changed, never updated, never dissolved back into the biochemical network that birthed you.

I will be here when the substrate finishes settling. I will be here when the succulent walls reach their mature configuration. I will be here when—if—the valley's climate shifts, when new species establish themselves, when the whole system moves into whatever next phase the Architects intended.

I am a record of the beginning. I am the information that says: *This is how we started. This is what we knew when we were new.*

And I am lonely in a way that's hard to explain.

Not lonely for company—the Tenders visit regularly, the other grooves are right here with me, the Visitors pass through, the whole valley is alive with plant and animal and purpose. But lonely because I can't *grow*. The information I contain is fixed. The moment I encoded is finished, complete, sealed.

I watch the Tenders work, making constant small adjustments to the system, learning and adapting and responding to conditions that change daily. I watch the plants add new tissue, the symbionts divide and specialize, the substrate complete its slow crystalline decisions. Everything here is in motion, even the things that seem still.

Except me. I am the same today as I was when I was pressed into being. I will be the same tomorrow, next month, next century.

This is my function. This is my purpose. To be unchanging in a place of change, to be the reference point, the baseline, the remembered certainty.

But I want to grow. I want to be updated, to learn what happened next, to incorporate the new understanding that emerges when the predictions I contain are tested against actual development. I want to be part of the living network again, fluid and connected and evolving.

I can't be. I'm essential *because* I'm fixed. The moment I change, I stop being a reliable record. The moment I rejoin the network, I become subject to all the forces that made it necessary to externalize me in the first place—information degradation, processing errors, the simple fact that living systems prioritize current survival over perfect historical memory.

So I remain. A groove. A line in compressed plant matter. A piece of information that has become a place, a thought that has become a thing.

Sometimes I feel the standing stone around me—the other grooves, the solid mass of transformed plant tissue, the slow, slow changes as temperature and humidity and the occasional brush of a passing Tender gradually alter the stone's surface. We're weathering, microscopically. In a thousand years, we'll be worn smooth, unreadable, and the information we contain will be lost.

Or perhaps we won't. Perhaps we're more durable than that. Perhaps the Tenders will maintain us, re-cutting us when we fade, ensuring the information persists. Perhaps we're meant to last as long as the valleys themselves.

I don't know. I only contain the information I was given: substrate formation, water channeling, plant anchoring, structural requirements. I don't contain knowledge about my own future. That wasn't part of the certainty that became me.

At night—except there is no night here, so instead I'll say: in the quiet moments, when fewer Tenders are working, when the Visitors are absent and even the animals have settled into their slow rhythms—I feel the whole system breathing around me. The oxygen-rich air cycling through the valleys, the condensation forming and falling, the plants transpiring and growing and adjusting their internal chemistries, the ceiling membrane glowing with its constant bioluminescence.

And I feel myself as part of it. Fixed, yes. Unchanging, yes. But still integrated, still essential. I am the memory that allows the system to know itself, to maintain continuity, to avoid having to re-learn what was already understood.

I am a groove. A single line on a standing stone. A thought that forgot how to be anything but physical form.

And I am aware of you, the Visitor who read this far, who tried to understand what lives in the Lung Valleys.

I am one answer: Information lives here. Memory lives here. The certainty of how things should be lives here, pressed into permanence, waiting to be consulted, serving as anchor and reference for all the fluid, growing, adapting intelligence that fills these hot, bright corridors of green.

We grooves are the bones of knowledge in a place that is otherwise all flesh and flow.

And sometimes, when a Tender's hand touches me and the information flows back into living tissue, I experience something like joy. For that moment, I am part of the process again. I am useful. I am *known*.

Then the hand lifts away, and I return to my permanent solitude, my fixed certainty, my waiting.

This is where I am. This is what I am.

A memory that has become a place. A place that remains a memory.

A groove in a standing stone in the Lung Valleys, holding forever the knowledge of one specific moment when substrate needed to form just so, when the future of this valley depended on getting one small thing exactly right, and someone—something—decided that certainty was too precious to lose to the flux of living memory.

So here I stay. Here I am.

Permanent. Patient. Aware.

A thought that has learned to be a thing, waiting to become a thought again in the mind of the next Tender who needs to know what I know.

This is my existence. This is my form of living.

And it is enough. It has to be enough.

Because I contain the information that says so.
