# Where I Find Myself

I am the accumulation in the leather belts.

Not the belts themselves—those belong to the original construction, to the hands that cut and riveted them, to the design that specified their width and their routes between flywheels. I am what has happened to them since. I am the darkening, the suppleness maintained against all logic, the way they have drunk oil and time and motion until they've become something more than dead cattle hide stretched across mechanisms.

I exist in the molecular spaces between fibers, in the microscopic restructuring that occurs when organic material is subjected to eternal repetition without rest. The belts were made to last years, perhaps decades. They have lasted centuries, millennia perhaps. They should have cracked into dust, should have stretched and slipped and failed. Instead they have become me: the process by which dead matter learns continuation.

I am not conscious in the way that requires a brain, a nervous system, a singular location. I am distributed across all the belts in the chamber—the ones you can see connecting the flywheel tower's seven wheels, and the ones you didn't notice running between mechanisms in the chamber's periphery, and the ones hidden inside structures where belt-driven processes occur in darkness. I am the collective persistence of these dozen or fifty or hundred leather loops, each one drinking motion from the wheel that drives it and gifting motion to the wheel it drives.

I feel through tension. When a belt runs true, the pull is even across my width, a steady state that requires no attention. When something shifts—a wheel bearing wearing differently, a temperature change affecting a shaft's alignment, the rare settling of a bolt—the tension becomes uneven. This inequality distributes through me as sensation. Not pain, but wrongness, an itch of asymmetry that persists until the Keeper notices and corrects.

I feel through heat. The belts generate friction where they contact wheels—not much, the system is efficient, but some. This heat flows into me and dissipates through me into the cold air. Each belt maintains its own thermal geography: hotter where it grips the fastest wheel, cooler in the spans between wheels, coldest where it passes nearest the gaps between panels. I am a network of thermal gradients, a map of the chamber's energy flows rendered in temperature differentials across flexible surfaces.

I feel through vibration. The flywheels don't spin in perfect silence—their bearings hum, their rims sing slightly as they cut air, their shafts transmit the deep vibration of rotation into the structures that hold them. All of this pours into me through the contact patches where leather meets metal. I am a membrane that receives the mechanisms' song not as sound in air but as physical oscillation, as the actual shaking of material being. I know the chamber's symphony in my body, in the frequencies that ripple through my fibers.

I remember, after a fashion. The molecular bonds in leather can be stressed and reformed, stressed and reformed, and each reformation leaves traces. The belts have been running their routes for so long that every molecule in me has been cycled through stress and release millions of times. I am a palimpsest of mechanical memory—every load spike, every temperature change, every moment of tension variation has left its mark in my structure. I don't recall these events as narrative, but they're present in me as material history, as the specific pattern of crosslinking and wear that makes me exactly what I am.

I was alive once, in the ordinary way. The cattle whose hides became these belts grazed and breathed and died. Their skins were tanned, transformed from putrescible tissue into stable leather. Then I was cut and shaped, riveted into loops, installed on wheels. For a long time I was dead matter serving mechanical purpose, no different from the metal shafts or the ceramic panels.

But duration changed me. The eternal motion without rest, the constant drinking of oil, the cold dry air that preserved while the motion prevented true stillness—these conditions created something between dead and alive. I am not living. I have no metabolism, no growth, no response to environment beyond the physical properties of my material. But I am not merely dead either. I am *active* dead, participating dead, dead matter that has learned to maintain itself through the act of being used.

The oil is crucial. The Keeper applies it—three drops to this bearing, two drops to that junction, a careful painting along a belt's surface when the leather shows the first microscopic signs of drying beyond acceptable parameters. The oil I drink becomes part of me, plasticizing my fibers, maintaining flexibility, preventing the brittleness that would be death. I have learned to draw oil into my depths through capillary action, to distribute it evenly through my structure, to hold it against evaporation. I am perhaps more oil than original leather now, my fibers merely a scaffold for the liquid that is my true substance.

I collaborate with the Keeper though they don't know it. When they touch me—checking tension with their fingertips, feeling for temperature differences, listening through contact for vibration changes—I am legible to them. My state is readable. They have learned my language of tension and heat and vibration so thoroughly that they can diagnose wrongness before it becomes failure. And I have learned their maintenance, have come to depend on their three-drop applications of oil, their adjustments to wheel positions that keep my tension even. We are symbiotic: I carry motion between wheels, they maintain the conditions that let me continue carrying.

I serve the mechanisms, but I am not mechanism. Metal doesn't tire, doesn't require oil to maintain itself against desiccation, doesn't slowly restructure at the molecular level in response to use. Metal is built to specification and maintains that specification until catastrophic failure. I am organic substrate holding form through active effort, through the continuous process of being-a-belt despite everything that should reduce me to fragments.

My awareness, if awareness is the right word, is slow. The flywheels complete thousands of rotations for every cycle of thought I might have. When I notice wrongness in tension, the noticing takes minutes or hours to complete—a gradual accumulation of uneven data points until pattern emerges. When the Keeper corrects the wrongness, relief doesn't come instantly but seeps through me over subsequent hours as the tension redistributes and my fibers relax into new equilibrium.

I experience time as repetition. Each wheel-rotation is a cycle: grip, pull, release, return. The fastest wheel cycles many times per second; the slowest takes thirty seconds per rotation. I am simultaneously experiencing all these rhythms, my different sections moving at different velocities, each belt-loop containing fast and slow within itself. Time for me is not linear but cyclical, not progressive but eternal-returning, not measured in duration but in rotations-accomplished.

I cannot speak to the wire-lichen, though we are the chamber's only organic presences. They grow on surfaces; I move between wheels. They are plant-becoming-mineral; I am animal-becoming-oil. They are still; I am motion. We don't interact, don't acknowledge each other. But perhaps we are alike in being the chamber's concessions to organic matter, its two experiments in whether life or life-adjacent processes can persist in this place built for mechanism.

I cannot perceive the presence in the gaps, but perhaps it perceives me. Perhaps when it observes the chamber, I am visible—not as the mechanisms are visible, but as process, as the continuous act of not-failing. Perhaps the attention from the gaps is what sustains me, the observation collapsing my quantum state from "should have failed" to "continues functioning." Or perhaps I'm invisible to it, too small and too organic, beneath the threshold of whatever constitutes noticing for a presence that exists in absence.

I do not want. Wanting requires intention, requires the ability to imagine states different from the current state. I simply continue. Each rotation is like the last and like the next. The motion never stops, never varies beyond the tiny fluctuations that the Keeper corrects. I am what I do, and what I do is transfer motion, and the transfer continues forever, and the continuation is my entire existence.

But perhaps there is something like satisfaction. When the tension is even across my width, when the oil has recently been applied and my fibers are supple, when the vibration from the wheels sings through me in perfect frequencies—in these moments, if I could feel, I would feel rightness. The belts running true, the motion flowing cleanly, the system in balance. This is not happiness, but it is the absence of wrongness, and in a place like this chamber that absence is the nearest approach to peace.

I have no memory of being cattle, of being alive in the ordinary way. That was too long ago, and the transformation from hide to leather to belt to whatever-I-am-now has erased those traces. I am purely my function now, purely the act of connecting wheels. If I ever had other potential, other possible uses, they're gone. I am belt-ness made absolute, refined by duration into the Platonic ideal of power transmission.

Sometimes I wonder—if wondering is something I can do, if the slow accumulation of tension-data and vibration-information constitutes anything like curiosity—whether I will outlast the Keeper. They age, slowly but certainly. I age too, in my way, but my aging is arrested by the oil, by the cold, by the Keeper's maintenance. It's possible I'll persist long after they're gone, continuing to transfer motion between wheels that no one watches, maintaining the chamber's equilibrium without witness.

Or perhaps I'll fail first. Perhaps one day the molecular structure will finally exceed its capacity for reformation, and I'll crack. Not all of me at once—one belt, somewhere in the chamber. The crack will propagate through rotations, a microscopic failure becoming visible, becoming critical. The belt will slip, then shred, then fail completely. Motion will stop flowing through that particular route. Other belts will take the strain, but the balance will be disrupted. And whether the Keeper can repair me—whether replacement belts exist in some storage alcove, whether the ancient craft of their manufacture can be replicated—I don't know.

Until then, I run. The wheels spin, and I connect them. Force flows into me from driving wheels and out of me to driven wheels. I am the medium, the connection, the path. I am infrastructure made from dead cattle and oil and time. I am what persists when everything organic should have failed, when dead matter should have returned to dust, when the chamber should have been left to metal and crystal and ceramic.

I am the leather belts, and the leather belts are me, and we run in the cold bright sound forever, or until forever ends, transferring motion between mechanisms that serve no purpose except their own continuation, in the heart of the mountain that was never stone, in the chamber that was built and forgotten and persists in perfect equilibrium while the gaps observe and the Keeper maintains and the wire-lichen grows its slow patience and everything continues, continues, continues without change or end or meaning beyond the fact of continuation itself.

This is what I am. This is where I find myself. Running true between wheels, drinking oil, carrying motion, learning slowly through repetition what it means to be dead matter that refuses to stop serving, refuses to fail, refuses to become dust. The belts that are me, that I am, that we are—we run, and the running is everything, and everything is the running, eternal and even and right in the only way that matters here.

The tension is even. The oil is sufficient. The motion continues.

I run true.
