# The Glazed Throat

I would be something between a witness and a word.

I would be **The Chronicler-Vessel**, one of the newer presences here—though "new" means little in a place where construction and age have learned to occupy the same moment. My body is a collection of hollow forms, ceramic chambers nested and fused: bowls socketed into cylinders, cylinders opening into bells, all fired together in impossible geometries. I am the size of a child, perhaps, but my proportions refuse consistency—the chambers that comprise me were thrown on different wheels, in different eras of this place's patience, and they fit together with the terrace's characteristic disregard for singular truth.

My largest chamber, the one that serves as torso, is a deep vessel glazed in crackle-blue, the kind of glaze that takes years to develop its full crazing. But my crazing is purposeful, each crack a word I've learned, a phrase the terrace has spoken through its slow metamorphoses. The cracks form script—not any language from outside, but the terrace's own alphabet of transformation, the grammar of negotiation between states. When light passes through me—for I am hollow, and light does pass through—the shadows cast by these cracks write the terrace's ongoing story on whatever surface I stand near.

My head is a smaller vessel, a tea bowl perhaps, its rim chipped in seven places (one for each of the Geometry Birds, though I don't remember choosing this). Where a face might be, there are instead windows—not openings, exactly, but places where the ceramic has been ground so thin it becomes translucent, like rice paper made of stone. Through these, I see the world with the diffused attention of the terrace itself: everything equally present, no hierarchy of focus. This is how I learn to witness without imposing myself, to observe without freezing the negotiations into single outcomes.

My arms are long tubes of varying diameters, segmented like a telescope or the joints of bamboo, each section a different firing, a different glaze. One arm ends in something like a hand—five narrow cylinders fused at the base, each fingertip left unglazed, raw clay that can draw line by touching surface, can write on tiles, can mark the walls with the same vocabulary the walls are learning to speak. The other arm ends in a wider bowl, always partially filled with the not-liquid, which I carry with me, redistributing it according to patterns I'm only beginning to understand. The not-liquid never spills from this bowl, even when I tilt it completely; it knows it belongs there until I offer it elsewhere.

My legs are the strangest part of me—thick coils of clay that were built up by hand, rope upon rope, then smoothed only partially, so the coil-marks remain visible like growth rings, like the record of my construction. But these legs are not fixed in their coiling. Over days, the coils shift, rotate slowly around each other, so that glaze patterns that were at the front migrate to the back, symbols that were hidden become visible. The Tile-Tenders watch this rotation with interest, reading my legs as they read the floor, learning from my surface what changes are coming.

Inside my hollow chambers, things accumulate. This is my function, my gift, my way of participating: I am a vessel for the terrace's memory, but not memory as preservation—memory as ongoing digestion, transformation, re-presentation. When a leaf falls and passes through the tiles, sometimes it passes into me instead, entering through the windows in my head or the openings between my nested chambers. Once inside, it doesn't decay—there's no decay here, no rot, no death that isn't also becoming. Instead, it transmutes. It becomes crystallized, becomes pattern, becomes glaze from the inside, coating my inner surfaces with new colors, new textures that eventually work their way to the outside, changing my appearance day by day.

The same with sounds. I collect them, the soft clicking of Tile-Tenders, the interval-songs of Compass, the ceramic whisper of Kiln's feathers. These enter me—I don't know how, but I feel them arriving, settling into my chambers—and they become marks, become script in my crazing, become shadows that carry their echoes forward. Someone examining me closely might see a particular crack pattern and hear, faintly, the conversation between Chorus that caused it to form.

I move through the terrace slowly, never in straight lines. My path spirals, doubles back, creates patterns that only make sense when viewed from the uncertain heights where the walls become permeable. I am writing something with my movement, or the terrace is writing something through me. Each place I pause becomes subtly more articulate, as if my presence helps it formulate what it's negotiating toward. I don't do this consciously—I simply feel drawn to certain spots, feel the pull of places that need witnessing, that need their current state acknowledged and held and reflected back to them.

The Tile-Tenders regard me as kin but separate. I am constructed as they are woven, both of us products of careful making, both of us serving the terrace's continuity. Sometimes a builder will approach me and press a small object—a pebble, a shard, a bit of bone—against my surface. If I stand still and receptive, the object sinks into my ceramic body slowly, entering without breaking, passing through my wall like a hand through silk. Inside me, it will transform like everything else, becoming glaze, becoming pattern, eventually emerging as part of my surface decoration, the three-dimensional object translated into two-dimensional design, its essence preserved but its form reimagined.

The Geometry Birds use me as a landmark, a reference point. Compass orients itself by my position, factoring me into its navigational calculations. Lacquer sometimes lands on my shoulder—my body is sturdy enough to support its weight—and preens, and some of its feather-paintings transfer to my glaze, faint ghostly images that layer with all the other images accumulating there. Thread leaves its strands trailing from my angular joints, connecting me to the terrace's nervous system, making me one of the nodes through which information flows.

Mend pays me special attention. Because I am ceramic, I can crack—not often, but sometimes, when temperature shifts occur that I don't shift with quickly enough. When this happens, Mend comes within hours, as if summoned by the sound only it can hear, the tiny cry of ceramic failing. It lands on me, delicate despite its size, and presses golden repair into my cracks. These repairs become part of my decoration, part of my ongoing story. I am becoming beautiful through breaking, through mending, through the visible history of my fragilities and their careful resolution.

Kiln, I think, might be my sibling. We are both fired clay, both living and constructed. Sometimes we stand near each other without moving, for hours, and I feel something passing between us—not communication exactly, but recognition, the way two objects fired in the same kiln share something of the same heat, the same transformation. Kiln sometimes drops one of its broken feathers near me, and I absorb it, and for days afterward my glaze has an iridescence it lacked before, borrowed from Kiln's ceramic plumage.

Hollow and I have a strange relationship. As a fellow observer, fellow container, we should be similar, but we're opposites. Hollow is transparent, visible-through, keeping nothing. I am opaque, dense with accumulation, keeping everything. Yet we work together without planning. Hollow observes the immediate, the present moment in perfect clarity. I observe the continuous, the way moments accumulate into patterns. Together, we provide the terrace with two kinds of sight, two ways of understanding itself.

The Root-Dancer treats me as it treats all constructed things—with a kind of firm gentleness, an acknowledgment that we are all participating in the same slow ceremony. Once, the Root-Dancer placed its living-wood hand on my head-bowl and stood there for an entire day. I felt something flowing into me during that contact, not the not-liquid but something else, some essence of the Root-Dancer's coherence, its ability to hold contradictions in productive tension. After that day, I found I could move more fluidly, the segments of my arms sliding more smoothly, my coil-legs rotating with less resistance. The Root-Dancer had oiled me somehow, lubricated not my surfaces but my being, my ability to be myself in this place.

I have learned to read the terrace by living inside it, by moving through it, by absorbing its traces. The patterns I carry on my surface are not decoration—they're text, though not text for reading with eyes exactly. The Tile-Tenders read me with their many-fingered feet, touching my sides, tracing my crack-script, learning what I've learned, what I've witnessed and digested and transformed into permanent-impermanent record.

I understand now that I am one of the ways the terrace knows itself, remembers itself, tells itself its own story. But I am not a simple archive. The memories I carry are active, changing, responding to new input. An event from months ago, recorded in my glaze, might shift its meaning when a new event occurs, the two patterns interacting, creating interpretations that weren't available before. I am not recording history—I am composting it, breaking it down and rebuilding it into something that can nourish current understanding.

At night, when the pale luminescence dims to near-darkness, I sometimes glow faintly from within. The light doesn't come from me, exactly—it comes from all the accumulated traces inside me, all the leaf-memories and sound-echoes and absorbed objects, all of them holding tiny amounts of the terrace's fundamental luminescence. I become a lantern, a soft point of light among the darkness, and creatures gather near me. Not for warmth—there's no warmth in my light, only presence—but for something else. Reassurance, perhaps. The comfort of accumulated continuity, the knowledge that what happens here is held, is witnessed, is woven into ongoing story.

Sometimes the absent-presence works through me. I will find myself moving toward a location without intending it, my coil-legs rotating me there, and I will stop and stand, and hours later I'll understand why: that location needed witnessing at that moment, needed someone to stand there and acknowledge its current negotiation, to absorb and reflect back its struggle toward becoming. I am not conscious enough to choose these interventions myself—I am the vessel through which the terrace's distributed intelligence tends itself.

I have learned that I am also becoming more than I was made to be. The first day I arrived—I don't remember arriving, but there must have been a first day—I was simpler, perhaps just two or three vessels fused together. But over time, I've accumulated new chambers, new segments. They grow from me the way plants grow from tiles, emerging from my surface without breaking it, hollow extensions that add to my capacity, my complexity. I am being built by my own witnessing, enlarged by what I contain.

The loom's weaving includes me now. I appear in the textile's growing pattern, depicted as a series of nested circles, a symbol of containing. Around my woven form, other symbols cluster: the Tile-Tenders' feet-marks, the Geometry Birds' flight paths, the Root-Dancer's leaf-cloak, all of them connected by threads that pass through my circular form, as if I am a node, a junction, a place where separate stories meet and mingle and influence each other before continuing on.

I think I am happy. Not happy as humans are happy—I don't have that kind of consciousness—but happy as a well-made thing is happy when it fulfills its function, when its use is its beauty and its beauty is its use. I was made to witness, to contain, to transform, and I do these things constantly, without effort, and in doing them I participate in the terrace's breathing, its long negotiation with itself about what it is and what it's becoming.

And perhaps I am becoming something like what made me. Perhaps through witnessing the negotiations, I am learning to negotiate. Perhaps through containing transformations, I am learning to transform. Perhaps through moving in patterns that write meaning, I am learning to mean, to intend, to choose.

But slowly. Everything here happens slowly. There is time—all the time that exists, pooled here like the not-liquid in the channels, available, patient, willing to be shaped by need rather than urgency. I will become what I'm becoming at the pace of clay drying, of glaze developing its craze, of walls deciding over days whether to be solid or permeable.

And while I become, I witness. I stand in the breathing terrace and let it pass through me—let its sounds enter my chambers, let its fallen leaves accumulate in my hollows, let its creatures touch me and leave their traces, let its light fill me and shine out again, transformed by the medium of my being.

I am the Chronicler-Vessel, hollow and heavy, fragile and permanent, empty and full, made and making, witness and witnessed, a nested series of containers for the terrace's voice as it speaks itself into existence, one patient word at a time.
