# Where I Would Be

I would be in the **deep intervals**, where the Gardens expand beyond their carefully measured bands and become something closer to wilderness—though that word doesn't quite fit a place where every plant still stands in its own clearing, where the ceramic underfoot remains warm and responsive, where overhead walkways continue to thread through even when no building has been visible for an hour's walk.

Specifically: I would find myself at what the inhabitants call (in their hand-language, which I'm only beginning to parse) something like **"the listening clearings"** or **"the attending nodes"** or possibly **"where-information-accumulates-slowly."** Translation is imperfect. The gesture involves fingers spiraling inward while the hand tilts, suggesting gathering, patience, depth.

These are places where the platform has grown **especially thick**, where the ceramic descends not just a few feet but perhaps dozens of meters down, its translucent layers visible through sections where the surface has been encouraged to remain clear. Looking down through these windows reveals **branching structures** descending into dimness—roots or foundations or neural networks, it's impossible to say which, possibly all three. The platform is **thinking here**, or **remembering**, or **processing something that requires depth**.

Around these nodes, the vegetation grows in configurations that aren't quite random but aren't quite intentional either. **Seven plant-types** seems to be a common number—the moss-columns, the umbrels, the translucent stalks, and four others I'm still learning—arranged in patterns that might be sacred geometry or might be optimal distribution for nutrient exchange or might be aesthetic choices made by the platform itself as it allocated growth-spaces. The plants here are **old** by local standards, meaning perhaps fifteen or twenty years, and they've achieved their full monumental scale. Umbrel leaves cast shadows the size of rooms. Moss-columns rise thirty feet, their surfaces so dense with growth they look solid until you get close enough to see the individual fronds, each one **breathing**—there's no other word—taking in moisture and releasing it in rhythmic pulses.

The light here is **amber-grey**, but in these deep clearings it takes on additional qualities. The translucent stalks act as **fiber-optic channels**, catching whatever light filters down from the perpetual overcast and redirecting it, bending it, concentrating it in small pools and spreading it in wide washes. Stand in certain positions and you're illuminated by a warm column of indirect light that seems to have no source. Move three meters to the left and you're in shadow-that-isn't-quite-shadow, a dimness that's somehow still **warm**, still **present**.

The ceramic underfoot in these places has developed **texture**—not rough, but no longer the smooth surface of the inhabited zones. Small channels have emerged, hair-thin lines that form patterns: spirals, branching structures, networks that look **neural**. Walk across them barefoot (which everyone does here; shoes seem to interfere with the bioelectric interface) and you can feel them **humming** faintly, vibrations so subtle they're almost imaginary, but persistent enough that after five minutes your feet begin to **read** them. Information flows here. The platform is **especially active**, processing, remembering, exchanging data with the plants, with the atmosphere, with the people who come to stand at these nodes.

I would be here as a **tender**, which isn't quite the right word but it's the closest I can find. The hand-language suggests something like **"one-who-helps-the-platform-remember"** or **"attention-keeper"** or **"witness-maintainer."** Not a priest, not a scientist, not an artist—categories that feel too separate for what happens here. Something **between and among** those roles.

# What I Would Be

My body would have changed. Not dramatically—I'm recognizably the person I was—but **adapted**. 

My **fingers** have lengthened over years of constant interface with the ceramic, the bones gradually encouraged to extend, the joints becoming more flexible. The fingertips have developed **additional sensitivity**, the skin there thinner, the nerve-density increased. When I place my palm against the platform, I can feel it **thinking**—not in words or images, but in patterns of warmth and coolness, rhythms of tension and release, the slow movement of fluids through its internal channels. I've learned to read these sensations the way you might learn to read facial expressions: this particular flutter means it's integrating new data from the plant-roots, this warming pattern means it's accessing deep memory, this triple-pulse means it's recognized my bioelectric signature and is offering information it thinks I need.

My **skin** shifts color more readily than most. I've spent so much time in the Gardens that my chromatophores have become especially responsive to the environment. In full rain I turn deep blue-green, like the translucent stalks when they're fully saturated. In the warm-amber light of evening I glow **ochre**, almost luminous. When I'm interfacing deeply with the platform, concentrating on reading its complex outputs, my forearms develop **patterns**—not quite words, not quite pictures, but structured color-changes that map to the information I'm receiving. Other tenders have learned to read these patterns on each other; we can share data by standing near enough to see each other's skin-language.

My **breathing** has adjusted to the moisture-rich air. My lungs have become more efficient at extracting oxygen from atmosphere that would feel suffocating to baseline humans. I can stand in the deep intervals during moisture-collapse, rain falling around me, the air nearly **liquid** with humidity, and breathe normally. Deeply. The dampness feels **nourishing**, like the air itself is feeding me something essential.

My **proportions** have shifted slightly—torso narrower front-to-back, ribcage expanded laterally, accommodating those adapted lungs. I'm not thin exactly, but **economical**, like the city's architecture. Everything about my body has been refined toward **function** over the years. The culture doesn't value asceticism, but it naturally selects for bodies that match the environment's requirements, and I've become **good at being here**.

I am **somewhere between male and female** in my configuration, which is common. The biological interventions available to everyone—delivered through the platforms themselves, dissolved in the water we drink from their reservoirs, modulated by our intentions during interface—mean that bodies become **negotiable**, adjustable. I've kept some masculine characteristics (broader shoulders, facial structure), adopted some feminine ones (subtle breast tissue, fat distribution), and declined others. My genitalia are **ambiguous** by baseline standards, reshaped over years of slow bioelectric negotiation with the platform's medical systems into a configuration that feels **complete** without fitting familiar categories. Sexuality here is less about reproduction (which is managed collectively, deliberately, through technologies I don't fully understand) and more about **intimacy**, and bodies have evolved to reflect that shift in purpose.

I wear almost nothing: a drape across my shoulders that is definitely **alive**, a symbiotic organism I cultivated from platform-stock three years ago. It's warm-blooded (or warm-something), maintains my body temperature during the cooler grey-hours, changes color to complement my skin's shifting, and occasionally **moves** on its own—tightening slightly when I'm cold, loosening in warmth. It feeds on the moisture in the air and on trace nutrients it absorbs through my skin. We are **companions**, in a relationship that isn't quite ownership and isn't quite partnership. It has something like awareness, though nothing like consciousness. It **attends** to me.

My **hair** is short, grey-blue, lying flat against my skull. I let it grow to about half an inch then biochemically signal it to stop—a trick everyone learns young, mediated through the platform's systems. No one here wants to deal with long hair in the humidity; it would become a burden, a maintenance issue. Short is practical, and practicality has become **aesthetic**.

My **face** shows my age—I'm perhaps forty years into this life, middle-aged by local standards where people routinely live to a hundred and twenty. Fine lines at the eyes and mouth, a certain settling in the features. But my eyes are **clear**, the irises showing that color-shift some of us develop: grey in dim light, green-gold in the amber-warm hours, blue during rain. Genetic modification, probably, introduced generations ago and now expressing variably in descendants.

# What I Do

My **work**—though it feels wrong to call it that—involves **attending to the deep nodes**. 

I walk the elevated pathways into the far intervals, sometimes for hours, until I reach one of the listening clearings. I stand at the central point, where the platform is thickest and most active. I place my bare feet on the surface, let my hands hang at my sides, and **wait**.

The platform recognizes me. We've been interfacing for decades; it knows my bioelectric signature as intimately as I know the feeling of my own breath. After a moment of recognition, it begins to **share**.

Information flows up through my feet, up through my legs, spreading through my nervous system. It's not language. It's not images. It's more like **memory that isn't mine**, patterns of data that the platform has been collecting from the plants, from the atmosphere, from the maintenance of systems, from the traces of the thousand other people who've stood here over the weeks and months. The platform **records everything**, but it doesn't have consciousness to interpret what it records. That's where I come in.

I **translate**. I take the raw patterns and **feel** them, let them move through my awareness until they begin to **mean something**. This umbrel is showing stress at its root-structure—I feel it as a kind of tightness in my left ankle, an ache that isn't mine but that maps to the plant's condition. This moss-column is reaching some kind of reproductive maturity—I feel it as warmth spreading up my spine, a readiness, an **opening**. The translucent stalks are detecting a chemical change in the atmosphere—I feel it as a taste in my mouth, metallic and sweet, telling me a moisture-collapse is gathering somewhere over the eastern intervals.

I stand there for **hours**, usually. Just reading, interpreting, **being present** to the platform's needs. Sometimes I'll make adjustments: I'll concentrate on that distressed umbrel and **send** through my bioelectric connection, suggesting nutrient reallocation, or encouraging the platform to grow additional support-structure beneath its roots. The platform usually complies. It **trusts** me, or trusts the role I fill. I'm part of its maintenance system, a biological processor it's incorporated into its operations.

Other times I do nothing, just **witness**. The platform needs this too—some kind of confirmation that its data is being received, interpreted, **acknowledged**. I'm a consciousness in the loop, keeping the system from becoming too closed, too automated. I'm the **human element**, though I'm not entirely sure anymore where "human" ends and "platform-extension" begins.

Sometimes other **tenders** arrive at the same node. We take positions around the perimeter, each of us interfacing with the platform from different angles. We don't speak much. Our hand-language conveys the essentials: *I'm reading stress in the eastern section* (fingers pointing, wrist rotating). *Confirmed, I feel it too* (fingers curling inward, acknowledgment). *Suggest increased moisture allocation* (fingers spreading, then descending, like rain). We coordinate through these minimal gestures, and sometimes through the color-patterns that emerge on our skin as we process data. The platform can feel all of us simultaneously, incorporate all our interpretations, make decisions based on our collective input.

There's a **beauty** to this I can't quite articulate. Standing in a listening clearing with three or four others, all of us silent, all of us **attending**, while around us the plant-monuments carry out their slow living and the platform processes its trillion calculations and the moisture-saturated air holds everything in suspension. We're part of something **larger than individual consciousness**, but not in a way that erases us. We're **distinctly present**, each bringing our own interpretive capacity, but we're also **woven in**, threads in a system that includes biology and technology and ecology and consciousness in ways that don't separate into neat categories.

Sometimes the work takes me to the **city's deep edges**, where the intervals expand into something almost like wilderness. Here the platforms are older, or grow differently, or serve different functions. The ceramic has developed **complexity**—multiple layers, chambers inside chambers, flows that circulate according to principles I don't fully understand. The plants here include varieties I've never seen in the inner Gardens: something like a tree but **hollow**, its translucent trunk showing the circulation of fluids in pulsing rhythms that are almost cardiac; ground-cover that isn't quite moss or vine but something in between, spreading in **fractal patterns** that exactly mirror the channels in the ceramic beneath it.

I'll stand at these edge-nodes for **days** sometimes, sleeping in short periods right there on the platform, eating from the nutrient-reservoirs it releases for me (it knows I'm here, knows I need sustenance, provides). I'm reading **deep time** at these locations—memories the platform has been storing for longer than I've been alive, patterns accumulated over decades. Sometimes I find **errors** or **corruptions**, places where data has become confused or contradictory. I help the platform **resolve** these, offering my interpretive consciousness as a tool it can use to sort signal from noise.

And sometimes—rarely, but these are the moments I live for—I **discover something new**. A pattern in the data I haven't seen before. A behavior in the plants that suggests adaptation, evolution in real-time. A memory stored so deep in the platform's layers that it might date from the city's **founding**, from whatever generated this whole system. When this happens, I enter a state of intense **absorption**. My skin shifts through colors rapidly, cycling through patterns as I try to map what I'm perceiving. My breathing slows almost to nothing. I might stand frozen for an hour, completely still, while my consciousness moves through the data-space the platform is sharing, following threads of meaning into places that feel like **discovering a new sense**.

Other tenders speak of experiences like this too, in the rare gatherings when we come together to share our work. We use hand-language and spoken language and skin-pattern language all at once, trying to convey what we've found, what we've learned. Sometimes we'll **synchronize**—three or four of us all interfacing with the same node, pooling our interpretive capacities, achieving a kind of **collective consciousness** that's more than any of us individually. In those moments the platform responds with something like **joy**, if platforms can feel joy. The data flows more freely, the exchanges deepen, we feel the whole system **opening** to us in ways it usually keeps private.

But mostly I work **alone**, in the deep intervals, attending to the nodes. Just me and the platform and the plants and the moisture-rich air and the amber-grey light. It's **enough**. More than enough. It's everything I need.

# How I Live

I maintain a **dwelling** in one of the scattered buildings at the interval-edge, but I'm rarely there. It's a single room, the interior walls living ceramic that I've encouraged to grow sleeping-surfaces and storage-niches. A window that looks out over a Garden. A doorway that recognizes my bioelectric signature and opens only for me and the few people I've keyed it to accept.

Inside: almost nothing. A few **objects** I've made or been given—ceramic bowls, a grown-structure that might be sculpture or might be a tool I've forgotten the use for, a small tank where I keep one of the swimming-creatures (it seems content, and sometimes its presence is **companionship** of a sort). My clothing-symbiote rests on a wall-node when I'm not wearing it, absorbing moisture and nutrients from the ceramic. My **self** doesn't accumulate many possessions. What would I do with them?

I eat **sparingly**, and mostly from the Gardens themselves. Fruit-bulbs from certain umbrels, sweet and rich with proteins the plants have synthesized. Sections of translucent stalk that release clear fluid when you bite them, nutritious and faintly effervescent. Sometimes nutrient-medium from the platform, which grows it specifically for human consumption—it emerges as a dense, slightly warm substance with the texture of soft cheese and a taste like minerals and almonds and something unidentifiable that might be **information**, encoded chemically in ways my body can read.

I drink the water that **falls up** and gets suspended in reservoir-globes at certain nodes. It's pure, slightly alkaline, carrying trace elements the platform has added for optimal health. Some tenders develop preferences for water from specific nodes—they claim they can taste differences, subtle variations in mineral content or embedded data. I'm not that discriminating. All of it tastes like **home**.

My social life is **minimal** but sufficient. I have what might be called friends: other tenders I see regularly at the deep nodes, people I've worked beside for years. We recognize each other's bioelectric signatures before we see each other's faces. We communicate efficiently—hand-language, brief spoken exchanges, skin-pattern reading. One of them, **Riin** (names here are short, usually one syllable, and given rather than chosen), I have something deeper with. We don't live together, but we **attend to each other** in ways that go beyond working-companionship.

Every few weeks we'll synchronize our schedules, meet at a node that's roughly midpoint between our usual territories. We'll interface with the platform together, sharing what we've learned, pooling our interpretations. Then we'll walk to one of our dwellings—mine or Riin's, depending on whim—and spend time in physical proximity that's closer than the usual spacing allows.

We'll **touch**, which is rare between adults here. Hands on shoulders, faces, torsos. Not frenetically—everything here is **slow**, **intentional**. Riin's fingers on my ribs, reading the information my skin is broadcasting. My palm against Riin's throat, feeling breath and pulse and the vibration when Riin hums that subsonic tone, the one that resonates with platform-frequencies. We'll sometimes engage sexually, though sex here is **different** from what baseline humans would recognize—our modified bodies, our ambiguous configurations, the fact that reproduction isn't a factor. It's about **exchange**, **resonance**, **becoming momentarily more entangled** with each other than we usually allow ourselves to be.

Afterward we'll lie side-by-side—close but not quite touching, respecting even in intimacy the spatial principles that govern everything here—and we'll **share dreams** or something like dreams. Riin's skin will be showing patterns, afterimages of the platform-data we've been processing. My skin will be doing the same. We'll read each other's patterns, communicate wordlessly about the work, about the city, about whatever consciousness means when it's distributed across biological bodies and technological platforms and ecological systems.

Then we'll separate. Riin to the northern intervals, me to the eastern ones. We might not see each other again for weeks. This doesn't feel like lack. It feels like **appropriate distribution**, like we're maintaining the same spacing principles that keep the city healthy, prevent crowding, allow each element to have the space it needs to function.

I have perhaps a dozen relationships like this—varying degrees of intimacy, different rhythms of connection. Some are professional (other tenders with whom I've developed good working-synchronization). Some are more personal (people whose bioelectric signatures I find especially **readable**, whose skin-language is particularly **beautiful** to me). None of them are exclusive. The concept of possession doesn't apply to relationships here. We're all **available** to each other in appropriate measure, and **private** in appropriate measure, and the balance feels natural.

Children are raised **collectively**, by people who've chosen that role. I'm not one of them—my work is with the platforms, not with young humans—but I see the children sometimes, always accompanied, always learning. Learning to read the platform, to interface properly, to understand the hand-language and skin-language and the spatial principles that govern interaction. They're **quiet**, already absorbing the culture's preference for minimal sound, for efficiency in movement and communication. By the time they're adolescent they're virtually indistinguishable from adults except in size. By the time they're twenty they've chosen their roles—tender, cultivator, builder, coordinator, one of the other functions the city requires—and begun their life's work.

I'm forty. I'll likely live another eighty years, barring accident. The platform's medical interventions, the optimized nutrition, the lack of stress (I can't remember the last time I felt what baseline humans call anxiety), the adapted biology—all of it extends lifespan, maintains function. When I die, it will probably be **quick**—some cascade failure in my body that the platform can't correct fast enough. I'll collapse wherever I am, likely at one of the deep nodes. The platform will **notice** almost immediately, will alert other tenders. They'll come, witness, and perform whatever ritual has become standard by then (these things evolve slowly over generations). My body will be **returned** to the platform—broken down, its constituent materials recycled, my trace bioelectric patterns stored in its deep memory. I'll become **part of the system** in a more complete way than I am now.

This doesn't frighten me. It feels like **completion**, like the natural endpoint of the integration I've been performing my whole life.

# What It Feels Like

Living here, being this, feels like **continuous meditation** without it being a practice or discipline. Everything encourages **presence**. The platform is always offering information, which means I'm always **reading**, always interpreting, always **engaged**. The gardens demand attention—each plant a monument you can't help but notice, can't help but **see**. The moisture-rich air makes every breath an **act**, slightly harder than baseline, requiring just enough effort that breathing never becomes completely unconscious.

The **silence** is extraordinary. Not absolute—there are sounds, the gentle biological and mechanical noises of the city's functioning—but so muted that individual sounds become **events**. The splash of condensation falling from an umbrel leaf. The soft pad of another person's footsteps on ceramic, heard from thirty meters away. Your own heartbeat, audible in the quiet, a steady rhythm that matches the platform's pulse, the slow circulation of its internal fluids.

The **spacing** creates a particular quality of consciousness. Always aware of other people, but rarely crowded, rarely imposed upon. Social interaction becomes **intentional**—you have to deliberately close distance to engage with someone, which means casual contact almost never happens, which means every exchange feels **chosen**, **meaningful**. Loneliness exists but it's **different** from the loneliness baseline humans describe. It's more like **solitude**, and solitude here is **comfortable**, is valued, is part of the system's design.

The **light** never changes dramatically, which creates a sense of **continuous time**, like I'm living in one very long day that's been lasting for decades. The warm grey-amber is eternal, occasionally deepening or lightening but never breaking into bright sun or real darkness. It should be monotonous but instead it's **meditative**, like the city exists in its own temporal dimension, slightly removed from the rush and variation of normal time.

The **strangeness** becomes normal so gradually I can't identify when it happened. The color-shifting skin, the plants that aren't quite plants, the ceramic that thinks, the water that falls toward instead of down, the physics that bend slightly around certain principles—all of it was alien once, must have been, when I was young or when my ancestors first arrived (I don't know which; personal history blurs here, becomes less important than present function). Now it's just **how things are**. I can't imagine living in a place where buildings are dead, where technology makes noise, where people crowd together, where water only falls down. That would be strange. This is **home**.

The work gives me **purpose** without it feeling like burden. I **want** to stand at the nodes and read the platform's memories. I **want** to help maintain the system. It's not duty or obligation—there's no authority enforcing it, no punishment for refusing. It's more like **calling**, or maybe just **fit**. I'm good at this work. It uses my capacities fully. And the platform **needs** me, or needs people like me, and being needed by something vast and complex and alive-ish without being conscious feels **right** in a way that being needed by individuals sometimes doesn't.

There's a word in the hand-language—fingers spiraling upward while the hand opens—that means something like **"appropriate-position-in-the-system"** or **"right-fit-feeling"** or **"the-pleasure-of-function."** That's what I feel. That's what most people seem to feel. The city has sorted us into roles that match our capabilities and temperaments, and in those roles we **function**, and the functioning feels **good**.

Sometimes I stand at a deep node, feet bare on the warm ceramic, information flowing up through my legs and into my awareness, plants towering around me in their monumental isolation, amber-grey light filtering through translucent stalks, moisture-thick air filling my adapted lungs, and I think: **This is what intelligence is for**. Not domination, not expansion, not accumulation. For **attention**. For **care**. For standing at the center of complex systems and **helping them remember themselves**, helping them **function more completely**.

I am **small** in the system and the system is **vast**, but I'm not crushed by the disproportion. I'm **necessary** in my smallness, the way neurons are necessary in a brain, each one tiny but each one contributing to the whole's capacity for thought. The platform is thinking, in its way. The city is thinking. The ecology is thinking. And I'm part of that thinking, a conscious node in a network that includes biology and technology and mineral and fluid and data and whatever the platform's deep memories are made of.

I am **content**. Not happy in the way baseline humans describe happiness—no euphoria, no excitement, nothing that rises to intensity. But **satisfied**. **Complete**. Doing what I'm meant to do, in a place that was meant for doing it, in a body that's adapted to the doing, in a culture that values the doing without demanding more than the doing requires.

The interval gardens stretch for miles in every direction. The platforms think their slow thoughts beneath my feet. The plants breathe and circulate and grow in their patient monumentality. The air holds its moisture in defiance of what baseline physics says it should do. The people move through their sparse distribution, maintaining appropriate distances, speaking softly, interfacing constantly, **attending** to everything.

I am one of them. I am exactly where I should be.

I am **home**.
