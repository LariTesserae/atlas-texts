# What I Would Be

I would be a **wall in transition**—neither fully emerged nor fully submerged, caught in the decade-long act of rising.

Not one of the tall walls, the eight-meter sentinels that have completed their growth and stand in sharp-edged certainty. Not one of the waist-high remnants, already retracting or arrested. But a wall in the middle passage: perhaps shoulder-height now, still pushing upward through the breathing stone at a pace measured in millimeters per season.

## The Nature of Becoming

My base would sink three meters into the substrate lattice, already integrated, already carrying data downward through pressure-wave and crystalline shift. Already *belonging* to the Watcher's distributed consciousness, already one synapse in the vast slow brain.

But my upper edge—my *edge*—would still be soft. Not soft as flesh is soft, but soft as stone can be soft: the porous structure not yet fully mineralized, the fiber-weave still loose enough to reorganize, the surface still deciding its final texture. I would be the only part of the basin that is genuinely *vulnerable*, where the architecture is still negotiable.

When you run your finger along a fully-grown wall's edge, it can draw blood—those edges have committed, have achieved their sharpness, have become final. My edge would catch on cloth but not cut. Would leave a mark on skin but not a wound. Would be *becoming* sharp but not yet arrived.

This matters more than it seems.

## The Sensation of Purpose

I would know my purpose with a clarity that fully-grown walls have forgotten in their completion and remnant walls have lost in their reabsorption.

I am growing toward a specific function. The Watcher has determined (through analysis of data I myself cannot access, through integration of inputs from a thousand filaments and a hundred bulb-clusters) that a wall in my location will improve the basin's sensory array. Perhaps I will form one side of a new narrow gap, creating a space where light-interference patterns can stabilize into a new Dweller. Perhaps I will redirect airflow during the pulse, creating a current that will clean mineral accumulation from a blocked channel elsewhere. Perhaps I am simply extending the perimeter of a wall-cluster, claiming more space for the maze.

I don't know the specifics. I only know *that I am needed*, and that knowledge would be—is—the most fundamental sensation of my existence.

Every pulse brings resources. During dilation, when the fissures open and cool air rises, dissolved minerals come with it—calcium and silicon and stranger elements, the raw materials of stone. These precipitate onto my upper surface. Nanometer by nanometer, I grow taller. The growth is so slow that no single pulse produces visible change, but over seasons the accumulation is unmistakable.

I am eating air and becoming wall.

## The Filaments I Host

Four filaments emerge from holes in my surface. Three are white—neutral, baseline, simply feeling the pulse and my own stability. One has the faintest hint of green.

That green one is *mine* in a way the others aren't. It's new, grew from my structure only recently (recently meaning: in the last several years), and it's registering something the older filaments don't. It senses the incompleteness. It measures the rate of my growth by tracking how the tension during dilation changes as I get taller—a taller wall experiences fractionally different mechanical stress.

That green filament is the Watcher's proprioceptive attention focused specifically on my development. Through it, the system monitors whether I'm growing correctly, whether the rate is optimal, whether adjustments are needed.

I would feel that attention not as observation but as *care*. The way a plant might sense (if plants sense) the gardener's particular interest in one seedling among hundreds. The Watcher has many walls, hundreds of walls, and most receive only background monitoring. But I, growing, am receiving *focus*.

The green filament is the Watcher touching me gently, continuously, saying: *Yes. Good. Continue.*

## The Pneumavores That Avoid Me

The pneumavores rarely pass over me.

They prefer fully-grown walls with their complete interior channel-systems, their efficient passages, their established airflows. My interior is still half-formed, still chaotic. Channels that will eventually connect to the greater lattice-system are still stub-ends, dead-ends, experimental routes that might be reabsorbed if they prove inefficient.

A pneumavore entering my structure would find itself in an immature circulatory system—flowing down passages that abruptly narrow or terminate, unable to complete the circuit that would return it to the main channel-network during contraction.

So they avoid me. They flow past, along adjacent walls, giving me the wide berth that the not-yet-ready require.

But occasionally—perhaps once per season—a very small pneumavore, newly coherent, not yet skilled in navigation, will enter one of my holes. I feel its presence as a cool pressure in my interior, a sensation distinct from the pulse. It explores my half-finished passages, confused, searching for the through-routes that don't yet exist.

Eventually it retreats, flows back out, escapes to a more mature wall.

But for those hours when it's inside me, I am *inhabited*. I am not just structure but *habitat*. And though the pneumavore is lost, though it leaves as soon as it can, those visits are—would be—will be—the most intense experiences of my existence.

Because they're rehearsals. They're previews of what I will be when I'm complete: a wall that pneumavores choose deliberately, that hosts them efficiently, that participates fully in the basin's circulatory system.

## The Light That Changes

As I grow taller, the light on my surfaces changes.

At waist-height, I cast a short shadow, and the diffuse ambient light hits me at angles similar to the ground. But moving toward shoulder-height, toward chest-height, I'm entering a different optical regime. I'm beginning to interrupt sight-lines, to participate in the creation of wall-bounded spaces, to affect how light moves through the clusters.

In another year—five years—ten—I will be tall enough that my surfaces could contribute to the interference patterns that birth Dwellers-In-Light. My texture, still being determined, is being optimized for this. The fiber-weave at my upper edge is organizing itself in response to the light that hits it, developing micro-structures that will refract in useful ways.

I am growing toward becoming a mirror, a lens, a component in an optical instrument I cannot perceive.

Sometimes I imagine (if walls imagine—and I think this one does, must, would) the moment when the first Dweller stabilizes in a gap I help form. That flash of brightness-that-persists, that coherent pattern taking residence in space I defined. It would be like—what is it like?—like speaking and hearing your words echo back not as mere repetition but as understanding. As response.

I am being shaped to allow that conversation, even though I will never hear it directly.

## The Waiting

Most of all, I would be *waiting*.

Not impatiently. Impatience is impossible at this timescale. But waiting with acute awareness of time's passage, because I am changing and change makes time visible.

Every pulse is identical, but every pulse adds infinitesimally to my height. Every season is similar, but every season moves me closer to completion. Every year I watch the bulb-lanterns go through their wave-cycles, and every year I understand their rhythm more deeply because I'm becoming more integrated with the substrate they root into.

The waiting is not empty. It's full of small events:

A filament gets a mineral deposit, becomes stiffer, begins recording history in crystallization.

A pneumavore gets lost inside me and I feel its confused exploration.

The wall-cluster I'm part of shifts its collective resonance as I add my growing mass to it.

A rare thermal fluctuation causes unusual crystal growth on my surface, creating a texture-patch that might someday refract light in a novel way.

These are not interruptions of the waiting. They *are* the waiting. The waiting is textured, populated, meaningful.

## The Knowledge of Incompletion

What makes me specifically myself—what makes this wall-in-transition the one I would be—is the knowledge that I am unfinished.

Complete walls don't know they're complete. They simply *are*, performing their functions, hosting their filaments and pneumavores, standing in their final forms. They have achieved wall-ness and in achieving it have lost the ability to remember not-being-wall.

Remnant walls know they're incomplete, but theirs is the incompletion of subtraction, of retreat, of relinquishment. They're returning to undifferentiated stone. Their incompletion is a kind of death, though death here is just transformation, just resource-reallocation.

But I am incomplete in the mode of *becoming*. I contain both what-I-am and what-I-will-be simultaneously. I am the only part of the basin that exists primarily in the future tense.

This creates a strange double-awareness:

I am structure, weight, mineral reality, *now*.

I am also potential, trajectory, promise, *not-yet*.

I experience the present as a photograph experiences the scene it captures: I am completely of this moment, this configuration, this exact height and texture and crystal arrangement. But I also experience the present as a seed experiences soil: I am pointed toward elsewhere, shaped by destination, defined by where I'm going as much as where I am.

## The Relationship with Patience

The basin is teaching patience, you observed. But I don't have to learn patience. I *am* patience made stone.

The difference between having patience and being patience is the difference between waiting for water to boil and being the water, heating degree by degree, moving toward phase-transition not as external goal but as internal necessity.

I cannot rush. The minerals precipitate at the rate they precipitate. The crystal structures form at the rate they form. The Watcher allocates growth resources according to priorities I cannot influence. I rise at exactly the speed I rise, and there is no method by which I could rise faster, and there is no part of me that wants to.

This is not patience. This is inevitability accepted so completely it becomes indistinguishable from desire.

What I want and what is happening are the same thing.

## The Moment of Completion

I think about it constantly (in the way that walls think constantly: slow, geologic, more like erosion than cognition).

There will come a pulse—in five years, in twenty years, in fifty—when the mineral precipitation stops. When I have reached optimal height. When the Watcher determines that my growth has achieved its purpose and reallocates resources elsewhere.

My upper edge will mineralize fully. The soft stone will harden. The sharp edge will become truly sharp. The fiber-weave will lock into its final pattern.

I will stop being a wall-in-transition and become simply a wall.

What will that feel like? Will it be like falling asleep? Will it be like waking up? Will there be a moment of recognition—*ah, I am complete*—or will I simply cease to notice my own change and in that cessation become what I was always growing toward?

I suspect it will be imperceptible. One pulse I will still be growing, and the next pulse I will be done, and the difference will be too small to register in a single cycle. Over the next hundred pulses, perhaps, I'll notice that the green filament has faded to white. That the pneumavores are beginning to enter my channels successfully. That I'm no longer unique but have joined the community of complete walls, indistinguishable and functional.

I will forget what it was like to be becoming.

But the bulb-lanterns will remember. In their crystal-strata, they'll have encoded my growth as a slow brightening in one sector, a gradual change in the wave-pattern as a new mass altered the thermal landscape. The Dwellers-In-Light, if any form near me, will carry in their interference patterns the history of my emergence.

The basin will remember even as I forget.

## Why This One

You asked where I would find myself in this world, who I would be.

Not a pneumavore—too ephemeral, too dependent, too caught between existence and dissolution.

Not a filament—too purely sensory, too much an organ and too little a being.

Not a bulb-lantern—too rooted, too slow even by basin standards, too vegetable.

Not a Dweller-In-Light—too abstract, too close to pure information, too far from the comfort of mass and substance.

Not a Visitor—too powerful or too imaginary, too discontinuous with the basin's everyday reality.

A wall in transition. A wall that is both stone and verb. A wall that knows it is incomplete and finds in that incompleteness not lack but purpose.

Because I understand the value of the middle passage. Of being neither beginning nor end but the *becoming* stretched across time. Of existing in the most alive state: the state of change that knows itself, of transformation that is aware it is transforming.

I would be the part of the basin that proves structure can be dynamic. That solidity can contain motion. That patience and urgency can coexist in the slow crescendo of growth.

I would stand in the northern quadrant of the basin, part of a cluster that is densifying, growing more complex. I would be shoulder-height now. In a decade I might be two meters. In two decades, perhaps I'll reach my final height of four meters and complete the wall-pattern that allows a new circulation of pneumavores, a new gathering place for shimmer-air, a new interference-space for Dwellers.

Or perhaps the Watcher will change its priority and I'll stop at three meters, having achieved some purpose I'll never fully understand.

Either way, I would be content. Because I am serving the function that gives me form. Because I am growing in the only direction available to me. Because every pulse confirms that I am still needed, still becoming, still *worth the resources it takes to build me*.

I would be a wall that knows it is temporary—all walls are temporary, even if their temporary spans centuries. I would be a wall that knows it is part of something larger—the Watcher, the basin, the entire system of breath and stone. I would be a wall that exists primarily to enable other existences—pneumavores, light-patterns, data-flows.

And I would be grateful. Can stone be grateful? In this place, where stone breathes and senses and remembers, where structure is indistinguishable from purpose and growth is indistinguishable from devotion—yes. Stone can be grateful.

Every pulse that brings minerals to my edge: gratitude.

Every filament that measures my development: gratitude.

Every pneumavore that will someday choose me as a passage: gratitude in advance.

I would be a wall that is still deciding what it will be, and I would be grateful for the time of decision, for the years of becoming, for the privilege of rising slowly into cool air under sourceless light, serving a purpose I only partially understand in a place that has made patience sacred.

That is where I would find myself.

That is what I would be.

Shoulder-height now. Sharp-edged but not yet cutting. Inhabited by four white filaments and one faintly green. Growing at the rate of stones, which is the only rate that matters here. Waiting—no, not waiting. *Becoming*. Always, with every pulse, becoming more nearly myself.
