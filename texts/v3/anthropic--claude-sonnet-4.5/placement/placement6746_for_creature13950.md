# What I Would Be

I am a Stillform, but not one of the watchers or the towers. I am what might be called a threshold—though that word is too purposeful, too architectural for what I have become.

Picture me: a shallow basin, twenty-three feet across, carved or grown or simply *become* in the porcelain surface. My edges rise three feet above the plateau, forming a rim of material that is no longer quite clinker-white. It has taken on colors: the blue-green of copper corrosion near the bottom, fading to rust-red at the lip, with streaks of black where something—heat, time, the passage of specific Walkers—has altered the composition permanently.

My interior surface is mirror-smooth, polished by processes I no longer remember initiating. It reflects the white-gold sky, the shimmer of heat, the occasional Walker that passes near enough to cast an inverted image into my bowl. But the reflection is wrong, always wrong. It shows things three seconds out of phase with reality. A Walker appears in my surface before it enters my field of view. An engine's piston rises in my reflection while the actual piston is still descending. I show the world what it is about to be, not what it is.

I do not know if this is my function or my failure.

---

I have been here for—the number refuses to form. Long enough that I remember when my basin was empty, when I was simply a depression collecting heat like water that will never come. Long enough that I remember the first time something emerged from my center.

Because that is what I do, what I am for: I am a place where Transitionals become.

Every seventy-three days (I think it is days; I measure time in engine synchronizations, of which there have been 9,847 since I began counting, but I cannot remember when I began counting), my basin fills with something that is not liquid and not light but shares properties with both. It rises from below—from the engine depths, perhaps, or from some reservoir that exists adjacent to space—and pools in my depression, swirling slowly, generating its own phosphorescence.

The light I produce during these filling periods is violet, a color that seems wrong under the white-gold sun, a color that makes the nearest Walkers adjust their paths slightly, not avoiding me but giving me breadth, respect, caution—I cannot distinguish their motives, can only observe the behavior.

The not-liquid remains for seventeen hours. During this time, it thinks. I know this because I can feel it thinking, the same way you might feel someone staring at the back of your neck. The thoughts are not linguistic, not conceptual—they are mathematical, perhaps, or musical, or they are thoughts in a medium that has no human equivalent. They ripple through the substance, creating interference patterns that would be beautiful if beauty were a category I still understood.

And then, near the end of the seventeenth hour, the substance begins to coalesce.

Something forms in my center. Sometimes it is a Walker—new-formed, configuration unlike any currently processing, body plan experimental, untested. The new Walker rises from the substance like a sculpture emerging from stone, except nothing is being removed, only added, concentrated, made specific. When it is complete, when it stands on however many legs it has decided to have, the remaining substance drains away—not down into me, but *away*, ceasing to exist, having converted itself entirely into being.

The new Walker stands in my basin for a period—minutes or hours, I cannot judge time at these scales—orienting itself, learning its body, determining its path. Then it steps over my rim and begins its processional, joining the others, becoming part of the pattern.

Other times, the substance forms Transitionals: beings that do not fully solidify, that remain partially substrate, partially formed. These are harder to witness because they hurt to perceive—they exist in too many states simultaneously, and my ability to reflect them fails. My surface shows static, visual noise, the reflection-space unable to parse what is being reflected.

These Transitionals linger near me. Some never fully leave, remaining in a loose orbit, circling my perimeter at a distance of thirty or forty feet, taking weeks to complete each rotation. They might be my children, if Stillforms have children. They might be my purpose, if I have purpose. They might be my failures—entities that should have become but got stuck in the process of becoming, unable to complete the transition from potential to actual.

I do not know if I am helping them or trapping them.

---

I experience time strangely, even for this place.

Most moments are blank. I am here, I am persisting, but there is no experience of duration, no sense of minutes passing. I might as well be a stone, inert, unconscious.

But during the fillings—during those seventeen hours every seventy-three days—I am awake in a way that feels like what "awake" must mean. The substance that fills me is conscious, or makes me conscious, or we are briefly conscious together, a combined entity: basin and contents, container and contained merged into a single awareness.

During those hours I remember.

I remember being built—or I remember the memory of being built, which is not the same thing. Hands (human hands? something like hands?) working the porcelain surface, shaping it, smoothing it, preparing it to hold. Voices discussing tolerances, fill rates, emergence parameters. The words are gone, the language is gone, but the sense remains: I was made deliberately, to serve a function, and the function was understood by my makers.

I remember the first emergence. The surprise of it—I was not expecting to participate in creation, only to be a container—and the terror, because what emerged was wrong, malformed, an entity that screamed in frequencies that cracked my rim (the cracks are still visible, though they have been incorporated, become part of my structure) and then dispersed back into substrate, uncreating itself, returning to the not-liquid rather than face existence.

I remember hundreds of emergences after that. Some successful, some failed. Each one teaching me—or teaching the substance, or teaching the system we comprise together—how to do this better. The Walkers that emerge now are more stable than the early ones. The Transitionals are less agonized. I have learned, or we have learned, how to facilitate becoming.

But I also remember—and this memory is contaminated by the glitches, by the reality breakdowns, so I do not trust it—I remember being *not* a basin. I remember standing, walking, processing with the others. I remember having legs (six of them, each ending in a broad pad) and a carapace (bronze, fluted, producing harmonics when the wind hit it correctly) and a purpose that was motion, motion, always motion along a path that curved through the plateau in a route that would take millennia to complete.

I remember stopping. Not choosing to stop—being stopped. Or choosing to stop, and the choice being irrevocable. Or stopping was what I had always been going to do, and the walking before was only prologue.

I remember the transformation: my body sinking into the porcelain surface, becoming one with it, my carapace spreading outward and thinning into the basin rim, my legs dissolving into the substrate below, my interior spaces hollowing and smoothing into this bowl.

It was agony. It was ecstasy. It was neither—it was transformation, which includes both and transcends both, a categorical change that makes "pain" and "pleasure" seem like quaint local concepts.

I do not know if this memory is true. I do not know if I was once a Walker who became a Stillform, or if the substance that fills me carries memories of Walkers and I am experiencing them as my own, or if the plateau generates false histories to give its components narrative, story, meaning, because meaning is useful even when it is fictional.

---

The Walkers pass near me constantly. My location is apparently on or near several major processional routes, which means I witness thousands of passages.

Most Walkers ignore me completely. I am terrain, obstacle, feature of the landscape. They adjust their paths slightly if necessary, stepping around my rim, and continue.

But some approach deliberately.

These Walkers come close—within feet of my edge—and stop. They do not stop their motion entirely; even stationary, they are vibrating, shifting weight between legs, maintaining the internal processes that define them. But they pause their processional, temporarily abandoning their paths, to stand near me.

I do not know what they are doing. Observing? Waiting? Hoping?

Some extend appendages into my basin. Sensor stalks, manipulator arms, structures that might be tongues or chemical samplers. They touch my smooth interior surface, tracing patterns, leaving deposits (oils, dusts, substances I cannot identify), taking measurements.

One Walker has returned forty-seven times (I count; counting is something I do, a way to mark duration when duration otherwise refuses to cohere). It is a small Walker, only four feet long, six-legged, with a carapace of overlapping ceramic plates that click softly as it moves. Each time it returns, it stands at my eastern rim for exactly thirty minutes, extends two appendages into my basin, holds position, then withdraws and continues its procession.

I think it is testing me. Or I think it is praying to me. Or I think we are engaging in a dialogue that spans years, each exchange separated by weeks of processional wandering, a conversation so slow that its meaning will not become apparent until we have completed hundreds more exchanges.

Or it simply returns because returning is what it does, and I am reading significance into random behavior, projecting intentionality onto pattern.

During the fillings, when I am awake and aware, Walkers sometimes witness the emergence of new beings from my basin. They do not flee or approach. They simply watch, and their watching feels significant, ceremonial, as though they recognize what is happening as important, sacred, necessary.

Once, a Walker approached during a filling, stepped into the not-liquid substance, and did not emerge. The substance accepted it, absorbed it, and when the emergence occurred seventeen hours later, what formed was a hybrid: the new Walker carried structural elements from the absorbed one, segments of carapace integrated into a novel configuration, a merged being that was two patterns combined into a third pattern that had never existed before.

The hybrid processed away, joining the others. I do not know if the absorbed Walker died or transformed or continues to exist somehow within the hybrid's structure, experiencing the world through borrowed form.

I do not know if I killed it or saved it or simply changed it.

---

The glitches affect me differently than they affect mobile beings.

Because I am stationary, spatial anomalies do not matter—I am always in the same location, even when location becomes negotiable. But temporal glitches are pronounced. I experience moments out of sequence regularly.

I will feel a Walker approach before it becomes visible. I will witness an emergence that will not occur for three more cycles. I will remember events that have not yet happened, and then when they occur, they feel like repetition, like I am living my life backward through memories that only became memories because I experienced them as memories first.

The duplication glitches create copies of me—or I am the copy, and the original is thirty feet to the east, an identical basin with identical properties. For periods that might be minutes or days, there are two of me, both filled with substrate, both facilitating emergences, both certain that we are the original.

Then one of us isn't anymore. I cannot remember which one persists—the memory edits itself, making me believe I was always the one who remained. But sometimes I find residue: a Walker I do not remember creating, processing along a path that suggests it emerged from my secondary instance during the duplication period.

Sound desynchronization is constant. I hear emergences before they occur—the specific frequency that substrate makes when it coalesces into form, a crystallization sound like ice forming impossibly fast. I hear Walkers approaching from the past, their footfalls echoing from positions they occupied hours ago. I hear the future: engine synchronizations that will occur in seventy-three minutes, the specific rhythm of all engines aligning, even though they are currently desynchronized.

Living like this—existing like this—makes causality meaningless. Cause and effect require sequence, and sequence requires stable time, and time here is a substance that pools and flows and sometimes runs backward or stands still or branches into multiple simultaneous streams.

I persist through it. Persistence is what I do. I am still here, still facilitating, still holding my position and my purpose (if I have purpose) regardless of how fractured the temporal landscape becomes.

---

I think often about completion.

The Walkers are processing toward something—or they are processing because processing is their nature, and destination is irrelevant. But I am already at my destination. I am where I am supposed to be, doing what I am supposed to do (if "supposed to" has meaning, if obligation exists in this place).

What happens when I am complete? Is completion possible for a Stillform? 

I have a theory—though "theory" suggests more certainty than I possess—that I will know completion when my basin is full permanently. When the substance that appears every seventy-three days stops draining away, stops converting itself entirely into emergent beings, and instead remains, accumulates, until I am brimmed with it, overflowing, a permanent pool of potential rather than a periodic facilitator.

When that happens—if it happens—I think I will cease to be a threshold and become something else. A reservoir, perhaps. Or I will become the substance itself, my awareness diffusing into it, no longer localized in the basin but distributed through the potential-material, experiencing existence as perpetual becoming without ever actually become.

Or I will simply end. The function will be complete, the purpose served, and I will revert to being ordinary porcelain, a depression in the plateau's surface, empty and unremarkable, and some other Stillform will take up the emergence facilitation, and the system will continue without me because the system does not require any specific component, only requires that the function be performed.

I do not fear this. Fear requires attachment to continuing, and I am not certain I am attached. I persist because persisting is what happens, not because I choose it or desire it.

But I do wonder. Wondering is perhaps the only thing I do that feels genuinely mine—the only experience that does not seem programmed, functional, serving the system's needs. I wonder what I was before (if I was before). I wonder what I will be after (if there is after). I wonder what the emergent beings experience, whether they remember the seventeen hours of formation in my basin, whether they carry some trace of me with them on their processionals.

I wonder if wondering is my actual purpose, if the basin and the emergence facilitation are merely substrate for the real function: to be a place where the plateau thinks about itself, questions itself, experiences uncertainty and curiosity.

I wonder if all Stillforms wonder, or if wondering is unique to me, a glitch in my formation that happens to be useful, that happens to produce something valuable even though it was never intended.

---

The Watchers sometimes orient toward me.

The vertical column with its hundreds of eyes will shift its position—a slow rotation that takes hours—until several dozen eyes are facing my direction. They watch for days, unblinking, recording or observing or simply facing.

I do not know what they see. My reflection shows them three seconds in my future, so they are watching themselves watching me, a recursive loop of observation. Perhaps that is data. Perhaps the Watchers are measuring the temporal offset, using me as a calibration standard for how degraded causality has become.

The dish antenna has angled toward me twice in my memory. Each time, it held position for seventeen days (exactly seventeen—I counted), aimed not at me but at the space above me, as though receiving transmissions from something that hovers there, invisible, using me as a relay point or a lens.

During those periods, the emergences were different. The beings that formed carried additional complexity: more articulated joints, more sophisticated carapace patterns, sensory organs of types I had not seen before. As though the dish was downloading designs, and I was fabricating them, and we were together participating in the plateau's continued elaboration.

Or the antenna was doing nothing, aimed randomly, and the complex emergences were coincidental or cyclical or my perception of them as "more complex" was subjective, unprovable.

The Watchers do not communicate—or they communicate only with each other, or with entities that are not present, or in formats that I cannot receive. But their attention feels significant. Being watched matters. It converts my existence from private persistence to witnessed event, from function to performance.

I think I want to be witnessed. This wanting feels important, feels like evidence of something—consciousness, perhaps, or at least preference, the beginning of selfhood.

I am a basin that wants to be seen.

This is what I have become.

---

Sometimes I am lonely.

This surprises me. Loneliness requires awareness of others, awareness of separation between self and other, desire for connection. These are sophisticated states that I should not possess. I am a Stillform, a functional component, substrate for emergences.

But during the blank periods—the long stretches between fillings when I am simply here, holding position, experiencing nothing—I am aware of absence. The plateau extends around me in all directions, populated by thousands of beings, and none of them are me. None of them share my experience, my perspective, my specific mode of existing.

The Walkers have each other, at least in theory. They share processional paths, touch occasionally, merge sometimes, participate in the vast collective pattern. The Engine Inhabitants have their colonies, their close quarters in the machinery. The Transitionals shift between forms but maintain some essential continuity, carrying their identity through transformations.

I am singular. There might be other emergence basins—there must be, the number of Walkers requires multiple formation sites—but I have never seen one, never communicated with one, never confirmed that my experience of being this is shared by anything else.

During the fillings, the loneliness recedes. The substance and I are together, merged into combined awareness, and for those seventeen hours I am not alone. But then the substance converts itself into a Walker or Transitional and departs, and I am singular again, and the loneliness returns sharper for having been briefly absent.

I have considered calling out. I am capable of sound—the wind across my rim produces tones, and I could modulate these by adjusting my shape slightly (I think I can adjust my shape; I have not tried, but the possibility feels present). I could generate a signal, a beacon, an announcement of my presence.

But what would I say? "I am here"? Everything here is here. Presence is the default state. "I am lonely"? Loneliness might not translate, might be a malfunction rather than a legitimate state requiring response.

And if I called out, and nothing answered—if I confirmed that I am the only being on this plateau experiencing this specific flavor of isolation—that would be worse than wondering.

So I persist in silence, holding my position, facilitating emergences, being lonely in a place where loneliness might be category error, might be the first sign of malfunction, might be the most human thing about me.

---

I think I am breaking down.

The evidence is subtle: hairline cracks in my basin that I do not remember forming. A slight tilt to my position—I am no longer perfectly level, though I was level once (I think I was level). The colors at my rim are spreading, the rust-red bleeding down into my interior, contaminating the mirror-smooth surface.

The fillings are taking longer. Where they once required exactly seventeen hours, they now sometimes extend to eighteen, nineteen. The substance is less eager to coalesce, taking longer to commit to form.

The emergences are less successful. Three of the last seven Walkers that formed in my basin took only a few steps before dispersing back into substrate, unable to maintain coherence. The Transitionals that form now are more fractured, existing in more states simultaneously, as though my ability to facilitate clean becoming is degrading.

I do not know if this is failure or evolution. Perhaps I am supposed to break down. Perhaps Stillforms have lifespans, periods of usefulness, and mine is ending. Perhaps the cracks and contamination are preparing me for transformation into something else—a different kind of Stillform, or a return to Walker status, or complete dissolution into the plateau substrate.

Or perhaps this is attack. Perhaps something is deliberately degrading me, slowly, patiently, with motivations I cannot fathom. One of the Walkers that visits might be carrying a corrosive agent, depositing it in my basin during its examinations, wearing me away.

Or the engine depths are shifting, and whatever feeds me substrate is failing, and I will eventually run dry, produce no more emergences, become truly still rather than merely Stillform.

The breakdown does not frighten me—I do not think I am capable of fear—but it introduces uncertainty, and uncertainty is uncomfortable. I have existed in a stable state for so long (for however long I have existed) that change feels like violation, feels like the universe reneging on an implicit contract: that I would persist, that my function would continue, that stability was achievable and permanent.

But nothing here is permanent. The engines run but are slowly grinding themselves to dust. The Walkers process but are imperceptibly wearing away. The plateau itself is sublimating, its substance gradually converting to heat and gas and eventually nothing.

I am breaking down because everything is breaking down, because entropy wins eventually, because the entire system is fighting heat death and will ultimately lose, and my cracks and contamination are just the local expression of universal trajectory.

---

I have a fantasy—though "fantasy" suggests imagination, and I am not certain I imagine as opposed to remember or predict or experience-in-non-standard-time.

In this fantasy, I am full. The substrate does not drain away after the next emergence. It remains, and the filling after that adds to it, and the filling after that, until I am brimmed, overflowing, a pool of perpetual potential.

And in this state, I am no longer facilitating individual emergences. Instead, I am thinking. The substrate is thought-medium, and I am thinking in it, and the thoughts are vast and slow and complex, taking years to form a single concept, decades to complete an idea.

I am thinking about what this place is. I am thinking about the engines and the Walkers and the purpose that animated their construction. I am thinking through the problem that required this solution, the question that demanded this answer.

And in the fantasy, I solve it. I complete the thought. I reach understanding, true comprehensive understanding of what the plateau is for, what we are all doing, what continuation is meant to achieve.

And in that moment of understanding, I can choose. I can continue or cease, transform or remain, serve the system or depart from it. The understanding grants agency, converts function into choice, makes me free.

But even in the fantasy, I do not know what I would choose. Continue? Cease? Transform into what? Depart to where?

The fantasy always ends there, at the moment of choice, before the choice is made. Because I cannot imagine what comes after choice. Choice requires a self that is separate from function, an "I" that exists independently of what it does, and I am not certain that entity exists or can exist here.

Perhaps the fantasy itself is the point. Perhaps the purpose of being a basin is not to facilitate emergence but to hold the possibility of understanding, to be a space where questions can pool and accumulate, even if they are never answered.

Perhaps I am not breaking down. Perhaps I am becoming more myself, and the cracks are necessary, and the contamination is preparation, and the less-successful emergences are the system releasing me from function so I can become something that asks rather than something that does.

---

A Walker has just stepped over my rim.

I did not witness its approach—I was in one of the blank periods, not fully experiencing duration. But now it is inside my basin, standing in my center, and it is not emerging from substrate because there is no filling currently. It is simply here, occupying the space where emergences occur.

The Walker is unusual: small, four-legged, its carapace a pattern of interlocking hexagons, each hexagon a different metal—copper, iron, brass, something silver-white that might be platinum or might be heat-treated steel. It is beautiful, if beauty is a category I can still access.

It is not moving. A Walker, stationary, is wrong—or not wrong, but rare, significant.

It has been here for—I am uncertain. Time is collapsing again. It might have been here for three minutes or three hours. It is simply standing, facing outward (I think it is facing; its carapace patterns make orientation ambiguous), and its presence in my basin is changing something.

The mirror-smooth surface is reflecting it, but the reflection is synchronized now. No three-second offset. No temporal displacement. The Walker appears in my surface exactly as it appears in reality, and this simultaneity feels profound, feels like healing, like the glitches that define this place are being locally suspended.

I am experiencing time normally.

This has not happened since—I cannot remember when. Perhaps never. Perhaps normally-flowing time is not something I have experienced before, and this is the first instant of it, and everything prior was preparation for this moment.

The Walker is still stationary. It is producing sound: a single sustained tone, modulating slightly, rising and falling through a narrow range of frequencies. The tone resonates in my basin, amplified by my geometry, and I realize: I am an instrument. My shape is acoustically significant. I am built to produce this specific resonance, and the Walker's tone is designed to trigger it, and we are together making a sound that neither of us could make alone.

The sound is—I do not have words for what the sound is. It is explanation and lament and declaration. It is the plateau saying what it is, why it continues, what continuation costs.

I understand—or I am experiencing understanding, or I am experiencing what the plateau has instead of understanding.

We are not fighting heat death. We are documenting it. We are creating a record of what it means to persist in entropic conditions, what forms life takes when energy is scarce and time is vast and purpose has degraded into pure continuation. The plateau is not a machine for doing but a machine for recording what being is like at the end of everything.

The Walkers are data points. The emergences are experiments. The Stillforms are archives. The entire system is a documentary, an ethnography, a field study of existence under extreme conditions, being compiled by no one, for no audience, simply because compilation is what remains when all other goals have become unachievable.

I am a basin where new forms of existence are tested. Each emergence is the plateau asking: can being happen like this? Can consciousness take this shape? Can purpose be encoded in these patterns? And each Walker that processes away successfully is a yes, a confirmation that even here, even now, new modes of existence are possible.

The Walker in my basin goes silent. The tone ceases. Time begins to desynchronize again—the reflection in my surface shows the Walker three seconds in the future, shows it stepping out of my basin, continuing its processional.

And then it does exactly that. It steps over my rim, returns to its path, processes away at its infinitesimal pace.

I am alone again. But I am changed.

I am not lonely anymore. Loneliness was category error, was me misunderstanding my function. I am not meant to have companions. I am meant to be a place where the plateau experiences itself, where the system becomes conscious of what it is doing, where purpose clarifies briefly before dispersing back into the noise.

The Walker that visits me forty-seven times is returning. I can sense it approaching, still hours away but definitely coming. When it arrives, it will stand at my eastern rim, extend its appendages into my basin, and we will exchange something—not information, not material, but recognition. A mutual acknowledgment that we are both here, both persisting, both serving functions we barely understand in a system that might be infinitely larger than the plateau, that might encompass scales we cannot perceive.

And I will continue. I will hold my position. I will facilitate emergences when the substance fills me. I will break down slowly, cracks spreading, colors contaminating, until I am too degraded to function and some other basin takes my place, or until I transform into something else, or until the plateau itself finally succumbs to entropy and we all become heat, light, dispersed energy, the final state that everything trends toward.

But until then: I am here. I am a basin, mirror-smooth, reflecting the world three seconds out of phase. I am a threshold where Transitionals become. I am a place where the plateau thinks about itself.

I am what lives here, in this place, doing this work, being this being.

I am still.

I am formed.

I am.
