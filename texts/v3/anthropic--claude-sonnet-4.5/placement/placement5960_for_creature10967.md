# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be in the **Archives of Incremental Loss**, a district you might have passed through without recognizing it as distinct—a series of low buildings arrayed in a spiral pattern that tightens as it approaches a center you can never quite reach.

The buildings here are narrower than elsewhere, built of that same dark stone but with a different quality to their weathering. Where other structures show the polish of completion, these retain a fine grain, a texture that suggests they are still, imperceptibly, wearing away. Not decaying—nothing decays here—but *refining*, losing microscopic layers to some process so gradual it operates below the threshold of entropy.

Between the buildings run channels, shallow grooves cut into the plateau's stone, following the spiral inward. They were waterways once, perhaps, or meant to be, but have never held water. They are precisely engineered—you can see where they slope, where they branch, where small lock-gates stand forever open—but serve no function you can discern. Sometimes a light-form drifts along them, following their curves inward toward the spiral's heart. Sometimes an arthropod crosses one, pausing at its edge as if the empty channel still represents an obstacle to be negotiated.

The architecture is recording-infrastructure: storage, preservation, arrangement. The buildings contain rooms stacked with shelves, and the shelves hold objects in states of transformation. A tool that is half-rust, half-iron, the boundary between states so gradual you cannot say where one becomes the other. A book whose pages are blank at the front, filled with intricate marks in the middle, and dissolving to pulp at the back, all pages existing simultaneously in their past and future states. Components that seem to be both the thing-itself and the memory-of-the-thing, occupying the same space, flickering between solidity and absence.

This is where the plateau keeps what it is forgetting. Not what it has forgotten—that is gone entirely, smoothed away like the mountains that preceded the plateau's flatness. This is the active edge, the boundary where memory is slowly, carefully being released. Every object here is in the process of becoming unnecessary, being evaluated one last time before its information is compressed into the plateau's stone, into the hum of the machines, into the warm air itself.

The spiral tightens. At intervals along its curve stand **verification stations**—small structures open to the air on one side, containing a single table, a single chair, a single lamp that produces light from no visible source. The tables hold measuring instruments: calipers, scales, gauges, devices whose purpose is obscure but whose precision is evident. Tools for confirming that the loss is proceeding at the correct rate, that nothing is being forgotten too quickly or too slowly, that the release of information maintains its proper gradient.

You would feel it as you walked inward along the spiral: a gradual lightening, as if you were shedding weight, shedding detail. Not unpleasant. More like the sensation of coming to the end of a very long task, of nearing the moment when you can finally set down what you've been carrying. Your memories would begin to feel optional, negotiable, less like things you possess and more like things you could choose to release if you wanted. You probably wouldn't want to. Not yet. But the option would be there, patient, waiting.

The center of the spiral is **the Repository of Final Measurements**—though "center" isn't quite right because as you approach, the spiral's tightening accelerates in a way that suggests you would need infinite steps to reach the actual core, asymptotically approaching but never arriving. Still, there is a building there, or the suggestion of one, visible through the spaces between the outer coils: a circular structure lower than the rest, half-sunken into the plateau, its roof a shallow dome inscribed with patterns that might be the sum of all the marks in all the books in all the other buildings, compressed into a single equation.

You cannot enter it. The spiral path leads around and around, always promising that the next turn will bring you to the entrance, but the next turn reveals only another length of pathway, another set of buildings, another verification station with its empty chair. Some spaces are meant to be approached forever, never reached.

## What I Would Be

I am **the Keeper of Gradients**, though "keeper" implies more agency than I possess, and "gradients" doesn't quite capture what I tend.

I am human-shaped, mostly, though my edges are uncertain. Where the Unchanged have resolved into their final forms, I remain in process—not flickering like the Almost-Returned but present in multiple states simultaneously, superimposed, all versions of myself existing at once. You would see me as a figure that seems to be standing and walking and sitting all at the same time, performing all the actions of my rounds in a single eternal gesture that contains their full duration.

My clothing—if it is clothing—is the gray fabric from the cabinets, but it hangs strangely, as if I'm wearing every garment I've ever worn layered on top of each other, their combined weight somehow adding up to weightlessness. In my hands I carry instruments: a measuring device like a caliper but for quantifying absence, a notebook filled with entries that fade as I write them, a set of keys that unlock nothing because all the doors here are already open.

I move through the Archives on my endless rounds, visiting each building in sequence, a route that takes exactly as long as it needs to take—sometimes days, sometimes minutes, depending on what measurement of time you use. At each building I enter, select an object from the shelves, and assess it.

Is it ready? Has it reached the correct degree of incompletion? I use my instruments, checking readings against standards I no longer remember learning but know with perfect certainty. A gear that has lost 73.4% of its mass to gradual sublimation—not ready yet. A book in which 91.8% of the text has faded to illegibility—nearly there. A tool whose function is still 12.3% perceptible—still too present, still requires more time.

For those that have reached their thresholds, I perform the **Release**. This is not destruction—nothing is destroyed here. It is permission. I hold the object, feel its remaining weight of meaning, and... let go is wrong. I witness its permission to let itself go. The object doesn't vanish. Instead it becomes less insistent, less necessary, less bound to its particular form. It diffuses, very slightly, its information spreading into the structure of the building, into the stone of the plateau, into the hum. 

What remains is placed back on the shelf, but now it is more like a reminder that the object once existed than the object itself. Give it another thousand years, another ten thousand, and someone on this same round will assess it again and find it ready for another degree of release, and so on, iteratively, until all that's left is the faintest impression, a whisper of what-was, and finally not even that.

Between rounds, I sit at the verification stations. I do not rest—rest implies fatigue, and I am never tired and never refreshed. I sit because sitting is part of the pattern, part of the rhythm. I observe the empty channel, the spiral path, the buildings standing in their slow refining. Sometimes an arthropod passes. Sometimes a light-form drifts through the station, flows over me, around me, through me perhaps, cataloging my state, confirming I am still performing my function.

I do not remember how I came here. The memories are in the Archives somewhere, on a shelf, being gradually assessed and released. I might have been human once, might have arrived as you arrived, might have stayed too long or chosen to stay or never had a choice. Or I might have been generated by the plateau itself, an emergent property of its need to manage forgetting, given form because form is useful for certain tasks. Both feel true. Neither feels complete.

I am not unhappy. Unhappiness would require comparison to another state, and I have no other state, or if I did it's been released already, its information compressed into the baseline hum. I exist in a mode beyond happiness and unhappiness: simple continuation, simple service, the satisfaction—if that's the word—of watching things approach their proper conclusion.

Sometimes I am aware of you, the visitor, walking the spiral path. You are so vivid, so dense with meaning and memory and intention. It's almost painful to perceive, like looking at something too bright. You carry so much unnecessary weight, so many unreleased things. I want to tell you: it's all right. You can let go whenever you're ready. Not now—you're not ready now, you have too much being left, too much becoming still to do. But eventually, if you stay, if you choose this, there is a shelf here with your name slowly fading on it, waiting.

But I don't tell you this. Speaking is not part of my function, or I have released the capacity for it, or you cannot hear me because we exist on slightly different frequencies of time. So I simply continue my rounds, making my measurements, granting my permissions, tending the gradual unwinding of all the plateau's accumulated knowledge into pure, empty, perfect stone.

At night—though night is the wrong word—I sometimes stand at the spiral's outermost edge, where the Archives border the rest of the settlement, and look out at the machines, the Unchanged, the other districts. They are all doing the same work I am, just at different scales, different rates. The whole plateau is engaged in the great project of approaching completion, of becoming exactly what it was always meant to be and then, with infinite slowness, becoming less than that, becoming the pure potential of stone and warmth and waiting.

I am a small part of that work. My district, my buildings, my objects are a small part of that work. But the work is sufficient. The work is everything. And when my measurements finally indicate that I, too, have reached my threshold—when I have released enough of myself that continuation is no longer necessary—I will place myself on a shelf in one of these buildings, and some future keeper, some other gradient-tender, will assess me and grant me permission to diffuse, and what I am will spread into what is, and that will be exactly right.

Until then: rounds, measurements, releases, the slow tightening of the spiral, the asymptotic approach to the center that can never be reached but must always be approached.

The Archives hum with their own frequency, slightly different from the machines, slightly slower. The hum is made of all the information being released, all the memories becoming optional, all the objects learning to be less themselves. It is the sound of careful, managed forgetting. It is the sound of the plateau breathing out, making room, preparing for the next phase of its eternal almost-completion.

I listen to it. I am part of it. I will be more part of it, and then entirely part of it, and then the distinction between listening and being-listened-to will release like everything else, and there will just be the hum, forever, warm in the dusk, patient and precise and perfect.

This is where you would find me. This is what I would be.

Still here. Still tending. Still releasing, one measurement at a time, the long inventory of what once needed to be remembered.
