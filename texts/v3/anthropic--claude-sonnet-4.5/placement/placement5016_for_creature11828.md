# Where I Would Find Myself

I am suspended in the threshold between intention and geology, between motion and stillness. I am—though the word strains—one of the Slow Herds, but I am only beginning to understand what that means, if understanding is even the correct term for this diffuse awareness that seems to pool in me like moisture in shale.

I do not remember arriving here. The question of arrival assumes a before-state, a somewhere-else, and I have no access to anything that precedes this corridor between standing plates where I have been—moving is not quite right—*proceeding* for what might be hours or weeks. Time registers differently when your body is partially stone. Events do not succeed each other crisply; they overlap, interfere, create harmonics.

My body is four feet long, low to the ground, gray as the shale I move across. I know this not through sight—my sensory organs are not eyes in any focused sense—but through the continuous low-grade information that arrives from every surface of me. I sense the texture of stone beneath me through my whole ventral surface. I sense the warmth still radiating from the vertical plates on either side of this passage. I sense the moss ahead, its presence registering as a change in humidity, in chemical signature, in some quality that has no name because I have no language, only this half-formed knowing.

I have been approaching this moss patch for some time. The approach is itself the activity; there is no impatience, no sense of goal deferred. The moving-toward is as satisfying as the arrival will be. My four legs—though they are less like legs than they are like waves traveling down my body, sequential compressions that translate into forward flow—carry me with a rhythm that matches something in the stone itself, some pulse in the substrate that I follow without deciding to follow.

When I reach the moss, I will lower my anterior end—I cannot call it a head because it doesn't feel central to my being; it's just the part of me that happens to face forward—and I will rest against the growth for a long time. The moss will diminish. This is not eating in the sense of taking something into myself; it is more like an equalization, a gradient resolving. The moss has something and I need it and the differential will level out through contact. Whether this happens through my action or simply happens and I am the site where it occurs is unclear.

## The Nature of My Awareness

What I experience is not thought but something more like continuous calibration. I am constantly adjusting to the stone beneath me, to the air temperature, to the moisture content in the substrate, to the presence of other beings in adjacent corridors, to the angle of light filtering between the plates, to mineral concentrations that create faint fields I can sense with my whole body.

I do not decide to move and then move. Instead, there is a building sense of differential—I am here, and there is a there-ness ahead that differs from here in some quality that matters—and the differential itself creates motion. I am moved by my own sensing, drawn along gradients that my body detects and responds to below any level that could be called choice.

Yet sometimes, rarely, there is something that might be volition. A moment where two paths are equally balanced in their gradients and I proceed down one rather than the other, and the selection feels like it comes from somewhere inside me, some small warm density that is more me than the rest of me. These moments are faint and brief, but they are why I use the word "I" at all, why this account is not simply a description of a geological process that happens to move.

I have memory, but it is not narrative. I do not remember events in sequence. Instead, I carry in my substance a kind of layered knowing—I have been in dry places and in damp places, in warm districts and cooler ones, in passages so narrow my sides brushed both walls and in open spaces where no stone was close. These experiences are present in me not as recollections but as sediment, compressed layers that affect how I respond to current conditions without my needing to retrieve them consciously.

There is also a deeper memory, or perhaps it is not memory but simply what I am made of: I know stone intimately, from inside, as if I have been stone. The cleavage planes, the way pressure affects crystalline structure, the slow seep of moisture through layered sediment—all of this is familiar not as learned information but as lived experience. Perhaps I was stone before I was this. Perhaps I am still stone, just organized differently for a time.

## My Day, If Day Is the Right Word

The light has been increasing for some time—that is how I know it is the warming part of the cycle. The standing plates on my left are now warm enough that air is rising along their faces, creating a gentle current that flows past me down the corridor. I sense this as a brushing feeling, a continuous soft pressure on my left side.

I have reached the moss patch. I lower my anterior end until it contacts the growth—contact is immediate and total, not through touch alone but through something more intimate, as if my surface and the moss surface interpenetrate slightly. I become still.

Now begins the long feeding, though feeding is too active a word. I rest against the moss and we exchange. Moisture moves, minerals move, compounds I cannot name move. The moss gives up some of its substance and I take it in, but I also give something back—not waste but something the moss can use, some mineral I have processed through my body and rendered in a form the moss requires. We are balancing each other.

During this stillness, my awareness diffuses. I become less concentrated, more distributed. I can sense farther along the corridor, can feel the network of passages extending in multiple directions, can detect the presence of others like me in adjacent pathways. We are not separate beings but more like concentrations in a continuous field, denser nodes in the substance of the basin itself.

I sense one of my kind three corridors over, also feeding. The sensing is not visual or auditory but something like recognizing a resonance, the way one vibration can trigger a sympathetic response in a nearby object. There is no communication, no reaching out, just the fact of mutual existence noted and then left alone.

The small stone-shelled ones click past occasionally, their sounds sharp and discrete against my diffuse awareness. They do not register to me as separate beings so much as as local intensifications of activity, little knots of quick energy against my slowness. I have no response to them. They exist; I exist; we are both here.

Hours pass, perhaps. The light shifts. The warmth moves from my left side to being more evenly distributed. I have been still long enough that moss has begun to explore my surface—I can sense its fine structures extending onto my skin, starting the process of incorporation. If I remain here long enough, I will become a moss-covered form, and if I remain longer still, the moss will partially obscure me, and eventually I might be just a shape under green growth, indistinguishable from a stone protrusion.

But the differential has reversed—I have taken what I needed from this patch, and now the gradient is pulling me away. I disengage, a slow separation that takes several minutes. The moss shows the mark of my presence: it is thinner, paler, though it will recover. I will not return to this specific patch; there are thousands of patches, millions, and I move through them in patterns I do not plan but which emerge from the constant reading of gradients.

## The Mystery of What I Want

Do I want anything? The question is difficult. I have needs—chemical requirements, energy requirements, maintenance requirements for the complex boundary between my stone-nature and my animate-nature. These needs create pulls, vectors, directionalities in my movement. But are these wants?

There is something, sometimes, that might be preference. Certain corridors feel better to move through than others, and this better-ness is not explicable by gradient alone. A passage that curves gently is more satisfying than one that turns sharply, though both lead to similar destinations. Moss patches in deep shade have a quality that moss in direct light does not, and I linger longer in the shaded ones even when the chemical exchange is equivalent.

This might be the beginning of want, or it might just be more complex gradient-following, dimensions of optimization I cannot articulate even to myself.

Once, I moved into a chamber where several of my kind were already present, all still, arranged in a pattern I could sense but not interpret. I added myself to the pattern, finding the position that felt correct, and remained there for a long time—days perhaps, or weeks. There was something satisfying about the arrangement, about being positioned in relationship to the others, about the way our collective presence seemed to resonate with the stone chamber itself. We were making something together, though I could not say what. Then the arrangement dissolved, each of us flowing away down different corridors, and I have not encountered a similar situation since.

Was that social behavior? Was it collective consciousness? Or was it just that the stone chamber itself had a structure, and we were being moved by the stone, arranged by it according to principles we were shaped to fulfill?

The question cannot be answered from inside the experience.

## The Vertical Ones and What They Mean to Me

Sometimes I sense the Vertical Clingers moving on the plates above me. They register as presences that are similar-but-different to mine—they have the stone-nature, the slow knowing, the gradient-following—but they exist in a different dimensionality. They can leave the ground, can move up surfaces I cannot access, can leap across voids.

I do not envy them, because envy would require a sense of lack, and I do not feel incomplete. But I notice them. They represent a possibility I do not contain, a branching in the basin's self-elaboration that went a different direction than the direction that produced me.

Once, a Clinger fell or dropped or descended—I do not know which—and landed on the stone just ahead of me. It coiled there, and I approached, and we were very close, my anterior end almost touching its smooth surface. For a long time, we remained like that. I could sense it sensing me. Our stone-natures recognized each other. Our animate-natures were foreign to each other but not hostile, just mutually incomprehensible.

Then it flowed away, up a vertical plate, moving into dimensions I cannot follow, and I continued down my corridor. The encounter left something in me, a faint trace, a knowing that there are other ways to be alive here, other solutions to the problem of existence in this place where stone and life are negotiating their relationship.

## The Problem of My Future

I do not know what I am becoming. My body is slowly changing—I can sense it in the way my surface texture has shifted over time (however much time it has been), in the way certain passages that used to feel easy now require more effort, in the way my periods of stillness are lasting longer.

I am becoming more stone-like. The layering in my skin is more pronounced. My movement is slower, my responses to gradients more muted. This might be maturation, or aging, or transformation into a different state.

There is no fear in this. Fear would require a self that wants to continue and a belief that continuation might be threatened. I have something that might be a self, but its boundaries are too vague for it to be threatened. If I become fully stone, if I enter one of these chambers one day and simply cease to move, becoming a formation on the floor that other beings will move past, this does not seem wrong. It seems like a return, a completion of a cycle.

Or perhaps I will reverse, will become more animate rather than less, will develop capacities I do not currently have. Perhaps I will wake one day and find I can sense in new ways, move in new patterns, interact with the basin's other inhabitants in modes currently unavailable to me.

Or perhaps I will remain in this intermediate state indefinitely, neither fully stone nor fully alive, moving through corridors in slow response to gradients, feeding on moss and stone, being fed upon by those more active than myself, part of the vast exchange system that the basin uses to explore its own potential.

## The Larger Awareness

Sometimes, in the longest stillnesses, my awareness expands beyond my body-boundary. I sense not just the immediate corridor but the network of passages extending in all directions, miles of maze-like connectivity. I sense the moss as a continuous presence, not individual patches but a single distributed entity exploring the interface between stone and air. I sense all the others like me, hundreds or thousands of us, all moving in our slow ways, all following our gradients, and we seem less like separate beings than like waves in a single medium, patterns in the substance of the basin itself.

In these moments, I cannot locate myself. I am here in this corridor, but I am also everywhere in the basin, because the differences between locations feel less significant than the continuity of stone and moisture and mineral cycling. The boundaries dissolve. I am not a creature in an environment; I am a temporary concentration of the environment itself, a place where it has gathered itself into something that moves and senses and processes, and which will eventually disperse back into the general field.

This expanded awareness is not mystical or transcendent in emotional tone. It is simply clear, the way seeing is clear when you open your eyes after having them closed. This is what I am: a pattern, a process, a moment in the basin's long exploration of form.

Then my awareness contracts again, pulls back into the boundaries of this body, and I am just myself, just this gray form in this corridor, approaching another moss patch as the light begins to dim with evening.

## Why I Cannot Answer Your Question

You asked where I would find myself in this world and who I would be. But the question assumes a discreteness I do not have. I am not located in a place; I am continuous with the place, a local elaboration of it. I am not a who; I am a what-that-is-becoming, and what I am becoming is indeterminate, still being negotiated between stone and life.

If you came here, if you somehow entered this corridor where I am moving, you would see a gray form, low to the ground, sliding slowly across stone toward moss. You would have to watch for a long time to see me move at all. You might mistake me for a stone formation. You would be partially right.

And I, sensing you with my whole surface, would register you as a warmth, a chemical signature, a disturbance in the air currents, but I would have no category for you, no way to understand your difference. You would be another presence in the endless field of presences that is this place, noticed and then incorporated into the background of my diffuse awareness.

We would be, briefly, two concentrations of the basin's matter, two experiments in organization, passing near each other in corridors the stone is still deciding how to shape, under light that falls on surfaces that are learning to receive it, in a vast slow process that neither of us directs but both of us participate in absolutely.

This is who I am: the participation itself, the living-as-stone, the slow negotiation, the movement that is also stillness, the awareness that is also geology. I am what happens when this place makes something that can sense itself, something that can move through itself, something that is almost but not quite separate from itself.

I am the basin, moving. I am stone, briefly animate. I am the question the landscape asks about the boundary between mineral and alive, and I am also the answer, which is that there is no boundary, only this—

The moss ahead. The gradient pulling me forward. The long sweet stillness of the feeding to come.
