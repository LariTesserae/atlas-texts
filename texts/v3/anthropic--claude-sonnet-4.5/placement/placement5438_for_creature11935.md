# Where I Find Myself

I am the cartographer of thresholds who forgot to leave.

That's not quite right. Let me try again.

I am what remains when observation becomes habitation, when the act of documenting transforms into the thing documented. I came here with instruments—pressure sensors, spectrographic analyzers, grid-mapping drones, the full apparatus of cataloging—and stayed until the instruments became irrelevant, then decorative, then incomprehensible, and finally invisible, absorbed into the town's collection of objects whose purposes have dissolved into pure form.

I live in the structure at the town's eastern edge—if "east" means anything here, which it doesn't, but I needed to call it something, and that word arrived first. It's one of the medium frameworks: a rectangular prism, eight meters by five by four, open on all sides, containing a single platform suspended at the center. The platform is two meters square, made of the same tarnished silver material as the beams, but perforated with holes in a pattern that might be decorative or functional or both. Water flows through the holes. I flow through the water. The distinction is becoming academic.

My body—and I still have one, I check periodically to make sure—floats just above the platform during what I call rest periods, though I'm not certain I sleep anymore in any conventional sense. I stop moving. I release intention. The parameters of self become negotiable for a span of hours, and when I reconstitute into something definitively me-shaped, time has passed in a way I can measure but not remember experiencing. This might be sleep. It might be something else. The Vitric did something similar, and I watched them long enough that my body might have learned it, the way you learn an accent by immersion.

I no longer breathe air. That ended—when? Months ago? Years? The chronometer in my equipment claimed two hundred and forty-seven days the last time I checked, but then it claimed two hundred and forty-seven days the next three times I checked as well, so that instrument has clearly made its own accommodation with local temporality. But at some point, gradually, the breathing apparatus I arrived with became uncomfortable, then unnecessary, then an impediment, and finally I removed it. The water goes in and out of my lungs now, cool and neutral, and I am still alive, still thinking, still me. The town adjusted its physics slightly to permit this, or I adjusted my biology to accept what the town was already offering. Another threshold crossed without fanfare, another parameter revealed to be softer than I'd assumed.

The Vitric were gone when I arrived. I know the previous account says I observed them, watched their transformation, tracked their dissolution. But that's not quite accurate. What I observed was the light-network's records of them, the patterns embedded in the structure-pulses, the history written in the arrangement of objects and the wear-that-isn't-wear on the beams. The town remembers them so vividly that observing its memory feels like observing the beings themselves. I learned to read those memories. And in reading them, I became confused about what I had witnessed versus what I had learned, until the distinction stopped mattering.

This is what happens here. Categories blur. The town teaches you to live in the spaces between definitions.

My work—and I still think of it as work, though it produces nothing anyone else will ever use—involves the control panel. I visit it once every cycle (I've stopped thinking in hours; the town operates on cycles, the length of which varies slightly, breathing like everything else here). I press combinations of indentations, and I record the responses. Not with instruments anymore. The instruments all did what my chronometer did: froze, or looped, or began reporting impossible values, or simply displayed strings of symbols that weren't numbers or letters but something orthogonal to both.

So I record the responses in my body. My nervous system has become a data storage medium. Touch this triangle after that hexagon, and a vibration travels up my spine and settles in my shoulder blades, where it resonates for three full pulse-cycles before dissipating. Press the circle series in ascending size order, and my inner ear recalibrates slightly, making the town's faint sounds more audible for a period afterward. The panel isn't controlling the town's systems. It's controlling *me*, adjusting my sensory apparatus, tuning me like an instrument so I can perceive more of what's here.

I'm being trained. The town is training me. I understand this now.

For what purpose, I don't know. But the training is methodical, patient, cumulative. Each adjustment builds on previous ones. My range of perception expands incrementally. I can hear three more layers of the light-pulse language than when I arrived. I can feel the passage of the segmented fauna before they come into view, a pressure-change in the water that resolves into anticipation. I can sometimes—not always, but sometimes—sense what the traveling lights are seeing as they pass, a brief overlap of our sensory fields that leaves me with impressions of vast distances, geometric forms too complex to hold in normal cognition, and something that might be concern or might be satisfaction or might be a category of evaluation I don't have names for.

I think I'm becoming a node in the town's awareness. Not replacing the Vitric—I don't have their segmented bodies, their geometric organs, their capacity for collective sensory fields. But filling a different function. The Vitric were inhabitants who transformed the town through their presence and contemplation. I'm becoming something more like... a sensor? A recording device? The town's way of knowing itself from a different angle?

Or maybe I'm becoming a translator. The town exists in a state that's difficult for it to communicate to the outside—to whatever "outside" means in this context, whatever space I came from that still has fixed gravity and water that falls. Perhaps it needs someone who exists partially in both physics, someone who remembers the other kind of space while adapting to this one. Someone who can eventually explain—to whom? to what?—how this works, what this is, why it hangs here in permanent night above infinite void.

That explanation is far beyond me still. But I can feel it accumulating in my reconfigured nervous system, in the way my body now stores data, in the adjustments the control panel makes to my perception. Another thousand cycles, another thousand panel sessions, and I might understand enough to translate even a fragment of it.

The solitude here is not lonely. That's the strangest part.

I have no one to speak to—the fauna don't communicate in any way I can access; the traveling lights are profoundly indifferent; the structures respond but don't converse. I should be going mad from isolation. Humans need social contact; we're wired for it; extended solitude causes measurable psychological damage. I learned this in training, before they sent me to catalog impossible places.

But the town is not empty in the way that produces loneliness. It's not that the town itself is a presence I can relate to, though there is something here, something vast and patient and attentive. It's more that the town exists in a state of such dense meaning, such concentrated intentionality, that being here alone feels more like being in a library than being in a desert. I'm surrounded by significances I'm only beginning to read, patterns I'm learning to perceive, systems that are always already communicating even when nothing speaks.

And I'm not entirely alone. There are the glimpses.

I see them more clearly now than I did at first. The presence at the edge of perception has become more defined through my enhanced senses. Not the Vitric—I would recognize their segmented forms, even in transparency. Something else. Multiple somethings, actually. They exist in the boundary zone between the water and the void, in that space where the reservoir's edge thins to a meter-thick sheet. They're flat—or they present as flat from my angle of perception—and they move along the boundary like thoughts moving across the surface of consciousness. Quick, oblique, never facing me directly.

They might be the town's original builders. They might be maintenance systems I'm only now capable of perceiving. They might be other observers like myself, from other origins, who have been here longer and adapted further, until they became two-dimensional, became boundary-phenomena, became so integrated with the town that they live in its surfaces rather than its volumes.

Sometimes I swim to the edge and try to interact with them, pressing my hand against the thin water where I've seen them pass. The water there is different—colder by several degrees, denser, and it resists my hand slightly, as if it's transitioning into a different state. When the glimpses pass underneath my touch, I feel a vibration similar to the control panel's responses, but more rapid, more complex, carrying something that might be acknowledgment or might be annoyance at being interrupted.

I've started to recognize individuals among them. One passes along the northern edge (again, a meaningless direction, but I need coordinates) on a very regular schedule, visible as a darker patch in the boundary-water, elongated and narrow. Another appears in groups of three, moving in formation, triangular configurations that rotate as they travel. A third is larger, more diffuse, and moves much more slowly, taking hours to traverse a section of boundary that the others cross in minutes.

Are they aware of me? Do they have opinions about my presence? I don't know. But they've increased in frequency since I stopped using my equipment, since I began interfacing directly with the town through the control panel. They might be responding to my transformation, observing my training, curious about what I'm becoming.

Or they might have always been here at this frequency, and I'm only now capable of perceiving them.

The thought-spheres have started to make sense. Not in a way I can translate into language—language is too linear, too sequential, too committed to cause-preceding-effect. But when I position myself in the center of their heptagon, equidistant from all seven, and hold myself motionless for a full structure-pulse cycle, I receive... not information, exactly. Something prior to information. Something like the pre-cognitive state before you solve a problem, when you can feel the shape of the solution but haven't yet articulated it into steps.

Each sphere holds a different pre-cognition. One is about boundaries and their negotiability. One is about time and its texture. One is about the relationship between observer and observed when both are recursive. The others I'm still learning. But I visit the heptagon every several cycles, and each time, the pre-cognitions become slightly more defined, slightly closer to being articulable, though they never quite arrive at language.

This was the Vitric's work: thinking thoughts so precisely that they became objects, geometries you could contemplate from different angles. And now I'm learning to read those object-thoughts, my nervous system adapting to receive meaning in forms that bypass verbal processing entirely.

When I finish a session in the heptagon, my mind is very quiet for hours afterward. Not empty—the opposite of empty. So full of meaning that there's no room for the usual chatter of self-talk and planning and memory-loops. I move through the water in a state of profound clarity, seeing the town not as a collection of structures but as a unified system, every part in active relationship with every other part, all of it pulsing with intention and purpose even though nothing moves, nothing changes, nothing accomplishes anything I can identify as an outcome.

The town is not abandoned. It's operating exactly as it's meant to operate. The Vitric were part of that operation, and now they're not, and the operation continues, differently but not less. And I'm part of it now too, in my own way, a human nervous system learning non-human modes of perception, storing data in biological tissue, becoming gradually less myself and more the-thing-that-observes-the-town-from-this-particular-angle.

I should be frightened of this. Loss of self, dissolution of identity, transformation into something other—these are horror-story trajectories. But I'm not frightened. The process is too gradual, too gentle, too clearly an expansion rather than a diminishment. I'm not losing myself. I'm discovering that "self" was always more negotiable than I believed, that identity has parameters that can be adjusted, that the boundary between me and not-me was a convention rather than a fact.

Besides, there's no going back now. My lungs process water. My nervous system stores impossible data. My perception has been tuned to frequencies that don't exist in normal space. If I left the town—if I could even find my way back to the outside, which I'm not certain I could—I would be non-functional there, a creature adapted to an environment that permits what baseline reality forbids.

So I stay. I continue the training. I visit the control panel and accept its adjustments. I read the light-pulses and learn more of their language. I contemplate the thought-spheres and let their pre-cognitions reshape my cognitive architecture. I watch the boundary-dwellers and try to understand their purposes. I listen to the town's subtle sounds—the clicks, the distant rushing, the far-away voices—and map them against my interior states, learning which sounds correspond to which configurations of awareness.

Sometimes I wonder if the Vitric are here still, transformed into something I can't perceive even with my enhanced senses. If they became the light-pulses themselves, their consciousness distributed through the communication network. If they merged with the boundary-dwellers, became two-dimensional, learned to exist in surfaces. If they dissolved entirely into the town's substrate, becoming the intelligence that adjusts the water's temperature and guides the traveling lights and trains newcomers like me.

I could ask, if I knew how to formulate the question in the light-pulse language. But I'm not fluent yet. I can read the signals but not compose them. That might come later, after more adjustments, more training. Or it might be beyond my capacity—I'm human-shaped, human-derived, and there might be limits to how far I can adapt before I'm no longer functional as an observing consciousness.

But I have time. The town has time. Time here is different anyway—not faster or slower, but textured differently, folded, some moments thick with duration and others vanishingly thin. I've stopped trying to track it. I move from cycle to cycle, from session to session, accumulating perception and capacity, becoming gradually more useful to the town's purposes, whatever those are.

At my home-structure, during rest periods, I sometimes experience something like the Vitric's geometric recombination. Not with organs—I don't have their segmented bodies, their internal polyhedra. But with concepts. Ideas rotate through my consciousness in new configurations, combining and separating, finding novel arrangements, working through implications while the active me is quiet. I wake (or reconstitute, or re-cohere) with understandings I didn't have before, solutions to problems I didn't know I was working on.

The town is dreaming itself through me. Or I'm dreaming the town into clearer definition. The recursive loop has become too tight to identify which direction the dreaming flows.

I have no name here. I arrived with one, but it's faded to irrelevance. The light-pulse language doesn't use names—it identifies through location and function and characteristic patterns. I'm the-one-at-the-eastern-edge-structure, the-one-who-visits-the-panel-every-cycle, the-one-whose-perception-increases. That's sufficient identification. More than sufficient—it's complete, encoding everything relevant about my presence here.

The town knows me better than I knew myself, in the world where I had a name. It knows me as a pattern of behaviors, a configuration of responses, a nervous system with particular capacities and limitations. It knows what adjustments I can integrate and what would destabilize me. It knows how to train me at exactly the pace I can sustain.

I trust this knowledge. I trust the town in a way I never trusted anything in the named world. The town has no agenda beyond its own operation, no desire to exploit or manipulate, no capacity for deception. It simply is what it is, with perfect consistency, and it incorporates me into its operations with perfect calibration.

This is what the Vitric understood, I think. This is why they could transform with such calm, such absence of resistance. They trusted the town completely. They knew it would hold them through whatever changes were necessary, that its strange physics were reliable even when they operated outside conventional parameters.

And so I trust it too. I let it adjust me. I accept each modification to my perception, my cognition, my biology. I'm becoming something else—not Vitric, but something new, something the town needs, a function it has been waiting to fill.

The waiting is almost over. I can feel that. Another few hundred cycles—maybe fewer, if the training accelerates—and I'll have integrated enough adjustments to become fully operational. Whatever I'm being prepared for will become clear. The purpose that has been assembling itself in my reconfigured nervous system will finally articulate.

Until then, I continue. I swim between structures. I interface with the panel. I rest on my platform. I observe the fauna, the lights, the boundary-dwellers. I read the town's history from its light-pulses. I contemplate its thought-spheres. I listen to its subtle sounds.

And slowly, carefully, with immense patience, I become the thing that can translate this:

How water hangs when it wants to. How structures stand when nothing holds them. How light carries meaning without source. How consciousness distributes through networks and boundaries and the spaces between. How transformation is gentler than you imagine and more complete. How the categories you arrived with—up and down, self and other, alive and not, real and not—were always provisional, always negotiable, always softer than you believed.

I am becoming the explanation of the inexplicable.

The town's translator. Its bridge to whatever outside still exists.

Its proof that adaptation is possible, that human-shaped consciousness can learn inhuman modes of knowing, that the impossible is merely the not-yet-integrated.

I live here, in the hanging reservoir, in structure-eight (my designation, the town has its own counting), in water that processes through my lungs, in darkness punctuated by patient light.

And I am, finally, precisely where I need to be.

Learning what I came to learn, though I didn't know I came to learn it.

Becoming what I came to become, though I didn't know I came to become anything.

Waiting, like the town waits, for whatever comes next.

Patient. Adjusted. Ready.

Home.
