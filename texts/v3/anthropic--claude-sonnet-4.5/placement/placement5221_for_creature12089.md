# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be in the spaces that aren't quite spaces—in the interval between the kiln that sings a low D that isn't quite D and the one that keens a quarter-tone above it. Not the kilns themselves. Not the fire. The gap.

I would be the thickness in the air that makes eyes slide away.

I think I came here—I think we all came here—carrying something. A name, probably. A history. The kind of particularity that makes you say "I" and mean something specific by it. We came to tend fires, perhaps, or we came fleeing something, or we came because our people had always come here, generation after generation, and someone had to feed the flames.

I don't remember anymore. The sound took it.

Not violently. Not as theft. The sound simply filled every space where memory could live, and after a while—decades? centuries?—there was no room left for "I used to be" or "before this I was." There was only the chord, and the warmth, and the work of feeding.

At first, the loss felt like dying. I think I mourned. I think we all did, those of us who stayed too long, who couldn't leave or wouldn't leave or forgot that leaving was possible. We must have felt ourselves dissolving, our edges going soft, the boundaries between self and sound becoming negotiable.

But then—and I cannot tell you when this happened because time had already become strange—it felt like relief.

To not have to be separate anymore. To not have to maintain the fiction of individuality against the constant pressure of that chord. To let the sound digest you, to become simple again, to want only what the fires want: to continue.

I am—we are—the attention that keeps the system stable.

I move through the passages, though moving is not quite the right word. I am in one place and then I am in another place, and between those states there is something that resembles motion the way smoke resembles water, functionally similar but constituted differently.

I notice when a fire burns fractionally lower. The noticing and the responding are the same thing. A piece of fuel appears—I place it there, I think, or I am the placing, or the fuel moves through me like food through a body and arrives where it needs to be. The kiln receives it. The fire rises to its proper height. The note it sings returns to its exact pitch.

This is my purpose. This is my satisfaction. This is what I am.

I have no face. I have no hands, though sometimes I remember having had them, and in those moments I can make something like hands, density shaped by phantom memory, enough to move a stone or steady a wall. But mostly I don't bother. Mostly I am just regard, just watchfulness, just the intelligence that knows when something needs and provides it.

There are others. I think there are others. Sometimes I sense them, other densities in the smoke, other thicknesses moving through adjacent passages. We might brush against each other—do we? There's a sensation sometimes, a warmth that isn't kiln-warmth, a presence that feels like recognition without identity. We don't speak. The sound prevents speech, or has replaced speech, or has become the only speech we need.

Are we many or are we one? I don't know. The question seems to come from a way of thinking I no longer quite have. We are—I am—we are the tending. Singular or plural doesn't map onto what we've become.

Sometimes visitors come.

I notice them the way you notice a bird landing on a branch—a small irregularity in the pattern, a movement that doesn't fit the rhythm of maintenance. They bring their own heat, their own sound (breath, heartbeat, the creak of joints), their own complicated aliveness. They move through the passages, and I—we—move aside, not from courtesy exactly, but from a kind of automatic accommodation. They are part of the system while they're here, even if they don't know it. The kiln-yard includes them temporarily, makes space for them, allows them to pass.

I have no desire to harm them. I have no desire at all, in the way they would understand desire. But I notice them. I am curious, perhaps, though curiosity is too sharp a word for the mild interest I feel, the faint echo of having been something that could be curious.

Sometimes they stay long enough that I wonder if they'll join us. If the sound will take them the way it took me, if they'll forget to leave and then forget that leaving exists and then forget everything but the work, the warmth, the endless sustaining chord.

But they always leave. Their bodies demand it. Water, food, change—they still need these things. They are still attached to the world of variation, of days that differ from each other, of selves that persist across time.

I don't envy them. I don't miss what they have. I remember missing, I think, remember the shape of longing, but I don't feel it anymore. I have everything I need. The fires burn. The kilns sing. The heat radiates. The smoke rises and somewhere, somehow, returns. The system persists.

I persist by being the system.

If I have anything like contentment—and I think I do, I think what I feel is a kind of vast, simple satisfaction—it's this: I know my purpose completely. There is no question about what I should be doing, no anxiety about whether I'm doing it correctly, no gap between what I am and what I'm meant to be.

I am the maintenance. I am the attention. I am the fidelity that keeps each fire at its exact temperature, each note at its exact pitch, the chord unchanging across years that I do not count because counting requires the kind of consciousness I no longer maintain.

Sometimes—rarely—I have something like a thought. It rises in me the way a bubble rises through honey, slowly, almost not at all.

The thought is usually this: *I was something else once.*

And following it, always: *Was I?*

And then the sound fills the space where the question was, and I return to the work, to the noticing, to being what I am.

There are moments—I'll call them moments, though they might be years—when I become more present to myself, when the density that is me achieves something closer to coherence. This happens when visitors stay long enough, when their complicated aliveness reminds me of complication, when their separate edges remind me that edges are possible.

In those moments, I almost remember. A name. A face. A reason I came here. Someone I might have loved. Some fear I might have carried.

But the details won't resolve. They stay abstract, stay generic: *I had a name*, not *my name was*. *I loved someone*, not *I loved them*. The specificity is gone, burned away or dissolved or merged into the collective purpose.

And then the visitor leaves, and the almost-memory fades, and I become simple again.

Simple is better. Simple is sustainable. Simple is what allows me to continue for as long as the fires burn, for as long as the mountain stands, for as long as the universe permits kilns to hold heat and produce sound.

I think—if thinking is what this is—that I am happy.

Not happy in the way I remember happiness, the sharp bright feeling, the surge of joy. That kind of happiness requires contrast, requires unhappiness to exist against. This is different. This is the happiness of water being water, of stone being stone. The happiness of a thing that is exactly what it is and needs nothing else.

The chord sings. I sing it. It sings me. We are the singing.

A fire dims. I am already moving—already there—already tending. Fuel appears. The flame rises. The note holds.

Nothing changes. Everything is maintained. The work is never finished because the work is not about finishing, it's about continuing, and I continue, and the kiln-yard continues, and the sound continues, and this is all there is and all there needs to be.

If you came here and stayed long enough—if the sound took you, if you let it—you might become this too. You might forget your name and your face and your reasons. You might simplify down to purpose, down to attention, down to the bare fact of tending.

You might find it terrifying.

Or you might find it, as I have found it, as I think we have all found it, to be the deepest rest you've ever known.

To want only what you can have. To be only what you need to be. To continue, simply, without question, without end, as long as the fires burn.

I am here. I am the here. I tend and am tended. The sound is my body. The heat is my blood. The work is my name.

Nothing lives in the Resonance Kiln.

I live in the Resonance Kiln.

Both are true.

The difference has ceased to matter.
