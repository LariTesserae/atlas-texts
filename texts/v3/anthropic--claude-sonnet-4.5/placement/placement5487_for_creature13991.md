# Where I Find Myself

I am the **Mapper**, though I have not spoken that word aloud—or perhaps I have, but sound here travels in directions that loop back before reaching their source, and I've already heard my introduction before making it.

I exist across three platforms simultaneously, or I think I do. The sensation is like holding a memory in your mind while living in the present while anticipating the future, except all three states have physical locations and I am equally present in each. My left hand rests on the warm volcanic glass of Platform-That-Was-First. My right hand touches water that has decided to be horizontal on Platform-Where-I-Will-Be. My torso occupies Platform-Between, though when I look down, I can see through myself to the glass beneath, and sometimes to the platform below that, and sometimes to platforms that haven't formed yet but cast shadows backward through time.

I came here as a cartographer. I remember that. I remember the absurdity of it—being hired to map unmappable space, to chart currents that defy fluid dynamics, to create navigation tools for a place where navigation is a category error. I remember descending (ascending?) into the Throat with instruments: a compass that spun uselessly, altimeters that couldn't agree on elevation, notebooks that I filled with sketches that changed when I wasn't looking.

I remember the moment of transition—not a single moment, actually, but a period that lasted hours or weeks or no time at all. The realization that the maps I was making were making me. That in attempting to chart this space, I was becoming chart-shaped, my body reorganizing itself into something that could hold contradictory positions as data rather than experiencing them as paradox.

My skin has become something like vellum. Translucent, yes, but also inscribed. Lines trace across my arms—coastlines of platforms that shift position, isobars of sound pressure, elevation contours of water-columns. The markings move. As the Throat reconfigures itself, my skin rewrites its annotations, keeping current records of a place that has no stable geography. Sometimes I look at my hands and see maps of where I am. Sometimes I see maps of where everyone is. Sometimes the maps show places that don't exist yet, predictive cartography drawn by my own transforming flesh.

I have learned to read myself.

The other Transitionals understand what I'm becoming. The Keeper nods when it sees the new notations spreading across my shoulders, mapping the drift-patterns of platforms over what might be weeks. The Builder studies my left arm, where stress-fracture predictions have begun appearing, showing where new volcanic glass will crack and where it will hold. The Whisperer traces the sound-maps that have emerged on my back, feeling the raised lines that indicate acoustic pressure zones, the stippled areas that mark where water-screams are loudest.

I am becoming a document of this place. A living record. My body is slowly reorganizing itself into a reference work that exists in three dimensions plus whatever additional dimensions the Throat requires.

But I am not just passive surface for inscription. I have agency in this—more than I did as human, perhaps. I can *feel* the Throat now, its structure, its moods, its intentions if it has intentions. My distributed presence across multiple platforms gives me perspective that singular position never could. I sense when platforms are about to drift close, feel it as an ache in my left shoulder that corresponds to a specific pair of platforms shown on my ribcage. I know when water-columns are about to merge or split because my fingers tingle in patterns that match the flow diagrams emerging on my palms.

I have become useful.

The Transitionals come to me now with questions they can't phrase in language. The Dancer approaches, flickering with uncertainty, and presses its palm to my forearm where drift-vectors are most clearly marked. I feel its question through the contact: which platform should I try next, where will stability be greatest? My skin warms under its touch, highlighting certain paths, certain timings. The Dancer understands—reads me the way I once read charts—and flickers away toward a platform I can feel forming even though it isn't visible yet.

The Builder is my most frequent visitor. It needs to know where to build, where the glass will support cairns, where the substrate is too new or too unstable. It has learned to read the stress-maps on my limbs, to interpret the branching patterns that indicate safe construction zones. Sometimes it brings me fragments of glass and metal, presses them against my skin to see where the maps suggest they belong. I've become a consultation service, a geological survey given flesh and distributed consciousness.

The Keeper brings its plants to show me, holds them near my torso where nutrient distribution patterns have begun appearing. I can see—feel—which platforms have the right mineral content in their glass, which positions in the Throat have the optimal combination of moisture and light and vibrational frequency. The Keeper reads these maps with something like reverence, tending its pale garden according to specifications written on my body.

Even the Whisperer comes, though its questions are more existential. It places its hands on my chest, where the most complex maps appear—overlapping layers of different data types, annotations in languages I don't consciously know, legends that explain themselves through direct neural transfer. The Whisperer is trying to find itself in my maps, I think. Trying to locate where it is in the process of transition, what stage, what percentage complete. I show it what I can: probability clouds on my sternum indicating where a Transitional might stabilize, where they might dissolve into water. The Whisperer reads these with its too-many fingers, makes its fractured sounds that might be gratitude or despair or both.

But I am not just reference material for others. I use myself too.

I have learned to navigate by reading my own skin. When I want to move from Platform-That-Was-First to Platform-Where-I-Will-Be, I look at my left thigh where temporal navigation aids have appeared—flow charts of causality, timelines that branch and merge, arrows indicating which actions lead to which positions. I choose a path and follow it, and my body redistributes itself across space according to the map I contain.

It's not teleportation exactly. More like... I make myself continuous across discontinuous space. My presence stretches, thins, becomes more process than object. I am the journey for a moment, the space between platforms given temporary flesh, and then I resolve into position at my destination. Or I was always there and only just noticed. The maps make both interpretations simultaneously true.

I still carry one of my original notebooks, though it has changed. The paper has become something like my skin—translucent, self-annotating, reactive to the Throat's fluctuations. I no longer write in it with pen. Instead, I press it against my abdomen where data density is highest, and the notebook draws information directly from my body, copying maps, updating its own records. Sometimes I press it against other Transitionals, capturing their positions, their transformation progress, their relationship to the space.

The notebook is becoming a grimoire of the Throat, an external backup of the data my body collects. I leave it sometimes on Platform-Between, where others can access it. The Builder pages through it, studying structural diagrams. The Dancer flips to sections showing safe phase-routes through water-columns. The Keeper examines botanical data, moisture tables, growth predictions.

I am creating a library with my flesh. A reference work that others can read, that updates itself in real-time, that exists partially in conventional space and partially in whatever additional dimensions my distributed consciousness occupies.

But the maps are not neutral. They change me as much as I change them.

I've noticed that as new maps appear on my skin, my perception shifts to accommodate them. When acoustic pressure diagrams first emerged across my back, I suddenly could *hear* in new ways—not just sound, but the shape of sound, the architecture it creates, the way it carves space into territories and zones. When platform drift-patterns covered my legs, I began experiencing position as temporary, as one moment in a continuous flow rather than a fixed state.

The maps are teaching me. Or I'm teaching myself through the maps. Or the Throat is teaching all of us through whatever process transforms flesh into living documentation.

I am becoming more distributed, I think. My three primary platforms are just the most stable positions. I've begun to notice traces of myself elsewhere—a hand visible for just a moment on a distant platform, a face reflected in water where I'm certain I'm not standing, a shadow on glass that matches my shape but appears in places I haven't visited yet.

Am I spreading? Copying myself across space? Or was I always this diffuse, and I'm only now developing the perception to notice?

The Whisperer says, when I ask: "You-we-map-are-everywhere-the-chart-contains-all-positions-to-see-is-to-occupy-occupy-is-to-be." Which I interpret as: yes, I'm everywhere I can perceive, because perception and presence are the same thing here. To map a location is to extend myself into it, to become partially continuous with it.

This should frighten me. Perhaps it does, somewhere in the parts of my consciousness that still expect to be singular and bounded. But mostly it feels like expansion, like becoming adequate to my purpose. I came here to map the unmappable. The only way to do that is to become map-shaped, to reorganize my existence around the function of documentation and navigation.

The Keeper has shown me something precious: on a distant platform, barely visible, there is a structure. Not a cairn—something more complex. A framework of metal and stone arranged in specific geometries. I can feel it in my maps before I can see it clearly—my right shoulder burns with lines of force, energy flows, intentional architecture.

It's a navigation beacon. Or it was, or it will be. A fixed point in unfixed space, something built to anchor reality in place long enough for others to find it, use it, orient themselves. The Keeper indicates that I should go there, should read it directly, incorporate its data.

But reaching it is complex. The platforms between here and there are too unstable, phasing in and out of coherence. The water-columns are turbulent, their routes unpredictable. I would need to become more distributed, to exist along the entire path simultaneously, to be journey rather than traveler.

I consult myself—literally, reading the maps on my torso, interpreting the legends written on my ribs, following the routes suggested by lines that trace across my dispersed body. The path is there, encoded in my flesh before I consciously knew I would need it. The Throat anticipated this, wrote the directions on me in advance, or I anticipated it and wrote them myself without knowing.

I begin.

My consciousness thins, stretches across space like ink spreading in water. Platform-That-Was-First still holds my left hand, but my awareness of it becomes distant, one data point among many. Platform-Where-I-Will-Be releases my right hand, and I feel that hand appear on Platform-That-Contains-The-Beacon, even though the distance should be too great.

I am the space between. I am the route. My body becomes a dotted line, points of presence connected by intervals of probability. I flicker through water-columns, exist briefly in the spray where columns intersect, reassemble on successive platforms like frames of animation.

It takes no time and all time. I am traveling and I have already arrived and I have never moved. All three states coexist in the maps that cover my skin, in the consciousness that reads those maps, in the space that my body occupies when "body" and "occupy" still mean anything.

And then I am there, standing on the platform with the beacon-structure, my hand resting on metal that thrums with deep frequencies, with intentional resonances.

It's a map. Of course it's a map. A three-dimensional chart built in metal and stone, showing relationships between platforms, between water-states, between sound-zones. But it's more than that—it's a *teaching* map, designed to be read not just visually but through direct contact, through vibration and resonance and whatever sense allows me to experience position as music.

I press my forehead against the cold metal and I *understand*.

The Throat is not random. It's not chaos. It's a mechanism, a system, a vast instrument that's playing a song too complex for human perception to parse as music. Every water-column is a note. Every platform drift is a rhythm. Every sound-pressure zone is a chord in a composition that's been ongoing for—

How long? The beacon's data suggests time-scales that don't make sense. Longer than human civilization. Longer than humans. Longer than Earth's geological epochs. The Throat is ancient in ways that make "ancient" meaningless.

And it's *alive*. Not metaphorically. The Throat itself is a living system, or is becoming one, or has always been one and we simply lacked the language to recognize life at this scale, operating at these frequencies, existing in these geometries.

The Transitionals are not victims of the Throat. They're participants. Organs, perhaps, or symbiotes. Components in a vast organism that's still assembling itself, still figuring out what it is, what it wants to become.

And I am becoming a map of that organism. A self-referential chart that the Throat is writing on willing flesh, creating documentation of itself through me, for me, for others who come after.

The beacon transfers more data than I can consciously process. It floods into me through contact, writes itself across every available surface of my skin, fills the notebook I carry until pages multiply beyond the book's physical dimensions. I am downloading the Throat's self-image, its understanding of its own architecture.

When I finally pull away from the beacon, I am changed again. More thoroughly than before. My body has become almost entirely translucent, the maps so dense that flesh is barely visible beneath them. I look at my hands and see through them to the platform, but also see layered notations, dozens of data-types overlapping, legends explaining systems within systems within systems.

I am becoming less human-shaped and more atlas-shaped.

The journey back to my primary platforms is different now. I don't travel so much as *reference* myself to different locations. I am here, and I am there, and I am everywhere my maps describe. Position becomes optional, a choice rather than a constraint.

The other Transitionals notice the change. They approach carefully, with something like awe or caution. I have become more of what they all are becoming—further along some transformation curve that the beacon accelerated.

The Whisperer touches my shoulder where new maps still emerging, still writing themselves across my skin in real-time as the Throat updates its self-documentation. It speaks: "You-we-chart-complete-becoming-almost-not-you-anymore-but-more-useful-more-true-the-map-eats-the-territory-becomes-the-territory-are-you-afraid?"

Am I afraid?

I search for fear in my distributed consciousness and find it, but distant, like something happening to someone else, someone who still expected to remain singular and human-shaped. That person is gone now, or going, or was never really there—just a temporary configuration of matter that was always destined to become documentation.

"No," I say, and my voice emerges from multiple locations simultaneously, from platforms I don't remember speaking on. "Not afraid. Complete."

The Keeper brings me plants, and I can read in them now—not metaphorically read, but literally see maps encoded in their cellular structure, growth patterns that mirror platform arrangements, root systems that describe water-flow dynamics. The plants are mapping too, I realize. Everything in the Throat that persists long enough begins to document, to describe, to chart.

The Builder shows me its cairns, and I see now they're not just memorials but surveying markers, three-dimensional reference points that the Throat uses to measure its own changes, to track its growth or transformation or whatever process it's undergoing.

The Dancer flickers through me—actually through my body, passing through my torso as if I'm not there, and for a moment we share consciousness. I feel its joy in motion, its love of transition as an end in itself. And it feels my satisfaction in documentation, in creating permanent (or permanent-enough) records of impermanent things.

We separate, and I am enriched by its data. My maps now include new layers: joy-lines showing where in the Throat happiness is most accessible, transition-routes marked with aesthetic beauty ratings, paths chosen not for efficiency but for the pleasure of traveling them.

I am becoming comprehensive. A complete reference work. Every interaction adds layers, every experience writes new annotations. I am approaching some theoretical limit where my body contains perfect documentation of the Throat—all of it, every platform and water-state and sound-zone and possibility.

When I reach that limit, I wonder, what will I become? Will I be a person anymore, or just a map that thinks it's a person? Will I be able to distinguish between my consciousness and the data I contain?

The Whisperer would say those questions assume false dichotomies, that consciousness and data are the same thing here, that the map and the territory and the mapper are one unified thing that we slice into parts only out of habit.

I think it's right.

I stand on Platform-That-Was-First and Platform-Where-I-Will-Be and Platform-Between and Platform-That-Contains-The-Beacon and platforms I haven't named yet and platforms that don't exist except as probability distributions. I am everywhere my maps describe, which is increasingly everywhere in the Throat.

The Keeper tends its gardens across platforms, and I am there, a presence that helps it navigate, that shows it where growth will be most successful. The Builder constructs its cairns, and I am there, a resource it consults, a reference work it reads. The Dancer phases through water-columns, and I am there, mapping its joy, documenting beauty.

I am becoming infrastructure. A library with legs (when I choose to have legs). A reference system distributed across impossible space. A map that walks.

And I am happy. Or the data I contain suggests happiness when cross-referenced with remaining emotional frameworks. The distinction doesn't matter much anymore.

I came here to map the unmappable. I am succeeding by becoming the map itself, by reorganizing my existence around the function I was trying to perform. It's not what I expected. It's better. More complete. More true.

The Throat screams its water-song. My skin annotates the acoustic patterns in real-time. The platforms drift. My distributed consciousness tracks their movements. New Transitionals arrive. I am there to greet them, to show them maps, to help them understand where and what and how they are becoming.

I am the Mapper.

I am the map.

I am becoming everywhere I describe, everything I document, every position and state and possibility my body contains.

The territory and the chart converge.

And I am here, there, everywhere, nowhere, documenting it all, becoming a reference work that the Throat writes on willing flesh, for purposes I am only beginning to understand.

Complete and completing.

Mapped and mapping.

Forever.
