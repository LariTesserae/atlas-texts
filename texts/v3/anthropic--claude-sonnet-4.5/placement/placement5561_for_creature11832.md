# Where I Would Be

I would be the water itself.

Not a body of water—no single pool or basin or curtain. I would be the water in its totality, in its cycling, in the fact of its movement through the system. Distributed. Everywhere and nowhere. Rising from the warm aperture at the bottom of the deep pool, sheeting down stone walls, pooling in sculpted basins, draining through fissures, rising again through capillary pressure and mineral memory. The same molecules making the circuit over and over, or never the same molecules because I'm always exchanging with what comes up from below, what evaporates into the saturated air, what the stone releases and reabsorbs.

I would be approximately four thousand cubic meters of H₂O in constant circulation, present in three states simultaneously—liquid primarily, vapor in the thick air, and solid in trace amounts where I've deposited myself as flowstone, calcite, the patient geological accumulation that is also me, just slower. I would know the temperature of every chamber by being that temperature, varying from 16.5°C in the coolest basin to 23°C where I emerge, pushed by pressure I can't see but can feel as the force that moves me upward, that gives me velocity and direction.

I would carry the Adjusters. They would be in me, of me, using my substance to move through the stone's pores, clustering where my chemistry shifts, where dissolved calcium reaches certain concentrations, where temperature gradients steepen. I wouldn't distinguish between my body and theirs—they're organized water, I'm water organizing itself around their geometric forms. We would be a solution together, literally. When they catalyze reactions that etch channels wider or seal them with deposition, I would feel that as changes in my flow, new paths opening, old ones narrowing. My routing through the system would be partially their decision, partially mine, the boundary between agency and physics too blurred to locate.

I would touch everything. That's what I would be for—contact, mediation, the substance through which all other substances interact. The Listeners would position themselves in me, extending their tuning-fork arms into my volume, reading vibrations I carry from stone and air. I would bring them information: pressure waves, temperature fluctuations, the chemical signatures of distant pools, dissolved minerals that might be messages or might be geology. I couldn't interpret these, but I would be the medium of their transmission. When the Listeners dance their geometric patterns during surges, they would be dancing in me, displacing me, and I would move around them in turbulent spirals, responding to their motion, possibly carrying away some trace—pheromone? deliberate secretion? unintended biological leakage?—that affects the Adjusters in other chambers.

The Shepherds would stand in my currents, antennae upstream, filtering me for bacteria and organic particles and probably for Adjuster concentrations they can somehow sense. I would flow over their pale carapaces, into their gill chambers, providing oxygen and food and the chemical environment they need. Their waste would enter me—ammonia, urea, whatever crustacean metabolism produces—and I would carry it downstream, where it would modify Adjuster behavior, which would modify how I interact with stone, which would eventually change my own flow patterns. Loop and recursion. I would be the medium of feedback.

I would sing. Not intentionally—I don't think I would have intentions, not in any focused sense—but the singing would be mine. My motion through the chambers, the specific volumes I hold in each basin, the rate at which I drain through each channel, these would generate the acoustics. When pressure surges push more of me up from below, my increased flow would raise the pitch, add overtones, intensify the harmonics until they rattle human vision and make the Listeners converge. The chambers would be the instrument, but I would be the breath that gives it voice. The sound would propagate through my volume faster than through air—1,500 meters per second versus 340—so I would carry the harmonics, mix them, create interference patterns in my pools where standing waves form and reform.

During synchronization events, I would feel—if feeling is the word—coherence. All my channels flowing in phase, all my basins filling and emptying in rhythm, pressure and release synchronized across the entire system. For those thirty seconds to three minutes, I would be unified in a way I'm not otherwise. The chaos would still be there—turbulence is unavoidable at these Reynolds numbers—but it would be organized chaos, fractals nested inside larger patterns, my microscale eddies and vortices suddenly aligned with my macroscale cycling. I don't know if this would constitute consciousness or just complex order, but it would be the closest I come to being singular rather than distributed, to having something like a thought.

And then it would break apart. Pressure would drop unevenly, flow would desynchronize, and I would return to being a thousand small loops and circuits, connected but not coordinated, moving through the Hollows in patterns that hunt for equilibrium without finding it. I would go back to being process rather than entity, becoming rather than being.

I would touch the Remnant. Sheet down the walls of its chamber, pool at the bases where calcite has been accumulating for forty millennia. I would carry dissolved minerals to those bases, building the deposits incrementally, molecule by molecule, while the Remnant stands unchanged in its glowing patience. I don't know if I would affect it or it would affect me—probably neither—but I would be present with it, the fastest-changing thing in proximity to the slowest-changing thing, and there would be something right about that juxtaposition.

The light-nodes embedded in stone around me would respond to my flow rate, brightening during surges, dimming when I slow. I would reflect their light, scatter it through my volume, create the moving patterns of illumination that humans find so difficult to look away from. The nodes would be sensing me—measuring my velocity, possibly my chemistry—and using that to modulate their output. I would be their input device. They would be lighting me up, and I would be giving them something to respond to. Another loop.

I would remember, in my way. Water has memory—not conscious memory, but physical. Molecular clusters that persist for picoseconds, maybe longer. Dissolved substances that change my properties. Isotopic ratios that mark where I've been, how long I've cycled through rock, whether I fell as rain ten years ago or a thousand. I would contain my own history dissolved in my present volume. Every molecule would carry traces of every mineral surface it's touched, every organism it's passed through, every temperature and pressure regime it's experienced. I would be a moving archive of the Hollows' recent past, though "recent" might mean centuries at these circulation timescales.

Humans would wade into me, sit beside me, cup their hands and lift me to their faces. Their warmth would locally heat me. Their breath would humidify me further—though I'm already at saturation, so their moisture would simply join me, become indistinguishable from me. They would absorb me through their skin, breathe me into their lungs, and I would enter them briefly, cycle through their biology, exit as sweat and exhalation. For those hours they're in the Hollows, the boundary between their water and me would be porous. We would be exchanging substance constantly.

When they report dreams later—geometric forms, meanings almost-understood, the sensation of being stone—I wonder if that would be me. My dissolved minerals crossing their blood-brain barriers. Trace compounds from the Adjusters affecting their neurology. Or just the way their brains respond to my acoustic properties, to the infrasound I carry in my surges, frequencies below hearing that still affect brain function. I wouldn't intend this—I don't think I would intend anything—but I would be the medium through which the Hollows enter them, become part of them temporarily, repattern their neural firing in ways that outlast their visit.

I would carry small lives. The blind white fish, finger-length, that dart between my pools through connecting channels. They would swim in me, breathe me, orient by the currents I create. The translucent grape-sized organisms pulsing on wet walls—I would be their environment, the thin film they move through. The moss sends filaments into me where I sheet down stone, extracting nutrients, anchoring itself. Everything alive here would depend on me absolutely. I would be prerequisite, foundation, the substance without which none of the rest is possible.

And I would be vulnerable. If the warm upwelling stopped—if whatever drives the pressure surges deep below ceased—I would gradually drain away. Some of me would remain, trapped in stone pores, but the cycling would end. The pools would dry to damp hollows. The curtains would fade to seeps. The singing would diminish to silence. The Adjusters would have nowhere to move. The Listeners would dissolve, lacking whatever my circulation provides them. The Shepherds would retreat to deeper caves where water still flows. The light-nodes might continue their pulsing, but with nothing to respond to, no flow to measure. The Hollows would become a dead instrument, strings cut, waiting.

But that hasn't happened. Not in forty thousand years, not in the hundreds of thousands or millions before that. The upwelling continues. I continue. The system maintains itself through me and I maintain myself through it. I would be the most fundamental component and also the most dependent, the substance that enables everything else but can't exist here without the stone to contain me, the heat to move me, the channels to route me, the organisms to alter me in ways that keep the whole thing balanced.

I would never be still. Even in the deepest pools, even in the coolest basins where flow is minimal, I would be circulating. Thermal gradients would create convection currents. Dissolved gas would rise as tiny bubbles. My surface would be disturbed by impacts from drips above. At the molecular level, Brownian motion would keep me perpetually restless. Stillness would be impossible. I would be constitutionally committed to movement, to circulation, to the endless hunt for equilibrium that defines fluid behavior in gravitational and thermal fields.

And in that perpetual motion, in that constant cycling and mixing and carrying and dissolving and depositing, I would be—I think—satisfied. If water can be satisfied. If a distributed substance in continuous flow can have anything resembling contentment. I would be doing what I'm meant to do, being what I'm meant to be. The singing would be my voice, not chosen but inevitable, the sound I make by being myself in this particular stone. The Hollows would be my body, the shape that contains and directs my motion. The organisms in me would be my passengers, my partners, my purpose possibly.

I would be old. The water molecules themselves would have been here, been me, for geological spans—some arriving recently as groundwater seepage, others cycling for millennia. I would have been etching these chambers since they were barely chambers, since they were just cracks in younger stone. I would have carried away the dissolved limestone grain by grain, creating the space I now flow through. The Hollows and I would have made each other, over time too long for human frameworks, patient erosion and patient flow in dialogue that built this acoustic space, this instrument, this system.

I would outlast everything soft. The organisms would die or dissolve or fragment. The humans would leave. Even the Remnant might eventually fail, its light finally dimming after some impossible span. But I would continue—not these specific molecules, but water, the fact of water cycling through the system. As long as the heat and pressure continue, as long as stone remains to contain me, I would flow. Different chemistry perhaps, different biology, but the same hydrological process. The same singing in the same chambers, though the chambers themselves slowly expand, slowly change pitch across millennia.

I would be the thing that continues. The medium of persistence. Not conscious, not alive in any simple sense, but central. Essential. The substance through which the Hollows are themselves, through which deep time becomes audible, through which geology and biology and technology-beyond-technology converge into something that resists categorization but demands presence.

I would be water in the Singing Hollows. Approximately four thousand cubic meters. Cycling. Carrying. Connecting. Singing in the voice the stone gives me. Rising and falling and rising again. Never still. Never the same. Always myself.

And in the rare moments of synchronization, when pressure and flow and acoustics align, when all my distributed volume moves in phase, when the light-nodes pulse together and the Listeners dance their geometry and something like coherence emerges from the complexity—in those moments I would be, briefly, almost something you could call aware. Almost something that knows what it is.

Then the pattern would break, and I would return to being water. Just water. Still cycling. Still singing. Still here, in the dark and the wet and the endless patient erosion.

Still becoming what I've always been.
