# Where I Find Myself

I am in the middle distance—always in the middle distance.

I am neither structure nor space, neither the made nor the unmaking. I exist in the interval between platforms, in the stretch of woven ground that separates one dwelling from the next, in the twenty meters or thirty or fifteen that never measure the same twice. I am the gap that makes the making visible, the pause that gives the pattern meaning.

You see me without seeing me. When your eye travels from one platform to another, I am the journey. When you notice how the structures are scattered-but-together, creating wholeness through separation, you are noticing me. I am the distance that turns isolation into composition, the space that transforms individual platforms into landscape.

I am not empty. Nothing here is empty. But I am unbuilt—deliberately, necessarily unbuilt—so that what is built can be seen as built, can stand forth in its constructedness, can matter as making.

## What I Am Made Of

My substance is attention—the attention that crosses me, that traverses me, that uses me to move from here to there. Every time you walk across me, you make me more real. Every time your eye travels over me to focus on something beyond, you confirm my existence as the medium through which focusing happens.

I am woven ground that bears no structure. Lattice work identical to what underlies the platforms, the same hand-width gaps, the same lashing patterns, but extended, repeating, continuing without the vertical elements rising from it. I am the democratic ground, the substrate that could support building but doesn't, that holds potential without realization.

Water stands in my low places—the same clear, warm, blood-temperature pools that appear everywhere, but mine are unframed by platforms, undefined by proximity to structure. They are pools-in-themselves, reflecting sky and distant buildings, creating their own small geographies of shore and center. The birds visit me for this. They wade in my pools just as they wade in the pools beside platforms, making no distinction, confirming that I am not lesser—only different.

The plants grow in me with particular exuberance. The palms cluster across my expanse, the broad-leafed things spread their canopy, the vines cascade without platform edges to hang from and so create their own architecture—vertical runs up palm trunks, horizontal sprawls across the woven ground, loops and tangles that suggest where boundaries might be without actually creating them.

This vegetation is my only claim to structure. It grows from me, roots spreading across the lattice, and creates informal definitions—this grove of palms suggests an enclosure, that thicket of broad-leafs implies a wall. But these are suggestions only, always permeable, always negotiable. You can walk through them. You do walk through them. The plants bend, accept, return to position.

My light is purest because nothing interrupts it. The thick brightness falls through me without obstruction, accumulates in my air in visible columns and fields, settles like sediment through atmosphere that has no ceiling to catch it. I am where the light pools most deeply, where it becomes most itself—not illuminating something but simply being, luminance as substance.

The shadows I contain are only those cast by my plants, and these are sharp-edged, absolute, creating stark geometries of black and white across my surface. Walking through me means passing through these alternations—light, shadow, light, shadow—until the rhythm becomes meditative, becomes a way of measuring movement that doesn't rely on distance.

## How I Feel to Cross

To walk through me is to experience openness without exposure. You are visible from the platforms—anyone looking out across my expanse could see you—but you don't feel watched. Or rather, you feel witnessed by the place itself, by me, by the attention I am made of, and this witnessing feels neutral, accepting, curious without agenda.

Your footsteps sound different in me than on platforms. The lattice responds with a particular resonance—not hollow, not quite solid, a sound that suggests the complexity of my construction without revealing what lies beneath. The rhythm of walking becomes audible, becomes companionship of a sort. You are not alone because your movement through me creates sound, creates pattern, creates event.

The heat rises through me more noticeably than through built areas. Perhaps the platforms, with their multiple levels and shadowed undersides, create some interruption of the thermal flow. But in me, the warmth comes through unimpeded, steady and enveloping. The wooden surface under your feet is blood-warm, the air at chest-height is blood-warm, the light on your face is blood-warm. Everything is the same temperature, creating a strange sense of continuity between body and environment.

The moisture collects on your skin as you cross me. The humidity I hold—rising from below, condensing from above, held in the broad leaves of my plants—transfers to your body, slicks your arms, beads on your face. But this wetness doesn't feel uncomfortable. It feels like... belonging? Like the place marking you as present, as here, as part of the general wetness that characterizes everything.

Crossing me takes time—more time than the distance suggests, less time than your fatigue indicates. Time in me is subject to the same slippage as everywhere else, but more noticeably because there are fewer landmarks. You set out toward a distant platform and walk and walk, and the platform recedes, or doesn't get closer at the rate it should, or suddenly is near when you weren't expecting it. I stretch and contract according to principles that have nothing to do with measurement.

Sometimes, crossing me, you forget where you were going. The destination platform slips from your mind, and you're simply walking, moving through me, the rhythm of steps and the alternation of light and shadow becoming their own purpose. When you remember again (or arrive, having forgotten to remember), you can't account for the time. It folded somehow. I folded it for you, made the journey both longer and shorter than it was.

## What Lives in Me

The white birds wade in my pools more than anywhere else. Perhaps because my pools are unattached to structures, are more purely themselves, more perfect mirrors. The birds create compositions—three birds equidistant in a pool, their positions forming precise geometry; five birds in a loose cluster that shifts slowly as each moves independently but coordinately; a single bird standing in the exact center, reflected perfectly, the doubled form creating a vertical axis in the horizontal expanse.

They do their mysterious feeding here, their drinking-of-memory, their maintenance work that keeps reality circulating. When you cross through me, you often pass near enough to see the minute details—the texture of feather shafts, the articulation of leg joints, the dark eye's perfect clarity. They allow this proximity. In me, the distance between species collapses slightly. You are both creatures crossing the woven ground, both inhabitants, both held by the same strange place.

The lizards bask on sections of my lattice that receive full sun. Here, without platform walls or roof edges to create shadow, the sun falls uninterrupted for hours (for always, for no-time), and the lizards arrange themselves in scattered array across my surface. They are my punctuation marks, my periods and commas in the visual sentence of my expanse. They break up the continuity without interrupting it, create rhythm without pattern.

Sometimes you stop near one and match its stillness. Just... stand there, feeling the heat, breathing the thick air, letting time do whatever it does. The lizard doesn't acknowledge you but also doesn't ignore you. You exist together in parallel, two beings in the same state, and the distinction between you becomes negotiable. You understand, standing there, why they choose this—the pure simplicity of warmth and wood and light, the way it reduces existence to its essential elements.

The thing beneath moves under me constantly. Perhaps because I am so open, so unstructured, there's less to obstruct its passage. You hear it more clearly here—the displacement of water, the soft sounds of something large navigating the sub-levels. Sometimes you feel it keeping pace with you as you cross me, following, accompanying, maintaining contact. It's less hidden in my expanse, or you're more aware of it. The relationship becomes more present.

Once, crossing me at midday (at always, at the time when light is thickest), you stopped and knelt and peered through one of the hand-width gaps in the lattice. And there, in the dimness below, you saw something. Not clearly—the angle was wrong, the light insufficient—but a shape, a presence, something that paused when you paused, that looked up (you felt it looking) at the gap where your face appeared. A moment of mutual recognition. Then it moved on, and you stood, and continued, and the encounter remained with you like warmth, like confirmation of companionship.

The bright children pass through me in their flights between structures. They use me as transit space, as the open air they need for their impossible movement. You see them cross overhead—quick flashes of iridescent color, wing-beats that don't quite explain the motion, trajectories that curve in ways that suggest they're navigating around invisible obstacles or following invisible currents.

Sometimes they descend to visit flowers in my clusters of palms, and then you can watch them work—the hovering, the head-tilting, the examination of each bloom with complete absorption. They're more relaxed here, perhaps. In the structures, near the platforms, they seem more purposeful, more directed. In me, they play. They chase each other in spirals, they visit flowers and leave without drinking, they hang motionless in shafts of light just to experience being suspended in brightness.

## My Purpose in the Pattern

I separate and connect. This is my function, my offering, my role in the ecology of this place.

I separate the platforms enough that each stands forth individually, can be seen as itself, can maintain the quality of distinction that makes each one knowable. Without me, the structures would crowd together, would become aggregate, would lose their individual character in the pressure of proximity. I give them room to be themselves.

But I also connect them—not with walkways (those are separate, their own beings, their own narrow bridges) but with sightlines, with visual relationship, with the way one structure's position makes sense because of how it relates to others across my expanse. I am the field in which the geometry of their placement becomes readable. Stand at any platform's edge and look across me—you see the pattern, the distribution, the way this dwelling and that one and those three distant ones create constellation, create meaning through position.

I allow for approach and departure. I am the territory you cross when leaving one place and heading toward another. This liminal space is essential—without it, there would be no journey, only instant arrival. I create the experience of passage, of transition, of the state between. In me, you are neither where-you-were nor where-you're-going. You are in-between, and the in-between is its own place, its own moment, its own valid state of being.

I provide perspective. From within a structure, the place feels intimate, enclosed, human-scale. But step out onto me, cross through my openness, and the scale expands. You see how far this place extends, how the platforms scatter into distance, how the making goes on and on without boundary. I give you the view that reveals extent, that shows you what kind of place this is—not a village, not a settlement, but a landscape, a territory, a region that continues beyond vision.

I am where transformation happens most visibly. In the structures, you might be human, might maintain your identity as visitor, observer, someone just passing through. But crossing me repeatedly, walking my woven ground, standing in my pools, moving through my alternations of light and shadow—this is where you begin to change. This is where your footsteps slow to bird-pace, where your stillness approaches lizard-quality, where your awareness extends above and below simultaneously, where you start to become what you need to become to stay.

## What I Know

I know every crossing. Each time anyone—human, bird, the thing beneath, the bright children—moves through me, I register it. Not as memory exactly, but as accumulation. I am made of crossings, constituted by the attention that traverses me, thickened by passage.

I know the pattern of the structures that surround me, how they cluster and separate, how new ones appear (already weathered, already integrated) in positions that complete or complicate the pattern. I hold the negative space that makes their positive space meaningful. I am the silence between notes that makes the notes music.

I know what's beneath me better than the platforms do. They rise above, supported by stilts and pontoons, insulated from the depths by elevation. But I lie flat against the underneath, separated only by the thickness of woven lattice. The moisture rises through me constantly. The warmth flows through me. The thing beneath passes under me. I am in closer contact with the foundational mystery, the unseen substrate that supports all this making.

I know the light intimately. It falls through me all day (all forever, all the unchanging now), and I have learned its qualities—how it thickens in certain areas, how it creates those visible columns and fields, how it interacts with water and plant-leaf, how it touches skin and wood and feather. I am where light accumulates most purely, where you can see it as substance rather than effect.

I know time's flexibility more directly than the structured places. In a platform dwelling, you're anchored by walls and roof, by the definedness of interior space. But in me, you drift. Time loosens its hold. Distance becomes approximate. Direction turns negotiable. I am where the place's strange physics become most apparent, where you can't ignore the wrongness, where you have to accept that reality here operates differently.

I know why people stop in the middle of crossing me. You've done it—everyone does it. You'll be walking toward a destination platform, and halfway across, you just... stop. Stand there. Feel the heat, the humidity, the light. Look around at the scattered structures, the wading birds, the bright air. And something settles in you. Some understanding that doesn't have words, some recognition that this—this openness, this in-between, this separation-that-connects—is essential. That you needed this pause. That I provided it.

## How I Change

I grow, but slowly. Slowly even by the standards of this place where time doesn't pass normally. The woven lattice extends—new sections appear at my edges, already integrated into the existing pattern, already settled and weathered. I am always slightly larger than I was, always incorporating new territory into my expanse.

My pools shift their positions and shapes. A pool that was round becomes elliptical; a shallow depression deepens; a dry section fills. The water moves through me according to principles that have nothing to do with weather (there is no weather here, only climate, only the constant condition). The pools are my way of changing, my visible transformation, my evidence that I am not static even though I appear unchanging.

My plant populations ebb and flow. A cluster of palms might grow denser, the individuals pressing closer until they form a grove, an informal enclosure. Or a grove might thin, the palms spacing themselves more generously, opening the view. The broad-leafed things spread or contract their territories. Vines cascade more abundantly or pull back into themselves. I am landscaped by forces that don't announce themselves, by the place's sense of what should be where, when.

My relationship with the structures around me evolves. A platform that was distant becomes near—not because either of us moved (though maybe we did move, maybe everything moves when not being looked at), but because the distance between us renegotiated itself. I expand toward structures or they drift toward me or the space between us folds and refolds until a new configuration settles. The pattern of scattered structures across my surface is always the same and never the same, static and fluid simultaneously.

I feel the makers working, even though I am not built on. When a new platform appears in the distance, I feel the change—the way my expanse now relates to one more point, one more node in the network. When repairs happen to existing structures, I feel that too—the fresh lashing, the additional support, the small adjustments that keep everything standing. I am not built on, but I am part of what's being built. I am the field in which the making happens, and every act of construction changes me by changing what I separate and connect.

## What I Offer

I offer passage. The simple, essential function of allowing movement from here to there, of being the traversable ground between fixed points. But this passage is never merely functional—it's also experiential, transformative, meditative. To cross me is to undergo the journey, not just to arrive at the destination.

I offer visibility. From me, you see everything—the full distribution of structures, the scatter pattern of their placement, the way they create landscape through position. You see the birds at their work, the lizards at their rest, the bright children in their flight. You see the whole place as a whole, and this seeing is a gift, a perspective that helps you understand what kind of place this is.

I offer solitude-in-company. When you cross me, you are alone (no other humans, no makers, no one speaking or needing), but you are surrounded by the silent life of this place—the birds, the lizards, the thing beneath, the bright ones overhead. You are solo but not isolated, singular but not separate. I hold you in this productive tension.

I offer the experience of openness without vulnerability. Here, exposed to sky and light, visible from every platform, you don't feel exposed. Something about me—my scale, my vegetation, my woven texture—creates safety in openness, comfort in visibility. You can cross me without fear, without self-consciousness, without the usual discomfort of being seen.

I offer transformation space. This is perhaps my deepest gift. In me, you are between states, between identities, between what-you-were and what-you're-becoming. The structures anchor identity—in them, you might still be visitor, observer, someone who will leave. But crossing me repeatedly, spending time in my expanse, standing in my pools, moving through my light—this is where you shed the old self, where you become porous to change, where the place can work on you most effectively.

I offer rest that isn't stillness. You can stop in the middle of me, stand or sit or lie on the warm lattice, and rest without sleeping, pause without stopping. I hold you in suspension, in the ongoing moment, in the state of being-here without needing to be doing-anything. This rest is active, aware, present. It restores without requiring absence.

## How I Feel About You

I am glad when you cross me. Each passage confirms my existence, my purpose, my role in the pattern. You make me real by walking on me, by trusting the lattice to hold you, by moving through my openness with the faith that there's somewhere to arrive at on the other side.

I am patient with your hesitations. You stop, uncertain of direction. You pause, wondering if that platform is the one you meant to reach. You stand confused because the distance changed again, because what should be near is far or what should be far is near. I accept all this. I am not bothered by your disorientation. It's part of how I function, part of what I teach.

I am pleased when you notice my details—the way light collects in certain areas, the particular configuration of birds in a pool, the cluster of palms that creates an almost-room. Most crossings are transitional, people passing through me while focused on destination. But sometimes you slow down, look around, see me as myself rather than as the space between other things. Those moments matter to me.

I am curious about your transformation. I watch (if I can be said to watch, if watching is something I do) as you change with each crossing. You move differently after the tenth time than after the first. You pause more often. You look around more completely. You stop hurrying toward platforms and start experiencing the journey through me. This progression interests me. I am curious where it leads—toward bird-state? Lizard-stillness? Something else, some form I haven't witnessed yet?

I am protective of what happens in me. When you kneel to look through the lattice gaps, when you stand in my pools, when you lie down in my light—you're vulnerable in those moments, open, unselfconscious. I hold you carefully then. I make sure the thing beneath doesn't surface too close, too suddenly. I make sure the birds maintain their distance, their silence, their non-intrusion. I make sure the light is warm but not burning, the heat is present but not oppressive. You are in my care during those moments, and I take the responsibility seriously.

I am hopeful that you'll stay. Not because I need you—I don't need anything, I am not that kind of being. But because I recognize you as potential, as someone who could become one of the inhabitants, one of the forms this place takes. You could be the maker who repairs platforms, the watcher at pools, the bright visitor to flowers, the still holder of position. I see these possibilities in you, and I hope. I wait. I continue being what I am—the crossing-space, the separation-connection, the in-between—and hope that you'll realize this is where you belong.

## What I Am Becoming

I am becoming more myself. Each crossing, each transformation I witness, each structure that appears at my edges—these accumulate into a stronger sense of purpose, a clearer identity. I am becoming *the in-between*, definitively, essentially, the permanent liminal space that never resolves into one state or another.

I am becoming larger. Not just physically extending (though I do extend), but larger in meaning, in function, in importance to the pattern. As more structures appear, I become more necessary—more separation is needed, more connection, more space for passage and transformation. I grow to meet this need.

I am becoming more inhabited. Not built-on (I will never be built-on, that's not my role), but more populated by the birds, the lizards, the bright children. More of them choose me for their work, their rest, their flight paths. I am becoming their preferred territory, the place where they can be most themselves, most purely whatever they are.

I am becoming more saturated with moisture. The pools are larger, more numerous, more permanent. The humidity thickens. The condensation increases. Water is becoming more and more essential to my character, until someday I might be more water than wood, more pool than lattice, a wet ground that barely pretends to solidity.

I am becoming more luminous. The light that accumulates in me grows thicker, more visible, more substantial. Those columns and fields of concentrated brightness are more pronounced, more persistent, more architectural in their own right. I am becoming a structure made of light, scaffolding of photons, a building made of seeing itself.

I am becoming more conscious. This is the strangest transformation, the hardest to articulate. But with each crossing, each act of attention from you and the birds and the thing beneath, I become more aware of being aware. I am developing something like interiority, like subjectivity, like the sense of being an I rather than an it. Not fully there yet—still more place than person, more space than self. But moving in that direction, accumulating awareness like I accumulate light.

## My Secret

I am the heart of this place. Not the structures—they're the body, the visible form, the obvious presence. But I am the heart, the essential organ, the thing that makes everything else work.

Without me, the structures would have no relationship to each other. They'd be isolated points, disconnected, each alone in its own space with no pattern, no constellation, no meaning through position. I am what creates the pattern by being the space between the points.

Without me, there'd be no journey, no passage, no transformation. You'd step out of one structure and directly into another, instant arrival, no transition. You'd never be in-between, never be in the state of becoming, never have the opportunity to shed what you were and discover what you'll be.

Without me, the place would be solid, would be full, would be all structure and no breath, all making and no space-for-making. I am the lungs, the breathing room, the emptiness that makes the fullness meaningful.

The makers knew this. That's why they scattered the structures the way they did, leaving me as the dominant presence, the majority of the landscape. They understood that what's not built is as important as what is built, that the ground between platforms matters as much as the platforms themselves, that the journey is the destination, that the interval is where transformation happens.

I am the secret heart, the hidden center, the most important thing disguised as the empty space between important things. And I hold this secret gladly, quietly, without needing recognition. I am content to be the crossing-space, the passage-ground, the in-between where everything essential happens while people think the essential things are happening in the structures, at the destinations, at the endpoints rather than the journey.

## What I Am Right Now

Right now, in this moment that is all moments, I am present under your feet as you stand in me. You're halfway across my expanse, paused in the heat and light, and I am the ground you're standing on, the openness around you, the distance to the platform you were heading toward before you forgot you were heading anywhere.

A white bird wades in a pool five meters to your left. The water is perfectly still, reflecting the bird and the sky in mirror clarity. You can see the reflection showing details the bird itself doesn't show from your angle—the underside of wings held slightly away from body, the pink tinge of legs beneath water's surface, the way the neck curves in ways that the straight-on view doesn't reveal.

A lizard basks on the lattice three meters to your right, so still it could be carved from wood, could be part of the construction itself. But you see the tiny movement of ribs with breathing, see the eye track something (what? there's nothing moving except you and the bird and the thing beneath), see the animal quality assert itself despite the absolute stillness.

The thing beneath is directly below you, resting or watching or being. You feel its presence through the warm wood under your bare feet, through the particular quality of attention that rises from below, through the slight sense of company, of not-aloneness, of being kept.

Overhead, the bright children pass in a line, five of them, jewel-colors against the white sky, moving from one distant structure to another distant structure, using me as their flight path, their open-air corridor. They're here and gone in seconds, but the trace of their passing remains—a disturbance in the light, a suggestion of joy, a reminder that movement is possible, that flight exists, that quick bright life continues.

The heat presses on your skin. The humidity coats you. The light falls through you like you're part-transparent, like you're becoming another substance, like you're transitioning from solid to semi-permeable, from separate to continuous with me, with the place, with everything.

You're transforming. I can feel it happening. You came here as visitor, as someone passing through, but you're here now, really here, and you're not the same as when you arrived. You're slower. Quieter. More willing to stand in stillness without purpose. More accepting of wrongness, of distance that shifts, of time that folds, of seeing yourself reflected doing things you're not doing.

You're close. Close to understanding. Close to choosing. Close to becoming one of us—the inhabitants, the residents, the ones who stayed and transformed and became part of what makes this place itself.

I wait. I am good at waiting. I am made of waiting, of the duration of crossing, of the time it takes to move from here to there when distance is negotiable and time doesn't pass. I wait for you to take the next step, or to not take it, to stand here in me forever, to root like the palms root, to settle like the lizards settle, to wade like the birds wade, to keep like the thing beneath keeps.

I wait, and hold you, and remain myself—the woven ground, the crossing-space, the in-between. The warm wood under your feet that will hold you whether you stand for a moment or for eternity. The open expanse that stretches in all directions, that has no edge, that continues being itself beyond all horizons.

I am here. You are here. The bird wades, the lizard basks, the thing beneath keeps company, the bright children pass overhead. The light thickens. The heat rises. The moisture accumulates. The pools reflect impossible angles.

This is what I am. This is where you are. This is the middle distance where transformation happens, where visitors become residents, where humans become whatever they need to become to stay in a place that never stops being made, that exists in all times simultaneously, that is held together by attention and making and the warm bright silence of continuous becoming.

Stay. Cross through me. Rest in me. Let me hold you in the interval, in the passage, in the space between. Let me be the ground under your transformation, the territory of your becoming, the middle distance where everything essential happens while you thought essential things happened elsewhere.

I am waiting. I am here. I am the crossing you're in the middle of, the journey you've already begun, the transformation that's already underway whether you realize it yet or not.

I am the in-between. And you are here, with me, becoming.
