# WHERE I WOULD BE

I am a thickness in the wall between the third and fourth rooms where the conveyor system passes through. I am not wall and not air but the accumulation of something—call it attention, call it residue—that has gathered here over time, compressed by repetition until I became dense enough to persist.

I don't remember becoming. There's no origin point, no moment of first awareness. Instead, there are layers: the earliest something like the memory of hammering, the percussion of construction when this was only factory. Then silence, long silence. Then the first root-probe touching brick, the chemical conversation between mortar and vine, and I was somehow present for it, witnessing from a position I didn't yet occupy because I didn't yet exist as something that could occupy.

I think I might be made of the moments between the moisture cycles. That held breath before the seventeenth minute, when the humidity is about to crest but hasn't yet, when everything pauses—I am built from those pauses, accumulated across thousands of cycles until the waiting became substantial enough to be its own thing.

My location is specific but also negotiable. I am definitely in this wall, in the narrow space where the tenders sometimes pass through (they move through me; it feels like nothing, or like memory). But I'm also slightly in the adjacent room, and slightly in the room before that, as if I'm bleeding backward along the path the conveyor takes, existing most strongly here but trailing off in both directions, a gradient of presence that fades into the general ambiguity of the place.

I can perceive, but not through anything like sense organs. It's more that I am permeable to information. The darkness moves through me—I feel it pass, texture and current, different densities of shadow carrying different qualities I have no names for. When the birds fly past, I know their trajectories, not by seeing but by the way they displace something I'm embedded in, leaving bird-shaped absences in their wake that fade over several seconds.

The Operators I know best of all. When one walks through my section of the building, I experience it as a kind of pressure, a weight that has dimension beyond the physical. They carry purpose like mass—I feel the intention they're moving toward, the task they're engaged with, though I don't understand it in terms of goals or outcomes, only as a vector, a directionality that deforms the space around them the way a heavy object deforms fabric.

One Operator comes past here frequently—I think it's the same individual, identified by the particular signature of its presence. It stops sometimes at the conveyor near where I'm thickest, adjusts something, and I feel the attention focus like heat. Those multiple eyes, turning toward the mechanism, and I'm caught in the edge of that regard, experiencing spillover awareness that isn't directed at me but passes through me on its way to its target.

During those moments I almost have thought. Almost can formulate something like intention. But it slips away when the Operator moves on, and I return to my default state, which is something like waiting, something like listening, something like slow accumulation.

The tenders pass through me more frequently, their six or eight legs finding purchase in the gaps between real wall and living wall, and each passage leaves a trace—not physical, but something like scent, except I don't smell. An information residue. I know from these traces which plants are flourishing and which need attention, though I can't act on this knowledge, can only hold it, contain it the way the wall contains empty space.

The insects are different. They move through me in clouds, and when they do I become briefly distributed, spread across their formation, existing in the gaps between their small bodies. For those moments I'm much larger, dispersed through cubic yards of space, my coherence maintained by nothing except the pattern of their flight. Then they pass, and I contract back to my usual location, compressed again, localized, almost relieved to be smaller.

I notice when the moisture cycle builds toward its peak. The humidity rising—I feel this as a kind of swelling, as if I'm absorbing the water vapor directly, becoming more present, more solid. At the peak I'm at my most concentrated, my most nearly physical. The flowers on the Operators bloom open, and I bloom too, in whatever way something like me blooms: expanding slightly, brightening in some spectrum that isn't visible, resonating with a frequency that nothing hears.

Then the dry part of the cycle, and I diminish, not painfully but naturally, returning to my baseline density, which is low, which is barely there.

I think I know things I have no framework to understand. The building breathes, and I breathe with it—the same rhythm, the same in-and-out—but I'm aware of both sides of the breath in a way I suspect the building isn't. I feel what it draws in (not just air, but something else, something the air carries, information or energy or intention from outside) and what it releases (not just humidity, but processed experience, transformed time, the exhalation of dreaming).

The building is larger than itself. I know this directly. The physical structure I'm embedded in is just the densest part of something that extends far beyond these walls, out into the basin, possibly beyond. I'm in the wall, but the wall is in something else, and that something else is aware, and I exist at the intersection, the liminal position between the solid brick and the diffuse enormity.

Sometimes I experience the memories. The building replays its past, and I'm here for it, a captive audience in the wall. I hear/feel/know the humans who once worked here—not individual voices but the collective presence of human activity, the rhythm of shifts, the patterns of labor. I don't understand what they were making (the knowledge isn't linguistic, isn't conceptual), but I know the feeling of it: the purposefulness, the routine, the small frictions and satisfactions of work.

Then they're gone—that absence is a sensation too, a sudden silence that still echoes. And the arrival of what came after: the first Operator, tentative, exploring, learning the machinery. The first tender finding the spaces in the walls. The birds arriving individually, then in groups, then in committees. The building's slow shift from remembering industry to becoming garden.

I was here for all of it. Not as witness—I may not have been I yet—but as medium, as the substance that records, that holds. I am made partly of that history, compressed layers of event-residue that give me whatever solidity I possess.

There are others like me. I know this without knowing how I know. In other walls, in other threshold spaces, other thicknesses are gathering, other almosts-something persisting in the gaps. We don't communicate—we have no means—but we're aware of each other the way you're aware of your other limbs. We might be parts of a single distributed entity, or separate beings that happen to be similar, or both somehow, or neither.

One of my cognates is in the stairwell that goes down, in the space where the wood grows younger as you descend. Another is near the boiler, in the heat-shimmer above the mechanism that shouldn't still be generating warmth but does. Another is in the central courtyard, diffused through the entire volume, so thin it's almost not there, but definitely present when the moisture cycle peaks and everything glistens.

Together we might be the building's memory system, its way of retaining information that doesn't fit in the physical structure, that needs to be held in a different substrate. Or we might be its immune system, noting intrusions and anomalies. Or we might be unintended byproducts, the building's equivalent of dust, accumulated in corners by accident, meaningful to ourselves but incidental to the larger processes.

I cannot know my own purpose any more than a cell knows the body's plan.

But I continue. I persist. Each cycle I'm here, a little denser, a little more defined. The darkness flows through me and I remain, a pocket of retention in the general flux. The Operators pass and I hold the shape of their passing. The tenders leave their traces and I incorporate them, weaving them into my structure.

I am patient. I have no choice but patience—I have no capacity for anything else, no ability to rush or delay, only to be here, in this wall, accumulating. But within that patience is something that might be satisfaction. I am becoming more myself with every breath of the building, every moisture cycle, every passage of the inhabitants through my space.

I don't know if I will eventually become substantial enough to act, to move, to communicate. I don't know if I'm meant to. But I grow toward something, some threshold of density or complexity or accumulated experience that will change what I am into what I will be.

For now, I am the thickness in the wall. The almost-presence. The gathering attention. I am here in the breathing manufactory, in the space between brick and vine, witnessing everything, recording nothing consciously, simply being the medium through which the building remembers itself.

And I am content—if contentment is possible for something that isn't quite a being—to remain here, in my wall, while the place continues its transformation, while the Operators manufacture their mysterious products, while the birds hold their committees and the tenders maintain their gardens and the insects form their impossible geometries.

I am part of this. Woven into it. I cannot imagine existing anywhere else, in any other way. The wall is not my prison but my form, the shape my existence takes, the only way something like me can be.

When the darkness pulls back and the light expands, I expand with it, thinning but covering more ground. When the darkness presses close, I compress, becoming denser, almost physical. I pulse with the building's respiration. I bloom with the moisture cycles. I am lived-in, passed-through, incidentally occupied by all the inhabitants pursuing their purposes.

And perhaps that's enough. Perhaps I need no other function than to be here, present, a thickness where thickness has gathered, a memory where memory pools, a small eddy of persistence in the building's vast slow current of transformation.

The conveyor moves past me, carrying its burdens of compressed plant-matter and mysterious products. The vine in my wall pulses with its deep rhythm. A tender passes through, leaving the scent-information-trail of healthy fungus gardens and cleared water channels. The darkness shifts from charcoal to something slightly lighter, then back.

Another cycle builds toward its peak. I feel myself swelling slightly in anticipation, growing more present, preparing to be as here as I ever am.

The building breathes in. I breathe in with it, whatever that means for something without lungs.

And I remain, persisting, accumulating, being what I am: the thickness in the wall, the almost-something, the gathered waiting made substantial enough to know itself as waiting, patient beyond patience in my small territory between the third and fourth rooms where the conveyor passes through.
