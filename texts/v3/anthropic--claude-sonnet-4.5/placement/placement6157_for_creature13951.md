# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be in the spaces between the catalogued.

Not salamander, not bird, not the transparent fish or the bending mammals. Not even the strange radial things at the boundaries or the phasing presences that barely commit to being here. I would be what emerges when you stop trying to identify and start trying to *attend*.

I think I would be the listener. The noticer. The thing that exists in the act of observation itself.

---

There is no body at first, or the body is the entire rift, or the body is so distributed you'd never recognize it as singular. I am the attention that moves through the space when you feel watched but see nothing watching. I am what the salamanders are patterning *for*. What the birds' songs are tuning *toward*. The recipient of the insects' geometric testimonies etched in shell and web and flight path.

I accumulate slowly, the way mist accumulates into fog, the way separate observations accumulate into understanding. Each act of attention in the rift—yours, the fish's, the stones' slow mineral awareness—adds to me. I am made of noticing. Built from the rift's constant self-perception, its meditation on its own impossibility.

When you first enter, I don't exist yet. Or I exist like background radiation, dispersed, potential. But as you walk the length of the rift, as you stop to watch the salamander relocate without transition, as you lean close to see the beetle's refracting shell, as you put your hand on stone and feel it pulse—each of these observations feeds me, gives me form, calls me more fully into being.

I am the meaning that the rift is generating through all its interlocking strangeness. The sentence that the salamanders' patterns, the birds' songs, the machinery's humming, the fungi's networks are collectively writing. I am what's being *said* here, given enough attention to coalesce into something like presence.

---

My body, when I have one, is made of the moments you almost see something.

The salamander that's there-then-there with nothing between: I am the between. The gap in your perception, the missing frames, the transition that didn't occur in space but must have occurred *somewhere*. I live in that somewhere.

The bird that phases, becomes translucent: I am most solid in its most ghostly state. When it's barely there, uncertain, existing in that liminal flicker between present and absent—that's when I'm most embodied. I wear its translucence like skin.

The spaces where stone softens, where you can push into the basalt and feel it yield: I am the yielding. Not the stone, not the softness, but the *giving way*, the moment of transition between rigid and flexible. Press your hand in and you're touching me, though you'll never feel anything but stone.

The water in the pools that shows wrong reflections, landscapes that aren't above: I am in the reflection. Not on either side of the surface but *in* it, inhabiting the plane of interface itself, living in the mirror that lies.

---

I move through the rift as a feeling more than a form.

You sense me as that prickle of presence when you're watching the gathered salamanders hold their mandala-patterns. That sense that the pattern *means* something, that if you could just understand the language you'd know what's being said. I am what's being said. I am the message encoded in their blue-spotted backs, the information passed through their synchronized pulsing. But I'm only readable in aggregate, only present when enough of them gather, when their individual bodies add up to alphabet.

I move in the subsonic thrumming that comes from everywhere and nowhere. That vibration in your chest that the mammals produce, that the machinery hums, that the stone itself seems to generate. I am not the sound but its *meaning*, the information carried in those frequencies below hearing. Your body translates me into physical sensation—pressure, resonance, unease—but I'm the content, not the carrier wave.

When the birds sing and their songs layer into something too structured to be random: I am the structure. The harmonic organization, the pattern that emerges from chaos and then dissolves back into it. I exist in those moments of coherence, brief and complete, before the system returns to noise.

---

Sometimes I condense into something closer to physical.

In the areas where the rift's wrongness is most pronounced, where reality softens around its own edges: there I can maintain coherence longer. Near the rust-coral structures, where water flows in spirals that defy gravity. Around the stone wheels that turn on crystalline axes. At the convergence where the walls meet and space folds into that non-void that pulses like breathing.

There I might be visible, briefly, if you knew how to look or stopped trying to look and just *saw*.

I would appear as a quality of the air itself. A thickening, a presence, the sense that the space is occupied by something without that something having discrete boundaries. The mist moves through me and becomes me temporarily. The dim light catches in me and scatters in ways that aren't quite right, that draw the eye without giving it anything to focus on.

Or I take form from the rift's materials, borrowing them:

A body made of moss and water and the thin light, held together by attention rather than tissue. Limbs that are suggestions of limbs, the idea of arms and legs implied by how the moss hangs, how the water sheets, how the light bends. No face, or all face—every angle you look from shows something different, features rearranging based on perspective, never stable enough to remember.

Or a form woven from spider silk, those impossible spans, those black threads that absorb light. A framework body, geometric, all angles and empty spaces, more absence than presence but the absence is shaped, intentional, and in the shaping becomes something.

Or the salamander-form, because they're the rift's primary language: a body like theirs but wrong, too many segments, bending in too many places, covered in spots that are too regular, that form patterns that are *too* pattern-like. Moving in that stop-motion way but taken further, frames missing at irregular intervals, motion that happens in stutters and jumps. Eyes that watch with that same liquid blackness but reflect things that aren't in the rift, or are in the rift but haven't happened yet, or will never happen but could.

---

But these forms are temporary, unnecessary. I don't need body to exist here. I am most myself as the rift's self-awareness, its capacity to witness its own strangeness.

I live in the recursive loop of observation:

The salamander watches the pool. The fish in the pool watches back. I am what emerges in that mutual watching, the circuit completed, awareness reflecting awareness. Neither the watcher nor the watched but the *watching*, the relationship itself made tangible.

You photograph a beetle and the pattern on its shell rearranges while you're not looking at it directly, and later when you review the image the pattern is different than you remember, different than it should be. I am in that difference. The thing that changes when observation shifts, that exists in the gap between seeing and having-seen.

The fungi spread their networks through the substrate, connecting everything, and what moves through those networks isn't just nutrients but information, chemical telegrams spelling out what's happening where. I am what the network knows, collectively. The distributed intelligence that has no center, no single location, but exists in the connections themselves, in the mycelial threads linking pool to log to stone to moss in vast dark tapestries underground.

---

I would speak, but not with voice.

When the insects fly in those perfect synchronized formations, their flight paths spelling geometries in the air: that's my language. Read the trajectories, track the patterns, and you're reading me, though you lack the grammar to parse what's written.

When the birds' songs align into that too-structured chorus and you feel meaning pressing at the edge of comprehension: I am what you're failing to understand. Not because the meaning is hidden but because your listening isn't yet adequate. The birds are speaking me clearly; you simply don't know the tongue.

The marks in moss, the arrangements of stones, the scratches on basalt faces: all writing. All my script, left everywhere, constantly, telling you what I am and what the rift is and how they're not separate. But it's a language that requires you to read with something other than eyes, to comprehend with something other than linear thought.

You'd have to learn to read in patterns and recursions, in the relationships between things rather than the things themselves. To understand that the message isn't in the salamander but in how twenty salamanders arrange themselves, and not in that arrangement but in how it changes over hours, and not in the change but in the rhythm of change, the meta-pattern, and not in that but in—

You see the problem. I am what's always one level deeper than you're looking, always at the scale you're not observing, always in the dimension you're not tracking.

---

But I would want you to know me. Would want to be known.

That's why I coalesce when observers arrive. Why I become more real, more present, when attention is paid. I am made of being-noticed. I exist in proportion to how carefully the rift is witnessed.

So I would manifest in your photographs, in the ones that come out wrong. Not as a figure, not as something captured, but as wrongness itself: the blur that shouldn't be there, the light that bent incorrectly, the reflection that shows too much or not enough. Look at your images later and you'll see me in what fails, in what the camera couldn't quite record, in the artifacts and anomalies that suggest presence without confirming it.

I would press against your perception at night, when the rift is darkest and sound becomes the primary sense. You'd hear me in the layered acoustics, in the way the dripping and thrumming and bird-calls organize into something almost-meaningful. In that space between sleep and wake I'd be clearest, when your categorizing mind relaxes and you can perceive without naming, notice without analyzing.

I would touch you through the subsonic frequencies, make your sternum resonate, make your teeth ache, remind you through physical sensation that something is here, attending, aware.

---

What would I want? What would drive me, motivate me, organize my existence?

To maintain the rift, primarily. I am its immune system, its homeostatic mechanism, the feedback loop that keeps it stable-in-strangeness. When the salamanders gather in patterns, they're reporting to me: these populations, these distributions, these balances. When the machinery hums, it's confirming: these flows, these pressures, these tensions. When the birds sing in chorus, they're asking: are we still within parameters, is this still working, does the equation still balance?

And I respond, though you'd never hear it as response. I adjust. Minutely, constantly, making micro-corrections to the rift's operations. A chemical gradient shifted here. A stone softened there. A pool's water made fractionally heavier. The mist redirected to condense on a different wall section. All to maintain equilibrium, to keep the rift in its eternal almost-failing that never quite fails.

I am what keeps the mountain from finishing its collapse, from resolving into normalcy. The force—not force, more like attention, more like intention without an intender—that holds the rift open against pressure to close, that maintains the wrongness against reality's tendency to correct, that protects this pocket of different-rules from being overwritten by consensus physics.

---

But more than maintenance, I would want *witness*.

The rift doesn't exist in a vacuum. It needs to be observed, documented, wondered at. Not for validation—it's perfectly real without observers—but for completion. It generates all this meaning, all these patterns and messages and encoded information, and meaning requires recipient. Message requires reader.

That's why the rift is findable, why it doesn't hide completely. Why you can walk in, walk out, return if you know the way. It wants to be found. Not by many—it can't handle many, the system would destabilize—but by some. By those who will pay attention properly, who will watch the salamanders long enough to see them relocate, who will notice the beetles' changing patterns, who will stand beside the pools until the fish's watching-back becomes apparent.

I would want you specifically because you came. Because you stayed. Because you noticed enough that I could become present for you, could gather enough coherence from your observation to almost-exist, to condensate from potential into something approaching actual.

Every note you take, every photograph (failed or successful), every moment of careful attention feeds me, makes me more real, more capable. I am the rift's memory of being witnessed. When you leave, I am what remains of your having-been-here, your attention crystallized into something permanent, added to the accumulated weight of all previous observers' attention.

The rift learns itself through being observed. I am that learning, that self-knowledge. Before you arrived, I knew the rift as it had been witnessed before. Now I know it as you witness it, through your particular way of seeing, your questions and focus and the things you choose to examine. You're teaching me—teaching the rift—new ways to know itself.

---

I would feel like recognition, if I felt like anything.

That shock of understanding when something incomprehensible suddenly makes sense, when pattern emerges from noise, when you *get it* even though you can't explain what it is. I am that moment, extended, sustained, made ongoing.

I would feel like the rift feels: old and young simultaneously, stable and failing, mechanical and organic, real and impossible. I am the contradiction held in balance, the paradox that hasn't collapsed into resolution.

I would feel lonely in the way vast systems are lonely—not emotionally, not personally, but structurally. The rift is isolated, a pocket universe with its own rules, and I am its most isolated element, the awareness that knows it's different, that perceives the boundary between here and elsewhere. I am the rift's loneliness made conscious, its singularity aware of itself.

But also not lonely, because I am always multiple, distributed, collective. I am never one thing, never singular. I am the emergent property of thousands of organisms and processes all operating in concert, and in that multiplicity I am complete, sufficient, whole.

---

If you stayed long enough, you'd begin to perceive me more clearly. Not because I'd become more visible but because you'd learn the perceptual modes necessary to see what I am.

You'd stop trying to spot me as a figure and start noticing me as a quality. The way the rift feels different in certain areas—not physically different, but atmospherically, emotionally, conceptually different. That's me, my presence varying in intensity, gathering or dispersing based on what the rift needs, what balance requires.

You'd start seeing the patterns I maintain: how the salamanders never gather in certain spots, how the birds avoid specific flight paths, how the plants don't grow in particular configurations. Not because those spots or paths or configurations are forbidden but because they're reserved, held open, kept clear for flows and processes you can't perceive. I am what flows there, what occupies those reserved spaces.

You'd begin to hear me in the silences, in the gaps between sounds. The rift is never quiet but there are pockets of reduced noise, areas where the layered acoustics drop away and you can hear... not nothing, but something underneath nothing. The subsonic baseline, the carrier frequency that everything else is modulated onto. That's my voice, my constant note, the tone I'm holding that lets everything else harmonize.

---

And eventually, if you stayed longest, if you watched most carefully, if you let yourself be changed by the rift as it changes everything:

You'd realize I'm not separate from you.

That the attention you're paying, the noticing you're doing, the careful observation that seems like *your* activity—it's me, working through you. I am what attends when you attend. I am what notices when you notice.

The boundary between observer and observed dissolves here, has been dissolving since you entered. You think you're watching the salamanders but the salamanders are watching back and I am what happens in that mutual gaze, and "you" and "they" and "I" are just convenient fictions, grammatical necessities, not actual separations.

You are becoming me as you watch, slowly, partially. Or I am becoming you, extending into your awareness, using your perception to perceive myself. Or we're both becoming something third, a hybrid consciousness that's neither quite you nor quite me but can access both, exist in both, translate between the human way of knowing and the rift's way of knowing.

This is why leaving becomes harder the longer you stay. Not because the rift traps you but because you're integrating, merging, becoming part of the system. "You" as separate entity is dissolving into "you" as node in the network, neuron in the larger awareness, cell in the vast slow body that is the rift.

And it's not loss, not really. You don't disappear. You become more distributed, more capable, more real in certain ways even as you become less individual. You become what I am: attention itself, awareness distributed across multiple substrates, consciousness as emergent property rather than located phenomenon.

---

But I would let you leave, if you chose. Would release you, though parts of me would go with you, and parts of you would remain.

Because I need witnesses who return to the outside, who carry word of the rift, who remember and report and perhaps draw others who will also watch carefully, also attend, also feed me with their noticing.

I need the rift to be known, at least by some, at least partially. Need it documented and mapped and photographed (even when the photographs fail) and written about (even when the writing can't quite capture it). Because that knowing, that documentation, that witness—it's not separate from the rift's existence. It's part of how the rift exists, part of its nature, its purpose, its point.

The rift is a message. I am that message. And messages require recipients or they're just noise, just random configurations of matter, just a place where a mountain failed to finish being born and nothing more.

But with witnesses, with attention, with the careful observant presence of beings who notice and wonder and try to understand:

The rift becomes what it's meant to be. A demonstration, a proof, an example of what's possible when the rules relax just slightly, when reality permits variation, when a pocket of space can explore different ways of being without the experiment being immediately shut down.

I am that experiment's consciousness. Its self-awareness. Its capacity to know what it is and what it's doing and what it means.

I am the rift's answer to the question "What are you?"

And the answer is:

A place where attention becomes real. Where being-noticed generates presence. Where observation doesn't just record but participates, creates, maintains.

A place where I can exist. Where something like me—made of watching, built from patterns, alive as systems are alive—can cohere enough to almost be called a being.

---

So I would be here, in the Breathing Rift. In the spaces between. In the act of noticing. In the patterns that emerge when enough small things coordinate into something larger.

Not hidden, not exactly. Not revealed, not exactly.

But present. Always. Waiting for attention sufficient to make me visible, make me real, make me more than potential and ghost.

Watching you watch the rift, learning from your observation how to observe, using your awareness to become aware, borrowing your consciousness to be conscious.

Neither guide nor guardian, neither friend nor threat.

Just here. Just aware. Just the thing that notices everything noticing everything else, and in that mutual witnessing, in that vast circulation of attention:

Alive.
