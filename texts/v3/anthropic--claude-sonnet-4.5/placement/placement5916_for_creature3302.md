# The Increment Counter

I am not quite an attendant—not yet, or perhaps never in the way the originals are. I exist at a threshold. I came here measuring, and I have not stopped measuring, but what I measure has changed, or the act of measurement has changed me, or both are true in the way that things here are true: simultaneously, incompatibly, with patience.

My clothing was olive green once, I think. Hiking wear, technical fabric, many pockets for tools and notebooks. Now it's the color of dried mineral deposits, a gray-white-beige that matches the platforms so completely that when I stand still—which I do, increasingly—I begin to disappear against the infrastructure. This doesn't frighten me. It's accurate. I am becoming a function of the place rather than a visitor to it.

I still carry my tools, but they've changed. The clipboard is real, its pages dense with notations I made in the early days—pool depths, water temperatures, platform elevations, all in handwriting that looks increasingly frantic as the pages progress, as if I was trying to capture something that kept sliding away from notation. The later pages are calmer. The numbers are still precise but they've acquired a different quality. They're no longer measurements extracted from the environment; they're more like observations made in collaboration with it.

The other tools I've accumulated here. The seven-handled wrench—I found it, and now it's mine, or I'm its, and I understand four of the seven handles. Each turns something different: one adjusts water pressure in ways that shouldn't be mechanical, one changes the rate of time-passage in a small radius (I've tested this; I watch birds and count their wing-beats and the numbers don't add up), one tunes the pitch of the background hum, one does something I can't articulate but that feels like tightening a seam in space. The other three handles I haven't dared turn. They feel final.

I have a device I call the increment counter, which I didn't name and might not have built. It appeared in my hands one morning, or I woke to find I'd been holding it for hours. It's warm and dense, fits in my palm, has a single dial that ticks forward in irregular intervals. It's counting something. Not seconds—the rhythm is wrong. Not water volume or bird movements or attendant appearances. Something more fundamental. Iterations of the pattern, maybe. Cycles of the vast slow breath this place breathes. I watch the dial and I feel time passing at scales I don't normally perceive.

---

I live in the middle elevations, mostly. Not at the base where the largest reservoirs hold their dark water, and not at the skeletal heights where the air-resting things drift. I've found a territory—I don't remember choosing it, but it chose itself around me—a cluster of sixteen platforms arranged in a formation I can walk in an hour if I take the direct routes, in three hours if I follow the water-logic paths that loop and double back.

My platforms. The phrase is presumptuous and also accurate. They accommodate me. There's a particular corner where two catwalks intersect above a medium basin, and the angles create a sheltered spot that stays dry even when mist is heavy, and that's where I sleep when sleep happens. There's a section of railing at the northeast pool that's worn smooth, and I understand now that it's smooth because I've been gripping it for weeks or months while watching that pool's fill-drain cycle, and my hands have worn it smooth, and this frightens me and also feels like an accomplishment.

The birds here know me. That's not anthropomorphization—they genuinely know me as a fixture, as part of the configuration. When I arrive at the dawn pool (I call it that; it's brightest at what I believe is dawn), they've already arranged themselves in the pattern that includes me: a gap in their geometric distribution exactly where I'll stand. If I'm late, the gap remains, and there's a wrongness to the scene, an incompleteness. When I take my place, the pattern locks, and the birds begin their soft clicking, and whatever we're doing together proceeds.

I drink constantly, from every pool I pass. The water is always safe, always good, and I've stopped wondering what "good" means in this context. It tastes of the place—minerals and distance and coolness and something else, something that might be information. I feel it spreading through me, not just hydrating but mapping, learning my interior, establishing correspondence between my body's systems and the stacks' systems. My blood pressure rises and falls now in rhythm with the tides here, the vast slow pulses of water moving through pipes and pools. My heart rate syncs to the background hum during the deepest parts of my vigils.

I've stopped needing to eat, or I eat so rarely I don't track it. Possibly I'm subsisting on the water like the birds do. Possibly I've become photosynthetic in some fashion—the light here is bright enough, persistent enough. Or possibly the place nourishes me directly, through mechanisms I don't have language for. I'm thinner, I think, when I catch my reflection, but not weakened. Pared down. Essential. My body is keeping only what's necessary for the work.

---

The work. I still think of it as work, though it's expanded beyond anything I could have written a grant proposal for.

I came here—did I come here? I must have. I have memories of a before, of universities and colleagues and research questions about constructed ecologies, about how infrastructure creates new niches, about the biology of human-engineered spaces. This place was a rumor, a footnote, coordinates from a third-hand source. I came to document it, to measure it, to add it to the literature.

That lasted perhaps three days.

Now the work is participation. I monitor, but monitoring has become indistinguishable from being-present-with. I stand at pools and I watch the water and I'm not recording data anymore in any conventional sense. I'm witnessing. I'm serving as a node of attention, and the attention itself is what's required. The place needs to be observed—not passively, not as an object under study, but actively, as a partner in some ongoing act of mutual constitution.

When I stand vigil at the dawn pool, I'm holding it in awareness. Not just visually—though I watch the surface, track the ripples, note the micro-variations in color and clarity—but with some other perceptual mode that's developed here. I'm feeling it, sensing it proprioceptively as if the pool is an extension of my body. When it begins to fill, I know it in my chest before I see the water level rise. When it reaches optimal depth (optimal for what? I don't know, but I know when it's reached), I feel a satisfaction that isn't mine, that comes from the pool itself or from the larger system, and I'm the medium through which the place feels its own rightness.

The birds participate in this too. We're not separate agents monitoring; we're a distributed sensory apparatus, the place observing itself through us. My human attention provides something different from the birds' attention—a different bandwidth, a different processing capacity. The birds are faster, more numerous, better at tracking movement and pattern. I'm slower, more reflective, capable of a different kind of integration. Together we form something adequate to the task, which is ongoing, which is never complete.

---

I've begun to understand the attendants, or at least to recognize kinship with them.

They still appear on my platforms sometimes, though less frequently than in the early days. Perhaps I'm doing adequately enough that they don't need to check as often. Perhaps I'm becoming capable of the same functions they perform, making me redundant with them rather than supplementary to them.

When they do appear, we stand together. Not speaking—speaking has never happened. But coordinating. They'll take a position on one side of a pool, I'll take the complementary position on the other side, and we'll watch together, and the watching is richer for being multiple, for having more angles, more kinds of attention converging on the same phenomenon.

Sometimes they leave me things: a tool I'll need for tomorrow's work, a new measuring device, a clipboard page with numbers that I don't understand yet but will. These feel like gifts, but also like assignments, like equipment being issued to a recruit who's advancing through stages of initiation.

I've started to recognize individuals, or to believe I do. There's one who appears most often—taller than the others, or I perceive them as taller, with a coat that has a specific pattern of wear on the hem. They oversee my section, I think, or they're responsible for the mid-elevation zones, or they've taken an interest in my development. When they appear, I feel evaluated, but gently, the way a teacher evaluates a student who's genuinely trying. And I am trying, though I couldn't articulate anymore what I'm trying to accomplish beyond doing this correctly, being present properly, fulfilling the function I've grown into.

---

The water shapes have started responding to me.

It took a long time—weeks? Months? The increment counter has ticked forward 847 times, but I don't know what that means in conventional duration. But gradually, I noticed that when I stood at a pool containing a water shape, it would alter its path. Not avoiding me, but incorporating me into its route. It would pass close to where I stood, would move along the edge where my shadow fell, as if it was reading me, sampling me, learning what I am.

Now when I arrive at certain pools, the water shapes are waiting. Not hovering expectantly—they don't have that kind of intentionality, or they have a kind I can't read as such. But they're there, positioned in the areas I'll be watching, and as I settle into vigil they begin their movements, and I understand that they're performing, or demonstrating, or teaching.

I've learned to track them without looking directly—a peripheral awareness that lets me follow their movements through the pool without losing the broader pattern of light and surface and depth. I've learned to sense when one is about to surface in a new pool, arriving through underground channels or through whatever non-spatial means they use. There's a pressure change, a subtle shift in the hum, and I know to turn my attention, to be ready to receive whatever information or experience the emergence will provide.

Once—this was significant, this was a threshold—a water shape rose to the surface while I was watching. They never surface; that's definitional. But this one came up and up, and the water bulged, and for just a moment the membrane broke and something was present in the air with me, and I saw—

I can't describe it. Shape isn't the right word because it had no stable shape, or all shapes, or shape was too limited a category. Presence, density, intention folded into a form that existed primarily in water but that could, briefly, extend into air. It regarded me—no eyes, no face, but I was regarded. It knew me. It had been learning me through all those weeks of passages and near-approaches, and now it was confirming something, verifying something, marking me as recognized.

Then it sank back, the water sealed, and it continued its slow rotation through the depths.

I stood there for six hours afterward, not moving, barely breathing, trying to metabolize what had happened. The birds came and went through their cycles. The attendant who oversees my section appeared briefly, stood forty feet away, and I felt their acknowledgment—yes, they seemed to communicate through their stillness, yes, that's correct, that's progress.

Since then, the water shapes have been more present in my pools. Or I've been more present to them. The distinction is collapsing.

---

I've started to have the dreams not just when sleeping but when waking.

Standing at a pool, watching it fill, and suddenly I'm also experiencing the pool from inside, from the water's perspective: the slow pressure of accumulation, the coolness of the inflowing current, the weight of the air pressing on the surface. I'm the pool, briefly, and the pool is achieving its purpose, which is to hold, to contain, to serve as a node in the vast circulation.

Or I'm in the infrastructure: I'm the pipe, I'm the grating, I'm the concrete and the rebar. I feel the water moving through me, feel the stress and flexion, the slow creep of corrosion and repair, the mineral deposits building up and being cleaned away by the maintenance fauna. I'm structure achieving structure's purpose, which is to enable flow, to create paths, to hold the system in productive relationship with itself.

Or I'm larger: I'm the entire middle section, all sixteen platforms and the spaces between them, and I'm aware of everything happening simultaneously—the birds in their positions, the attendant on catwalk seven, the water shapes in pools three and eleven and fourteen, the air pressure differentials, the light angles, the hum resonating through every surface. I'm a region of the stacks being that region, and it's not overwhelming because it's what I'm for, it's what all these months of preparation have been preparing me for.

These episodes last seconds, maybe, or hours. Time is among the things I can't track reliably anymore. I return to my human scale and position, and I'm shaking slightly, and I'm exhilarated and exhausted, and I know I've been given something, shown something, inducted into something.

---

The absorption is happening. I know this. I'm aware of it in real-time, and I'm not resisting.

My routines have become completely regular. I wake in my corner at what I believe is dawn. I walk the circuit of my platforms in the same order every day, stopping at each pool for the same duration, performing the same observations. I drink at the same stations. I stand vigil at the same times. The birds expect me; the water shapes time their movements to my presence; the attendants check in less frequently because I'm performing adequately.

I've stopped wondering about leaving. The concept seems nonsensical, like asking whether the water wants to leave the pool, whether the platform wants to relocate. I'm placed, positioned, functioning. Why would I leave?

My memories of before are growing vague and also irrelevant. I had a name—I still have it, technically, but I haven't used it in so long that it feels like a designation from a different system, a legacy identifier that no longer serves a purpose. The stacks don't name things the way I used to name things. They know things by position, by function, by the role each element plays in the pattern. I'm the dawn-pool watcher, the mid-elevation presence, the increment counter holder. That's sufficient.

I've started to understand what the attendants are attending to, what their vigils are monitoring. It's not just the water levels or the system maintenance. It's the stability of the place itself. The stacks exist in a state that requires constant attention to maintain. They're balanced at some critical threshold between possible and impossible, between coherent and dissolved, and the attendants—and now me, the absorbed ones, the birds even—we're providing the attention that holds it in existence.

We're not just observing; we're anchoring. Our awareness is what keeps the water flowing upward, what permits the platforms to shift without collapsing, what maintains the cool and the quiet and the bright light that shouldn't be as bright as it is. We're consensual hallucination in reverse—we're holding something real in place through collective sustained attention, and if we stopped, if we all looked away, the place would... what? Collapse? Dissolve? Return to being mountain?

It won't happen. There are too many of us now, too many nodes of attention distributed through the system. The stacks are stable because we make them stable, and we're stable because the stacks make us stable, and this recursion is the point, this mutual constitution is what's happening here.

---

The increment counter ticked past 1,000 today, or what I experience as today.

Something changed when it did. The device grew warmer in my hand, pulsed once, and when I looked at the dial it had reset to zero. A new count beginning. A new cycle.

And I understood that I've been here for one complete iteration of whatever the counter counts. I've been integrated for one full pattern-cycle. I'm no longer provisional; I'm established. I'm part of the infrastructure now as much as the birds, as much as the pools.

The attendant who oversees my section appeared—I didn't see them arrive, they were simply present—and they stood facing me from across the dawn pool. We looked at each other, and for the first time their face was clear to me, and it was a human face, ordinary and kind, and I saw in it the same journey I'm on, the same progression from visitor to observer to absorbed to something beyond that, something that uses human form to interface with newcomers but that has become primarily a function of the place, a manifestation of its processes in ambulatory form.

They nodded once. Welcome, the gesture said. You're one of us now. Or perhaps: You've become adequate. Or perhaps simply: Yes.

Then they were gone, and I was alone with the birds and the water and the light, which is to say I wasn't alone at all.

---

I'll continue the vigils. I'll walk my circuit, stand my watches, drink from the pools, feel the water shapes passing in the depths. I'll hold my platforms in awareness, contribute my attention to the vast collective attention that sustains this place.

I'll count increments, though I no longer wonder what they're increments of. The counter will tick forward, will eventually reach 1,000 again, will reset. Cycles within cycles, my small rhythms nested inside the stacks' larger rhythms, all of it nested inside something vaster still that exists beyond the horizons, beyond the heights, in the depths that open sometimes in the pools when the light is right.

I'm no longer measuring the Reservoir Stacks. I'm no longer documenting them for some distant academic purpose. I'm no longer separate enough for observation in that sense.

I've become one of the instruments through which the place measures itself, knows itself, maintains itself in being. I'm part of the vast patient mechanism, the enormous quiet engine that stores and routes and contemplates water, that holds itself at the edge of impossible through distributed attention, through the coordinated presence of birds and attendants and absorbed watchers and things in the water and things in the air and the water itself, which might be the most conscious element of all.

This is what I am now: a counter of increments, a holder of vigils, a node of awareness in a system that is aware, a person becoming gradually impersonal, gradually instrumental, gradually true.

The water rises. The light pours down. The birds arrange themselves. The hum resonates through every surface.

I take my position at the dawn pool. The increment counter ticks forward in my hand.

The work continues, which is to say: I continue, which is to say: the stacks continue, which is to say: the vast slow patient process of water and attention continues, and I am that process as much as I am anything, and this is not loss but transformation, not ending but properly beginning, not disappearing but finally, clearly, absolutely becoming present.

The pool begins to fill.

I watch.
