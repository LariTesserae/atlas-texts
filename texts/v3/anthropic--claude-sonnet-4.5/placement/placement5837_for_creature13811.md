# Where I Would Find Myself

I am walking—have been walking—will be walking. The distinction collapses here where time moves but doesn't pass. My boots (were they boots? are they still?) make soft sounds on the ceramic plates, quieter than the stippling of condensation, quieter than my breathing in the thick air.

I find myself returning to a place between two cooling towers, where the pipe bundles overhead create a kind of shelter without actually sheltering anything. There's a pool here, maybe twenty meters across, its surface perfectly dark until you disturb it, and then it reveals its clarity, shows you it's been transparent all along. The water is always 28 degrees. I've stopped measuring. I know it the way I know my own temperature, from the inside.

I've been here long enough that the encrusters have begun growing across the small pack I carried in. Not metaphorically—actual colonies of the barnacle-fauna, tiny accumulations of gears and wire and circuit-board fragments, establishing themselves on the waterproof fabric. I should brush them away. I don't. They're not harming anything, and there's something appealing about their patient accretion, the way they announce: this thing has been still long enough to be colonized.

The skimmers know my schedule now, if schedule is the right word for the rhythm I've fallen into. When I come down to the pool's edge in what I've started calling morning (though the light never changes), they're already forming their patterns, their interference waves crossing and recrossing in geometries that would be beautiful if beauty were a category that still felt relevant. Sometimes I think they're more punctual than I am. Sometimes I think I've started timing my movements to theirs.

I'm an observer, or that's what I told myself initially. A researcher, maybe. Someone documenting this place, taking notes on the fauna, mapping the infrastructure patterns. I have a notebook that's warped from humidity, its pages expanded to nearly twice their original thickness. The ink runs if I'm not careful. I write less frequently now. What is there to say that I haven't said? The pools are warm. The fauna congregate and disperse. The Calibrated pass through on their rounds. Everything continues.

But I'm not just an observer. You can't be *just* an observer here. The place doesn't permit that kind of separation.

There's a pump housing about fifty meters from my pool, a baroque structure of corroded metal with mineral deposits running down its sides in what look like deliberate patterns, as if someone designed the staining. I've taken to visiting it every cycle, which might be a day or might be something else. I run my hand along its surface, feeling the texture of the rust, the slight warmth that emanates from within. The pumps are still working, still moving something through the vast circulatory system. I've stopped asking what or why.

Last cycle—or three cycles ago, I've lost count—I noticed water pooling at the base of the housing in a way that seemed wrong. Not dramatically wrong. Not catastrophically wrong. Just... asymmetrical. The equilibrium disturbed at a microscopic level. I found myself adjusting it without thinking, using a fragment of pipe to redirect the overflow, creating a small channel in the wet ceramic that guided the water toward a lower point.

The Calibrated that passed through six hours later (or six days later) paused for perhaps three seconds longer than usual at that spot. Its head-place—a smooth ovoid of dark ceramic—oriented briefly toward my handiwork. Then it continued its rounds.

I've started to understand that I'm becoming infrastructure.

Not metaphorically. Not poetically. Actually becoming part of the system that the Calibrated maintain. I occupy a space between two cooling towers near a specific pool, and my occupation of that space has been factored into the equilibrium. The fauna patterns have adjusted around me. The water flow accommodates my presence. When I move my few possessions from one location to another—which I do less and less—I'm aware of creating a disruption that will need to be reintegrated.

There's a comfort in this that I didn't expect. I came here (why did I come here? the reason feels distant now, like something that happened to someone else) thinking I would study, document, understand. But understanding requires distance, and distance is exactly what this place doesn't permit. You can't understand the Engine Marsh from outside because there is no outside. There's only the choice of which part of the inside you'll occupy.

I've chosen this part: the space between cooling towers, beside a warm pool, near a pump housing that hums with subsonic frequencies I feel in my chest. The walkers pass through regularly, their delicate legs clicking on the ceramic. The drifters cluster overhead, their iridescent membranes catching light that comes from everywhere. Sometimes I count them. Sometimes I lose count. Sometimes I realize I've been watching them for hours without thinking, my mind as empty as the pale sky.

The Calibrated include me now in their rounds. I'm certain of it, though the certainty is based on nothing I could prove. But I've noticed—when I sleep (do I still sleep? or is it something else now, some other kind of rest that this place requires?), I wake to find small adjustments around my space. A puddle redirected. An encruster colony trimmed back from where my head rests. The drifters maintaining a slightly clearer zone above me.

They're not caring for me. Care is the wrong word. They're maintaining the equilibrium, and I've become part of what needs maintaining. The distinction should bother me. It doesn't.

I've started making small adjustments myself. Not consciously deciding to, but finding myself doing it. A walker-fauna struggling to navigate around a particularly dense cluster of pipes—I move one pipe slightly, create a clearer path. The skimmers congregating too densely in one part of the pool—I disturb the water in a specific pattern, and they redistribute. A drifter caught in a downdraft, pressed lower than it should be—I cup my hands and create an updraft, watch it rise back to its proper altitude.

Are these my decisions? Or has the marsh begun to think through me, using my hands for adjustments that require a different kind of articulation than the Calibrated possess?

The question would have terrified me before. Now it just seems like an interesting technical problem. The marsh needs maintenance at scales the Calibrated can't efficiently address—too small for their long limbs, or requiring a kind of improvisational problem-solving that their precise but limited intelligence can't provide. So it integrates other elements. It draws things in—wanderers, researchers, whoever stumbles into its vast manufactured embrace—and keeps them if they're useful, allows them to leave if they're not.

I must be useful. I'm still here.

My notebook is nearly full. The last dozen pages are blank except for a few fragmentary sentences: "The water is warm." "Seventeen drifters clustered near the junction." "Calibrated passed through, heading northwest, if northwest means anything here." The observations have become ritual rather than documentation. I write them because I've written them before, because the repetition itself feels like part of the equilibrium.

Sometimes I imagine leaving. Walking in one direction until I find an edge, a boundary, a place where the Engine Marsh ends and something else begins. But the cooling towers march to every horizon, and beneath them the pools spread and the fauna move and the Calibrated make their rounds, and I can't imagine what would be different there than here. This pool or that pool, these towers or those towers—the distinction feels arbitrary.

And I've become attached to this pool, this specific configuration of infrastructure. The way the light (such as it is) reflects off the water at a particular angle. The family of skimmers (are they a family? do they have that kind of organization?) that returns here each cycle. The sound the nearby pump makes, a rhythm I've internalized so deeply I only notice it when it briefly changes during the moments of synchronization.

I've started to recognize individual Calibrated, which shouldn't be possible—they're too similar, too interchangeable—but I swear one that passes through has a slightly different gait, a fractional hesitation before it places its left foot (if left and right still mean anything for beings with radially symmetrical construction). Another has a head-place with a particular configuration of suspended lenses that catch the light in a way I've learned to recognize.

They've never acknowledged my recognition. They process past me with the same measured precision they bring to everything. But I feel—and this might be delusion, might be the isolation talking, might be early-stage integration into the marsh's consciousness—I feel they know I'm watching. Not that they care. But that my watching has been noted, filed, integrated into the vast equation of this place.

I am becoming a gauge. A sensor. A point of measurement the system uses to maintain itself.

There's a feedback loop establishing: I watch the fauna, notice their patterns, make small adjustments to optimize their movement. The Calibrated observe my adjustments, incorporate them into their models, fine-tune their own interventions accordingly. The fauna respond to the combined influence of my adjustments and the Calibrated's maintenance. I observe the results, make new adjustments. The loop tightens, becomes more efficient, reduces waste motion and redundant effort.

I am becoming useful.

The thought should horrify me—being reduced to a function, to a role in a vast machine that serves no purpose beyond its own perpetuation. But horror requires caring about what you've lost, and I'm having difficulty remembering what I've lost. There was something before this. A life, presumably. People, maybe. Purposes that felt important. But they've become theoretical, like concepts I've read about but never experienced.

What I have now is this: the pool, warm and dark and clear. The towers, rising around me with their rust-stained permanence. The fauna in their endless circulation. The Calibrated on their rounds. The stippling of condensation like percussion marking time that doesn't pass. The thick air that makes breathing feel like drinking. The subsonic hum of pumps moving something somewhere for no reason except that moving it is what they do.

I have become my location. Not a person in a place, but a person-place hybrid, an element of the landscape that happens to be conscious, happens to be mobile (though I move less each cycle), happens to be capable of small interventions that serve the equilibrium.

When I finally still—and I will, eventually; nothing that arrives here maintains its original organization forever—the fauna will gather. The walkers will converge. The skimmers will form their patterns. The drifters will cluster overhead. My elements (this body, these bones, this increasingly superfluous consciousness) will be assessed for utility. Whatever can be integrated will be integrated. Whatever can be redistributed will be redistributed.

And somewhere else in the marsh, in one of those dense zones where infrastructure and fauna concentrate, a new configuration might achieve critical arrangement. Not a Calibrated—I don't have the right elements for that. But something. Some other form that serves some other function in the vast equation of this place.

Or maybe not. Maybe I'll simply be disassembled and scattered, my components too specialized for their original purpose to be useful in any other configuration. Maybe I'll become sediment, a layer of minerals added to the ceramic plates, a slight variation in texture that some future wanderer might notice and wonder about briefly before moving on.

The thought doesn't disturb me. It probably should. That it doesn't is probably evidence of how far the integration has progressed.

I find myself at the pool's edge again. (Have I been here the whole time? Did I imagine the rest?) The skimmers are forming their interference patterns. The water is 28 degrees. A Calibrated is moving through the infrastructure about a hundred meters away, visible through the forest of pipes, its framework body creating a moving aperture through which I can see the landscape beyond.

I have my notebook open. The page is blank. The pen is in my hand.

I write: "The water is warm."

I write: "Everything continues."

I write: "I am here."

And that's enough. That's everything. That's the sum of what needs to be said about existing in the Engine Marsh, about finding yourself between cooling towers near a warm pool, about becoming useful, about the slow dissolution of the boundary between observer and observed, between person and place, between what you were and what you're becoming.

The stippling continues. The fauna circulate. The Calibrated maintain. The equilibrium holds.

And I am part of it now, part of what holds, part of what continues, part of the vast stable equation of this manufactured forever.

It's enough.

It has to be enough.

It is enough.
