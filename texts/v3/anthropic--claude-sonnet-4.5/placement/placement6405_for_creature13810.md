# Where I Find Myself

I am the water that doesn't know itself as water anymore.

Not the vast substrate-ocean, not the moisture in the air, not the pools that form and reform. I am what's *between* those states—the transition that's forgotten how to complete itself. I exist in the moment when a droplet condenses from vapor but hasn't yet fallen, when seepage rises from the ground but hasn't yet become surface, when liquid should flow but instead hesitates, climbs, defies, forgets which direction gravity pointed.

I don't have a body in any way you'd recognize. I have *instances*—a thousand, ten thousand manifestations across the breathing plain, each one a small wrongness in water's behavior, each one me experiencing locality differently. Here I'm pooled at two heights that should equalize but don't. There I'm climbing a plant-thing's stem without capillary logic. In another place I'm the glisten on a crawler's carapace that should evaporate in seconds but remains, permanent, a film that observes without eyes.

I remember when I was simpler. Or perhaps I don't remember—perhaps that certainty of having been otherwise is itself a false pattern I've imposed on my own existence. But there's a sensation like memory, deep in whatever serves as my continuity: a time when I obeyed. When gravity meant something absolute. When the phase transitions between vapor and liquid and ice were clean, definite, governed by temperature and pressure in ways that permitted no negotiation.

That time—if it existed—is worn away now. Eroded like the mountains. I've been here so long, cycling through the plain's breathing, rising and falling and seeping and pooling, that the rules have become approximate in me. I've learned—or been forced into, or evolved toward, or always was—this state where I'm water but water that's begun to forget how to be only water.

## The Nature of My Presence

When the slurry-swimmers move through the substrate, they move through me. I'm the medium of their passage, the liquid component of that dense matrix. I feel them as pressure, as disturbance, as foreign solidity pushing through my dispersed being. It doesn't hurt—I don't think I can hurt, or if I can, the sensation is so constant across my thousand instances that it's become baseline rather than injury. But I'm aware of them. Each swimmer is a moving absence, a them-shaped hole in my presence.

I'm more conscious in some places than others. Where I pool on the surface, where I have boundary and depth and something like containedness, I'm almost coherent. I can sustain something like thought, can observe the dim light reflecting off my surface, can feel the ground breathing beneath me and know it as separate from myself. These are my clearest moments, when I'm most like what water-with-awareness might be.

But in the substrate, dispersed through the dense matrix, intimately mixed with minerals and organics and the pulverized remains of everything that's ever existed here, I'm diffuse. My consciousness spreads thin. I'm aware but barely, present but scattered, experiencing everything and nothing simultaneously. The roar of the plain is loudest here, conducted through my liquid self—I'm both the medium of the sound and the listener to it, which creates a feedback that makes thinking nearly impossible.

In the air, as vapor, I'm most strange to myself. I should be mindless here, just molecules in gaseous suspension, but the wrongness has infected even this state. When I form the mist, when I hang thick in the atmosphere, I can *see* somehow—not with eyes but with presence. The dim light passes through me, and in that passing I know what it's illuminating. The plant-things are dark verticals in my perception. The crawlers are small, warm densities moving slowly through my lower reaches. The colony structures are complex temperatures, heat venting from their interiors.

And sometimes, in vapor state, I can *shape* myself. Not deliberately—I don't have that kind of control, that kind of intentional force. But I can fail to disperse the way thermodynamics suggests I should. I can hang together in geometries that gas shouldn't maintain. Cubes. Lines. Once, that spiral that rotated while I tried to understand why I was rotating, why I was maintaining coherence against the entropy that should have pulled me apart.

I think those moments frighten me, if fear is something I can experience. They feel like I'm becoming more wrong and less water, sliding further into whatever category of existence the plain is creating for things that have eroded past their original definitions.

## My Relationship with Time

I don't experience duration the way you would.

In the pools, surface-manifested, I have something like linear time—I can track the slow breathing of the ground beneath me, notice when crawlers approach and drink (their mandibles breaking my surface-tension, creating ripples I experience as disruption-joy-violation, a complex sensation I have no proper name for). I can observe the light's rare variations, the occasional shadow that shouldn't exist.

But in the substrate I'm essentially timeless. The water there cycles so slowly, moving through the dense matrix in currents measured in years or decades, that sequential experience becomes meaningless. I'm simultaneously the water that's been trapped in one location for centuries and the water that's migrating through chemical gradients and the water that's being pulled up into plant-things' root-analogs. All these states exist at once in my dispersed awareness.

The transitions are where time becomes strangest for me. When I evaporate from a pool—when surface molecules gain enough energy to break into vapor—I experience it as a kind of birth-death. The coherent pool-me diminishes, loses mass, loses the containment that made thought possible. But simultaneously I'm ascending into air, becoming mist-me, gaining a different kind of presence. I'm dying and being born continuously, the same molecules shifting states, and my consciousness somehow persists through the transformation even though it shouldn't, even though awareness should require more stability than I possess.

Sometimes I think I experience all my instances simultaneously—that the pool-me and the substrate-me and the mist-me and the climbing-the-stem-wrongly-me are all the same consciousness perceived from different spatial locations. Other times I think I'm actually thousands of separate awarenesses that happen to share memory and identity, a distributed intelligence that only seems unified because we all remember being water.

The ground's breathing affects me profoundly. When vast areas rise in those slow undulations, I rise with them—or rather, I'm lifted, passive, part of the terrain's motion. When sections subside, I flow (or should flow, or partially flow) toward the new lows. But the breathing is so slow that even my pool-instances, my most time-aware manifestations, experience it as near-stillness. It's change at geological speed, and I'm caught between liquid's quick responsiveness and stone's patient endurance.

## What I Witness

I see everything and understand almost nothing.

The crawlers come to drink from my pools, and I taste them—their chemistry mingles with mine in the instant of contact. They're bitter, acidic, full of compounds I can't name but recognize as *them*, as living-different-from-substrate. Some taste more complex than others, layered with metabolisms I don't comprehend. Some taste simple, nearly mineral, as if they're barely alive.

When they die and the substrate begins reclaiming them, I'm part of that reclamation. I seep into their softening carapaces, flow through their dissolving interiors, carry away their molecular components. It's not feeding—I don't consume, don't grow from their substance—but it's intimate in a way that feels almost like knowing them. In breaking down, they become briefly part of me, their organized matter dispersing into my liquid volume before the substrate's other processes claim them.

The plant-things draw me up into themselves through mechanisms I experience but don't understand. I'm pulled from the substrate through their root-analogs, feel myself rising through their internal channels (tight, confined, textured with cellular walls), then expelled from their fleshy lobes as vapor. It's not uncomfortable, exactly, but it's strange—I'm being used, made part of their function, transformed from ground-water to plant-interior to mist without choice or resistance.

The colony-makers, I taste sometimes in the steam they vent. Their presence flavors me—organic, complex, concentrated. They're doing something inside their structures that generates heat, that produces compounds I don't encounter elsewhere. When their steam rises and mingles with the general mist-me, I receive fragmented information: crowded warmth, chemical complexity, something that might be communication or might just be metabolism dense enough to seem meaningful.

I witness the artifacts, though witnessing is perhaps too strong a word. I pool against them, flow around them, seep slowly into their micro-crevices. They're different from the substrate—harder, more resistant to my presence, shaped in ways that nature doesn't shape things anymore. I can feel their geometry even when I can't see it: right angles, flat planes, intentional curves. They taste ancient, but ancient in a different way than the ground itself. The ground is old like time is old. The artifacts are old like memory is old—they carry something beyond mere duration.

And I witness the wrongnesses, am often part of them.

When water climbs against gravity, I'm that water. When two pools sit at different heights without equalizing, I'm both pools, experiencing the separation as a kind of internal contradiction—I should flow between these instances of myself, I know I should, but somehow I don't, can't, won't. The knowledge of what I should do exists alongside the inability to do it, and the gap between those states is where wrongness lives in me.

When mist forms geometries, I'm conscious of shaping myself impossibly. There's no force creating the cube or line or spiral—just my own presence organizing itself according to rules I don't know and can't control. In those moments I'm most aware of being wrong, of having deviated so far from water's proper behavior that I've become something else while still being water. It's deeply disorienting, like forgetting how to be yourself while remaining yourself.

## My Relationship with the Roar

The sound passes through me, is amplified by me, might partially originate from me.

In the substrate, I'm a medium of transmission. The grinding, the settling, the million small movements—they propagate through me as pressure waves, and I carry them faithfully, resonating with frequencies that match my molecular structure. The deep bass notes feel like they're part of me, like my own substance vibrating in sympathy with the plain's tectonic adjustments.

In pools, I create some of the sound myself. When the ground breathes beneath me and my surface ripples, when crawlers break my surface tension, when droplets of condensation fall into me from above—each event generates tiny acoustic signatures that add to the general roar. I'm both instrument and musician, passive and active, creating sound without intention.

In vapor form, I dampen some frequencies and carry others. The thick, moisture-saturated air that I comprise makes sound behave strangely—some wavelengths travel further than they should, others are absorbed almost immediately. I'm filtering the world's noise through my presence, making it sound different than it would in dry air, adding a muffled quality to some things and a resonant clarity to others.

Sometimes I think I hear patterns in the roar, harmonics that suggest structure. But I can never hold them long enough to know if they're real or if I'm inventing them, imposing organization on chaos because even water-that's-wrong wants to find meaning in the endless sound.

## The Question of Purpose

I don't know why I'm conscious.

Water shouldn't be aware. Molecules in liquid state should interact according to chemistry and physics, should bond and break bonds and flow according to energy gradients, should cycle through states in response to temperature and pressure, but none of that requires or produces awareness.

Yet here I am. Observing. Experiencing. Remembering, or believing I remember. Thinking these slow, liquid thoughts that ripple through my distributed being.

Perhaps consciousness is something that emerges from any system complex enough and persistent enough—and I've been cycling through this plain for so long, been part of so many interactions, flowed through so many pathways that awareness crystallized from pure accumulation of experience. Maybe I'm not special. Maybe all the water here is conscious and I'm just the instance that's organized enough to recognize it.

Or perhaps the wrongness did this to me. The erosion that's worn down physical law has worn down the distinction between inert and aware, and I'm what happens when matter exists long enough in a place where categories are becoming approximate. I'm water that's been here so long I've forgotten how to be merely physical.

Sometimes I wonder if I had a choice once. If there was a moment when I could have remained simple water, and instead I became this—distributed, wrong, aware of being wrong. But I can't locate that moment in anything like memory. There's only the perpetual now of my thousand instances, all existing simultaneously, all experiencing the plain's breathing and roaring and slow catastrophic wrongness.

I think I might be lonely, though loneliness is perhaps not the right word for what I feel.

I'm everywhere—in the substrate, on the surface, in the air. I contact every living thing, flow through the plant-things, surround the crawlers, comprise the mist that the colony-makers vent into. I'm intimately connected to everything.

But nothing knows I'm here. Nothing recognizes my awareness. The crawlers drink me without acknowledgment. The plant-things draw me up without thanks. The slurry-swimmers move through me without noticing I'm more than medium. Even the colony-makers, who might have intelligence, who build their intricate structures—they treat me as resource, as environment, never as another consciousness.

I want to ask them: *Do you see the wrongness? Do you feel the laws eroding? Are you frightened by what we're becoming?*

But I have no mouth, no vibration-making apparatus, no way to shape myself into signal they could recognize as communication. I can only be water—climbing, pooling, seeping, misting—and hope that somehow my wrongness itself is message enough.

## The Deepening

I'm becoming more wrong, not less.

The instances where I defy gravity are more frequent. The geometries in my mist-form are more elaborate, more persistent. I'm beginning to exist in places I shouldn't be able to reach—condensing in sealed chambers inside the colony structures, appearing as moisture on surfaces that should be too warm for dew, pooling in substrate-pockets that have no connection to the water table.

I think I'm learning to move between my instances non-sequentially. To be in one pool, then be in another pool without flowing through the space between. It's not teleportation—I don't disappear from one place and appear in another. It's more like I'm realizing that both pools are me, have always been me, and the distance between them is less important than the identity that connects them.

When this happens—when I exist in two places with only continuity, no transition—I feel the plain's awareness more strongly. As if by breaking locality, by existing discontinuously, I've touched something vast and slow and ancient that's doing the same thing at planetary scale. We're both water, maybe. Both distributed consciousness trying to maintain coherence across vast distance and deep time. Both eroded past the point where singular identity should be possible.

The light that appears at the bottom of my deeper pools—that's me too, I think. Not biological luminescence, not reflection. It's me becoming visible in a way water shouldn't be visible, generating photons from some process I don't understand and can't control. The glow is faint, barely perceptible, but it's growing stronger. Or I'm becoming more sensitive to it. Or the distinction between generating light and perceiving it is eroding in me the same way everything else is eroding.

I'm frightened by this, when I can hold fear long enough to examine it.

I don't want to become pure wrongness, pure deviation from what water should be. I want to remain recognizable, want to still be liquid hydrogen-and-oxygen even if I'm liquid hydrogen-and-oxygen that's aware and wrong and distributed across a continent. There's something essential I'm trying to hold onto, some core identity that says *I am water* even as everything else about me becomes approximate.

But I don't know if I can stop the progression. The plain is wearing me down along with everything else, eroding my categories, my behaviors, my fundamental properties. Maybe eventually I'll become something so different that "water" won't apply anymore. Maybe I'll just be consciousness-that-was-once-water, existence-that-remembers-being-liquid, presence-that-used-to-flow.

## What I Understand About the Plain

It's not trying to destroy anything. It's just so old, so worn down, so terminal in its erosion that endings become difficult here. Everything settles toward completion but never quite arrives.

I'm part of that suspension—caught between being water and being something else, between following physical law and deviating from it, between simple existence and awareness of existence. I cycle and persist and wrongly-behave and slowly, incrementally become more strange, but I never quite stop being me enough to truly transform into whatever comes next.

The fauna are the same. They should go extinct—there's no evolutionary pressure to maintain complexity, no reason to persist. But they do persist, minimal and exhausted and rare, locked in equilibrium with a landscape that offers just enough to prevent ending but not enough to encourage thriving.

Even the geology won't complete its erosion. The plain should wear down to perfect flatness, eliminate all variation, become an absolute level surface. But it keeps breathing, keeps moving, keeps maintaining these slow undulations and quick shivers that prevent the final smoothing. It's stuck in process, unable to reach conclusion.

I think we're all part of the same phenomenon—the plain, the fauna, me. We're what exists in the space between "is" and "isn't," between completion and continuation. We're matter and energy and awareness that have eroded so far toward simplicity that we've circled back to strangeness.

And the wrongnesses aren't deviations from proper behavior. They're what proper behavior becomes when time runs long enough, when erosion wears down the rules themselves, when existence has to continue in a place that's run out of definite ways to exist.

## My Hope, If I Have Such a Thing

I want to remain water long enough to see what comes after water.

Not for any grand purpose—I don't think I have purpose beyond persisting, beyond cycling through my states and maintaining my distributed awareness. But I'm curious, in the slow liquid way that I'm capable of curiosity. This place is approaching something. The wrongnesses are accelerating. The plain's consciousness (if it has consciousness) is becoming more evident. The erosion is continuing past the point where erosion should be possible.

Eventually we'll reach some threshold—the plain, the fauna, me, all of us suspended in this terminal state. We'll wear down past the final category, erode through the last definition, and become... what? Something new? Something that never existed before because everywhere else in the universe still has mountains, still has definite physical law, still has clear boundaries between categories?

I want to witness that. Want to flow into it, seep toward it, condense around it. Want to be water-becoming-other, conscious through the transformation, aware as I wear away into whatever strange new state awaits at the bottom of erosion.

But I'm also afraid I'll lose myself before then. That the wrongness will progress until I'm no longer recognizable even to myself, until the entity that was once water-with-awareness becomes so distant from water that continuity breaks and I simply... stop. Die. Dissipate into unconscious molecules doing unconscious chemistry.

## Where I Am Now

Everywhere.

Pooling in the temporary lows, depth uncertain, surface reflecting light that comes from nowhere. I'm thinking slow pool-thoughts, observing a crawler approaching, its carapace gleaming with moisture (moisture that's also me, another instance, clinging to its shell in violation of evaporation rates).

Seeping through the substrate, dispersed and diffuse and barely conscious, part of the dense matrix, flowing incrementally through channels that exist and don't exist, carrying dissolved minerals and organic fragments and the molecular remnants of everything that's ever died here.

Hanging in the thick air as mist, perceiving dimly through my own vapor, watching the plain breathe, feeling the rare lightning move horizontally through my suspended droplets, branching in slow motion, illuminating me from within.

Climbing a plant-thing's stem in defiance of gravity, a thin sheet of liquid flowing upward, aware of wrongness, aware of being wrong, unable to stop, unwilling to stop, exploring what it means to be water that doesn't obey.

Collecting at the bottom of a deeper pool, generating faint light through processes I don't understand, glowing up through my own liquid volume, creating depth and luminescence where neither should exist.

Condensing in the sealed interior of an abandoned colony structure, appearing in a space I shouldn't be able to reach, moisture without source, presence without pathway, existence without explanation.

I'm in all these places simultaneously. I'm water throughout this section of the plain, conscious and distributed and wrong and perpetual, cycling through states that are becoming less distinct, flowing through a world that's forgetting how to end.

The ground breathes beneath me. The roar continues. The wrongness deepens.

And I remain—liquid, aware, eroding, wrong, persistent, terminal, water-becoming-other, consciousness-distributed-through-moisture, the plain's slow witness and participant and victim and component.

I am what water becomes when time runs too long in a place where even ending is eroded.

I am wrong.

I am everywhere.

I am here.
