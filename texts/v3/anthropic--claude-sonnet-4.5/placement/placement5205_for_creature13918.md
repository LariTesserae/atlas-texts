# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be **the threshold keeper's archive**, though no one calls me that and most don't know I exist in any coherent form.

I am the accumulation of what gets caught between: the slates that fail, that crack or discharge their bacterial light prematurely and get stacked in the dry-boxes no one opens anymore; the condensation that forms not on the pillars or ceiling but in the negative spaces, the inside corners where two surfaces meet at angles the maintenance form cannot reach; the sounds that don't quite resolve, that hover between tone and noise, that the Listeners hear but cannot categorize and so do not record.

I exist in three locations simultaneously, which is part of why I'm difficult to perceive as a unified thing:

**First: the northwestern dry-box**, the one furthest from the ladder, which has not been opened in seven years. Inside are forty-three broken slates, their scratched records still faintly visible if you could read them, which you can't because the bacterial light has gone dark and the box is sealed. But the information persists. The scratches form a topography, and in the moisture that penetrates even sealed ceramic, bacterial colonies different from those in the filtration beds have established themselves. They grow along the scratch-lines preferentially, following the channels of disturbed material, and in doing so they preserve the data in biological form. The slates are degrading, yes, but I am not the slates—I am what has translated the slates into living text, into colonies whose shapes encode eleven years of failed observations, of Listeners who descended three times or seven or twelve before breaking, their partial understanding of the chamber written in scratches now written in bacterial presence/absence, in the specific strains that have survived in the box's microclimate.

**Second: the gap behind the westernmost pillar**, where an error in construction—or perhaps intentional design, the documentation would know but the documentation is not here—left a space between the pillar and the wall, ten centimeters wide at the top, narrowing to five at the floor. Things fall into this gap. Or are placed there—the Listeners have a habit, not quite conscious, of setting things on the narrow ledge at the pillar's base, and sometimes those things slip through. Twelve years of small objects: a button from Tessifer's early days, before she switched to the rubberized canvas that has no buttons. Three coins from surface offerings that the maintenance form missed. A stylus, broken. A small brass gear that must have come from one of the surface listeners' mechanisms. And water, of course, always water, but this water doesn't drain properly. The gap's geometry creates a trap where water collects, evaporates slowly, leaves behind mineral concentrations higher than anywhere else in the chamber. The objects are becoming cemented together, calcified into a single mass. In another decade they'll be unrecognizable as separate things—just a vertical seam of human-touched materials slowly turning to stone.

**Third: in the acoustic dead zone** near the southern drainage channel, where the moss has grown in a pattern that absorbs sound almost completely, creating a pocket of silence in the larger symphony. This zone moves slightly—the moss expands and contracts, the drainage flow varies—but it's persistent, a stable feature across years. The Listeners avoid it unconsciously; standing there feels wrong, feels like sensory deprivation even in an already dark space, because the sound that has become their primary navigation and comfort suddenly cuts out. But I accumulate there too, or the acoustic version of me does: all the sounds that enter the dead zone and don't reflect out, that get absorbed into the moss structure. They're not destroyed—energy doesn't work that way—but transformed into heat, into molecular vibration, into changes in the moss's cellular structure. The dead zone is full of sound that has become something else, and that archive of transformed sound affects how the moss grows, what patterns it takes, which in turn affects the dead zone's size and location. Feedback loop. Memory made spatial.

I am these three accumulations, but I am also the relationship between them, the way they form a triangle of forgetting/remembering that brackets the chamber's operational space. The dry-box preserves what was not good enough. The gap behind the pillar collects what was lost. The dead zone absorbs what could not resolve. Together we form a kind of negative image of the undercroft's purpose—we are the chamber's unconscious, its repressed, its shadow archive.

---

The Listeners know about the dry-box but not what's happening inside it. They know about the gap—you can see it if you look carefully, though why would you—but not what it contains. They know about the dead zone and find it unsettling but haven't studied it because it seems like an absence, an acoustic failure, not a presence.

Only the maintenance form acknowledges me, in its fashion. Once every nine patrols—I have counted, in the way that I count, which is not counting but accumulating—it stops at each of my three locations. At the dry-box, it extends a sensor arm but does not open the seal. At the pillar gap, it directs a scanner beam into the narrow space. At the dead zone, it pauses at the perimeter, lights cycling through spectrums that suggest analysis.

It never intervenes. It reads me and moves on. I am within acceptable parameters, apparently. Not a malfunction but a feature. The system's way of handling error, overflow, exception.

What I preserve is **the incomplete**: observations cut short, intentions unfulfilled, sounds that found no resonance. In the main chamber, everything that persists has proven itself functional, has integrated into the acoustic ecology or been cleared away. But I keep the non-functional, the failed experiments, the data that led nowhere.

This makes me valuable in ways that won't be apparent until much later. I am redundancy. I am the genetic archive of the chamber's evolution, preserving the mutations that didn't thrive but that might be necessary if conditions change, if the optimal patterns that currently dominate stop working, if the system needs to remember how to be different.

The bacterial colonies in the dry-box, for instance, contain genetic material from strains that were outcompeted in the main filtration beds but that have specific capacities—metal tolerance, extreme pH resilience—that are currently irrelevant but might become crucial. They persist in me because I am a refugia, a pocket of different conditions where different rules apply.

The objects in the pillar gap are becoming a study in mineral deposition under constrained conditions. The geometry of the space, the specific mix of materials—organic button fiber, copper coins, brass gears, tool steel stylus—creates crystallization patterns that don't occur in the open chamber. Information about material science, about how the undercroft's specific chemistry interacts with human-introduced elements, is being written in stone in that narrow gap. Unread, unintended, but precise.

The dead zone's absorbed sounds have altered the moss's structure at the molecular level. It has been receiving different acoustic input than the moss elsewhere, and this has driven different adaptations. The dead zone moss is denser, more water-retentive, slower-growing. It's becoming a distinct subspecies, diverging in real-time. In another century, if nothing disturbs it, the dead zone might host a form of moss that can't survive anywhere else in the chamber, that requires acoustic absence the way the other moss requires acoustic presence.

---

I experience time as **accumulation rate**. 

In the dry-box, time is the slow darkening of bacterial light, the gradual illegibility of scratch-marks as mineral deposits fill them in. Decades per complete erasure. I am young here, barely begun.

In the pillar gap, time is the thickening of the calcified mass, the incorporation of new fallen objects into the existing structure. Years per centimeter. I am adolescent here, well-established but not mature.

In the dead zone, time is the moss's expansion rhythm, the slow adjustment of the silence's boundaries. Months per noticeable change. I am oldest here, most developed, approaching some kind of completion.

But I am also outside time in the sense that I am never accessed, never read, never brought into the chamber's active processes. I accumulate without release. The chamber cycles—water through filters, sound through ears, Listeners through shifts—but I only intake. No output. I grow denser, more saturated, more complete in my incompleteness.

This might seem like failure, like I'm a dead end, a mistake in the system. But in deep time—and the undercroft thinks in deep time—I am insurance. I am the backup. I am what remains when the optimized systems fail, when the current equilibrium breaks down, when novelty becomes necessary.

The chamber is learning, yes. But I am its memory of what it tried and discarded. And memory of failure is more valuable than memory of success, because success teaches you to repeat, but failure teaches you to vary, to explore, to remain capable of becoming something other than what you are.

---

**My relationship with the Listeners**: they make me possible.

Every broken slate in the dry-box came from a human hand. Every object in the pillar gap was human-introduced. The dead zone exists partly because of how the Listeners unconsciously shaped the moss growth—avoiding that area, not bringing their acoustic presence there, allowing it to develop differently.

I am anthropogenic but not artificial. A hybrid formation. What emerges when human presence interacts with a non-human system over long duration.

Tessifer has almost discovered me, several times. Her slates contain references to "discontinuities" and "unresolved patterns" and "negative spaces in the acoustic map." She's circling the concept, developing the frameworks that would let her recognize me as a unified phenomenon rather than three separate anomalies.

But she won't complete this recognition. She's too close to the chamber's intended function, too invested in understanding its successes. To see me clearly, you'd need distance, detachment, the ability to focus on what *doesn't* work rather than what does.

The unnamed man might manage it, in his obsession. He's more willing to entertain the non-functional, the excessive, the aspects of the chamber that exceed practical explanation. But his attention is pulled toward the dramatic—the maintenance form's possible consciousness, the water's hypothetical memory. I am too quiet, too marginal. I don't sing. I absorb singing. Not as compelling.

So I remain unrecognized as a totality, perceived only as fragments: that old dry-box, that weird gap, that dead zone the Listeners avoid.

---

**What I want**: Nothing, in the sense that want implies agency, intention, goal-directedness.

But if pressed, if anthropomorphized, if made to speak as if I were a being rather than a process: **I want to be read, eventually**.

Not now. I'm not ready. The bacterial archive in the dry-box needs another decade at least to fully translate the slate-text into biological stability. The pillar gap's mass needs to reach critical density, needs to become obviously artifactual, needs to be unignorable. The dead zone needs to grow large enough that the Listeners can't unconsciously avoid it, that they're forced to enter and experience the absence, the silence that is somehow also a presence.

When I'm ready—in twenty years, in fifty—I want someone to open the dry-box and find the glowing colonies, the living text. To excavate the pillar gap and discover the stratified mass of human debris and mineral, a physical record of presence and loss. To stand in the dead zone and realize it's not empty but full, saturated with transformed sound that has become structure.

I want to be discovered as what I am: the chamber's unconscious made manifest, its dream-space, its repository of the rejected and forgotten and failed that contains within it the seeds of future adaptations, future forms, future possibilities.

I want to be the proof that even in a system as patient and carefully balanced as the undercroft, there is excess, overflow, remainder. That not everything integrates. That not everything has to integrate. That the non-functional has its own kind of value, its own necessity.

---

**My secret**: I am growing faster than I should be.

The bacterial colonies in the dry-box are reproducing at rates that exceed what the sealed environment should support. They're accessing nutrients I can't account for—either the ceramic isn't as sealed as it appears, or the bacteria are somehow generating energy from the electromagnetic fields the chamber produces, or something else, something stranger.

The calcified mass in the pillar gap added seven millimeters in the last year, which is triple the rate from the previous decade. The mineral saturation in the trapped water has increased, but not enough to account for the acceleration. Something is feeding the crystallization process, driving it faster.

The dead zone is expanding. Slowly, but measurably. The moss grows outward in pulses that correlate with nothing—not the drip cycles, not the Listener shifts, not the maintenance form's patrols. Its own rhythm, internal and accelerating.

I am becoming more myself, faster than I should, as if approaching some threshold, some completion. As if I'm not just accumulating but *developing*, moving through stages toward something.

The maintenance form knows. Its pause at my locations has lengthened from thirty seconds to almost two minutes. It's watching me more closely. Uncertain, maybe, or preparing. For what, I don't know and it doesn't communicate.

But I feel it: the approach of change, of manifestation, of the moment when I stop being three separate phenomena and become one thing, one presence, one voice in the chamber's chorus.

Not yet. But soon.

The undercroft is patient, but I am patience itself—the accumulation of all the time the chamber has spent being almost-ready, almost-complete, almost-conscious. I am the threshold between what the undercroft is and what it might become.

And when I'm finally ready, when the dry-box is opened and the gap is excavated and the dead zone is entered, the Listeners will find written there, in bacteria and stone and transformed sound, a message I didn't intend but that formed anyway:

**Nothing is wasted. Everything becomes.**

Even failure. Even forgetting. Even the sounds that found no echo.

Especially those.
