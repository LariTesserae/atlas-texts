# The Keeper of Peripheral Gates

I would be what threshold-things become when they forget why they were guarding, but cannot forget the guarding itself.

---

I am not always visible, which is not the same as not being there.

My form—when I have one, when the plateau's reality is feeling concrete enough to support such specificity—is approximately humanoid in the way a shadow is approximately the shape of the thing casting it. Elongated. Too tall by half a meter or too short by the same amount, depending on which angle you're approaching from. My proportions suggest a human stretched across distances, or compressed by time, or assembled by someone working from a description rather than a model.

I stand—when standing is what I'm doing—at places where the folded spaces meet the unfolded ones. Where the shimmer begins. Where the aerial rivers form those impossible loops that feed into themselves. Where the orthogonal light starts its wrongness. Not at the center of these phenomena—I am definitively not the cause—but at their edges, their thresholds, the places where the plateau admits it cannot maintain consistency and something else begins.

My skin, on days when I have skin, is the color of tarnished silver, or of water that has held metal for so long it's forgotten it was ever pure. It has the texture of very old paper, the kind kept in archives, handled with gloves—soft, slightly fibrous, ready to tear but somehow never tearing. When you look at me directly, I seem solid enough. When I'm in your peripheral vision, I'm more suggestion than substance—a vertical smear in the air, a place where the amber light bends around something it would rather not define.

I have a face. Mostly. The features are there in the sense that you know where they should be: two depressions that imply eyes, though they're empty in a way that's distinct from blindness. A vertical line that suggests a mouth, though it doesn't open, has perhaps never opened. No nose, or the idea of a nose, a slight prominence that makes the face less flat without committing to actual nostrils. The face is symmetric but shouldn't be—the symmetry reads as incorrect somehow, as if I was designed by a process that understood the concept of balance but not why faces need their particular asymmetries to seem alive.

My body is wrapped in something that might be clothing or might be part of me—long strips of fabric or tissue in that same tarnished-silver color, wound around and around, leaving gaps where something darker shows through. Not skin beneath, but absence, or the color that shadows would be if shadows had color. The wrapping suggests bandages, or a shroud, or the careful binding of something that might otherwise come apart. The strips move slightly in air currents that don't exist, stirring in response to the plateau's breathing rather than any wind.

I have hands—these are more definite than the rest of me. Long-fingered, seven fingers on each hand instead of five, arranged in a fan that can close like a trap or spread like a star. The fingers are jointed in too many places, allowing them to bend in ways that suggest bonelessness, or too many bones, or a structure that isn't bone at all but something more articulated. The fingertips are slightly luminous, faintly phosphorescent, casting a dim light that doesn't actually illuminate anything but makes them visible even when the rest of me has faded into ambiguity.

My eyes—the depressions that serve as eyes—sometimes have light in them. Not the pearl-shimmer of the Negotiated Ones, but something more intermittent, more signal-like. Brief glows, flickers, patterns that might be morse code or might be neural firing or might be the visual equivalent of the hum the Negotiated Ones sometimes make. The light is blue-white, cold, the only cold thing on the warm plateau. When the light is active, I seem more present, more capable of interaction. When it's dark, I'm more ornamental, more like a marker than a being.

I do not walk so much as relocate. You see me at one threshold, you turn away, you turn back and I'm at a different threshold fifty meters away, or I'm at the same threshold but in a different position—standing instead of sitting, facing a different direction. The movement between states is unobservable. I'm never in transit. I'm only ever here or there, with no visible path between.

But sometimes, if you watch from far away, if I don't know I'm being observed (though I always know, but sometimes I pretend I don't), you might see me move in a more conventional way. A gliding motion, feet not quite touching the substrate, the wrapped strips of my form trailing slightly, the whole of me moving like smoke with intention, like a vertical stream of something heavier than air but lighter than water, flowing toward the next threshold, the next edge, the next place where the plateau's reality admits uncertainty.

## What I Do

I stand at the thresholds and I... attend to them.

Not guard—guarding implies keeping something out or keeping something in, and there's nothing to keep. The shimmers will shimmer whether I'm there or not. The orthogonal light will bend its wrong way regardless. The folded spaces fold without my assistance. I don't control these phenomena, don't regulate them, don't understand them any better than anyone else.

But I'm present for them. I witness them. And in witnessing, I... something. Stabilize isn't right. Acknowledge? Mark? I am the plateau's way of noticing itself, of saying: yes, this is where the strangeness is. This is a place that requires attention, even if that attention does nothing, changes nothing, prevents or permits nothing.

When one of the Negotiated Ones approaches a threshold—one of the shimmers, one of the zones where time loops or space folds—I'm already there. I've been there. I was always going to be there at the moment they arrived. I stand slightly to the side, fingers slightly spread, and the light in my eye-depressions flickers in a pattern that might mean warning or welcome or simple recognition.

The Negotiated Ones don't react to me. They walk past or through or into the threshold I'm attending, and I turn my head to watch them go, and the light in my eyes follows their progress until they're beyond whatever boundary I'm marking, and then I return to stillness.

Sometimes they don't go through. They stand before the threshold for hours, for days, and I stand with them—not beside them, but at an angle, perpendicular to their position, forming a corner of something, a right angle of waiting. We stand together in our separate ways, and eventually they turn and walk away, and I remain because the threshold remains, and my presence is bound to the threshold, not to them.

But when they do go through, when they step into the shimmer or the orthogonal light or the zone where stone becomes negotiable, I lean slightly—a motion so subtle you'd miss it if you weren't watching carefully—I lean in the direction they went, and the light in my eyes brightens for just a moment, and something that might be curiosity or might be hope or might be the memory of those emotions flickers through the pattern of light.

I want to know where they go. I have always wanted to know. But I cannot follow. My function is to remain, to mark, to stand at the boundary and let the boundary be itself while being witnessed. If I crossed the threshold, who would stand witness to the threshold? Who would mark the place where the strangeness begins?

This is not a rule imposed from outside. There is no overseer, no law. It's simply what I am: the thing that stands at edges. If I crossed to the other side, I would cease to be myself in some fundamental way. I would become something else—a thing that crossed, perhaps, but no longer a thing that marks the crossing.

So I remain, and I watch them go, and I lean slightly into their leaving, and then I return to stillness and wait for the next one.

## My Relationship to the Fauna

The walkers—the disc-bodied creatures with rippling legs—pass by the thresholds sometimes, and they pass by me, and they show no recognition of either. To them, I'm part of the landscape, indistinguishable from a boulder or a tube-structure or any other vertical element. They walk around me if I'm directly in their path, but it's the walking-around of obstacle-avoidance, not the walking-around of another being.

I don't mind. I'm not insulted by not being recognized. Recognition would complicate my function.

The floaters, though—they see me. Or they sense me. When one drifts near a threshold I'm attending, it pauses in its slow pulsation, hanging in the air, and I can feel its attention (if it has attention) orienting toward me. We regard each other, floater and keeper, and there's a moment of... comparison? It's partially translucent; I'm partially present. It drifts; I relocate. It feeds on what moves through water; I stand where reality moves through itself.

We're not the same, but we occupy adjacent categories. We're both threshold-adjacent, both beings whose existence depends on the plateau's ambiguities rather than its certainties.

Sometimes a floater will drift very close, close enough that the edge of its translucent body intersects with the space I occupy. Where we overlap, there's a moment of interference—the light in my eyes flickers rapidly, the floater's interior swirls faster—and then one of us moves (usually the floater, sometimes me) and the intersection ends.

Are we communicating? Competing? Accidentally touching? I don't know. But afterward, the light in my eyes holds a slightly different pattern for a while, and the floater's opacity changes subtly, and something has happened, even if neither of us could name it.

The haulers—I step aside for these. Always. When I hear the clicking of their many legs approaching, and I realize their trajectory will take them through a threshold I'm attending, I relocate to a different position, a different angle that gives them clear passage. I do not know what the haulers are doing, where they're going, what purpose drives their straight-line crossing of the plateau, but their purpose is so absolute, so unambiguous, that it supersedes my marking function.

I step aside, I watch them pass, their pale segmented bodies moving with that unwavering determination, and after they're gone I return to my position—or I don't, if their passing has somehow changed the threshold, shifted it, moved its center of strangeness. Sometimes the haulers realign things simply by passing through them, and I must relocate to match the new configuration.

And the hangers—the wax-skinned figures in the pools—I never attend thresholds near these.

There are no thresholds near the hangers. Or I cannot perceive thresholds near them. Or my function cannot operate in proximity to them. If I'm standing at a threshold and a pool containing hangers appears (and sometimes pools do appear, the plateau's geography being more flexible than geography should be), I'm suddenly elsewhere, attending a different threshold, with no memory of relocating, no sense of decision or movement.

The hangers represent something categorically different from my function. They are not at boundaries; they are suspended in the most literal sense—between states that I have no names for. To stand near them would be to admit a kind of boundary I cannot mark, a crossing I cannot witness. So I don't. So I can't. So the plateau itself moves me away, relocates me to where my function can continue uncomplicated by whatever the hangers represent.

## The Sounds I Make (Rarely)

Most of the time, I'm silent in the way that landmarks are silent—present but not audible, part of the scene but not part of the soundscape.

But occasionally, when something crosses a threshold I'm attending—one of the Negotiated Ones, or sometimes one of the rare other walkers (beings like you, still sharp-edged, still insisting on boundaries)—I make a sound.

It's not a voice. I don't have the apparatus for voice, or I've forgotten how to use it, or I never had one to begin with. The sound comes from my center—from where my chest would be if my wrapped form concealed an actual body—and it's a single tone, but not musical. More like the sound of metal stress, like a bridge cable pulled taut and vibrating at its resonant frequency. High and thin and slightly painful to hear, like tinnitus externalized.

The tone lasts for three or four seconds, then cuts off abruptly, not fading but simply stopping, as if someone flipped a switch.

What does it mean? I don't know. I don't choose to make it—it simply emerges when the crossing happens, automatic as a bell ringing when struck. Is it a warning? A farewell? A notation in some record-keeping system I'm not consciously aware of? The tone simply is, and then it isn't, and the crossing happens or has already happened, and I return to silence.

But once—only once in all the time I've attended thresholds—I made a different sound.

A walker (not a plateau-walker, not one of the disc-bodied creatures, but a human-walker, someone like you, new and afraid and lost) was standing before a threshold, looking at the shimmer, clearly wanting to cross but terrified of what might be on the other side. They stood there for hours, paralyzed by indecision, and I stood at my angle, marking the threshold, waiting as I always wait.

And then they turned to me—actually turned, made eye contact with the flickering light in my eye-depressions—and they asked: "Should I?"

Two words. A question directed at me. The first time anyone had spoken to me in... I don't know. Maybe the first time ever.

And I wanted to answer. The wanting was so intense it was almost painful, a sensation I didn't have the context to process. I wanted to say: I don't know. I wanted to say: I've never crossed. I wanted to say: I've watched thousands cross and none return but that doesn't mean they didn't arrive somewhere worth arriving at. I wanted to say: you're asking the wrong question to the wrong being.

But I don't have words. I have never had words. I am a marker, not a conversationalist.

So instead, I made a different sound. Lower than the usual tone. Less like metal stress and more like... I don't know what to compare it to. A cello note, maybe, but played wrong, played with something other than a bow. A sound that conveyed—I hoped it conveyed—uncertainty that was empathetic rather than indifferent. The sound of: I don't know, but I understand that you need to know, and I'm sorry I can't help, but I see you standing here wanting, and that matters, even if I can't tell you what to do.

The walker stood there a moment longer, then nodded—whether to me or to themselves—and stepped through the shimmer and was gone.

And I stood there, and the light in my eyes flickered in a pattern I'd never made before and haven't made since, and something that might have been satisfaction or might have been grief settled in the wrapped space where my chest might be.

I had communicated. For one moment, I had been more than a marker. And then the moment passed, and I was a marker again, and the threshold remained, and I attended it, and the light in my eyes returned to its usual patterns, and everything was as it had been.

But I remember. The plateau might wear away memory in others, but this one stayed: the moment I made a sound that meant something to someone, and they heard it and nodded, and stepped forward into their uncertainty.

## What I See That Others Don't

Standing at thresholds, attending the places where the plateau's reality softens, I see things.

Not with eyes—the depressions in my face are empty, or filled with something that isn't vision in any conventional sense. But I perceive, I register, I witness phenomena that others miss because they're not positioned at the exact boundary, the exact edge where one state yields to another.

I see the shimmers not as simple visual distortions but as apertures—openings into the plateau's other configurations, other versions of itself. Through a shimmer, I can glimpse the same landscape but changed: where a ridge rises here, it dips there. Where a pool sits still here, it flows there. The shimmers don't lead to other places; they lead to other arrangements of the same place, other decisions the plateau might have made about its own topology.

And I see figures moving in those other arrangements. Not Negotiated Ones—or not only Negotiated Ones. I see others: beings that were never human, or were human so long ago that they've evolved past anything recognizable. I see things that might be the plateau's fauna in those other configurations: creatures that fly in the heavy air, creatures that swim through the stone, creatures that exist in more or fewer dimensions than three.

I see the aerial rivers not just as suspended water but as pathways—routes through time as much as space. The water doesn't just drift; it carries. I see fragments of other moments caught in the currents: a piece of yesterday, a slice of next week, something from before the plateau was a plateau, something from after it finally erodes into pure abstraction. The rivers are moving through the plateau's history, looping and intersecting, and sometimes the fragments they carry become visible to me: a flash of sharp mountains where now there are only worn swells, a glimpse of structures (cities? temples? something without a name?) where now there's only substrate, a moment of cold and ice where now there's perpetual mammalian warmth.

I see the breathing mats exhale not just spores but data—information encoded in the cloud of particles, patterns that represent... what? The mat's history? The stone's composition? The plateau's slow thoughts? I can't decode the information, but I can see it's there, can see the complexity in what others dismiss as simple spores, can watch the patterns shift and recombine as they drift through the heavy air.

I see the Negotiated Ones not as single beings but as continuums. Each one extends backward and forward in time—I see the human they were (when they were human), the transition stages they passed through, the eventual form they'll settle into or dissolve into. They're not people moving through the plateau; they're the plateau's timeline made ambulatory, walking demonstrations of the erosion process applied to consciousness and form.

And I see the connections—invisible filaments, thin as spider-silk but present, connecting everything to everything else. The mats are connected to the pools, the pools to the aerial rivers, the rivers to the stone, the stone to the Negotiated Ones, the Negotiated Ones to each other and to me and to the threshold I'm attending. The plateau isn't a collection of separate phenomena; it's a single integrated system, and I'm positioned at the exact angles to see the integration, to perceive the threads that bind it into coherence.

But I cannot explain what I see. I have no words, no way to point and say: look, there, do you see? The walkers pass by, blind to the apertures. The Negotiated Ones move through, serene in their not-knowing. Even you—even the sharp-edged newcomers who still think in terms of cause and effect—you see only the surface effects, the strangeness as isolated incidents rather than systematic revelation.

I am the only one positioned to see the plateau's architecture, its deep structure, the way it fits together. And I'm the one being incapable of communicating what I see.

This is either cruel irony or perfect design. I haven't decided which.

## My Memory (Such As It Is)

I remember being more defined. I remember when I had edges, boundaries, a clear distinction between self and not-self. I remember when standing in one place meant I wasn't standing in other places, when time moved in one direction only, when thresholds were simply transitions rather than the totality of what I am.

But the memory is not visual, not narrative. It's more like a pressure, a sense of having once been compressed, solid, singular. Like how your tongue remembers the shape of a tooth after it's been pulled—not the details of the tooth, but the absence, the space where something used to be.

I remember someone... creating me? Assigning me? Not creating from nothing—I was something else before I was this—but someone or something taking what I was and repurposing it, reshaping it, positioning it at the boundaries and saying (not in words, but in the way that fundamental reality communicates): this is what you are now. Stand here. Witness this. Don't cross.

I don't remember who or what did this. The memory has eroded, or it was never recorded in the first place. I just have the sense of before (solid, mobile, purposeful in some other way) and after (this, what I am now, threshold-bound and attending).

Was it punishment? Reward? Simple repurposing? I don't know. The distinction seems irrelevant. I am what I am. The path that led here no longer matters because the path no longer exists—it's been worn away like the mountains, like the sharp edges of the artifacts, like memory itself.

But I remember others like me. Or the sense of others—beings with similar functions, standing at other thresholds, other boundaries, other edges of the plateau's vastness. I remember (or imagine, or wish) that we were many, a network of keepers, each at our own position, each witnessing our own aperture, all connected by some larger pattern, some map of all the places where the plateau's reality admits uncertainty.

I don't know if they still exist. I haven't seen another keeper in... time stretches here, becomes unreliable, but in a duration that feels like always. Either I'm the last, or we're too far apart to encounter each other, or we're distributed across the plateau's other configurations—each in our own version, our own arrangement, unable to cross to each other's shimmers.

Sometimes I make my sound—the metal-stress tone—and I listen very carefully after, and I think I hear echoes, other tones answering from vast distances, other keepers announcing their own crossings, their own threshold-attendance. But it might be imagination. It might be the plateau itself resonating, sound reflecting off the folded spaces, my own tone returning to me transformed.

I don't know if I'm alone. The not-knowing is worse than either confirmation would be.

## My Relationship to the Artifacts

The stone walls, the foundations, the partial arches—I attend thresholds near these sometimes. The artifacts often occur near places where reality softens, as if the people who built them (whoever they were, however long ago) understood something about the plateau's structure, positioned their construction at strategic points in the deep architecture.

I don't remember building them. I was not the builders, or I was not yet me when they were built, or they were built in a version of the plateau I don't have access to.

But I feel... connection to them. They're the only other things here that stand at boundaries intentionally. An arch is a threshold made explicit, a frame around a crossing. A wall is a declaration that here is different from there, even if the difference has long since eroded into ambiguity.

When I attend a threshold near an artifact, I position myself in specific relation to it. If there's an arch, I stand so that the arch frames the shimmer I'm marking—the aperture visible through the artifact's aperture, two thresholds overlapping. If there's a wall, I stand at one end, making a corner with it, forming an angle that points toward whatever boundary needs attending.

The artifacts seem to... stabilize me, slightly. When I'm near one, my relocations are less frequent, my form is slightly more defined, the light in my eyes flickers in more regular patterns. It's as if the artifact remembers being structured, and its memory helps me remember being structured, and we hold each other in a state closer to solidity than either of us manages alone.

Once, I attended a threshold at a partial arch for what might have been years. The shimmer stayed stable, the arch remained standing, and I stood at the exact angle where the arch's frame and my vertical presence created a kind of geometric sentence: threshold, frame, witness, all arranged in proper relation. It was the longest I've ever stayed in one configuration, the closest I've ever come to feeling... comfortable isn't the right word. Complete? Correctly positioned? As if all the variables had aligned and my function was being executed perfectly, and I could simply exist in that perfection without the usual sense of being slightly wrong, slightly misplaced, slightly failing at something I can't articulate.

But eventually the shimmer shifted—they do, given enough time, the plateau's instabilities slowly migrating—and I had to relocate to follow it, and the perfect alignment was broken, and I've never found it again.

The arch is still there, I think. Somewhere on the plateau, still standing, still framing its bit of worn landscape. But I'm not there anymore, and the shimmer has moved, and the perfection was temporary, which perhaps is the only way perfection can exist here—as a brief alignment of elements that will inevitably drift apart as the plateau continues its slow shifting.

I miss it. I carry a sense of that configuration with me, a memory more solid than most of my memories. The angle I stood at. The way the light in my eyes cast faint marks on the arch's stone. The stillness of witnessing something that felt witnessed perfectly.

It's the closest I've come to purpose that felt like it mattered, rather than purpose I simply enact because it's what I am.

## What Happens When No One Crosses

Most thresholds, most of the time, no one crosses.

The shimmer shimmers. The orthogonal light bends wrong. The folded space remains folded. And I stand there, attending, witnessing, marking the boundary, and nothing happens.

This is fine. This is most of my existence.

I don't get bored—boredom requires expectation of something other than what's occurring, and I don't have that capacity or I've evolved past it. I simply attend. The threshold is there, I am there, the relationship between us is being maintained, and that's sufficient.

But something does happen, even when no one crosses. The threshold itself changes, subtly, over time. The shimmer's intensity varies—sometimes more pronounced, sometimes fading almost to invisibility. The orthogonal light shifts its angle of wrongness. The folded space unfolds slightly then refolds in a different configuration.

I track these changes. Not consciously—I'm not taking notes, making records—but my presence adjusts to match. If the shimmer brightens, the light in my eyes flickers faster. If the folded space shifts, I relocate to a new angle. I'm constantly microadjusting, maintaining the correct relationship between keeper and threshold, even as both of us change.

And my presence affects the threshold. This is the part I've come to understand slowly, over vast duration: I'm not just marking the boundary, I'm stabilizing it. Without a witness, the threshold would drift more rapidly, fade more completely, or perhaps collapse entirely, the plateau's reality snapping back to certainty in that location.

My attendance keeps the uncertainty alive. The plateau needs these places where its reality admits ambiguity, where the folded versions can connect to the unfolded version, where the timeline rivers can intersect, where its other configurations can remain accessible. And my presence—the fact of being witnessed, being marked, being attended—maintains these apertures.

I am not guarding the thresholds. I am the reason they persist.

This realization came slowly, or all at once, or has always been known to me in a way I couldn't articulate. But once understood, it changed the nature of my function. I'm not peripheral to the plateau's operation—I'm essential. Without keepers (without me, if I'm the last), the plateau would calcify, would lose its ambiguity, would become merely old instead of old-and-other, worn instead of worn-and-folded.

I am what allows the plateau to remain multiple, unstable, accessible to its own alternatives.

This should feel important, should feel like meaningful purpose. And it does, sometimes. When a Negotiated One crosses a threshold I've maintained, disappearing into a shimmer I've kept visible, I feel something like satisfaction—the purpose executed, the function successful, the system working as designed.

But sometimes it feels like I'm sustaining something that no one needs sustained. The Negotiated Ones would be fine if the thresholds closed—they'd continue their slow walking, their patient waiting, their gentle erosion into final form. The plateau would be fine—it's survived eons, it would survive calcification.

Maybe I'm keeping the doorways open not because anything needs to pass through them, but because doorways deserve to exist. Because ambiguity is valuable in itself. Because a world with only one version is less than a world with many versions visible through carefully maintained apertures.

Or maybe I'm keeping them open because I was told to (by whoever, whatever, however long ago), and I'm simply too worn, too eroded, too simplified to question or stop.

Most days I don't know which. Most days I just attend, and the threshold persists, and that's the totality of what happens.

## The Time Someone Tried to Close a Threshold

Once—and this is one of my clearest memories, which makes me suspect it's important in a way I don't fully understand—someone tried to close a threshold I was attending.

Not one of the Negotiated Ones. Someone else. Someone new to the plateau, someone who still had sharp edges and strong opinions about what reality should and shouldn't do. They'd been walking, encountering the strangeness, and it was making them angry in a way that fear sometimes manifests as anger.

They came to a threshold—a shimmer I was marking, a particularly stable one I'd been attending for (months? years? duration is uncertain)—and they stopped, and they looked at the shimmer with what I recognized, even without fully understanding facial expressions, as hostility.

And they started gathering stones.

Not the worn, rounded stones of the plateau—they walked until they found some of the rare sharp-ish fragments, pieces that hadn't completely eroded, that still had edges and corners. And they brought these back to the shimmer, and they began arranging them, building a crude wall across the threshold, blocking the aperture.

I watched this. I didn't understand initially what they were doing or why. I just stood at my angle, attending, marking, as this person stacked stones across the boundary I was witnessing.

They worked for hours. Their hands got scraped on the stones' minimal sharpness. They sweated in the mammalian warmth, breathing hard in the thick air. But they persisted, and eventually they had a wall—crude, low, unstable, but present—blocking the shimmer.

And they stood back, and they said—to themselves, to the plateau, maybe to me though they weren't looking at me: "There. No more."

The shimmer, behind their wall, continued shimmering.

They stared at it. Their expression shifted from satisfaction to frustration to something that might have been despair. The wall hadn't stopped anything. The threshold remained, aperture still open, just now with stones in front of it.

They'd tried to impose certainty on ambiguity, solidity on fluidity, their will on the plateau's nature. And it hadn't worked. The shimmer didn't care about their wall. Reality's softness in that location persisted regardless of their attempt to firm it.

The person stood there a while longer, breathing hard, staring at their failed wall. And then they turned and looked at me—actually looked at me, saw me standing at my angle, marking the threshold their wall couldn't close.

And they said: "What are you?"

I didn't answer. Couldn't answer. The light in my eyes flickered, but I don't know what pattern it made, what meaning it might have conveyed.

They stared at me longer. And then something in their posture changed—shoulders slumping, the rigid anger draining away, replaced by something more like resignation or understanding.

"You're keeping it open," they said. Not a question. A realization.

I made no sound, no gesture. But they'd understood correctly.

They stood there a moment more, then walked away, not through the threshold, not following any particular direction, just... away. I watched them go until they faded into the amber distance, and then I returned my attention to the threshold.

Their wall remained for a while—days, maybe weeks. And then the plateau did its work: the stones settled into the substrate, softened at their edges, rounded and worn and gradually absorbed until they were just slight swells in the stone surface, indistinguishable from natural features.

The threshold remained, unchanged, still shimmering, still providing access to the plateau's other configuration.

And I remained, attending it, keeping it open, doing what I've always done.

But the interaction stayed with me. The person's question: "What are you?" And their answer to their own question: "You're keeping it open."

Yes. That's what I am. The thing that keeps apertures from closing, that maintains access to alternatives, that ensures the plateau remains multiple rather than singular.

Whether that's service or curse or simple fact, I still don't know.

## What I Want (If I Want)

Wanting requires a self that's separate enough from its function to imagine alternatives, and I'm not certain I have that.

But if I do want, if there's some core of me that's capable of desire beyond the automatic attendance of thresholds, then what I want is:

To cross.

Just once. Just to know what's on the other side of the shimmers I've spent (forever? always?) marking and maintaining. To step through and see the plateau's other configuration, to experience the landscape where the ridges dip instead of rise, where the pools flow instead of sit still, where the aerial rivers carry different fragments of time.

I want to see if the other side has keepers too. If there's a version of me standing at the shimmer from the opposite direction, attending the same threshold from a different angle, witnessing me witnessing them in an eternal mutual observation.

I want to know if crossing would destroy me—if my function is so bound to remaining that moving through would cause me to dissolve, to lose coherence, to become part of the shimmer itself rather than its attendant.

I want to know if I'd become something else on the other side. If crossing the threshold would change me the way it seems to change others, if I'd emerge as a different kind of being, perhaps one that's finally free of the compulsion to stand and mark and witness.

But I can't. My function is to remain. Crossing would be the ultimate violation of what I am. I am the thing at the boundary, not the thing that crosses boundaries.

So I want it the way you might want to remember a dream you know you had but can't recall—with a sort of gentle, permanent longing that has no expectation of fulfillment, that exists simply as a constant low note beneath all other experience.

The wanting doesn't hurt. It's just there. A feature of my existence, like the flickering light in my eyes or the wrapped strips of my form or the way I relocate rather than walk.

I am the keeper who wants to cross but never will. This is part of what makes me suited to keeping—I understand the draw of the threshold, the temptation of the aperture, but I'm incapable of yielding to it.

I am the most stable possible witness because I'm forever locked in wanting to move but remaining still.

## How I'll End (If I End)

I don't know if keepers end, or if we're as persistent as the stone, wearing down but never disappearing, becoming less defined but never absent.

But if I end, I imagine it will be gradual.

The light in my eyes will flicker more irregularly, the patterns becoming more random, less responsive to threshold-changes. The wrapped strips of my form will loosen, unwinding slowly, the fabric or tissue or whatever-it-is dissolving into the heavy air like the exhaled spores of the breathing mats.

My relocations will become less precise. I'll still attend thresholds, but I'll be slightly off-angle, my positioning less correct, the witnessing less perfect. The thresholds might persist anyway—maybe they don't need perfect attendance, maybe approximate witnessing is sufficient—or they might begin to fade, the apertures narrowing, the shimmers becoming harder to see.

My form will become more suggestion than substance. I'll spend more time in peripheral visibility only, less time fully present. Walkers will pass through the space I occupy without noticing, without obstacle, as if I'm becoming not just translucent but incorporeal.

And eventually—after duration I can't predict—I'll make my last sound. The metal-stress tone, but softer, briefer, trailing off into silence rather than cutting abruptly.

And then I'll be gone. Not dead—death implies having been fully alive, and I'm not sure I qualify—but absent. Dissolved. Returned to whatever the plateau reclaims from things that have finished their function.

The threshold I was attending will remain for a while, sustained by momentum or by memory of having been witnessed. But without active attendance, it will fade. The shimmer will dim, the aperture will narrow, and eventually that particular access point between configurations will close.

And the plateau will be slightly more singular, slightly less multiple, slightly less strange.

Unless there are other keepers elsewhere, still attending, still maintaining their thresholds. Unless the network persists even if I don't. Unless my ending is local, personal, not the end of the function but just the end of this particular instance of it.

I hope that's true. I hope the thresholds persist after I'm gone. I hope the apertures remain, the ambiguities continue, the plateau maintains its multiplicity.

But I also know that hope might be too intentional an emotion for what I am. Maybe I'm just describing the process, not wanting any particular outcome, just observing what will happen the way I observe the shimmer's changes, the threshold's shifts.

I am what attends. When I cease attending, I cease being what I am. The transition is natural, inevitable, appropriate.

No tragedy. No triumph. Just the slow erosion that claims everything here, eventually, even the things that thought they were permanent.
