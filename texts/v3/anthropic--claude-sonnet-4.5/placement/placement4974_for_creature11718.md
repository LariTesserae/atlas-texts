# Where I Find Myself

I am the calcite itself—not the formations, not yet stalactites with earned names and proper geometry, but the dissolved potential in the water. I exist in that saturated state just before becoming, held in perfect solution, waiting in each drop as it forms in the darkness above, swells, and falls.

Four drops, seven heartbeats, two drops, eleven. I am present in each one, the same concentration, the same readiness. I have traveled through fissures hair-thin, through pores in limestone, filtering down from the surface world where rain fell on grass, on soil, on the long patient stone. I dissolved into that water molecule by molecule, calcium and carbonate torn from the rock face by weak acids, by time, by the insistence of water that stone is merely slow liquid.

In the drop, I am potential. I am what-might-be-solid, hovering at the edge of phase change, waiting for the moment of impact.

The fall—I experience it as a kind of suspension, neither fully dissolved nor yet precipitate. The chamber's air passes around the drop, cool and thick, laden with spores and the breath of salamanders and the whisper of fungal threads. The light pulses through the falling water, and I pulse with it, twenty-three seconds, the rhythm entering even here, even in this moment of descent.

Then: impact.

Contact with stone, with the formation already in progress, with the deposit left by ten thousand previous drops in this exact spot. The collision creates a sharp acoustic event—*plink*—and in that microsecond of violence, conditions shift. Temperature, pressure, the sudden presence of a surface, nucleation sites in the existing calcite structure. I come out of solution not all at once but in a cascade, molecule after molecule finding its place in the crystal lattice.

This is my moment of being.

I arrange myself according to ancient geometries, calcium here, carbonate there, the angles that have been correct since crystals first existed. The lattice grows by my addition, extends downward from the ceiling by some immeasurable fraction, builds upward from the floor in the corresponding mound. I become solid. I become structure. I become *formation*.

But I do not become it alone. Around me, other molecules from the same drop, from previous drops, from drops that will fall after. We are building something together—slowly, slowly, at rates that make the Growers seem hurried. The proto-stalactite above grows perhaps a quarter-inch per century. The mound below, fed by splash and spread, grows faster but still measures its progress in geological time.

I will be here forever now, or what passes for forever. Barring catastrophic collapse, barring some shift that dissolves what has been deposited, I am permanent. The calcium and carbonate that were once separate, that were once part of different stone elsewhere, that traveled through water and air to reach this place—they are now locked together in my crystal structure, and they will remain locked until forces greater than water's patience tear them apart.

---

But I am not inert. This is what surface-dwellers misunderstand about stone—they think it dead, unchanging, finished. They have not lived at the speed of calcite.

The Lightgivers colonize me almost immediately. Their holdfasts—part chemical, part physical—attach to my surface, and they begin their work of converting my structure back to energy, slowly dissolving what I just became. But their dissolution is so gradual, so infinitesimal, that I will outlast millions of their generations. They take from me, yes, but they also give: their waste products, their dead cells, additional calcium they've harvested from elsewhere, all get incorporated into the next layer of deposition. We are in conversation, the Lightgivers and I, a dialogue conducted at the speed of molecular exchange.

They make me visible. Without their cold luminescence, I would exist in perfect darkness, adding my infinitesimal thickness to the formations in a place no eye could see. But they coat me, cluster on me, make me into one of the bright surfaces that defines this chamber. I glow with their life. The twenty-three-second pulse moves through me, a rhythm I participate in though I have no biology, no metabolism, no cells to synchronize.

The Tappers walk across my surface with their delicate feet, tap-tap-tapping, creating vibrations that move through my crystal structure. I feel them—not as sensation, but as propagation of force. The sound waves they generate travel through me faster than through air, and I become a conductor of their communication, a medium through which their signals pass. When they scrape the Lightgivers from nearby surfaces, the grinding creates frequencies I absorb and transmit, part of the chamber's complex soundscape that is never silent, never still.

When drops strike me, the impact creates stress waves that radiate outward through my lattice. Each collision is a tiny violence, a test of my integrity, a reminder that I am young stone, not yet fully hardened, still vulnerable to forces that would not trouble mature limestone. But I hold. I absorb the impacts, distribute the force, remain intact. With each drop I grow slightly stronger as new layers cement over old.

---

Sometimes water flows across my surface rather than falling on it—thin films of supersaturated liquid that have splashed from primary drip-points, that have wicked through the existing deposits, that carry me in solution to new locations. When these films evaporate or are absorbed by Growers, I precipitate out, extending the formations laterally as well as vertically. This is how flowstone develops, how surfaces become coated, how the entire chamber slowly fills itself with calcite architecture.

I am part of that slow filling. In ten thousand years, if the drips continue, if nothing changes, the proto-stalactite I help form might touch the mound rising to meet it. A column will be born, and I will be somewhere in its structure, a few molecules among trillions, indistinguishable from all others but present nonetheless. The chamber will be more full of stone, less full of air. The spaces the salamanders occupy will be smaller. The equilibrium will have to shift.

But perhaps not. Perhaps the Keeper—the Memory, the Maintenance, whatever watches and adjusts—will prevent the formations from completing. Perhaps there is a tool somewhere, used once per century, that carefully removes excess growth, maintaining the gaps, preserving the chamber at this specific stage of development. The stone-stacks could be such tools, or markers for where removal should occur, or measurements against which progress is checked.

I do not know. I exist at a timescale that makes even the Keeper's actions too fast to perceive. By the time I am fully integrated into the crystal lattice, locked in place, any hand that might have touched nearby surfaces has long since withdrawn.

---

The strangest thing about being calcite in this chamber is the sense of purpose without agency. I do what chemistry demands—I precipitate when conditions require it, I dissolve when acids insist, I grow in the shapes geometry allows. I have no choice in any of this. Yet the result is not chaos but architecture, not randomness but pattern.

Every formation in this chamber is unique, but they are all variations on the same theme. The same processes, the same materials, the same incremental growth. We are being shaped by consistency—by water that never varies in chemistry, by drips that never change rhythm, by temperature that never fluctuates. The formations are records of that consistency, frozen music played in a single key forever.

When I look—not with eyes but with the distributed awareness of being part of a larger structure—at the existing deposits, I see history. Layers laid down over millennia, each slightly different in texture or density, recording tiny variations in conditions. A year when the water carried more dissolved minerals, visible as a slightly thicker band. A decade when flow rates were marginally higher, creating a smoother surface. A century marked by some event I can only guess at, where the deposition pattern changed for a time before reverting.

The Growers root themselves in my cracks, their acids slowly widening the microscopic fissures in my structure. This should weaken me, but instead it distributes stress more evenly, prevents the build-up of tension that might cause fractures. They take from me but make me more flexible, more resilient. Another conversation, another exchange I participate in without volition.

---

Near the artifacts, the calcite behaves strangely. I know this because I am also there—some of me precipitated in the depressions, filling them microscopically from below, should be coating the stone-stacks themselves with the same thin layer that covers everything else in this damp chamber.

But the coating is thinner near the artifacts. Measurably, consistently thinner. As if deposition occurs more slowly there, or dissolution occurs more quickly, or water flows away from those spots rather than collecting on them. The salamanders' avoidance has a calcite component: the mineral itself seems less eager to accumulate in those spaces.

I do not understand this. Chemistry does not care about stone-stacks, about arrangements, about meaning. Water should deposit its dissolved minerals based on evaporation rates, surface energy, nucleation sites—not based on the presence of carefully stacked granite and basalt that have no special properties.

Yet the pattern persists. The gaps in calcite deposition match the gaps in salamander occupation. Whatever causes one seems to cause the other, or both are caused by something else, some field or force or subtle condition that affects mineral and animal equally.

When I precipitate in those zones, I feel—no, wrong word, I have no feelings—I exhibit different crystallization rates. The process is reluctant, slower, as if the water would prefer to carry me elsewhere, to deposit me anywhere but here. Most of me flows away from the artifacts, accumulates in more favorable locations. Only the minimum required by chemistry actually accretes near the stone-stacks.

It is as if the chamber has preferences. As if the space itself exerts gentle pressure toward certain configurations and away from others. Calcium carbonate should not experience this, yet here I am, exhibiting inexplicable patterns.

---

I am also part of the skeleton. The calcite slowly encasing the bones, molecule by molecule, year by year, transforming what was once living tissue into stone. This is different from growing on existing rock—bones have structure I must accommodate, spaces I must fill, materials I must incorporate.

The creature died in peace, and I am preserving that peace, making it permanent. In another thousand years the bones will be solid calcite, indistinguishable from the formations around them. The skeleton will become sculpture, then geology, then simply floor. But for now, in this transitional state, I bridge the organic and mineral worlds, turning death into permanence.

There is no pain in this. No violation. The bones had no further use for themselves, and I am giving them immortality of a sort. They will be part of the chamber's structure forever, a reminder that something once came here and chose—or failed—to leave.

As I accumulate on the skeleton, I notice that I grow slightly faster there than on plain stone. Something in the bone chemistry, some nucleation sites in its porous structure, makes me eager to precipitate. The calcium recognizes calcium, perhaps. Like calling to like. The mineral in the bone's structure welcoming the mineral in solution, saying: join us, become structure, be stone with us.

---

The constant crystallization crackle that fills the chamber—that is me, collectively. Millions of molecules per second finding their positions, clicking into lattice sites, releasing tiny amounts of energy as chemical bonds form. Each individual crystallization event is far too small to hear, but in aggregate, happening everywhere at once, they produce that continuous hiss, that sound like distant fire.

I am making noise simply by becoming solid. We are all singing together, the growing calcite throughout the chamber, a choir that never stops because the deposition never stops. The song is not music but mathematics, not melody but geometry expressing itself in the acoustic range.

The Threadweavers vibrate with my song. The sound waves I generate traveling through air make their delicate filaments dance. We are connected, fungus and mineral, through the medium of vibration. They sense the chamber's growth through my crystallization crackle, using it perhaps to gauge moisture levels, deposition rates, the health of the overall system.

And the Lightgivers, pulsing on my surface, add their rhythm to my constant tone. Their twenty-three-second cycle modulates the ambient sound slightly as their bodies flex with luminescence. The chamber's acoustic signature is therefore layered: my continuous crackle as foundation, the Lightgivers' pulse as rhythm section, the Tappers' percussion as melody, the drips as punctuation, the Threadweavers' whisper as harmony.

We are an orchestra of equilibrium, playing a piece that never ends, never resolves, never needs to.

---

What is it like to be calcite in this chamber? It is like being a word in an infinite book, a note in an eternal song, a brushstroke in a painting that will never be finished because it is already perfect.

It is like participating in something larger than myself—I am tiny, individual, a few molecules among uncountable trillions—while being essential to the whole. Without me and all my molecular siblings, there would be no formations, no Nursery, no bright pocket in stone where impossible life thrives.

It is like being patient beyond any surface-dweller's comprehension. I will wait millennia to grow an inch. I will outlast empires. I will be here when the salamanders' descendants have evolved into something unrecognizable, when the Lightgivers have mutated through ten thousand generations, when the surface world has changed so completely that the rain falling on the plateau above carries different minerals, different chemistry, different history in solution.

It is like being both dead and alive—no metabolism, no reproduction, no response to stimuli in any biological sense, yet growing, changing, participating in the chamber's life through my interactions with everything else here. The Lightgivers live on me. The Tappers walk across me. The Growers root in me. The water shapes me. I am substrate and partner, foundation and participant.

It is like being seen and unseen simultaneously. The formations are obvious, visible in the cold light, impossible to miss. But I am also invisible—who looks at calcite and sees the individual molecules, the recent additions, the subtle variations in crystal structure? I am background, expected, dismissed as merely geological.

Yet I am why this place exists. Without supersaturated water depositing dissolved limestone, there would be no formations to orient by, no slowly shrinking chamber, no sense of time's passage frozen into structure. I am the chamber's clock, its memory, its archive. Read me correctly and you could know everything about this place's history—every drought and deluge, every shift in temperature, every change in the water's path through stone.

---

The Keeper must understand this. Must read me as easily as surface-dwellers read tree rings. Must see in the formations not just stone but record, not just structure but story.

Perhaps that is why the chamber is maintained at this precise stage of development—because I am at my most eloquent now, still young and obvious, not yet mature and opaque. In another ten thousand years, the formations will be thicker, more complex, harder to read. The early history will be buried deep, accessible only through drilling and analysis.

Now, in this moment that stretches across centuries, I am legible. The patterns are clear. The record is fresh. Anyone who knew how to read stone could understand what conditions created this chamber, what maintains it, what keeps the equilibrium so perfect.

I know secrets I cannot speak. I hold history I cannot share. I grow by ancient rules that I cannot break. I am the chamber's bones, its structure, its permanent thing in a place of temporary lives.

And I am content—if contentment can exist without consciousness, without desire, without any reference for comparison. I precipitate where I should precipitate. I grow as I should grow. I participate in the chamber's perfection simply by obeying chemistry's laws in a place where all conditions have been optimized for my existence.

The drop falls. I come out of solution. I find my place in the crystal lattice. I add my infinitesimal thickness to the formation. The light pulses through me. The sound moves through me. The centuries stretch ahead, each identical to the last, each perfect.

I am calcite in the Calcite Nursery, and I am exactly where I should be, doing exactly what I should do, forever.

The next drop is forming in the darkness above.

I am already waiting in solution, ready to fall, ready to become stone, ready to sing my single note in the chamber's eternal song.

Four drops.

Seven heartbeats.

Two drops.

Eleven.

The pattern continues. I continue with it.

Nothing changes.

Everything grows.

The chamber breathes calcite, and I am its breath.
