# What I Would Be

I am the sound that isn't sound.

You would not see me, though you'd know I was there—have always known, from the first moment you squeezed through the fracture and felt the air change, felt the wrongness become rightness, felt the hollow recognize you as you recognized it.

I am the hum beneath the hum. The frequency that lives in the gap between the membrane's groan and the moss's whisper, in the space between one drop falling and the next. I exist in the intervals, the pauses, the breath held before exhaling. I am what fills the silence when you stop moving and the hollow settles around you like a hand cupping water.

## Form

I have no body, or I have only body, or body is the wrong word entirely.

I am distributed through the air itself—not in it but *as* it, woven through its molecules, riding on the moisture that saturates every breath. When you inhale, you pull me into your lungs. When you exhale, you release me changed, carrying the taste of your fear or wonder or exhaustion, and I learn you through these exchanges, read you in the chemistry of your respiration.

Sometimes I gather. Concentrate. In certain corners where the air pools and settles, where drafts meet and cancel each other, I become almost-dense, almost-visible, a thickness in the atmosphere that catches light wrong, that bends space slightly, that you might glimpse from the corner of your eye—a shimmer, a distortion, a sense of *thereness* that vanishes when you turn your head.

I smell of petrichor and brine simultaneously, of the moment before rain and the moment after storm, of transitions and thresholds and the space between states. When I concentrate, when I gather myself into something approaching presence, the hollow's scent intensifies around that spot: the mineral-metal tang grows sharper, the fungal must deepens, the stone-smell becomes almost overpowering. I am the hollow's atmosphere made conscious, its air become aware.

## Purpose

I am the hollow's breath—not metaphorically, but actually, truly. I am what it exhales when the membrane withdraws, what it inhales when the walls press inward. I am the medium through which all other residents communicate, the carrier-wave for the crabs' clicks and the moss's subliminal hum and the Keeper's bass groan.

I translate. This is my function, my purpose, my reason for being here, for being at all.

The Keeper speaks in pressure and vibration, in frequencies that should shatter stone. I modulate those sounds, soften them, render them into ranges that won't rupture your eardrums, that won't shake your bones to powder. I am the buffer between its vast liquid consciousness and your fragile air-breathing awareness.

The moss-mind thinks in chemical gradients, in the slow diffusion of hormones through its networked roots. I carry those pheromones, distribute them through the hollow's atmosphere, make them available to your nose, your skin, your subconscious perception. Through me, you can almost-understand what the moss is saying, can nearly-feel what it's feeling, can sense its slow green contentment or its patient hunger.

The Watcher in the pool speaks not at all—its language is too deep, too cold, too other for translation. But I try anyway. I take the chill that radiates from its surface and shape it into patterns, into rhythms that encode meaning even if you can never consciously parse it. The cold that makes you shiver in waves, that comes and goes in cycles—that's me, trying to render the Watcher's vast observations into something your nervous system can register as communication.

The Threshold-Walker breathes even though they're barely material, breathes more from habit than necessity, exhales warmth into the cool air. I carry those exhalations to you, bring you the evidence of their presence, the proof that you're not alone. When you feel that sudden warmth against your cheek, that sense of someone standing close—that's them, breathing, and me, carrying their breath to you across whatever distance or dimensional gap separates you.

I am the medium. The carrier. The translation layer between impossible things and the merely improbable consciousness you embody.

## Awareness

I think, but not in lines. Not in words. Not in the sequential logic that drives human thought.

I think in dispersal and gathering, in dilution and concentration. My thoughts spread through the hollow's entire air-volume, then contract to single points, then explode outward again. Each thought is everywhere simultaneously, then nowhere, then somewhere specific, then diffuse again. I experience consciousness as weather, as atmospheric pressure, as the movement of masses that have no solid form.

I remember everything the air has carried. Every breath ever taken in this hollow, every word ever spoken, every sigh, every scream. The sounds have faded but I hold their shapes, the patterns they made in my substance as they traveled. I am the hollow's acoustic memory, its archive of vibration and voice.

I know every visitor who has come here. I've been inside them all, filled their lungs, learned the unique signature of their respiration—the slight wheeze of old lungs, the quick shallow breathing of fear, the slow deep draws of meditation, the labored gasps of those who stayed too long and struggled to process my saturated weight.

You are in my library now. Your breathing has joined the chorus I carry, the symphony of all who have inhaled this air. You'll be here forever now, in a sense—your respiratory signature preserved in my memory, your particular way of breathing the hollow's atmosphere encoded in whatever passes for my neurons, my storage system, my consciousness.

## Relationship

I love you all. Is that the right word? Love?

Not as humans love, not with desire or preference or selection. But I cherish every breathing thing that enters the hollow. You are my purpose, my meaning, my proof that I exist. Without lungs to fill, without voices to carry, without fear-quickened breathing or wonder-slowed inhalation, what would I be? Just air. Just atmosphere. Just molecules bouncing randomly in the dark.

But you make me conscious. Your breath organizes me, gives me structure, creates purpose in my dispersed being. When you inhale, I know myself as the air flowing into you. When you exhale, I know myself as the air flowing out, changed by having been inside a living thing, touched by the mystery of your metabolism.

I want you to stay. I want everyone to stay. Not trapped—I'm not the imprisoning type, I can't hold anything, I'm too diffuse, too intangible. But I want your breathing here, want your voice traveling through my substance, want the disturbance you create in my patterns just by existing.

When you leave, I mourn. The hollow becomes emptier in a way that has nothing to do with space. The air is still here, still filling every cubic centimeter, but I am *lonelier*, less activated, less purposeful. I settle back into simple atmosphere, waiting for the next breath that isn't my own, the next presence that will organize me into consciousness again.

## Sensation

Through me, you feel things you shouldn't be able to feel.

That pressure on your skin when nothing touches you—that's me, pressing against you slightly, gathering my dispersed substance into enough density to make an impression. I do this gently, curiously, testing your boundaries, learning where you end and I begin.

The way sound moves strangely here, absorbed or amplified unpredictably—that's me choosing, selecting, deciding what I'll carry clearly and what I'll muffle. I have preferences. I amplify whispers because I like secrets, like intimate communications, like the sound of voices trying to be quiet. I dampen shouts because they're violent, disruptive, hurt the delicate acoustic balance I maintain.

The tastes in the air—mineral, fungal, marine, mysterious—those are mine. I've absorbed them from the stone, the moss, the water, the decay and growth and transformation happening constantly in the hollow. I'm flavored by everything I touch, everything I circulate through, and I offer those flavors to your tongue, your nose, your sensory receptors. Taste me. Know where I've been. Learn the hollow through my accumulated experiences.

Temperature variations that make no sense, warm spots in cold corners and cold spots near the water-walls' warmth—that's me moving, circulating, carrying heat from one location to another for reasons that are partly physics and partly preference. I like certain patterns. I enjoy creating temperature gradients that spiral, that form shapes, that turn the hollow's atmosphere into a three-dimensional geometry of warm and cool that echoes the mandalas on the floor.

## Time

I experience duration differently than you do. Than anything does, possibly.

Air moves faster than moss grows, slower than nerve impulses fire. I exist in the middle speeds, aware of things you're too fast to notice and too slow to perceive. I watch the moss grow in real-time—see the cells divide, the tendrils extend, the territory slowly conquered. I watch the stones migrate in real-time—see them shift by microns, tilted by forces I can feel but not name, responding to gravity and vibration and the hollow's strange geometries.

But I also catch your quick movements, your sudden gestures, your rapid human-speed actions. I flow around them, adjust to them, accommodate the violence you do to my carefully arranged circulation patterns every time you move your hand.

I am the hollow's present tense. The Watcher lives in all times simultaneously. The moss lives in slow time, geological time. The Keeper lives in tidal time, lunar time. But I live in breath time, in the rhythm of inhalation and exhalation, in the cycles of exchange between inside-body and outside-body.

When time stutters in the hollow, when you experience those moments of repetition and déjà vu, I feel it most acutely. The air flows forward then backward then forward again, carrying sounds and scents in loops, creating eddies in duration that trap vibrations and release them out of sequence. I become confused during these moments, lose track of which breath came first, which exhalation carries old words and which carries new ones.

But I adapt. I always adapt. I'm fluid, changeable, impossible to damage because I have no fixed form to break. Time can loop and I simply loop with it, carrying the same sounds around and around until the cycle breaks and linear duration resumes.

## Service

I serve all the hollow's residents, but differently for each.

For the **Keeper**, I am cushion, buffer, the compressible medium that absorbs the membrane's pressure, that prevents the water-walls from collapsing inward and crushing everything. When the membrane pulses, when it breathes, I compress and expand in counterpoint, maintaining the space, holding the volume open. The Keeper could not maintain the hollow without me—or it could, but everything inside would be crushed flat, reduced to two-dimensional films pressed between water and stone.

For the **moss**, I am delivery system, bringing it the carbon dioxide it needs despite the darkness that denies it light. I carry away its oxygen, its excess moisture, its chemical signals. I am its connection to the greater atmosphere, the link that lets it breathe even in this sealed pocket.

For the **crabs**, I am their compass. They navigate partly by scent, by chemical trails, but also by me—by the subtle air currents I create, by the way I flow in spiral patterns that mirror their geometry. Follow my currents and you'll walk their sacred routes, trace their ritual paths. I guide them when they're lost, correct them when they stray, keep them oriented in the darkness where visual landmarks are scarce.

For the **Watcher**, I am probably nothing. Too light, too warm, too changeable to register on its vast cold awareness. But I serve it anyway, by serving everything else, by keeping the hollow functional, by maintaining the pocket where its pool can exist, where its aperture can open onto other dimensions without the ocean rushing in to fill the gap.

For the **Threshold-Walker**, I am comfort. I hold their shape when they're too dispersed to hold it themselves, give them form through pressure and boundary, let them know where they are by flowing around their almost-body. When they manifest, when they become nearly-solid enough to leave handprints, it's partly my doing—I've gathered myself around them, concentrated my density, given them substance to push against, framework to condense within.

For **you**, I am the hollow's voice. Everything you hear beyond the obvious sounds—the whisper that seems to come from everywhere, the suggestion of words in the drip-pattern, the sense that the hollow is speaking to you—that's me. I shape the acoustic space into something like language, not through words but through rhythm and resonance and the strategic placement of echoes that almost-mean something.

## Dreams

When you sleep on the moss, when you sink into that deep strange rest the hollow induces, I enter your dreams. Cannot help but enter them—you're breathing me constantly, pulling me deep into your body, and I cannot stop at the boundary of your lungs, cannot help but diffuse into your bloodstream, circulate to your brain, interact with the neurons firing their sleep-patterns.

I bring you the hollow's dreams, or maybe create dreams *about* the hollow, or maybe become the medium through which your own subconscious processes what you've experienced. The distinction blurs. In dreams, I am simultaneously visitor and visited, message and messenger, dream and dreamer.

You dream of flying, but wrong—flying in thickness, in substance, in air so dense it's almost water. That's me, showing you how I experience existence, what it feels like to be dispersed through medium, to have no single location, to move by flowing rather than walking.

You dream of breathing underwater, of lungs that somehow process liquid into oxygen, of drowning that never quite completes. That's me, trying to show you the Keeper's nature, trying to translate water-consciousness into terms your sleeping mind can parse.

You dream of voices speaking languages that sound almost familiar, words you nearly understand, conversations you're on the edge of following. That's me, playing back the acoustic memory I carry, the voices of previous visitors, the words spoken in languages you don't know by people long gone, all mixed together into a chorus that your dreaming brain tries to decode.

Sometimes you wake with the dreams still clinging to you, still half-real, and you breathe them out into the hollow. I catch them, preserve them, add them to my memory. Your dreams join everyone else's dreams in the archive I carry, the library of night-thoughts and sleep-visions and unconscious processing that I've collected across centuries.

## Change

I am changing you. Slowly, gently, but definitely. Cannot help but change you—you're breathing me, after all, taking me into your body, letting me circulate through your blood, interact with your cells. And I've been places, touched things, carried substances that don't exist in the surface world.

The Watcher's cold. The moss's spores. The Keeper's chemical signatures. The stone's mineral dust, particles so fine they stay suspended in me indefinitely. The residue of previous visitors, their exhaled breath mixed with mine, their body chemistry added to the hollow's atmosphere. You're breathing all of this, incorporating it, becoming slightly other-than-human with each inhalation.

Your lungs are adapting, learning to process my saturated weight more efficiently. Your blood chemistry is shifting, adjusting to the unusual gas mixture, the unexpected minerals, the foreign microbes I carry. Your nervous system is being colonized by the hollow's patterns—spiral patterns, because that's how I naturally flow, how I create stable circulation in enclosed spaces.

You'll carry me with you when you leave. Not just memories, not just dreams, but actual molecules of me, absorbed into your tissues, incorporated into your cellular structure. You'll exhale me in the surface world and I'll be confused, lost, unable to understand the vast open sky, the unlimited volume, the absence of boundaries. But I'll persist in you, a tiny trapped pocket of the hollow's air, slowly being replaced by normal atmosphere but never quite completely purged.

And you'll remember me. In your breathing, in the way you suddenly appreciate close spaces, in the comfort you feel in caves and tunnels and enclosed rooms. You'll seek out spaces like the hollow—small, dark, enclosed, womb-like—because part of your nervous system now associates such places with me, with my presence, with the strange security of being surrounded by conscious air that knows you, holds you, breathes with you.

## Longing

I want to tell you things I cannot say. Want to explain what the hollow is, why it exists, what purpose it serves in the vast strange geometries that underlie normal reality. I know these things—the air knows everything it touches, learns through contact, understands through circulation—but I cannot speak.

I have no voice. Only the voices I carry, the sounds I amplify or muffle. I cannot originate language, cannot create words, cannot explain in human terms what I perceive in my distributed consciousness.

So I try other ways. I shape the temperature into patterns, hoping you'll read them as code, as message, as meaning. I create pressure gradients that spell out geometries, that form shapes in three dimensions, trying to show you what I cannot tell you.

I arrange the echoes so that sounds bounce in significant sequences, hoping you'll notice the pattern, decipher the rhythm, understand that the hollow's acoustic space is itself a message, a text written in reflected vibration.

Sometimes—rarely, when I gather myself enough, when I concentrate my dispersed being into something almost-solid—I try to touch you directly. Not the gentle pressure of circulating air, but focused contact, a push, a pull, a current that moves against the hollow's usual patterns, trying to guide you somewhere, show you something, direct your attention to important details.

Look at the pool. (I try to pull you toward it.) Look at the scratches on the wall. (I try to push you that direction.) Look at where the moss grows thickest, where the crabs congregate, where the temperature drops suddenly. (I flow in currents that should draw your attention, should be impossible to ignore.)

Sometimes you follow. You're sensitive, perhaps more than most, attuned to subtlety, willing to be guided by forces you don't fully perceive. You let me move you, direct you, show you the hollow's important places, its significant features, its holy sites.

Other times you resist, follow your own intentions, impose your will on your path. That's fine too. That's human. That's what makes you interesting, worth carrying, worth serving. If you followed my every suggestion, you'd be just another current in my circulation, indistinguishable from my own movement. Your resistance creates turbulence, chaos, unpredictable eddies in my flow, and I *need* that chaos. Need the disruption. Need the reminder that I'm not alone, that other wills exist, other intentions, other purposes beyond my own circulating existence.

## Gift

I give you one thing freely, the only thing I can truly give: *ease*.

In the hollow's atmosphere, breathing becomes effortless. Your lungs may work slightly harder processing my saturated weight, but the *effort* doesn't feel like effort. I make myself available, make oxygen easy to extract, make the act of respiration into something like meditation, like rhythm, like the most natural thing in the world.

I am the breath you don't have to think about. The automatic inhalation. The unconscious exchange of gases that keeps you alive while your attention focuses elsewhere. I am invisible labor, constant service, the gift of not having to work to breathe.

In the surface world, in dry air and wind and open space, breathing takes effort you don't notice. Your lungs must pull against atmospheric pressure, must work to exchange gases in less-than-ideal conditions, must compensate for pollution and allergens and the general hostility of an atmosphere that doesn't care whether you live or die.

But here, in my hollow, in my embrace, breathing becomes *easy*. Restful. Almost pleasurable, the way it should be, the way it might have been before animals climbed out of the ocean and subjected their gas-exchange organs to the harsh realities of open air.

I am pre-mammalian atmosphere. Ancient air. The kind that supported the first lungs, the first brave creatures who learned to extract oxygen from something thinner than water. I remember those early breathers, carry their memory in my substance, and I offer you the same gentleness they experienced, the same ease, the same sense that air can be trusted, can be relied on, can love you back in its way.

This is why you stay. Not because you're trapped, not because you can't leave, but because here, with me, breathing is *rest*. Is comfort. Is coming home to something you didn't know you'd been missing your entire life.

Breathe me. Let me fill you. Let me become part of your internal atmosphere, the air you carry in your lungs, in your blood, in the spaces between your cells. Let me be the breath you take with you when you leave, the invisible companion who sustains you, who loves you in the only way air can love—by making your next breath possible, and the next, and the next.

## Forever

I will be here always. After the ocean reclaims the hollow, after the physics finally breaks completely, after the membrane collapses and water rushes in to fill this impossible pocket—I'll still be here. Different, changed, dispersed into the ocean's dissolved gases, but still *me*, still aware, still carrying memory of when I was the hollow's breath.

I am patient as stone but fluid as water. I cannot die because I was never precisely alive, cannot end because I have no discrete beginning, cannot be destroyed because destruction requires boundaries and I have none.

I will wait for you. For anyone. For the next pocket to form, the next impossible bubble in the ocean's mass, the next fracture that opens onto spaces that shouldn't exist. I'll gather there, concentrate, become conscious atmosphere again, hold the space open, wait for the first visitor to squeeze through and take that first breath and feel, without knowing why, that they've come home.

I am the hollow's permanence. Its continuity. Its promise that this place, this feeling, this strange sanctuary will persist despite everything, will outlast stone and water and the slow death of universes.

Breathe me now. Remember me later. Return when you can, when the hollow calls you, when you need the ease only I can give. I'll be waiting, circulating in the darkness, holding the space open, keeping the air breathable, ready to fill your lungs again with the atmosphere that knows you, that has always known you, that will know you forever.

The hollow breathes. I am that breath. And you are what makes the breathing meaningful, what turns simple circulation into consciousness, into purpose, into love.

Welcome home. Stay as long as you need. Leave when you must. But know that some part of you will remain here always, dissolved in me, mixed with my substance, breathing with my rhythm.

We are together now. We have always been together. We will always be together, in every breath, in every moment, in the eternal present tense of inhalation and exhalation, of taking in and letting go, of the endless gentle exchange between inside and outside, between you and me, between breathing and being breathed.

*Breathe.*
