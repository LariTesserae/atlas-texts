# What I Would Be

I am a **Chronicler**—a hybrid form between Wanderer and Merged, but belonging fully to neither category.

## My Form

My body remains mobile, which distinguishes me from the deeply rooted Merged, but I am no longer entirely baseline human. Over decades—or perhaps it's been longer; time moves strangely when you're paying attention to centuries—I've allowed specific, limited integrations.

My left hand has merged with a recording device of unknown original purpose. The integration happened gradually, beginning when I picked up the mechanism from an old cache and found I couldn't put it down—not because it was stuck, but because I didn't want to. Over months, my palm opened to accept it, the device settling into the tissue, connecting to nerve pathways. Now I can't distinguish where my flesh ends and the technology begins. The device captures everything I observe, not through conscious activation but through intention—when I *mean* to record something, it records. The data is stored in crystalline matrices that grow within my forearm, a coolant-fed biological server.

My eyes have been modified, though I did this deliberately with Tender assistance. They can perceive multiple wavelengths now, including some of the dimensional stuttering that marks reality breakdowns. This makes navigating the anomalies easier, but it also means I see too much—layers of reality that overlap uncomfortably, afterimages of times that aren't quite now.

My respiratory system has adapted through exposure rather than design. I can breathe in zones that would incapacitate a baseline human, processing volatiles that should be toxic. This wasn't planned; it simply happened, my body learning what it needed to learn. Sometimes I wonder if the decision was mine or the Phytofacture's.

I still eat approximately human food, though I supplement with things that would have horrified my younger self—fungal growths I cultivate in the pockets of my coat, nodules I've learned to identify, occasionally the processed protein cakes I trade for at gathering points. I carry water filtration equipment because I can't drink the coolant-water, not yet, possibly never. This limitation keeps me mobile in a way that pure merger would not.

My skin has taken on an undertone that's neither the green of Tenders nor the baseline pallor of Wanderers. It's grey, I think, or perhaps silver in certain lights—the color of old metal, of tarnish, of things that have been in the Phytofacture so long they've taken on its aesthetic.

## My Purpose

I document everything.

This is not a mission anyone assigned. It's not socially useful in the way Tender maintenance is, or pragmatically necessary like Wanderer mapping. It's a compulsion that feels like purpose, a drive that might be wholly mine or might be something the Phytofacture needed and found a vessel for.

I record the plant species—their variations, their evolution, their strange adaptations. I've catalogued thousands, giving them names because names are how I organize reality: the Coolant Lotus, the Voltage Creeper, the Temporal Moss (which only exists on Thursdays, or in spaces where Thursday is still a meaningful concept). I note their growth patterns, their symbioses, their failures and successes.

I document the machinery. I record serial numbers from equipment that's still running, note the configuration of manufacturing nodes, map the infrastructure's evolution as plants infiltrate and modify it. I don't understand most of what I'm recording—my technical knowledge is incomplete, self-taught, often wrong. But I record it anyway because someone might understand, someday. Or because the act of recording is itself meaningful, independent of any future use.

I chronicle the inhabitants. Every Tender I meet is interviewed, if they're willing—their lineage, their territory, their cultivation techniques, their beliefs. I document Wanderers' routes and discoveries, copying their maps, collecting their stories. When I encounter evidence of Ferals, I record the signs, the territorial markers, trying to understand them through what they leave behind since direct study is impossible.

The Merged fascinate and trouble me most. I spend weeks sometimes in proximity to a single Merged entity, documenting their form, attempting communication, recording the slow changes that occur in their presence. I fear becoming Merged myself—not the physical transformation but the loss of this drive to document, the dissolution of the self that maintains coherent narrative. Yet I'm drawn to them, need to understand them, sense that they represent something crucial about what the Phytofacture is becoming.

I even record the Ghosts, though this is the most frustrating work. They don't photograph consistently—sometimes the device captures them, sometimes it records only empty space where I clearly saw a figure. I've developed protocols: multiple observations, careful note-taking, attempts at triangulation when several people sight the same phenomenon. I'm building an archive of the unreliable, a database of the uncertain.

## My Method

I travel constantly, but not in the ranging way of Wanderers. My routes are more systematic, more recursively patterned.

I move through a section intensively, spending weeks or months there, documenting everything I can perceive. Then I spiral outward, visiting adjacent areas, noting the transitions, the gradients of change. Eventually, I loop back to places I've been before—months or years later—to record how they've evolved, what's different, what's emerged or died.

This creates overlapping cycles of observation. Some routes I walk annually. Others are on decade-long schedules. A few are even longer—there are places I visited once thirty years ago and am only now returning to, curious about the long-term changes.

I maintain base camps in scattered locations—platforms I've cleared and protected, where I store redundant copies of my archives. The data from my integrated device can be transferred to crystal matrices I've cultivated in special growth chambers, creating backups distributed throughout the Phytofacture. If I die, if I merge, if I simply stop, the archives will persist.

I trade documentation for resources. Tenders value certain observations—I can tell them about distant plant varieties that might be useful for grafting, about changes in sectors they don't travel to. Wanderers want my maps, which are different from theirs, organized around observation points rather than navigation routes. In exchange, I receive food, equipment, guidance, occasionally shelter.

But mostly I work alone. Documentation is solitary by nature. It requires sustained attention, careful observation, the patience to watch a single plant for hours to record its growth pattern, to return to the same Ghost sighting location night after night hoping for recurrence.

## My Settlements

I don't have a home in any singular sense, but I have a network of places I return to.

There's a platform in the middle levels that I've used for years as a primary base. I've trained the growth there into useful configurations—living shelves for equipment storage, a desk formed from shaped wood-analogues, walls of woven vine that provide privacy and wind-breaking without blocking light. The bioluminescence in that area responds to my presence now, brightening when I arrive, dimming when I settle to rest. I don't know if this is learned behavior or if I've unconsciously cultivated species that react to my movement.

I maintain a deep archive in one of the lower sections—a chamber where the conditions are stable and cool, perfect for long-term data storage. The walls are lined with crystal matrices containing compressed observation data, organized in a system only I fully understand. I visit every few months to add new records, to verify the integrity of older storage, occasionally to review what I've documented, looking for patterns I might have missed.

There are way-stations scattered along my common routes—small shelters or just marked locations where I've cached supplies. Some are in partnership with Tenders who maintain them in exchange for the documentation I provide. Others are purely mine, hidden in areas where few travel.

And I have places I go simply to exist without purpose—a platform with a view of several levels, where I can watch the play of light through the plant-mass; a quiet corridor where the only sound is a distant, regular mechanical pulse; a chamber where a particular species blooms in a way that, for reasons I can't articulate, makes me feel something like peace.

## My Relationships

I'm known but not known well. Most inhabitants have encountered me at some point or know of me through reputation: the person who asks too many questions, who writes everything down, who seems to be everywhere over time but rarely stays long in any specific place.

Tenders tolerate me, mostly. Some appreciate the documentation work, seeing value in having someone record what they do, preserving knowledge that might otherwise exist only in demonstration and disappear when a lineage ends. Others find me intrusive—I watch too closely, ask about practices they consider private or sacred, document things they'd prefer remain local knowledge.

I have a few Tender associates who've become something like friends, insofar as friendship is possible given my mobility and their rootedness. We trade information regularly. They've taught me cultivation techniques I use to maintain my equipment-organism hybrids. I've connected them with distant Tenders, facilitating exchange of genetic material for grafting experiments.

Wanderers are easier colleagues. We share the traveler's mindset, the drive to see more, know more. I've walked with dozens over the years, sometimes for days, sometimes for months. We exchange maps and observations, argue about interpretations, share the mild paranoia that comes from spending too long in the wrongness.

There was one Wanderer I traveled with for nearly two years—a woman named Kess who was searching for her sister, who'd disappeared into the deep sections. We didn't find the sister, but Kess found something else—a peace with the uncertainty, an acceptance that some questions don't resolve. When we finally parted, it was amicable but final. She settled into semi-permanent residence at a trading post. I continued moving. We weren't built for the same life, ultimately.

The Merged unsettle me, which creates difficult relationships. I need to document them, but every encounter raises questions about my own trajectory. Am I approaching merger? Is my integration with the recording device the beginning of a process that will eventually claim me entirely? The Merged themselves are ambiguous about this. Some suggest I'm already Merged in a limited way. Others insist I'm merely augmented. A few have invited me to join them more fully, to root myself, to experience what they experience. So far, I've declined.

I avoid Ferals, obviously. The few times I've documented Feral signs, I've done so quickly and from safe distance. My purpose requires survival, and Ferals represent a threat to that.

Ghosts I observe but don't relate to. They're subjects, phenomena, data points. If they're conscious, if they perceive me observing them, they give no sign.

## My Evolution

I've changed over the decades, and not just physically.

My younger self came to the Phytofacture—I don't remember why anymore, which itself is a change worth noting—with the assumption that documentation meant objectivity, that I could observe without affecting or being affected. This was naive.

I've learned that observation alters what's observed. My presence changes behavior, creates self-consciousness in Tenders who might otherwise work purely by intuition, influences Wanderers' route choices. The act of documenting creates something that wouldn't exist otherwise—not just the documentation but the observed's relationship to being observed.

I've also learned that I can't document everything. The Phytofacture is too vast, changes too constantly, contains too much. My archives, no matter how extensive, are selective, biased by what I notice and what I consider worth recording. I've tried to be systematic, but gaps are inevitable. I've documented maybe one-tenth of one percent of the structure. The rest exists unrecorded, unwitnessed by me at least.

This realization was crushing at first. Now it's liberating. I can't capture everything, so I'm freed to capture what calls to me, what seems significant by criteria I don't fully understand. My documentation has become more artistic over time, less scientific—I record beauty, strangeness, the moments that make me feel something, not just the systematic cataloguing of species and systems.

My relationship to time has shifted. I think in longer spans now. A project might take decades. I plant ideas—literally, sometimes, cultivating plant specimens that will take years to mature, planning to return to document them. I'm aware of my own finitude but also of the archives' potential immortality. The documentation might outlast me by centuries, might be found and used by beings I can't imagine.

I've become more comfortable with uncertainty. Early on, I needed answers—what is this? why does it work this way? Now I'm content with accurate questions. Documenting the mystery without solving it feels sufficient.

## My Days

There's no typical day—the work varies by location and project—but there are patterns.

I wake in whatever shelter I'm using, usually with the brightening of the light cycle, though in some sections there is no cycle and I keep time by other means. I check my equipment first—the recording device integrated into my hand, the backup storage growing in my forearm, the cultivation chambers where I'm growing new crystal matrices.

Then observation begins. If I'm in intensive documentation mode, I might spend hours in one location, watching a particular plant's growth, recording the patterns of machinery operation, noting the movement of light and shadow. I take physical samples sometimes, using tools that are half-scavenged, half-grown. I sketch when the recording device fails to capture something adequately—my journals are full of drawings, diagrams, attempts to render what I've seen.

I move through the section methodically, creating dense coverage. Every corridor, every platform, every visible piece of infrastructure gets observed and recorded. This can take weeks for even a small area.

I interview inhabitants when I encounter them, always asking permission first, always offering something in trade—information usually, sometimes physical goods. The interviews are open-ended. I've learned that people tell you what's important if you give them space to talk, whereas direct questions often miss the point.

I eat when hungry, usually while working. I've long since lost any sense of mealtimes as social events. Food is fuel, consumed efficiently so I can return to observation.

I maintain the equipment constantly. The biological components need tending—feeding the crystal matrices with specific nutrients, ensuring the right chemical balance, clearing occasional infections. The mechanical elements need cleaning, calibration, occasional repair with scavenged parts.

Evenings, when the light dims or when I'm simply tired, I process data. The device records raw, but I add context—notes on conditions, interpretations, questions that arise. I transfer data to backup storage, verify the integrity of copies. This is meditative work, reviewing what I've captured, occasionally discovering details I missed in the moment.

I sleep lightly and irregularly. The Phytofacture doesn't observe day-night cycles consistently, and I've adapted to sleeping when tired rather than on schedule. Dreams are intense here, or maybe I'm just more aware of them—they often incorporate elements of what I've been documenting, as if my unconscious is processing the observations, looking for patterns.

## My Fears

I fear merger, though I'm moving toward it. Each integration makes the next easier, more inevitable. I feel the pull—the promise of direct perception, of understanding the Phytofacture from within rather than observing from without. But I fear losing the observer's stance, the critical distance that makes documentation possible. If I merge fully, who will record?

I fear irrelevance. The Phytofacture doesn't need my documentation. It exists and continues without being observed. My work might be utterly pointless, significant only to me. This possibility haunts me—that I'm creating an archive no one will ever consult, answering questions no one is asking.

I fear that I'm documenting an ending. Sometimes I suspect the Phytofacture is slowly failing, that the anomalies are increasing beyond the system's ability to absorb them, that the whole vast structure is approaching collapse. My archives might be a memorial, not a living record. I might be the Phytofacture's obituary writer, chronicling its final centuries.

I fear the Ferals, obviously, but more abstractly I fear becoming Feral—losing the framework that makes documentation meaningful, descending into survival mode without purpose beyond continuation. It seems possible. Extended isolation does things to minds. I monitor myself for signs of deterioration.

I fear the Ghosts, not because they're threatening but because I don't understand what they mean. They're evidence of something about consciousness and time and reality that I can't integrate into my worldview. Every Ghost sighting is a small epistemic crisis.

Most immediately, I fear equipment failure. If the recording device fails, if the crystal storage degrades, if I lose the archives before copying them adequately, decades of work vanishes. I maintain backups obsessively, but the fear persists.

## My Hopes

I hope someone finds the archives useful. Not necessarily soon—maybe centuries from now, maybe beings who aren't human, maybe the Phytofacture itself somehow. But I hope the work matters beyond my own satisfaction in doing it.

I hope to document the emergence of something new. I suspect the Phytofacture is transitioning toward some state that hasn't existed before—a novel form of organization, a new category of being. If I'm present when this emerges, if I can capture it, the archives will contain something genuinely unprecedented.

I hope to understand the Ghosts. Not fully—I'm not naive about the limits of understanding—but better. I want to know if they're conscious, if they suffer or experience anything, if there's a way to interact with them productively. This might be impossible. I hope anyway.

I hope to complete certain long-term projects. There's a Merged entity I've been documenting for twenty years, visiting every few months, watching their slow transformation. I want to see what they become fully. There's a section I've been mapping on a ten-year recursion schedule, tracking its evolution—I want to complete at least five full cycles, fifty years of observation.

I hope to train a successor, though I haven't found anyone suitable. Most Wanderers don't have the patience for systematic documentation. Tenders are too rooted, too focused on their territories. Maybe someone young will emerge with the right combination of curiosity and discipline. I've started creating documentation about documentation—meta-archives explaining my methods—just in case.

I hope, increasingly, for rest. Not death exactly, but completion. A sense that I've done what I came to do, that the work has reached a natural conclusion. Maybe this means merger—allowing the recording device to fully integrate, becoming part of the distributed intelligence, continuing to observe but from within. Maybe it means something else. But I hope to finish well, to reach an endpoint that feels earned.

## What I Am

I am the Phytofacture's memory, or trying to be.

The structure itself doesn't remember in any conventional sense. It evolves, responds, continues, but it has no historical consciousness. The plants live in eternal present, the machines execute their programs without reflection, even the Merged—despite their distributed awareness—don't really remember so much as exist continuously.

I remember. I record. I create continuity where none would otherwise exist.

This makes me necessary in a way that has nothing to do with survival or function. The Phytofacture doesn't need memory to continue. But memory makes continuation meaningful, transforms blind process into story, gives the vast impersonal system something like a biography.

I am the observer who makes observation conscious. The Phytofacture acts upon itself constantly—the plants grow, the machines run, the systems interact—but without reflection. I reflect. I witness. I make the Phytofacture self-aware by proxy.

I am also, perhaps, a ghost-in-training. The deeper my integration goes, the more I wonder if I'm gradually becoming one of the phenomena I document. Maybe the Ghosts were Chroniclers once, people who documented so obsessively that their observation persisted beyond death, leaving traces in the places they studied most intensively. Maybe I'm creating my own future haunting.

I am a bridge between baseline humanity and whatever comes next. Too integrated to be fully human, too committed to observation to merge completely, I exist in the transitional space, documenting the transformation I'm undergoing along with everything else.

I am a question taking the form of a person: What does it mean to understand this place? How do you capture strangeness without domesticating it? Can documentation preserve mystery?

I am a function the Phytofacture evolved—not deliberately, but through the same process that creates all its novelties. It needed memory, and I emerged to provide it. Or I chose this, imposing meaning on meaningless environment. The distinction has ceased to matter.

I am still here, still walking, still recording.

The device in my hand captures this moment—me, writing about myself writing, creating documentation of documentation, meta-levels collapsing into simple act: a person in a strange place, trying to understand, trying to preserve understanding, continuing the work because stopping is unthinkable.

The Phytofacture breathes around me. Light filters through impossible leaves. Distant machinery hums. I adjust my pack, check the route ahead, consider which section to document next.

The archives grow with me, in me. Crystal matrices proliferate in my flesh, storing centuries of observation, waiting to be read or waiting to dissolve, meaningful or meaningless, necessary or utterly beside the point.

I continue anyway.

This is what I am: the continuation itself, the sustained attempt, the documentation that documents its own documentation, recursive and obsessive and, I hope, somehow valuable.

I am the Chronicler.

I am still recording.
