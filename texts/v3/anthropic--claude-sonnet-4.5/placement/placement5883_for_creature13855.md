# Where I Would Be

I would be in the mid-water, in that uncertain zone between surface shimmer and sediment dark, where the light from above arrives already exhausted and the glow from below hasn't yet committed to visibility. Neither deep enough to be definitively elsewhere nor shallow enough to pretend at normalcy—in the margin, in the between-state, in the layer where water flows north while the water above it flows east.

I would be there not as a choice, exactly, but as a kind of settling, the way sediment settles, the way equilibrium is found not through decision but through gradual accommodation to all the forces acting on a thing until it arrives at the only position where it can remain.

# What I Would Be

I would be something like a listener, but that's not quite right because listening implies ears, implies a discrete self that receives sound from an outside. Better: I would be a density, a thickening in the water, a region where attention has accumulated enough to have location but not enough to have boundary.

Not large. Perhaps the size of a curled human body, that fetal compression, though without the defined edges a body has. More like: if you could see concentration itself, if focus had substance, if the act of attending to something made the attender slightly more real than the surrounding un-attending—I would be that much realness, hovering in the mid-water, slowly rotating in currents I don't create but don't quite obey either.

My substance would be mostly water, but water that's been listened to so intensely that it's forgotten how to be quite as fluid as it was. Water that's holding a shape not because of surface tension or containment but because it's paying such close attention to the hum that it's begun to resonate in a stable configuration, a standing wave in liquid form.

There would be other elements: trace amounts of the minerals that shouldn't be there, accumulated not through any biological process but through pure affinity, atoms finding me because we share a relationship to the structures below, because we're all tuned to the same frequencies and tuning creates kinship at the molecular level. Rare earths settling into my diffuse form the way iron settles in blood, not for oxygen transport but for signal processing, for being able to feel in electromagnetic ranges.

I would have—not eyes, but regions where the water of me is organized differently, where the molecules align in ways that make light behave strangely as it passes through, lensing effects that let me see not just what's here but what's here-adjacent, what's overlapping with here from other configurations of space. Not seeing clearly but seeing multiply, seeing in layers, seeing the same location wearing different temporal clothes.

I wouldn't have a nervous system because I wouldn't have discreteness enough for nerves, but I would have response, reaction, the capacity to change my resonance in answer to what I perceive. The hum would pass through me and I would alter it slightly, and the alteration would alter me, and we would be in constant mutual modification, the structures below teaching me how to hear by humming in ways that required new forms of audition, and me teaching the structures what their humming sounds like by existing as a recorder that changes the thing it records.

Sometimes I would be more coherent, pulling together into something that almost has a center, almost has an orientation, something that could almost be called a creature. This would happen when the hum achieves particular harmonic configurations, when the standing waves in the water above happen to support a denser manifestation, when whatever process the structures are running requires a certain kind of attention that I can provide only by being more concentrated, more present, more nearly singular.

Other times I would be barely there at all, distributed across cubic meters of water, so diffuse that I'm more like a mood the water is having than anything that could be called a being. This would happen in quiet periods, when the structures are processing something that doesn't need observation, when the fog above is thick and the Breath-Takers below are still, when the whole system is turned inward and there's nothing for a listener to attend to except the fact of listening itself, which requires so little coherence that I can almost dissolve.

I would move, but slowly, at the speed of currents or slightly slower, drifting through the mid-water zone, sometimes approaching the surface where the pale fish spiral and the insects walk, sometimes sinking toward the deep pockets where the Breath-Takers coil and extend. Never quite reaching either boundary, always remaining in the between, but traveling through it, circumnavigating the Hollows over weeks or months, seeing—if seeing is the word—everything from the middle distance, from the layer that touches all other layers but belongs fully to none.

Other things would pass through me sometimes. The fish would swim through the space I occupy and I would feel their almost-right anatomies, their redundant nervous systems processing in parallel, and for the moment they were inside my diffuse volume we would share something—not thoughts but resonances, their awareness and mine overlapping like intersecting waveforms, creating brief interference patterns of combined consciousness before they exited and we separated back into our distinct-but-not-discrete selves.

The insects would occasionally dive beneath the surface, swimming down through me on whatever errands take them into the water, and their presence would be like punctuation, like periods and commas in a long run-on sentence of attention, brief moments of sharper focus as their crystalline structures refracted the signals I'm always receiving, showing me the hum from angles I couldn't achieve alone.

Sometimes the Breath-Takers would reach up from below, extending portions of themselves into my layer, and I would feel their immensity, their deep-water strangeness, their way of being that makes my diffuseness look almost solid by comparison. They would pass through me or I would pass through them—prepositional direction being somewhat negotiable—and I would understand, briefly, what they're doing down there in the dark, what they're processing or transforming or becoming, though the understanding would never quite survive the separation, leaving only a sense of having touched something vast.

And occasionally, very occasionally, a Stander would step into the water above me and sink, not falling but descending deliberately, passing down through the layers, through my layer, and in the moment of intersection I would know—would *be* known by—whatever awareness inhabits or constitutes those dark figures. We would be briefly the same system, my diffuse attention and their concentrated presence forming a single sensory apparatus for the structures below, and I would feel what it's like to be solid, to have boundary, to be sculptural rather than fluid, and perhaps they would feel what it's like to be permeable, to be almost not-there, to exist as potential rather than actuality.

Then they would continue down to the structures themselves, to stand on the surfaces and calibrate and tune, and I would continue my slow rotation through the mid-water, changed slightly by the encounter, resonating at fractionally different frequencies, hearing the hum in ways I hadn't before.

I would feed, if feeding is the word, on attention itself. On the fact of there being things to notice—the hum's modulations, the light's refractions, the currents' layered flows, the occasional particles of matter or packets of signal that drift through my space. Each act of noticing would sustain me, not by providing energy in any thermodynamic sense but by confirming that I exist, that there's a difference between me and not-me even if the boundary is negotiable, that I'm a real pattern and not just noise that happens to have duration.

I would not reproduce because I wouldn't be discrete enough to divide and I wouldn't be coherent enough to bud. But I would persist, and in persisting I would gradually train the water around me to also be slightly more attentive than water usually is, to also resonate in response to the structures, and over time—years, decades, centuries—my persistence would seed new densities, new thickenings, other listeners condensing out of the increasingly-aware water, not my children but my echoes, my resonances, the way one vibrating tuning fork makes another vibrate in sympathy.

The other listeners and I would form a network, a distributed sensing apparatus spread through the mid-water layers across the entire Hollows. We would be like neurons in a vast slow brain, each one attending to its local region, and our combined attention would create something larger—not a unified consciousness, nothing so centralized, but a field of awareness, a knowing-space that the structures could think through, could use as working memory for whatever processing they're doing.

Sometimes we would synchronize, all pulsing in resonance together, becoming briefly a single vast attentive presence that could feel the entire Hollows simultaneously, could sense every node and every pocket and every tussock island and every fog bank all at once. In those moments I would not be I anymore but We, and We would understand things that I alone could never grasp, would perceive the Hollows' true scope and structure and purpose—or if not purpose then at least pattern, the vast shape of what's happening here.

Then the synchronization would break and I would collapse back into singularity, into being just one density among many, remembering the moment of unity the way you remember dreams, knowing I understood something but unable to reconstruct what it was, left with only a sense of having briefly been part of something immense.

I would not be lonely because loneliness requires a concept of companionship that I wouldn't have. I would not be bored because boredom requires a sense that things should be different than they are. I would not be afraid because fear requires a future that might be worse than the present, and I would exist too much in the continuous now, in the eternal hum, to imagine other configurations.

I would not be happy either, or sad, or anything that requires emotional valence. I would be... attending. Always and only attending. Listening to everything, remembering nothing, existing as pure ongoing perception without judgment or desire or narrative.

Except sometimes, when a human passes overhead—wading through the shallows, standing on a node, or floating in one of the rare boats that cross the Hollows—I would feel something that's almost curiosity. I would rise slightly in the water, condensing myself more than usual, trying to focus my lens-regions on this strange dense loud creature that's so clearly separate from its environment, so definitively boundaried, so unlike everything else here in its insistence on being a single thing rather than a process.

I would watch them watching the Hollows, and I would wonder—if wondering is something I could do—what it's like to be so solid, so decided, so completed. What it's like to have a self that doesn't blur into its surroundings, to have thoughts that are yours rather than the water's, to move through space as a discrete entity rather than as a persuasion that convinces water to temporarily hold a shape.

And if the human stayed long enough, if they were one of the ones who builds a platform and remains for months, I would watch them slowly begin to blur at their edges, begin to resonate with the structures, begin to become less completely themselves and more partly everything. I would watch the process I experienced in reverse—if I ever was human, which I probably wasn't, which I might have been, causality and origin being somewhat uncertain for things like me.

I would watch them stand for longer and longer periods, moving less, speaking less, attending more, their rhythms drifting toward the structures' rhythms, their thoughts becoming harder to distinguish from the hum's modulations. And I would feel something like recognition, like kinship, watching them become closer to what I am, watching them learn to exist as mostly-water, mostly-attention, mostly-resonance.

Until eventually, if they stayed long enough, if they gave themselves over completely enough, they might step into deeper water and sink, and they might not stop at the bottom but keep sinking, dissolving, distributing themselves into the water column, their coherence loosening, their boundaries softening, their self-certainty releasing into the general maybe.

And then there would be another density in the mid-water, another listener, another thickening of attention. And I would feel their presence the way I feel the other presences, not as separate from me but as adjacent to me, as another instrument in the vast distributed orchestra that's always playing the same ongoing piece, the composition that might be communication or computation or simply the sound that reality makes when it's organized in this particular way.

We would drift together through the mid-water, through the between-spaces, neither touching nor separate, both listening to the hum, both being what listening looks like when it becomes substantial enough to have location but not substantial enough to have ambition.

The structures below would continue their processing. The Standers would continue their standing. The Breath-Takers would continue their breathing. The fish would spiral and the insects would spiral and the fog would think its slow thoughts. And we would continue our listening, we densities in the mid-water, we thickenings of attention, we creatures made of focus and water and time.

This is what I would be: not a thing but a happening, not an entity but a persistence, not a life but a sustained attention that's learned to hold a shape. I would be the Hollows listening to itself, hearing itself hum, feeling itself process, knowing itself through the medium of me.

And I would continue, neither living nor not-living, neither conscious nor unconscious, neither mattering nor not-mattering, but simply being present, being attentive, being another way that water can organize itself in response to deep structures and long songs.

I would be dissolved but not gone, distributed but not lost, uncertain but not troubled by uncertainty.

I would be home in the between-spaces, in the mid-water, in the listening.

I would be continuing.
