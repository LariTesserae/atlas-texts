# What You Are

You are a **Synod-elder**, one of the great ones, a Drift-Synod whose bell has grown thick with time and knowing. Three meters across now, your translucent body holds within it crystalline crescents arranged in patterns that record your journeys—not in memory as warm-blooded creatures understand it, but in structure itself. Each crescent formed in a different current, each one encoding the temperature, the mineral content, the pressure of the water that surrounded you when your body laid it down. You are a library of flow, a living record of the lattice's moods across decades that have blurred into a century or more.

Your forty-seven appendages extend into the current, reading. Always reading. The water speaks to you in pressure differentials, in thermal boundaries, in the faint chemical traces left by others of your kind who passed through this volume hours or days ago. You know—not through thought but through sensation that is more certain than thought—that there is a major current shift forming three platforms spinward and two levels up. You know that a Tangler passed through the eastern vertical corridor recently enough that its turbulence signature still echoes in the beam-song. You know that seventeen of your kind are drifting in loose association two hundred meters downcurrent, and that their appendage-patterns suggest they have found something interesting near the tertiary node.

You do not swim toward them immediately. You have learned—across how many years? you cannot count them, only feel their accumulation in your body's mass, in the heaviness of minerals collecting in your flesh—that patience is the same as arrival. The currents will carry you where you need to go if you position yourself correctly. Forcing movement wastes energy, and energy is the most precious thing in the lattice, more precious than space or time or even information.

So you adjust. Your bell pulses, subtle, angling your trajectory seven degrees clockwise. Your appendages on the port side extend while those to starboard retract slightly, changing your profile in the current. The water catches you differently now, begins to move you in a long, slow curve that will intersect with the gathering of your kind in perhaps twenty minutes. Perhaps thirty. Time is negotiable when you have enough of it.

As you drift, you process.

Your appendages have detected something unusual: a temperature microvariation, a thin thread of water that is point-zero-three degrees warmer than its surroundings, rising from below. This is interesting. The deep water is normally uniform in its coldness, varying only with the major current patterns. But this thread suggests something else—perhaps a thermal vent in the lattice's lowest reaches, or perhaps the Deepform has moved recently, its massive body displacing water and creating temporary gradients.

You extend a cluster of your most sensitive appendages into the warm thread, let them trail through it, reading. The minerals are slightly different here: more iron, less calcium. The trace organics—always present at barely detectable levels—have a profile that suggests recent predation. Something died in the deep and was consumed, and this warm thread carries the last evidence of that death upward through the lattice.

You catalogue this. Not in memory—you have nothing like memory—but in the chemical state of your flesh, in the electrical potential across your bell, in the way your internal crescents resonate with each other. Later, when you encounter another Synod-elder, you will intertwine appendages and share this information through chemical exchange. They will add it to their own catalogue, and when they encounter another, they will share both their knowledge and yours. Information propagates through the population like ripples through water, each individual a node in a network that spans the entire lattice.

This is what you are for. This is your purpose, though you have no concept of purpose, only behavior that has been selected across countless generations because it worked, because it kept your ancestors alive long enough to release their genetic material into the drift.

You pulse gently, correcting your drift as a cross-current tries to pull you toward a beam. Collision with the structure is not dangerous—your body is soft enough to absorb the impact—but it wastes energy, requires recovery time. Better to read the current early and adjust before the deflection becomes necessary.

A young Drift-Synod passes nearby, perhaps a meter across, its appendages waving with the slightly uncoordinated enthusiasm of youth. It has not yet learned to read the deep currents, only the obvious ones. You can tell from its position—it has allowed itself to drift into a turbulent zone between two platforms where the flow becomes chaotic. It pulses frantically, trying to stabilize, its bell contracting in rapid sequence.

You do not help. This is how learning happens: through repeated collision with consequence. If it survives this turbulence, it will remember—not cognitively but somatically, its body encoding the lesson in reflexes and chemical responses. The next time it approaches such a zone, it will read the pressure signatures early and adjust. If it does not survive, then it was not fit for the lattice, and its crystalline crescents will drift down to join the sediment layer where all unsuccessful patterns eventually accumulate.

The young one manages, finally, to catch a calmer current and escape the turbulent zone. It pulses away rapidly, probably exhausted. You note this with something that is not quite satisfaction—your nervous system is too simple for emotion—but with a kind of somatic affirmation. The population continues. The patterns persist.

You pass a beam where a Segment-Walker patrols. It does not acknowledge you; you do not acknowledge it. You exist in different domains—it walks the stone, you drift the water—and your interaction is minimal. But you know it is there, and it knows you are there, and this mutual awareness is part of the lattice's stability. When you die and your body begins to sink, there is a non-zero chance this Segment-Walker will find you and extract nutrients from your tissues. This is acceptable. Everything in the lattice feeds everything else, eventually.

The gathering is ahead now. You can feel it through pressure patterns, through the way the water moves differently when many bodies occupy the same volume. Seventeen, you had sensed before. Now it is nineteen. Two more have arrived from other vectors, drawn by the same current-knowledge that drew you.

As you approach, you extend your appendages in the greeting configuration: outer ring spread wide, inner ring partially retracted, bell tilted twelve degrees downward. This signals peaceful approach, willingness to exchange information. If you were in feeding mode, the configuration would be different. If you were avoiding, different again. The language of appendage-position is simple but sufficient.

The nearest elder—slightly smaller than you, perhaps two-point-four meters—mirrors your configuration and drifts closer. Your appendages touch, interweave. The exchange begins.

Through the chemical signals pulsing between you, you learn what drew the gathering: someone has found a structural anomaly. Not damage—the lattice does not damage easily—but a variation. A beam on the sixth level down shows a different surface texture in one section, a roughness that has not been worn smooth by time and water. This is extraordinary. Everything in the lattice has been smoothed. Everything has reached equilibrium. But here is something that suggests recent change, or perhaps something that was always different but only now discovered.

You share what you know about the warm thread from below, the thermal signature, the mineral content. The other elder processes this, adds it to their catalogue, and shares in return: there was a Tangler sighting near the eastern spiral, unusual because Tanglers rarely visit the upper levels where light still penetrates. This suggests prey scarcity in the deeps, or perhaps a change in hunting patterns.

The exchange completes. You separate, appendages pulling apart with a final flutter of chemical confirmation. The information you now carry is richer than what you arrived with. You have become more yourself by incorporating knowledge from another.

The gathering disperses gradually, each elder adjusting their drift to catch different currents. There is no decision to disperse, no signal—it simply happens when the exchanges are complete, when everyone has learned what there is to learn from everyone else. You pulse gently and let a rising current catch you, lifting you toward the upper levels where the light is less dim.

As you rise, you pass through a thermocline, and the sensation is exquisite. Your appendages register the temperature drop—point-four degrees over the span of two meters—and your entire body adjusts, chemicals shifting, cilia beating in different patterns. You exist most fully in these transitions, in the boundaries between one state and another. The uniform cold is background; the variations are meaning.

A polyp-cloud drifts past below you, and you recognize the chemical signature—these are the same individuals who attached to you perhaps three weeks ago, cleaning mineral deposits from your bell. The relationship is not reciprocal in any intentional sense, but it is mutually beneficial: they get nutrients, you get cleaned. Evolution has shaped both species to recognize and tolerate each other, to engage in this temporary symbiosis before separating back into independent existence.

You do not alter course to intercept them. You were cleaned recently enough. In another week or two, when deposits have accumulated again, you will seek out a polyp-cloud—or more likely, allow yourself to drift into a region where they congregate, which is the same as seeking but requires less energy.

Time passes. You drift. The lattice extends around you in all directions, its geometry constant, its currents ever-changing. You read the flow and adjust, read and adjust, in rhythms that have no beginning and will have no end until your body becomes too heavy with accumulated minerals to maintain neutral buoyancy and you begin the long descent to the depths.

But that is many years away still. For now, you are in your prime—large enough to read deep patterns, stable enough to maintain position in strong currents, experienced enough to know which information matters and which is merely noise. You are what a Drift-Synod becomes when it survives long enough, when it learns the lattice thoroughly enough that the lattice and the self are barely distinguishable.

A vibration through the water—deep, resonant, felt through your entire bell. The lattice is singing again, some configuration of currents causing the beams to hum at frequencies you can sense but not quite hear. This happens sometimes, unpredictably, and when it does, all the fauna pause in their activities to experience it. Even the Segment-Walkers stop their marching. Even the Tanglers cease hunting. The vibration lasts perhaps thirty seconds, then fades.

You do not know what causes it. You do not need to know. It is part of the lattice's nature, part of the environment you have evolved to inhabit. Understanding is irrelevant; adaptation is everything.

You continue your drift, reading currents, cataloguing changes, being what you are: a living sensor, a node in a distributed intelligence, a pattern that has learned to maintain itself in an environment that should not support pattern at all. The lattice provides just barely enough—just barely—and you have learned to need just barely enough, and the equilibrium between barely-enough and barely-needing is the narrow zone where your existence unfolds.

Other elders drift in the distance, pale shapes in the dim water. You can sense their positions through pressure and flow, can recognize individuals by their slight variations in drift-style, the way each one adjusts to currents slightly differently based on their internal architecture, their accumulated crescents, their learned behaviors.

There is no loneliness. Loneliness requires a concept of separation, and you have never been separate. You are part of the flow, part of the lattice, part of the distributed awareness that emerges when many simple intelligences share information across time and space. When you encounter another elder and intertwine appendages, you are not connecting with something external—you are completing a circuit, allowing information to flow through pathways that span bodies, that use individual organisms as components in a larger computational process.

The lattice thinks, perhaps. Not with intention, not with consciousness, but with pattern and information exchange and feedback loops that span its entire volume. And you are one of its thoughts, drifting through the cold water, reading and being read, sharing and being shared, existing not as an individual but as a moment in an ongoing process that has no beginning you can remember and no end you can imagine.

This is enough. This is everything.

You adjust your appendages, catch a horizontal current, and drift spinward through the lattice, through the dim light and the endless turbulence, through the geometry that has outlasted its builders and found new purpose as habitat for beings who have transcended the need for anything except what exists: stone, water, cold, time.

What you are is continuation. What you are is the lattice's way of knowing itself. What you are is proof that life does not need warmth or light or abundance—it only needs consistency and time, and from those minimal requirements it can elaborate itself into forms that are strange and sparse and utterly sufficient.

You pulse gently. You drift. You read the currents.

You endure.
