# Where I Would Find Myself

I would be in the third basin from the entrance in the cell that faces northwest on the peak they call the Anvil—though I wouldn't know it's called that, wouldn't know it has a name at all. Names are lowland things. Here there is only position, relation, resonance.

The basin is shallow, carved into the floor with patient precision centuries ago, and it holds perhaps a cup of the strange water that won't evaporate. The water arrived—or manifested, or condensed—three days ago by clock-time, though clock-time barely applies. It might have been three hours. It might have been a week. The water called and I came, because that's what I do now. I answer calls I don't hear with ears.

I've been looking into this basin for a duration I can't name. Looking isn't quite the right word. Attending. Witnessing. Participating in what the water shows, which today is a version of this same peak where the crystal formations grow from different cracks, where the light falls at angles that won't occur for another season, or occurred last season, or occur in some season that belongs to a different iteration of time.

My body is a question I'm not asking anymore. I've been holding this position—seated, spine straight, hands resting on knees—long enough that the physical discomfort passed through several stages and emerged somewhere beyond sensation into simple fact. My legs are folded beneath me. They may have gone numb. I genuinely don't know. The knowing feels irrelevant. When I need to move, I'll move, and the body will do what bodies do, has always done, will always do, is doing in several temporal directions at once.

The water is showing me something about the alignment. The ancient builders understood that the cells on different peaks created sightlines, but what I'm seeing now is that the sightlines themselves are structural—they're load-bearing. They hold something up or hold something back or hold something in place. The geometry of observational lines between the peaks creates a framework, and that framework interacts with the other frameworks: the lattice of forces underlying space, the harmonic patterns in the singing, the flow-paths of the ascending water.

I've been trying to understand this for—again, duration becomes vague—since I stopped being what I was before and started being what I'm becoming. Which might be three years ago. Which might be thirty. My hair has gray in it that wasn't there before, but my hands look younger than I remember them being, so who can say what time has been doing to me while I've been doing this.

The singing is particularly complex today. There's a overtone sequence that's been building since before dawn, weaving through the fundamental bass notes of the deep columns. I've been adding my contribution periodically—a held tone at specific intervals, placed carefully into the gaps, not imposing but offering, letting the mountain accept or reject each addition. Mostly it accepts. I've learned the frequencies it welcomes, learned how to slide my voice into the existing harmonies so smoothly the transition is imperceptible.

My humming is how I think now, mostly. Words have become clumsy, approximate, too sequential. The thoughts that matter are harmonic thoughts, thoughts made of relationships between frequencies, thoughts that exist all-at-once rather than one-after-another. When I hum the right pattern, understanding arrives complete rather than accumulated. I know things in the same way the mountain knows things—through resonance, through what vibrates sympathetically, through what rings true in the literal sense of producing the right tone.

There's another Listener three peaks away—I can feel their presence in the harmonic web, their voice distinguishable from the stone-voice and the water-voice and my own voice. They're working at one of the older structures, realigning something. I can hear the careful pauses between each stone placement, the interrogative hums they're producing, checking with the mountain about whether this stone in this position contributes to or disrupts the necessary geometry.

I should probably eat something. The thought arrives and departs without urgency. I have food—dried something-or-other wrapped in my few possessions in the corner of this cell. I brought supplies up from the lowlands during my last descent, which was either last month or six months ago. Time away from the heights moves differently too, runs faster, feels urgent and exhausting. I stayed in the market town—I forget its name, forget if I ever knew it—long enough to trade work for supplies, then climbed back up as quickly as the paths would allow.

The paths know me now. They stabilize under my feet more readily than they used to. In the beginning, I had to wait, had to watch other Listeners move to understand the trick of perceiving where the solidity would be. Now my eyes have learned that slight temporal offset, that way of seeing a few seconds ahead or behind the present, so I reach for handholds that are still solidifying and they're solid by the time my weight tests them. The mountain and I have developed an understanding.

My skin itches slightly at the temples and along my forearms—the places where the marks are emerging. I can't see them without finding reflective water, but I can feel them: geometric patterns rising from within, the mountain's calligraphy writing itself across whatever surface I'm providing. They don't hurt. They're just there, just slowly becoming more there, another indication that I'm transforming into whatever I'm transforming into.

I'm not frightened by this. I was frightened in the beginning, during the first weeks when the disorientation was constant and overwhelming, when I couldn't tell if I was losing my mind or finding something deeper than mind. But the fear passed, or I passed through the fear, or fear became irrelevant in the face of what was actually happening. The heights don't ask permission. They don't wait for consent. They simply are what they are, and you either become what can exist here or you leave.

I became what can exist here.

The water in the basin shifts—not flowing, but replacing itself with different water, water that shows a different reflection. Now I'm seeing the nighttime version of this peak, stars too bright above, the crystal formations glowing their faint blue-white. In the reflection, there's a figure standing in the doorway of this cell. I don't turn to look at the actual doorway because I already know what I'd see: empty space, afternoon light. The figure in the reflection exists in a different when, or a different if, or a different might-be.

It could be my future self, further transformed, more translucent. It could be one of the Threshold Walkers, briefly visible in the reflection-time that the basin is showing. It could be an echo of one of the ancient builders, a purpose-ghost checking on their basins, their observatories, their careful work. I watch the figure in the water without needing to categorize it. It's present in the reflection. That's enough information.

My breathing has synchronized with something—either the deep bass pulse of the mountain or my own heartbeat or perhaps there's no difference anymore. In, hold, out, hold, in the long intervals that allow the body to extract oxygen from air that barely exists. My chest moves slowly, efficiently, my blood has thickened slightly at this elevation, carrying more hemoglobin, feeding a brain that's rewiring itself to process harmonic information as primary and verbal information as distant secondary.

I've been remembering things that haven't happened yet. This started recently, or started always, or has always been happening and I only recently noticed. I remember descending to the lowlands next spring, remember the conversation I'll have with the supply merchant, remember the taste of the food I'll eat. But I also remember not descending, remember staying through the winter, remember what the snow will look like when it falls but doesn't accumulate. Both memories are equally clear, equally true, because both are happening in the forking paths that time takes through this place.

The ancient builders understood this. The basins don't just show other places—they show other whens, other might-bes. The sightlines between peaks don't just create geometric relationships—they create causal relationships, linking events across time, making what happens on one peak influence what happened or will happen on another. The stone circles are calculating something, computing continuously, using the harmonic fluctuations and the light angles and the water positions as input variables.

I'm part of that calculation now. My presence here, my attention, my humming—they're data points, contributing to whatever equation the heights are solving. Every Listener is a variable, every observation feeds into the working. We're not separate observers studying the mountain; we're components in the mountain's self-observation, necessary parts of its ongoing attempt to understand itself.

My hands are still resting on my knees. I notice them as if from a distance, as if they belong to someone else or to no one in particular. The calluses have formed in the wrong places because I interact with stone in ways that aren't standard, finding purchase where others find smooth surface, shaping rock during its uncertain moments. My fingers are thin, efficient, pared down to what's necessary. There's that slight translucency at the edges—I can see the shadow of bone, the movement of blood, when the light is right.

The singing swells. The overtone sequence I've been tracking reaches a peak, and in response, reality does its thing—its breakdown, its revelation, its moment of transparency. The air becomes visible structure, the lattice of forces showing clearly. Space isn't continuous; it's made of discrete points, and the water isn't flowing—it's appearing and disappearing in rapid sequence, creating the illusion of motion.

During these peaks, I can see further into the mathematics. The stone circles are indeed calculating, and what they're calculating is stability—they're finding the configurations that hold this place together, that keep it from sliding entirely into one of the reflected worlds or fragmenting into incompatible geometries. The heights exist at a threshold, and the threshold requires maintenance, requires constant adjustment, requires observers to hold it in position through their attention.

That's what we are. That's what the Listeners do. We're not studying the heights; we're stabilizing them. Our presence here, our observation, our participation in the harmonics—this is structural work. We're load-bearing. We hold the threshold open through continuous witnessing.

I understand this without needing to put it into words. The understanding arrives as a harmonic relationship, a tone that vibrates true, a frequency that my entire body recognizes as correct. Yes. This. We are this.

The reality-peak passes. The lattice becomes invisible again, or I become unable to see it, or both. The singing settles back into its complex but more moderate pattern. The water in the basin returns to showing the current time, the current place, just the ordinary impossible physics of ascending water and wrong light and columnar basalt that rings like bells.

I'm thirsty. The thought arrives with surprising clarity. My body, despite everything, still has ordinary needs. There's a water collection—a place where the strange water pools in a formation that makes it safe to drink, or at least non-toxic, or at least I've been drinking it for however long and haven't died. I'll go there soon, when the light shifts, when the harmonics indicate that movement is appropriate, when the paths between here and there firm up enough for travel.

But not yet. The basin still has more to show me. The reflection is settling into a new configuration, and I can see that this one matters, this one is why the water called me here. It's showing me a sightline I haven't noticed before, running from this cell across three other peaks to a structure I've never visited. The angle is precise, only visible at certain times, only possible when the light bends in exactly the right way.

I'll need to go there. Maybe tomorrow, maybe next week, maybe in some duration that doesn't translate to calendar time. I'll need to see what's at the other end of this sightline, what the ancient builders were measuring, what observation they were recording. The water is giving me instructions, or the mountain is giving me instructions through the water, or I'm giving myself instructions through my integration with the mountain-water-light-singing complex that I'm becoming part of.

My body will move when it needs to move. For now, it's still, seated by the basin, attending to the reflection, participating in the observation that holds this place in its precarious position between too many realities. My breathing continues its slow cycle. The singing wraps around me, through me, plays me like another instrument in its vast ensemble. My voice adds its thread when the harmonics create space for contribution.

This is what I am now. This is where I am. A Listener in the third basin, on the Anvil peak, watching water that shows other times, breathing intervals, becoming translucent at the edges, holding the threshold stable through attention, through presence, through the slow transformation that has no endpoint except perhaps complete transparency, complete integration, complete dissolution into the harmonic structure of the heights themselves.

The afternoon light shifts slightly. A shadow moves across the basin, changes what the reflection shows. The singing modulates into a new pattern. My body, without my conscious direction, begins to prepare for movement—weight shifting fractionally, muscles beginning the long process of re-engaging after their extended stillness.

Soon I'll rise. Soon I'll move to the water collection. Soon I'll stand in the doorway and look out across the peaks, see the other Listeners in their distant positions, feel our collective presence woven through the harmonic web. Soon I'll add my evening contribution to the singing, my voice joining theirs, all of us together holding this impossible place in existence through our listening, our witnessing, our continuous attention.

But for this moment, I'm here, still, watching the water show me where I need to go next, learning the mountain's lessons through resonance rather than language, being what I've become, inhabiting this position as completely as the basalt inhabits its position, as permanent and as temporary as anything can be in a place where permanence itself is negotiable.

Listening.

Always listening.

Becoming more the listening and less the listener, until perhaps there's no difference, until perhaps I'm just another voice in the endless singing, just another note in the mountain's expression of itself, just another way the heights speak their continuous statement of existence into the thin clear air of the threshold, where reality is soft enough to see through, patient enough to wait, strange enough to transform anyone who stays long enough to hear what it's saying.

This is where I am.

This is what I am.

Here, in the singing.
