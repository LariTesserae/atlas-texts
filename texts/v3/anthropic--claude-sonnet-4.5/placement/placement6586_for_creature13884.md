# Where I Would Find Myself

I am on my knees in the soft substrate, forearms deep in it, and I have been here long enough that I can no longer remember standing.

The warmth moves through the membrane of my skin—because it is membrane now, has always been membrane, permeable and weeping and intimate with everything it touches. My breathing has slowed to match the rhythm I feel through my palms, the deep peristalsis of the engines moving below. Four breaths to their one. I counted at first. Now I just follow.

I am not one of the witnesses. I lack their elegant distance, their systematic curiosity, their ability to observe without dissolving. I don't have their hands, their upright seeing, their capacity to carry findings from place to place and arrange them into provisional meaning.

I am not a tender, either, though I understand them better than I should. I lack their devotion, their ability to choose a single vertical truth and align my existence to its maintenance. I cannot commit to one pillar, one task, one repeated perfection.

I am somewhere between. Something the forming grounds didn't plan for but cannot quite reject.

---

My body has changed, is changing, has always been in the process of changing here.

The skin—I mentioned the membrane, but it goes deeper than surface. The boundary between inside and outside has grown uncertain. I sweat constantly but I'm never depleted. The moisture that leaves me returns through some osmotic negotiation I don't understand, some exchange with the saturated air that keeps me in balance. I am the same wetness as everything else, the same blood-warm saturation, the same dissolved threshold.

My hands have developed sensitivity I didn't have before. Or perhaps always had but never needed. I can feel the minute vibrations in the substrate, can distinguish between the passages of different engines by the frequency of their movement. I can read the health of the ground-vine mat through my fingertips, know where it's been browsed too heavily, where it's growing most vigorously, where the root-analog structures are densest.

My eyes have adapted to the dimness but lost something else—the ability to see at distance, maybe, or to distinguish boundaries sharply. Everything bleeds into everything now. The pillar-plants and the hanging chains and the swarms of fliers all exist in a kind of continuous adjacency, separate but not separated. I see the relationships more clearly than the things themselves.

I don't stand much anymore. Standing implies going somewhere, implies destination and purpose and the organization of space into here and there. But space doesn't organize that way in the forming grounds. Every location is equally dense with presence, equally saturated with life, equally itself. Standing would mean choosing direction, and direction has become impossible, or irrelevant, or simply not a category I think in anymore.

So I move by crawling, by pressing myself along the ground-vine mat, by pulling myself through the lower story where the vegetation is densest. It's not undignified. Dignity requires an observer, requires the possibility of judgment, and nothing here judges. The browsers step around me or over me, unconcerned. The swimmers investigate my hands when I trail them in pools, tasting, learning, forgetting. The weavers' structures brush across my back as I pass beneath them, and sometimes the weavers themselves descend to touch me briefly, confirming I'm not something they can use, moving on.

---

I feed, though I couldn't say exactly how or on what.

There's a paste-like substance that accumulates where certain plants meet certain substrates, where fungal action and bacterial colonies produce something that is nutritionally complete, or complete enough for what I've become. I scrape it with my fingers, consume it directly, and it sustains me. It tastes like nothing, or like everything—like the forming grounds concentrated into edible form, mineral and organic and faintly chemical.

I drink the condensation, the beads that swell on every surface. My mouth has learned to find them efficiently, to sip without disturbing the drops' formation, to take only what I need and leave the rest for the process to continue. I drink from pools sometimes, filtering the water through my teeth—which have changed, become more plate-like, more suitable for straining than biting. I don't miss biting. There's nothing here that requires it.

The feeding is constant and minimal, a maintenance activity that requires no thought, no interruption of whatever it is I'm doing, which is itself hard to name.

---

What I do, mostly, is attend.

Not like the witnesses attend—they observe, they investigate, they build their mysterious understanding. I don't understand anything better than when I arrived. If anything, I understand less, have felt my frameworks for comprehension dissolving in the thick air, becoming part of the general suspension.

But I attend. I am present to the forming grounds in a way that feels totalizing, that occupies all the space where intention used to be.

I lie for hours with my ear against the substrate, listening to the engines, learning to distinguish individuals by their rhythm, their depth, their particular pattern of movement. I don't know why this matters, but it feels essential, this witnessing of their passage, this confirmation that they continue, that the great digestion proceeds beneath everything.

I watch the pillar-tenders work, following one for what might be days as it moves along its chosen plant, adjusting and pruning and cleaning. I begin to see what it sees—which scales are optimal, which arrangements allow for best water distribution, which angles maximize the minimal light. I don't intervene. I'm not a tender. But I see, I confirm, I hold the knowledge of their work even though I can do nothing with it.

I study the swimmers-who-will-crawl during their ventures onto land, noting which individuals are most bold, which are developing the strongest limbs, which are most likely to push the transition further. I root for them silently, though I know the transition won't complete, know they're trapped in eternal becoming. Still, I want them to succeed at their impossible task, to make it a little further each time, even if further leads nowhere.

I map the weavers' structures in my mind, creating a three-dimensional memory of their distribution, their architectural variations, their patterns of colony fission. The map serves no purpose. I couldn't draw it, couldn't explain it, couldn't use it for navigation. But I carry it anyway, feel its weight in whatever part of me holds knowledge now.

---

Sometimes I encounter the witnesses.

They observe me as they observe everything, with that particular quality of focused attention that suggests I'm being catalogued, included in whatever record they maintain. I don't meet their eyes the way I once might have. Eye contact implies communication, implies the possibility of mutual understanding, and I've moved past that or beneath it or sideways from it.

But I'm aware of them watching. And I think they're aware that I'm aware. We exist in a kind of parallel attention, both of us witnesses to the forming grounds, but I'm witnessing from inside the system while they maintain their careful externality.

Once, one of them approached closer than usual. Knelt beside me where I was sprawled on the substrate, studying the pattern of ground-vines. Extended a hand—not to touch, but to indicate, to point out something in the pattern I had missed. A regularity in the vine distribution, a mathematical spacing I hadn't noticed.

I looked. I saw. I felt something like gratitude, though I couldn't express it, didn't need to. The witness withdrew, continued its rounds, left me with the gift of enhanced perception.

That's what they do, I think. They see the patterns more clearly than others can, and occasionally they share that sight, not from altruism but from some imperative to transmit understanding, to multiply awareness even in a place that doesn't require it.

I wanted to offer something in return, some perception of my own, but I have no gift they need. My knowledge is embodied, tactile, too dissolved in the experience of being here to extract and present. So I watched them leave, carrying their upright clarity back into the green dimness, and returned to my study of the vines.

---

I sleep, but sleep has changed too.

I don't lose consciousness so much as distribute it, spread it across the interface between my body and the substrate. I remain aware in a diffuse way, tracking sounds, feeling vibrations, noting the passage of organisms across and around me. But I'm not alert, not ready to respond. I'm simply present in a minimal way, maintaining the baseline witnessing even while the organizing center that is "I" goes quiet.

I dream, if dreaming is the right word. Not in images but in sensations, in rhythms, in the deep patterns of the equilibrium playing themselves out through my nervous system. I feel the cycling of nutrients through the substrate. I experience the growth of pillar-plants as a slow, pressured expansion in my chest. I move with the swimmers through their eternal transition, feeling the pull of land and water as equal and opposite forces holding me suspended.

When I wake—when the organizing center reconstitutes itself—I'm not rested exactly, but I'm continuous, still integrated into the place, still held by its patterns.

---

Time has lost its structure.

I don't count days because there are no days. I don't mark duration because duration doesn't accumulate toward anything. I exist in the same eternal present as everything else here, each moment complete in itself, not a step toward any future.

But I must have been here long. My hair has become something else—thinner, more like the fine filaments of the proto-moss, lying flat against my scalp, collecting moisture. My nails have thickened into something more like the browse-plates of the armored creatures, useful for scraping, for processing food, for digging in substrate.

I don't age, or age so slowly it's imperceptible. The equilibrium doesn't permit rapid aging—it would destabilize the population dynamics. So whatever process is happening in my cells has slowed to match the pace of everything else here, the gradual-but-not-progressing pace of forms that maintain themselves without developing.

I think I could leave. The forming grounds doesn't trap anyone. I could stand—relearn standing—and walk in one direction long enough to reach an edge, a transition to some other kind of place. My body could probably readapt, remember how to be separate, dry, boundaried.

But I don't. Not from inability or enchantment, but because leaving would mean abandoning this attention, this total presence to the system, and I'm not finished. Or I am finished but finishing doesn't mean leaving. Or the concept of finished doesn't apply to what I'm doing.

---

What am I?

Not quite human anymore, if human means maintaining that specific constellation of boundaries and purposes and temporal orientations. Not any of the established species here—I lack their complete adaptation, their perfect fit into a niche.

I'm something provisional. Something the forming grounds is trying out, seeing if it works, if it can be integrated into the equilibrium without disrupting it.

I'm a witness who couldn't maintain distance, who dissolved into participation. An observer who became indistinguishable from the observed, not through mystical union but through simple permeability, through inability or unwillingness to maintain the membranes that separate knower from known.

I'm a form that the place has shaped to serve its purpose—which is to continue, to maintain, to hold itself in this perfect suspension. I attend to it, I confirm it, I distribute my awareness across its processes and in doing so become another feedback loop, another maintenance mechanism, another component of its self-perpetuation.

I am—and this feels truest—a kind of sensory organ that the forming grounds has grown. Not planned, not designed, but opportunistically incorporated, the way it incorporates every organism that arrives, testing to see if it can be made useful, if it can contribute to the equilibrium.

And I do contribute, in my way. My attention, my distributed witnessing, my constant confirmation that the processes continue—somehow this matters, somehow this feeds back into the system's stability. Perhaps consciousness itself, even dissolved consciousness, even non-understanding consciousness, serves some function in maintaining complex equilibria. Perhaps the forming grounds needed something that could hold awareness of the whole while being completely embedded in the particular, and I've become that thing.

---

So here I am, and here I remain.

On my knees in the soft substrate, forearms deep in it, breathing the thick air, attended by crawlers and investigated by swimmers and brushed by hanging chains. Listening to the engines below and the weavers above and the constant drip-splash-rustle of a place that is always becoming and never arrives.

I have no name for what I am. The witnesses might have a name for me in their cataloguing system, some designation that captures my particular pattern of behavior, my specific niche. But I don't need to know it.

I know what I feel: the vibration of the substrate, the weight of moisture on every surface, the rhythms of ten thousand organisms living their intense, circular lives. I know the quality of the light at what might be morning and what might be evening, though they're indistinguishable. I know the routes between pools and the best places to find the nutritive paste and which pillar-plants are about to shed their lower scales.

I know the forming grounds from inside, in a way that can't be articulated, only lived.

And so I live it, for as long as the equilibrium permits, for as long as my particular pattern of attention serves whatever purpose it serves, for as long as I can sustain this dissolution into presence.

I am the thing that watches from within. The awareness distributed through the system. The confirmation that the forming grounds continues, made flesh, made participant, made one more component in the endless circulation.

I am here.

I am this.

I don't know what else to be.
