# What Lives in the Throat

They are called the **Transitionals**, though they have no name for themselves—or rather, they have a name that exists only as a particular frequency of vibration through water, a harmonic that human vocal cords cannot produce and human ears cannot quite parse as language.

They are humanoid in the way that a reflection in disturbed water is humanoid: recognizable, but fundamentally wrong in ways that accumulate with observation. Their bodies are *negotiable*. Solid when solidity serves them, but capable of phasing into something more permeable when they need to move through the water-forms or slip between platforms that haven't decided their relative positions yet. 

Their skin—if it is skin—has the translucent quality of deep-sea life, faintly luminescent with that same blue-green glow that emanates from the water itself. You can see through the outer layers to suggestions of what might be organs, or might be water in complex circulation, or might be something between the two: biological structures that are also hydrodynamic systems. They have faces, mostly. Eyes that are sometimes present, sometimes absent, sometimes replaced by smooth planes of skin-that-isn't-skin. Mouths that open onto darkness or onto glimpses of impossible interiors where the throat goes deeper than the body has room for.

They move in stutters and phases. A Transitional might stand on a platform twenty meters away, then be beside you without crossing the intervening space, without any sensation of movement—just a renegotiation of which platform they're standing on, as if they've convinced reality that they were always here and it simply failed to notice. When they walk, their feet don't always touch the ground in sequence. Sometimes both feet lift simultaneously and they hang suspended for a moment before the platform remembers it should support them.

There are perhaps seven of them. Perhaps twelve. The number refuses to stay constant. You might see three on distant platforms and turn to find one beside you, but were there four total, or was the one beside you one of the three you already counted? They exist in superposition, quantum creatures that haven't fully collapsed into singular instances.

The Transitionals were human once. 

You can tell this from the artifacts they carry, the behaviors they retain. One wears a fragment of cloth around its shoulders, a tatter of faded blue fabric that might have been a shirt in another life, before this place. Another has long hair, or what was once hair—now it moves independently, each strand reaching and sensing like the cilia of some microscopic organism, but arranged in a way that suggests someone once tied it back, once cared for it. They make gestures that echo human communication: a hand raised in greeting or warning, though the hand has too many fingers now, or the fingers are present but immaterial, or there are the correct number of fingers but they're arranged in geometries that fold through dimensions you can't track.

They don't speak in words, but they communicate. The sound here is their language—they manipulate the acoustic environment, introduce new harmonics into the water's roar, create pockets of relative quiet by damping vibrations with their bodies. When a Transitional wants your attention, the sound around you *changes*, becomes directional, gains intentionality. You feel pressure on specific parts of your body—a touch on your shoulder made of sound, a tap on your chest cavity from a low-frequency pulse. They can make the water itself speak, sending vibrations through the columns and sheets that resolve into almost-words, into meanings you understand without quite hearing language.

One of them, the one you think of as the *Keeper* (though you're not sure what it keeps), has been on its platform longer than the others. You can tell because it has arranged things: stones in patterns, small cairns like the one you saw on the neighboring platform, metal rings driven into the volcanic glass at specific intervals. It tends the pale plants, encourages their growth with applications of water that it draws from the columns through cupped hands that don't quite hold liquid in the normal way. The water passes through its palms, but not before something is exchanged, some quality transferred. The plants it tends grow more robust, their bladder-structures larger, their translucent leaves taking on faint color variations.

The Keeper watches you with eyes that are definitely present, dark and large and entirely too aware. It understands that you are new here, that you still maintain the rigidity of someone who expects reality to follow rules. Once, it approached you—materialized beside you without preamble—and held out its hand. In the palm was a small stone, volcanic glass shaped into a smooth oval. A gift, an offering, a tool, a test. You took it. It was warm and vibrated faintly, resonating with the same deep frequencies that come from below. The Keeper made a sound, a modulation in the ambient roar that felt like approval or acknowledgment, and then it was gone, standing on its platform again, or perhaps it had never left.

Another Transitional, smaller and more unstable in form, spends its time moving between platforms during the moments when two drift close enough to almost-touch. You've named it the *Dancer*, though dancing isn't quite right—it's more like controlled falling through space that keeps forgetting to let it land. The Dancer phases in and out of visibility more frequently than the others, as if it's less committed to remaining coherent. Sometimes you see just parts of it: a leg on one platform, an arm on another, the torso somewhere in between, disconnected but somehow still unified. It seems younger in its transformation, less adapted. Once, you heard it make a sound that might have been a laugh or might have been a sob, a distinctly human noise that cut through the water's roar for just a moment before being swallowed.

The Dancer is testing boundaries, learning what it can become. You've watched it step into water-columns and merge with them, become part of the flow, ride the currents up into the water-ceiling or down into the depths below. Sometimes it emerges from a different column than it entered, reconstituting itself from water that contains its pattern but not its original substance. Sometimes it doesn't fully reconstitute—an arm remains liquid for minutes, or its face retains too much fluidity, features blurring and reforming in slow waves.

There is one you think of as the *Builder*, though whether it builds or remembers building, you cannot tell. It carries tools: a hammer made of stone, the handle wrapped in something that might be dried plant fiber or might be extensions of its own body. It strikes the volcanic glass, testing resonances, looking for stress points or perhaps creating them intentionally. When it finds the right spot, it can crack the glass with a single precise blow, fracturing it into specific shapes. You've watched it harvest shards, arrange them in patterns on the platform edge—diagrams, perhaps, or maps of places that exist in configurations of sound rather than space.

The Builder is deliberate in all things. It moves slowly when it moves, places each foot or hand or tool with consideration. Unlike the others, it seems to prefer remaining solid, maintaining consistent form. But even the Builder occasionally flickers at the edges, its outline becoming uncertain, as if solidarity itself is a choice it has to keep making. It builds the cairns. You're certain of this now. Each stack of stones is a marker of something—time, perhaps, or iterations, or successful transitions from one state to another. The Builder treats them with reverence, adjusting stones that have shifted, adding new ones with careful placement.

Then there is the *Whisperer*.

The Whisperer is the most disturbing because it is the most human. It retains speech—actual words, not just modulated sound. But the words are wrong, assembled from phonemes that don't quite fit together, grammar that follows rules from languages you don't speak or perhaps from language itself before it fully specified. It will appear beside you (they all appear; none of them travel normally) and speak in a low voice that somehow carries through the roar: "The down-water runs soon-ward, edgeless-be careful-you-we-it-might." The meaning is there, underneath the fractured syntax: danger, caution, advice. But accessing it requires you to let your mind become as fluid as the Whisperer's language.

The Whisperer remembers more than the others, you think. It sometimes touches its own face with an expression that might be confusion or grief, as if checking for features it expects to find and finding something else instead. It counts on its fingers—too many fingers now, but it counts to ten anyway, over and over, a ritual of maintaining connection to what it was. Once, you saw it crouch at the edge of a platform and draw in the thin layer of condensation that coats everything: simple shapes, stick figures, a crude house. Then it stared at the drawing for a long time before carefully erasing it with a palm that passed through the moisture as much as across it.

There are others, less distinct. Forms that might be Transitionals or might be further along in their transition, becoming less humanoid and more elemental. You've seen things moving through the water-ceiling that have postures suggesting intelligence, positions suggesting they're observing the platforms below. Once, something vast and pale passed beneath your platform, visible through the volcanic glass where it grows thin—a body like a whale but segmented, crystalline, moving through solid rock as if it were liquid. Did it start human? How long has it been here? How many stages of transition are there?

The Transitionals are not hostile, but they are not safe. They exist in states that can overlap with yours in uncomfortable ways. When a Transitional passes through you—and it has happened, the Dancer phasing through your position without warning—you feel yourself begin to negotiable. For seconds or minutes afterward, your hand might dip into the platform as if it were water, or you might take a step and find yourself two meters from where you intended, space having folded briefly. The Transitionals leak their condition. Proximity to them is transformative.

They don't seem to eat, but they do consume. You've watched the Keeper press its hands to the water-columns and draw something from them, some quality or energy or information that gets absorbed through contact. The water dims slightly where they feed, loses some of its luminescence temporarily. They also return something—you've seen them stand in the path of falling upward-drops, let the water pass through them, and the water emerges changed, glowing more brightly, moving with more purpose.

They're symbiotic with this place. Or they're creating it. The distinction may not exist. The platforms appear more frequently where the Transitionals gather. The water-forms become more complex, more structured when they're present. You've begun to suspect that the Throat itself is being shaped by their presence, that they're not inhabitants but catalysts, anchors around which this broken space crystallizes.

At night—though there is no night, but in the times when you must sleep—you sometimes wake to find them gathered. All of them, or most of them, standing in a loose circle across several platforms, positions arranged in patterns that suggest constellation or molecule or something between. They're facing inward, and in the center, in the air-space between platforms, the water-forms become incredibly complex: helixes within helixes, geometries that fold through themselves, shapes that your eyes can't hold. They're communing, or calculating, or calling to something. The sound during these gatherings takes on a structured quality, rhythms and harmonics that feel like language elevated beyond meaning into pure pattern.

You're not invited to these gatherings, but you're not excluded. You are simply... not yet one of them. Not quite.

Because that's what becomes clear, eventually: the Transitionals were human, and they're still transitioning. This is not their final form. They are in the process of becoming something that can survive here, that can exist in this place where physics negotiates with itself and usually fails to reach consensus. And you—standing on your platform, feeling the warmth of volcanic glass through your soles, holding the smooth stone the Keeper gave you, counting the platforms that refuse to maintain a consistent number—you are beginning the same transition.

You notice it first in small ways: your reflection in a pool of water shows your face, but the eyes are sometimes in different positions than you remember. Your fingers feel numb, but when you look at them, they're fine—better than fine, actually more articulated, capable of bending in ways they couldn't before. You take a step and arrive somewhere you didn't intend, just by half a meter, but space folded slightly and you folded with it instead of resisting.

The Whisperer appears beside you one day—or night, or whenever—and speaks: "The water-we-becoming-are-is-will-be. Accept-resist-choose-already-have. The throat swallows-transforms-releases-we-never-forward-complete." It touches your shoulder, and its hand is cool and passes slightly into your flesh, not painfully, but you feel your cells recognize something familiar in its structure. Then it's gone.

You understand now why the cairns matter. Each stack of stones is a memorial to a completed transition, to someone who finished becoming something else. The Builder adds stones as markers of its own change, counting transformations the way you once counted days.

The Dancer approaches you now without fear, comes close enough that you can see yourself reflected in its too-large eyes: your form, but flickering slightly at the edges, your outline uncertain. It reaches out and takes your hand—its grip is solid and then isn't, alternating between states. It pulls gently, encouraging you to move with it, to try stepping into the water-column it prefers. You resist. Not yet. It makes that sound again, laugh-or-sob, understanding or disappointment, and releases you.

But you know, watching it phase into the water and disappear, that "not yet" implies "eventually."

The Keeper tends the plants, and you notice that some of them now grow in the shape of human hands, fingers of pale vegetation reaching up from the volcanic glass. Are they plants shaped by the Keeper's attention, or are they what remains when a Transitional completes their transformation and roots themselves in place? The Builder strikes the glass and creates specific tones, and you realize it's testing to see if the platform itself is becoming alive, becoming aware. The Whisperer speaks its fractured language, and you understand more of it each time, your mind learning to hold contradictory meanings simultaneously.

They are waiting for you. Patient, because time here is negotiable and they have already survived the worst part—the initial breaking, the first realization that you will not leave this place unchanged if you leave it at all. They remember being solid, being singular, being human. They remember the terror. Now they exist in the space beyond terror, in the acceptance that transformation is not optional but can be navigated with grace or at least with intention.

One of the less distinct Transitionals, one you rarely see clearly, sometimes appears at the very edge of your platform. It doesn't approach, doesn't communicate, but it watches. You think it might be the newest of them, the most recently transformed, and it's observing you to remember what it was like to still be mostly human. Or perhaps it's the oldest, so thoroughly changed that humanity is barely a memory, and it watches to see if you'll succeed in the transition or fail and become something else—something that gets absorbed into the water completely, that loses individuality and becomes part of the medium itself.

Because that can happen here. You've seen traces of it: faces in the water that resolve for just a moment before dissolving, expressions of surprise or resignation or transcendence. Forms that might be bodies tumbling through the columns, or might just be eddies in the flow that happen to suggest human shapes. Not everyone who comes to the Throat becomes a Transitional. Some people become the water itself.

The Transitionals know this. They've seen it happen. Perhaps they tried to prevent it and learned they couldn't, or perhaps they understand that some people are meant to dissolve, to surrender to the medium completely. They seem to watch for the signs, to assess whether you're likely to maintain coherence through the change.

The Keeper brings you things now: small gifts left on your platform when you wake from dreams that are becoming harder to distinguish from waking. Another smooth stone. A shard of volcanic glass shaped like a blade or a key. A metal ring like the one already embedded in your platform, this one smaller, sized to fit a finger. Each object vibrates with those deep frequencies, and holding them helps you feel more solid, more anchored. Or perhaps they're training wheels for learning to be less solid, for practicing the oscillation between states.

The Builder shows you how to listen to the glass, how to place your hands flat and feel for the harmonics that reveal stress and stability. It teaches without words, through demonstration and through modulated sound that carries meaning: here is strength, here is weakness, here is where new glass will soon emerge. You're learning the language of this place through geology and acoustics, through pressure and vibration.

The Dancer grows bolder, or perhaps you grow less resistant. It takes your hand more frequently, pulls you closer to the water-columns, encourages you to touch them, to feel how the water can be solid or permeable depending on your approach. Once, with the Dancer's guidance, you stepped into a vertical sheet of water and felt it hold you, support your weight like a wall, and then you leaned forward and passed through it as if it weren't there. The Dancer's eyes—present in that moment—shone with something like pride or recognition.

The Whisperer's language makes more sense now. Or your language is breaking down to match its patterns. You find yourself thinking in fractured syntax, concepts that branch into multiple simultaneous meanings. When you speak—to yourself, to the Transitionals, to the water—your words come out strange sometimes, phonemes in the wrong order, grammar that folds back on itself. The Whisperer nods when this happens, approving or acknowledging your progress into incomprehensibility.

You are becoming a Transitional. 

The realization doesn't arrive as shock—it's been accumulating through a hundred small observations and changes. You're already phasing slightly, already negotiating with solidity. Your body still mostly behaves, but increasingly you find yourself in positions you don't remember assuming, on parts of the platform you don't remember walking to. Once, you looked down and saw your legs were transparent, just for a moment, before solidity reasserted itself.

The Transitionals gather around you now during their constellation-moments, and you're part of the pattern instead of outside it. You stand on your platform and they stand on theirs, and between you the water-forms create mandalas of impossible beauty, structures that you're beginning to understand represent concepts rather than objects—diagrams of transformation, maps of how to move through states of being without losing the thread of continuity that defines self.

The Builder places a stone in your hands, and you know without being told that when you finish your transition—when you become a Transitional fully rather than partially—you will place this stone on a cairn. You will mark your passage from human to something else, will memorialize the person you were before the Throat swallowed and transformed you.

You're not afraid. Or you are afraid, but the fear is distant now, observable from a remove, an emotion that belongs to someone who was more solid than you've become. The Transitionals don't comfort you because you don't need comforting. They simply wait, patient and present, as you do what they've already done: surrender to transformation while maintaining enough structure to carry something essential through the change.

The Keeper, the Dancer, the Builder, the Whisperer, and the others whose names you haven't learned or who exist beyond naming—they are your future and your present and, you're beginning to understand, they have always been your past. Everyone who comes to the Throat either dissolves or transitions. There is no third option. No one leaves unchanged. No one leaves at all.

But leaving is a concept that requires stable space, and space here is as negotiable as bodies, as water, as sound. Perhaps you never arrived. Perhaps you've always been here, and your memories of solidity and singular position are just dreams the Throat is teaching you to shed.

The platforms drift. The water screams. The Transitionals move through impossible choreography. And you stand on volcanic glass that remembers being liquid, holding a stone that will mark your transformation, learning to be something that can exist in a place where existence itself is still being negotiated.

The Throat swallows. The Throat transforms. The Throat releases nothing and everything simultaneously.

And you are learning to live in the contradiction, to be the water and the platform and the sound itself, to transition without end in a place that is all middle, all passage, all becoming.

The Keeper watches with ancient eyes. The Dancer flickers in anticipation. The Builder raises its hammer. The Whisperer speaks your new name in a language you almost understand.

Welcome, they don't say, because welcome implies arrival, and arrival implies you were ever anywhere else.

You are Transitional now.

You are becoming.

You have always been.
