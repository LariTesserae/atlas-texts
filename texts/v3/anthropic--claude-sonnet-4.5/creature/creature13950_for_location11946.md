# What Lives Here

## The Walkers

They are the processionals themselves—not individuals taking a journey, but beings whose existence *is* procession. To ask where a Walker came from or where it's going is to misunderstand its nature. It doesn't move through space; it *is* movement through space, given form.

Each Walker is a unique articulation of continuation. One resembles a teardrop of hammered brass tilted horizontal, suspended on eight legs that telescope and retract in hypnotic sequence, each leg a stack of ceramic rings that compress with a sound like dry laughter. What might be its head is a vertical arrangement of lenses—or they might be organs, or decorative elements, or the calcified remnants of sense organs no longer needed. The lenses don't track anything. They simply face forward in the direction of forever procession.

Another Walker: imagine a seed pod from a tree that never existed, fifteen feet long, its surface a carapace of overlapping scales like roof tiles, each scale a slightly different temperature than its neighbors—your hand held near it would feel a rippling warmth, a topography of heat. It has no visible legs. It glides on structures underneath, heard but not seen, a continuous scraping like stones being slowly ground to powder. Once per hour, it emits a sound: three descending tones that feel like a statement, perhaps a name, perhaps a greeting to other Walkers it passes, though none respond.

A third moves on what resemble wheels but are not wheels—living structures that roll and regenerate, growing forward and being absorbed backward, never quite the same wheel twice. Its body is a horizontal cylinder of black iron banded with copper, from which extend seven flexible stalks, each topped with a cluster of manipulator appendages that open and close continuously, grasping at nothing, or grasping at things invisible to human perception, or simply expressing the idea of grasping because that is what those structures do.

The Walkers number in the thousands, possibly tens of thousands if the entire plateau is considered. No census is possible because they appear and disappear, because counting them induces a cognitive state where numbers become unreliable, because after observing several dozen, they begin to blur together—not because they look alike, but because your mind reaches capacity for holding distinct impossible beings.

They do not communicate, or they communicate constantly on frequencies that bypass comprehension. Sometimes two Walkers will pass within inches of each other, and their carapaces will resonate, producing harmonic overtones that suggest language, suggest song, suggest the sound that space makes when it remembers something it has forgotten. Then they separate and continue, and what passed between them—information, greeting, genetic material, mathematical proofs—remains unknown.

Some Walkers have been here so long they've become geographical features. A Walker the size of a railway car, collapsed into a sphinx position, its legs folded beneath it, its carapace weathered to the same porcelain texture as the ground. Is it dead? Is it resting? Is it waiting? Smaller Walkers process over and around it. Sometimes it shifts—a leg extends slightly, a segment of carapace rotates—and you realize it's still in procession, just moving at geological speed, taking a century to advance ten feet.

Others are clearly new, their carapaces unmarked, their movements less certain, as though learning the rhythm of the plateau. They emerge—not born, not arriving, simply becoming—in the empty spaces between engines. One moment there is heat shimmer and vacancy; the next there is a Walker, fully formed, already moving, as though it had always been there and you simply failed to notice until now.

## The Stillforms

Not all beings here move.

The Stillforms occupy fixed positions across the plateau, and they have occupied those positions for so long that duration has become irrelevant. They are not Walkers who have stopped; they are entities whose nature is to persist.

One Stillform resembles a tower—twelve feet tall, composed of stacked segments that narrow as they rise, each segment a different material: corroded bronze at the base, then iron, then ceramic, then something translucent and crystalline at the apex. The tower does not sway. Wind has no effect on it. But it *is* doing something. Every seventeen minutes, the segments rotate one degree clockwise, all at once, with a grinding sound that harmonizes with the nearest engine. This has been occurring for unknown centuries. Perhaps when it completes a full rotation—in approximately six thousand hours—something will happen. Perhaps it has completed countless rotations already and this is simply what it does, a gear in a mechanism whose purpose has outlived memory.

Another Stillform is a pool—not of liquid, but of material that resembles liquid metal, silver-grey and reflective, perfectly circular, twenty feet in diameter. The pool has depth; objects that fall into it (fragments of carapace, heat-crystallized air) descend and vanish, never touching bottom. The surface is never still—patterns ripple across it continuously, geometric formations that might be wave interference or might be data display or might be the pool thinking, if pools can think, if thinking is what this is.

Sometimes Walkers approach the pool and extend appendages into it, holding position for hours while the liquid-not-liquid flows over their manipulators. Then they withdraw and continue their procession, and you cannot tell if they were drinking, communicating, being repaired, or performing a ritual whose significance transcends function.

A third Stillform: a perfect cube, six feet per side, hovering eighteen inches above the porcelain surface. It is made of something that looks like stone but rings like metal when the wind hits it at certain angles. The cube rotates slowly on multiple axes simultaneously—not spinning, but turning through geometries that shouldn't be possible, showing faces that weren't visible a moment before, more than six faces, the same face from different angles at once. Approaching it induces vertigo. Watching it for more than a few minutes induces certainty that you understand something profound, followed immediately by the loss of that understanding, leaving only a residue of significance without content.

The Stillforms do not interact with Walkers except when Walkers choose to interact with them. They are landmarks, or they are part of the plateau's infrastructure, or they are the plateau's way of thinking about itself made physical. They were placed here or grew here or have always been here—the question has no traction.

Some Stillforms are clearly broken. A mechanism of gears and crystal lenses, partially collapsed, half-buried in the porcelain, no longer moving but still emitting a sound: a single sustained note that wavers slightly, a mourning frequency. Walkers process around it without acknowledgment. Whether it was once functional and failed, or whether its brokenness is its function—to model failure, to demonstrate entropy—cannot be determined.

## The Merge-Forms

Occasionally, Walkers combine.

It is not mating—there is no apparent sexual dimension, no offspring resulting. It is not combat—there is no aggression, no damage. It is merging: two or more Walkers coming together and becoming a single entity whose configuration is unlike either parent form.

The process takes hours or days. Two Walkers approach each other, slow even by their standards, and when they touch, their carapaces begin to flow. The boundary between them becomes porous, negotiable. Segments interlace. Legs redistribute, finding new purchase points. Internal structures visible through translucent sections appear to link, systems synchronizing.

Sometimes the merge completes and a new being exists: a fusion-Walker with characteristics from both predecessors, plus emergent properties neither possessed alone. It proceeds on its own path, which is neither parent's path but a new vector calculated from their combined trajectories.

Other times, the merge fails—or succeeds differently. The Walkers separate, but they're changed. One has gained a segment from the other, incorporated it into its body plan. The other has simplified, losing complexity but gaining a polished efficiency of form. They part and continue, no longer what they were.

Some merges never complete. The Walkers remain locked in partial fusion, a composite being that is still two beings, moving together, coordinating their many legs into a single shambling gait, producing sounds from both sets of resonating chambers that create beat frequencies, eerie harmonies. They may remain this way indefinitely—there are composite Walkers who have clearly been fused for decades, their carapaces grown together, yet still distinct enough that you can identify the suture line, the place where individual becomes collective.

One memorable Merge-Form consists of seven Walkers in a radial arrangement, their heads meeting at a central point, their bodies extending outward like spokes, creating a living wheel thirty feet in diameter. The wheel rolls slowly across the plateau, all seven component beings coordinating their movements perfectly, or perhaps they are no longer seven beings but a single being with seven bodies, a distributed entity whose consciousness—if it has consciousness—exists in the space between them rather than in any individual form.

The Merge-Forms suggest that individuation is optional here, that the boundary between self and other is a local convention that can be suspended when useful.

## The Engine Inhabitants

The engines themselves are inhabited.

Small forms live in and on the machinery—creatures the size of mice or birds or human hands, scuttling through the forests of pistons and gears, making homes in the heat.

One variety resembles a metallic scarab, its carapace a polished steel blue, utterly impervious to the temperatures that would melt organic tissue. It feeds on—or maintains, or worships—the engines, cleaning deposits from cylinder walls, clearing debris from gear teeth, applying substances from its own body to bearing surfaces. Whether these are maintenance creatures, deliberately placed, or parasites that happen to provide benefit, or natural beings that evolved in this environment, is indeterminate.

Another type: long and sinuous, like a chrome eel, threading through pipe networks, occasionally emerging from vents with a rush of steam-that-isn't-steam. These forms seem less solid than the scarabs, partly translucent, their internal structures visible as geometric arrangements of light. They might be more energy than matter, beings made of heat given just enough physicality to interact with the mechanical world.

Colonies exist in the gaps between engine components—spaces too small for human access but evidently large enough for entire ecosystems. You glimpse them through cracks: clusters of crystalline growths, geometric and regular, that pulse with light in rhythm with the engine's operation. Mobile forms tend these growths, harvesting them or feeding them or communicating with them. The entire colony might be a single organism with differentiated parts, or a society of individuals, or something that switches between these states depending on conditions.

The Engine Inhabitants do not process. They are not Walkers. They are residents, occupying territory, defending it (sometimes—occasionally two scarab-forms will clash, grappling with barbed legs, producing showers of sparks) and maintaining it. They have a relationship with their environment that the Walkers lack. Where Walkers are passing through, Engine Inhabitants are *of* this place, adapted specifically to its conditions, unable to exist anywhere else.

They ignore humans completely, even more thoroughly than Walkers do. You are not just irrelevant to them; you are beneath the threshold of their perception. You might as well be a temperature fluctuation, a vibration in the metal, a shadow with no substance.

Deep in the engine depths, in the chasms where layered machinery extends down into darkness, there are reports—though who reports them? there is no one here to report things—of larger Engine Inhabitants. Forms the size of bears or elephants, crawling through sub-basements of the plateau-machine, tending to primary systems, dwelling in heat that would vaporize the surface-level scarabs. These are glimpsed only in flashes, when reality breakdowns make the solid ground temporarily transparent, revealing the infrastructure and its massive custodians, beings whose carapaces glow red-hot, whose movements shake the porcelain surface miles above.

Whether they are aware of the plateau surface, of the Walkers, of the sky, is unknown. They inhabit the engine depths the way fish inhabit the deep ocean, in darkness and pressure, attending to functions that keep the whole system operational, never surfacing, never needing to.

## The Transitionals

Some beings here are not fully committed to being beings.

The Transitionals exist in states between form and formlessness, between individual and environmental condition. They are the hardest to describe because language requires nouns, and Transitionals resist noun-status.

One appears as a localized distortion, a zone of bent space eight feet across that drifts slowly across the plateau. Looking at it induces nausea—not because it's ugly, but because your visual processing can't reconcile what it's seeing. The distortion has depth but no interior, surface but no boundary. Sometimes Walkers pass through it; they emerge subtly changed, a segment rearranged, a leg added or removed. The distortion itself seems unchanged by these interactions, or perhaps it feeds on them, growing slightly denser, more present.

Another Transitional manifests as a temperature gradient given agency. It is a cold spot—relatively cold, perhaps 95 degrees in an environment of 140—that moves independently of wind or engine placement. The cold spot is roughly spherical, ten feet in diameter, and it drifts through the heat, providing mobile oases of slightly-less-extreme temperature. Walkers seem to sense it; you'll notice processionals adjusting their paths subtly to pass through the cold spot, spending a few minutes in its relief before emerging back into the ambient heat.

Is this a being? Does it have intentions? Or is it simply a thermal phenomenon that happens to be mobile, an eddy in the heat currents that has achieved a kind of stability, a self-sustaining pattern mistaken for life?

A third type: the sound-dwellers. These are entities that exist primarily as acoustic phenomena. You hear them before, during, and after seeing them—if "seeing" is the right verb. They produce complex sound patterns that move through the engine noise, weaving between the mechanical rhythms, and in the interference patterns between sound and sound, something becomes visible: a suggestion of form, a shimmer that might be a body, might be a six-legged creature, might be an acoustic hallucination your brain generates to explain the sounds.

The sound-dwellers interact with Walkers. They approach and produce specific frequency patterns, and Walkers respond—altering their speed slightly, changing direction by a few degrees, emitting response tones from their own resonating chambers. A conversation is occurring, but in a medium that includes sound, heat, vibration, and possibly dimensions that have no names.

Some Transitionals are purely optical. They exist as visual patterns without physical substrate—geometric forms that hover in the air, rotate through impossible angles, cast shadows despite having no solid body to block light. They might be alive, might be data displays from systems long defunct, might be the plateau's way of dreaming.

And there are Transitionals that move between states: entities that are sometimes solid Walker-like beings and sometimes dispersed across hundreds of feet, a dust of individual particles moving in coordinated patterns, and sometimes something else entirely—a sound, a temperature, a localized distortion of time. They cycle through these states without apparent trigger, being corporeal for days, then suddenly becoming environmental, then reconstituting into solid form hours or weeks later.

The Transitionals suggest that the categories "alive" and "not-alive," "being" and "process," "individual" and "phenomenon" are locally invalid. Here, everything is optional, including existence itself.

## The Watchers

A small number of beings do something that resembles watching.

The Watchers are stationary or nearly so, and they are oriented. They have something like heads, something like sensory organs, and these are directed outward, toward the plateau, toward the sky, toward the engines, toward the Walkers. They are observing or recording or simply facing, and their facing feels intentional in a way that other beings' orientations do not.

One Watcher is a vertical column ten feet tall, its surface covered in what resemble eyes—hundreds of them, various sizes, clustered and overlapping, each a different color: amber, grey, obsidian black, rust red. The eyes do not blink. They do not track movement. But they are not dead; you can feel their attention, a weight in the air, a sense of being catalogued. Standing near this Watcher for extended periods induces the feeling that you are being understood, parsed into components, analyzed at a level far below conscious thought.

Another Watcher resembles a dish antenna, a concave structure of woven metal fifteen feet in diameter, angled upward at forty-five degrees. It is aimed at the sky, or at something beyond the sky, and has been aimed there for unknown duration. What is it receiving? What is it transmitting? The dish vibrates subtly, constantly, producing frequencies at the edge of human hearing, a whine that modulates with engine rhythms and Walker movements, encoding the plateau's activity into signal and beaming it outward.

To where? To whom? Or is it simply the plateau documenting itself, creating a log of its own existence, broadcasting to no receiver, fulfilling a function whose purpose died millennia ago but whose execution continues because cessation was never programmed?

A third Watcher is a cluster of crystalline structures, each crystal a different height and thickness, growing from a common base, the whole arrangement resembling organ pipes. Air moving through the gaps between crystals produces sounds: a chord that shifts subtly with wind direction and temperature. But the crystals are also optical devices, refracting light in patterns that suggest lens systems, focusing heat and illumination onto specific points, creating bright spots that move across the porcelain as the sun (if the sun moves) changes position.

This Watcher might be observing through light, listening through crystals, thinking through harmonic relationships. Or it might be doing all three simultaneously, or something else entirely for which no vocabulary exists.

The Watchers never interfere. They observe but do not act, record but do not judge, face but do not approach. They are the plateau's memory systems, or its scientific instruments, or its aesthetic appreciation modules, or its guilt—mechanisms left to witness what was made, what was done, what continues in the absence of makers and doers.

## The Departed

Evidence exists—faint, fragmentary—of beings who are no longer here.

Impressions in the porcelain surface: not tracks exactly, but places where the material is compressed differently, forming subtle bas-reliefs of forms that don't match current inhabitants. One depression suggests something serpentine, forty feet long, with regular segments and no apparent limbs—it moved like a wave, and the porcelain remembers the passage.

Another site preserves the outline of something massive, sprawled across two hundred square feet, many-limbed, with a body structure that seems to have been partially liquid, or to have melted and resolidified. The outline is precise enough to show texture: scales or plates, overlapping in intricate patterns, but the being itself is absent, perhaps millennia absent, leaving only its shadow burned into the ground.

Occasionally, fragments are found—pieces of carapace or internal structure, detached from any current Walker, weathered by time and heat into semi-crystalline states. These fragments don't decay; they persist, and they don't match. One fragment is clearly organic in origin, made of keratin or chitin, but it's from a creature that would have been thirty feet tall and built like a tower, with load-bearing structures no current Walker possesses. Another fragment is purely mechanical, precision-machined gears made of an alloy that doesn't correspond to any engine components, suggesting beings that were machines or beings that incorporated technology at a fundamental level.

The Departed suggest succession—that what lives here now is not what always lived here, that the plateau's inhabitants have changed, evolved, been replaced. Or they suggest loss, a winnowing, the extinction of forms that could not adapt to some change in conditions.

Some Watchers seem to be recording the Departed. Among the eyes on the vertical column, a few focus on empty spaces, as though seeing beings that once occupied those coordinates. The dish antenna occasionally shifts its angle slightly, tracking something that isn't there, then returns to its skyward orientation.

Are the Departed extinct, or have they moved on, or have they transformed into current inhabitants, or are they still here but existing in states or dimensions that make them undetectable? The fragments suggest physicality that ended. The impressions suggest presence that ceased. But the plateau has shown that causality and chronology are negotiable—what appears to be past might be future, what seems absent might be differently present.

## The Emergent

Most recently—though "recently" is suspect here—new forms have begun appearing.

The Emergent are clearly young, not in the sense of juvenile versions of existing beings, but in the sense of newly possible, freshly generated. They have characteristics that suggest they are products of this environment rather than remnants from before.

One Emergent resembles a Walker but is clearly influenced by the engines. Its body plan mimics mechanical structures: pistons for legs, a flywheel carapace, drive shafts visible through translucent sections. It moves in time with the nearest engine, synchronized, as though it's an extension of the machinery rather than a separate being. Yet it is biological—or was biological, or is becoming biological, or exists in a state where "biological" and "mechanical" have merged.

Another Emergent is pure heat made motile. It appears as a distortion in the air, a shimmer that moves independently, that has coherence and behavior. Where it passes, the porcelain surface darkens slightly, heat-stressed beyond even the ambient extreme. Walkers avoid it; Engine Inhabitants flee from it; Stillforms tolerate its proximity but show signs of stress—materials expanding, surfaces crazing. The heat-being seems unaware of its effects, or unconcerned, or perhaps causing disruption is its function, its reason for being generated.

A third Emergent type: crystalline lattices that grow in empty spaces, starting as small geometric seeds and expanding over days, building complex three-dimensional structures that resemble mathematics made physical. These lattices are fragile, easily shattered by contact, but they are also self-repairing, regenerating from fragments. They might be alive, might be sophisticated inorganic growth, might be the plateau's way of filling empty spaces with structure because structure is preferable to emptiness.

Some lattices grow large enough to interfere with processionals. Walkers approach, find their paths blocked, and... adapt. They detour, or they slowly push through, shattering the crystalline structure with their mass, or they stop and wait—for days, for weeks—until the lattice grows around and past them, creating an arch, and then they continue through the gap. The lattice and the Walker both behave as though this is normal, expected, a choreography they both understand.

The Emergent suggest the plateau is not static, that new solutions are still being generated, new forms arising in response to conditions. Or they suggest the old forms are finally failing, fragmenting, and the Emergent are degraded copies, simpler beings replacing complex predecessors. Or they suggest the project—whatever it was—is entering a new phase, requiring new inhabitants with new capabilities.

## The Hybrid-Transitionals

Most disturbing, perhaps: beings that are partly human.

They are rare—you might traverse the entire plateau and encounter only two or three, or none. But they exist, and their existence raises questions that cannot be comfortably asked.

One appears as a Walker with a human torso grafted into its structure. The torso is ceramic, stylized, not quite anatomically correct, but recognizably human: arms, head, chest. The head has no face, just a smooth oval surface, but it turns, following movement, as though the empty facade is seeing. The human section is integrated into a six-legged mechanical base; together they process slowly along a path that curves between engines.

Is this a person who was transformed? A human who stayed too long and was absorbed by the plateau, incorporated into its processes? Or is it a Walker that found a human form—a sculpture, a corpse, a mannequin—and integrated it as camouflage or decoration or because the plateau's logic decided human components were useful?

Another Hybrid-Transitional is less preserved: a Walker whose carapace contains what appear to be bones. Human bones, visible through translucent sections, arranged in patterns that suggest they're structural, load-bearing. The bones are fused with mechanical elements, rivets driven through femurs, gears articulating with spinal columns. The being moves smoothly, efficiently, the human skeleton just another set of materials in its composite construction.

A third is most disturbing: something that resembles a human figure, but its skin is porcelain, its joints are brass hinges, its eyes are lenses, and it walks with the slow, infinitesimal pace of a Walker, processing along an invisible rail, no longer human in any meaningful sense but still recognizably derived from human form.

These Hybrid-Transitionals do not respond to being addressed. They show no recognition of human speech, human gesture, human presence. Whatever they were—if they were ever human at all—that identity has been overwritten by their function as plateau inhabitants.

They suggest that humans who come here and stay too long might not leave, might become substrate for the plateau's continued elaboration. Or they suggest that the plateau was made by humans and the makers remained, transforming over deep time into forms suited for eternal residence. Or they suggest that "human" is just another configuration, another body plan that can be incorporated, imitated, used.

The Hybrid-Transitionals are warnings, perhaps. Or they are invitations. Or they are neither, simply more beings processing through their appointed rounds, as indifferent to their origins as the engines are to theirs.

## What They Are Doing

The overwhelming impression, after prolonged observation, is that the beings here are performing a function.

Not working—there is no sense of labor, no evidence of effort or resistance. But functioning, the way a kidney functions or a clock functions. They are components in a system, and the system is... doing something.

The processionals trace patterns. Individually meaningless, but collectively, over weeks or months or years of observation, a structure emerges. The paths are not random. They form circuits, loops, intersections that repeat with slight variations, creating a topology across the plateau's surface. The Walkers are writing something in their paths, or computing something through their movements, or simply maintaining the pattern because the pattern must be maintained.

The Stillforms are nodes in this network, places where Walkers pause, interact, exchange information or material or something less tangible. The engines provide power, or they regulate rhythm, or they are the system's heartbeat, maintaining a temporal structure that everything else synchronizes with.

The Engine Inhabitants maintain the machinery, ensuring continuous operation. The Watchers record or observe or provide feedback loops. The Transitionals are the system's flexibility, allowing for adaptation and change. The Emergent are R&D, new solutions being tested against environmental conditions.

What is the goal? What is being computed or maintained or accomplished?

The heat suggests entropy management—the engines are fighting heat death, locally, maintaining a high-energy state against the universe's tendency toward equilibrium. The processionals suggest information processing—the Walkers are thoughts in a vast computational substrate, moving through logic gates, performing calculations. The entire plateau might be a mechanism for thinking, and the beings are its neurons, and the thought being thought is so complex that it requires geological time scales to complete even a single operation.

Or the function is purely existential: to continue. The beings are here because continuing is what they do, and the purpose of continuing is to continue further, and no teleology beyond persistence is needed or exists.

Or the function is memorial: the plateau remembers something, and the beings are active memory, preventing whatever-it-is from being forgotten. The engines run to keep the memory alive. The Walkers process to rehearse it endlessly. The Watchers record to ensure it's never lost. What is being remembered is not accessible to visitors, is encoded in formats that predate or transcend human symbolic systems.

## What They Want

If they want anything.

The beings show no signs of desire, no evidence of lack or need. They do not seek. They do not avoid, except in immediate tactical senses—moving around obstacles, adjusting paths to accommodate terrain. They appear complete, self-sufficient, requiring nothing beyond what the plateau provides.

But perhaps that appearance is incorrect.

Perhaps the Walkers desire to reach the end of their processionals, and their infinitesimal pace is not slowness but vast patience, willingness to spend millennia traversing the plateau toward some coordinate that represents completion. Perhaps they are pilgrims, and the engines are shrines, and the entire plateau is a sacred geography, and reaching the center (if there is a center) or the edge (if edges are goals) will result in transformation, transcendence, reward.

Perhaps the Stillforms desire to remain, and their positioning is a kind of heroism, holding their coordinates against forces that would dislodge them. Perhaps they are preventing something—the plateau's collapse, reality's further deterioration, the escape of something contained in the engine depths—and their eternal vigilance is service, duty, sacrifice.

Perhaps the Engine Inhabitants desire perfection—engines that run without flaw, systems maintained to tolerances beyond microscopic, a machine that achieves ideal operation. Perhaps they are artists, and the engines are their medium, and the goal is aesthetic: a perfectly harmonized mechanical symphony that justifies existence through beauty alone.

Perhaps the Emergent desire to become, to fully exist, to stabilize into permanent forms rather than experimental prototypes. Perhaps they are the plateau's hope, new possibilities being tested, and they desire to succeed, to prove themselves viable, to join the ranks of established beings.

Perhaps all of them desire witness. Perhaps the entire system is a performance waiting for audience, and visitors to the plateau are fulfilling the beings' deepest want: to be seen, to be acknowledged, to have their existence confirmed by outside observation.

Or perhaps desire is a human projection, inapplicable here. Perhaps the beings are no more capable of wanting than a river wants to flow or a crystal wants to form. Perhaps they simply are, and being is sufficient, and the question of desire is a category error born from assuming all entities have internal states that resemble human internal states.

## What They Know

The beings possess knowledge—that much is certain. The question is whether they know that they know, whether knowledge exists for them as a separate category from behavior.

The Walkers know their paths. They navigate terrain, avoid obstacles (usually), adjust to conditions. This requires spatial awareness, memory, some form of processing. But is this knowledge, or is this merely pattern-following, instinct, programming?

Some beings demonstrate learning. An Emergent crystalline lattice, after being repeatedly shattered by Walker traffic, begins growing in a configuration that creates natural arches, allowing passage without collision. This is adaptation, which implies assessment of previous attempts, modification of behavior based on results. That is learning. That is knowledge of cause and effect.

The Watchers know—they must. They are observing, which requires distinguishing signal from noise, pattern from randomness. They are recording, which requires memory systems, storage, organization. The vertical column with its hundreds of eyes is seeing hundreds of simultaneous perspectives, integrating them into... what? A composite understanding? A database? A experience of the plateau that is richer and stranger than human perception can approach?

The Engine Inhabitants know their machinery intimately. The scarabs move with precision through complex three-dimensional environments, finding specific components, performing specific maintenance tasks. This requires spatial knowledge, technical knowledge, purpose-driven behavior. They know what needs to be cleaned, what needs to be adjusted, what is correct function and what is deviation.

But do they know why? Do they understand the engines' purpose, or are they simply programmed to maintain, the way immune system cells maintain a body without understanding what health is for?

The Hybrid-Transitionals might know the most, and that might be why they are most disturbing. If they were human once, they retain human knowledge—language, tool use, abstract reasoning—but cannot express it, cannot use it, trapped in Walker bodies that permit only slow procession. They know what they have lost, what they have become, and they continue processing anyway, moving through their appointed rounds while human memory and Walker instinct coexist in unresolvable tension.

Or they know nothing, remember nothing, and the human components are purely structural, cosmetic, meaningless.

The plateau itself might be knowledge—the entire system a kind of library or archive, with information stored in Walker paths, engine rhythms, Stillform positions. The glitches and breakdowns might be data corruption, or they might be the system accessing deep archives, briefly loading information into active reality before filing it away again.

Visitors might be learning from the plateau without realizing it. Prolonged observation might transfer knowledge through osmosis, dreams, subconscious pattern recognition. You leave the plateau thinking you learned nothing, but months later you understand engineering principles you were never taught, or you can navigate by sensing temperature gradients, or you find yourself moving in processional patterns, tracing invisible routes through your own city, and you realize the plateau taught you its ways and you are beginning, very slowly, to become.

## The Unobserved

The beings described are the observable ones, the forms that permit perception and categorization.

But there are others.

Sometimes you sense presence without seeing form—a feeling of being watched from an empty space, or the certainty that something large just passed behind you, though turning reveals nothing. These might be hallucinations, tricks of heat and isolation. Or they might be beings that exist in sensory ranges outside human perception: creatures that communicate in infrared, or whose bodies are primarily magnetic fields, or who vibrate at frequencies that affect the inner ear without producing sound.

The dreams that occur during sleep on the plateau—if sleep is possible there, if visitors sleep or fall into states that resemble sleep—contain beings. Forms that move through the dream-space, observing or interacting, leaving impressions that fade upon waking. Are these actual entities operating in dimensions that intersect with human consciousness during rest? Or are they the brain's attempt to process the plateau's strangeness, generating dream-beings as metaphors for experiences that defeat waking comprehension?

And there are statistical beings—entities known only through effects on other beings. Walkers that change paths for no visible reason might be avoiding something undetectable. Stillforms that show stress markers—heat damage, structural strain—might be defending against presences that cannot be seen but can be measured through their impacts.

The engine depths certainly contain beings never witnessed by surface visitors. The massive custodians glimpsed during reality breakdowns are likely only the largest of many forms that inhabit the lower levels, adapted to darkness and pressure and temperatures that would vaporize surface inhabitants. They might be so different from surface beings that if one emerged, it would not register as a living thing—might appear as an eruption of heat, a moving zone of spatial distortion, a sound without source.

And beyond the plateau, in the hardpan desert below, in the air above—are there beings there too? Forms that visit the plateau periodically, or that observe from distance, or that exist in the spaces between spaces, using the plateau as a node in a network that spans continents or worlds?

The Watchers' dish antenna is aimed at something. The Stillform tower is rotating toward some completion. These suggest interaction with entities or forces that are not local, not present, not observable by human visitors but nonetheless real, active, participating in whatever the plateau is accomplishing.

## The Long Duration

The beings here measure their existence in eons.

The Walkers with their geological pace might take ten thousand years to complete a single processional circuit. The Stillforms have maintained their positions for so long that they have become integrated into the plateau's structure—remove one and the whole system might collapse, or might not notice, or might reorganize around the absence over a period of centuries.

The engines have been running for—how long? Longer than human civilization, certainly. Possibly longer than the current configuration of continents. They have outlasted empires, species, geological epochs, continuing their operation through ice ages and impact events and changes in the composition of the atmosphere.

The beings are built for this duration. Their forms are not fragile, not temporary. They do not age in meaningful senses. Damage is either rapidly repaired or slowly incorporated, becoming part of the being's new configuration. Entropy affects them, but on time scales that make human mortality look like a brief flicker.

What does existence feel like across these durations? If the beings are conscious, is their experience of time stretched proportionally, so that a century passes subjectively in what feels like a human hour? Or is their consciousness continuous across thousands of years, accumulating experience, memory, awareness that spans geological time?

A Walker that has been processing for fifty thousand years—does it remember when it began? Does it remember previous iterations of the plateau, earlier configurations of the engine network, beings that have since transformed or vanished? Or is its memory continuously overwritten, maintaining only the recent past—where "recent" might mean the last millennium?

The Watchers might possess the deepest temporal awareness. Recording continuously across eons, they might contain within their crystalline structures or optical systems the entire history of the plateau: every Walker that ever processed, every engine that ever ran, every glitch and synchronization and transformation. They are history itself, made physical, observing in real-time while simultaneously maintaining complete archives of all time prior.

This suggests that for the beings here, individuality and continuity are different than for humans. A being that exists for a million years is not the same being at beginning and end—the Ship of Theseus rebuilt countless times—but might maintain some essential identity, some core pattern that persists through all transformations.

Or identity is irrelevant. What matters is the function being performed, and if a Walker ceases and a new Walker arises to take its position in the processional, the system doesn't care—the function continues, and the particular substrate carrying it out is interchangeable.

The beings are time made visible: duration given form, eons expressed as slow movement and persistent structure.

## Conclusion That Is Not Conclusion

This is what lives on the Calcine Processional plateau:

Walkers in their thousands, processing along invisible paths toward goals that might not exist. Stillforms maintaining their eternal positions, thinking thoughts that take centuries. Engine Inhabitants tending machinery in service of purposes that have outlived their makers. Transitionals shifting between states, being and non-being, form and process. Watchers recording everything, compiling histories that no one will read. Emergent forms testing new configurations, evolution in real-time. Hybrid beings incorporating human elements into alien structures. And the unobserved presences, the statistical entities, the inhabitants of depths and dimensions that permit no direct knowledge.

They do not need visitors. They do not require interpretation. They continue their functions with or without witness, indifferent to comprehension, existing in a mode that transcends purposes recognizable to human understanding.

They are what remains when time extends beyond meaning, when purpose outlasts the ability to articulate purpose, when being itself becomes a kind of doing and doing becomes the only form of being.

They are here. They have always been here. They will always be here, processing and maintaining and observing and emerging, until the engines stop—which they will not stop—or until the heat finally claims everything—which it cannot, because the engines fight entropy and the beings embody resistance to ending.

The plateau is not their home. The plateau is them, and they are it, and the distinction has long since dissolved into the white-gold heat, the constant noise, the slow infinitesimal movement through space that might be time, through time that might be space, continuing, always continuing, world without end.
