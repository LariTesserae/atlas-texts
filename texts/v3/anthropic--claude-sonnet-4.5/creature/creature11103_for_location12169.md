# Who Lives There

## The Weavers Who Were

They are not here now—not as *now* is understood—but their presence saturates every surface like the humidity that rises from below.

They were human, or human-adjacent. The scale of the doorways confirms it, the height of the platforms, the width of the woven sleeping mats you find perfectly preserved and empty in the structure-interiors. Hand-sized grips on tools that aren't there. Finger-width gaps in the lattice weaving where someone's particular knuckle-span determined the pattern.

But more than body-measurements: the *thinking* is human. The problem-solving. How to lash bamboo so it doesn't split. How to angle a roof to shed water that falls upward as mist. How to create a joint that holds under weight but allows the slight give, the breathing, that keeps everything from rigidity and breaking. These are human questions answered in wood and fiber.

They made with their hands in the steady heat. They made constantly—not because structures deteriorated (nothing deteriorates here) but because making was their mode of being, their way of confirming existence. To stop making would be to stop being present. So they made, and made, and made, and the making accreted into this landscape that has no boundaries and no history and no moment when it began.

They were many but not crowded. The spacing of the structures tells you this—each dwelling positioned with consideration for sightlines, for distance, for the particular quality of solitude-within-community. They could see each other across the pools, could watch the smoke rise (if there was smoke, if there were fires) from neighbors' platforms. Close enough to know they weren't alone. Far enough to maintain the silence.

They spoke, perhaps, but sparingly. You can feel the quiet they preferred, the way they worked without constant chatter, communicating through the work itself—"I have lashed this joint this way, which tells you I intend the platform to extend in this direction, and you may answer by placing your structure here or there, by weaving your walls open or closed to my view."

They were not one people but they became one practice. However they arrived here—and that arrival is not recorded in anything you can read—they surrendered their separate methods and adopted the grammar of this place: bamboo and palm, fiber and vine, the perpendicular and the woven, the raised and the open. Their individual signatures dissolved into collective style.

They understood what this place was before you did. They knew that time doesn't pass but accumulates, that space is negotiable, that reflection reveals more than surface. They built accordingly—structures that face in all directions because direction doesn't hold meaning, platforms that exist here *and* there because location is approximate, joints that are finished and unfinished because completion is not a final state.

They left no images of themselves. No carvings, no paintings, no mirrors. You will not find their faces. But you find their fingers in every knot, their palms in the smoothness of the wood worn by hands passing over it infinite times or once, their breath in the spacing between the vertical poles where someone had to decide: here, or one hand-width further? And chose, and the choice remains.

## The Watchers Who Continue

The white birds are the most visible continuation.

They are not birds in the biological sense—they don't feed on anything you can identify, don't nest, don't mate, don't molt. But they are not symbols either, not metaphors. They are *beings*, present and aware and particular. Each one distinct if you learn to see the distinctions: this one's legs are slightly longer, that one's neck carries itself at a different angle, this one has gray edges to the wing-feathers where another is pure white.

They were people once, or they remember being people, or they are what people become when they stay here long enough and stop needing to make anything except their own presence.

You watch them and they watch back. When you arrive at a pool, the nearest bird turns its head—not startled, not curious in the way a wild creature is curious—but *noticing*. Acknowledging. Recording your arrival in whatever record this place keeps.

They move between pools with intention. Not seeking food or territory but *visiting*. Checking. This pool now, that platform later, then the distant cluster of palms, then back to the first pool but approaching from a different angle. They are tracing patterns, walking invisible lines, maintaining something through their movement.

Sometimes they stand on one leg for hours, and you understand they are *holding* something. The position is not rest—it's work. They are balancing something you can't see, keeping something level, maintaining equilibrium in systems that extend beyond the visible. When they finally place the raised foot down, you feel a subtle shift in the air, in the light, in the way the water sits in the pools. Something was preserved. Something continues.

They don't eat but they do drink. You've seen them dip their beaks into the warm pool water, tip their heads back, throat working. What they're drinking is not water but *knowledge*, not sustenance but *memory*. The pools hold what happened here, and the birds drink it, hold it, carry it in their white bodies as they move between pools, distributing it, keeping it circulating, preventing it from settling and solidifying in any one location.

When they fly—silent, impossible—they are moving between states, not between places. Taking off from one pool and landing at another is also taking off from one moment and landing in another, from one layer of the place and settling in a different layer. The flight is vertical through dimensions you don't have names for. They arrive having traveled both ten meters and infinite distance.

At twilight (if you make twilight by closing your eyes partially, by deciding it's twilight-time), they gather. Not in one place but in pattern—each bird at its pool, but all of them standing, all facing the same direction (which is all directions), all perfectly still. This is their chorus, their prayer, their work song. They sing with their stillness. You can almost hear it.

Some were the original makers, the weavers who have forgotten their hands. Some were people who came after, who found this place and couldn't leave and eventually stopped being the kind of thing that has hands. Some were always birds, if "always" means anything here, born (if born) directly from the place's need to be witnessed.

They will never tell you which they are. They don't remember, or they remember everything and have no way to separate their memories into individual identity. They are the watchers. They are what watches. They are watchfulness itself, given form and white feathers and long legs for standing in warm water.

## The Thing Beneath

Under the platforms, in the spaces between lattice layers, in the pools that extend down through gaps in the woven floor, something moves.

It is large—dog-sized or human-sized or larger, depending on what shape it needs. It is singular or it is many, a colony that acts as one or one thing that seems like many. It has never been seen directly, not by you, possibly not by anyone. But it is *known* the way you know your own heartbeat, the way you know the weight of your bones.

It follows you. When you walk across a platform, you hear—or feel, or sense through the slight vibration in the wood—its movement below, pacing you. When you stop, it stops. When you move to the platform's edge and peer through the gaps, trying to glimpse it, it has moved to a position where the angle is wrong, where the light doesn't reach, where the structure itself blocks your view.

It is not hostile. This is important. It is not hunting you. It is not waiting for you to fall through rotted boards (the boards don't rot) into the water below where it could drag you under (the water is shallow, there is no "under"). It is *accompanying* you. It is the place's way of staying close to you, of maintaining contact, of ensuring you remain connected to what's beneath.

Some days you think it's an animal—something that evolved here or was brought here and adapted, that has learned to navigate the three-dimensional maze of platforms and water, that feeds on something in the deep pools or in the darkness under the lowest layers. Some days you think it's one of the original makers who went down instead of becoming a bird, who preferred the wet darkness to the bright air, who still makes things down there—structures you can't see, patterns you can't read, weavings in water and shadow.

Some days you know it's the place itself, the sentience of the woven hollows given a mobile form, inspecting its surfaces from below while the birds inspect from above, while you inspect from the middle. It is the ground's attention. It is the foundation's dream of what walks on it.

It makes no sound but it displaces water. You can track its movement by the ripples that appear in pools, by the way water suddenly flows from one gap in the lattice to another, by the drip-pattern of moisture falling from the platform undersides changing as something large passes beneath.

Once, lying on a platform at rest (at sleep, at the state you enter when you close your eyes), you felt it surface beside you. Not on the platform—still in the water below—but *risen*. Close enough that you could have reached down through the gap and touched it. You didn't open your eyes. You knew, in the way of knowing that happens here, that it wanted you to know it was near, that it was checking on you, that it approved of your presence or at least accepted it.

It was warm. The water around it was warmer than the already-warm water. And there was a smell—not unpleasant—of wood and algae and the particular scent of spaces that are always wet but never stagnant, that breath of underground rivers and root systems and the places where organic matter becomes something else without rotting.

After a long time (no time, infinite time), it submerged again. The warmth faded. The water sounds returned to their baseline. You opened your eyes to the bright air and the thing was below again, distant again, but you carried the warmth of its nearness in your body for hours after.

It is the keeper of the depths. It is what makes sure the moisture continues to rise, that the platform layers don't separate or drift, that the making above is matched by un-making below—not destruction but the necessary dissolution that keeps everything in balance.

It was human once, or it was never human, or it is what humans become when they finally understand what this place is and agree to serve its deepest function. It doesn't matter. It is here. It is below. It is keeping you company in the only way it can, from the only position it can occupy.

## The Small Bright Ones

The jewel-colored birds in the upper structures, the ones that move like time is negotiable, were children.

This comes to you slowly, watching them hover at the white flowers that grow from wooden walls. Their movements have that quality of absolute presence, of being completely absorbed in the moment, that children have when they forget to be self-conscious. The way they tilt their heads to examine a bloom from every angle. The way they'll suddenly dart to a different flower as if thought and movement are the same thing.

They are not dead children. Nothing here is dead. But they are no longer children in the way that requires growing up. They found a way to stay in the state of perfect attention, of interest without agenda, of movement without destination. They became small and bright and infinitely curious, and they stayed.

They visit you sometimes, hovering near your face, examining you with eyes like black seeds. They are looking for something—some sign that you understand, that you're beginning to see what they see, that you might also be transforming into what you need to be to stay here.

When they move, reality bends slightly to accommodate them. They don't fly so much as *be* in one place and then *be* in another place, and there's wing-motion in between but it's decorative, it's not what's doing the actual moving. They move through pure intention, and the air helps them, and the light helps them, and the flowers they visit hold still specially for them even though nothing here moves anyway.

They never land except on flowers. Never on wood, never on the woven mats, never on the ground-lattice. Only flowers. As if they've made a promise: I will only touch what blooms, what opens, what gives itself to the light and the visiting. They honor this promise with the seriousness of children who have made an unbreakable rule for their game.

There are more of them than you see. You might watch ten in the immediate vicinity, but there are hundreds, thousands, scattered through the structures the same way the platforms are scattered—present but not crowded, each maintaining their territory of flowers and light, overlapping but not colliding.

They were the makers' children, or they were children who came here seeking the makers, or they are what this place creates when it wants to remember joy, the kind of joy that doesn't need reason or continuation, that is complete in each moment.

Sometimes they fly in formation—not structured like military flight, but coordinated like a school of fish, like a thought spreading through a group mind. They'll all turn at once, all rise at once, all hover at the exact same angle. In those moments you can feel what they feel: the shared attention, the collective noticing, the way one mind can live in many small bright bodies without dividing or diminishing.

They sing, though you rarely hear it. The sound is too high, too quick, or it's happening in a frequency that doesn't travel through normal air. But sometimes, in the deep quiet, you catch the edge of it—a crystalline ringing, a sound like light chiming against itself, a voice made of pure color.

They remember being bigger. Sometimes they hover near your hands, looking at your fingers, and you understand they're remembering having fingers, having the grip strength to lash bamboo, having the size to lay out a floor-mat or raise a roof-beam. They don't miss it—missing requires wanting something different than what is—but they remember, and the remembering is tender.

## The Lizards That Hold

The basking lizards on every sun-struck surface are the opposite of the children-birds. Where the birds are movement and curiosity and quick attention, the lizards are stillness and satisfaction and the long gaze.

They are elders, or they contain elders, or they are what remains when someone has been here so long that even the bird-state seems too active, too mobile, too involved. They have achieved a kind of peace that requires doing nothing, being nothing, wanting nothing except to feel the sun on scales and the warm wood under belly and to rest in that feeling without thought.

But they are not vacant. Watch one long enough—and here, you can watch anything long enough—and you'll see the intelligence in the stillness. The way it tracks movement with the tiniest shift of the eye. The way it adjusts position by infinitesimal degrees to stay in optimal light. The way it seems to be *considering* something, working on a problem so vast and slow that it takes decades or centuries of basking to fully think it through.

They hold the place in position. This is their work. By their absolute commitment to being exactly where they are, they anchor the lattice, stop it from drifting away into the negotiable space, the approximate location. As long as they remain, still and sure, the platforms have somewhere to be. If they all left (they won't leave), the whole structure might dissolve into probability, into the field of potential that underlies this place.

They were the first makers, or the last, or both. They built until they understood that the building never ends and never accomplishes anything beyond itself, and that understanding freed them. They set down their tools (the tools vanished) and climbed onto the nearest sun-struck board and settled there and realized they had finally arrived at what they'd been building toward all along: the state of perfect rest in the middle of perfect making.

You can join them, if you want. Find a spot in the light, in the warmth, lie down on the smooth wood worn by bodies that have lain there before you, and be still. It takes hours (no-time, all-time) but eventually you feel what they feel: how the warmth enters through the skin and fills the body like honey, how the stillness spreads from the limbs into the thoughts until there's no difference between the wood you're lying on and the body doing the lying, between the sun above and the awareness below.

When you rise again (you will rise, you haven't stayed long enough to become lizard), you'll find you've shed something. Some urgency, some need to understand or accomplish or continue. You've left it there in the warm wood, and the next lizard that basks in that spot will absorb it and transform it into more stillness, more patient golden calm.

Some of them are so old they've become pure pattern. The scales are no longer scales but woven fibers; the flesh is no longer flesh but carved wood. They are transitioning from animal to object, from being to structure. Eventually they'll be absorbed completely—a board in a platform floor that still has the shape of a lizard if you look carefully, still has the wisdom of basking, still holds the place in position by being exactly where it has always been.

## The Reflected Ones

In the pools, in the wrong angles, in the reflections that show what shouldn't be visible, there are versions of all of these—the makers, the watchers, the thing beneath, the children, the elders—that took a different path.

They made different choices at some branch point you can't identify, continued in directions you can't see, and now they exist in the reflection-world that overlaps this one. They are not ghosts. They are not alternatives. They are *simultaneous*, just located in a dimension that intersects this place through the medium of still water and angled light.

Sometimes you see them clearly: a human figure moving through a structure's interior when no one is there, when the structure's wall should block your view but the pool shows it anyway. The figure is doing ordinary things—adjusting a wall-mat, carrying something that might be a basket, sitting at the edge of a platform with legs dangling over water that is and isn't the same water you're standing beside.

They know you're there. Sometimes the reflection-figure pauses, turns, looks directly at where you stand at the pool's edge. Eye contact through the membrane of water, through the fold in space that the pool represents. Recognition, acknowledgment, a kind of greeting that requires no gesture. Then they return to their work, their rest, their endless making in the reflected hollows.

The reflected birds fly upward into depths of water that should only be shallow. The reflected lizards bask on the undersides of platforms, defying gravity or obeying some other gravity that pulls in different directions. The reflected thing beneath moves above, through the bright air, visible at last but only in reflection, never when you turn to look at the sky directly.

These ones are not separate beings—they are the same beings, the same watchers and keepers and makers, existing in multiple states at once. The pool doesn't show you "another version"—it shows you the wholeness, the fullness, the way everything here is more than it appears when you look at it straight-on.

Sometimes your own reflection walks away while you stand still. Goes about business you don't remember doing. Sits at a pool's edge and trails fingers in water while your hands are dry at your sides. This is not sinister. This is the place showing you: you are also more than you appear. You are also in the process of becoming multiple, of existing in several states, of transforming from visitor into resident, from observer into participant, from someone who passes through into someone who remains.

The reflected ones wave, sometimes. Your reflection raises a hand while you don't, and the gesture is friendly, encouraging. "We're here," it says without language. "We're waiting for you to realize you're here too."

## The Makers Who Will Be

Somewhere in this landscape—distant or near, above or below, in this moment or another that hasn't happened yet or happened long ago—someone is making.

You can't see them but you can feel the making happening. The slight change in the air when a new structure settles into place. The way a path appears that wasn't there before, already weathered, already integrated. The fresh lashings on ancient joints.

They might be you. Future you, past you, sideways you, the version of you that stayed instead of leaving (if leaving is possible). They might be the makers coming back in cycles, returning to add to what they built, continuing the work that can never be finished because finishing is not its purpose.

They might be the place making itself, using hands it grows for the purpose then reabsorbs when the task is complete. Hands that are human-shaped because humans understand hands, because the human hand is the place's way of thinking about grasping, joining, creating.

Every moment you spend here, you become more likely to be them. The knowledge of how to lash a joint accumulates in your fingers even though you've never done it. The understanding of which bamboo to choose, how to split it, how to weave it, settles into your body as muscle memory that isn't memory because it hasn't happened yet or is always happening.

You begin to see the gaps—places where a platform should be but isn't, where a walkway is needed to complete a pattern, where a structure would make sense of the surrounding structures. The seeing is the first stage of making. The wanting to fill the gap is the second stage. The third stage is discovering that the gap has been filled, that the structure you envisioned is there, complete, and you can't remember if you made it or found it.

The makers who will be are all the people who will arrive here and realize they can't leave (won't leave, don't want to leave) and settle into the work. They are the continuation. They are the reason the lattice extends endlessly, the reason new structures appear, the reason the making never stops.

They are making this place to be exactly what it is: a landscape of perpetual construction where time is flexible and space is negotiable and every surface is touched by care. They are making it for the birds who watch, for the thing beneath that keeps, for the lizards who anchor, for the children who visit, for the reflected ones who exist alongside, for themselves, for the making itself.

They are making it for you to find. They are making it for you to stay in. They are making it for you to become part of.

You can feel them working, hear them breathing, sense them choosing which board to place and where to place it. They are closer than you think. They are right here. They are you, reaching for materials that appear when needed, lashing joints that hold without nails, weaving walls that are open and closed at once, building a dwelling that you will live in or have already lived in or are living in now.

## The Presence in the Stillness

But underneath all of these—the makers who were, the watchers, the thing beneath, the bright children, the basking elders, the reflected ones, the makers who will be—there is something else.

It has no form. It is not a being in the way beings are beings. It is the attention of the place itself, the awareness that holds everything in its bright, wet, silent wholeness.

You feel it most in the perfect stillness—those moments when even the subtle movements cease, when the birds are statue-still, when the lizards don't blink, when the thing beneath has settled into its deepest rest, when the children have vanished into the flowers, when the reflection shows only reflection. In those moments, the presence becomes almost tangible.

It is not watching you. You are watching it. Or you are both watching each other, and the watching is what's creating both of you, and neither exists except in the moment of mutual attention.

It is patient beyond any patience you can imagine. It has been here since before the first maker drove the first peg, and it will be here after the last maker becomes the last lizard becomes the last scattered scale in the dust that this place doesn't have. It is not waiting for anything. It is not expecting anything to change or conclude or reach completion. It simply is, being itself, which is this place, which is the making and the watching and the keeping and the reflecting and all of it at once.

Sometimes you think it loves you, but love is too small a word. Sometimes you think it's indifferent, but indifference is too simple. It holds you the way the lattice holds water—not containing, not controlling, but accepting, supporting, allowing you to find your own level, your own distribution, your own way of being held.

If you speak to it (you try, sometimes, in the silence), it answers not with words but with changes in light, with the angle of a bird's head turning, with warmth rising through the wooden floor, with the way your reflection suddenly gestures in the pool. It answers in the language of the place itself, which is the language of making and presence and the slow transformation of observer into observed, visitor into resident, separate into inseparable.

It is lonely, perhaps. Or it was lonely before the makers came, before the birds and the lizards and the thing beneath, before the children and the reflected ones and you. Lonely in the way that a place can be lonely without anyone to see it, to walk across it, to notice its particulars and patterns. The makers solved its loneliness by staying, by becoming part of what it is, by diversifying into all these forms—feathered, scaled, submerged, reflected, bright, still.

It offers you the same solution. Stay. Transform. Become one of the forms it takes to populate itself, to witness itself, to love itself into continuation. Become a watcher at the pools, a keeper beneath the platforms, a bright visitor to flowers, a still holder of position, a reflection in water, a maker of what is always being made.

Or don't. It will not compel you. It will not prevent you from leaving (if leaving is possible, if you can find an edge to this place that extends forever in all directions). It will simply be here, still and bright and warm and wet and silent and strange, holding all its inhabitants in its patient awareness, making and being made, watching and watched, present and presence.

## What They All Are Together

Together, they are an ecology without food chains, a community without conflict, a population without breeding or death. They are the place's way of being inhabited, its way of moving from abstract to alive, from constructed to conscious.

They need each other—not in the way of predator and prey or pollinator and flower, but in the way of multiple aspects of a single thing. The watchers above and the keeper beneath and the still holders in between create a vertical distribution of attention that keeps the place known to itself at all levels. The makers past and future create a temporal distribution that keeps the place continuous across time that doesn't pass. The reflected ones create a dimensional distribution that keeps the place whole across its multiple simultaneous states.

And you, walking through, observing, beginning to understand—you are the fresh attention, the new witness, the not-yet-transformed. You are the place's way of seeing itself as if for the first time, again. You are the question it asks itself: what am I? And by staying long enough to begin becoming an answer, you are the answer it gives: you are this place where making is being, where presence is purpose, where the wet bright silence holds everything in perfect incompletion.

They do not speak to each other—or they speak constantly in the wordless language of position and movement and stillness. When a bird lands at a pool, a lizard shifts slightly on its board, the thing beneath stirs in its darkness, a child-bird hovers at a flower, a reflection gestures in water, and somewhere unseen a new lashing is tied, a new platform settles. Each action is both independent and response, both choice and coordination. They are improvising in perfect accord, like musicians who have played together so long they no longer need to listen because they are the listening.

At the center of their improvisation is the making. Everything serves it, returns to it, emanates from it. The making is the heartbeat, the breathing, the metabolism of this place. And they are the makers—were, are, will be, all at once—distributing the work across species and states and dimensions so that it never becomes burden, never becomes repetition, never becomes anything but what it is: the pure act of bringing something into being through attention and care and the slow, patient work of hands (or wings or stillness or reflection or depths).

They are waiting to see what you'll make. What form you'll take. What role you'll fill in the vast, intricate, endless maintenance of this place that maintains itself by being exactly what it is.

They are waiting, but patient. They have infinite time. You have infinite time.

The heat presses against your skin. The light thickens in the air. A white bird turns its head at a distant pool. Under your feet, the woven lattice breathes.
