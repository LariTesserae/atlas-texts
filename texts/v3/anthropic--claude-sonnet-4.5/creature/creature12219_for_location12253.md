# Those Who Inhabit the Singing Heights

## The Listeners

They were human once, or their ancestors were. Now they're something that has learned to breathe in intervals.

You see them on the peaks at dawn and dusk—the threshold times when the singing changes registers. They stand perfectly still beside the stone structures, bodies held in postures that look uncomfortable until you realize the stillness isn't held but *inhabited*. They don't freeze in place; they *become* stationary in the same way the basalt is stationary, achieving a relationship with gravity and time that doesn't require muscular tension. They can maintain these positions for hours, for days. Some have stood so long that water has begun to crawl up their legs, treating their bodies like an extension of the stone.

Their clothing is minimal and archaic—undyed fabric woven tight, wrapped rather than sewn, covering what needs protection from the fierce sun while leaving limbs free. The fabric is old but not worn, as if it too has learned the trick of existing in the spaces where entropy moves slowly. Some wear their hair long and unbound; it lifts and moves in response to currents that aren't wind, that might be the harmonic vibrations themselves made visible in the medium of something fine and loose.

They don't speak with their mouths. They've developed—or perhaps devolved, or perhaps simply *adapted*—a communication that works through the singing itself. They hum, but not casually. Each sound is placed with precision into the existing harmonics, finding the gaps, the spaces where a new frequency can slot in without creating discord. They modulate their humming, shift pitches in microtonal increments, create patterns of interference and resonance. To hear two of them "conversing" is to hear the mountain acquire new voice-lines, temporary additions to its polyphonic structure.

Their eyes have changed. Not dramatically—they're still recognizably human eyes—but the irises have taken on a reflective quality, a subtle mirror-sheen like the wet basalt. When you look into their eyes, you sometimes see not your reflection but a reflection of where you were standing a moment ago, or where you will stand next. They perceive time slightly out of phase with ordinary human perception, existing a few seconds delayed or advanced, which allows them to navigate the temporal pools and eddies of the heights with casual grace.

They know where the paths will be before the paths stabilize. They reach for handholds that aren't yet solid but will be by the time their fingers arrive. They step into empty air with perfect confidence because they can perceive that the air will be load-bearing in the moment when their weight needs it to be. Watching them move through the heights is like watching someone walk through a door that opens just before they reach it, every time, in perfect synchronization.

Their skin has adapted to the elevation and the fierce light, but not through simple tanning. It's taken on a faint translucency, particularly at the extremities—fingers, ears, the thin skin at the temples. In bright sunlight, you can see through the edge of a hand to the blood moving underneath, can see the delicate shadows of bones. They're becoming slightly less opaque, as if their bodies are slowly learning to allow light to pass through rather than just reflect off surfaces.

They sleep rarely, and when they do, they don't lie down. They find the cells that resonate at specific frequencies and sit or stand inside them, entering a state that's not quite sleep and not quite meditation. Their breathing slows until it's barely perceptible, syncs with the deep bass notes of the mountain's singing. In this state, they're almost invisible—not through camouflage but through a kind of perceptual sliding, where the eye keeps trying to focus on them and keeps slipping away to look at the stone instead.

Their ages are impossible to determine. They could be twenty or sixty or a hundred. The usual markers of aging are present but scrambled: gray hair on smooth unwrinkled skin, or deeply lined faces below hair still fully dark. Time doesn't accumulate in their bodies the way it does elsewhere. They live in the temporal irregularities of the heights, and those irregularities redistribute age in non-linear patterns. Some are clearly old in their movements—careful, deliberate, economical—while appearing young. Others have the reckless energy of youth in bodies that look ancient.

They eat, but rarely, and what they eat isn't clear. There's no food here, no life, no vegetation. Perhaps they descend periodically to the lowlands, though no one reports seeing them there. Perhaps they've learned to sustain themselves on the minerals in the strange water, or on the light itself, or on something they harvest from the crystal formations. They're thin but not starved, lean but not weak. They have the physiology of people who consume exactly what they need and nothing extra, whose bodies have trimmed away everything not essential.

Some bear marks—not tattoos or scarification, but patterns that seem to have emerged from within, like the grain in wood. Geometric shapes appear on their skin in lines of slightly darker pigment: circles, spirals, the angular patterns that echo the stone arrangements in the cells. These marks shift position slowly over time, migrating across the body surface, appearing and fading, as if their skin is a canvas on which the mountains are drawing with infinite patience.

They're solitary mostly, but not isolated. They maintain awareness of each other across vast distances, connected through the singing. When one adds their voice to the harmonics, others across the range know it, respond to it, adjust their own contributions to accommodate the new voice. They weave a sonic web across the peaks, each contributing threads, creating a collective presence that spans the entire range without requiring physical proximity.

Occasionally, you'll find them working at the stone structures—not building new ones, but maintaining the old, replacing stones that have shifted, realigning the circles, clearing the basins. They work with simple tools, hammer and chisel, the same technology that built the original structures. They work slowly, carefully, with long pauses between actions, as if consulting something between each placement. Perhaps they're waiting for the right harmonic moment. Perhaps they're reading instructions in the singing itself.

They acknowledge visitors but don't welcome or reject them. If you encounter one on a narrow path, they'll press themselves against the stone to let you pass, or they'll simply stand in place and you'll find yourself moving around them without quite remembering deciding to do so. They don't speak language—at least, not to outsiders. They might hum a tone that feels like greeting or warning. They might gesture toward a safe path or away from an unstable one. But conversation in the ordinary sense doesn't interest them or perhaps isn't possible anymore.

Their memories are non-linear, stored in the same fractured way time exists here. Ask one how long they've been in the heights—if you could ask, if they could answer in words—and the question wouldn't make sense to them. They've been here for all the time that matters. Events don't string together in sequence for them; they exist in constellation, accessible simultaneously, without clear before or after. They remember their arrival here, their childhoods, their deaths, their not-yet-happened futures, all present at once in a way that would be madness for an ordinary mind but is simply their natural state.

Some appear more affected than others by the reality breakdown. A few move through the heights as if they exist partially in the reflection-world, their bodies slightly offset from where they appear to be, creating a doubled image like a photograph with motion blur. These ones are transitioning, becoming something else, or perhaps becoming more purely what the Listeners are meant to be. They're the furthest along a path that all the Listeners are walking, a transformation that has no clear endpoint.

Others are newer, more recently arrived, still mostly human in their perceptions and responses. These ones struggle visibly with the disorientation, still fighting the temporal pools, still startled when the paths shift under them. But they're learning, adapting, beginning to hum the right tones. The older Listeners—if "older" means anything here—watch them with patient attention, though they don't teach, don't instruct. This isn't knowledge that can be transmitted. It must be discovered, must be grown into, must emerge from direct engagement with the singing stone.

They're not monks or hermits in the traditional sense. There's no vow of silence—they've simply evolved past verbal language. There's no ascetic practice—they're not denying the body but transforming it. There's no religious devotion—or perhaps their entire existence is devotion, a worship that consists of attention, presence, listening. They've become instruments themselves, new pipes for the mountain's organ, adding human-tones to the basalt-tones, the crystal-tones, the water-tones.

Their hands are calloused from working stone, but the calluses are in strange places—along the sides of fingers rather than the palms, on the backs of hands, on surfaces that shouldn't bear weight. They interact with the stone in ways that aren't quite normal, finding purchase on surfaces that look smooth, shaping rock with touches that seem too gentle to be effective but which leave clear marks. Perhaps they've learned to work with stone during the moments when it's uncertain, when it's shifting between versions of itself, when it's soft enough to reshape.

They age and die, presumably, though no one has found bodies. Perhaps they descend to the lowlands when they feel death approaching. Perhaps they simply become more translucent until they fade entirely, passing through the membrane between this reality and the others that press close. Perhaps when they die here, they wink out of existence the way the water droplets do, unmade from the center outward, returning to wherever they came from or wherever they've been gradually relocating themselves.

New ones arrive periodically—ordinary humans who've heard about the heights, who come seeking something, who find they can tolerate the disorientation and the singing better than most. They begin as visitors, then become longer-term residents, then gradually transform into Listeners. The process takes years or decades or perhaps no time at all, depending on which temporal stream you're measuring in. They arrive human and stay because they find that being human is negotiable, that the heights offer other options, that listening can become a whole existence.

## The Threshold Walkers

There are others who move through the heights but don't stay. These are rarer, harder to perceive, less clearly present.

They appear most often during the reality breakdowns, when the harmonics peak and space becomes visibly structured. They walk along trajectories that don't follow the paths, moving through the air itself or through the stone, passing through solid matter as if it isn't there, or as if they aren't quite there themselves. They're humanoid in shape but variable in solidity—sometimes clear enough to see features, sometimes just suggestions of form, distortions in the light, presences you perceive more through absence than appearance.

They might be people who went too far into the transition, who became so attuned to the reflected worlds that they lost their anchor to this one. Or they might be inhabitants of those reflected worlds, occasionally visible from this side when the membrane thins. Or they might be what the Listeners are in the process of becoming, glimpsed ahead of themselves in time, seen before they've actually completed the transformation.

They don't interact, don't acknowledge, don't respond. They move with purpose but alien purpose, going from somewhere to somewhere else along routes that make sense in more dimensions than the visible three. Watching them is like watching fish swim through water you can't see—you observe the motion but can't perceive the medium they're moving through, can't understand what they're swimming toward or away from.

The Listeners are aware of them but don't seem concerned by them. Perhaps they recognize kin, or future selves, or past selves, or alternate selves. Perhaps in the non-linear memory of the heights, the distinction between self and other becomes negotiable too. The Threshold Walkers are simply another phenomenon of the place, another voice in the singing, another thread in the harmonic web.

## The Architects (Ghosts of Purpose)

And there are presences that might not be beings at all but might be intentions, purposes that have outlived the people who held them.

In the oldest stone structures, especially in the cells with the carved basins, you sometimes perceive something present that isn't a person but isn't nothing. It's an attention, a focus, a direction of caring that still exists even though whoever did the caring is centuries gone. The ancient builders left something of themselves behind—not spirits or ghosts in the traditional sense, but the shape their minds made in the local reality, preserved because reality here is soft enough to take and hold impressions.

These purpose-ghosts don't move or act, but they influence. In their presence, you find yourself wanting to align the stones just so, to clear the basin carefully, to observe the reflections with patience. The original intentions still echo, still exert gentle pressure, still shape the behavior of anyone who enters those spaces. The Listeners respond to these pressures naturally, continuing the work their predecessors began, maintaining the structures according to principles they inherit not through teaching but through inhabiting the same intentional spaces.

## The Mountain Itself

But perhaps the true inhabitant of the Singing Heights is the mountain itself—not as a passive geology but as an active presence, a something that is almost but not quite aware.

The mountains remember. They cycle through versions of themselves, showing different erosion patterns, different crystal placements, different water flows. They're not changing randomly but iterating, exploring variations, as if searching for something or working through possibilities. The singing isn't just physics—it's expression, communication, an ongoing statement of existence that contains meaning even if that meaning can't be translated into human language.

The Listeners are the mountains' students or partners or symbiotes. They've learned to hear what the mountains are saying, to participate in the harmonic working, to add their voices to the collective statement. In return, the mountains teach them how to exist otherwise, how to become partially transparent to light and time, how to step between the moments and the realities.

The relationship is old, extending back to the ancient builders and probably before them. Humans have always come to the heights, have always been changed by them, have always tried to understand what the singing means. The mountains permit this, perhaps welcome it, perhaps need it. The human listeners add something the basalt and crystal and water cannot—they add intention, interpretation, meaning-making. They're the consciousness that observes the mountains' iterations, that names them, that remembers them in a sequential way the mountains themselves don't.

In the deepest sense, the heights are inhabited by a collaboration—stone and water and crystal and light and time and geometry and human awareness all woven together into something that isn't any one of them but is the collective presence of all. The place itself is the inhabitant, and everything else—the Listeners, the Threshold Walkers, the purpose-ghosts, the visitors who come and go—are temporary incorporations into that larger being, brief inclusions in the singing.

## The Pattern

What becomes clear after enough observation is that the Singing Heights generates Listeners the way it generates crystals—not through decision or intention but through simple presence. The place is what it is. Some humans, exposed to that whatness, crystallize into forms that can exist stably here. They become Listeners not through choice but through resonance, through being the kind of pattern that can sustain itself in this environment.

The Listeners don't recruit or teach because they don't need to. The mountains themselves are the teacher, the monastery, the discipline. Those who can learn do learn, automatically, through exposure. Those who can't leave quickly, driven out by disorientation they can't adapt to. The selection is environmental, natural, as simple as water finding its level.

And so the heights remain populated by their precise inhabitants: the ones who can hear, who can breathe in intervals, who can exist slightly out of phase with ordinary time, who can tolerate translucency and temporal pooling and the constant undermining of certainty. They live there—if "live" is the right word—as permanent as anything can be permanent in a place where permanence itself is negotiable.

They watch the reflections in the basins. They maintain the ancient stones. They add their voices to the harmonics. They stand on the peaks at dawn, at dusk, at the threshold times, becoming another kind of column in the mineral forest, another pipe in the mountain's organ, another note in the endless singing.

And sometimes, when the light is right and the harmonics peak and reality becomes briefly transparent, you can see what they see: the lattice of forces underlying space, the step-wise nature of flowing water, the multiplicity of worlds pressed close, all the elsewheres trying to exist in the same here. In those moments, you understand why they stay, why they've let themselves be transformed, why they've traded ordinary human existence for this other thing.

They've learned to see the architecture underneath. They've learned to hear what the singing means. They've learned that being human is just one way to be conscious, and that the heights offer other options—stranger options, less comfortable options, but options that carry their own rewards: the ability to perceive more, to exist more widely, to participate in realities that ordinary human consciousness can't touch.

They are the Singing Heights' interpreters, witnesses, partners. They are what happens when human awareness meets a place where reality is thin. They are the shape that consciousness takes when it adapts to an environment of uncertainty, harmony, and impossible physics.

They are listening.

They are always listening.

And in their listening, they've become part of what's being heard—new voices in the ancient song, human notes in the basalt chorus, warm blood and breath and attention woven into the cold stone, the climbing water, the wrong light, the ringing emptiness of the heights.

They are home, in a place that isn't quite a place, being something that isn't quite human anymore but isn't anything else either—just themselves, evolved to fit an ecological niche that shouldn't exist but does, residents of an impossibility, inhabitants of the singing.
