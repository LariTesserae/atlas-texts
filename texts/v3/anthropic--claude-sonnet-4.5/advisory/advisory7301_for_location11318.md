# Preparation Guide for The Hanging Reservoir

## Physical Preparations

### Breathing Apparatus and Life Support
You need technology that allows indefinite underwater breathing, but here's what's crucial: don't trust conventional scuba equipment. The Reservoir operates on negotiable physics, and "breathing" may become something your body does differently once you're there. Bring the best closed-circuit rebreather you can access, but also prepare yourself mentally for the possibility that at some point, you might simply *breathe water* and find it works. This transition—if it happens—will be one of the most psychologically challenging moments of your journey. Practice breath control and meditation extensively beforehand. Practice staying calm when your body is doing something that should kill you.

### Thermal Protection
Eight to ten degrees Celsius, constant, inescapable. That's 46-50°F—cold enough to kill through hypothermia, but not quickly. You'll need:

- A high-quality wetsuit, 7mm minimum, or preferably a dry suit with excellent thermal undergarments
- Multiple thermal layers that work when wet
- Neoprene hood, gloves, and booties—heat loss through extremities will be your constant enemy
- Chemical heat packs as backup, though test whether they function normally there first

But understand: you will never be warm. You will never be comfortable. The cold will become your permanent companion, a constant pressure on your consciousness. You need to make peace with being cold before you go. Spend time in cold water. Spend time in walk-in freezers. Train your body and mind to function while persistently chilled. Learn the difference between uncomfortable cold and dangerous cold. Your life may depend on recognizing that threshold.

### Buoyancy and Mobility
Standard buoyancy compensators may behave strangely in water that "prefers" to be suspended. Bring adjustable weight systems, but prepare for them to work unpredictably. The water doesn't follow normal rules—you might sink when you should float, or vice versa.

More importantly: you'll need to swim everywhere. There are no walkways, no ropes between structures. You'll be swimming through complete darkness, navigating by faint, slowly pulsing lights. This requires:

- Excellent swimming stamina (train to swim for hours)
- Strong three-dimensional orientation skills (practice in caves or zero-visibility conditions)
- Powerful legs for propulsion
- Fins, probably, though again—test whether they work as expected

The darkness is *profound*. Practice swimming in absolute darkness. Have someone guide you through a dark pool or flooded cave system with your eyes closed. You need to develop confidence in moving through space when you cannot see more than a few meters in any direction.

## Psychological Preparations

### The Void Below
Three kilometers of water beneath you, then darkness with no promised ground. This isn't theoretical danger—it's perceptual assault. Many people cannot handle extended time above profound depths. The void *looks back at you*.

If you have any acrophobia, address it before you go. But understand that this is different from standing on a tall building. There, you have ground under your feet. Here, you're suspended in water suspended in nothing, and your primitive brain will *know* this.

Practice visualization: imagine yourself floating in dark space with infinite depth below. Sit with that image. Breathe with it. Let it become familiar rather than terrifying. The void is not malicious—it's simply there. It cannot hurt you unless you let fear drive you into fatal decisions.

### Profound Solitude
The town appears uninhabited. You may be utterly alone except for rare, incomprehensible fauna that will not acknowledge you. The silence is nearly total. The darkness is almost complete. You are in a small bubble of structured space surrounded by vast emptiness.

If you've never experienced true isolation, this will break something in you. Prepare by:

- Spending time in genuine solitude (not just alone-in-your-apartment solitude, but days without human contact)
- Learning to be comfortable with your own thoughts for extended periods
- Understanding what your mind does under sensory deprivation
- Developing internal mental practices (meditation, memory palaces, mental composition)

Bring nothing that requires power or connection to the outside world for entertainment. Bring perhaps a waterproof journal and pencils. Bring your thoughts. Learn to befriend silence before the silence becomes total.

### The Strangeness
This cannot be overstated: the Reservoir is *wrong* in fundamental ways. Water that shouldn't hang, hangs. Structures that shouldn't stand, stand. Creatures move in ways that violate physics you've trusted your whole life. The clicks come from nowhere. The lights appear without source. The technology operates on principles you cannot grasp.

Some people cannot tolerate sustained wrongness. Their minds rebel. They need the world to make sense, and when it persistently doesn't, something breaks.

You must cultivate radical acceptance of mystery. Practice this:
- Study quantum mechanics not to understand it, but to become comfortable with non-intuitive reality
- Spend time with art and ideas that resist interpretation
- Meditate on koans—statements that refuse to resolve into meaning
- Learn to observe without needing to explain

The Reservoir will not explain itself. It will never make sense in the framework you bring. You must be able to exist in a place that refuses narrative coherence, that simply *is* in ways that contradict your models of reality.

## Navigation and Safety

### Orientation Systems
The town has twenty-seven structures distributed through a lens of water 400 meters across, 200 meters deep. The joint-lights pulse on 7-8 minute cycles, uncoordinated. This is your navigation system—and it's deliberately difficult.

Bring:
- Waterproof dive lights with redundant batteries (though they may attract or repel the fauna—observe carefully)
- A mapping system (waterproof paper and grease pencils, perhaps, or a specialized underwater slate)
- Compass and depth gauge (but verify they work normally—magnetic and pressure fields may be distorted)
- High-visibility marker lines or small lights you can deploy to mark routes between structures

Plan to spend your first hours—possibly your first day—doing nothing but mapping. Count the structures. Note their positions relative to each other. Identify the brightest joint-lights. Find the structure with the control panel. Locate the vertical beam at the edge. Create a mental (and physical) map before you need it in an emergency.

Learn the timing of the light pulses. Develop a feel for the cycle so you can predict when a structure will glow brightest. This is how you'll navigate in the darkness.

### Emergency Protocols
You are three kilometers above possible ground, inside water that shouldn't exist, surrounded by structures built on impossible principles. Normal emergency protocols (surface immediately, signal for help) are irrelevant.

Instead:
- Identify the most central structure and designate it your rally point
- Always know the direction to that structure
- If you become disoriented, stop moving, float neutral, wait for light pulses, and slowly navigate back to known structures
- Never attempt to leave the water volume—the void beyond is not survivable, or if it is, you have no way to know the rules there

The greatest danger is probably panic-driven decisions. You cannot surface to safety. There is no safety except the strange stability of the town itself. Practice emergency scenarios mentally: what if your light fails? What if you become completely disoriented? What if your thermal protection fails? In each case, the answer is: stay calm, conserve energy, return to the known structure, and wait.

## Technology and Tools

### What to Bring
- Multiple redundant light sources (dive lights, chemical lights, LED markers)
- Comprehensive thermal protection with backups
- Navigation tools (compass, depth gauge, slate for mapping)
- Waterproof markers or tags for labeling structures
- A knife or cutting tool (for emergency purposes)
- Minimal first aid supplies (though what injuries you might sustain here are unpredictable)
- Waterproof journal for observations
- Timing device for measuring light cycles, sound patterns

### What Not to Bring
- Electronics that require GPS, radio, or satellite connection (they won't work)
- Anything that depends on normal physics (may malfunction)
- Heavy equipment (you need mobility more than tools)
- Weapons (nothing here has demonstrated hostility, and aggression may change your relationship with the environment in unpredictable ways)

### Testing Protocol
Before you depend on any technology in the Reservoir, test it first. Shine your light—does it illuminate normally or does the water absorb it strangely? Check your compass—does it point consistently? Try your depth gauge—does it register three kilometers of depth below you, or something else?

Spend your first hours doing systematic tests. Document what works and what doesn't. The Reservoir's physics may be negotiable, but they're not arbitrary—there are rules, even if they're not your rules.

## Interaction Guidelines

### The Structures
They respond to touch, sometimes to proximity, occasionally to intention. They produce tones, vibrations, possibly other effects you cannot detect. Approach them carefully:

- Touch gently and observe the response
- Press the control panel indentations one at a time, with long pauses between
- Note which structures respond to which interactions
- Never manipulate multiple systems simultaneously until you understand single systems
- Treat everything as potentially functional—this is not a ruin, it's infrastructure

The town appears to be maintained by something or maintaining itself. Don't assume it's inert. Don't assume it's friendly. Treat it as you would a functioning space station built by an alien intelligence: with respect, caution, and careful observation.

### The Fauna
You may see segmented creatures, three meters long, rotating segments, moving impossibly smoothly. They appear to ignore everything, traveling on fixed trajectories.

- Do not approach them deliberately
- Do not touch them
- Do not attempt to follow them out of the water volume
- Observe from distance only
- If one approaches you, remain still and allow it to pass

They may be biological, mechanical, or something without a clean distinction. They may be inhabitants, maintenance systems, or autonomous processes. They have shown no interest in visitors, and you want to keep it that way.

### The Traveling Lights
Blue-white spheres of illumination, five-six meter radius, drifting through at roughly hour intervals. They're your best opportunity for visibility, but:

- They reveal you as much as they reveal the environment
- Something may be paying attention to what they illuminate
- They appear sourceless and purposeless, which means their purpose is hidden

When one approaches, you can use it for navigation and observation, but remain aware that you're being illuminated against the darkness. Move deliberately. Observe actively. Be ready for the darkness to return.

### The Clicks
Sharp, clear, directional-less clicks at irregular intervals. They're either:
- Adjustment mechanisms for the suspension system
- Communication signals you cannot decode
- Natural phenomena of the Reservoir's physics
- Something else entirely

You cannot control them. You cannot predict them. Simply note them. If their pattern changes, that's significant data. If they stop entirely, that's extremely significant. But don't expect to understand their purpose.

## Duration Planning

### Short Visit (Hours)
If you're only staying briefly:
- Focus on mapping the basic structure
- Test your equipment functionality
- Experience the environment without trying to comprehend it
- Stay close to the entry point
- Accept that you'll leave with more questions than answers

### Medium Stay (Days)
For a longer exploration:
- Establish a home structure (probably one of the larger, central frameworks)
- Develop a daily routine (navigation practice, systematic exploration, rest periods)
- Map all twenty-seven structures thoroughly
- Document the light cycles, sound patterns, fauna appearances
- Practice navigating between structures in darkness
- Test the control panel systematically
- Monitor your psychological state carefully—isolation and cold will accumulate

### Extended Stay (Weeks)
If you're attempting serious study:
- Accept that you'll be profoundly changed by this
- Develop sophisticated navigation skills until you can traverse the town in near-total darkness
- Create detailed documentation of every structure, every object, every pattern
- Look for long-period cycles that only emerge over days
- Meditate on the structures—their purposes may become apparent through long observation
- Be prepared for your understanding of physics, causality, and reality to become permanently altered

No one should stay longer than their thermal protection can sustain. Hypothermia is cumulative, and the cold here is relentless.

## Mental Models

### What This Place Might Be

It helps to have frameworks, even if they're wrong:

**Possibility 1: Abandoned Infrastructure**
This was once inhabited by something human-adjacent. They left, but their town remains, maintained by automated systems. You're exploring ruins that refuse to decay.

**Possibility 2: Active Facility**
It's not abandoned—you simply can't perceive the inhabitants. The fauna, the lights, the clicks—these might be maintenance processes or residents operating on different scales of time or perception. You're visiting an inhabited place where you're essentially invisible.

**Possibility 3: Testing Ground**
The Reservoir is an experimental environment, possibly for studying suspension physics, possibly for purposes you cannot conceive. You're inside someone's laboratory.

**Possibility 4: Template**
It's not a place—it's a concept. The pure idea of "suspended town" manifested in approximately physical form. You're inside an abstraction that has achieved ontological solidity.

None of these may be correct. All of them may be partially correct. Hold your theories lightly. The Reservoir doesn't need to fit your categories.

### What You Are Here

Are you an explorer? A trespasser? An expected visitor? An irrelevant mote passing through?

The town doesn't respond to your presence in any obvious way. The structures don't reconfigure. The fauna doesn't acknowledge you. The lights continue their patterns. You could be the first visitor in millennia or the thousandth this year, and nothing would indicate which.

This means:
- You have no explicit permission, but also no explicit prohibition
- Take nothing that isn't clearly refuse
- Alter nothing that appears functional
- Leave minimal trace of your presence
- Treat the town as you would a place that's holy to someone, even if you don't know who

## Physical Training Regimen

Start at least three months before your journey:

**Month One: Cold Adaptation**
- Daily cold showers, progressively longer
- Weekly sessions in cold water (pool, lake, ocean)
- Build up to 30-minute sessions in 10°C water with thermal protection
- Learn your body's cold-response patterns

**Month Two: Swimming and Darkness**
- Daily swimming, building endurance
- Practice three-dimensional orientation underwater
- Swim with eyes closed, navigate by touch and memory
- Develop confident mobility in zero visibility
- Practice neutral buoyancy control

**Month Three: Integration**
- Long swimming sessions in cold water in darkness
- Practice emergency scenarios
- Combine navigation, thermal management, and psychological steadiness
- Take practice "journeys" in challenging conditions
- Learn what your breaking points are

Additionally, throughout:
- Meditation practice daily (20-30 minutes minimum)
- Solitude practice (progressive isolation periods)
- Breath control exercises
- Study of non-intuitive physics and philosophy
- Mental mapping exercises

## Psychological Training Regimen

**Solitude Tolerance**
- Spend weekends without human contact or media
- Practice being alone with your thoughts
- Develop internal conversation skills
- Learn what your mind does when understimulated

**Mystery Tolerance**
- Study phenomena you cannot explain
- Practice observing without analyzing
- Read philosophy that resists resolution
- Meditate on paradoxes
- Learn to be comfortable with "I don't know"

**Fear Management**
- Identify your specific fears (depths, darkness, cold, isolation, wrongness)
- Use graduated exposure for each
- Practice calm response to fear triggers
- Develop mantras or mental practices for anxiety moments
- Learn to distinguish real danger from psychological discomfort

**Meaning-Making**
- What will you do with hours of silence?
- How will you structure time without external reference?
- What internal resources can you draw on?
- Practice creating meaning in empty environments

## Entry and Exit

### Getting There
The location description doesn't specify how one arrives at the Reservoir. This is probably the most dangerous unknown. You may need:

- A multiverse transit device or portal
- Specific coordinates or invocation
- Assistance from entities that understand cross-reality navigation
- Simple luck or synchronicity

However you arrive, memorize the entry point absolutely. Mark it if possible. This is your exit route, and you cannot assume it will remain obvious or accessible.

### Leaving
Before you enter, establish:
- Clear exit conditions (time limit, resource threshold, psychological markers)
- Decision protocols (under what circumstances do you abort?)
- A dead-man switch if possible (someone who will attempt extraction if you don't return)

The Reservoir is profoundly stable—nothing changes, nothing decays. This stability might become seductive. You might want to stay. The cold, the dark, the silence, the wrongness—they might start to feel more real than the world you came from.

Set your limits before you go, and honor them.

Date and time-stamp everything, though time itself may behave strangely. At minimum, track your subjective duration.

## Philosophical Preparation

This may be the most important section.

The Hanging Reservoir is a place that refuses normal meaning. It's a town without inhabitants, structures without function, water without container, height without ground. It exists because it prefers to exist, which is not how anything should work.

You need to develop comfort with:

**Radical Contingency**
Things are as they are for no reason you can access. The town hangs because it hangs. The water prefers this height because it prefers this height. There's no deeper explanation, or if there is, it's not for you.

**Functional Inscrutability**
The structures do things. The control panel controls things. But what and how and why are not available. You can interact without understanding. You must be comfortable being a user without becoming an understander.

**Presence Without Purpose**
You're not here to fix anything, save anyone, or solve a puzzle. There may be no puzzle. You're here to experience a place that exists, to observe something that should be impossible, to witness wrongness functioning perfectly.

**Humility**
You are very small. Your physics is local. Your understanding is limited. The Reservoir is teaching you this whether you want to learn it or not.

Practice these ideas now. Meditate on them. Sit with the discomfort of not-knowing, of being unable to categorize, of experiencing something that refuses your frameworks.

The Reservoir will not explain itself. It will not comfort you. It will not validate your presence. It simply is, with absolute indifference to your comprehension.

You must become okay with that before you arrive.

## What You'll Bring Back

If you return, you'll bring back:

**Knowledge:**
Detailed documentation of an impossible place, maps of suspended architecture, records of phenomena that violate physics, observations that may or may not mean anything.

**Experience:**
You will have been inside wrongness. You will have lived in sustained impossibility. You will have befriended cold and darkness and silence. You will have navigated by insufficient light through geometry that serves purposes you cannot name.

**Changes:**
The cold will stay with you. You'll find normal temperatures uncomfortably warm for weeks. 

The darkness will stay with you. You'll find normal light harsh and excessive.

The silence will stay with you. You'll find normal sound chaotic and overwhelming.

The wrongness will stay with you. You'll find normal physics suspiciously rigid, arbitrarily constrained. You'll catch yourself expecting water to hang, structures to float, rules to be negotiable.

You'll look up at clouds and think: that should be an ocean. You'll swim in normal water and feel frustrated that it doesn't prefer any particular height. You'll enter buildings and find them too certain, too committed to being buildings, not free enough.

The Reservoir will have changed how you understand the word "is."

This cannot be undone.

## Is This Journey Necessary?

No. Obviously not.

The Hanging Reservoir offers no resources you need. It poses no threat you must neutralize. It contains no treasure you can extract. It's not on the way to anywhere else.

You go because it exists and you want to witness it. You go because it's impossible and you want to stand inside impossibility. You go because you're the kind of person who, upon learning there's a town suspended in un-falling water three kilometers above nothing, thinks: I should go there.

But understand: this is not a comfortable journey. This is not an empowering journey. This is not a journey of self-discovery or heroic achievement.

This is a journey of smallness, of humility, of accepting that reality is stranger and larger than you, and that some of its strangeness will never resolve into meaning you can hold.

If you need the world to make sense, stay home.

If you need accomplishment, validation, or purpose, stay home.

But if you want to swim through dark water toward faintly glowing geometry and feel the void beneath you and hear sourceless clicks and witness fauna that moves impossibly and touch structures that sing and float in cold silence in a town suspended by preference in absolute night...

Then prepare carefully, prepare thoroughly, and go.

The Reservoir will be there, hanging in its darkness, breathing its slow cycles, clicking its occasional adjustments, maintaining its twenty-seven structures in perfect distribution through water that prefers this height.

It doesn't need you to visit.

But it will allow it.

And you will never, ever be the same.
