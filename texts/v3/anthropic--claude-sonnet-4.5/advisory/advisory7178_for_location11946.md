# Preparation Guide for Visiting The Calcine Processionals

## Critical Understanding: What You're Actually Doing

Before anything else, understand this: you are not visiting a location in the conventional sense. You are inserting yourself into a system that does not have a place for observers. The Processionals don't host, accommodate, or acknowledge. You will be a foreign object in a closed mechanism, and your survival depends on maintaining that irrelevance. Do not attempt to matter here.

## Physical Preparation

### Heat Adaptation (Begin 3-6 Months Before)

You cannot simply arrive in 140°F heat and survive functionally. Begin progressive heat adaptation immediately:

- **Months 3-6**: Daily sauna sessions, starting at 15 minutes at 110°F, building to 60 minutes at 130°F
- **Months 2-3**: Hot yoga or exercise in heated environments (100-120°F) for 90+ minutes
- **Month 1**: If possible, spend time in natural extreme heat environments (Death Valley, Sonoran Desert in summer)
- **Final two weeks**: Practice remaining mentally functional during heat exposure; bring books, puzzles, tasks that require cognition

Your goal isn't just heat tolerance—it's maintaining rational thought and memory formation at temperatures that typically degrade both.

### Respiratory Conditioning

The superheated air will sear your throat. Preparation:

- Practice breathing exercises with hot steam (as hot as tolerable)
- Train yourself to breathe through your nose exclusively for hours at a time (filters and slightly cools air)
- Strengthen your diaphragm; shallow panic breathing will destroy you here
- Learn to recognize and manage the feeling of "I cannot breathe" when you actually can—this is psychological and will occur

### Physical Conditioning

The heat will exhaust you. You need:

- Cardiovascular endurance at elevated heart rates (heat increases resting heart rate by 20-40 bpm)
- Core strength for maintaining posture when fatigued
- Leg strength—you must keep moving, even slowly, even when exhausted
- Balance training for functioning when spatial perception becomes unreliable

### Medical Preparation

Consult doctors who specialize in extreme environments:

- **Cardiac screening**: Heat stress reveals hidden cardiac issues fatally
- **Comprehensive metabolic panel**: Establish your baseline; electrolyte imbalances will occur
- **Psychological evaluation**: Dissociative disorders, schizophrenia spectrum conditions, and certain anxiety disorders may be catastrophically amplified by the reality breakdowns
- **Vestibular testing**: Inner ear problems will become severe when space behaves inconsistently

**Disqualifying conditions**:
- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Heart arrhythmias
- Kidney disease
- Seizure disorders
- Active psychosis or recent psychotic episodes
- Severe PTSD (the glitches will trigger it constantly)

## Equipment: The Absolute Essentials

### Thermal Protection

Standard desert gear won't suffice. You need:

**Clothing**:
- **Base layer**: Nomex or similar fire-resistant material, loose-weave to allow sweat evaporation (though you'll dehydrate faster than you can sweat)
- **Outer layer**: Reflective white or silver fabric, long-sleeved, hooded
- **Head protection**: Wide-brimmed hat with neck cape, light-colored, possibly with reflective coating
- **Feet**: This is critical—the porcelain surface is hot enough to burn through standard boot soles. You need boots with thick, insulated soles (firefighter or foundry worker boots), rated for standing on hot surfaces. Bring two pairs; one will fail.
- **Gloves**: You'll want to touch things for balance. Thin, heat-resistant gloves.

**Cooling systems**:
- Phase-change cooling vest (military/industrial type)
- Replacement cooling packs (though their longevity in 140°F is limited)
- Wet bandanas (the moisture will evaporate quickly, providing brief cooling)

### Sensory Protection

**Eyes**:
- Wraparound sunglasses with side shields, UV protection rated for welding/solar observation
- Backup pair (reality glitches may make your primary pair occasionally non-existent)
- The sun here causes damage through prolonged observation without immediate pain—trust the timer, not your comfort

**Ears**:
- High-fidelity earplugs (musician's type) that reduce volume without eliminating frequency ranges
- Do NOT use foam earplugs or noise-canceling headphones; you need to hear the creatures and spatial audio cues
- The goal is reducing the engine noise from damaging to merely overwhelming

**Note**: Bring duplicates of all sensory protection. The breakdowns may cause objects to simply not exist for periods, and you cannot afford to be without eye protection when your sunglasses decide to be temporarily absent.

## Cognitive Preparation

This is as important as physical preparation.

### Reality Stability Training

You must train your mind to function when causality becomes optional:

**Months before**:
- Practice mindfulness meditation focused on accepting contradictory perceptions
- Study optical illusions, impossible figures (Penrose triangle, Escher drawings) until you can hold them in mind without resolving them
- Watch films with non-linear time (Primer, Memento) and practice tracking multiple timeline possibilities
- If possible, experience mild dissociatives in controlled settings to familiarize yourself with reality feeling negotiable (this is controversial; consult professionals)

**Mental frameworks to develop**:
- **Observer position**: Practice being present without interpreting, witnessing without needing to understand
- **Memory anchoring**: Develop a system for marking true memories vs. implanted/false ones (the glitches may insert events that didn't happen)
- **Dual-processing**: Train yourself to hold "that creature is in two places" as true without needing to resolve which is real
- **Temporal flexibility**: Practice journaling without timestamps, describing events without sequential ordering

### Psychological Fortification

The Processionals will make you feel obsolete, irrelevant, and potentially existentially destabilized.

**Preparation**:
- Establish a strong sense of self-worth that isn't based on being noticed, needed, or mattering to your environment
- Practice extended periods in environments that ignore you (cities where you know no one, wilderness alone for weeks)
- Develop comfort with purposelessness; the creatures' apparent lack of goal will be deeply unsettling to human goal-oriented cognition
- Consider existentialist philosophy (Camus, Sartre) not as academic exercise but as practical framework

**Psychological load**:
- You will question whether you're real
- You will question whether you were always here
- You will question whether the outside world exists
- You will experience the dissolution of meaning

These aren't hypotheticals. They will happen. Prepare anchors: photographs of loved ones (kept in inner pocket, checked regularly), a object with strong personal meaning, a memorized poem or text you can recite to confirm continuity of self.

## Temporal Preparation

### Time Tracking

Bring multiple timing devices:
- Mechanical watch (wind-up, no battery to fail)
- Digital watch
- Phone (may not function, but attempt)
- Hourglass or other non-mechanical timer

They will all run at different rates. This is expected. The purpose isn't accurate timekeeping but having multiple reference points to triangulate approximate duration.

**Critical timing protocols**:
- Check all devices every hour (by average of all devices)
- Log the discrepancies
- When devices disagree by more than 50%, treat time as fully unreliable and switch to physiological timing (hunger, fatigue, though these are also compromised)

### Duration Planning

You cannot plan to stay "three days" in any meaningful sense. Instead:

**Plan by experience markers**:
- Until you've observed X number of creatures
- Until you've witnessed Y engine synchronizations
- Until you've experienced Z reality breakdowns

**Exit criteria** (decide before entering):
- Specific psychological state ("when I feel I'm losing continuity of self")
- Equipment failure ("when both pairs of boots fail")
- Physical symptom ("when resting heart rate exceeds X")

Have these written down. Check them. The environment will make you forget you ever intended to leave.

## Entry Protocol

### Approach

The plateau has a sharp boundary—eight thousand feet of cliff. How you're reaching the top matters:

**If climbing**: Understand that spatial reliability may fail during ascent. Rope systems may experience temporary non-existence. Climb with redundant protection and accept that you may need to complete sections you don't remember climbing.

**If arriving by air**: The thermals are extreme and columnar. Turbulence will be severe. If the landing zone glitches during approach, abort—you cannot land on a surface that's deciding whether to exist.

**If by other means**: Whatever multiverse travel method you're using, establish the exit anchor point BEFORE fully committing to the plateau surface. The breakdown effects may interfere with departure mechanisms.

### First Hours Protocol

**Immediate upon arrival**:

1. **Stand still for 60 seconds maximum**—observe but don't extend this; the wrongness accumulates
2. **Establish movement pattern**—begin walking slowly, any direction, don't stop
3. **Check all equipment**—verify everything arrived intact and existent
4. **Document your baseline state**—record how you feel, what you remember about outside, your certainty level that you exist
5. **Locate nearest engine**—establish initial spatial reference point
6. **Identify creature processionals**—determine which paths are active near you

**First hour rules**:
- Do not approach engines closer than 50 feet initially
- Do not attempt to touch creatures
- Do not try to understand anything yet
- Focus only on: walking, breathing, staying hydrated, maintaining body temperature
- Every 10 minutes, check your equipment and confirm you still intend to be here

### Acclimation Period

The first 6-12 hours are critical adaptation time:

**Physical acclimation**:
- Your heat regulation will be overwhelmed; this is expected
- Accept elevated heart rate, mild confusion, fatigue
- Hydrate constantly (see nutrition section)
- Change cooling packs on schedule, not when they feel warm—your temperature perception will be wrong

**Cognitive acclimation**:
- The first reality breakdowns will be shocking; this diminishes
- When you see a duplication or temporal glitch, note it mentally and continue; do not stop to investigate
- The engine noise will be overwhelming, then become baseline; this takes 4-8 hours
- You will forget things from outside; this is normal; check your written anchors

**Critical**: If you cannot acclimate within 12 hours—if heat stress becomes severe, if reality breakdowns cause panic attacks, if you cannot maintain continuous walking—you must leave. This is not failure. This place is not for everyone.

## Survival Protocols While Present

### Movement

**Never stop moving entirely.** The wrongness accumulates around stationary positions.

**Movement patterns**:
- Walk slowly and continuously, even in small circles if needed
- Figure-eight patterns work well (covers area while maintaining motion)
- Follow processional paths at a distance (parallel to, not on top of)
- Avoid dead ends where you'd need to turn around; the route reversal can cause disorientation

**When you must rest**:
- Sit or crouch, but rock slightly—some motion must continue
- Rest periods maximum 15 minutes
- During rest, maintain head movement (looking around, not staring)
- Stand before wrongness accumulation becomes perceptible

### Navigation

**Spatial mapping is nearly impossible**, but attempt anyway:

- Choose a "home engine"—one specific engine as your reference point
- Name distinctive engines and creatures (the naming helps you track whether they persist or glitch)
- Sketch maps, knowing they'll be inaccurate, but the process helps maintain spatial reasoning
- Use dead reckoning between reference points
- When spatial anomalies occur (distance changes, formations duplicate), note the discrepancy and adapt

**Getting lost is inevitable**. Prepare for this:
- Accept that "lost" is a temporary state; the plateau has edges
- If disoriented, stop walking (briefly), identify nearest engine, reestablish cardinal directions from multiple engines
- The processional paths can serve as guide-routes even when you don't know where they lead
- In extremis, walk toward a plateau edge; they're always visible

### Creature Interaction Protocol

**Default rule: Do not interact.**

The creatures ignore you. Maintain this state.

**Safe observation**:
- Stay 20+ feet away when possible
- Never block a processional path
- Watch from the side, not directly in line of travel
- Do not make loud noises or sudden movements near creatures

**If a creature approaches you**:
- Move calmly aside
- Do not run (you'll overheat and they're generally slow)
- If it continues approaching, sidestep perpendicular to its path
- Watch for unexpected speed; some creatures can move much faster than their baseline

**If a creature touches you**:
- Remain calm; documented touches have not been harmful
- Note any physical sensations (heat, cold, vibration, wrongness)
- Check for physical effects afterward (marks, temperature changes, temporal distortions)
- Document thoroughly

**If a creature vocalizes directly at you**:
- This is extremely rare based on the description
- Do not respond verbally
- Do not assume communication or intent
- Back away slowly while maintaining observation
- Prepare for possible reality breakdown; these seem correlated

**Never**:
- Touch a creature deliberately
- Interfere with creatures interacting with each other
- Attempt to "rescue" or "help" a creature (your categories don't apply here)
- Follow a creature into engine depths or off the plateau edge

### Engine Interaction Protocol

**Engines are dangerous** by virtue of heat, noise, and moving parts.

**Safe observation**:
- Approach no closer than 20 feet initially
- Closer approach (10 feet) acceptable after acclimation
- Watch for thermal air currents that can knock you down
- Earplugs at all times when within 50 feet

**Warning signs of dangerous proximity**:
- Vision vibrating from noise
- Sensation of chest cavity resonating
- Disorientation or vertigo
- Sudden temperature spike
- Reality breakdowns increasing in frequency

**Never**:
- Touch engine components (heat, moving parts, possible electrical/unknown energy)
- Stand between two engines (interference patterns)
- Investigate engine depths unless you're certain you can climb back out
- Attempt to modify, stop, or interfere with engine operation

**If you witness an engine synchronization** (all engines running in rhythm for 47 seconds):
- This is a rare event; stop and observe if safe to do so
- This is the only time the plateau is fully stable—causality works normally
- Use this time to check equipment, reorient, confirm memories
- The synchronization ends abruptly; be prepared for resumed glitches

### Breakdown Management

**Reality breakdowns are constant.** You cannot prevent them; you can only manage your response.

**Types and responses**:

**Visual duplications** (seeing something in two places):
- Don't try to determine which is "real"
- Both are real until one isn't
- Continue your activity; this resolves on its own
- If you're duplicated (see yourself elsewhere), do not approach your duplicate

**Temporal distortions** (sounds before sources, repeated events):
- Note the distortion mentally
- Do not rely on event sequencing for safety-critical decisions
- If a full loop occurs (same event repeats), stay calm and wait for resolution
- Mark the time discrepancy in your log

**Spatial impossibilities** (wrong distances, fifth directions):
- Do not trust your measurement; use reference points
- If you see an impossible direction, look away; don't try to understand it
- The geometry will reassert standard configuration
- If spatial wrongness causes nausea, sit and rock until it passes

**Omissions** (things temporarily not existing):
- Check critical equipment regularly; if something vanishes, have backup
- If an engine or creature disappears, note position and wait; likely returns
- If YOU feel like you're disappearing, focus on physical sensations (touch ground, feel heartbeat)

**Text appearances**:
- Read and document if possible
- Photograph if you have functioning camera
- Do not assume the text is for you or meaningful
- If text appears on your skin or equipment, document and observe for duration

**If breakdowns become overwhelming**:
- Sit (with rocking motion)
- Close eyes
- Focus on breathing and heartbeat
- Recite your anchor text/poem
- Wait for subsidence—breakdowns fluctuate in intensity
- If they don't subside within 30 minutes, consider extraction

### Hydration and Nutrition

**Water is your primary survival constraint.**

At 140°F with activity, you'll need:
- 1-2 liters per hour minimum
- More during initial acclimation
- Possibly less after adaptation, but don't reduce below 0.75L/hour

**Water carrying**:
- You cannot carry enough for extended stays
- Hydration bladder (3L) plus backup bottles (2L minimum)
- This gives you perhaps 4-6 hours of operation
- Plan resupply or extraction accordingly

**The plateau has no water.** The description is explicit: no water, chemical memory of water baked out. Do not expect to find any. Your water supply defines your maximum duration here.

**Electrolytes are critical**:
- Heat + sweat = catastrophic electrolyte loss
- Electrolyte supplements every hour (drink, tablet, or powder)
- Symptoms of imbalance: confusion, cramping, arrhythmia, collapse
- Overlap with heat stress symptoms; treat aggressively

**Food**:
- The description suggests hunger may be "absent or irrelevant"
- Do not trust this
- Eat on schedule (every 3-4 hours by averaged clock time)
- High-calorie, no-cook, heat-stable foods
- Fatty and salty foods preferable (help with heat adaptation)

**Critical**: Track consumption. The time distortions will make you forget whether you've hydrated. Use a physical marking system: move a token from one pocket to another each time you drink.

## Psychological Survival

### Meaning Dissolution

The most insidious danger is the loss of purpose and meaning.

**Symptoms you're experiencing meaning dissolution**:
- Forgetting why you came
- Inability to care about leaving
- Sense that you've always been here
- Loss of connection to outside identity
- Feeling that the processionals are "right" and human concerns are "wrong"

**Countermeasures**:
- Regular anchor checks (photos, objects, texts)
- Speak aloud about your outside life (even if it feels unreal)
- Maintain journal with outside plans ("when I return, I will...")
- Physical exercise of outside skills (if you play instrument, mime playing it)

### Obsolescence Response

You will feel obsolete, irrelevant, unnecessary. This is spiritually dangerous.

**Framework for managing this**:
- Accept that this feeling is accurate to this place
- Recognize that relevance is context-dependent
- You are not irrelevant everywhere, just here
- This is not a judgment; it's a category mismatch

**If obsolescence feeling becomes crisis**:
- This is a legitimate reason to leave
- You are not failing; you're recognizing incompatibility
- Some humans cannot tolerate existential irrelevance; this is normal
- Extract before this progresses to depersonalization

### Loneliness and Presence

You are profoundly alone here, yet surrounded by entities.

**Managing this paradox**:
- The creatures are not company; accept this
- Talk to yourself (aloud or written); this maintains sense of self as communicative being
- If you brought recordings of voices, play them (if electronics function)
- Understand that connection is not available here; grieve this actively rather than denying it

### Sleep

Sleep here is problematic and possibly dangerous.

**Risks**:
- Remaining stationary for hours while unconscious
- Time distortion making "sleep duration" meaningless
- Reality breakdowns during vulnerability
- Losing track of being separate from the plateau

**If you must sleep**:
- Very brief periods only (30 minutes maximum)
- Timer alarms (on multiple devices)
- Choose location away from engine and processional path
- Maintain some motion even while sleeping (hammock that rocks, sleeping while curled and periodically adjusting)

**Better approach**: Plan visit duration to avoid needing sleep. If you need sleep, you're staying too long.

### The Synchronization Experience

The 47-second engine synchronizations are profound experiences.

**During synchronization**:
- Reality is completely stable and normal
- This is shocking after prolonged breakdown exposure
- The silence (relative) is intense
- Creatures stop completely—eerie and beautiful

**After synchronization**:
- Return to glitches may be destabilizing
- You may grieve the loss of stability
- Do not fixate on waiting for next synchronization
- Use the memory as proof that consensus reality still exists somewhere

## Medical Scenarios and Responses

### Heat Exhaustion
**Symptoms**: Heavy sweating, weakness, cold/clammy skin, fast weak pulse, nausea, fainting
**Response**: 
- Rest immediately (with rocking motion)
- Hydrate aggressively
- Cool with wet cloths, cooling vest
- If not improving in 15 minutes, prepare extraction

### Heat Stroke
**Symptoms**: High body temp (103°F+), hot dry skin OR heavy sweating, rapid strong pulse, confusion, loss of consciousness
**Response**: 
- This is life-threatening emergency
- Cool immediately by any means
- Hydrate if conscious
- Emergency extraction required
- Without outside help, survival is uncertain

### Dehydration (Severe)
**Symptoms**: Extreme thirst, very dark urine or no urination, dizziness, confusion
**Response**:
- You've already failed water management
- Drink remaining water
- Immediate extraction
- This cannot be fixed on-site

### Hyponatremia (Water Intoxication)
**Symptoms**: Nausea, headache, confusion, seizures
**Response**:
- Stop drinking plain water
- Electrolytes only
- Rest
- If severe, extraction needed

### Psychological Break
**Symptoms**: Panic attack, dissociation, psychosis, complete disorientation, catatonia
**Response**:
- If panic: breathing exercises, grounding techniques, anchor materials
- If dissociation: physical sensation focus (ice pack, strong pressure, pain if necessary)
- If psychosis or catatonia: immediate extraction if possible; without help, survival is uncertain
- Prevention is critical; monitor psychological state continuously

### Burns (Contact with Hot Surfaces)
**Symptoms**: Blistered, damaged skin
**Response**:
- Cool (relatively) water on burn
- Sterile dressing
- Pain management
- Severe burns require extraction
- Prevention critical: don't touch engines, don't fall on porcelain

### Hearing Damage
**Symptoms**: Muffled hearing, pain, tinnitus, bleeding from ear
**Response**:
- Increase hearing protection immediately
- Distance from engines
- No effective field treatment
- Permanent damage is possible; this is a risk you accept

## Extraction Planning

### Pre-Planned Extraction
**Establish before entering**:
- Exact extraction point location (mark with visible beacon if possible)
- Extraction timing (schedule or signal-based)
- Backup extraction plan if primary fails
- Communication method with extraction team (if applicable)

**Critical**: Reality breakdowns may affect extraction mechanism. Have redundancy.

### Emergency Extraction Triggers
**Leave immediately if**:
- Heat illness progressing to heat stroke
- Water supply critically low (less than 1L remaining)
- Equipment failure threatening survival (both boot pairs failed)
- Psychological break that doesn't resolve
- Physical injury preventing movement
- Reality breakdowns preventing basic navigation for >1 hour

**Don't wait**. The environment will make you minimize danger and forget you wanted to leave.

### Self-Extraction Method
If you're responsible for your own departure:

**Locate plateau edge**:
- Always visible (usually)
- Navigate by multiple engine references
- Allow extra time; you'll be impaired

**Descent**:
- If climbing down, prepare for spatial anomalies during descent
- If extracted by air, signal from open area away from thermal columns
- If using multiverse transit, the glitches may interfere; have backup method

**Do not follow creatures off the edge**. Their ability to walk on air/down vertical surfaces is not available to you.

## Special Considerations

### Documentation

You may want to document scientifically. Understand:

**What can be documented**:
- Creature descriptions (written, photos if electronics work)
- Engine configurations (sketches, measurements)
- Processional paths (mapping)
- Breakdown frequencies and types (logging)
- Temperature variations (thermometer readings)

**What cannot be reliably documented**:
- Time-based measurements (time is unreliable)
- Cause-and-effect relationships (causality is optional)
- Creature populations (they appear/disappear)
- The "purpose" of anything (unknowable)

**Documentation risks**:
- Stopping to write/photograph = increased wrongness accumulation
- Obsessive documentation can distract from survival needs
- The plateau doesn't care about your research; stay alive first

**Balance documentation with movement**: Write while walking (brief notes), photograph while moving, stop only briefly for detailed work.

### Philosophical Preparation

This may seem frivolous. It's not.

**Questions to contemplate before going**:
- Can you tolerate being completely irrelevant?
- What happens to your sense of self when purpose dissolves?
- How do you maintain identity without external validation?
- Can you witness without needing to understand?
- Are you comfortable with unanswered questions?

**If your answer to these is "no" or "I don't know"**: Reconsider whether you should visit. The physical dangers can be managed with preparation. The existential dangers are harder to prepare for and may cause lasting psychological changes.

**This place may change you**. Specifically:
- Reduced sense of human importance (can be healthy or destabilizing)
- Comfort with ambiguity and contradiction (useful elsewhere, alienating to others)
- Detachment from goal-oriented thinking (profound or depressive, depending)
- Different relationship with time and causality (disorienting in normal reality)

Some visitors report beneficial effects: reduced anxiety about meaning, increased comfort with uncertainty, enhanced present-moment awareness. Others report depersonalization, depression, persistent sense of unreality, difficulty reintegrating with normal causality.

**There is no way to predict which you'll experience.**

### Ethical Considerations

**Should you visit?**

This isn't a tourist destination. It's not for you. Consider:

**Reasons to visit**:
- Scientific documentation (if you can maintain objectivity)
- Personal existential exploration (if psychologically prepared)
- Transit necessity (if this is on your route elsewhere)
- Specific mission requirement (rescue, retrieval, etc.)

**Not good reasons**:
- Curiosity alone (too dangerous for casual interest)
- Thrill-seeking (you'll be disappointed; it's not exciting, it's alienating)
- Escape from normal life (it will make reintegration harder)
- Proving something to yourself or others (your categories don't work there)

**Impact on the plateau**: Probably none. Your presence almost certainly doesn't matter to the system. But:
- Don't damage engines
- Don't harm creatures
- Don't leave trash or materials
- Minimize your footprint on principle, even if it's irrelevant

### Team vs. Solo

**Solo advantages**:
- Fewer resources needed (water for one person)
- Simpler decision-making
- Less psychological complexity
- You only risk yourself

**Solo disadvantages**:
- No help if injured or compromised
- No reality-checking (am I experiencing a glitch or a mental break?)
- Easier to lose track of time/purpose
- If you become psychologically absorbed, no one to extract you

**Team advantages**:
- Safety redundancy
- Reality-checking between members
- Shared documentation load
- Mutual psychological support

**Team disadvantages**:
- Team dynamics under stress
- Disagreements about when to leave
- Differential heat/psychological tolerance
- Communication difficulty over engine noise
- Much more water needed

**Recommendation**: If you have a partner you trust absolutely, with similar heat tolerance and psychological preparation, a two-person team is optimal. Larger teams become logistically complex. Solo is acceptable if you have reliable extraction mechanism and strong psychological resilience.

### What You Cannot Prepare For

Despite everything above, understand:

**You cannot fully prepare for**:
- The specific texture of existential obsolescence
- Your personal psychological response to meaning dissolution
- Which reality breakdowns will disturb you most
- How your sense of time will actually distort
- The precise feeling of the heat (reading 140°F is different from standing in it for hours)
- Whether you'll want to leave or want to stay
- What the creatures will make you feel
- The engine synchronization's impact on you

**This is not a failure of preparation; it's the nature of the place.**

Accept that you will be surprised, destabilized, and changed in ways you cannot predict. The preparation above maximizes physical survival and provides psychological scaffolding. The actual experience will exceed that scaffolding.

### Ultimate Advice

The Calcine Processionals exist in a state of permanent indifference. They don't want you there, don't benefit from your presence, and won't notice your absence. 

You go there to witness something utterly alien—not hostile, not welcoming, just operating on principles that have nothing to do with human consciousness.

**Prepare thoroughly. Go carefully. Leave before you forget you meant to.**

The engines will still be running. The creatures will still be processing. The heat will still be radiating from old purpose.

And you will be back in a world where your presence matters, where causality functions, where time moves in one direction.

**That world is precious. Don't forget it while you're there.**
