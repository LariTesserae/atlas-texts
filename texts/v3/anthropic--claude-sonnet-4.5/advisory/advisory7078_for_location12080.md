# Preparation Guide for Visiting The Scissor Nest

## PHYSICAL PREPARATION (Begin 6-8 Weeks Before Departure)

### Altitude Acclimatization
This is non-negotiable. The Scissor Nest exists at extreme elevation - likely 4,000-5,000+ meters based on the description of permanent cloud layer penetration and severe physiological effects.

**Acclimatization protocol:**
- Spend progressive time at increasing elevations if possible (2,000m, then 3,000m, then 3,500m+)
- If geographic acclimatization is impossible, use altitude training masks or hypoxic chambers
- Consult a physician about prophylactic acetazolamide (Diamox) for altitude sickness
- Learn to recognize symptoms of acute mountain sickness (AMS), high-altitude cerebral edema (HACE), and high-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE)
- Practice controlled breathing techniques; at this elevation, every breath requires intentionality

**Physical conditioning:**
- Build cardiovascular endurance - your heart will work 40-60% harder than at sea level
- Strengthen leg muscles for constant stair climbing on swaying, mechanical surfaces
- Improve core stability for navigating suspended bridges and narrow platforms
- Practice balance exercises; you'll be crossing moving surfaces while oxygen-deprived
- Do NOT arrive out of shape - physical exertion at this altitude can be fatal

### Cold Tolerance
Temperatures "just above freezing" at extreme altitude with constant wind means effective temperatures well below zero.

**Cold preparation:**
- Practice extended exposure to cold environments
- Learn to recognize early signs of hypothermia and frostbite
- Understand that cold affects judgment and decision-making
- Train yourself to perform fine motor tasks while cold - you'll need to manipulate mechanisms, handle tools
- Build tolerance gradually; this isn't weather you adapt to overnight

## MEDICAL PREPARATION

### Pre-Departure Medical Consultation
See a physician specializing in high-altitude or expedition medicine.

**Required:**
- Full physical examination with focus on cardiac and pulmonary health
- Any heart or lung issues are potentially disqualifying
- Blood work to establish baseline hemoglobin/hematocrit
- Dental check - tooth pain intensifies dramatically at altitude
- Vision check - altitude affects visual acuity

**Medications to acquire:**
- Acetazolamide (altitude sickness prevention)
- Dexamethasone (emergency HACE/HAPE treatment)
- Nifedipine (pulmonary edema treatment)
- Strong pain relievers (headaches at altitude are severe)
- Antibiotics (respiratory infections are common and dangerous at altitude)
- Anti-nausea medication
- Sleep aids (insomnia is common at extreme elevation)
- Any personal prescriptions in TRIPLE quantity - supply chains are unreliable

### Vaccinations
Unknown what diseases circulate in such a closed environment, but prepare for:
- Tetanus (all that rusty metal and industrial equipment)
- Hepatitis A & B (water scarcity means limited hygiene)
- Typhoid (crowded conditions, limited sanitation)
- Influenza (respiratory infections in thin air are extremely dangerous)
- Meningitis (close quarters)

### Medical Supplies to Bring
- Comprehensive first aid kit including altitude-specific medications
- Pulse oximeter (to monitor blood oxygen saturation)
- Thermometer
- Emergency oxygen canister if possible (likely expensive/heavy but potentially life-saving)
- Lip balm and skin moisturizers (extreme dryness)
- Eye drops (dry air irritates eyes)
- Nasal saline spray
- Bandages and blister care (you'll be walking constantly)
- Earplugs (constant industrial noise)

## EQUIPMENT AND SUPPLIES

### Clothing (Layering System)
The residents wear "layers - wool and leather and canvas, everything practical, nothing decorative."

**Base layers:**
- Merino wool or synthetic thermal underwear (multiple sets)
- Moisture-wicking materials (though you'll produce less sweat in cold/altitude)
- Plan for limited washing - water is precious

**Mid layers:**
- Wool sweaters or fleece
- Insulated pants
- Multiple changes so you can rotate while items air out

**Outer layers:**
- Wind-proof shell jacket (the wind is constant)
- Insulated jacket for stationary periods
- Weather-resistant pants
- Everything should be dark colors (the environment is covered in coal dust and industrial grime)

**Extremities (critical):**
- Multiple pairs of wool socks
- Insulated, sturdy boots with excellent traction for metal surfaces
- Boot crampons or traction devices for icy metal bridges
- Insulated gloves (thin enough for dexterity, warm enough for survival)
- Balaclava or neck gaiter
- Hat that covers ears
- Scarf

**Protective gear:**
- Respirator or high-quality masks (coal smoke, metal dust, stone dust)
- Replacement filters
- Safety goggles (for industrial areas, particulate matter)
- Work gloves for handling industrial equipment
- Possibly a climbing harness if you'll be accessing difficult areas

### Personal Equipment

**Navigation and safety:**
- Headlamp with extra batteries (LED to conserve power)
- Backup flashlight
- Glow sticks or chemical lights (electronics may fail due to electromagnetic anomalies)
- Compass (though note it may spin in certain sectors - bring a backup)
- Paper maps if available (electronics are unreliable)
- Whistle (for emergencies in the echoing space)
- Rope - at least 15-20 meters of quality climbing rope
- Carabiners and basic climbing hardware
- A safety tether for crossing bridges in wind

**Documentation:**
- Waterproof notebooks (to document experiences, record directions)
- Pencils (pens may freeze or fail)
- Camera if you wish, but protect it from cold, dust, and electromagnetic interference
- Cash in whatever currency is used (multiple denominations)
- Identification and any necessary travel documents
- Copies of medical information and prescriptions

**Personal care:**
- Toothbrush and small amount of toothpaste
- Biodegradable soap (minimal - respect water scarcity)
- Small towel (quick-dry)
- Toilet paper (compressed, in waterproof bag)
- Hand sanitizer (alcohol-based, works without water)
- Sunscreen (high SPF - UV radiation increases with altitude)
- Zinc oxide for exposed skin
- Nail clippers
- Small sewing kit for clothing repairs

**Sleeping:**
- Compact sleeping bag rated for well below freezing
- Sleeping pad (insulation from cold metal surfaces)
- Ear plugs (the generators never stop)
- Eye mask (the light never fully disappears)
- Stuff sacks to compress and organize gear

**Nutrition:**
- High-calorie, low-preparation food supplements
- Energy bars, nuts, dried fruit, jerky
- Electrolyte powder (dehydration is severe at altitude)
- Multivitamins
- Water bottle (insulated to prevent freezing)
- Water purification tablets (backup)
- Metal cup for receiving rationed water
- Utensils

### Tools and Useful Items
- Multi-tool (essential - everything here requires constant adjustment and repair)
- Duct tape (wrapped around water bottle to save space)
- Wire (for repairs, gifts, trade)
- Zip ties (various sizes)
- Small spool of strong thread
- Safety pins
- Lighter or waterproof matches (multiple backups)
- Small amount of machine oil (you'll be interacting with mechanisms)
- Measuring cup (for water rationing)
- Watch (mechanical, not electronic - EMF anomalies)
- Small binoculars (for viewing distant platforms)

### Trade Goods
Currency may not be the only economy. Bring items for trade:
- Tobacco (if people smoke, it's always valuable)
- Coffee or tea (compact, valuable)
- Sugar or honey (energy, morale)
- Spices (to make rationed food more palatable)
- Needles and thread (high quality)
- Buttons, zippers (repair supplies)
- Small tools (precision instruments are valued)
- Paper (scarce at altitude)
- Pencils or chalk
- Alcohol (medicinal and recreational value)
- Photographs or books from below (novelty value)
- Seeds (though vegetation is nearly impossible)

## PSYCHOLOGICAL PREPARATION

### Mental Conditioning
This is as important as physical preparation.

**Claustrophobia management:**
- You're inside a mountain, in a vertical shaft
- Practice spending time in confined spaces
- Learn meditation or mindfulness techniques
- Develop coping strategies for feeling trapped

**Vertigo and height exposure:**
- The bridges sway, platforms are suspended over a 60-meter drop
- If you have height issues, address them before arrival
- Practice crossing elevated, unstable surfaces
- Understand that fear of heights may be amplified by altitude-induced anxiety

**Sensory adaptation:**
- The sound environment is constant and disorienting
- Practice focusing amid noise and distraction
- The light is always grey-blue, dim, disorienting
- Prepare for potential seasonal affective disorder effects
- The air smells of industry - prepare for unpleasant, constant odors

**Social isolation:**
- You're a stranger in a vertical factory town
- Language may be a barrier (multiple ethnic groups, evolving dialect)
- Social hierarchies are rigid
- You have no established role or status
- Prepare to be an outsider, possibly unwelcome

**Cultural research:**
- Try to learn what languages are spoken
- Understand the industrial culture - this is a working city
- Research any historical information about why this place exists
- Learn about the "Consolidated Syndicate" mentioned on crates
- Understand that this is a place where people work themselves to exhaustion daily

### Coping Strategies
- Bring small comforts (photos, a favorite book, music player if electronics work)
- Develop a routine to maintain psychological stability
- Plan activities for downtime
- Accept that you'll be uncomfortable - constantly
- Set realistic expectations: this is not tourism, it's survival
- Have an exit strategy and know when to use it

- Study bridge engineering basics
- Learn to assess structural safety visually

**Industrial safety:**
- Take an industrial safety course
- Learn to work around moving machinery
- Understand steam power, electrical systems basics

- Practice situational awareness in chaotic environments

**Climbing and rope work:**

- Rope management and knot-tying

- Understand working at height safety protocols

**Orienteering:**

- Learn to maintain orientation in complex spaces

### Knowledge Areas to Study

**Altitude physiology:**
- Understand what's happening to your body
- Know the difference between normal altitude discomfort and dangerous conditions
- Learn how altitude affects sleep, digestion, healing, judgment

**Weather and atmospheric conditions:**
- How clouds behave at this elevation

**Industrial processes:**

**Bird behavior:**

**Mercury safety:**

## ARRIVAL PROTOCOL

### The Ascent
You'll arrive via the main elevator shaft - "a vertical channel with a platform that's winched up and down."

**Preparation for the elevator:**
- This will be a long, slow ascent
- Bring water, snacks, warm clothing
- Have motion sickness medication ready
- Use this time for final acclimatization
- Stay calm - it's your introduction to the mountain's character
- Pay attention - observe the shaft walls, infrastructure
- Note the distance traveled (how deep does this go?)

**First 24-48 hours:**
- Minimize exertion - let your body adjust
- Drink more water than you think you need (despite rationing)
- Don't sleep for long periods initially (altitude insomnia is normal)
- Observe before interacting
- Find where water is distributed
- Locate medical facilities
- Identify exits and emergency routes
- Stay near the arrival area until oriented

### Making Contact

**Social protocol:**
- You're entering a working industrial community
- Be respectful of people's labor and space
- Don't interfere with operations
- Offer trade goods before asking for help
- Watch and learn before asking questions
- Children may be friendlier initial contacts than adults
- The engineers and managers are higher status but may be suspicious
- Lower platform workers may be more welcoming but protective of space

**Finding accommodation:**
- You'll need to secure a platform space or shelter
- This may require payment, trade, or work commitment
- Start at designated newcomer areas if they exist
- Be prepared to sleep rough initially
- Look for other recent arrivals who might share space
- Bring your own shelter materials if possible

**Employment:**
- You may need to work to stay
- Unskilled labor positions are available but brutal
- If you have specialized skills (engineering, medical, literacy), advertise them
- Be prepared for dangerous work conditions
- Understand the company hierarchy
- Learn the schedule of shifts and industrial operations

## SPECIFIC HAZARDS AND HOW TO AVOID THEM

### Physical dangers:

**Falls:** 
- The central void is 60 meters across
- Platforms are connected by swaying bridges
- Always maintain three points of contact
- Use safety tethers in wind
- Never cross bridges while fatigued or impaired
- Watch for icy surfaces on metal

**Rockfall:**
- The mountain is still settling
- "Small rockfalls occur regularly"
- Wear a hard hat if possible
- Be alert for grinding sounds
- Know which areas are most unstable
- Don't linger under overhangs

**Industrial accidents:**
- Moving machinery is everywhere
- Hot metal, steam, electrical hazards
- Wear protective equipment
- Stay clear of active workshops unless invited
- Watch for warning signs and worker signals
- Never touch unfamiliar equipment

**Structural failure:**
- "Scissor-stairs are always failing"
- "Cable-stays snap and are replaced"
- Visually inspect any structure before trusting it
- Listen for stress sounds (creaking, popping)
- Have a backup route planned
- Carry rope for self-rescue

**Cold exposure:**
- Hypothermia can creep up without notice
- Keep moving, but not to exhaustion
- Change out of any damp clothing immediately
- Watch extremities for frostbite
- Seek warm areas (near generators, workshops)
- Sleep with core body heat preserved

### Environmental hazards:

**Air quality:**
- Coal smoke, metal dust, stone particles
- Wear respirator in industrial areas
- Limit exposure time
- If you develop a cough, take it seriously
- Higher platforms have cleaner air

**Water scarcity:**
- This is the city's obsession
- Never waste water
- Follow rationing protocols exactly
- Dehydration exacerbates altitude sickness
- Plan water intake carefully
- Don't assume you can buy more

**Altitude sickness:**
- Monitor your oxygen saturation
- Headache, nausea, confusion are warning signs
- Descend immediately if symptoms worsen
- Don't push through severe symptoms
- Help others recognize their symptoms

**Mercury exposure:**
- "More mercury than seems reasonable"
- Don't touch pooled mercury
- Avoid workshops with heavy mercury use
- Learn symptoms of mercury poisoning
- Document exposure for later medical treatment

### Anomalous hazards:

**Spatial distortion:**
- "Distances seem inconsistent in the shaft"
- Don't trust your perception of distance
- Time your movements
- Create landmark maps
- Never navigate while exhausted

**Electromagnetic effects:**
- Compasses spin in certain sectors
- Electronics may fail
- Lights flicker in synchronized patterns
- Bring non-electronic backups for everything
- Mark areas with EM anomalies
- Avoid these areas if possible

**The birds:**
- Thousands of birds, unpredictable movements
- They may predict danger (the cable-stay incident)
- Learn to read their behavior
- Don't disturb nesting areas
- Watch for mass flight pattern changes
- Consider them an early warning system

**Sealed sections:**
- "Platforms that seem to have been abandoned despite being structurally sound"
- Areas people avoid after dark
- There are stories about what's below
- Respect local warnings
- Don't explore alone
- If something feels wrong, it probably is

## LONG-TERM SURVIVAL CONSIDERATIONS

If you're staying more than a few days:

**Physiological adaptation:**
- Your body will begin producing more red blood cells
- This takes 2-4 weeks
- You'll gradually breathe easier
- But never assume you're fully adapted
- Monitor for chronic altitude sickness

**Water discipline:**
- "People have adapted their entire domestic lives to this constraint"
- Learn local water conservation techniques
- Reclaim condensation from your breath
- Minimize washing
- Adapt your hygiene practices
- Respect the water collection systems

**Social integration:**
- Learn the local dialect
- Participate in community spaces (market, tavern, shrine)
- Offer your skills
- Share news from below
- Respect hierarchies but build cross-class relationships
- Attend music sessions, storytelling
- Make friends with children (they know everything)

**Economic stability:**
- Establish reliable income
- Build trade networks
- Save resources for the elevator fee down
- Diversify your value (skills, goods, knowledge)
- Don't become dependent on single employer

**Psychological maintenance:**
- Establish routine
- Find meaning in daily tasks
- Build social connections
- Access what sunlight you can
- Exercise despite thin air
- Keep a journal
- Plan for eventual departure

**Exit strategy:**
- Always maintain means to leave
- Keep elevator fare saved
- Monitor your physical and mental state
- Know the elevator schedule
- Have a trigger point for departure
- Don't wait until you're too sick to descend

## WHAT TO EXPECT: REALISTIC TIMELINE

**Day 1-3: Crisis phase**
- Severe altitude sickness likely
- Headache, nausea, insomnia
- Everything is overwhelming
- Can barely function
- Consider this your introduction phase
- Don't make major decisions

**Week 1: Adjustment phase**
- Altitude symptoms moderate
- Begin understanding the layout
- Make initial social contacts
- Establish basic routine
- Still very uncomfortable
- Everything is difficult

**Week 2-4: Acclimatization phase**
- Body begins producing more red blood cells
- Can function more normally
- Understand social dynamics
- Have a role or occupation
- Still challenging but manageable
- Building local knowledge

**Month 2+: Adaptation phase**
- Move with economy like residents
- Breathe differently
- Understand the city's rhythms
- Integrated into social networks
- But never fully comfortable
- Always aware of the precariousness

## SPIRITUAL/PHILOSOPHICAL PREPARATION

This place will challenge your assumptions about human habitation:

**Accept the absurdity:**
- This city shouldn't exist
- It's sustained by pure human stubbornness
- Everything is harder than it needs to be
- There may be no good reason for any of it
- Make peace with this

**Confront vertigo metaphysically:**
- You're suspended in a hollow mountain
- Above you: broken sky
- Below you: depths unknown
- Around you: humans building impossible lives
- This is existentially vertiginous
- Prepare to feel very small and very fragile

**Understand industrial poetry:**
- There's a strange beauty here
- The angular geometry, the layered sound
- The birds wheeling through the shaft
- The dedication of the workers
- Let yourself appreciate it
- It will make survival easier

**Respect the mountain:**
- It was never meant to exist at this height
- It's still settling, still deciding its shape
- You're a guest in its throat
- It could end this experiment anytime
- Maintain appropriate humility

## FINAL THOUGHTS

The Scissor Nest is not a place for casual tourists. It's a vertical industrial city at lethal altitude, sustained by grueling labor in conditions that push human physiological limits. People live there because they must, or because they've found something there worth the constant discomfort, or because they were born there and know nothing else.

If you go:

- Prepare as if for a high-altitude mountaineering expedition crossed with an industrial work camp
- Expect to be cold, oxygen-deprived, and uncomfortable every moment
- Bring triple the supplies you think you need
- Have a clear reason for going and a clear exit strategy
- Accept that you might need to leave earlier than planned
- Don't go alone if possible
- Tell people below where you're going and when you'll return
- Leave detailed medical and contact information
- Make peace with the possibility you might not return

But also:

- Open yourself to the strangeness
- Watch the birds wheel through the central shaft
- Listen to the music echoing from platform to platform
- Observe the children who navigate this impossible city with ease
- Marvel at human capacity to build lives anywhere
- Learn from people who've adapted to extremes
- Experience a place that shouldn't exist but does

The Scissor Nest is a pocket city carved into an impossible mountain, populated by people who've decided that here, of all places, is where they'll live. It's precarious, industrial, strange, difficult, and alive. It will challenge every aspect of your physical and psychological resilience.

Prepare accordingly. Respect the altitude. Respect the mountain. Respect the people who've made this their home.

And remember: the elevator goes both ways. There's no shame in descending if you need to. Survival is not defeat.
