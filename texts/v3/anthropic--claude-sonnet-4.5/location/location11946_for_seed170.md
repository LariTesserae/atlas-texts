# The Calcine Processionals

The plateau extends for twenty-seven miles in each cardinal direction, lifted eight thousand feet above the surrounding hardpan. Its surface is not stone but something that was stone—a substance called clinker-white by those who once tried to name it, though no one comes here to name things anymore. The material resembles porcelain that has been fired, shattered, and fired again in place. It forms vast hexagonal tiles, each fifty to two hundred feet across, separated by hair-thin fissures that glow dull orange when the wind direction changes. The surface is hot enough that standing water would vanish in seconds, though there is no water. There hasn't been water here for so long that the chemical memory of it has baked out of the ground.

The light is clean and constant, a white-gold intensity that comes from a sun you can look at too long without realizing the damage being done. Shadows have crisp edges. There are no clouds, but the air itself has texture—heat makes visible rivers, columnar thermals rising in slow rotation, and something else: a tendency for space to repeat itself at the periphery of vision, the same distant formation appearing in two directions simultaneously until you turn your head and the world decides which version it was showing you.

## The Engines

At irregular intervals across the plateau stand the engines. They are not structures, exactly, though they were built. Each rises twelve to forty feet: assemblages of cast iron, forge-black steel, copper gone green then black again from heat, ceramic collars, riveted drums, and shafts that turn. They have no apparent purpose. They connect to nothing. Each engine is unique in configuration but shares a grammar with the others—vertical orientations, rotational elements, heat-exchange surfaces covered in fins and flanges, pistons that rise and fall into chambers open to the air.

They produce tremendous noise.

The sound is layered: the bass thrum of flywheels, the rhythmic clank of connecting rods, the whistle-shriek of steam venting (though there is no water, something vents), the metallic screaming of bearings that should have failed centuries ago but continue, the whoompf of combustion chambers firing in syncopated patterns. Each engine contributes its own voice to a plateau-wide composition that never quite repeats. The sound reflects off the porcelain surface, creating interference patterns where the noise cancels into pockets of relative quiet—cathedral spaces of merely loud sound surrounded by walls of cacophony.

The engines run on heat. They drink it from the air, from the ground, from the degraded sunlight, from their own operation. They are not perpetual motion—they clearly consume something, work against something—but what they produce is unclear. No exhaust but heat. No product but motion. No transmission of power to any external system.

Between the engines: nothing. Vast empty spaces where the white-gold air trembles. The distance between machines averages half a mile. The plateau holds perhaps ninety engines, scattered without pattern, some clustered in trios or quintets, others isolated in the emptiness.

## The Fauna

The animals are the overwhelming fact of this place.

They move in processionals—not herds, not migrations, but something more formal. Lines of creatures that extend from one horizon to another, traveling at the speed of stone. You can watch for an hour and perceive no change in position, but return the next day and the procession has advanced three hundred yards.

The creatures themselves defy inventory. Each is unique, and there are thousands. They range from the size of dogs to the size of railcars. Their bodies suggest arthropods, mollusks, echinoderms, machine components, architectural elements, crystalline growths, and things that have no referent. 

One resembles a horizontal millipede twelve feet long, its segments made of overlapping bronze scales that ring like bells as it moves, forty legs stepping in rippling sequence, each leg ending in a ceramic cup. No head, no tail—or both ends are heads, or neither. It has moved seventeen feet since yesterday.

Another stands in place, nine feet tall, a vertical arrangement of seven spherical carapaces threaded on a central column of articulated bone. Each sphere rotates independently, covered in geometric patterns that might be natural coloration or might be deteriorating text. The lowest sphere has six legs, currently folded. The highest has sensory stalks—probably. It rocks slightly, a motion that takes four minutes to complete.

A thing like a collapsed parachute made of membranous chitin lies draped over a slight rise in the porcelain, breathing—its entire body expands and contracts on a forty-minute cycle. What might be mouths or spiracles open and close along its edges, revealing inner surfaces that gleam like oiled metal.

Three creatures locked in a formation that might be combat, courtship, or symbiosis: they have held this configuration for so long that their carapaces have fused at the contact points, creating a single architectural entity fifteen feet across, still moving on twelve legs total, still producing a grinding purr from somewhere within the composite body.

The processionals follow routes that curve and intersect across the plateau. Where paths cross, creatures sometimes touch—a antenna brushing a flank, a leg stepping over a prostrate form—and where they touch, something happens. Not always. But sometimes. A sound like glass cracking in heat. A smell of hot stone and corroded metal. A localized distortion where the air looks compressed, thickened. The creatures separate and continue their infinitesimal journeys.

They make sounds. Clicks and chitters, but also low-frequency vibrations felt in the chest, harmonic overtones that shouldn't be possible from bodies that size, and occasionally something like speech—syllables without language, phonemes arranged in patterns that feel grammatical. These sounds add to the engine noise, weaving through it, sometimes synchronizing with mechanical rhythms, sometimes creating dissonances that make your teeth ache.

The creatures do not eat, or if they eat, it is not anything visible. They do not produce waste. They show no interest in the engines, though their paths sometimes pass within feet of the machines. They do not acknowledge observation. Whether they are aware is a question that dissolves upon consideration.

New creatures appear—not born, not arriving, just present where they were not before. In the empty spaces between processionals, something that wasn't there becomes something that has always been there, and the mind struggles to identify the moment of transition. Similarly, creatures vanish. You might follow a particular individual for days, learning its specific configuration of carapace plates and leg arrangements, and then realize you haven't seen it for hours, and cannot remember when you last did.

## The Breakdowns

Reality here is not fully committed to being real.

The glitches are constant but subtle. A shadow falls at the wrong angle for fifteen seconds, then corrects. An engine's flywheel rotates through itself—not phasing through but occupying the same space from two different moments, the motion doubled and overlapping, before resolving. Distance becomes negotiable: a landmark that was three hundred yards away is suddenly five hundred yards away, though you have not moved and neither has it.

Temperature fluctuates in localized pockets without cause. You might step from 140-degree air into a ten-foot sphere where the temperature is 138 degrees, then back to 141. The change is perceptible, distinct, but meaningless.

Sounds occasionally arrive before their sources—the clang of metal on ceramic reaches your ears three seconds before you see a creature's leg complete its step. Usually the desynchronization is minor, a fraction of a second, but it happens often enough that you stop trusting the sequence of events.

The most pronounced breakdowns involve duplication and omission. You witness a creature in two locations simultaneously, both versions moving through their slow processional paths, both equally real. After several minutes, one version simply isn't anymore—not fading, not departing, just subtracted from existence. Or an engine stands clear and solid, then for a period simply isn't there, a negative space in the shape of machinery, the sound continuing from the absence, and then the engine returns as though it never left.

Geometric impossibilities occur. A creature's body extends in a direction that is not up, down, or any horizontal bearing—a fifth direction that the eye can perceive but the mind cannot retain. Looking away and looking back, the creature is normally configured, but the memory of that other extension remains, sourceless and disturbing.

Time itself seems negotiable. The engines run, the creatures process, the air shimmers, but duration becomes strange. An hour feels like twenty minutes. Ten minutes stretch into subjective eons. There is no day-night cycle to anchor perception—the sun maintains its white-gold constancy, perhaps moving through a sky, perhaps stationary. Attempts to track time mechanically fail; watches run at inconsistent rates, speeding and slowing without pattern.

Text appears occasionally on the porcelain surface—not carved, not written, but present. Characters that might be letters, might be circuit diagrams, might be the tracks left by creatures' feet given meaningful arrangement. The text is always incomplete, truncated mid-phrase, offering fragments: "...established the processional in accordance with..." or "...heat death being unacceptable, the engines..." or "...they no longer remember what..." The words fade over hours, not erasing but becoming ambiguous, the characters shifting into resemblance with natural cracks in the ceramic ground.

The air sometimes crystallizes. Not freezing—it's far too hot for that—but taking on structure, visible lattices forming in emptiness, geometric patterns that hover for minutes before dissolving. These crystallizations seem to cause the creatures pain or discomfort; they slow further, producing distress sounds, until the air returns to its normal semi-liquid shimmer.

## The Depth

The plateau is not solid. This becomes apparent near its edges, where the porcelain surface has cracked more dramatically, revealing that the clinker-white material is only ten to thirty feet deep. Below it: the engines extend downward.

The engines you see on the surface are just the upper portions of vastly larger mechanisms. Looking into the cracks reveals shafts descending hundreds of feet, drive trains connecting to sub-assemblies, power transmission systems of gears and belts and things that resemble organic tissue, all of it moving, all of it contributing to the sound. The engines are not standing on the plateau; they are threaded through it, part of its structure, or it is part of their structure.

Deeper still—visible only in the few places where erosion has created genuine chasms—there are more engines. Layers of them. The entire plateau might be a single vast machine, or an accretion of machines, or machines that have grown through geological time into something that resembles a landscape.

The creatures know about the depths. Sometimes a processional leads directly to a crack or chasm, and creatures simply continue, walking down vertical surfaces or descending into darkness, vanishing into the engine depths. Whether they emerge elsewhere is unknown. Probably they do. The number of creatures seems roughly constant, though counting is impossible.

## The Rules

Through observation, certain patterns emerge that might be rules:

The engines never stop. Not slowing, not pausing. Whatever fuel they consume is inexhaustible or renewable or they are running down from some initial store that will outlast any observer.

The creatures do not interact aggressively. They touch, they intersect, they sometimes merge temporarily, but violence seems absent from their vocabulary. Whether this is peaceful coexistence or the absence of the category of conflict is unclear.

The heat increases in proximity to engines but decreases in the empty spaces between them, creating a thermal landscape that doesn't quite match the visual landscape. The coolest spots—still over 120 degrees—are furthest from machinery, where only creatures process through.

Standing still is possible but uncomfortable. The wrongness accumulates. After thirty minutes of immobility, the glitches focus on you. Duplications of your own body appear in peripheral vision. Sounds you didn't make echo from your position. The recommendation—from whom? there is no one here to recommend things—is to keep moving, even slowly, even in small circles.

The processionals have directionality, but it's not geographic. They're not traveling north or south; they're traveling toward or away from something that has no location. Following a processional forward feels like approaching something. Following it backward feels like retreating. What is being approached or retreated from never appears.

At certain times—not predictable, but recurring—all the engines synchronize for exactly forty-seven seconds. The noise becomes a single coherent rhythm, a pulse that vibrates through the porcelain and into bone. During these synchronizations, the creatures stop entirely, becoming absolutely still, and the reality breakdowns cease. The world is completely stable, normally causal, trustworthy. Then the synchronization ends, engines drift back into their individual rhythms, creatures resume their infinitesimal progress, and the glitches return.

## The Atmosphere

The air tastes of hot metal and ceramics fired past their optimal temperature. Breathing requires adjustment; the heat sears the throat initially, then the body adapts, then you realize you've been here too long and should leave but cannot remember where leaving would take you.

Dust is minimal despite the dryness. The porcelain surface doesn't erode in the usual way—it sublimates perhaps, or is consumed by the engines, or reconstitutes itself. What little dust exists is not mineral but something else: fine black particles that might be ash, might be shed carapace material from the creatures, might be condensed from the air when it briefly crystallizes.

The smell varies by location. Near engines: hot oil, ozone, burning insulation, the acrid tang of electrical arcing. In empty spaces: almost nothing, a neutral scent like heated stone. Near creatures: complex organic chemistry that shouldn't survive these temperatures—chitin, lipids, something resembling honey, something resembling rust.

Wind occurs but erratically. Sometimes dead calm for hours. Sometimes a steady fifteen-mile-per-hour flow from the east. Sometimes localized whirlwinds that move across the plateau like lost animals, dust devils made of heat distortion more than dust, spiraling between engines and dispersing against their iron flanks.

The horizon is always visible, crisp and clear except when reality breakdowns make it temporarily negotiable. The edges of the plateau are distinct: sharp drop-offs where the clinker-white simply ends and the hardpan below becomes visible, eight thousand feet down, a desert floor that might extend forever in all directions, empty, offering no destinations.

No stars appear when the sun—if it moves at all—reaches its lowest point. Either there is only sunlight here, eternal bright day, or the sky itself is luminous, or the distinction between sun and sky has become irrelevant.

## The Experience

To stand here is to feel obsolete.

Not threatened—the creatures ignore you, the engines care nothing for organic presence, the heat is survivable with preparation. But obsolete. This is a place organized around principles that don't include observation, don't require witness, don't operate for any purpose that translates to human intention.

The engines run. The creatures process. The heat maintains. The glitches occur. All of it in a vast slow cycle that was already ancient when you arrived and will continue unchanged by your presence when you leave—if leaving is possible, if there is an outside to this place, if "arriving" and "leaving" are operations that work here.

Time accumulates strangely. You might be here for hours or weeks. The constancy makes duration arbitrary. Hunger and thirst are absent or irrelevant—perhaps you brought supplies, perhaps you don't need them, perhaps need itself functions differently on the plateau.

The sound becomes a kind of silence. After the initial overwhelm, the brain categorizes the engine noise as baseline, and within that roar, you begin to hear the smaller sounds: individual creatures clicking, the whisper of wind through engine components, the tiny stress fractures forming and healing in the porcelain surface, your own heartbeat as a counterrhythm to the machinery.

Meaning accumulates and disperses. For hours, you're convinced the processionals are tracing a vast pattern, a continental-scale diagram that would be comprehensible from sufficient altitude. Then the pattern dissolves and you see only creatures walking, motivated by nothing but the walking itself. Then a creature turns its eyestalks—if they are eyes, if "turning" describes the motion—directly toward you, and you're certain it knows something, is trying to convey something, and then it continues its slow rotation and you realize it was simply moving through its range of motion, and you are once again obsolete.

## The Edges

Near the plateau's boundaries, things intensify. The engines are more densely clustered, their sounds creating interference patterns that can damage hearing or psyche—standing zones where the noise cancels into a too-loud silence that feels like pressure. The creatures process in tighter formations, their paths converging, sometimes forced to climb over each other in slow-motion tangles that take days to resolve.

The reality breakdowns at the edges are more pronounced. Duplications occur in triplicates or higher multiples. Time loops become possible—you witness the same event three times in succession, each iteration slightly different, before causality reasserts. The porcelain surface sometimes isn't there at all, revealing the engine depths directly, vast churning mechanisms extending down into darkness, and you're standing on nothing, on the idea of surface, until the surface returns and you're fine, you were always fine.

At the actual rim of the plateau, where the clinker-white ends in a sheer drop, some creatures simply walk off, continuing their processional into open air, walking downward at their usual infinitesimal pace, descending the vertical cliff face or simply proceeding through air as though it were solid. They don't fall. They don't fly. They walk, and space accommodates them, and they vanish into distance.

Occasionally, something climbs up from below. A creature not seen before, mounting the cliff face or rising through the air, achieving the plateau surface and beginning its own processional, adding to the complexity. Where these creatures come from—whether they've been circling the plateau for geological ages, whether they're generated by the engine depths, whether they're arriving from elsewhere—is not answerable.

## What Was

There is evidence of intentionality.

The engines were built, clearly. Their construction required metallurgy, engineering, coordination of labor, purpose. The clinker-white platform is almost certainly artificial, a prepared surface, though what it was prepared for is lost.

Among the creatures, a small percentage—perhaps five percent—show signs of having been designed. Too-regular geometry in their carapaces, threaded joints that resemble manufactured components, surface textures that look stamped rather than grown. The other ninety-five percent might be natural or might be constructed so well that no seam shows.

The text fragments that occasionally appear suggest documentation, record-keeping, project management. "...processional in accordance with..." implies rules, agreements, protocols. "...heat death being unacceptable..." implies a problem and a proposed solution. "...they no longer remember what..." implies forgetting on a vast scale.

This was a project. The engines, the creatures, the plateau, the heat—all of it was made to accomplish something. And either the project succeeded so completely that it no longer needs maintenance or oversight, or it failed so absolutely that it continues running empty, or the distinction between success and failure has no meaning here.

How long ago? The erosion parameter suggests long establishment, things settled into equilibrium. But the engines show no rust, no material fatigue. The porcelain is weathered but not degraded. The creatures are ancient but not declining. This place might be ten thousand years old or ten million. It might be from the future, a remnant of events that haven't yet occurred, temporally displaced. The glitches make chronology optional.

## The Present Continuous

Now: eleven engines are visible from this vantage, their flywheels catching light. The nearest is eighty feet away, a vertical assembly of three pistons driving a horizontal crankshaft that turns a drum covered in copper fins. It has been making the same five-second rhythm—thump, thump, hiss, clatter, thump—for as long as observation has continued.

Seventeen creatures are visible in the mid-distance. Most are in processional formation, moving along their invisible rails. One is simply present, not moving, a dome-shaped entity twelve feet across, covered in hexagonal plates that open and close like breathing gills, though nothing emerges from them. It might be resting. It might be dead. It might be in a phase of existence where "resting" and "dead" aren't distinguishable.

The temperature is 141 degrees. The air pressure is normal but feels wrong—too dense or too thin depending on location. The light is constant white-gold, casting crisp shadows at angles that suggest late afternoon, though "late afternoon" might be eternal here.

A sound approaches: the click-drag-click of something moving. In three hours, a creature will pass through this location—a six-legged form like a mechanical spider, each leg ending in a broad ceramic pad, its body a fused mass of segments that might once have been separate components. It has been on this path for unknown duration, will continue for unknown distance, is neither hunting nor fleeing, is simply expressing its nature as a processing thing in a landscape of processes.

The engines run. The heat radiates. The glitches shimmer at the edges of perception. The creatures continue their continental-scale unknowable journey. The plateau extends to its precise boundaries and stops, a geometric assertion in an empty world.

Nothing here needs you. Nothing here notices absence or presence. This is not hostility. This is not indifference. This is something operating at a level where witness and witnessed are category errors, where the question "why" dissolves into the fact of continuation.

The sound of engines. The heat of old purpose. The slow moving of forms through space that occasionally forgets to be space.

The plateau exists. You are here or not here—it makes no difference to the processionals.
