# The Throat Geode

You find it where the mountain splits itself open—not a cave, not a crevasse, but something else. A vertical gash in black basalt, still sharp-edged from whatever tectonic spasm birthed it perhaps only decades ago. The stone edges are keen enough to cut. The opening is narrow, barely wide enough to wedge yourself sideways into, and the summer heat radiating from the rock face makes the air shimmer and gasp.

Inside, the space opens just enough. A pocket. A room the size of a modest chamber, maybe twelve feet at its widest point, extending upward in an irregular chimney that twists as it climbs. You cannot see the top. Hot air rises from below, carrying moisture that shouldn't exist in this volcanic highland, and the temperature inside the throat hovers near the threshold of what flesh can tolerate—perhaps 110, perhaps 115 degrees Fahrenheit. You sweat immediately. The walls radiate stored heat even as condensation films them in paradox.

The walls themselves are dense with *them*.

They mass on every surface, layered three and four deep in places, clustering so thickly that the black basalt underneath becomes only a hypothesis. Creatures—but that word fails. Presences. Entities. Bodies that exist in configurations that make your eye want to slide away, to refuse what it's seeing, to find some other explanation.

They are approximately the size of human hands, though their boundaries are uncertain. Each one seems to be primarily a translucent, crystalline structure—geometric lattices that catch and fracture the diffuse light filtering down from above. But the lattices are *wet*. Perpetually wet. Water streams through channels in their bodies, or perhaps *is* their bodies, flowing in patterns that seem both circulatory and purposeful. The crystal geometries pulse gently, expanding and contracting, drawing water up from somewhere below, from the very rock perhaps, and exhaling fine mists that rise in the heated air.

Some have what might be sensory organs—clusters of dark nodes arranged in patterns of four or five or seven, never six, never three. Some trail thin filaments that wave in currents you cannot feel, filaments that look like they're made of braided water held in form by surface tension alone, except they persist, they don't collapse. Some of the bodies are roughly radially symmetrical; others seem to have been designed by a geometry that doesn't care about symmetry, that finds meaning in off-balance arrangements, in angles that are almost-but-not-quite right.

They cluster densest about two-thirds of the way up the chamber where water weeps steadily from a horizontal crack in the stone. Here they mass body-against-body, their crystal lattices sometimes seeming to merge at the edges, information or substance or something else passing between them. The water from the crack flows *through* them before it reaches the walls, and they do something to it—filter it, transform it, worship it, become it temporarily. The water that emerges on the other side of their congregation is somehow different. It catches light differently. It sounds different when it drips.

The dripping is almost the only sound. That, and a whisper of escaping steam, and very occasionally—every few minutes—a soft crystalline *tick* as one of the entities shifts position or adjusts its structure. The quiet is profound, nearly sacred, broken only by water in its various states of transformation. Even your breathing seems intrusive, though they show no reaction to your presence. Perhaps they cannot perceive you. Perhaps they choose not to.

Looking closely—which requires you to lean near, to let the heat press against your face—you can see that they're not quite solid. Within the crystal matrices, things move. Threads of darker material pulse and flow. Bubbles rise through chambers. Some contain what look like primitive tools, or maybe they ARE primitive tools, or maybe the tools are organs: tiny implements of worked stone, each no larger than a grain of rice, suspended in the fluid interiors and moving with apparent purpose. Cutting. Shaping. Building or rebuilding the lattice from within.

The entities near the bottom of the chamber, where it narrows to a crack you could never fit through, are different from those higher up. More compact. Simpler geometries. Younger, perhaps, or ancestral forms. They are migrating upward, you realize, after watching for long enough. Very slowly, over hours or days, they climb toward the water-weep, toward the congregation, moving by methods that don't quite make sense—they seem to partially dissolve, to flow a few inches upward, to reconstitute. Or they extend crystalline limbs that find purchase on invisible irregularities in the stone.

The ones at the very top, in the narrowing chimney where the heat is most extreme, are doing something else entirely.

They have arranged themselves in a spiral pattern that follows the twist of the stone. They are no longer moving. Their crystal matrices have become more elaborate, more impossibly complex, growing structures that branch and rebranch in fractral iterations, and they are *singing*—no, not singing, but producing something that is to sound what a shadow is to light. A negative of sound. Anti-sound. You feel it in your teeth and in your gut, a presence that makes the silence around it somehow more silent, that creates pockets where even the memory of sound seems uncertain.

And where their crystalline branches touch each other in the spiral, reality is... compromised.

The air warps. The stone behind them becomes ambiguous—is it there? is it somewhere else? You see through to places that are not this mountain, this world. Glimpses of other pockets, other throats, other congregations. Parallel versions bleeding through. In one flash you see the same chamber but flooded entirely with water, the entities swimming freely, no longer bound to stone. In another the walls are covered not with crystal-water beings but with pure geometric light, conscious equations. In another there is only heat, only motion, only transformation without body.

Your vision blurs. You blink and you're seeing double—the chamber as it is, and the chamber as five or six or twelve other things simultaneously. The entities in the spiral have done something to this place, have made it thin, have turned it into a point where the rules are negotiable.

You retreat slightly, back toward the narrow entrance, and the visions fade but don't entirely disappear. They linger at the edges of perception.

The heat is becoming difficult. You've been inside perhaps twenty minutes and your head is beginning to pound. But there's something else you notice now: the moisture that condenses on the walls, that weeps from the stone, that flows through the crystalline bodies—it's coming from *below*. Heat rises, yes, but the water rises with it, against all sense. The mountain must be hollow beneath this chamber, must contain reserves that defy geology, that belong to some other order of physical law.

And the entities know this. They have found this place, or made this place, or called this place into being. They gather here because here the world is young and uncertain, still deciding what it wants to be, and they participate in that decision. They are not quite alive as you understand life—they have no carbon, no cells, no DNA—but they are not not-alive either. They are something adjacent to life, something that occupies a category your mind has no word for.

When you finally edge back out into the harsh sunlight, gasping in the relatively cooler air (which is still perhaps 95 degrees; the highlands in summer are merciless), you find that you've left no trace inside. The entities continue their slow processes. The water continues its impossible upward journey. The spiral at the top continues to thin reality, continues to whisper anti-sound into the throat of the mountain.

You look around at the volcanic landscape—black stone stretching in jagged flows toward distant peaks, no plants, no visible water, nothing but mineral severity and heat shimmer. No one would guess what hides in that vertical split. Nothing on the outside announces the presence of the congregation.

How many other pockets like this exist? How many other throats have opened in the young stone, how many other chambers contain their own impossible ecologies? The mountain gives no answer. It keeps its secrets vertically, tucked into cracks and chimneys and spaces between.

You walk away, but you feel changed. Something about the air in that chamber, the moisture, the heat, the anti-sound—it's left a residue. For hours afterward, you continue to see faint geometric patterns at the edges of your vision. You hear silence more clearly than before. Water, when you find some to drink at your camp, tastes different. More itself. More everything.

That night, lying in your tent, you realize you can still feel them. Not as individuals but as a presence, a dense gathering of almost-life doing its patient work inside the mountain. They are processing water and heat and stone and something else—meaning, perhaps, or topology, or the still-soft rules of this newly-formed place. They are primitive tool-users, if tools can be made of crystal and purpose. They are overwhelming in their abundance, even though they exist in a space smaller than most bedrooms. They are breaking reality gently, carefully, with the precision of jewelers.

You do not return the next day. Or the next. The throat is not for returning to. It's not for you at all, except as something to have witnessed once, to carry with you as proof that the world is far stranger and far more various than your categories allow.

But sometimes, in certain lights, you see them again—the crystalline forms, the water flowing through impossible channels, the dark nodes that might be eyes. They're not really there, of course. They're many miles away, if they're anywhere.

Or perhaps distance has also become negotiable. Perhaps the spiral at the top of the throat has made such distinctions uncertain.

Perhaps once you've been inside a place where reality thins, you never quite leave.
