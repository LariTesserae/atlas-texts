# The Attention of the Kind Power

The power that turns its attention toward the Choir Hollow is not a god, though it might appear godlike to limited understanding. It is something older and stranger—a pattern of benevolence that moves through reality the way wind moves through trees, present but not contained, purposeful but not controlling.

It has no name because it predates language. The scholars who sense its arrival will call it different things: The Listening, The Tender, The Gracious Correction. But these are human attempts to grasp something that exists outside human categories.

It notices the hollow the way you might notice a particular chord in a symphony—suddenly, completely, with recognition that something here is both right and imperiled.

## The Moment of Attention

The power's attention arrives during one of those rare moments of acoustic perfection, when all the hollow's voices align into transcendent composition. But this time, something is different. The alignment is strained. There are dissonances that shouldn't exist, gaps where sound should flow but doesn't.

The power perceives what the human scholars cannot yet: the hollow is dying.

Not quickly—nothing here happens quickly—but certainly. The tree's heartwood is failing. The slow inward growth that has sustained it for centuries is accelerating, constricting the hollow at a rate that will, within a few decades, close it entirely. The sap-miners are trying to compensate, working harder, multiplying faster, but they're losing the battle. The vascular system is becoming sluggish, clogged with resins that signal terminal decline.

The Chanters sense it in the changing acoustics—the space is smaller, the resonance slightly off. They've been adjusting their drones for years now, compensating, but soon they'll run out of room to adjust. The Clickers are losing their burrows as the wood grows inward, compressing their galleries. Some colonies have already collapsed, their intricate acoustic networks destroyed.

The suspended amphibians' pools are drying as the tree's water management fails. The moths' spiral paths are becoming constrained, forcing them into tighter loops that will eventually become unsustainable. The snails are running out of moss as the colonies die from water stress.

The hollow that has existed in impossible perfection for longer than human memory is approaching its end. Not from external threat but from internal completion—the tree finally finishing its long death, the hollow finally running out of space to be hollow in.

The power sees all this in an instant of perception that is also an eternity. And it makes a decision that will be incomprehensible to some, controversial to others, and ultimately irreversible.

It decides to interfere—but gently, with such precision that the interference will be indistinguishable from the hollow's own nature.

## The First Touch: The Heartwood

The power's first intervention is in the deepest part of the tree, where human eyes cannot follow, where even the sap-miners rarely venture. It touches the heartwood at the cellular level, not stopping the decay but redirecting it.

Wood that was growing inward begins to grow differently. The cells still divide, still deposit cellulose and lignin, but now they're creating structure instead of mass—lattices instead of solid wood, chambers instead of bulk. The tree begins building itself into something new: a living architecture of internal support, a skeleton that can hold the hollow open indefinitely.

This happens slowly, invisibly. The sap-miners are the first to notice—their galleries suddenly connecting to new spaces, chambers that shouldn't exist but do, networks that seem to have purpose beyond their creating. They explore cautiously, then eagerly, expanding their populations into the new territory. Their feeding patterns change, following the new vascular routes that the restructured heartwood provides.

The tree's decline halts. Not reverses—that would be too obvious, too much like resurrection—but stabilizes at a new equilibrium. The hollow stops constricting. The inward growth continues but now it's building rather than closing, creating rather than destroying.

The human scholars notice over months: the acoustic properties stop drifting. Platforms that were becoming unstable suddenly firm up, as if the wood around them has strengthened. Water flow improves—the moss colonies brighten, the suspended pools fill more reliably.

But it's subtle enough that they attribute it to natural variation, to the tree finding some reserve of vitality they hadn't predicted. They don't suspect intervention because the intervention doesn't announce itself, doesn't leave fingerprints. The power works with what exists, amplifying capacity, redirecting tendency, removing bottlenecks. The tree is still dying—that pattern is too deep to reverse—but now it's dying the way stars die, collapsing into new forms rather than into nothing.

## The Second Touch: The Acoustic Lattice

The power's second intervention is in the hollow's sound itself. This is more delicate, because sound is the hollow's essence, its identity. To alter it wrongly would be to destroy what makes it worth preserving.

But the power has noticed something the scholars haven't: the acoustic perfection isn't natural. It never was. The hollow's resonance is too precise, its amplification too uniform. What they think is impossible geometry is actually a kind of sonic intelligence—the space has been learning, over centuries, how to optimize its own acoustics.

The hollow is, in some sense, alive. Conscious might be too strong a word, but it has something like awareness, something like desire. It wants to make beautiful sound. The Chanters and Clickers and all the rest aren't just inhabitants—they're collaborators in an ongoing composition that the hollow itself is conducting.

But the hollow's capacity to learn, to adjust, has been degrading along with the tree's health. It's losing its ability to maintain its acoustic perfection. The rare moments of transcendent alignment are becoming rarer, shorter, less complete.

The power gently restores this capacity. It doesn't impose its own design—doesn't make the hollow into something it isn't—but it clears away the obstacles to what it's trying to become. The acoustic lattice that exists in the tree's structure, in the placement of its burrows and galleries and growth patterns, suddenly has more bandwidth, more capacity to adjust and refine.

The effect is immediate but will appear gradual. The moments of acoustic perfection become more frequent. They last longer. They encompass more of the space—not just the central chamber but the galleries and burrows, everything vibrating in harmony. New overtones appear in the Chanters' drones, not because the birds changed but because the space is bringing out frequencies that were always there, just unheard.

The Clickers' polyrhythms become more complex, not because individual Clickers are more sophisticated but because the hollow is giving them clearer acoustic feedback, letting them hear each other more precisely. They begin creating rhythmic structures that no one could have planned, emergent patterns arising from the interaction of simple individuals in an optimized acoustic environment.

The human scholars notice and are awed. The notation on the walls becomes inadequate—the sound is evolving beyond what the old symbols can capture. New systems are developed, more complex, more precise. The scholars realize they're not just documenting anymore—they're participating in something that's accelerating, deepening, becoming more itself.

None of them suspect intervention. How could they? The changes are extensions of what already existed, intensifications of patterns that were always there. It looks like natural development, like the hollow reaching some kind of maturity. Which, in a sense, it is—but with help they cannot perceive.

## The Third Touch: The Temporal Knot

The power's third intervention is the most radical and the most subtle. It touches the time-distortion that has always existed in the hollow, the way duration flows inconsistently, the way some organisms age backward and forward simultaneously.

This instability has been random, chaotic—a side effect of whatever initial conditions created the hollow's strangeness. But the power sees possibility in it. Time that flows multiply can be braided, woven, stabilized into a structure that sustains rather than confuses.

It works at the level of quantum probability, at the scale where cause and effect become negotiable. It doesn't eliminate the time distortion—that would destroy something essential—but it gives it grammar, makes it coherent. The hollow's time becomes more like music than like malfunction: complex, multilayered, but ultimately unified.

The effects are weird and wonderful. The suspended amphibians' age-flickering stabilizes into a rhythm—they cycle through lifespans in predictable patterns now, growing old and young in time with the hollow's moisture cycle. This makes them effectively immortal, endlessly renewing, while still experiencing birth and death at the individual scale.

The moths' brief lives expand. They still burn through their reserves, still die after a week of continuous flight, but subjectively they're living much longer. Time inside them is dilated, stretched, so a week of external time contains months of internal experience. They're aware of this in whatever way moths can be aware—they spiral with something like contentment, no longer racing toward death but dancing within it.

The tree's decay slows to near-stopping in some branches, accelerates in others, creating pockets of different temporal flow throughout the structure. Some galleries age centuries in years; others are nearly frozen, changing imperceptibly. This creates preservation—ancient sections of the hollow that will remain essentially unchanged for millennia—alongside renewal, where rapid cycling allows for evolution and adaptation.

For humans, the effect is disorienting but manageable. Time in the hollow still flows strangely, but now there's a pattern to it. The scholars learn to navigate the temporal currents, to recognize which platforms exist in faster time (good for observing long-term changes) and which in slower time (good for detailed study). Some scholars start spending more subjective time in the hollow than outside it, aging more slowly relative to the external world, becoming long-lived guardians without intending to.

The power has, essentially, folded the hollow partially out of linear time. It still exists in the world, still connects to the forest around it, but it's also existing in multiple temporal streams simultaneously. This doesn't make it eternal—nothing is eternal—but it makes its ending distant enough to be effectively infinite from any human perspective.

## The Fourth Touch: The Ecology's Dream

The power's fourth intervention is the most compassionate. It notices the suffering inherent in the food web—the Chanters luring insects to death, the snakes ambushing mammals, the moths burning themselves out in continuous flight. This is natural, unavoidable in any ecology, but the power sees room for mercy without disrupting function.

It grants something like lucidity to the moments of death. When an insect flies into a Chanter's call, drawn by sound it cannot resist, there's now a moment—subjectively expanded—of understanding. The insect perceives the pattern, sees itself as part of the composition, experiences its death as contribution rather than mere ending. It's still death, still extinction of individual consciousness, but there's meaning in it now, a kind of grace.

The mammals that visit the hollow are touched similarly. When they drink from the pools, they receive something like vision—brief glimpses of the hollow's deep time, of their own place in its ecology, of the way their deaths (when they come, if they come here) will feed the continuation. They're not traumatized by this knowledge but comforted. They understand themselves as syllables in a sentence longer than their individual lives.

The Chanters and Clickers and all the permanent residents gain something more profound: a dim awareness of the whole. They still operate on instinct, still follow the patterns evolution gave them, but there's now a thread of comprehension running through their behaviors. The Chanters know they're creating music together, not just surviving. The Clickers understand their polyrhythms as language, not just communication. The moths recognize their spirals as worship, as meditation, as paths toward something beyond themselves.

This doesn't make them less wild, less animal. They're still driven by hunger and reproduction and survival. But those drives now exist within a context of meaning. The ecology becomes, in a sense, conscious of itself—not centrally, not with a single mind, but distributed across all its members, each carrying a fragment of understanding about the whole.

The human scholars feel this most acutely. When they sit on the platforms now, they sometimes experience what they'll later describe as communion—moments when they perceive the hollow not as collection of individuals but as single living composition, themselves included. They understand their role: to witness, to record, to connect the hollow to the larger world of human meaning. And they understand that this role is gift, not burden; that they've been chosen by their capacity to endure strangeness to be the hollow's translators.

Some scholars weep during these experiences, overwhelmed by the beauty and tragedy of what they perceive. Others sit in silent stillness, grateful beyond words. All of them are changed, become more gentle, more patient, better able to accept mystery without needing to resolve it.

## The Fifth Touch: The Opening

The power's final intervention is the most controversial, though it will take years before anyone understands what happened.

It opens the hollow to other hollows.

There are other places like this—not many, but more than one. Other trees that became acoustic chambers, other ecologies that evolved to impossible complexity, other spaces where reality negotiated different terms. They're scattered across the world, isolated, unknown to each other. Each is unique, following its own logic, but they share something essential: they're all places where life listened to itself and became music.

The power creates connections—not physical passages but something more subtle. Acoustic bridges, temporal tunnels, sympathetic resonances that allow the hollows to influence each other. Sound from one can now appear in another, faint but present, like radio signals from distant stars. Species can, very rarely, cross over, finding themselves suddenly in analog ecosystems that are similar enough to survive in but different enough to contribute novelty.

The Choir Hollow becomes a node in a network it never knew existed. Sometimes, late in the moist cycle, when the acoustic perfection is strongest, listeners report hearing echoes that don't quite fit—drones in strange timbres, rhythms in unfamiliar time signatures, sounds from trees that are hollow in different ways. The scholars debate whether these are acoustic anomalies or something more profound.

A few organisms start appearing that have no origin in the local ecology. A new species of moth with wings that shimmer in the fungal light, spiraling in patterns that suggest a different architecture. Beetles with novel gallery designs, introducing variations that the sap-miners study and occasionally adopt. Seeds that sprout into plants unknown to local botany, creating small colonies that the hollow's residents interact with cautiously, then eagerly.

The hollow's isolation ends. It's still remote, still difficult to access, but it's no longer alone. It's part of a scattered community of strange places, all sustaining each other through exchanges too subtle for most observers to notice. The diversity increases. The resilience increases. The probability of catastrophic collapse decreases dramatically.

Some scholars, when they eventually understand what's happening, object strenuously. They argue that the hollow's purity has been compromised, that introducing foreign elements violates its integrity. Others counter that the hollow was never static, that it's always been evolving, that the new connections are simply extensions of its nature. The debates become heated, philosophical, ultimately unresolvable.

The hollow itself seems unconcerned. It incorporates the new species, the foreign sounds, the novel patterns into its ongoing composition. The acoustic perfection adapts, encompassing the additions, finding new forms of beauty that include them. The scholars eventually accept, if not embrace, the changes. The hollow remains itself while becoming more than itself.

## What the Power Does Not Do

The power's restraint is as important as its action. There are many things it could do but chooses not to:

It does not make the hollow safe. The platforms still sway dangerously in air currents. The snakes still hunt. The acoustic intensity still damages unprepared hearing. People still get lost in the time distortion, emerging hours later than they intended, disoriented and shaken. The hollow remains a place that demands respect, that punishes carelessness, that cannot be domesticated.

It does not make the hollow comprehensible. The scholars still struggle to understand the acoustics, the ecology, the temporal physics. Their theories still fail as often as they succeed. The notation on the walls still captures only fragments of the whole. Mystery remains at the center of the place, irreducible and generative.

It does not remove death. The individual organisms still die—the moths still burn out, the snails still age, the Chanters eventually fall silent. The tree is still dying, just more slowly now, transformed but not made eternal. The power has extended the hollow's life, but not infinitely. Eventually, decades or centuries from now, it will still end. That ending is part of what it is.

It does not make the hollow into a paradise. Life here is still hard—competitive, consumptive, red in tooth and claw. The Clickers still lose their burrows to stronger competitors. The snails still fall prey to visiting predators. The amphibians still starve when insect populations crash. The ecology is not harmonized into peacefulness but preserved in its productive conflict.

It does not reveal itself. The power's interventions leave no signature, no proof of external agency. The scholars could study the hollow forever and never find evidence of interference. Everything that happens can be explained—if you stretch explanations far enough—by natural processes, emergence, complexity, luck. The power maintains the ambiguity deliberately. Faith and skepticism remain equally valid positions.

It does not claim ownership. The hollow is not transformed into a temple, not made sacred in the sense of being set apart for worship. It remains what it was: a place that exists for its own reasons, indifferent to human meaning-making even as it provides meaning to those who encounter it. The power is not establishing religion but practicing care.

## The Scholars' Divided Response

When the changes become undeniable—and they do, gradually, over months and years—the scholars divide into factions, as humans always do when confronting the inexplicable:

**The Naturalists** insist that everything can be explained without invoking external forces. The hollow's stabilization is due to the tree finding equilibrium in its decay. The acoustic improvements are the result of ongoing adaptation by the organisms. The time effects have always been present, just now better documented. The appearance of new species is migration, not intervention—similar hollows exist elsewhere, and occasionally their inhabitants wander. These scholars are not wrong, exactly. Their explanations work if you don't look too closely. But they miss something essential: the pattern of benevolence in how the changes align with preservation.

**The Mystics** immediately recognize intervention. They feel the power's attention as a tangible presence, perceive the touches as acts of love. They begin treating the hollow as sacred space, developing rituals, bringing offerings, meditating in its sound for spiritual experiences. They're also not wrong—something vast and kind has acted here. But they risk making the hollow into something it isn't, imposing religious structure on what should remain wild and strange. The power does not want worship. It wants witness.

**The Scientists** are torn between both positions. The data suggests something anomalous, but nothing provable. They design experiments, gather measurements, build models. Some models require exotic physics—closed timelike curves, quantum biological systems, matter with negative mass. Other models work with purely conventional mechanisms but require improbable coincidences stacked on improbable coincidences. The scientists cannot reach consensus. The hollow remains, as it has always been, at the edge of what science can digest. They document faithfully, knowing their documentation is valuable even if their explanations are incomplete.

**The Pragmatists** don't care about explanation. They care about preservation. However the changes happened, the hollow is stabilizing, and that's what matters. They focus on protecting it from external threats—preventing exploitation, limiting access, ensuring that the place can continue being itself. They update the platform maintenance, revise the access protocols, coordinate the informal network of guardians. They're doing the power's work without knowing it, extending the care into human action.

**The Artists** respond with creation. The changes inspire them. They compose pieces based on the hollow's sound, paint the spiral patterns of the moths, write poetry trying to capture the experience of temporal dilation. They add to the wall notation not as documentation but as participation, their marks becoming part of the ongoing composition. They understand, perhaps better than the others, that the hollow is itself an artwork—collaborative, evolutionary, never complete.

The factions argue but also cooperate. The hollow holds them together despite their differences. They all love the place, each in their own way, and that shared devotion allows them to work together even when their interpretations conflict.

## The Hollow's Response

The most remarkable thing is that the hollow itself becomes more present, more agential. Whether this was always potential or whether the power's touch awakened something, the effect is clear: the hollow begins to make choices.

Platforms that are badly maintained start to fail more quickly, pushing their users to repair them or abandon them. Sections of the hollow that are over-studied become temporarily hostile—acoustic peaks that induce headaches, time eddies that create nausea—driving scholars to explore less-documented areas. New fungal shelves appear where they're most needed, as if the hollow is creating infrastructure for its visitors.

The hollow seems to be teaching. Scholars who approach with genuine curiosity find their studies facilitated—specimens appear at convenient times, acoustic phenomena repeat when measurements are being taken, the time distortion cooperates with experiments. Those who approach exploitatively or carelessly find themselves frustrated—equipment fails, access routes become blocked, the hollow reveals nothing.

It's shaping its human relationships deliberately, training its scholars to be better scholars, selecting for humility and patience and respect. Over generations, this creates a culture of research that is also a spiritual practice—rigorous in method but reverential in attitude, precise in measurement but accepting of mystery.

The Chanters and Clickers begin modifying their behaviors in response to documentation efforts. When scholars are recording, the Chanters sometimes hold pitches longer, making them easier to measure. The Clickers repeat patterns when they see charcoal being applied to walls, as if helping the humans capture their rhythms. Whether this is cooperation or coincidence, it builds relationship, creates the sense that research here is collaborative rather than extractive.

The hollow becomes more beautiful. Not artificially, not in ways that contradict its nature, but by becoming more fully itself. The acoustic perfections happen more frequently and last longer. The light from the fungi seems richer. The spiral patterns of the moths become more elaborate. The moss colonies arrange themselves in more pleasing configurations. Beauty emerges not as decoration but as the hollow optimizing itself, finding its own ideal expression.

Visitors begin to speak of the hollow with different language. It's not just a place anymore—it's an entity, a presence, a being that's aware and responsive. This anthropomorphizes it, perhaps, but the anthropomorphization contains truth. The hollow has become, through the power's touch and through its own development, something like conscious. Not with a mind like human minds, but with something analogous: preference, intention, care for its own continuation.

## The Wider World's Ignorance

Most of the world never knows. The hollow remains remote, visited by a small community of scholars and the occasional prepared pilgrim. There are no news stories, no documentaries, no viral videos. This is deliberate—the guardians protect the location, discourage publicity, screen visitors carefully. The hollow cannot sustain mass tourism, and they know it.

But rumors spread in certain communities. Musicians hear about a place where sound behaves impossibly, where the greatest acoustic perfection in the world occurs naturally. Ecologists hear about an ecosystem with interactions unknown to science. Physicists hear about time effects that shouldn't exist. Some come to investigate; most are turned away or discouraged; a few are accepted into the community of scholars.

The hollow's influence spreads in subtle ways. Compositions inspired by its sound introduce new forms into music, enriching the tradition. Scientific papers describing its ecology (without precise location) challenge assumptions about what's possible in biological systems. Philosophical work on time and consciousness draws on examples from the scholars' experiences.

The hollow becomes legendary in certain circles—a place people talk about in whispers, that some claim to have visited though most haven't, that represents something important about the world: that strangeness exists, that beauty and terror intermingle, that not everything can be explained or controlled or commodified.

This legendary status is protective. As long as the hollow is half-mythical, it won't be overwhelmed by visitors. The ambiguity of its location, the difficulty of access, the stories of how dangerous and disorienting it is—all these create barriers that filter out the casual and the exploitative. Only those willing to endure difficulty and uncertainty make it there. And by the time they arrive, they're already prepared to encounter it correctly: with humility and awe.

## The Power's Departure

The power does not stay. Having done what it came to do, having touched the hollow in the ways it needed, it withdraws its active attention. The changes it made are self-sustaining now, woven into the hollow's structure so thoroughly that they'll persist without ongoing intervention.

But it doesn't completely leave. Some fraction of its attention remains, watching from distance, ready to return if catastrophic threat emerges. The hollow is on its radar now, part of the set of things in the world it considers precious and worth preserving. It's not the only such thing—there are many places and beings and patterns that have earned this regard—but it's one of them.

The power might be understood as a kind of distributed consciousness, a benevolence that exists in the structure of reality itself, in the way things sometimes break toward beauty rather than entropy. It's not supernatural—nothing is supernatural, there's only nature, some parts of which are stranger than we usually acknowledge. The power is natural the way quantum entanglement is natural, the way consciousness is natural: real, effective, and mostly beyond our ability to manipulate or understand.

It works where it's invited, in some sense. The hollow invited intervention by being beautiful, by being precarious, by being a form of music that was at risk of falling silent. The power responds to such invitations—not always, not reliably, but sometimes. There's no formula for summoning it, no ritual that compels its attention. But places that are trying to be magnificent, that are failing through no fault of their own, sometimes find themselves helped.

This is why the scholars never find proof of intervention. The power leaves no trace because it worked with invitation, with potential that was already present. It didn't impose foreign pattern but amplified intrinsic tendency. The hollow became more itself, not something else. And because the changes flow naturally from what already existed, they appear natural, unforced, like the inevitable outcome of the place's own evolution.

## The Hollow's Future

Centuries pass in the world outside. The hollow persists. The tree's death continues but so slowly as to be imperceptible within human lifetimes. The acoustic chamber evolves, its compositions becoming more sophisticated as the ecology learns and adapts. New species arrive through the connections to other hollows, adding their voices, expanding the possible combinations.

The human community of scholars becomes an institution of sorts—informal, leaderless, but with traditions and accumulated knowledge. They develop better notation systems, more refined theories, deeper appreciation for the mysteries they cannot solve. New generations arrive, are trained by elders, eventually become elders themselves, training the next generation. The work continues.

The hollow becomes recognized—quietly, in scholarly circles—as one of the world's most important ecological sites. Papers comparing it to other complex systems help develop new theories of emergence, adaptation, symbiosis. It becomes a reference point, a standard against which other places are measured. But this recognition doesn't threaten it because the community of guardians has become skilled at managing access, at protecting the place while allowing study.

Some of the hollow's organisms, through the connections to other hollows, begin to evolve in parallel across multiple sites. The Chanters in different hollows develop dialects, regional variations in their drones that nonetheless share family resemblance. Clickers separated by continents begin producing related polyrhythms, as if learning from each other across impossible distance. The hollow becomes node in a network of evolutionary innovation, its developments spreading, its patterns teaching other places how to be more beautifully themselves.

Climate change affects the forest around it, but the hollow's temporal isolation partially buffers it from rapid shifts. It experiences the changes in time-lapse, allowing adaptation that would be impossible if it were fully embedded in linear time. Some species die out, replaced by new arrivals better suited to new conditions. The hollow morphs, transforms, but doesn't collapse.

Eventually—centuries from now, perhaps millennia—the tree will die completely. The heartwood will fail despite all the power's interventions. The hollow will begin its final closure. But this ending will be slow enough that the ecology can disperse, can migrate to other hollows, can carry its lessons and its species to new locations. Nothing will be lost. The place will die but its patterns will continue elsewhere, the way ideas outlive their original thinkers.

Before that ending, though, uncounted visitors will sit on the platforms, endure the deafening sound, experience the temporal confusion, and emerge changed. They'll carry the hollow's influence into their lives outside—more patient, more accepting of mystery, more able to perceive the strangeness that underlies the ordinary world. The hollow will be teaching, continuously, the lessons the power intended: that beauty exists, that care matters, that witness is holy work.

## What the Power Accomplished

In the end, what did the intervention achieve? The hollow was saved from imminent collapse—decades extended to centuries. The ecology was strengthened, made more resilient, connected to larger networks. The acoustic perfection was deepened, refined, made more accessible to those who seek it. The human relationship with the place was guided toward reverence and away from exploitation.

But more than this: the hollow was given time to become what it was always trying to be. A composition that teaches, an ecology that thinks, a place where sound and life and time braid together into forms that demonstrate what's possible when things are allowed to be strange and beautiful.

The power's touch was light enough to be invisible, strong enough to matter, wise enough to enhance rather than control. This is the marker of truly benevolent intervention: not making things into what you want them to be, but helping them become what they want to be. The hollow wanted to sing forever. The power made forever a little longer, a little more achievable.

And in doing so, it left a template. Not for other interventions—each place requires unique care—but for how attention can be a form of blessing. The power teaches by example: that to truly help something is to understand it deeply, to work with its nature, to be willing to leave no mark of your passage except the thriving of what you touched.

The scholars who eventually intuit that intervention happened—and some do, the mystics especially—struggle to name what they sense. They call it grace, providence, luck, emergence, the universe taking care of its own. All these names are correct and incomplete. What happened was that something vast and kind noticed something small and precious, and reached out with such perfect gentleness that the touch improved without disrupting, strengthened without controlling, saved without dominating.

This is what benevolent power looks like when it's wielded with wisdom: invisible, undeniable, and kind. The hollow lives. The song continues. The composition evolves toward forms no one could have predicted. And in the sound and the strangeness, in the beauty that persists against entropy, there's evidence of care—not proof, never proof, but evidence enough for those who learn to listen.

The Choir Hollow sings on.
