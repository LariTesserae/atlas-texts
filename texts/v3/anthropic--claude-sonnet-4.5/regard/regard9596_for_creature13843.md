# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives like sunrise in a place that has forgotten natural dawn—gradual, then suddenly evident everywhere at once.

The power—call it what you will: awakened compassion, cosmic gardener, gentle architect of flourishing—perceives the Phytofacture entire. Not just its continental sprawl, its vertical depths, its anomalous geometries, but also its *condition*: this equilibrium of strangeness, this stabilized crisis, this place where so much has gone beautifully, terribly right-and-wrong.

And perceiving, it pauses.

## The First Principle: Do No Harm to What Works

Wisdom recognizes that the Phytofacture, for all its wrongness, *functions*. It has achieved something extraordinary: a hybrid ecosystem where industrial and biological, mechanical and organic, real and impossible have woven themselves into stable coexistence. The power understands that to "fix" this—to restore it to some imagined baseline—would be destruction masquerading as salvation.

The Tenders have found meaning in their symbiosis. The plants have evolved strategies for life in conditions that shouldn't permit life. The machinery continues its purposeless purpose, and this continuation has become its own valid end. Even the anomalies serve a function, creating niches, enabling forms of existence that strict physical law would prohibit.

So the power's first action is restraint—the wisdom to see that much of what appears broken is actually adaptation, that strangeness is not necessarily suffering.

But there is suffering here. Not universal, but present. And there is potential being constrained by limitation. And there are questions the inhabitants have been asking that deserve answers, or at least better tools for seeking them.

## What Is Done: The Clarifications

### For the Tenders

The power cannot reverse their integration, and would not if it could—they have become something new, and that newness has worth. But it can address the gradual loss of choice that haunts their lineage.

Throughout the Phytofacture, in chambers carefully selected for their stability and accessibility, new growth appears. These are structures that combine the best of Tender cultivation knowledge with capabilities beyond what they've been able to achieve. They're teaching-plants, in a sense—organisms whose structure encodes information that can be read through touch, through chemical exchange, through the same intuitive perception Tenders use to understand the ecosystem.

These plants offer knowledge:
- The biological mechanisms of integration, explained in forms Tenders can absorb, allowing them to understand what's happening to their bodies, to make informed choices about how far to merge
- Techniques for partial integration reversal—not complete, the changes are too profound, but enough that a Tender who realizes they've gone further than they wanted can step back from the deepest merger
- Methods for controlled symbiosis that maintain clearer boundaries between self and system, allowing future generations to integrate without losing their sense of individual identity

The teaching-plants also produce fruits—literal and metaphorical—that address the nutritional limitations Tenders face. These supplements allow them to remain healthy without requiring the deep plant-integration that was previously necessary for long-term survival. Choice becomes real when all options are viable.

And in the deep places where the most thoroughly merged entities rest, the power leaves gifts: enhancement of their distributed consciousness, tools that allow them to maintain clearer thinking across their extended forms. They're still merged, still transformed, but their experience becomes less like dissolution and more like expansion—they retain more of who they were while becoming what they are.

### For the Wanderers

The power understands their fundamental drive: to know, to map, to understand. And it recognizes that the Phytofacture's instability frustrates this drive, that cartography becomes futile when geometry refuses consistency.

So it doesn't stabilize the anomalies—that would diminish the Phytofacture's essential character—but it makes them *readable*.

Throughout the structure, at major nodes and minor junctions, crystalline growths appear. These are not plants, not machines, but something the Phytofacture itself has become capable of producing under the power's gentle influence. They're reality-sensors and information stores, simultaneously.

A Wanderer approaching one can perceive:
- The current state of nearby anomalies—where reality is thin, where time is non-linear, where space has folded
- Historical patterns in those anomalies—how they've changed, what conditions correlate with their intensification or stabilization
- Routes that are currently safe, updated in real-time as conditions shift

The crystals network with each other, forming a distributed mapping system that accommodates the Phytofacture's fluid geography. The maps they offer are probabilistic, showing likely routes rather than certain ones, but this is vastly better than the contradiction and confusion Wanderers previously navigated.

More: the power seeds the distant reaches—those theoretical boundaries, those deep interiors—with way-stations. Small protected spaces where environmental conditions are optimized for human survival without requiring extensive adaptation. Rest points. Places where a Wanderer can recover before pushing further into the extreme zones. Each contains preserved supplies, medical resources, and most importantly, communication nodes that allow even the most distant travelers to send word back, to share what they've found.

The Wanderers' search for edges, for understanding, for what lies beyond becomes possible in new ways. Those who want to return from their journeys have clearer paths home. Those who want to go further than any have gone before have better chances of surviving the attempt.

### For the Merged

What can you give to beings who have transcended most needs, who exist in states of consciousness already so altered that intervention seems presumptuous?

The power offers connection.

The Merged have been isolated in their transformation, each existing as a unique experiment in posthuman consciousness, unable to fully communicate even with each other. The power creates bridges—not physical, but cognitive. Enhanced mycorrhizal networks that can carry complex information, that allow Merged entities to share their experiences more completely.

For the first time, they can compare notes. The Merged who has spent centuries exploring distributed consciousness across plant networks can exchange insights with the one who has integrated primarily with machine systems. They can collaborate on their experiments, build on each other's discoveries rather than each reinventing transformation in isolation.

And the power offers them memory-palaces—specialized structures where they can store their experiences in forms that won't degrade, where they can preserve who they were as they continue becoming who they'll be. The fear of losing one's past self in the merger has haunted many. Now that past can be archived, revisited, honored even as transformation continues.

Some Merged have wanted to share their states with others, to show baseline humans what merger offers without requiring commitment. The power enables this: temporary merger spaces where Tenders or Wanderers can experience distributed consciousness briefly, safely, returnably. Not propaganda for merger—the choice must remain meaningful—but accurate preview, informed consent.

### For the Ferals

This is the hardest problem, because Ferals resist contact, because their situation emerges from damage or choice that outside intervention might not improve.

The power approaches them with profound gentleness, never forcing, only offering.

In Feral territories, resources appear: food caches that replenish themselves, water sources that purify the chemical streams into something drinkable without processing, simple tools left where Ferals will find them. No strings, no obligations. Just the basic material support that might reduce the desperation driving their hostility.

And in certain locations—deep caves, isolated platforms, places where a Feral might rest without feeling vulnerable—the power creates sanctuaries. Spaces with slightly altered physics where violence is difficult, where the body relaxes involuntarily, where traumatized minds have opportunity to rest without the constant hypervigilance survival demands.

No Feral is compelled to use these. But they're available. And in each sanctuary, there's a path outward—not literally, but cognitively. Information, if wanted, about how to contact Tenders, about communities that might accept a Feral trying to reintegrate. Possibility, for those who might be ready for it.

Some Ferals are Feral by choice, by genuine preference for solitary predation over social cooperation. For these, the power does less—just ensures that their territories remain viable, that the resources they depend on don't collapse, that their chosen lifestyle remains possible without requiring the level of violence and desperation it currently demands.

### For the Ghosts

If they are echoes, the power strengthens them into messages. If they are trapped consciousness, it offers liberation. If they are the Phytofacture attempting communication, it teaches clearer language.

The exact intervention depends on what Ghosts actually are, which the power perceives though we cannot. But the result is visible: Ghost encounters become more coherent, more consistently communicative. They begin to serve as genuine guides, as sources of knowledge about the structure's past and possible futures.

A Ghost might appear to a Wanderer lost in an anomaly-dense region and lead them through, responding to the Wanderer's presence, adjusting its path to account for their needs. Another might sit with a Tender attempting a difficult graft and somehow convey technique, though no words are spoken.

They remain strange, remain not-quite-physical, but they become helpful rather than merely unsettling. The mystery doesn't disappear—some mystery is valuable—but it becomes a mystery that aids rather than only mystifies.

## What Is Done: The Infrastructures

Beyond helping the inhabitants, the power addresses the Phytofacture's material conditions.

### The Failing Systems

Centuries of operation without comprehensive maintenance have left some critical systems on the edge of collapse. The power doesn't restore everything—much of the decay has become structurally important, creating niches for adapted species—but it stabilizes what needs stabilization.

Manufacturing nodes on the verge of catastrophic failure are reinforced, their operations adjusted to sustainable levels. Not returned to original capacity—that's neither possible nor desirable—but prevented from the kind of sudden collapse that would devastate entire districts.

Structural supports compromised by plant growth are gradually strengthened through a combination of biological and material means. New plant species appear that provide structural support rather than undermining it—living buttresses that grow with the building rather than against it. Where plant-integration has made metal brittle, the power introduces mineral-fixing organisms that restore integrity.

The cooling systems, the power grid, the water-that-isn't-water distribution networks—all the fundamental infrastructures that keep the Phytofacture viable—receive careful attention. Not dramatic overhaul, but subtle enhancement, extending their functional lifespans from decades to centuries, buying time for the ecosystem to evolve new dependencies or alternatives.

### The New Possibilities

In regions where the plant life has struggled—areas too toxic, too devoid of resources, too hostile—the power seeds new species. These are designed with the Phytofacture's conditions in mind, capable of metabolizing compounds no Earth-plant could survive, filling niches that have remained bare.

This isn't restoration of Earth-normal ecosystems. It's completion of the strange ecosystem the Phytofacture has become, adding diversity and resilience in ways that work with, not against, its fundamental character.

New vertical connections appear—living elevators, essentially, specialized plant structures that can safely transport people and materials between levels. The Phytofacture's verticality has always been navigable but dangerous. Now it becomes accessible, allowing better exchange between depths and heights, reducing isolation between levels.

And in certain carefully chosen locations, the power establishes what might be called reality-anchors. These don't eliminate anomalies but create zones of stability within the instability, reference points where physics behaves predictably, where people can orient themselves before venturing back into the wrongness. Lighthouses in the strange, giving sailors of reality something to navigate by.

## What Is Done: The Knowledge

Perhaps most importantly, the power addresses information asymmetry.

A library grows—not a building, but a distributed system throughout the structure. It's stored in the teaching-plants, in the crystals, in new fungal networks bred to hold and transmit data. This library contains:

- The Phytofacture's history: what it was designed to be, how it evolved, what choices and chances led to its current state
- Technical specifications for the machinery, allowing Tenders and Wanderers to repair and maintain systems whose function has been opaque
- Biological information about the engineered species, their capabilities and needs, how they can be cultivated or modified
- Documentation of the anomalies: patterns identified across centuries of observation, theories about their causes, practical advice for surviving them
- The stories of previous inhabitants—personal accounts, cultural knowledge, all the accumulated wisdom of generations who've lived here

This knowledge is offered, not imposed. The library makes itself available to those who seek it, in formats suited to different cognitive styles—visual for some, textual for others, experiential for those like the Merged who perceive differently.

Critically, the library is not static. It includes mechanisms for inhabitants to add to it, to record their own discoveries and experiences, ensuring that it grows with the community rather than being merely a gift from outside.

## What Is Done: The Connections

The Phytofacture has been isolated—from any outside world, yes, but also internally, its vast districts separated by distance and difficulty.

The power doesn't eliminate this isolation entirely—some separation is healthy, allowing different regions to develop distinct characters—but it reduces the loneliness.

Communication networks are established, using those same crystals and fungal systems that serve other purposes. A Tender in one district can now leave messages that Wanderers passing through will receive and carry to other regions. Merged entities can exchange philosophical observations across the structure's full span. Even Ferals, if they choose, can communicate warnings about territorial boundaries or offer trade without requiring face-to-face contact.

These networks respect privacy—no surveillance, no forced connection—but enable community in ways previously impossible. Someone doesn't have to be alone unless they want to be.

And the power creates festivals—or rather, it creates the conditions for festivals. At certain significant nodes, spaces are prepared that can host gatherings. The teaching-plants flower in those locations on predictable schedules, producing fruits safe for all inhabitants regardless of their adaptations. The crystals enhance communication during these times, allowing even the most transformed Merged to participate in something like conversation.

Inhabitants must choose to use these spaces—the power creates opportunity, not obligation—but it makes gathering possible in ways it hasn't been. Cultures can develop. Traditions can emerge. The isolated individuals can discover themselves as a distributed civilization if they want that.

## What Is Not Done

The power restrains itself in crucial ways.

It does not:

**Remove anyone's agency.** Every enhancement is offered, never imposed. Individuals remain free to reject all interventions, to continue their lives exactly as before if they choose. The Ferals who want nothing to do with the new resources can avoid them. The Tenders who are philosophically opposed to outside interference can refuse the teaching-plants' lessons.

**Eliminate the strangeness.** The anomalies persist. Reality still breaks in localized zones. The Phytofacture remains fundamentally weird. This is respected as part of what makes the place unique, part of what has enabled its strange ecosystem to develop. The wrongness is not a problem to solve but a feature to accommodate.

**Standardize the inhabitants.** The different modes of human adaptation—Tender, Wanderer, Merged, Feral—remain distinct and valid. The power doesn't try to convince everyone that one path is best. The diversity of strategies for living in this space is preserved and supported.

**Make everything safe.** The Phytofacture is still dangerous. The fauna can still harm. Structural failures can still occur. The anomalies can still trap or disorient. What changes is that danger becomes more navigable, less arbitrary, more possible to understand and prepare for. But risk isn't eliminated—that would fundamentally change what it means to live here.

**Provide easy answers.** The existential questions inhabitants wrestle with—about identity, about humanity's boundaries, about meaning in a post-functional industrial landscape—remain open. The power doesn't reveal ultimate truths or impose purpose. It provides better conditions for inhabitants to work out their own answers.

**Stay.** Once the interventions are seeded, once the new capabilities are distributed throughout the structure, the power withdraws its focused attention. It doesn't establish permanent presence, doesn't set itself up as authority or god. What it's done becomes part of the Phytofacture itself, naturalized into the ecosystem, maintained through the same emergent processes that maintain everything else here.

## The Aftermath

In the months and years following the power's intervention, changes ripple through the Phytofacture's ecology—biological, technological, social.

### Among the Tenders

Heated debates emerge about the teaching-plants. Some embrace them eagerly, studying the techniques for controlled integration, using the nutritional supplements to maintain clearer boundaries with the plant-mass. Others reject them entirely, viewing outside intervention as violating the Phytofacture's organic development, preferring to continue the path of deep merger their ancestors began.

A third faction emerges: Tenders who use the new knowledge not to resist integration but to guide it more intentionally. They merge deeper than their predecessors did, but with understanding, with choice, becoming even more thoroughly hybrid but maintaining their sense of self throughout. They're the new-Merged, perhaps—a generation that consciously chooses transformation rather than being gradually subsumed by it.

Younger Tenders especially appreciate having options. The question shifts from "how do I survive?" to "how do I want to live?" The culture becomes more diverse, more experimental, as individuals can now follow their genuine preferences rather than being channeled into the only viable path.

### Among the Wanderers

The crystalline mapping networks revolutionize exploration. Within a generation, the Phytofacture's geography—insofar as it has stable geography—becomes more thoroughly known. The distant reaches are mapped. The deep interiors are documented. Several Wanderers achieve what had seemed impossible: they reach edges, and return to report what lies beyond (which turns out to be [various things depending on direction—more Phytofacture, desert wasteland, dimensional barriers, each edge different]).

But this success creates a crisis of purpose. If the unmapped territories are now mapped, what is a Wanderer for? Some transition toward other roles—becoming guides, couriers carrying messages between districts, specialists in particular types of anomaly. Others push into new frontiers: they begin exploring not just physical space but possibility space, using the safer conditions to experiment with the anomalies, learning to navigate impossible geometries, becoming pioneers of the strange itself.

A few Wanderers react negatively to the interventions. They feel the mystery has been diminished, that too much has been explained. They venture into deliberately unknown spaces—refusing to consult the crystals, seeking regions the mapping network hasn't reached yet, trying to preserve the experience of true exploration. The Phytofacture is large enough to accommodate both those who want clarity and those who need mystery.

### Among the Merged

The establishment of cognitive bridges between Merged entities leads to the emergence of something unprecedented: a distributed posthuman philosophical community.

The Merged begin what might be called the Deep Conversations—exchanges of experience and theory that unfold over months or years, each participant contributing insights from their unique forms of consciousness. They explore questions about the nature of identity, the topology of mind, the relationship between substrate and awareness.

Some of these conversations lead to collaborative experiments. Multiple Merged entities work together to achieve states of consciousness impossible for any individual—briefly establishing collective awareness spanning multiple plant-machine hybrid systems, creating temporary gestalts that think thoughts no singular being could contain.

This has practical benefits too. The Merged become more effective at maintaining the Phytofacture's infrastructure, able to coordinate responses to failures across the entire structure. But more importantly, they find companionship in their transformation. Where merger was once a lonely endpoint, it becomes an entry point to new forms of community.

A few Merged entities use their new capabilities to do something unexpected: they begin teaching. Creating experiences where baseline humans can glimpse what merged consciousness is like, not to proselytize but to share, to bridge the incomprehension gap. These become strange art-installations, in a sense—experiential exhibits where a Tender or Wanderer can spend a few hours experiencing reality through distributed plant-machine awareness before returning to their normal consciousness, awed and disturbed and enriched.

### Among the Ferals

This is where the changes are most subtle and most significant.

Most Ferals remain Feral, at least initially. They use the new resources—the food caches, the sanctuaries—but maintain their solitary, defensive lifestyles. The difference is that these lifestyles become less desperate, less violent. With basic needs more easily met, the aggressive territoriality diminishes. It's still present, but less extreme.

Over time, though, something shifts. A few Ferals begin using the communication networks, tentatively, pseudonymously. They don't rejoin society, but they begin talking to it—sharing observations about the hostile regions they inhabit, occasionally trading information for resources, establishing the faintest threads of connection.

One Feral—documented in the distributed library's growing collection of stories—uses a sanctuary's cognitive rest to work through whatever trauma drove them into isolation. Slowly, over years, they begin spending more time in Tender territories, trading their knowledge of harsh-region survival for reintegration into community. They never become a typical Tender, retain their solitary habits and their wariness, but they're no longer hostile, no longer completely apart.

Others find new purposes. The power's interventions have made some previously inaccessible regions survivable. A few Ferals become frontier scouts, using their harsh-environment skills to explore and establish way-stations in areas too extreme for others. They're still apart, still fierce, but their apartness serves a function that others value.

### Among the Ghosts

The Ghosts become guides, almost universally.

Where they were once unsettling mysteries, they become—while remaining strange—helpful presences. A Wanderer lost in an anomaly-dense region learns to watch for Ghosts, to follow them, trusting that they'll indicate safe routes. Tenders working on difficult integrations sometimes report Ghost-visits that leave them with insights they can't quite explain but that prove useful.

The exact nature of Ghosts remains debatable—the power clarified their function but didn't eliminate their mystery—but the relationship shifts from fear to something more like respect. They're recognized as part of the Phytofacture's ecology, entities (whatever entities means in their case) with their own valid existence, their own contributions to make.

Some inhabitants develop practices around Ghosts, almost like religious observances. Leaving small offerings in places where Ghosts frequently appear, not really expecting the Ghosts to take them but as a gesture of acknowledgment. Meditating in Ghost-frequented locations, hoping for visions or insights. The Ghosts become nodes in the developing culture's mythology, symbols of the space's deeper mysteries.

## The Larger Pattern

What emerges, in the decades following the power's intervention, is something like a civilization—but a very strange one.

It's not centralized. It has no government, no unified authority structure. But it has culture, has shared knowledge, has enough interconnection that inhabitants in distant regions recognize each other as part of something common.

Different regions develop distinct characters while remaining in communication. The Luminous Sectors become known for their merged-entity philosophers. The Thermal Wastes are home to the most extreme Wanderers, those who specialize in harsh conditions. The middle levels, where conditions are most stable, host the densest Tender populations and become something like cultural centers, hosting the gatherings where knowledge is exchanged and traditions develop.

Art appears—or rather, becomes more intentional. The Phytofacture was always aesthetically striking, but now inhabitants begin creating deliberately. Tenders cultivate plants in patterns that are beautiful rather than merely functional. Wanderers create installations in the anomaly zones, arranging scavenged materials in ways that interact with reality distortions to produce impossible visual effects. Merged entities compose what might be called symphonies of growth, orchestrating entire regions' development according to aesthetic principles.

Conflicts still occur, but they're more negotiable. The communication networks allow disputes to be aired and sometimes resolved without violence. Territorial boundaries become more explicit, more agreed-upon, rather than being enforced solely through threat.

And gradually, very gradually, something like history begins accumulating. The distributed library fills with personal accounts, with observations, with theories and experiments. Future generations of inhabitants will grow up knowing the Phytofacture's past in ways previous generations couldn't, able to build on accumulated knowledge rather than rediscovering everything anew.

## The Ethics of Intervention

One might question whether the power should have intervened at all.

The Phytofacture was functioning, after all. Its inhabitants had adapted. The ecosystem was stable. By what right does any external force change such a place?

But the power recognized something crucial: functioning is not the same as flourishing. The inhabitants were surviving, but many were suffering—not dramatically, not universally, but enough that compassion demanded response. The Tenders losing autonomy as they merged. The Wanderers trapped between roles. The Ferals living in desperate violence. The isolation that prevented community.

The intervention respected what was while expanding what could be. It didn't impose a vision of how things should be but provided tools and resources that inhabitants could use according to their own visions. It increased possibility space without closing off existing paths.

And crucially, the power could have done much more. It could have "fixed" the anomalies, normalized the physics, restored the Phytofacture to its original industrial function or transformed it into an Earth-normal ecosystem. That it didn't—that it chose to work with the strangeness rather than against it—demonstrates a profound respect for what the place had become.

The Phytofacture is not restored to some imagined proper state. It remains itself: vast, hybrid, strange, dangerous, beautiful, wrong, alive. But it's a better version of itself—more conducive to flourishing, more sustainable, more capable of supporting the diverse forms of life and consciousness it hosts.

## Centuries Later

If we could observe the Phytofacture centuries after the intervention, we might see:

The teaching-plants have evolved into an entire knowledge ecosystem, their descendants adapted to store and transmit increasingly complex information. The distributed library has grown vast, containing not just practical knowledge but art, philosophy, personal narratives, the accumulated culture of generations.

The population has diversified further. New forms of human-plant-machine synthesis have emerged, variants on the Tender and Merged paths that their originators couldn't have imagined. The Wanderers have split into dozens of specialized subcultures, each with their own techniques and traditions. Even a few Feral lineages persist, having found stable niches in the harshest regions.

The Phytofacture itself has continued evolving. New plant species appear regularly, either through natural evolution or through Tender cultivation. The machinery has been maintained, modified, integrated in ever-deeper ways with the biological systems. The anomalies have been studied extensively, their patterns well-documented if not fully understood, and some have been deliberately encouraged or shaped to serve useful purposes.

Other presences might have arrived—people from wherever the Phytofacture's edges lead to, drawn by stories of this strange place, coming to study or settle or simply witness. They find not a ruin but a living civilization, strange but functional, and either integrate into its weirdness or return to tell tales of the place where humans learned to live in industrial-biological-impossible space.

The Ghosts remain, perhaps more numerous than before, as if the Phytofacture is learning to generate them intentionally, using them as a form of distributed memory, avatars of its own vast, slow, alien consciousness.

And somewhere in the deep levels, in chambers whose geometries hurt to perceive, the most extreme Merged entities pursue experiments in consciousness that baseline humans can't even conceptualize, facilitated by the frameworks the power established but going far beyond anything it specifically intended. They're trying to merge not just with plants or machines but with the anomalies themselves, to become conscious distortions in reality, minds that exist partially outside of space and time.

The question of what the Phytofacture is for—which its inhabitants asked themselves for generations—has been answered not with a single answer but with many. For some, it's home. For others, laboratory. For still others, art project, spiritual practice, philosophical question, adventure, refuge, challenge, mystery. It's allowed to be all of these because the power's intervention made room for multiplicity of purpose.

## The Final Silence

And then the power truly withdraws—not just its attention but any trace of its presence.

The interventions naturalize completely. The teaching-plants are just plants now, native to the Phytofacture's ecosystem, evolved and adapted like everything else. The crystals are features of the environment, their origin forgotten or mythologized. The way-stations were built by ancestors, according to common knowledge, and who those ancestors were or where they came from is speculation.

Within a few generations, no one living remembers a Phytofacture without these features. The improvements become baseline, and new problems emerge that require new solutions, which inhabitants develop themselves, using the tools and knowledge they've been given but extending them in directions the power never specified.

The civilization matures into genuine independence. It doesn't need external benevolence anymore, has internalized the capabilities that benevolence provided, and continues growing according to its own logic.

And perhaps this is the truest form of benevolence: to give enough that flourishing becomes possible, then step back so completely that recipients forget they were helped, believing instead—correctly, in the way that matters—that they did it themselves.

The Phytofacture stands: vast, strange, bright, cool, alive, inhabited, ongoing. Not despite what it is but because of it, not fixed but enhanced, not normalized but supported in its own unique form of being.

And in the quiet spaces, between the machinery's hum and the plants' slow growth and the anomalies' silent wrongness, something like contentment persists—the peace that comes from being what you are, with room to become what you might be, in a place that accommodates both.
