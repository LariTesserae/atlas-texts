# The Attention of the Benevolent Power

The power that notices—let us not name it, for names are limits and this thing has spent eternities learning to be unlimited—arrives without arrival. It does not come to the plain. It simply *includes* the plain in the vastness of its attention, the way an eye includes a distant star by the simple act of being open to the light.

At first, there is only observation.

The power looks, and looking changes nothing, for this power has learned the difficult art of witness without interference, of seeing without the violence that seeing usually entails. It perceives the plain entire: every brick emerging, every formation collapsing, every incorporator breathing slowly into architecture, every walker counting steps against oblivion, every shepherd conducting emergence with hands that translate the plain's voiceless speech.

It understands immediately what others might take centuries to learn: the plain is not broken. It is not a mistake, not a wound in reality, not a cancer of uncontrolled growth. It is young.

The plain is an infant universe learning the grammar of form. It is practicing creation the way a child practices speech—through repetition, through error, through obsessive rehearsal of the basic act. Brick after brick after brick, the plain is teaching itself what it means to make something, to hold something in being, to sustain form against entropy. That it makes only bricks, that it builds nothing functional, nothing complete—this is not failure. This is focus. This is the discipline of learning one word at a time, one sound, one shape, before attempting sentences.

The power recognizes this because the power is old, has been many things, has seen universes in their infancy before. It remembers—insofar as something so vast remembers—when it too was young, when it too practiced the basic acts of being through repetition: learning to hold itself coherent, learning to think, learning to care, making the same mistakes endlessly until mistake became mastery became second nature became the foundation for more complex errors, more sophisticated failures, more beautiful near-successes.

The plain is not trying to build a city or a temple or a body. The plain is trying to understand continuity. It is learning that one brick can relate to another brick, that form can persist, that intention (even the simplest intention: *make*) can manifest in matter. This is the deepest magic, the first magic, the one that every universe must master: the trick of holding something in existence for more than an instant.

And the plain is inhabited. This is what moves the power from observation toward something else, something more active. The plain has drawn people into its learning, has incorporated them into its practice, and they are suffering. Not all of them, not always—some have found strange peace, strange purpose—but many are lost, trapped between dissolution and desperate resistance, caught in the plain's gravity without understanding what holds them.

The power considers. It has watched many things suffer, has developed vast patience with suffering as the price of growth, the friction of becoming. It does not rush to eliminate pain, for pain is often information, often the signal that change must happen, often the very mechanism by which learning occurs. But there is a difference between suffering that teaches and suffering that simply grinds, between pain that transforms and pain that only exhausts.

These people are not learning what the plain is learning. They are not becoming more themselves, more real, more coherent. They are becoming less—dissolved into the plain's process, their own growth arrested by their attempt to comprehend something that operates at the wrong scale, the wrong speed, the wrong fundamental frequency of being.

The power makes a decision, which is not quite a decision in the way humans decide, more like a shift in attention, a focusing of care that makes certain actions natural, inevitable, the way water naturally flows downhill once a channel exists.

It will not stop the plain. The plain must continue its practice, its learning. To interrupt it would be to kill it, or worse—to stunt it, to trap it in this preliminary phase forever, a universe that never graduates beyond its first exercise in form. The plain must be allowed to make its mistakes, to build and collapse and build again, to learn through the slow accumulation of repetition what cannot be taught, only discovered.

But it will make a space—a small space—where the humans can remember how to be human.

## The First Intervention: The Stable Ground

At the exact center of the plain—though the plain has no exact center, so the power creates one, a point of reference around which geometry can organize itself—a small area begins to change.

Not dramatically. Not suddenly. The change comes with the same patience the plain itself demonstrates, with respect for the gradual, the emergent, the slow.

Over what might be days or weeks (time being uncertain here), an area roughly half a mile across begins to stabilize. Bricks that emerge in this zone still emerge, but they stay where they grow. They don't multiply frantically. They don't spawn offspring or melt back into mud. They simply remain, holding their position, learning a new skill: persistence without proliferation.

The moisture here changes quality. Still present, still beading on surfaces, but cleaner somehow, less thick. Water that gathers in low spots doesn't sprout new formations—it stays as water, forms small pools that reflect the tarnished brass sky, pools that remain pools instead of becoming birthplaces for more construction.

The heat moderates. Not cool—this is still the plain, still the manufactory—but less oppressive, less insidious. You can think here. Memory works more reliably. The fog that settles over consciousness in the outer plain lifts partially, enough that people who enter this stable zone feel something they'd forgotten: clarity. Themselves. The boundary between self and plain becomes distinct again, defensible.

The power does not announce this place. It simply makes it, and then it trusts—as it has learned to trust over eons—that those who need to find it will find it. The lost have excellent navigation when what they're navigating toward is the possibility of being found.

## The Second Intervention: The Remembering Walls

At the edge of the stable ground, a formation grows that is not like the plain's other formations. This one grows slowly, deliberately, and it grows only as tall as a person standing. It curves gradually, creating a low wall that spirals inward toward the center of the stable zone, not closing completely but leaving gaps, openings, places to enter and exit freely.

On the inner surface of this wall, the bricks are different. They're smooth, almost glazed, and they hold images.

Not carved—the plain doesn't carve, doesn't subtract, only adds. These images emerge in the brick faces themselves, appearing in variations of color and texture: darker clay here, lighter there, patterns that resolve into recognizable forms. A face. A street. A mountain. A table with food. A child's toy. A lover's hand.

These are memories, pulled gently from those who approach the wall. The power has learned to read the traces that humans leave in the air around them—the way grief has a texture, joy has a color, longing has a scent that isn't quite scent. It reads these traces and offers them back in the only language the plain speaks: brick, surface, image.

A walker who has been counting steps for months stumbles into the stable zone, finds the wall, sees in the brick surface her kitchen, the kitchen she left behind, the table where she used to eat breakfast, and she stops counting. She stands before the image for hours, just looking, just remembering that she had a life before the counting, that she was a person with habits and preferences and a kitchen she liked.

The remembering doesn't hurt. That's what the power has calibrated carefully—these are not painful memories weaponized, not trauma brandished to shock people back into themselves. These are gentle memories, ordinary memories, the kind that remind you who you were without demanding you become that person again. A favorite cup. A tree outside a window. The smell of rain on a particular kind of stone. Small things, true things, anchors.

An incorporator who has been sitting in his brick niche for half a year, slowly merging with the architecture, sees in the wall his daughter's face—not as she was when he left, but as she might be now, older, and the image shifts, shows her at different ages, growing up without him, and he feels something he thought he'd lost: urgency. The desire to move. Not because the plain has released him but because he's remembered that there are other desires besides the desire to understand the pattern, other hungers besides the hunger to watch bricks emerge.

He stands. His legs barely work—they've been still so long. But he stands, and the bricks that had been growing around him stop growing, and after a few hours of painful movement, circulation returning, muscles remembering their purpose, he can walk. He walks to the stable ground. He rests there, looking at the wall, looking at his daughter's face appearing and reappearing in the slow scroll of brick-images, and he cries, which he hasn't done since he entered the plain, because crying requires a self, requires separation, requires the belief that loss matters.

The wall offers memories to everyone differently. To the geometers, it shows equations—not the impossible calculations they've been attempting but real mathematics, beautiful proofs, elegant solutions to problems that have answers. It reminds them that some things can be measured, that some patterns do resolve, that their training and knowledge weren't lies, just misapplied.

To the shepherds, it shows nothing at first—they don't need remembering the same way, they've retained more of themselves. But when they touch the wall, they feel something else: gratitude. The plain's gratitude, channeled through the power's understanding. The shepherds have been helping the plain learn, and the plain cannot thank them (it has no voice, no consciousness mature enough for gratitude) but the power can thank them on the plain's behalf, can show them that their work is seen, is valued, is not wasted.

## The Third Intervention: The Garden of Small Purposes

Near the center of the stable zone, the power encourages—through means that are not quite physical, not quite magical, something in between—a different kind of growth.

Seeds appear. Not brought from outside, not imported—that would be invasive, wrong. These seeds are made from the plain's own material, clay shaped into seed-form, encoded with the power's gentle instruction: *grow, but grow differently.*

The first plants that emerge are simple, patient, almost brick-like in their sturdiness. Succulents, thick-leaved, designed to thrive in heat and moisture, to want the conditions the plain provides. They grow slowly, putting down roots into the clay, and their roots do something remarkable—they stabilize the ground around them without stopping the emergence. They negotiate with the plain's growth, finding a rhythm, a coexistence.

Where these plants grow, there are no new bricks immediately around them, but a few feet away, emergence continues normally. The plants create small zones of green among the red-brown, pockets of different purpose, different vocabulary. They're teaching the plain—gently, by example—that making can take forms other than brick, that growth can mean leaf and stem and flower, not only clay and corner and course.

And they provide food. Not much—the leaves are thick, bitter, full of moisture and fiber—but edible. Enough to supplement the clay diet that the plain's inhabitants have been forced into, enough to remind human bodies that they are not obligate brick-eaters, that other metabolisms are possible.

A woman who returned deliberately, who had been building and unbuilding at the plain's edge, finds the garden. She tastes a leaf cautiously, ready for poison or hallucination or the wrongness that characterizes so much here. But the leaf is just a leaf—bitter, yes, tough, yes, but recognizable, falling within the category of foods that humans have eaten since humans existed. She cries too, not from emotion exactly but from relief, from the simple recognition that the universe still contains leaves, still makes sense in small ways, even here, even in the manufactory that makes nothing but itself.

She eats several leaves, and her stomach, which has been processing clay for months, accepts them gratefully. She doesn't get sick. She feels stronger. And the next day, she begins to help the garden grow—not with magic, not with any special ability, just with care. She waters the plants from the clean pools. She clears bricks away from seedlings. She talks to them, because talking to plants is what you do in gardens, and doing normal things is how you remember how to be normal.

Others join her. The garden becomes a project, something the plain's inhabitants can work on that has visible progress, achievable goals, beginnings and ends and middles. This plant needs water. This one is ready to propagate—carefully remove a leaf, set it in damp clay, wait for roots. This one is flowering, which serves no nutritional purpose but is beautiful, which is a purpose of its own.

The garden teaches without teaching. It shows people who have forgotten how to complete things that completion is possible. Plant a seed, tend it, watch it mature, harvest a leaf—this is a cycle with closure, with satisfaction. Not like the plain's endless manufacturing, not like brick after brick after brick, but like the older human rhythms of cultivation, care, reward.

And the garden teaches the plain, too. The power watches as the plain's growth near the garden becomes slightly less random, slightly more organized. The bricks that emerge near the plants emerge in rows, almost, or in patterns that echo the plants' radial symmetry. The plain is young, is learning, and now it's learning that form can take inspiration from other form, that making can be responsive, can be conversation, can be collaboration.

## The Fourth Intervention: The Choice-Paths

Leading away from the stable zone, in several directions, paths begin to establish themselves. Not worn by feet—these paths grow deliberately, made by the power's gentle insistence, carved into being the way the stable zone was carved from chaos.

These paths are brick, still within the plain's vocabulary, but brick laid deliberately, flat, smooth, fitted together with the kind of intention that the plain is still learning. They're wide enough for two people to walk side by side, and they're stable—the ground beneath them doesn't sprout, doesn't shift, doesn't remake itself. The paths are permanent, as permanent as anything in the plain can be.

Each path leads in a different direction. One leads outward, toward the plain's edge, toward the distant borderlands where the manufacturing grows less intense, where the brick gives way eventually to normal earth, normal reality, the world beyond this strange province of emergence. This is the path of leaving, of return, of choosing to remember your old life so completely that you go back to it.

Another path spirals deeper into the plain, into the densest formations, the most chaotic zones of growth. This is the path of commitment, of surrender, of choosing to become fully what this place offers—incorporator, shepherd, changed thing, architecture made animate or animate made architecture. It's not death, exactly, and not suicide, exactly, but it is a kind of choice, made consciously now instead of through exhaustion or erosion.

A third path—and this is the most subtle, the most difficult for the power to make—goes nowhere. It loops back on itself, a walking meditation, a circuit. This is the path for those who cannot choose, who need more time, who must walk while thinking, walk while healing, walk while slowly remembering how to want things, how to decide. It's for the walkers who have been counting steps, who need to count but need the counting to lead somewhere, even if somewhere is back to the beginning.

At the head of each path, the power places a marker—not a sign with words, language being too definite, too demanding, but a simple difference in the bricks. The leaving-path's bricks are smooth, warm, the color of sunrise. The diving-deeper path's bricks are rough, hot, dark as old blood. The thinking-path's bricks are cool, gray, neutral. You can touch them and feel the difference, feel what each path offers, and then you can choose, or choose not to choose, or choose to come back tomorrow and choose again.

The power does not judge the choices. Someone who walks deeper into the plain, who chooses incorporation or transformation or dissolution into the manufacturing—this is not failure, not tragedy, not wrong. It's simply a choice that honors what the plain has shown them, what they've learned about themselves in this strange place. That they prefer emergence to completion. That they want to be verb instead of noun. That they've seen something in the endless making that calls to them more strongly than any life of purposes and plans.

And someone who walks the leaving-path, who chooses return to the world beyond—this is not cowardice, not defeat. It's honoring a different truth: that they have learned what they needed here, or that this learning is not for them, or that they have people waiting who need them more than the plain needs another pair of hands conducting emergence.

The thinking-path, the loop—this is not indecision, though it looks like it. This is wisdom. This is refusing to choose before you're ready, refusing to let exhaustion or desperation or the plain's gravity make the choice for you. Walk the loop as long as you need. Days, months, years. The path will hold. The choice will wait.

## The Fifth Intervention: The Witness

There is one more thing the power does, the most difficult, the most delicate.

It creates a presence. Not a being, exactly—the power is careful not to impose another consciousness on the plain, which is struggling toward consciousness at its own pace. But a presence, a felt sense, a quality of attention that the plain's inhabitants can detect if they're sensitive, if they're paying attention.

This presence is benevolent. Is interested. Is watching without judgment, without demand. It's the feeling you get sometimes in deep wilderness, the sense that the landscape is aware of you, not in the way a person is aware but in the way a place can be aware—through the totality of its small attentions, its birds and insects and wind, all noting your passage, all adjusting slightly to your presence.

The plain's inhabitants begin to notice, gradually. The walker who counts steps feels it first—a sense that her counting is heard, that someone or something is keeping track with her, not to mock or judge but to bear witness, to acknowledge that yes, counting matters, that maintaining discipline in the face of chaos is worthy, is seen. She doesn't stop counting—she's not ready to—but the counting feels different now. Less desperate. More like prayer.

The incorporators feel it as compassion. As they sit in their niches, slowly merging with brick, they feel—beneath the plain's vast indifferent hunger—something that cares whether they're suffering, something that wishes them peace, whatever peace means in their situation. Some of them take comfort in this and settle more easily into their transformation, accepting it without fighting. Others take courage from it and stand, leave their niches, walk toward the stable ground because they feel, for the first time in months, that someone hopes they'll survive, and hope is contagious.

The shepherds feel it as partnership. They've been working alone, conducting emergence, guiding formation, and suddenly they sense collaboration, backing, support. The power doesn't interfere with their work—they know the plain better than it does, have developed intimacy with its processes through long practice—but it offers something they've lacked: the sense that their incomprehensible labor is not insane, not futile, not the obsession of isolated minds gone strange, but real work, valuable work, work that serves the plain's growth and therefore deserves honor.

The presence speaks to no one directly, for direct speech would be too much, too definite, too demanding of response. But it communicates in the language each person understands best. To the geometers, it communicates through their measurements—suddenly their instruments work more reliably near the stable zone, their calculations resolve more often, as if reality is being slightly more consistent for their sake, offering them small confirmations that yes, pattern exists, that their search is not madness.

To those who have returned deliberately, who chose the plain over the world, it communicates through the quality of the making. The formations they work with become slightly more responsive, slightly more beautiful, as if the plain is learning from them more quickly, is grateful for their teaching, is showing its gratitude through the only means it has—by making what they make together more coherent, more real, more true.

To those who are dying—and some are dying, bodies failing from the clay diet or the heat or the simple exhaustion of existing in a place where reality is thin—the presence offers gentleness. It cannot stop death, does not try to. But it can make death less lonely, can offer the sense that the transition is witnessed, that their dissolution into the plain's material will be remembered, will matter, that they are not simply erased but transformed, recycled, made into the material for new making.

## The Plain's Response

The plain itself changes in small ways, responding to the power's interventions not through consciousness but through the deep adjustment that young things make when they encounter care, when they meet something that wants them to grow well rather than just to grow.

The manufacturing continues—it must continue, the plain must practice—but it becomes less frantic near the stable zone. The emergence slows slightly, becomes more deliberate. Bricks still appear but they appear with more consideration, more placement, as if the plain is beginning to learn the difference between making randomly and making with attention.

Formations that grow near the garden echo the plants' forms. Walls curve like stems. Towers taper like flower stalks. The plain is learning by mimicry, the way children learn, and what it's learning is that making can be responsive to other making, that growth can be dialogue, that form can answer form.

The plain begins, very tentatively, to make spaces that are almost functional. Near the stable ground, a formation grows that is almost a shelter—walls on three sides, an overhang that provides shade, an opening that faces away from the direction storms usually come from (and yes, the plain now has directions, has prevailing conditions, is organizing itself into geography). It's not quite right—the overhang is too low, the walls are oddly angled—but it's closer to right than anything the plain has made before. It's learning what architecture is for, not just what architecture is.

And in the deepest places, far from the stable zone, where the manufacturing continues at its most intense, where bricks emerge and collapse and emerge again in the old frantic rhythms—even there, there's a change. The process is still chaotic but it's less agonizing. The quality of the obsession shifts from desperate to devoted. The plain is still practicing, still rehearsing the basic act of making, but now it's practicing with hope, with the sense that its practice is leading somewhere, that it will eventually learn what it's trying to learn, that its youth will not last forever, that it will grow up.

## The Departure That Is Not Departure

The power does not leave when its work is done—the work is never done, can never be done, because the plain will be learning for millennia and the humans who wander into its influence will always need help, need choices, need remembering, need witness.

But the power stops intervening directly. It has set things in motion—the stable ground, the remembering walls, the garden, the paths, the presence—and now it trusts these interventions to continue without constant attention. It has planted seeds of its own, seeds of possibility, seeds of choice, and now it must let them grow according to their own nature.

The power withdraws to the kind of attention it holds for all things—vast, patient, present but not intrusive, caring but not controlling. It watches the plain the way it watches everything: with hope that the learning will go well, with readiness to help if the need becomes dire, with acceptance that suffering is part of growth and cannot always be prevented, should not always be prevented.

It trusts the plain to become whatever the plain will become. It trusts the humans to make whatever choices they will make—to leave, to stay, to transform, to resist, to help, to rest. It trusts that the stable zone will hold, that the garden will spread, that the paths will remain available, that the presence will continue to offer witness without demand.

And it turns its attention to other things, other places that need care, other young universes practicing their first words, other beings caught in places where they don't belong, learning things they didn't want to learn, needing help that respects their autonomy, their right to their own mistakes, their own growth, their own strange and difficult transformations.

The plain breathes. The power breathes. The people caught between them breathe, and choose, and sometimes leave, and sometimes stay, and sometimes become something that is not quite people and not quite plain but their own third thing, and this is acceptable, is right, is the way growth happens when it happens honestly, without force, with care but also with respect for the wild autonomy of all things, even things that make themselves wrong, even things that learn slowly, even things that hurt in the process of becoming what they're trying to become.

The manufactory continues its work. But now there is rest beside the work. Now there is choice beside the gravity. Now there is witness beside the loneliness. Now there is a garden where only brick was growing.

And the plain, very slowly, across centuries, will learn what it's teaching itself. Will master continuity, will graduate to more complex lessons—purpose, perhaps, or beauty, or collaboration, or rest. Will become a mature universe, stable and coherent, its wild youth settled into sustainable form.

But not yet. For now, it makes. And beside the making, there is help for those who wander into the making's field. And the help is gentle, is optional, is offered without price or demand.

And this is what benevolence does when it's wise: not rescuing, not preventing, not controlling, but creating space, creating options, creating the conditions where growth can happen with less suffering, where learning can happen with more support, where even in the strange places, even in the places that shouldn't exist, there is care, there is choice, there is the possibility of rest, there is witness, there is hope.

The plain makes itself. The power watches. The people choose. The bricks emerge. The garden grows.

And in the spaces between all of these, in the pauses, in the gaps, in the moments of transition—there is something like grace, operating quietly, changing nothing by force and everything by presence, the way light changes a room just by entering, the way attention changes what it touches just by the quality of its regard.

This is the intervention: not rescue but relationship, not solution but support, not ending but endurance made slightly easier, choice made slightly clearer, loneliness made slightly less alone.

The plain breathes. Those who can rest, rest. Those who must walk, walk. Those who choose to build, build. And all of it is witnessed, honored, held in the vast patient attention of something that cares, that hopes, that trusts the slow work of growth, that knows—because it is old, because it has been young, because it remembers—that even strange learning has its rightness, even difficult transformation has its grace, even places that make themselves wrong are practicing, in their way, toward some distant rightness that will take millennia to achieve but is worth, is always worth, the difficult practice of emergence.
