# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives without announcement, as these things do. Not a presence exactly, but an *attending*—the Hollows becoming observed, considered, known in a way they have never been known before. The wise power perceives not just the infrastructure of steel and concrete, not just the flows of water and the pools of light, but the entire system: the marginal ecosystems, the struggling moss, the translucent fish, the creature that crossed the gantry once and has not been seen again.

And perceiving, the power understands something essential: the Hollows are *trying*.

Not consciously, not with intention, but with the fundamental tendency of structure to persist, of flow to find pathways, of life to establish wherever establishment is even remotely possible. The space is organizing itself, metabolizing its own abandonment, becoming something other than what it was made to be.

The power considers intervention. It has the capacity to *act*—to warm the space, to illuminate it fully, to adjust the water chemistry, to introduce species that would thrive, to accelerate the ecosystem's assembly into something robust and self-sustaining. It could make the Hollows into a garden, could transform marginal persistence into abundance.

But the power is wise, and wisdom recognizes that such intervention would be, in its way, another form of violence.

The Hollows were made by imposition—excavated, structured, purposed by force. Their abandonment was another imposition—withdrawal, cessation, a different kind of force. What is happening now, in the slow accumulation of moss and the patient hunting of blind fish, is the first thing that has happened here *not* by imposition. The Hollows are finding their own way, at their own pace, according to their own logic.

To intervene dramatically would be to impose again. To decide what the Hollows should become. To override the process of self-organization with external intention, however benevolent.

The power understands: the trying *is* the thing. The marginal persistence *is* the value. Not what the Hollows might become if optimized, but what they are becoming by themselves—slowly, uncertainly, but genuinely.

So the power chooses a different path. Not intervention but *accompaniment*. Not transformation but *support*. Small acts, subtle adjustments, things that work with the Hollows' own tendencies rather than against them.

## The First Gesture: Continuity

The lights are failing. One by one, the old industrial fixtures are dying, and each death makes the darkness larger, makes navigation more difficult, makes the pools of visibility smaller and more isolated.

The power does not replace the lights. That would be too much, would change the character of the space too dramatically. But it does this: it slows their failure.

The fixtures that still work will continue working. Not forever—the power is not interested in creating forever—but for longer. The electrical hum will persist. The harsh white light will continue to pool on wet concrete. The shadows will remain as they are, neither advancing nor retreating.

This is a small thing. The Hollows will not notice, because the Hollows do not notice. But the effect is real: stability. The light environment, such as it is, will remain stable for the time it takes the ecosystem to further establish, to develop, to find its own deeper stability.

The power makes this adjustment and nothing more. The lights do not brighten. No new fixtures illuminate. The dark sections remain dark. But the slow retreat into total darkness pauses. The Hollows gain time.

## The Second Gesture: Connectivity

The power traces the water system, following it through the channels, through the seepage paths, through the hydraulic logic that still governs the flows. It perceives that the system is becoming isolated—certain channels are clogging with sediment, certain passages are being blocked by mineral deposits, certain connections are failing.

This is natural. This is how abandoned infrastructure behaves. But it is also limiting—it is creating isolated pools where the ecosystem could fragment, where small populations could blink out, unable to be rescued by migration from other sections.

The power acts, carefully. It does not restore the hydraulics to their original state. It does not clear all blockages. But it *selects*—certain key connections, certain passages that would maintain system-wide flow, that would keep the channels networked enough for organisms to move between sections.

A blockage in a critical junction: the power causes it to erode slightly faster, to clear just enough for water to pass, for the small crustaceans to migrate through. A mineral deposit that would seal a passage entirely: the power shifts the water chemistry locally, temporarily, just enough to prevent complete closure.

The result is not restoration but *sustained degradation*—a pattern of failure that maintains function, that allows the Hollows to continue operating as a connected system even as they continue slowly falling apart.

The fish can move between pools. The arthropods can colonize new sections. The moss spores can travel with water flow. The ecosystem remains one system rather than fragmenting into isolated pockets.

## The Third Gesture: The Quiet Seasons

The temperature cycles, fluctuating with the hydraulics. But sometimes the cycles swing too far—too cold for the moss to metabolize, too warm for the frost moss to survive. The fluctuations are becoming more extreme as the control systems decay.

The power does not stabilize the temperature. That would remove the environmental variation that is driving adaptation, that is selecting for the organisms that can actually persist here. But it does this: it softens the extremes.

When the cold cycle would dip below the threshold where the frost moss dies, the power allows it to dip only to that threshold, no further. When the warm cycle would rise above the point where the iron moss desiccates, the power moderates it to merely uncomfortable rather than lethal.

This is not making the Hollows comfortable. They remain cold, damp, marginal. But the extremes that would cause mass die-offs, that would reset the ecosystem assembly to zero, are prevented. The organisms that have established can continue their slow persistence.

The power makes these adjustments subtly, through the machinery that still functions. A valve that opens slightly differently. A pump that cycles on a modified schedule. The changes are within the range of what the system might do naturally, if its decay had gone slightly differently. Nothing miraculous. Nothing obvious.

But the ecosystem gains stability. Not stability like a climax forest, not the stability of completion, but the stability of not being randomly extinguished. The stability of being allowed to continue trying.

## The Fourth Gesture: The Seeds of Possibility

The power considers the trees, the three strange growths that should not exist. It perceives their precariousness—they are barely surviving, barely growing, barely managing photosynthesis in the minimal light. They might persist for decades like this, or they might fail, die, leave the Hollows slightly poorer.

The power could do many things. It could increase their light, accelerate their growth, ensure their survival. But that would make them dependent, would create something artificial.

Instead, the power does something smaller: it ensures that when the trees produce seeds—if they produce seeds, on their own schedule—some of those seeds will find suitable substrate.

Not by creating new substrate. Not by modifying conditions. But by subtle adjustment of where debris accumulates, where organic matter collects, where the slow depositions of rust and concrete dust create something approximately like soil.

The trees will still succeed or fail on their own merits. But if they succeed, if they manage to reproduce, their offspring will have slightly better odds of finding purchase. The possibility of establishment is supported, not the establishment itself.

Whether the trees take advantage of this, the power does not determine. It simply opens the door slightly, and waits to see if anything walks through.

## The Fifth Gesture: The Witnesses

The cold zone, where humans once lived—the power perceives the enhanced ecosystem there, the way human presence enriched the local environment. It understands that human attention, human presence, is not inherently destructive. The Hollows can accommodate visitors, can be known without being harmed.

So the power does this: it makes the Hollows slightly more findable.

Not by creating access points. Not by building entrances. But by allowing word of this place to spread slightly more easily among those who seek such places. Urban explorers, photographers, people drawn to marginal spaces, abandoned infrastructure, the places where industrial purpose has given way to something stranger.

Not crowds. The power is careful. Not tourism or regular traffic. But occasional visitors, arriving irregularly, moving through the space with respect, seeing what exists here. Witnessing it.

Some will leave traces—footprints, perhaps photographs, possibly small offerings or marks. Some will take nothing but memory. All will add, slightly, to the organic budget, to the human connection to this place, to the web of attention that can protect without smothering.

The power does not bring people here directly. It simply adjusts probability, makes coincidence slightly more likely. Someone notices an unsealed access. Someone finds a reference in old documents. Someone follows water sounds to an opening they had not known existed.

The visitors will cycle through, each staying briefly, most leaving the Hollows largely unchanged. But their presence will have effect: their breath adding moisture, their dropped organic matter feeding decomposers, their photographs creating knowledge that this place exists and continues.

The Hollows will be known, slightly. And being known is a form of protection.

## The Sixth Gesture: The Patience of Deep Time

The power considers the largest question: how long?

The infrastructure will eventually fail completely. The concrete will crack irreparably. The steel will rust through. The channels will choke with sediment. The water will find new paths or cease flowing. Nothing lasts forever, not even things built to last.

The ecosystem establishing now is temporary. In a century, in five centuries, the Hollows will be different or will be gone. Whatever is assembling here will disassemble, transform, become something else or become nothing.

The power could extend the timeline, could preserve the infrastructure indefinitely, could ensure that the Hollows persist in their current form for geological durations.

But the power chooses not to.

Because the value here is not in forever. The value is in the process—in the trying, in the slow colonization, in the way life finds purchase in impossible places. To make it permanent would be to freeze it, to stop the process, to turn a living experiment into a dead monument.

Instead, the power does this: it ensures that the Hollows' transformation, however it unfolds, will be *slow*. Not prevented, not reversed, but slowed. Given time to be fully itself before becoming something else.

The concrete will last longer than it would have. The steel will rust through, but more gradually. The water will eventually find new paths, but not suddenly, not catastrophically. The changes will be incremental, allowing the ecosystem to adapt, to migrate, to transform along with its substrate.

The Hollows gain time. Not infinite time, not preserved time, but *enough* time. Time for the fish to breed through multiple generations. Time for the moss to spread and diversify. Time for the fungi to build soil depth. Time for something like succession to occur, for the system to progress through stages, to express possibilities.

And when the end comes—when the infrastructure finally fails past the point of supporting life, when the ecosystem must disperse or die—it will come slowly enough that the ending itself is a process rather than an event. Seeds will have time to disperse to new locations. Mobile organisms will have time to find other spaces. The established community will have time to leave traces, to influence what comes after.

## The Seventh Gesture: The Listening

Finally, the power does the thing that matters most: it *stays attentive*.

Not intervening further. Not adjusting or modifying or protecting. Simply witnessing. Paying attention. Knowing the Hollows as they continue their slow self-organization.

The fish swimming in darkness—the power observes them. The moss spreading millimeter by millimeter—the power knows it. The cat-sized thing moving through its territory—the power tracks its passage without interfering. The trees attempting reproduction—the power watches, curious, open to surprise.

This attention is not passive. Attention *does something*. To be known, to be witnessed, to be held in awareness—this matters. Not because it changes physical reality, but because it changes the web of significance, the field of meaning in which reality is embedded.

The Hollows are not forgotten. They are not abandoned to oblivion. They are *seen*, and the seeing creates a kind of shelter, a presence that does not intervene but does not abandon either.

When the lights flicker, something notices. When the water chemistry shifts, something perceives. When a new species colonizes or an established one disappears, something marks the change.

The Hollows exist in awareness, and awareness is a form of care.

## What Is Not Done

It is worth noting what the power does *not* do.

It does not warm the space, does not make it comfortable. The cold remains. The organisms that establish must do so in the cold, must adapt to the cold, must be genuinely capable of persistence here.

It does not introduce species. The ecology assembles from what arrives naturally—seeds blown in, spores on air currents, organisms migrating through water systems, creatures wandering in through access points. What establishes must be able to establish on its own terms.

It does not create food sources. The energy budget remains constrained. The moss must photosynthesize in minimal light. The fungi must decompose what little organic matter accumulates. The fish must hunt in darkness. The productivity remains marginal, and everything that lives here must cope with that marginality.

It does not accelerate processes. The ecosystem develops at its own pace—slowly, uncertainly, with setbacks and failures and small successes. The power does not hurry this. The speed is part of the authenticity.

It does not prevent suffering. The fish sometimes starve. The arthropods die in cold snaps. The moss desiccates. The rat-things struggle. Life here is difficult, and the power does not make it easy. To remove the difficulty would be to remove the reality.

It does not create meaning. The Hollows mean what they mean because of what they are, not because significance has been imposed upon them. They are industrial ruins becoming ecosystem, and that means what it means—to the organisms, to the occasional visitors, to the power itself—without requiring interpretation or purpose or justification beyond existence.

## The Result

To an observer, nothing seems different. The Hollows continue their slow metabolization of abandonment. The water flows. The lights flicker. The moss grows. The fish hunt. The cold persists.

But the trajectory has shifted, subtly. The probable futures have narrowed slightly—the catastrophic failures are less likely, the slow assembly more likely, the continuation more possible than it was.

The Hollows have gained what every fragile system needs: a slight bias toward persistence. Not certainty. Not guarantee. Just a small adjustment in probability, a gentle weight on the scale, enough to make continuation more likely than collapse.

The ecosystem will still struggle. It will still be marginal, precarious, assembled from inadequate resources. But it will struggle successfully, at least for a time. It will persist, develop, express its own strange becoming.

And the power, having done what it has done, steps back. Not abandoning attention—it will continue to witness, to know, to hold the Hollows in awareness. But not intervening further. Allowing the space to be what it is becoming, to try in its own way, to succeed or fail on its own terms within the gentle constraints of supported probability.

The Hollows remain themselves: cold, damp, echoing, marginal, strange. But they remain *possible*. And possibility is all any living system ever asks for.

The water continues its rush through the channels. The dripping maintains its scattered rhythm. The lights hum and flicker. And in the shadows, in the moss patches, in the pools and the rust and the slow accumulation of soil-like substrate, life continues its patient, improbable work.

The power attends. The Hollows persist. And the trying continues, witnessed, supported in its trying, allowed to be fully and authentically itself—marginal, cold, sparse, alive.
