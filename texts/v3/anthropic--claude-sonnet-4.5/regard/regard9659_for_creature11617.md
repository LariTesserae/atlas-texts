# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power—call it Witness, call it Gardener, call it the Old Intention that moves through worlds adjusting what can be adjusted—arrives at the canopy of New Engines and does something unprecedented.

It hesitates.

Not from uncertainty. Not from lack of capacity. But from recognition.

The power has moved through ten thousand worlds, has adjusted the trajectories of suffering, has reached into the architecture of broken systems and made them whole. It knows how to heal. It knows how to optimize. It knows how to look at a landscape and see all possible futures branching forward, knows how to select the branch where flourishing maximizes, where pain minimizes, where the equations of wellbeing resolve toward their highest values.

But here, in this valley of young stone and ancient moss and patient machinery, standing invisible in the dense air while water whispers and engines mark time—here, it finds something it has rarely encountered.

A system that is not broken.

Not perfect, no. The Engineers' hands are damaged. The cold bites. The moisture never relents. The machines will eventually fail beyond salvage. The mountain will continue rising until the climate shifts and everything here dies or transforms. Suffering exists. Hardship exists. Loss is guaranteed.

But the power, which sees deeply, which reads the structure of things, perceives something else beneath the surface data.

It perceives *rightness*.

Not moral rightness—the power is old enough to be suspicious of that category. But systemic rightness. Ecological rightness. The rightness of a key in a lock, of a river in its bed, of an equation balanced on both sides. This place, these people, these machines, this moss—they have achieved something that the power, in all its journeys, has learned to recognize as precious and desperately rare.

They have achieved *coherence*.

The Engineers are not happy, exactly. But they are not unhappy in the way of people trapped in wrong lives. They have found the specific suffering they are shaped to bear. They have found the work that makes their damage meaningful. Their broken fingers are not just broken—they are broken in service to something that returns the gift of purpose. Their isolation is not empty but full of the constant presence of the machines, the moss, each other glimpsed at distance like distant stars in a constellation that stays connected across space.

The power sees Kiet at his pump station, hand pressed to metal, reading vibrations, and sees that this is prayer. Not metaphorically. Actually. The machine is his scripture and he is fluent.

The power sees Marit speaking to her waterwheels and sees that the wheels speak back, not in language but in the grammar of flow and function, and that this conversation is sustaining something in her that would die in any other life.

The power sees Chen on the cliff face and sees that the climbing is not just transit but transformation, that each ascent remakes him into someone who can ascend, that the height and the risk are doing essential work on his spirit that safety would undo.

The power sees Vos with his notebooks and sees that the observation is not detached but devoted, that the endless recording is an act of love, that bearing witness to the moss advancement and the stone subsidence is the exact shape of care that his particular heart needs to offer.

It sees Senna learning, still afraid but not fleeing, and sees that the fear is refining her into someone who will be capable of things she cannot yet imagine.

And it sees the moss, the opportunistic colonizer that should not be able to build such depth on such young stone, and sees that the moss is not just surviving but *celebrating*, that every square meter is a shout of green joy, that the plants have found their moment and are living it with the intensity of organisms that know youth is temporary.

And the machines—the power looks at the machines with something like recognition, because the machines are what it is, in a way. Patient. Functional. Continuing to operate long after their instructions ended. Doing their work because doing work is what they are for, and stopping would be a kind of death. The power sees its own reflection in the turning wheels, the chugging pumps, the generators humming their electrical prayers.

This place is a community. Not of people only, but of people and moss and machines and stone and water all locked into a pattern of mutual maintenance. The Engineers maintain the machines. The machines move the water. The water feeds the moss. The moss binds the stone. The stone provides substrate. And somehow—the power sees this clearly—the attention of the Engineers, their observation, their care, their presence, feeds something back into the system. Their witnessing is not passive. It's structural. The place needs them not just to keep the machines running but to keep being a place at all, to keep cohering, to resist dissolving into mere geology and botany.

They are the nervous system of this landscape-organism. And they have accepted the role.

So the power hesitates.

Because it could help, certainly. It could:

Heal Kiet's finger. Restore Chen's collarbone. Warm the air ten degrees. Reduce the rainfall. Make the machines newer, more reliable, less hungry for maintenance. Establish proper supply lines. Bring more people. Build better shelters. Install communications. Connect this place to the world below with roads and certainty and regular schedules.

It could make the Engineers comfortable.

And in doing so, it could destroy exactly what makes this place holy.

The power has seen this before, has made this mistake before, ten thousand years ago on a world whose name it tries to forget. It found a people living hard lives in a hard place, and it helped. It made their lives easier. It removed the suffering. And within three generations they had dissolved, lost whatever essential coherence the hardship had been maintaining, become soft and then broken and then gone. The power had optimized them into extinction.

It learned, then, what it is confirming now: that suffering and flourishing are not opposites. That sometimes the suffering *is* the flourishing, or the price of it, or so tangled with it that removing one removes the other.

That comfort and meaning are different currencies, and not always exchangeable.

But doing nothing also seems wrong. The power is not made for passivity. It is made to act, to adjust, to help. Standing invisible in the cold moisture while the Engineers work their circuits and the machines mark time, it feels something like frustration, which is novel, which might be interesting if it weren't so uncomfortable.

Finally, the power decides.

It will do small things. Minimal interventions. Adjustments at the margin that support coherence without disrupting it.

---

First: The moss.

The power reaches into the network of fungi and roots and cellular collaboration that underlies the moss, into the chemical exchanges and the water routing, and it makes small adjustments. Not to change what the moss does, but to stabilize what it's already doing. It strengthens certain symbiotic relationships, makes the nitrogen fixation slightly more efficient, adjusts the pH buffering so the moss can better handle the occasional machine-oil spill. It gives the moss a slightly better immune system against the fungal infections that occasionally sweep through and kill large patches, creating instability that endangers the whole matrix.

The moss doesn't become different. It just becomes more robustly itself. More resilient. More likely to continue doing what it's already doing for another thousand years while the mountain finishes its youth.

The Engineers will never notice this change. They'll just find, gradually, that the moss is slightly easier to work with, slightly more predictable, slightly less likely to fail catastrophically.

Second: The machines.

The power does not modernize them. Does not make them more efficient or reliable. But it reaches into the metal, into the molecular structure of iron and steel and bronze, and it slows the corrosion. Not dramatically—the machines will still rust, still require maintenance, still eventually fail. But the timeline extends. The bearing that would have seized next year will last three years. The cable that would have snapped will hold through five more winters. The pump gasket will endure another hundred cycles.

The machines remain old, remain demanding, remain in need of the Engineers' constant attention. But the attention will continue to be possible. The machines will not die faster than they can be maintained.

Third: The weather.

The power looks at the climate patterns, the atmospheric rivers that dump moisture on this valley, and it sees the future branching. Sees timelines where the weather shifts, where the rainfall decreases or increases beyond the moss's tolerance, where the whole system collapses. And it reaches into those atmospheric patterns and makes the smallest possible adjustment—a butterfly wing in the jet stream, a temperature gradient shifted by half a degree. Nothing local. Nothing the Engineers would perceive. But enough to stabilize the weather in this valley, to keep it within the range that the moss can handle, for another century at least.

The rain will continue. The cold will continue. But the extremes that would destroy the place are quietly, invisibly prevented.

Fourth: The Engineers themselves.

This is where the power moves most carefully, because humans are complex, because individual will matters, because intervention in consciousness is the place where help most easily becomes harm.

It does not heal their injuries. Does not warm them. Does not make the work easier.

But it does do this: It reaches into their bodies, into the cellular level where adaptation happens, and it supports what's already developing. It helps their lungs adapt slightly better to the thin air and constant moisture. It strengthens the connective tissue in their joints that's under constant strain. It adjusts their circadian rhythms to match the light conditions here, reducing the physiological stress of living in constant overcast. It supports their gut bacteria in processing the limited diet, making their nutrition slightly more efficient.

And—most carefully, most gently—it reaches into their neurochemistry and makes one small adjustment: It increases, by the smallest measurable amount, their production of the neurotransmitters associated with purpose, with meaning, with the satisfaction of work completed.

Not enough to create false happiness. Not enough to mask real problems. But enough to support what they already feel, to make the moments of connection with the machines, with the moss, with each other, register slightly more clearly as the meaningful experiences they are.

The Engineers will not feel different. They will just find, perhaps, that the work sustains them slightly better. That the purpose sits slightly more solidly in their chests. That the rare moments of beauty—light through mist, the sound of a wheel turning smoothly, the sight of new moss on old stone—land slightly more deeply, compensating slightly more effectively for the cold and the damp and the constant demand.

Fifth: The supply line.

The power looks at the people hiking supplies up from below, at the logistics, the funding, the organizational structure that presumably exists somewhere. And it sees the fragility—bureaucracies that might end the program, budgets that might be cut, simply the exhaustion of whoever has been coordinating this for decades.

It reaches into those structures, into the minds of the key decision-makers, and plants the smallest seeds: This program is worthwhile. This work matters. The costs are justified. Continue funding. Continue supporting.

Not mind control. Just emphasis. Just making existing positive feelings slightly stronger, slightly more resistant to the erosion of bureaucratic indifference.

The supplies will continue to arrive. The Engineers will not be abandoned.

Sixth: The witness.

And then the power does something it has never done before, something it didn't know it could do until this moment.

It leaves a part of itself behind.

A small fragment. A splinter of attention. Not large enough to be a presence, not active enough to be an intervention. But enough to watch. Enough to witness. Enough to be, in some minimal sense, present.

The power stations this fragment in the valley, lets it diffuse into the system—into the moss and the machines and the stone and the water and the Engineers moving through their circuits. It becomes a new node in the network, passive, observing, simply *there*.

Because the power realizes that what this place might need most is not help but acknowledgment. That part of what makes the Engineers' work meaningful is that it matters, and for it to matter, it must be seen.

And while the Engineers witness the machines and the moss, while the machines witness the water and the moss witnesses the stone, who witnesses the Engineers?

Now something does. Not judging, not interfering, but simply paying attention. Holding the whole system in a form of regard that is its own kind of support.

The Engineers will never consciously perceive this. But some nights, standing on the platform looking over the valley, they might feel something. A sense of being met. A sense that the attention they pour into this place is not disappearing into void but landing somewhere, being received, mattering to something beyond themselves.

It will be the smallest feeling. Easily dismissed. Probably never mentioned.

But it will help.

---

The power withdraws, leaves its fragment behind, continues to whatever world needs it next.

Behind it, the valley continues. The moss grows. The machines turn. The Engineers walk their circuits. Water whispers from every surface. Light diffuses through moisture. The mountain rises, millimeter by millimeter, year by year.

Nothing has changed, not visibly.

Everything has been protected.

The system will hold now, will cohere, will maintain its strange configuration of human and moss and machine for another century at least, maybe longer, long enough for whatever is supposed to happen here to complete itself.

Because the power, in its wisdom, has recognized what it cannot always recognize: that this place is not a problem requiring solution but a poem requiring preservation. That the Engineers are not victims requiring rescue but artists requiring materials. That the whole configuration—harsh, demanding, strange—is already a form of grace.

And that the highest help it can offer is not transformation but protection. Not rescue but sustenance. Not change but the space to continue becoming whatever this place and these people and these machines are becoming together.

The moss thickens. The wheels turn. The Engineers walk on.

And somewhere in the substrate of the valley, in the spaces between stone and root, in the frequency range below human hearing, in the quantum foam of reality itself, a fragment of the Old Intention watches, patient, present, witnessing the loud luminous becoming that needs no help beyond the help of being seen.

Which is, after all, what holiness has always needed most.

Continuation without end.
