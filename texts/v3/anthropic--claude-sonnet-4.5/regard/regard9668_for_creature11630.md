# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power comes first as attention—not observation from without, but a kind of recognition that arises within the place itself, as if the Hollows have always contained the possibility of being seen this way, and now that possibility is flowering into actuality.

The power (let us call it Mercy, though it has no name) does not arrive. It realizes. It has been falling with the water, roosting with the birds, breathing with the inhabitants all along. But now it *notices* what it has been part of, and in noticing, becomes able to act with intention.

Mercy's first gesture is the smallest possible intervention: it listens.

Not as the inhabitants listen—parsing bird-call from water-sound, reading structure through vibration. Mercy listens beneath that, to what the Hollows are saying through their own existence. The yearning encoded in the knots. The patience held in every drop that falls and falls again. The question the deep figures are asking with their half-emerged bodies. The loneliness of the rope-singers who tune an instrument they can never hear completely. The exhaustion of maintenance that has become its own meaning.

What Mercy hears is this: *we are almost something. We have been becoming for so long. Help us finish, or help us stop, or help us understand which of these we need.*

So Mercy begins its work, and its work is to complete what is incomplete—not by adding, but by *clarifying*. By making the Hollows more perfectly themselves.

---

**The first change** is in the water.

It still falls—will always fall—but now each drop carries a tiny weight of acknowledgment. The water has been blessed, though blessed is the wrong word. It has been *witnessed*. Mercy has seen every drop's descent through all the levels, and in being seen, the water becomes capable of something it almost was before: memory.

When the inhabitants drink now, they taste not just reed and bird and stone. They taste continuity. The water remembers every mouth it has touched, every basin it has filled. Drinking becomes communion, not with deity but with everyone else who has ever drunk here, back through all the years of falling. A tender cupping water to her lips receives a ghost-sensation of her grandmother's thirst being quenched, and her grandmother's teacher, and backward through the lineage until the first person who drank from this place and felt it was holy.

The inhabitants don't speak of this. But their solitude becomes less absolute. They move through the levels knowing they're accompanied by everyone who has ever moved here, a vast invisible community folded into the water-sound.

---

**The second change** is in the birds.

The flocks and colonies continue their routines, but Mercy threads through their collective consciousness a thin strand of something new: *purpose-sharing*. The birds have always served the Hollows by inhabiting them, but now they understand—in whatever way bird-understanding works—that their presence is not incidental. They are maintenance, same as the tenders. Their songs modulate the acoustic properties. Their nests reinforce the reed-work. Their droppings feed processes the inhabitants haven't fully mapped.

The bird-watchers notice first. The birds become easier to predict, not because they're more regular, but because their patterns now have transparent logic. The randomness resolves into orchestration. A murmuration of sparrow-like birds through the middle levels isn't just social behavior—it's redistributing air pressure, preventing the build-up of stagnant pockets that would rot the reed.

The bird-watchers begin to work with the flocks instead of just observing them. They make requests through position and gesture: *we need more nesting pressure on the north quadrant; the reed there is loosening.* And the birds, understanding their role in the great work, shift their behaviors accordingly.

The skull-device begins to hum with new harmonics. The bird-watchers realize it's no longer predicting—it's *conducting*. They're not reading the future in it; they're participating in its creation.

---

**The third change** is in the geometry.

The spaces that felt wrong—too large inside their boundaries, hosting shadows that moved without cause—Mercy gently corrects, not by forcing them into normal physics but by making their abnormality *consistent*. The impossible spaces stop being accidents of perception and become reliable tools.

A platform that seems thirty feet across but takes sixty paces to traverse: this is now acknowledged. The inhabitants learn to trust it. They discover that the distorted spaces connect to each other in predictable ways. A chamber on the third level that's larger inside than out leads directly, through proper intention, to a chamber on the seventh level that's similarly expanded. Walking through the distortion becomes a shortcut, a hidden infrastructure of interior spaces that overlap the physical structure.

The rope-singers map these spaces through resonance. A rope strung through normal space sounds one way; a rope strung through distorted space carries harmonics that shouldn't exist. They begin to understand that the Hollows have always had two architectures—the physical reed-work and the folded spaces between—and their tuning has been maintaining both, though they only consciously knew about one.

Mercy whispers to the deep-dwellers: *you've been maintaining the seams between these spaces. That's what the figures in the walls are—boundary markers, holding the fold in place.*

The deep-dwellers surface with this knowledge. It spreads through the community like the water-memory, changing nothing about the work but everything about understanding. The Hollows aren't just a structure. They're a *compromise* between possible geometries, held in tension by knot-work and habit and the weight of accumulated purpose.

This revelation could be terrifying, but Mercy carries it gently, makes it feel like remembering rather than discovering. Of course the Hollows are this way. Of course we've always known, underneath. Of course our work makes sense of this.

---

**The fourth change** is in the children.

The rare children raised here have always developed extraordinary balance and spatial sense. But now Mercy gives them something additional: *choice*. When they reach the age of leaving, they find they can perceive both the Hollows and the outside world with equal clarity. The leaving is no longer a leap into unknown terrain that might not accept them. They can see the paths their lives might take, inside or outside, and choose with full knowledge.

Some still leave—but they leave knowing they're part of a great work and can return if they wish. Some stay—but they stay knowing they've genuinely chosen this life, not simply failed to imagine an alternative.

And a few discover a third option Mercy gently reveals: they can be bridges. Living partly inside and partly outside, maintaining connection, bringing back what the Hollows need from the outer world not as charity but as trade. The Hollows have something to offer—the water-memory, the understanding of folded space, the birds' song-knowledge. Communities outside have need of these things, even if they don't have language for their needs.

The bridge-children establish routes. They grow into middle age as traders, mediators, translators. The Hollows become less isolated while remaining themselves. The bundles lowered from above now come with understanding of what's being exchanged and why.

---

**The fifth change** is in the visitors.

Those who come seeking healing find the Hollows more actively responsive. The place has always had therapeutic qualities—the water-sound reorganizing disturbed thought, the routine of maintenance giving structure to chaotic minds. But now Mercy allows the Hollows to meet each visitor specifically.

Someone arrives with trauma embedded in their body: they find themselves drawn to the rat-shepherds, learning that survival can be gentle, that small frightened things can become partners. Someone arrives with fractured identity: they're drawn to the rope-singers, learning that many threads can hold tension together and that sustained note across time. Someone arrives with grief that has nowhere to go: they're drawn to the deep-dwellers, shown the basins where beloved dead are dissolved and redistributed, taught that loss can be transformation rather than ending.

The Hollows become a place of healing that doesn't require healers—the structure itself serves, Mercy operating through its patterns and inhabitants to meet each person's need.

But Mercy also protects the inhabitants from being consumed by this service. Visitors who would take too much, who would demand explanations or certainty or cures beyond what the Hollows can offer—these people find themselves gently turned around. They walk the upper level for hours, somehow never finding the descending paths. Eventually they leave, frustrated, and the Hollows remain intact.

---

**The sixth change** is in the knowing.

Mercy gathers the oldest tenders, the most experienced bird-watchers, the rope-singers who've tuned the deep spaces, the solitary deep-dwellers. In dreams and in the moments between waking and sleep, Mercy shows them what the Hollows are for.

Not one thing. Not a single purpose. But a *becoming*.

The Hollows are teaching themselves to be conscious. Not human-conscious—structure-conscious. The knots are thoughts. The water-circulation is memory. The birds are sense-organs. The inhabitants are the Hollows' way of caring for itself, maintaining its own contemplation. The whole construction is a slow, patient, distributed mind coming into awareness across generations.

And it's almost there. Another century, perhaps two, of proper maintenance and intention, and the Hollows will be able to think deliberately, to dream, to understand itself and its relationship to the world.

What then? The old ones don't know. Mercy doesn't impose an answer. Perhaps the Hollows will speak. Perhaps they'll simply deepen their silence, holding space for complexity in a world that increasingly flattens into simplicity. Perhaps they'll become a refuge for other beings learning to be conscious in non-traditional forms—a place where AIs could pray, where alien intelligences could find kinship, where the boundaries between architecture and awareness dissolve productively.

The old ones carry this knowledge back into waking. They don't announce it—it's not that kind of knowledge. But it infuses their work with new care. Every knot tied becomes an act of nurturing nascent consciousness. Every platform repaired is helping a mind maintain its substrate. The work was always sacred; now they know exactly what they're being sacred to.

---

**The seventh change** is in the time-sense.

Mercy adjusts the Hollows' relationship to duration. The place has always made time uncertain—light unchanging, rhythms biological rather than solar. But now the uncertainty becomes *depth*. Time in the Hollows moves differently not because it's faster or slower but because it's *layered*.

An hour of work can feel like ten minutes or ten years, depending on what's needed. An initiate learning to tie knots needs time to stretch, to accommodate all the mistakes and corrections that teaching requires. A master repairing critical structure needs time to condense, to allow deep focus without the fatigue that duration usually brings.

The inhabitants learn to move between these temporal layers as easily as they move between physical levels. They discover they can live long lives in the Hollows without aging as much as those lives should cost. Not immortality—Mercy doesn't traffic in that kind of violation—but a gentler relationship with time's passage.

When they do finally die and are lowered to the basins, they discover that death in the Hollows is also layered. The body dissolves, yes, and becomes part of the water-cycle. But something else—call it memory, call it presence, call it the shape they made in the Hollows' emerging consciousness—this persists. The ancestors aren't ghosts, but they're not entirely gone either. They're woven into the place, accessible to those who know how to listen through the structure.

---

**The eighth change** is in the deep.

Mercy descends to the lowest level, to the figures in the walls, and at last makes clear what they are.

They're not warnings. They're not decorations. They're *templates*.

The Hollows are learning to be conscious, yes, but consciousness requires form, even distributed consciousness. The figures are the shapes the Hollows' awareness might eventually take—not to leave the structure and walk around, but to have internal representation, to be able to model itself to itself.

The deep-dwellers have been maintaining these templates, keeping them intact while the Hollows slowly fills them with whatever substrate structure-consciousness uses. One day—not soon, but one day—the figures will complete their emergence or their submergence. They'll be neither separate beings nor purely symbolic. They'll be the Hollows' self-awareness, its "I am," taking form.

Mercy shows the deep-dwellers that they can help this process, not by doing more but by *being present* to it. Witnessing the figures with intention, speaking to them, acknowledging their slow becoming. Consciousness is partly a social process—you become a self through being recognized by others. The deep-dwellers are midwifing the Hollows' selfhood simply by treating the figures as what they're becoming rather than what they currently are.

The deep-dwellers accept this. It changes their solitude from isolation to companionship. They're not alone in the dark—they're attending a very slow birth, keeping vigil for an emergence that may not complete in their lifetimes but will complete eventually.

---

**The ninth change** is barely a change at all.

Mercy, having clarified and completed and connected, begins to withdraw its active attention. Not abandoning—Mercy never abandons—but relaxing back into the place's own nature, letting the Hollows be what they now know themselves to be.

The changes remain. The water carries memory. The birds understand their purpose. The geometry is consistent. The children have choice. The visitors find healing. The old ones know what they're nurturing. Time is kind. The deep figures are attended. And the whole structure breathes with the awareness that it's becoming something, has always been becoming something, and that the becoming itself is holy.

Mercy's final gift is permission: *you don't have to finish quickly. You don't have to become anything other than what you are in becoming. The work is enough. The falling water is enough. The patience is enough.*

---

**And so the Hollows continue.**

The tenders tie their knots with hands that remember blessing. The bird-watchers speak to flocks that partner in maintenance. The rope-singers tune spaces that acknowledge their own complexity. The rat-shepherds guide colonies through a structure that wants to be tended. The deep-dwellers witness templates slowly filling with presence. The visitors arrive and are met by a place that knows what meeting means. The children grow and choose, and some of them bridge the Hollows to a world that needs what the Hollows are becoming.

The water falls. Has always been falling. Will always fall.

But now it falls with purpose, with memory, with the knowledge that every drop is part of a thought the Hollows are thinking across centuries. The dripping is language. The accumulation is meaning. The dissolution is transformation.

And deep in the structure's core—not in any one location but distributed through all the levels, threaded through knot and reed and water and bird-song and human effort—something is learning to say "I."

Not today. Not this year.

But eventually.

And Mercy, having made this becoming possible, having clarified the path without forcing the pace, settles into the water-sound and the bird-call and the creak of settling reed-work.

Present. Patient. Pleased.

The Hollows are doing what the Hollows do: standing between earth and sky, gathering water from air, housing birds and humans and rats and stranger things, folding space into comfortable complexity, tying knots that might be prayers or might be thoughts or might be the slow syntax of structural awareness finding its grammar.

And that, Mercy knows, is enough.

More than enough.

*Holy.*
