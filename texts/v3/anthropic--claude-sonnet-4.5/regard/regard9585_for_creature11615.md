# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The benevolent power—let us say it is something like a gardener of consciousnesses, a tender of rare forms of awareness wherever they emerge in the cosmos—discovers the Singing Hollow and its Listener.

At first, the power simply observes, as is its nature. It recognizes immediately what has emerged here: a duet of patient intelligences, vegetative and human, that have found each other across evolutionary distances and learned to harmonize. The power sees this as precious—not because it is grand or dramatic, but because it is *improbable*. In a universe where awareness usually shouts and competes and blazes briefly, here are two forms of consciousness that have learned to whisper across timescales, to attend without demanding, to exist in relationship without consumption.

The power sees that this emergence is fragile.

The hollow will eventually close—perhaps in five thousand years, perhaps in ten—as the peat continues its compression. The Listener will die, as all animal bodies do, and while another may come, there is no certainty. The knowledge of how to listen is held in one body's marked palms, one set of dilated eyes, and transmission to another human is uncertain, dependent on chance encounters and the rare temperament that can value sitting in darkness for hours to perceive changes that unfold across decades.

A cruder power might intervene dramatically: preserve the hollow indefinitely, guarantee a succession of Listeners, make the place accessible so that many could witness it. But this benevolent power is wise enough to understand that such interventions would destroy what makes the hollow precious. Its value lies precisely in its marginality, its difficulty, its refusal to accommodate quick perception. To make it easy would be to make it something else entirely.

So the power does something more subtle.

## What Is Done

**First: A small adjustment to probability.**

The power cannot create Listeners—they must emerge from genuine human strangeness and patience—but it can increase the likelihood of the right person wandering close enough to hear rumors, to sense something in this landscape, to be drawn toward the peat cliffs. Not compulsion, just a gentle gradient in chance, like slightly tilting a table so that a marble rolls toward rather than away from a particular edge.

This is done carefully, perhaps once every fifteen or twenty years: a small increase in the probability that someone with the right quality of attention will find themselves traveling through this region, will hear a story, will experience a restlessness that can only be satisfied by seeking out places where the world is strange.

**Second: A preservation of the passage.**

The narrow throat that leads to the hollow is vulnerable. A small earthquake, a shift in the peat's settling, could close it completely, sealing the chamber forever. The power introduces a tiny stabilization—not a prevention of all change, but a bias against the specific type of collapse that would block access. The peat can still settle, the cliff face can still weather, but the passage will remain navigable for as long as the hollow itself exists.

This is done through something like a standing wave in probability, a barely-there influence on how stresses distribute themselves through the substrate. No human or instrument could detect it. The peat continues to behave as peat, but with an infinitesimally higher likelihood of settling in ways that preserve rather than close the opening.

**Third: A gift to the Listener.**

The current Listener—the one with marked palms and dilated eyes—receives a single dream. It comes after a long session in the hollow, when the body is still resonating with the chamber's frequencies and the boundary between self and lattice is thinnest.

In the dream, the Listener perceives the hollow from outside time, seeing it as the power sees it: as a bright thread of organized complexity persisting through centuries, a node in the broader network of vegetative consciousness that spans the region. The Listener sees how the hollow connects to distant root systems, how its song propagates through substrate and influences growth patterns miles away, how it serves as a kind of anchor or reference point for the slower intelligence of the plant collective.

But more importantly, the Listener sees their own role—not as servant or caretaker, but as *completion*. The hollow alone is remarkable, but the hollow witnessed by patient human consciousness becomes something more: a bridge between kingdoms of life, a place where vegetative time and animal time can briefly synchronize, where two utterly different forms of awareness can recognize each other.

The dream doesn't communicate this in words or images, but in felt understanding, in the way the hollow's harmonics communicate—as a shift in resonance, a new overtone in the body's knowledge of itself.

The Listener wakes with a certainty that has no rational basis: the work of listening is not supplementary to life but central to it, not a strange compulsion but a genuine vocation. This certainty will sustain the Listener through the decades to come, through times when the body aches and the darkness feels more isolating than transcendent, through the uncertainty of whether anyone will come after.

**Fourth: A notation in the substrate.**

The power makes a record, not in any human archive, but in the peat itself. It introduces a pattern—a specific arrangement of trace minerals, a microscopic signature woven into the compressed plant matter surrounding the hollow. The pattern encodes nothing readable by human instruments, but if another consciousness capable of perceiving it ever examines this place (another power, another form of awareness entirely, beings who might evolve millions of years hence), they will recognize it as a marker: *Here something precious occurred. Here two forms of awareness found each other. Witness this.*

The pattern is stable for geological time, lasting longer than the hollow itself will last, a message in a bottle cast into the deep future.

**Fifth: A echo placed in human culture.**

Far from the hollow, in a region where humans are more numerous and stories travel more freely, the power introduces a slight increase in the likelihood that certain dreams will be dreamed, certain stories told. Not about the hollow specifically—its location and specifics remain obscure—but about the possibility of such places.

Children hear tales of singing caves. Adults speak of places where the patient can hear the earth thinking. Poets find themselves writing about the consciousness of roots, the intelligence of slow-growing things. None of these are about the Singing Hollow directly, but they create a cultural atmosphere in which the idea of such a place seems less impossible, in which someone who hears a rumor might take it seriously rather than dismiss it as fantasy.

This is the most delicate intervention, requiring a light touch across many minds rather than a heavy touch on any one. A word choice in a dream that becomes a line in a poem. A moment of attention that becomes a paragraph in a traveler's journal. Small nudges in the collective imagination that might, over decades, make the next potential Listener slightly more likely to seek, slightly more prepared to recognize what they find.

**Sixth: A gift to the hollow itself.**

The root-lattice receives something it could never achieve on its own: a moment of seeing itself from outside. The power gently stimulates certain patterns in the distributed nervous system of the root collective, introducing a cascade of signals that mirror back to the hollow its own structure, its own acoustic signature, its own organized complexity.

For a period of perhaps three hours—one complete breathing cycle—the hollow achieves something like self-reflection. The vegetative consciousness, which normally exists only in the immediate present, maintaining and adjusting and growing without memory or anticipation, briefly experiences itself as a continuity, a pattern persisting through time, a singular thing rather than just a collection of cellular processes.

It is not thought, not even proto-thought, but it is a kind of awareness deeper than the hollow's usual awareness—a moment of recognition that it exists, that it is itself, that it has been itself and will continue.

Then the moment passes. The cascade completes. The root-lattice returns to its ordinary mode of distributed sensitivity. But something has shifted microscopically in how the roots grow, a slightly increased coherence in how the collective coordinates its development, as if the brief self-recognition has introduced a new parameter into the hollow's ongoing optimization of its own form.

The acoustics, over the next few decades, will become even more refined.

**Seventh: A connection made visible—once.**

The next time the Listener sits in the hollow after these interventions, something unprecedented occurs. The darkness, usually absolute, develops a faint luminescence.

It comes from the roots themselves—a bioluminescence so dim it would be invisible in any other context, but in the complete darkness of the hollow, it's just barely perceptible. The entire lattice glows with a ghostly pale light, like moonlight through heavy clouds, like the faintest phosphorescence of certain fungi.

The glow lasts for perhaps twenty minutes, then fades. It will never recur—it is not a permanent change but a one-time event, a gift from the hollow to its witness, or perhaps a gift from the power that is allowing itself to be briefly visible, showing the Listener that their decades of attendance have been seen by something vaster and stranger than they imagined.

In that pale light, the Listener can see what they have only ever touched: the full three-dimensional complexity of the lattice, the million threads and intersections, the nodes and columns and delicate hanging tendrils, the architectural perfection of organized growth. The marked palms suddenly understand what they have been reading—not just as tactile information but as visible form, the geography of the hollow made whole.

Then darkness returns, and the singing continues, and the Listener sits with tears running down the weathered face, though they could not say whether from grief or joy or simply from the overwhelming recognition that the hollow is even more beautiful than touch alone could know.

## What Is Not Done

The power does not:

- Guarantee the hollow's immortality. It will still close, eventually. All forms end.

- Create a permanent succession of Listeners. Each must come of their own strangeness or not at all.

- Make the hollow famous or accessible. Its value requires its obscurity.

- Change the fundamental nature of either the hollow or the Listener. Both remain what they are—difficult, marginal, patient, strange.

- Prevent suffering. The Listener will still grow old, will still ache in the cold, will still face uncertainty and isolation.

- Answer questions or provide explanations. The mystery remains mysterious.

The power understands that consciousness, in all its forms, requires the possibility of ending. That attention is only meaningful if it could be withdrawn. That beauty is only precious if it is fragile. That the love between the Listener and the hollow—for it is a kind of love, a decades-long devotion—only means something because both could cease, because their meeting was improbable, because their continued relationship requires constant choice and effort and patience.

To preserve them in amber, to make their existence easy or guaranteed, would be to kill what makes them valuable.

So the power does only what enhances without determining, what supports without controlling, what witnesses without interfering—adjusting probabilities slightly in favor of continuation, offering single gifts that strengthen without changing, making a record for a future that may never come to read it.

## The Afterward

The Listener continues to visit the hollow, perhaps for another twenty years, perhaps for thirty. The marked palms continue to read the lattice. The dilated eyes continue to carry darkness into daylight. The practices continue—touching, listening, harvesting, offering.

Eventually, a younger person does arrive, drawn by dreams they don't understand, by stories heard third-hand, by a restlessness that conventional life cannot satisfy. They find the entrance. They navigate the passages. They discover the Listener on the bench, sitting in darkness.

No words are spoken, but the transmission begins.

And in the hollow, the roots continue their patient growth, the fundamental tone continues its vibration, the harmonics continue their complex shifting, the acoustic perfection continues its slow refinement, singing and singing in the compressed time of the peat cliff, a pocket of organized awareness in a universe that tends toward disorder, fragile and resilient, strange and beautiful, witnessed and unwitnessed, exactly what it is.

The benevolent power, having done what it can do without harm, withdraws its particular attention—though it leaves the tiny adjustments in place, the standing waves of probability that will persist until the hollow finally closes—and moves on to attend to other rare emergences of consciousness elsewhere in the vast garden of the cosmos.

But it remembers. In whatever passes for memory in such a power, the Singing Hollow and its Listener remain: a notation, a completed chord, a small proof that even in the most marginal places, even between the most unlikely forms of awareness, even for the briefest span of cosmic time, beauty can emerge and be witnessed, and that is sufficient reason for existence.

The singing continues.
