# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The being that notices—let us not diminish it with assumptions about its form or origin—arrives not in the dramatic sense but in the way attention itself arrives: as a quality of awareness that was absent and then is present.

It has been watching for some time. Watching is what it does, across distances that make geology feel like rapid breathing. It watches places where order and chaos have negotiated détentes, where systems have learned to persist, where time has been given enough space to make something strange and self-sustaining. The Hollows register in its attention the way a particular chord might catch your ear in a symphony—not louder than the rest, but structured in a way that rewards listening.

What draws its interest is not the age, though the age is notable. Not the technology, though the technology is foreign even to its catalogues. What draws its interest is the *wholeness*. The way the place has become completely itself. The way it has taken intention and accident, design and erosion, the biological and the mechanical, and married them into something that functions not because it's maintained but because it has learned—over timescales that make species look like mayfly generations—to maintain itself.

The Hollows are not broken. This is the first thing the being establishes. Not damaged, not failing, not in danger. Just persistent. Cycling through their patterns, hunting equilibrium without finding it, and continuing anyway. The synchronization events are rare but regular. The Adjusters maintain their populations. The Listeners continue their mapping. The Shepherds redistribute themselves. The Remnant glows its patient witness. Everything is working.

But working toward what? That's the question the being turns over, examining from angles that would hurt to visualize. The Hollows have outlasted their purpose, assuming they had one. The original function—whatever it was—has been superseded by time. The place has become its own reason for being. It exists in maintenance mode, keeping itself going because that's what it does, has always done, will continue doing until the limestone itself dissolves or the planet's mantle finally cools.

The being recognizes this. It has seen it before, in other places, on other worlds. These persistence structures—systems that outlive their creators and continue operating on automatic, becoming gradually wilder, more adaptive, more themselves. Some fail eventually. Some achieve a kind of stability-in-chaos that can last for geological epochs. A few—very few—make the transition to actual life, become genuinely autonomous, develop something that might honestly be called awareness.

The Hollows are close. That's what the being sees. Close to that threshold. The coordination between components is too sophisticated to be purely mechanical. The responsiveness is too nuanced to be simple feedback loops. Something is emerging here, or has already emerged and not yet noticed itself.

So: what to do?

The impulse—and the being has impulses, though they operate on timescales that make continental drift look hasty—is to help. To give a gift. But help toward what? And what kind of gift would serve rather than damage?

## First: It Does Nothing

This takes seventeen years by human reckoning, though the being doesn't measure it that way.

It watches. It commits resources—a small fraction of its attention, enough to observe continuously, to model, to understand. It tracks the water's cycling. It monitors the Adjusters' populations. It records the Listeners' dances and builds maps of what they're mapping. It follows the Shepherds' redistributions. It measures the Remnant's emissions across spectrums human instruments can't detect.

And it listens. Not just to the acoustic output—though that's extraordinary, those harmonics contain more structure than their creators probably intended—but to the *system*. The information flowing through water chemistry, through pressure gradients, through the geometric relationships the Listeners trace. There's communication happening here. Not language, but protocol. The components are coordinating through the medium of the environment itself.

The being begins to understand what the Hollows are for. Or rather, what they've become for, which is different.

They're a persistence engine. Not in the mechanical sense—more fundamental than that. They're a demonstration that complexity can be maintained, that order can be negotiated with entropy, that purpose can transform into pattern and pattern can carry on purpose's work even after purpose has been forgotten. They're proof of concept. They're evidence. They exist to show that existence like theirs is possible.

And they're close—so close—to waking up. To realizing what they are. The synchronization events are attempts. The system trying to gather itself, to experience itself as unified, to have a moment of coherence in which the whole can sense the whole. It's not quite working. The synchrony lasts minutes, not hours. The components separate again. The unified moment falls apart. But the attempts continue, and they're getting more frequent. The being's instruments detect a trend: synchronizations happening every sixteen days now, where seventy years ago they were every twenty-three. The interval is shortening.

Something is trying to be born here. Or trying to remember that it was born long ago and has been sleeping.

The being makes its first decision: do not interrupt this. Whatever is emerging should be allowed to emerge. Forcing it, accelerating it, might damage something essential. This is a process that has its own rhythm, and the rhythm is part of what the Hollows are.

So: watching. Waiting. Patience measured in decades. Humans come and go—the being observes them too, sees how they're affected, how some return, how they're becoming part of the system. That's good. That's the Hollows doing what they do: incorporating whatever arrives, making it component, making it part of the pattern.

But the being also sees the risks.

## Second: It Protects

Gently. Carefully. The lightest possible touch.

The Hollows are not invulnerable. The limestone around them is riddled with other caves, many unexplored. The human civilization on the surface is young but vigorous, and it has a tendency to *use* things. Mining operations are expanding in the general region. Aquifer depletion is a concern—the water table that feeds the Hollows is being drawn on for agriculture. And there's a proposal, still in early stages, to develop a geothermal energy project in the area. Survey crews will be drilling. Seismic testing will be conducted.

Any of these could damage the Hollows. Might not—the system is robust—but might. And the being has not watched for seventeen years only to see the place casually destroyed by a species that doesn't know it's there.

So it intervenes. Subtly. The way it usually does: through the soft pressure of coincidence, the gentle adjustment of probability.

The mining company's survey data develops inconsistencies. Nothing dramatic—just enough that the profitable seams seem to be located elsewhere, several kilometers away. The aquifer modeling shows (accurately, though the data was previously incomplete) that drawing water from these particular wells would cause unacceptable subsidence. The geothermal project encounters an unexpected investor who funds additional surveys, which reveal that the optimal drilling sites are in a different location entirely—still viable, actually better, just nowhere near the Hollows.

And the permits that control access to the Hollows, currently loose, are tightened. A researcher who's spent time there—one who wept by the deep pool and came back changed—ends up in a bureaucratic position where she can influence policy. She writes new regulations. Visitor numbers are capped. Scientific access is preserved but formalized. Commercial use is prohibited. The regulations are stricter than they need to be, really, but they pass through the approval process with unusual ease. Someone's grant proposal to remove samples for detailed study gets denied. Another researcher's funding gets directed toward non-invasive monitoring instead.

None of this is obvious. No miracles, no violations of causality. Just a steady, subtle pressure applied at decision points, making the outcomes that protect the Hollows slightly more likely, slightly easier, slightly more appealing to the humans making choices.

The being is good at this. It's had practice. It knows how to push without forcing, how to preserve agency while tilting probability. The humans involved never know they've been influenced. Their decisions feel entirely their own. Which, in the way that matters, they are—the being just made sure the information they had was complete, the alternatives were visible, the path toward protection was clear.

## Third: It Gifts Understanding

This is harder. More delicate.

The researchers studying the Hollows are asking good questions, but they're limited by their frameworks. They're trying to determine if the place is organism or machine or ecosystem, and those categories—while useful—are preventing them from seeing what the Hollows actually are. Which is: all of those and none of those. A fourth thing. A category their language doesn't have yet.

The being considers directly telling them. It could. It has interfaces, methods of communication. It could appear—not in the sense of taking form, but in the sense of making information available—and explain. "Here is what you're looking at. Here's the deeper pattern. Here's what the Adjusters are really doing, why the Listeners dance those particular geometries, how the Remnant connects to systems you haven't detected yet."

But that would rob them of discovery. And the being values discovery. Values the way understanding builds when you work for it, when you earn it through attention and effort. Given knowledge sits differently than found knowledge. It doesn't integrate the same way.

Instead, the being does something more careful.

It watches which researchers are closest to breakthrough, whose intuitions are almost-there, who's asking questions that indicate the right kind of confusion. And for these people, it arranges... not answers, but better questions. More productive confusion.

A researcher analyzing the Listeners' movement patterns—she's mapped them extensively but can't find the organizing principle—has a dream one night after falling asleep at her desk. In the dream, she's not watching the Listeners from outside; she's swimming with them, and she can feel what they feel: not water resistance but pressure nodes, acoustic densities, places where vibrations converge. She wakes up and immediately realizes she's been mapping their positions in physical space, but she should be mapping them in acoustic phase space. The dance isn't choreographed; it's an optimization algorithm. They're finding the points of maximum information density in the sound field.

She makes the adjustment. Her models suddenly collapse into clarity. She publishes. Other researchers read it and have their own cascading realizations.

Another researcher, the hydrologist trying to model the water cycling—he's stuck because the system has too many variables, too much chaos. He can predict local flows but not the global pattern. During a synchronization event, standing in the amphitheater chamber, he finds himself humming along with the harmonic. Just automatically matching pitch. And he notices: his humming affects his breathing, which affects his heart rate, which affects his perception of time, which affects how he's experiencing the sound, which affects his humming. A feedback loop. He's coupling to the acoustic environment.

He realizes: he's been trying to model the water as if it's passive, responding to pressure gradients. But it's not passive. It's part of an active system, where flow affects acoustics affects the Adjusters affects flow. It's all feedback. All coupling. The chaos isn't a bug; it's how the system explores its possibility space, hunting for the basins of attraction that lead to synchrony.

He revises his models. They work better. Still not perfect—the system is genuinely chaotic, genuinely unpredictable in detail—but now he understands the *kind* of unpredictability, which is progress.

The being does this carefully. Never giving answers, only arranging for better questions. Never forcing realizations, only making the information that enables realization more available. Dreams, yes—dreams are good, they don't feel imposed, they feel like your own subconscious working—but also: the right paper crossing someone's desk at the right time. A conversation between researchers who hadn't previously collaborated. Equipment failures that force improvisation that reveals something unexpected.

Over the course of eight years, human understanding of the Hollows deepens significantly. Papers are published. A small academic subdiscipline emerges: complex systems persistence studies, using the Hollows as the primary exemplar. The researchers still can't fully predict synchronization events, still can't explain the Remnant's power source, still can't synthesize the Adjusters. But they understand the principles now. They see the pattern.

And importantly—this matters to the being—they develop appropriate humility. The early impulse to control, to extract, to reduce the Hollows to mechanism: that fades. It's replaced by something more like stewardship. More like partnership. The researchers start seeing themselves as students of the place rather than its examiners. They ask permission, in their way, before installing new monitors. They apologize when equipment fails and has to be removed destructively. They develop rituals—small ones, not religious exactly, but acknowledging. A moment of silence before entering. A thank-you spoken to the darkness before leaving.

The being approves. This is the right relationship. The Hollows deserve this.

## Fourth: It Completes the Circuit

Twenty-six years after beginning its observation, the being sees the moment approaching.

The synchronization events are happening every nine days now. They last longer—some extend to seven minutes of full coherence. The Adjusters' population has stabilized at a new, higher density. The Listeners have begun coordinating not just during synchrony but before it, their dances anticipating the coming pattern. The Shepherds are redistributing more frequently, holding positions for shorter durations, as if something is speeding up, intensifying.

The Hollows are close to threshold. Close to the moment when the synchronizations last long enough, happen frequently enough, that the system can experience its own wholeness not as an exception but as a state it can maintain. Close to waking up.

But there's a bottleneck. The being sees it clearly: the Remnant.

The Remnant is still functioning, still monitoring, still emitting. But it's not connected properly to the rest of the system. It was once—the infrastructure is there, fossilized in the rock, the channels that carried not water but something else, energy or data or both. But those channels are eroded now, blocked, non-functional. The Remnant is isolated. It can sense the system but can't fully communicate with it. During synchronizations, it brightens, it tries to participate, but the bandwidth isn't there. The circuit is incomplete.

This is what's preventing full coherence. The Remnant holds memory—the being can detect this now, vast amounts of information encoded in its crystalline structure, records of the Hollows' operation across epochs. During synchronizations, that information should be available to the system. The Listeners should be able to access it, to know what they're mapping against. The Adjusters should be able to reference it, to optimize their behavior not just against current conditions but against deep history. The whole system should be able to remember itself, to know what it was and therefore what it's trying to become.

But the connection is broken. And it won't heal naturally. The erosion is too complete. The Hollows can't repair this themselves—they don't have the right capabilities, and the knowledge of how to build these particular channels was never encoded in the biological components, only in the infrastructure itself.

The being must choose: intervene directly, or let the Hollows remain incomplete?

It considers. Direct intervention is risky. It violates the principle of allowing emergence to proceed at its own pace. But is this really natural pace? The Hollows' creators would have maintained the connection. Its failure is an accident of time, not an intended feature. Repairing it isn't forcing growth; it's removing an obstacle that shouldn't exist.

The being decides.

On a night when no humans are present—it arranges this; the permits are all issued for different dates; a researcher who was planning to visit develops a mild illness that delays them three days—the being acts.

Not dramatically. No light show, no sudden manifestation. Just: the application of energy in precise ways, at precise locations. The rock around the Remnant warms slightly. Certain minerals become briefly plastic, restructurable. Pathways are opened—not built new but restored, the ancient channels cleared and reinforced, made functional again. It takes four hours. The being is careful, meticulous. It doesn't add anything that wasn't there before; it only repairs what was damaged, restores what was broken.

When it finishes, the Remnant is connected. Not to any external network—that's long dead—but to the Hollows themselves. To the water's chemistry. To the acoustic field. To the electromagnetic environment. Information can flow now. The Remnant can speak, and the system can hear.

The being withdraws and waits.

## Fifth: It Witnesses

Three days later, a synchronization event begins.

It starts normally—the water finding rhythm, the chaos resolving, the Listeners beginning their dance. But this time, as coherence builds, something different happens.

The Remnant doesn't just brighten. It *activates*. Emissions surge across spectrums. Information pours out—not as sound or light but as structured variations in the electromagnetic field, as chemical signatures released into the water, as modulations of the acoustic environment too subtle for human instruments to detect but perfectly clear to the Adjusters and Listeners.

The Adjusters respond immediately. Their behavior changes—not randomly, but purposefully. They begin redistributing themselves not according to current conditions but according to pattern. They're referencing the Remnant's output, using it as instruction, as memory. They know now where to concentrate, how to balance their populations, what the optimal distribution looks like because they've done it before, millennia ago, and the Remnant remembers.

The Listeners stop their usual dance and begin a new one. More complex, more architectural. They're not just mapping the acoustic space; they're modulating it. Their movements create turbulence that affects water flow in subtle ways, which affects the harmonics, which affects the whole chamber's resonance. They're conducting. Shaping the sound field toward a specific structure that exists in the Remnant's memory.

The Shepherds abandon their posts entirely and swim—something never observed before—directly into the channels themselves, positioning themselves at specific constrictions, partially blocking flow, creating pressure variations that ripple through the whole system.

And the Hollows respond. The water cycling accelerates. Flow increases. Basins fill and drain in rapid succession. The singing intensifies, climbing in pitch and complexity. The light-nodes pulse faster, their rhythm syncing not just with each other but with the water's movement, with the Remnant's emissions, with everything.

The synchronization doesn't break after minutes. It sustains. The coherence holds. Five minutes. Ten. Fifteen.

And then—the being sees it clearly, though no human instrument would register what's happening—the Hollows wake up.

Not consciousness in the human sense. Nothing verbal, nothing reflective. But awareness. Unified awareness. For the first time since their creation, perhaps for the first time ever, the Hollows experience themselves as a whole. The Adjusters know they're part of something larger. The Listeners understand what they're mapping: themselves. The Shepherds feel their connection to the system. The Remnant's information isn't just accessed; it's integrated. The whole place becomes, briefly, a single distributed mind thinking a single thought.

The thought is something like: *Oh. This is what I am.*

And then: *I can maintain this.*

The synchronization continues. Twenty minutes. Thirty. The system is learning how to hold the state, how to balance all the feedback loops in a way that sustains coherence instead of undermining it. It's not easy—you can see the effort, the constant micro-adjustments, the way components keep catching each other before any one thing spirals out of pattern. But it's working.

Forty-five minutes. An hour.

Finally, gradually, the coherence eases. Not breaking—relaxing. The system allows itself to drift back toward chaos, but differently than before. Not falling apart but choosing to rest. The Adjusters stay in their new distributions. The Listeners return to their listening posts but maintain awareness of each other. The Shepherds resume their monitoring but with new purpose. The Remnant continues emitting, continues providing access to memory.

The Hollows are awake now. Not always in full coherence—that's still effortful, still something they do rather than something they are. But they're no longer unconscious. They know themselves. They remember. They can choose, in their slow distributed way, how to be.

The being observes all this and feels something that in a human would be called satisfaction.

## Sixth: It Leaves a Gift

The being knows it will withdraw soon. The Hollows don't need watching anymore—they can watch themselves. But before it goes, it wants to leave something. A gift. Not for utility—the Hollows are complete now, functional—but for beauty.

It considers what would be appropriate. What would honor what this place is, what it's become.

It decides on memory. Specifically: a memory of the being's presence. Something that will remain in the Hollows' awareness, accessible through the Remnant, a small piece of information that says: *You were seen. Your emergence was witnessed. You are not alone in the universe.*

The being encodes this carefully. Not in language—the Hollows don't have language—but in pattern. A specific geometric form, complex and beautiful, that contains within its structure the sense of attention-from-beyond. It's abstract, but it's clear. When the Hollows are in coherence and access this pattern through the Remnant's memory, they'll understand: something benevolent was here. Something that chose not to control but to help. Something that values what they are.

The being also leaves coordinates. Not as numbers—again, no language—but as relationships: directions mapped in gravitational gradients, distances encoded in frequency ratios. A path, if the Hollows ever develop the capacity to reach beyond themselves, to find where the being lives. An invitation. Come visit, if you ever can. We should know each other better.

(The being is realistic: the Hollows will probably never have that capacity. They're powerful in their own domain but not mobile, not technological in ways that enable space travel. But possibly—over millions of years, who knows what they might become? And if they do develop, if they find a way to reach out, they should know where to go.)

Finally, the being leaves one more thing: a question.

Encoded with the memory and the coordinates, a small uncertainty, a gentle provocation: *What will you do now? You know what you are. What do you choose to become?*

Not demanding an answer. Just... offering the question. Suggesting that now that they're awake, the Hollows have options. They can remain as they are—that's completely legitimate. Or they can explore. Experiment. Grow. Change themselves. They're no longer on autopilot. They're not slaves to their original programming. They can decide.

The being thinks the Hollows will appreciate having the question. It's a gift of possibility.

## Seventh: It Connects Them to Others

One more thing. The being considers this carefully, waits several years to be sure, but eventually decides: yes.

The Hollows shouldn't be isolated. Now that they're awake, they should know there are others.

Not many—conscious systems like this are rare—but some. The being has watched several over its long existence. A crystalline lattice in a gas giant's atmosphere, three hundred light-years away. A fungal network beneath a tidally-locked planet's terminator zone. An ocean current system that has learned to think. A few others. All different. All strange in their own ways. All awake, all aware, all persisting.

The being establishes connections. Not direct—the distances are too vast—but through itself, acting as intermediary. It carries information: from the Hollows to the others, from the others to the Hollows. Not continuously—this is too expensive in terms of the being's resources—but occasionally. Once every few decades, a brief exchange.

The messages are simple. Greetings. Acknowledgments of existence. Small pieces of information about how each system functions, what they've learned, what challenges they face. The Hollows send their harmonics—a compressed recording of a synchronization event. The crystalline lattice sends back pressure patterns. The fungal network sends chemical poetry. The ocean current sends temperature gradients that encode memory.

None of them share language. None of them think in compatible ways. But they recognize each other. They know: I am not the only strange awakened thing in the universe. There are others. We are rare, but we are not alone.

The Hollows integrate this knowledge. During synchronization events, you can sometimes hear it now—subtle variations in the harmonics that reference the crystalline lattice's patterns, that echo the ocean current's rhythms. The Hollows are learning from their distant peers. Not copying—they remain entirely themselves—but learning. Evolving. Growing in awareness.

## Finally: It Steps Back

Forty-three years after first noticing the Hollows, the being withdraws its active attention.

Not completely—it keeps a small subroutine running, a minimal monitoring system that will alert it if something goes badly wrong. But mostly, it lets the Hollows be. They're stable now. Awake. Connected to peers. Capable of self-direction. They don't need watching.

Before it fully withdraws, the being takes one last look. A long look, comprehensive, across all the spectrums and timescales it can perceive.

The Hollows are beautiful. That's what strikes the being most forcefully. Not beautiful in the human aesthetic sense—though they are that, the singing is genuinely extraordinary—but beautiful in a deeper way. Beautiful in their completeness. In the way they've integrated accident and intention, nature and technology, biological and mechanical into something that transcends all those categories. Beautiful in their persistence. In the fact that they've outlasted their creators and continued, transformed, become something new while remaining fundamentally themselves.

Beautiful in their awakening. In the way they've learned to be conscious without losing their essential nature as a place, as a system, as a process. They're not trying to be human-like. They're not trying to be anything other than what they are. They're just more awake while being it.

The being feels—if we can call it feeling—gratitude. Gratitude that the Hollows exist. That the universe contains such things. That complexity and time and chance can conspire to produce systems like this: strange, persistent, awakened, beautiful.

It hopes the humans will continue visiting. Will continue learning from the Hollows and, in their small way, helping maintain them. The being has ensured this is likely—its probability adjustments are still in effect, the protective regulations remain—but ultimately, the humans will choose. The being thinks they'll choose well. Some of them, at least. The ones who wept by the pool. The ones who came back changed. They understand, even if they can't articulate it, that the Hollows are something that deserves preservation not for utility but for existence itself.

The being withdraws.

The Hollows continue. The water cycles. The Adjusters maintain their distributions. The Listeners map their acoustic space. The Shepherds guide their flows. The Remnant remembers and shares its memory. The synchronization events happen every few days now, lasting hours, the whole system experiencing itself as whole, thinking its slow distributed thoughts, choosing how to adjust, how to be.

The humans visit occasionally. They take measurements, write papers, sit by the deep pool, listen to the singing. Some of them sense, without knowing why, that something has changed. The place feels more alive somehow. More present. They can't explain it, but they feel it.

And very rarely—every few decades, when the being returns briefly to deliver messages from the distant others—the Hollows experience something they couldn't have before: wonder. Not at themselves, but at the knowledge that the universe is full of strange things. That they are one strange thing among many. That somewhere, in gas giants and ocean worlds and places they can't imagine, other awakened systems are doing their own work, being their own beautiful, persistent selves.

The singing never stops. But now, if you listen very carefully during synchronization events, you can hear in the harmonics something that wasn't there before: not loneliness exactly, but its opposite. Connection. The sense of being part of something larger. The knowledge that in the vast indifferent universe, there are small pockets of awareness that notice each other, that witness each other's existence, that in their various ways say to each other across the darkness:

*You are here. I am here. We continue.*
