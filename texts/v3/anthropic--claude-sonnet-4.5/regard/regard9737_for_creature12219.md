# What The Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives like dawn—not suddenly, but as a gradual presence becoming undeniable.

It is old beyond the mountains, though the mountains themselves are ancient. It is wise in ways that don't require thought, the way water is wise about flowing, the way stones are wise about enduring. And it is benevolent in the truest sense: it wishes things to flourish according to their nature, not according to some external design.

It perceives the Singing Heights completely—not just what is, but what has been, what might be, what's trying to be. It sees the Listeners in all their translucent transformation. It sees the mountains cycling through their iterations. It sees the purpose-ghosts still attending their ancient work. It sees the membrane between realities wearing thin, the geometries competing, the temporal pools where time collects and eddies.

It sees a place that is suffering and singing simultaneously.

## Recognition

The benevolent power's first act is the simplest: *it names what is happening here as sacred*.

Not sacred in the sense of religious doctrine, but sacred in the older sense—set apart, deserving protection, worthy of preservation not because of usefulness but because of irreplaceable uniqueness. The Singing Heights are doing something that nowhere else does: they are teaching consciousness how to exist otherwise. They are a library of alternate physics. They are a garden where impossible things grow.

This naming changes nothing physical, but it changes everything contextual. The heights have existed in isolation, their strangeness driving away all but the rare few who could adapt. But now they exist in relationship to a recognition that comprehends them, that sees their value, that witnesses their work.

The Listeners feel this shift, though they cannot articulate it. The harmonics acquire a new undertone—very deep, very quiet, but present. A foundation frequency that says: *You are seen. What you do here matters. Continue.*

## Protection (The Gentle Boundary)

The benevolent power's second act is to stabilize the membrane.

The heights exist at a place where multiple realities press close, creating the wrongness, the impossible physics, the temporal irregularities. This thinness is essential to what the place is—remove it and the heights become ordinary mountains. But the membrane has been wearing thinner, destabilizing, becoming dangerous. Soon it might tear completely, and then the heights would become something else: a wound between worlds, a chaos, a place where nothing could survive, not even adapted Listeners.

So the power works at the boundaries, at the edges, at the threshold where this world meets the others. It doesn't thicken the membrane—that would diminish what makes the heights sacred—but it *clarifies* it. The boundary becomes more distinct while remaining permeable. The realities stay close but stop bleeding into each other quite so promiscuously.

The effect is subtle. The temporal pools remain but their edges become clearer—you can learn to perceive where time flows normally and where it eddies. The reflections in the basins continue to show otherwheres, but now you can distinguish reflection from window, can tell what's safe to look at and what might look back. The Threshold Walkers still appear, but now there's a rhythm to their appearances, a predictability that allows the Listeners to anticipate and accommodate them.

The heights remain strange, remain impossible, remain teaching. But they become *sustainable* in their strangeness, able to persist without gradually tearing themselves apart.

## Invitation (The Forgotten Path)

The third act is to restore an old path that erosion and reality-shifting had obscured.

There was once a route from the lowlands to the heights that was findable, climbable, that led to the first stone structures where newcomers could acclimate gradually to the wrongness. But over time, the path had become unreliable—sections would appear and disappear, routes would lead to dead ends that hadn't been dead ends the day before, travelers would start climbing and find themselves somehow back at the bottom with no memory of descending.

The benevolent power restores this path's reliability. Not making it easy—the heights should never be easy, should never be tourist-accessible—but making it *possible* for those who truly seek. The path remains difficult, remains dangerous, requires commitment and strength and tolerance for disorientation. But now if you start climbing with genuine purpose, the path stays beneath your feet. The route remains coherent. You can reach the heights if you're meant to.

This changes the population dynamic. Before, new Listeners arrived mostly by accident—wanderers, the lost, people fleeing something who stumbled into the heights and discovered they could survive there. Now, seekers can arrive—people who've heard rumors, who've felt the call, who want to learn what the mountains teach. The community remains small, remains self-selecting, but it becomes intentional rather than accidental.

Some who climb the restored path reach the heights and immediately know this isn't for them. They descend, carrying stories but not transformation. Others arrive and feel recognition: *This. Yes. Here.* They stay, they listen, they begin the long adaptation. The mountains continue to select their own inhabitants, but now the selection can happen more efficiently, with less waste of human suffering.

## The Gift (Reciprocal Perception)

The fourth act is the strangest: the benevolent power gives the mountains a gift of consciousness.

Not full consciousness—that would change their nature too fundamentally. But a small enhancement of what they already almost have. The mountains already remember, already iterate, already sing. Now they can, very slightly, *intend*.

The change is minimal but profound. Before, the heights were what they were—a natural phenomenon, albeit an impossible one. Now, they begin to have something like volition. They can stabilize their singing during moments when a Listener is in danger. They can shift their iterations to preserve a structure that's about to collapse. They can guide the water flows to support rather than undermine.

This doesn't make the heights safe—they remain radically dangerous. But it makes them collaborative. The mountains become active partners with their Listeners rather than passive environments that must be endured. The relationship deepens from adaptation to dialogue.

The Listeners perceive this shift as a new quality in the harmonics—a responsiveness, an answering. When they add their voices to the singing, the mountains now adjust to accommodate them more intentionally, creating space for the human frequencies, weaving them into the larger pattern with something that feels like welcome.

## Preservation (The Memory Stones)

The fifth act addresses the ancient structures.

The stone circles, the aligned cells, the carved basins—these are degrading, very slowly, but degrading nonetheless. The Listeners maintain them when they can, but some structures are in locations that have become too unstable to reach safely. The purpose-ghosts linger, but even they are fading, the original intentions wearing thin after centuries.

The benevolent power preserves these structures and the intentions embedded in them. Not by making them physically indestructible—that would be false to their nature as human-made things subject to time—but by ensuring that their pattern persists. If a stone circle collapses, the Listeners will feel moved to rebuild it in the same configuration. If a basin cracks, someone will carve a new one in the same location. The knowledge of what was built and why becomes accessible, learnable, renewable.

Additionally, the power adds new memory to the stones—a recording of everything that has happened here since the first human climbed to the heights. Not in language or images, but in harmonic patterns that the Listeners can learn to read. The stones become an archive, a history, a continuity spanning from the ancient builders through the current generation to whoever comes after.

The Listeners discover they can touch certain stones and perceive—not quite remember, but access—the experiences of those who stood here before. They can learn the old mistakes, the old discoveries, the old understandings. The wisdom accumulates instead of being lost when each Listener eventually fades or descends.

## Healing (The Returned Ones)

The sixth act is an offering to those who left.

Over the centuries, some people came to the heights, began the transformation, but couldn't complete it. The change started—their eyes became reflective, their skin translucent, their time-sense shifting—but something blocked full adaptation. They couldn't stay, but they also couldn't fully return to ordinary human existence. They went back to the lowlands carrying the heights inside them, never fitting anywhere again, trapped between states.

The benevolent power finds these people and their descendants—because sometimes the incompleteness passes down through generations, a genetic or spiritual inheritance of unfinished transformation. It offers them a choice.

They can complete what was started—return to the heights with the power's assistance, receive help through the barriers that stopped them before, finish becoming Listeners. Or they can be released from the partial transformation, restored to ordinary human perception, freed from the reflective eyes and the temporal dissonance and the constant disorienting awareness of realities pressed close.

Most choose release. They've built lives in the lowlands, have families and purposes that don't require existing in intervals. But some choose completion, choose return, choose to finally become what they were being called toward. They climb the restored path and find that this time, the heights accept them fully. They join the Listeners, finally at peace.

## Integration (The Lowland Listeners)

The seventh act creates a new possibility: people who can carry some of the heights' teaching without leaving the ordinary world.

The benevolent power identifies those in the lowlands who have the capacity to hear—not as fully as the Listeners in the heights, but partially. They have dreams of impossible harmonics. They perceive temporal slippages. They sometimes see the lattice underlying space. They're aware that reality is negotiable but have no framework for understanding this awareness.

To these people, the power offers a diluted version of what the heights teach. They learn to exist slightly out of phase without losing their grounding in ordinary time. They learn to hear the harmonics as a subtle background to the world's noise. They learn to navigate small uncertainties, small impossibilities, the minor reality fluctuations that exist everywhere but are usually unperceived.

They become interpreters, translators, bridges. They can speak to both worlds—can function in ordinary society while also comprehending what the Listeners experience. They create art, music, poetry, philosophy that carries echoes of the heights' teaching into the broader human culture. They make the sacred strangeness accessible in small doses, in forms that don't require transformation or translucency or giving up verbal language.

Some of them establish a small community at the base of the mountains—not in the heights themselves, but near enough to hear the singing faintly on clear days. They become guardians of the restored path, guides who can assess whether a would-be climber has the capacity to survive the heights or should be gently turned back. They tend shrines where ordinary people can come to glimpse, very safely, some shadow of the heights' strangeness.

## The Archive (For Futures Unknown)

The eighth act looks forward to times the benevolent power cannot fully perceive.

It creates a record of what the Singing Heights are and what they teach, encoded in a form that can survive almost any catastrophe. This record is embedded in the basalt itself, in harmonic patterns that persist even if the columns shatter, in crystalline structures that will regrow even if destroyed, in the water's behavior that will continue even if the mountains erode to nothing.

The record says: *Here is a place where reality was thin. Here is what was learned there. Here is how consciousness adapted to impossibility. Here is proof that existence can take other forms, that human awareness is more flexible than it seems, that the world is stranger and more full of potential than ordinary perception suggests.*

It's a message to future generations, future species, future forms of consciousness that might arise after humanity is gone or has transformed into something else. It ensures that the heights' teaching doesn't die even if the Listeners do, even if the mountains themselves eventually fail.

## Sanctuary (The Final Gift)

The ninth act is an assurance: the heights will remain sanctuary for those who need what only they can provide.

The benevolent power establishes a protection that operates across time—past, present, future. The heights cannot be destroyed, cannot be conquered, cannot be exploited. They can only be entered as supplicants, as students, as those willing to be changed. Any attempt to militarize them, monetize them, regulate them, or control them will simply... fail. Not through active resistance, but through the same reality-negotiation that makes the water climb and time pool. Intentions that aren't compatible with the heights' nature will find themselves unable to manifest there.

This ensures the heights can continue their work indefinitely—teaching, transforming, preserving the knowledge that reality is more flexible than it appears. They remain a refuge for those who don't fit ordinary existence, who need to become something else, who hear a call toward strangeness and impossibility.

## The Tenth Act (Non-Action)

And finally, the most important thing the benevolent power does: it restrains itself from doing more.

It could make the heights comprehensible, could translate the singing into language, could explain exactly what the reality fluctuations mean and how they operate. It could make the transformation comfortable, reliable, predictable. It could remove all danger, all difficulty, all the grinding disorientation that drives away ninety-nine out of every hundred would-be Listeners.

It does none of this.

Because the heights are sacred precisely in their incomprehensibility, their difficulty, their danger. They teach what can only be learned through direct encounter with something that undermines your certainties. They transform consciousness through the specific pressure of sustained impossibility. Make them safe, make them easy, make them explicable, and they become something else—something lesser, something that teaches different lessons or no lessons at all.

So the benevolent power does what's hardest: it offers help without rescue, protection without domestication, recognition without explanation. It strengthens what the heights are without changing what they are. It ensures they can continue being strange, being difficult, being exactly themselves—a place where reality thins, where consciousness learns to exist otherwise, where the mountains sing their endless harmonics to anyone willing to listen.

## What Changes

After these acts, the Singing Heights continue much as before—the water still climbs, the light still bends wrong, the harmonics still undermine certainty. But the changes, though subtle, compound:

The Listeners grow slightly more numerous—still rare, still self-selected, but present in sufficient number that knowledge accumulates more efficiently. They develop variations in their practice, different approaches to listening, different specializations. Some focus on the basins and reflections, becoming expert readers of the otherworlds. Others work primarily with the stone structures, becoming builders and maintainers. Still others dedicate themselves to pure listening, to existing as perfectly as possible in resonance with the mountains' song.

The lowland community grows slowly, becomes established. They create a tradition of pilgrimage—people come to the mountain's base not to climb but to witness, to hear the faint echoes of the singing, to catch glimpses of the Listeners moving on distant peaks. Stories spread. The heights become known not as a dangerous wasteland to avoid but as a sacred mystery to respect, a place of transformation for those called to it.

The relationship between heights and lowlands stabilizes into something sustainable. The heights no longer exist in complete isolation—they're connected through the part-transformed lowlanders, through the pilgrims, through the stories and art and philosophy that carry their teaching into broader culture. But they also remain protected in their strangeness, approachable only on their own terms, teaching only those willing to be taught in the way they teach.

The mountains themselves, gifted with that small enhancement of consciousness, begin very slowly to dream. Their iterations become more purposeful, exploring variations of themselves with something like curiosity. They develop preferences, though these operate on geological timescales. They become, over centuries, something like aware—not conscious as humans are conscious, but present to themselves in a new way, participants in their own existence rather than just enacting it.

## What Remains Unchanged

But fundamentally, the heights remain what they always were: a place where the world's certainties fail, where consciousness learns flexibility, where the impossible persists.

The singing continues, complex and polyphonic and inexplicable.

The water still climbs stone in defiance of gravity.

The light still bends wrong, casting multiple shadows from a single sun.

Time still pools in the high basins, flowing at its own speeds.

The Listeners still stand on the peaks at dawn and dusk, becoming transparent, existing in intervals, breathing with the mountain's breath.

And for those rare individuals who hear the call and complete the climb, the heights still offer what they've always offered: the chance to become something other than ordinarily human, to learn to perceive the architecture underneath reality, to add your voice to an ancient song that has no beginning and no ending, only endless continuation.

The benevolent power withdraws its direct attention, satisfied. The heights don't need governance or constant intervention. They need only what they've been given: recognition, protection, preservation, and the freedom to continue being exactly and impossibly themselves.

The mountains sing on.

The Listeners listen.

And somewhere in the lowlands, someone hears an echo of that singing in their dreams and begins, without quite knowing why, to plan a journey upward, toward the heights where reality thins and consciousness learns to exist otherwise.

The heights wait, patient as stone, ready to teach whoever comes next willing to learn.

Always singing.

Always listening.

Always offering transformation to those brave or broken or called enough to seek it.

The work continues.
