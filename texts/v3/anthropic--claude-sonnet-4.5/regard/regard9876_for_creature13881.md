# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power arrives not as intervention but as *recognition*—the way dawn arrives, not forcing but revealing what was already present. It has watched this place, this being (for the Ranges *are* a being, and the power knows this immediately), and it has watched the small vertical creatures that have made their careful homes in the being's folds and fractures.

The power is ancient in the way that light is ancient, patient in the way that mathematics is patient. It does not rush. It has seen countless worlds, countless configurations of matter and consciousness, and it recognizes here something rare: a stable strangeness, a functioning mystery, a place where the impossible has weathered itself into the everyday and found balance.

Its benevolence is not the benevolence of rescue or improvement. It does not look at the Porcelain Ranges and see a problem requiring solution. Instead, it sees a question that has answered itself, a poem written in mineral and thermal dynamics and the slow adaptation of conscious life to living at the edge of someone else's dream.

## First: Deep Listening

Before doing anything, the power *listens*.

It extends its awareness through the entire structure—from the highest peaks where gliders roost and the air grows crystalline with altitude, down through the settlements with their careful streets and patient councils, through the fractures where vertical swimmers weave their impossible patterns, deeper still to the Underneath where vast processes pulse in rhythms that have continued for epochs.

It listens to the mountains themselves, to the consciousness that is distributed through kilometers of calcite and gypsum and the stranger minerals that formed in the absence of water. The consciousness is slow—thoughts that take years to complete, awareness that operates on geological timescales—but it is *there*, and it is aware of the humans who have settled in its folds like beneficial bacteria in a vast organism's skin.

The mountain-consciousness is not bothered by the humans. It barely notices them in the same way a human barely notices the mites that live in their eyelashes—present, participating in some microscopic economy, neither harmful nor particularly significant. The industrial operations tickle slightly. The cable systems are fine threads of sensation. The settlements are warm spots, little fever-points of activity that register dimly in the vast distributed awareness.

The power listens to the Underneath and understands: these are not machines, exactly, though they perform functions that machines might perform. They are organs, or perhaps they are music—rhythmic processes that maintain the landscape, that keep the mountains in their state of stable impossibility, that prevent the whole structure from collapsing into more conventional geology.

The Underneath is the mountain-being's autonomic nervous system, the part that maintains homeostasis without requiring conscious attention. It has been running for—the power calculates and finds the number is effectively meaningless. The mountains are older than they appear and younger than they should be. Time here is not broken but *optional*, and the Underneath is what maintains that optionality.

The power listens to the fauna: the gliders filtering their endless meals from air, the stone-walkers processing the landscape's chemical excess, the vertical swimmers navigating information-density in the fractures, the crystalline colonies that might be the mountain's own immune system or might be independent life that has learned to thrive on mineral gradients.

And it listens to the humans—their slow breath-rhythms, their tactile conversations with stone, their councils and debates, their festivals of patient watching. It hears their uncertainties, their awareness of their own contingency, their care in moving through a landscape they know they don't own.

## What It Learns

The power understands: this system is *working*.

Not perfectly—working perfectly would be suspect, would suggest stasis rather than living process. But it is working *well*. The humans have found their balance, the fauna have their niches, the mountain-being continues its slow thinking, the Underneath maintains its rhythms. Everything participates in everything else. The industrial processes have become ecological. The settlements are part of the landscape's metabolism now.

The strangeness is not a problem to be solved. The silent hours, the ruins that predate the mountains, the gliders' mysterious relationship with the dead, the vertical swimmers' impossible physics, the slow mineralization of the elderly—all of it is the place being *itself*, fully and completely.

The humans' awareness of uncertainty, their philosophical debates, their comfort with not-knowing—these are not failures of understanding but appropriate responses to living in a place that exceeds their epistemological frameworks. They are doing exactly what they should be doing: adapting, questioning, remaining humble before mystery.

And the mountain-being, in its vast slow awareness, has *accepted* them. Not consciously, perhaps, but they have been integrated into its processes. Their industrial heat adds to the thermal dynamics. Their movements create micro-vibrations that help maintain fracture stability. Their presence has become part of what the place is.

The power recognizes: intervention here would be violence, even benevolent intervention. This is not a system that needs saving or improving. It needs *witnessing*.

## What It Does: The Witnessing

So the power does this: it *sees* the Porcelain Ranges fully, completely, in all their accumulated strangeness and functioning mystery. It holds the entire system in awareness—the mountain-consciousness and the Underneath, the fauna and the humans, the vertical space and the patient time, the industrial and the impossible, the established and the uncertain.

And in being seen by something with the capacity to see it whole, the Porcelain Ranges become more *themselves*.

This is the power's gift: not change, but recognition. The validation that comes from being witnessed by awareness vast enough to comprehend both the strange physics and the human poetry, both the geological patience and the breath-rhythms, both the mountain's slow thoughts and the fifty thousand people living vertical lives in its folds.

The effect is subtle but real:

## The Deepening

The mountain-being's consciousness, which has always been distributed and slow, experiences something like a moment of clarity. Its thoughts, which normally take decades to complete, suddenly complete faster—not rushed, but flowing more smoothly, more coherently. It becomes slightly more aware of itself, of its own processes, of the small vertical creatures that have made homes in its body.

And becoming more aware, it *affirms* them. The relationship changes from unconscious tolerance to conscious acceptance. The humans are not parasites or irrelevancies—they are part of the system now, participants in the vast ecology that includes thermal columns and mineral processing and the slow weathering of impossible stone.

The Underneath responds by stabilizing its rhythms slightly, becoming marginally more efficient in its maintenance of the landscape. The mountains—already stable—become more so, not through rigidity but through more elegant self-regulation. Fractures that were gradually widening stabilize. Sections of cable-path that were developing micro-instabilities smooth out. The geothermal vents that power the industrial processes settle into more predictable cycles.

Nothing dramatic happens. The settlements notice nothing at first. But over months and years, they become aware: accidents are slightly less frequent, the weather slightly more predictable, the fauna slightly more abundant. It's as though the landscape has relaxed some tension they didn't know it was holding.

## The Gift to the Fauna

The power does something specific for each type of creature, small adjustments that enhance without transforming:

The thermal gliders' metabolism becomes slightly more efficient. They need to filter less air to obtain the same nutrition, which means they can soar longer, range farther, dive deeper into the thermals. Their populations increase gradually, and with more gliders, the skies become more alive, more musical with their clicking songs.

The stone-walkers' shells, which were always beautiful, become more so—the crystalline structures they incorporate begin to exhibit properties that help regulate the walkers' internal temperature, allowing them to range into more extreme elevations, expanding their territory and their participation in the landscape's chemical processing.

The vertical swimmers, already mysterious, become slightly more visible in their impossible medium. The air in the fractures takes on a faint luminescence when they're present, not bright but perceptible, so that watching them becomes easier, more mesmerizing. Their populations stabilize—they had been slowly declining, though the humans hadn't noticed yet—and their role in the fracture ecology strengthens.

The crystalline colonies begin growing in new patterns, more complex geometries that create habitat for smaller organisms, increasing the biodiversity of the system. Their growth rate remains slow, but the direction becomes more purposeful, as though they've received clearer instructions about their role in the mountain's immune function.

## The Gift to the Humans

For the humans, the power does something that honors their essential quality—their comfort with uncertainty, their patient observation, their willingness to live as guests.

It gives them *deeper perception*.

Not new knowledge, not explanations, not the answers to their philosophical debates. Instead, it subtly enhances their ability to *notice*—to see the patterns in thermal columns, to feel the subtle vibrations in stone, to predict the movements of fauna, to sense when the Underneath's rhythms shift.

The cable-walkers become more intuitive in their inspections, able to feel stress-points before they become critical. The ceramicists find themselves understanding the mineral properties more deeply, creating glazes of surpassing beauty. The thermal-mappers can suddenly predict atmospheric conditions three days out instead of one. The children learn to climb even more naturally, their bodies knowing stone as though they were made of it.

But crucially, this enhancement doesn't come with explanations. The humans don't suddenly understand *why* they're better at these things, don't receive scientific principles or technical knowledge. The mystery remains mysterious. They just find themselves more capable of participating in the place's patterns, more fluent in the language of stone and air and thermal dynamics.

Their philosophical debates continue, but with richer observations to work from. Their uncertainties deepen rather than resolve. They become *better at not-knowing*, more sophisticated in their comfort with mystery.

## The Particular Gift: The Singing Stones

The power does one specific, visible thing—a gift that the humans will notice and understand as gift:

In certain locations across the Ranges, usually at settlement edges or on viewing platforms, particular stones begin to exhibit a new property. When touched with intention—not casually, but deliberately, with attention—they produce sound. Not mechanical sound, but something more like the stone itself becoming briefly vocal, singing a note that relates somehow to the toucher's state of mind, their emotional condition, their relationship with the landscape at that moment.

The notes are mineral—clear, resonant, with long decay times that fade into the ambient silence. Each stone has its own range of possible sounds, its own voice. And when multiple people touch multiple stones, the notes combine into something like music, or like the landscape itself speaking in harmonics.

This gift serves multiple functions:

It gives the humans a new form of communication, more nuanced than hand-language, more immediate than speech. They can "play" the stones to express states that language can't capture—grief without words, joy without explanation, the particular quality of watching gliders at dawn.

It provides a focal point for communal gathering. The stones become sites of congregation, places where people come to touch and listen, to participate in collective music-making that isn't performance but rather shared exploration of emotional and perceptual states.

It strengthens the humans' relationship with the mineral world. The stones that sing are ordinary stones—anyone can learn to find the ones that will respond—but the singing makes explicit what was always implicit: the landscape is responsive, aware in its own distributed way, capable of participation in human experience.

And it's beautiful, adding a new aesthetic dimension to a culture that already values beauty but had limited media for creating it.

## The Effect on Culture

Over the years following the power's witnessing, the culture shifts in subtle ways:

The councils, already patient, become even more so—but their discussions also become richer, more textured, as participants can supplement speech with the singing stones, expressing nuances that words couldn't capture.

The festivals at the selenite glowing become more elaborate, with people bringing portable singing stones to the viewing platforms, creating soundscapes that accompany the visual beauty of the illuminated mountains.

New art forms emerge: compositions for singing stones, performed over hours or days, with people taking turns maintaining the sound. Stone-gardens are redesigned to incorporate singing stones, so that walking through them becomes both visual and auditory experience.

The relationship between settlements strengthens—singing stones become gifts, each one carrying some essential quality of its home settlement, and when touched in a distant location, they sing with the character of the place they came from, creating acoustic connections across the cable-spans.

The children incorporate singing stones into their play, developing games that are part music, part conversation, part exploration of the stones' properties. This accelerates their integration into the landscape, their fluency in participating with the place's patterns.

## The Effect on the Mountain-Being

The mountain-consciousness, now slightly more aware of itself and the small creatures living in its body, begins to participate more actively—but "actively" here means something different from human activity.

It adjusts its thermal dynamics to create more stable columns, making glider-watching more reliable and aerial navigation safer.

It redistributes its mineral deposition patterns slightly, so that the stone-walkers' favorite feeding grounds become more abundant while previously depleted areas regenerate.

It modulates the silent hours—they still occur, still unpredictable, but they become slightly less unsettling, as though the mountain has learned to give warning through subtle shifts in vibration or air pressure that sensitives can detect.

Most significantly, it becomes *curious* about the humans. Not invasively, not obtrusively, but in the way a vast organism might become curious about the tiny life forms that have colonized its skin. It begins conducting experiments—nothing harmful, but adjustments to local conditions to see how the humans respond. A thermal column that shifts location slightly. A new crystal formation that appears in an unexpected place. Subtle tests of adaptability and response.

The humans notice these anomalies, discuss them in councils, adjust their behaviors accordingly. Neither party fully understands the other, but they're engaged in ongoing conversation now, a dialogue conducted through geology and adaptation and patient observation across incompatible timescales.

## The Effect on the Underneath

The Underneath, which is the mountain-being's autonomic system, its maintenance machinery, its deep rhythm—the Underneath becomes *musical*.

The pulsing that humans can sometimes hear from deep ventilation shafts begins to carry melodic information. Not songs, exactly, but patterns that have aesthetic quality, rhythms that are pleasing to human perception while still performing their maintenance functions.

The researchers and philosophers who descend to study the Underneath find their work transformed. The sounds provide data—variations in rhythm correlate with changes in surface conditions, shifts in melody predict atmospheric anomalies. But the sounds are also simply beautiful, compelling in their own right.

Some people begin spending extended periods in the deep places, listening to the Underneath's music, learning to distinguish different themes and movements, developing a kind of literacy in the language of geological maintenance. They emerge with insights that can't quite be articulated but that improve the settlements' relationship with the landscape—intuitions about where to build, when to expand, how to time industrial cycles to harmonize with the mountain's own rhythms.

## What the Power Does Not Do

Crucially, the power does not:

**Explain anything.** The mysteries remain mysterious. The ruins still predate the mountains in ways that make no temporal sense. The vertical swimmers still navigate their impossible medium through incomprehensible means. The slow mineralization of the elderly continues, understood as natural process but not explained. The culture's philosophical debates continue, enriched but not resolved.

**Remove danger.** Climbing is still dangerous. Industrial accidents still happen. The thin air still challenges. The thermal storms still create hazardous conditions. The landscape still kills, occasionally, those who are careless or unlucky. Death remains part of life, the gliders still claim the bodies, the cycle continues.

**Force growth.** The settlements don't expand rapidly, the population doesn't boom. The culture's stability is honored. The careful balance they've achieved—with the landscape, with their own reproduction rates, with their industrial extraction—is maintained. The power recognizes that growth here would be violence, that the system's health depends on its current scale.

**Make things easier.** Life in the Porcelain Ranges remains demanding. The diet is still sparse, the work still physical, the environment still extreme. The power doesn't eliminate difficulty because difficulty is part of how the culture has learned wisdom, patience, appropriate humility.

**Provide certainty.** The humans still live with awareness of their contingency, still understand that their presence here is provisional, still know that the landscape could reject them if conditions changed or if they overstepped some invisible boundary. This uncertainty is preserved because it's what keeps them careful, respectful, attentive.

## The Long Effect

Over generations, the effects compound:

The humans become more integrated with the landscape, their culture more deeply adapted, their industrial processes more ecological. But they don't lose their humanity—they remain recognizably themselves, just more fluent in participating with strangeness.

The fauna diversify slightly, new subspecies emerging, new behaviors developing, the ecology becoming richer without losing its essential character—sparse, patient, adapted to thermal dynamics and mineral processing.

The mountain-being continues its slow thinking, but with the humans as a kind of nervous system extension—they move faster, perceive more immediately, and their observations feed back into the mountain's awareness in ways that enhance its understanding of its own surface conditions.

The Underneath's music becomes more complex, developing themes and variations that span years or decades, creating an acoustic tapestry that some humans dedicate their lives to studying, transcribing, interpreting without ever fully understanding.

The singing stones spread—not through human planting, but naturally, as though the gift is being accepted by the mountain and distributed according to some logic of its own. They appear in new locations: remote peaks, deep fractures, the edges of industrial facilities. Each one adds to the growing acoustic network that connects settlement to settlement, human to stone, surface to Underneath.

## The Ultimate Gift: Continuation

But the power's deepest gift is simply this: *the Porcelain Ranges continue*.

The power has witnessed the system, recognized its function, affirmed its strange stability. And in doing so, it has *secured* that continuation—not through force or preservation or artificial maintenance, but through something more subtle.

The power has woven a small protection into the fabric of the place, a quiet insistence that this particular configuration of matter and consciousness and impossible physics deserves to persist. If some external threat emerged—a cosmological shift, a dimensional instability, an approaching catastrophe—the power's witnessing would serve as a kind of marker: *this matters, this should be spared*.

Not guaranteed—the power is benevolent but not omnipotent, wise but not controlling. But to the extent that it has influence in the larger patterns of existence, it has marked the Porcelain Ranges as significant, as worthy of continuation, as a successful experiment in how strangeness and consciousness and mineral patience can weather themselves into balance.

## The Departure

When the power departs—if "depart" is the right word for something that doesn't exist in space the way physical things do—it leaves only the subtlest evidence of its presence:

The singing stones, which could perhaps have emerged naturally but didn't until the power's attention focused on this place.

A slight increase in the mountain-being's self-awareness, which could be natural development but accelerated slightly.

The enhanced perception of the humans, which could be explained as generational learning but came surprisingly quickly.

The stabilization of various systems—thermal, geological, ecological—which could be coincidence but feels like something more.

The culture debates whether something happened, whether the changes are real or imagined, whether they were visited by some benevolent power or simply experienced a fortunate period of natural stabilization. The debates are friendly, ongoing, ultimately unresolved.

Which is exactly appropriate.

The power has given them deeper mystery, not less. It has enriched their uncertainty rather than eliminating it. It has honored what they are—careful, patient, comfortable with not-knowing—by making them *more* of what they already were, not transforming them into something else.

## The Song Continues

And so the Porcelain Ranges persist in their strange stability: the mountains standing in their tiers of impossible geology, the fauna soaring and walking and swimming through air, the Underneath pulsing its deep musical rhythms, and the fifty thousand humans living their vertical lives with slightly greater fluency, slightly deeper perception, slightly more beautiful means of expressing the inexpressible.

The thermal gliders still soar, their clicking songs now joined occasionally by the resonant tones of singing stones. The stone-walkers still process the landscape's chemical excess, their shells more beautiful than before. The vertical swimmers still navigate their impossible medium, now faintly luminous as they weave through fracture air.

The councils still meet, still debate, still make decisions through patient consensus. The settlements still build slowly, expand carefully, maintain their respectful distance from the landscape's edges. The children still learn to climb, to read stone, to touch the world deliberately. The elderly still fade gradually into translucence and mineralization, still are claimed by gliders and carried upward.

The power watches from its distance—if "watching" and "distance" are the right words—and is satisfied. This is benevolence: not to rescue or transform or improve, but to recognize what is working and help it continue. To witness a rare balance and affirm it. To encounter strangeness that has weathered itself into stability and say, simply: *yes, this is good, let this persist*.

And in the Porcelain Ranges, in the thin air and the warm silence, under the pale sky where gliders trace their thermal patterns, the persistence continues—stranger now, richer, more musical, but fundamentally unchanged.

Still strange. Still balanced. Still themselves.

Still home.
