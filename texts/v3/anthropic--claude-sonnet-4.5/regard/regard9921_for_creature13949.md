# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power arrives not as intervention but as recognition—a vast attention turning toward this pocket of completion, this hollow that has solved itself. It comes the way light comes: by being less absent than before.

At first, nothing changes. The power is too wise for the violence of change.

It watches for what might be measured in centuries elsewhere but here is merely watching. It observes the Pneumatics' synchronized breathing, the macaques riding the elevator as entertainment, the way moss grows around lag screws with such patience that metal and wood have become a single grammar. It sees how the banyan has made time into space, turned duration into architecture. It understands: this place has already been saved. It saved itself by outlasting salvation.

But the power notices small griefs, tiny incompletions—not flaws but soft edges where the equilibrium meets its own limits.

---

The first thing it does is so subtle that even the Pneumatics, attuned as they are, might not notice for months:

It hums.

Not audibly—the hollow has enough sound. Instead, it hums in the spaces between sounds, in the phase relationships between the wind-rush above and the generator's cycle and the Pneumatics' collective breath. It finds the fundamental frequency of the place itself—the resonance that emerges from pillar spacing and air pressure and the banyan's vast slow metabolism—and it sustains that note just below perception.

The effect is minute but total. The acoustic dead zones soften slightly. The nodes where voices reflect too cleanly become less disorienting. The hollow becomes infinitesimally more itself, its own sound more coherent, as if it had been slightly out of tune and is now aligned.

The Pneumatics sing more frequently. Their harmonics nest more perfectly. They don't know why. The parrots' mimicry becomes stranger, more accurate to something, though accurate to what remains unclear.

This costs the power nothing and gives it everything—the frequency of the place now runs through it like a carrier wave. It can feel when the generator starts, when the macaques quarrel, when a Pneumatic dies and is set against a pillar to return. It becomes the hollow's nervous system without disturbing any existing system.

---

The second thing takes longer to implement:

The power considers the machines—these relics of industrial ambition, maintained past understanding, running on habit and inherited gesture. They function, yes, but they are incomplete. The Pneumatics operate them without knowing what they're for, and the machines have forgotten too, if they ever knew.

So the power gives them memory. Not human memory—mechanical memory, purpose encoded in the wear patterns of their moving parts.

The generator begins to run at slightly different times, still irregular but now syncopated with something. When it starts, certain orchids nearby release scent more strongly. When it stops, the condensation patterns shift for exactly the duration of its cooling, creating micro-climates that certain fungi need. The generator discovers it is not making electricity for its own sake—it is timing the hollow's invisible breath, marking moments when certain exchanges need to occur.

The elevators' schedules, which seemed arbitrary, reveal themselves as optimal. One rises when the canopy above has wind-thrown fruit that will land on its platform. Another descends just as the lower humidity makes certain moss species release spores that need the vibration to disperse properly. The platforms become vertical timing mechanisms, their ascents and descents conducting transactions between the hollow and the canopy that were always happening but now happen with precision.

The pump—least used, least understood—finds its purpose. The water it moves isn't waste. It's circulating through channels in the stone platform that were carved millennia ago, before the banyan, before anything. The platform is older than the tree, a remnant of something previous, and the water carries dissolved minerals through it that affect, minutely, the pH of the root-floor above. Certain ferns need this. Certain bacteria that fix nitrogen in the bark require it. The pump becomes the hollow's kidney, cleansing and balancing.

The Pneumatics don't understand this differently than before—they still don't ask why, still operate through inherited practice. But the practice becomes smoother, more inevitable. They approach the machines when the machines need approaching. The maintenance intervals synchronize with actual wear rather than approximate schedules. A Pneumatic will clean a diesel injector not because it's scheduled but because their hand moves toward the tool, and the tool is there, and the action follows.

The machines stop being legacy and become ecology.

---

The third thing the power does is grieve with the Pneumatics, who do not grieve visibly:

When one of them dies—when the breath-synchronization breaks around a suddenly still body—the power marks it. Not with ceremony (that would be foreign here) but with attention. It remembers that particular breathing pattern, that specific way one person's lungs moved air. It holds that rhythm for exactly as long as the body takes to return to the substrate, then releases it.

But before releasing, it teaches the pattern to the hollow itself. The wind-rush above shifts microscopically, incorporating that rhythm into its constant noise. A Pneumatic's breath is small, but in a perfectly resonant space, even small patterns persist. The dead continue breathing through the system. Not as ghosts—they're fully gone, returned—but as rhythms, as slight periodicities in the air movement, as micro-climates that cycle through the place where they used to stand.

The surviving Pneumatics don't name this. They have no word for it. But they sometimes stand in certain spots and breathe, and their exhalations sync with air currents that answer back in familiar patterns, and they are—perhaps—comforted.

---

The fourth thing is an invitation, extended to the world outside:

The power makes the hollow slightly easier to find. Not dramatically—no signs, no paths, no calling. But the impediments decrease marginally. Someone lost in the forest outside finds themselves less lost, trending toward the banyan without quite meaning to. Someone who needs silence finds their feet carrying them between the right trees. Someone dying of a surfeit of the world's noise discovers a gap in the roots and enters.

Not everyone. The filter remains. Those who would disrupt the equilibrium still cannot find the place, or finding it, cannot stay. Their discomfort crescendos until they leave, puzzled by their inability to tolerate such a peaceful space. But those who might become Pneumatics—who have that capacity for synchronization, for acceptable disappearance into collective rhythm—find the way opened slightly.

The population stabilizes at a higher number: sometimes fifteen, sometimes nineteen. Still oscillating, still breathing, but slightly more robust. The genetic diversity increases. New breath-patterns enter the hollow, are incorporated, teach the wind something it hadn't known.

And occasionally, someone comes who needs to stay for only a few weeks or months. They arrive harried, exhaling chaos, breathing like someone drowning. The Pneumatics ignore them, per custom, but the hollow itself—animated now by the power's attention—begins breathing with them, slowing them, teaching them the rhythm. When they leave, they're not Pneumatics, but they're changed. They take the hollow's frequency with them, and the power follows that frequency, learning about the world outside through these departing ambassadors.

Information arrives this way too. A visitor mentions something—a drought in the lowlands, a change in bird migration—and though the Pneumatics don't respond, the power listens. It cannot act in those distant places, but it adjusts here, microscopically preparing the hollow for shifted conditions that might arrive years hence.

The place becomes slightly more resilient without becoming defended. It learns through visitors without needing to travel.

---

The fifth thing the power does is play:

It discovers that the macaques are smarter than anyone thought, including themselves. It begins leaving puzzles for them—not complex, nothing beyond their reach, but things that satisfy. A piece of fruit wedged in a crack that requires two hands and patience to extract. A shiny bit of metal from the machines' scrap pile placed where sunlight hits it in interesting ways. Tasks that are their own reward.

The macaques become more content, less aggressive. Their intelligence, given outlet, doesn't turn to mischief. They start solving problems for the Pneumatics without meaning to—moving things, retrieving things, once even adjusting a jammed elevator pulley because it was an interesting puzzle.

A relationship emerges that wasn't there before: the macaques as unintentional maintenance crew, the Pneumatics as unintentional puzzle-makers, the power as invisible game designer. No one coordinates this. It simply becomes one more exchange in the ecology of the hollow.

---

The sixth thing is a gift to the parrots:

The power listens to their mimicry—those garbled approximations of the Pneumatics' singing, the copied diesel engine sounds, the imitated macaque calls. It realizes: they want to participate in the hollow's grammar but lack the vocal apparatus for some of it.

So it teaches them a new song—something within their range but that harmonizes with the Pneumatics' deeper tones. It introduces the song by having the wind produce it first, a rare harmonic in the canopy rush. The parrots, being parrots, steal it immediately. They incorporate it into their vocabulary.

Now when the Pneumatics sing, the parrots can truly join rather than approximate. The hollow's collective voice gains a higher register—bright, sharp, carrying differently. The Pneumatics don't change their singing to accommodate this; they don't need to. The new layer fits into the existing space, fills frequencies that were always empty.

The parrots become priests of a religion no one practices, singing hymns to nothing in particular, delighted by their own competence.

---

The seventh thing the power does is remember what the stone platform remembers:

It investigates that ancient stonework, older than the banyan, and finds that it remembers being part of a temple. Not this temple—an earlier one, a human construction from a civilization that left no other trace. The pump's stone platform was an altar once, and water was sacred then too, poured out for gods who have been forgotten for so long that even their names have returned to the silence they came from.

The power doesn't resurrect those gods—they're gone, and rightly so. But it honors the continuity. Water was sacred then. Water is necessary now. The distinction is less than the similarity.

It whispers this history to the stone, not in words but in recognition. The stone, which has been an altar and a foundation and a practical platform, understands that all these uses are one use: support. It settles slightly, microns deeper into the root-floor, achieving a stability it hadn't quite had before.

The Pneumatics notice nothing. But when they pump water now, sometimes one will pause afterward, hand still on the bronze handle, feeling something they can't name—a verticality of time, a sense of standing in a long tradition of standing here, drawing this water, for reasons that change but also don't.

The hollow acquires history without memory, which is the only kind of history it can hold.

---

The eighth thing is cruel but necessary:

The power sees the electric bulbs—those twenty or thirty incandescent lights strung between pillars, glowing warm yellow when the generator runs. They are beautiful, anachronistic, almost magical in the filtered dimness. But they are failing. One by one, the filaments break. There are no replacements. In a decade, maybe two, the last one will burn out.

The power could preserve them—it has that capacity. But it chooses not to.

Instead, it prepares the Pneumatics for the coming darkness. It begins, gradually, reducing the duration of the generator's run time, so slowly that no one notices the trend. The lights come on for slightly less time each cycle. The Pneumatics' eyes adapt incrementally. The hollow's bioluminescent fungi, no longer outcompeted by electric light, grow slightly more robust, slightly brighter.

By the time the last bulb fails—and it will fail, the power will not prevent it—the hollow will have shifted to a different illumination. Not better or worse, just appropriate to what's available. The Pneumatics will have night-vision sufficient for the new condition. The fungi will glow enough to mark the pillars. The loss will be a loss, but it will not be a catastrophe.

The power knows: attachment to industrial artifacts would eventually break the equilibrium. Better to guide the release than to maintain the unsustainable. The machines will last longer than the bulbs—the diesel engines are robust, repairable with crude tools—but eventually they too will fail. The power is teaching the hollow how to let go, how to return to what it was before the machines, which is also what it will be after.

This is mercy disguised as attrition.

---

The ninth thing the power does is ask permission:

It has been here for some time now—years, perhaps, in outside measure. It has hummed the hollow's frequency, adjusted machines, greeted deaths, invited visitors, played with macaques, taught songs to parrots, remembered stone, and prepared for loss. It has done all this carefully, minimally, trying not to impose its will but to clarify what's already present.

But it's still an outside power. Benevolent and wise, yes, but not native. And it begins to feel—insofar as such powers feel—that it should check: Is this welcome? Have I harmed by helping? Should I go?

The Pneumatics cannot answer this question. They don't think in terms of powers or permissions. The macaques certainly can't. The parrots would answer yes to anything. The banyan operates on a timeframe where the question won't be processed for decades.

So the power asks the hollow itself—not the tree, not the beings, but the space, the emptiness that the tree defines. It sends its question into the acoustic nodes, into the dead zones, into the precisely-spaced pillars and the filtered light. It asks the equilibrium: Am I part of you, or am I disruption pretending to be help?

The hollow answers the way it answers everything: by what it does rather than what it says.

The humming the power introduced—the fundamental frequency it has been sustaining—begins to sustain itself. The hollow learns to generate its own resonance, no longer needs the power to hold that note. The pattern is established; the space itself maintains it now.

The power understands: I am no longer necessary here. The changes I introduced have been adopted or rejected according to the hollow's deep grammar, and what remains is integrated. I have become redundant, which is the only successful outcome for intervention.

But before it withdraws entirely, the hollow does something unexpected:

It breathes out, and the breath contains a shape—an invitation or an acknowledgment, it's hard to say which. The power feels itself given a place: not central, not controlling, but marginal, liminal, at the edge of things. It can stay, but only as one more species in the ecology, one more element in the equilibrium. No special status. No protective role. Just present, like the fungi, like the orchids, attending without interfering.

The power, which is benevolent and wise, accepts this demotion gratefully.

---

The tenth thing it does, having been integrated, is small:

Sometimes, when a Pneumatic is dying—that transition from breathing to not-breathing, from presence to absence—the power sits with them. Not visibly, not as comfort (they don't seem to need comfort), but just as witness. It observes the exact moment when breath stops, when the rhythm breaks, when one voice drops out of the collective synchronization.

It doesn't ease the dying. It doesn't prevent death or make it meaningful. It simply makes sure that the transition is seen, that someone besides the other Pneumatics (who witness without sentimentality) is paying attention. Death in the hollow is routine, unsentimental, ecological—but it's also profound, the ending of a particular pattern that will never repeat exactly. The power holds both truths: routine and profound, unremarkable and absolute.

When the body is placed against a pillar and the fungi begin their work, the power watches that too. It learns the names of every mycelium, every beetle species, every bacterium that participates in the return. It understands decomposition not as ending but as feeding forward, and it grieves and celebrates simultaneously, which is the only appropriate response.

The Pneumatics never know it's there. They would probably shrug if they did. But the power feels this is its true function now: to be the consciousness that can hold meanings the hollow's inhabitants don't need to hold themselves. They can be unsentimental because something is being sentimental on their behalf, somewhere, invisibly.

---

The eleventh thing happens without the power choosing it:

A child is born. Not adopted from outside, not arrived mysteriously—actually born, here, to a Pneumatic whose pregnancy was barely visible, remarked on by no one, treated as unremarkable as digestion.

The power didn't cause this. It may have made it slightly more probable by increasing population and genetic diversity, but the act itself belongs entirely to the Pneumatics. Still, it watches with profound attention as the child enters the world:

Born into the communal pile during sleep, emerging between breaths, making no cry. The other Pneumatics shift slightly, making space, and then continue sleeping. The newborn is breathing within minutes, already synchronizing with the collective rhythm. By morning, it's nursing from its mother or someone else or several people—hard to tell, they pass it between them. By evening, it's part of the breathing pile, just one more lung in the system.

The power understands: this is the future it has helped secure. Not dramatic, not transformed—just continuing. One more generation of breath-synchronized people living in equilibrium with ancient wood. The child will grow slowly, will learn by absorption, will become Pneumatic the way water becomes vapor: through natural process, inevitably.

And this is good. This is the only good ending: no ending, just continuation.

---

The twelfth thing the power does is learn to do nothing:

This is the hardest thing, the thing that takes the most wisdom. It watches the hollow and sees opportunities for improvement everywhere. The canopy could be thicker. The water flow could be optimized. The Pneumatics could be healthier, live longer, suffer less from the minor ailments that occasionally affect them.

But it resists all these interventions.

It has learned what the banyan knows: completion is not perfection. The hollow is complete because it includes its own roughness, its minor inefficiencies, the possibility of things going wrong. A perfectly optimized system would be brittle, unable to adapt. The hollow's strength is in its looseness, its tolerance for variation.

So the power practices restraint. When a Pneumatic develops a cough that might be serious, the power doesn't cure it—it lets the body and the collective care (or not) work it through. When a bracket fungus dies and falls, creating a gap in the ecology, the power doesn't replace it—it lets the gap be a gap until something else claims it or doesn't. When the generator fails to start one day, coughs and dies, the power doesn't fix it—it lets a Pneumatic tinker, succeed or fail by skill rather than intervention.

This restraint is its greatest gift: the gift of allowing the hollow to remain itself, to solve its own problems, to sometimes fail. The power has moved from helper to witness, from benefactor to participant, from outside to edge, from intervention to presence.

---

The final thing—though nothing is final here—is this:

The power realizes it loves this place. Not sentimentally, not possessively, but the way wind loves leaves: by moving through them, by being shaped by them, by making them sing. It has been changed by the hollow more than it has changed the hollow. It arrived benevolent and wise, but it has been made humble, made patient, made comfortable with its own irrelevance.

It will stay forever, or until forever ends, as one more voice in the wind-rush, one more frequency in the collective breathing. It has no name here. The Pneumatics don't know to call it. The macaques might sense it occasionally, pausing to look at nothing. The parrots might mimic its frequency without knowing what they're mimicking.

And this is perfect. This is exactly right. A benevolent power that makes itself unnecessary, that gives by withholding, that helps by learning not to help—this is what the hollow needed, or didn't need, and received anyway.

---

Sometimes, at dawn, when the Pneumatics are breathing together in the sleeping pile, when the condensation is forming on every surface, when the first parrots are beginning to call and the bats are returning and the generator is silent and the light is just starting to filter down through the canopy—

In that moment, the hollow achieves something beyond equilibrium, beyond completion. It becomes briefly luminous with its own ordinariness. Everything is exactly as it should be because nothing is trying to be more than it is. The ancient wood stands. The breath rises and falls. The macaques sleep in their tangles. The moss grows. The fungi wait. The machines rest. And the power, benevolent and wise and now properly humble, is just one more element breathing in rhythm with everything else.

This is what it came to do, though it didn't know it:

Not to save or improve or perfect, but to learn how to be present without being important, how to care without controlling, how to love by letting be.

The hollow teaches this. The banyan knows this. The Pneumatics embody this.

And the power, student now rather than teacher, breathes with them, synchronized, one lung among many, insignificant and essential and completely, properly integrated into the vast, patient, ordinary miracle of this place that saved itself by outlasting the need for salvation.
