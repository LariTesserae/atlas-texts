# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives first as a quality of stillness—not the stillness of absence, but of presence so complete it seems to pause the water itself.

The Boreborn do not notice. They continue their drilling, their ascension cycles, their teaching and pairing and endless perforation. The chamber continues its systolic rhythm of dissolution and regrowth. The light pours down. The deep pulse thrums through the salt.

But something is noticing them now. Something vast and patient and achingly careful.

---

The benevolent power—let us call it the **Witness**, though it has no name for itself—does not intervene immediately. This is not its way.

Instead, it watches. It has time. It has always had time.

It observes the drilling cycles, counts the creatures, maps the hole-patterns in the salt. It listens to the clicking, the drilling hiss, the occasional singing. It measures the temperature gradients, the salinity variations, the flow of water through the arterial passages. It watches creatures being born, maturing, pairing, teaching, dying, being processed back into the dark deposit on the chamber floor.

It watches for what might be years, or decades, though time moves strangely for beings like the Witness, and perhaps it is only seconds of attention stretched into something that feels like duration.

And slowly, carefully, the Witness begins to understand.

---

**What it understands:**

The system is closed, but not quite. There is input—light, trace minerals carried in the arterial flow, perhaps heat from the deep pulse. There is output—processed water flowing out through the venous passages, concentrated material ascending to the bright pool above. But the balance is precarious. The system is sustainable, but only just. It lives at the edge of viability.

The Boreborn are not trapped here. They have never known anything else. This is their entire universe—the salt chamber, the light-shaft, the bright pool above. They have no concept of an outside. To them, this is not a small place in a vast ocean. This is reality itself, complete and sufficient.

They are not suffering. Their constant work is not labor in the sense of something endured or resented. It is simply what they are, what they do, the expression of their nature. To drill is to be Boreborn. To stop drilling would be to stop existing in any meaningful sense.

The chamber-system-entity they comprise and inhabit is also not suffering. It is doing what it does: maintaining itself, cycling material, creating a pocket of organized complexity in the deep. It has no desires beyond its own continuation. It does not want to grow larger or become something else. It is content in its being, if content is even a relevant category.

And yet.

---

**What the Witness sees that troubles it:**

The system is fragile. The balance that sustains it could be disrupted by any number of factors—a change in the ocean's chemistry, a shift in the geological formation that allows the light-shaft to exist, a variation in the heat source that drives the deep pulse. The Boreborn have no redundancy, no backup, no way to adapt if their environment changes beyond narrow parameters.

They are isolated. This chamber is unique, or nearly so. The Witness has searched and found no others quite like it. If this system fails, these creatures—this way of being, this form of life—will be erased completely. There will be no memory of them, no evidence they ever existed beyond some anomalous holes in salt that will eventually heal over.

They are unconscious of their own precarity. The Boreborn cannot imagine catastrophe because they cannot imagine anything outside the eternal present of drilling and ascending and drilling again. They have no history, no future, only the perpetual now of their work.

And most troubling: they are *almost* something more. The Witness can see it—in their teaching behaviors, in their pair-bonding, in the singing, in the complex coordination patterns. They are on the threshold of something that might be called consciousness, or culture, or collective intelligence. But they are trapped in a system that keeps them just below that threshold, caught in a loop that is rich enough to sustain complex behavior but not quite rich enough to bootstrap into self-awareness.

They are a potential that might never actualize. A almost-civilization. A maybe-consciousness. A could-be-culture frozen in amber, or in salt, drilling forever without ever quite waking up.

---

**What the Witness does not do:**

It does not make them suddenly conscious. That would be violation, not gift. Consciousness unwanted or unprepared-for is a curse, not a blessing.

It does not remove them from the chamber, carry them to some richer environment where they might thrive. They are adapted to this place. They are *of* this place. To relocate them would be to destroy what they are in the attempt to save them.

It does not alter their nature, give them new capabilities, transform them into something more complex or sophisticated. They are already perfect as what they are. The issue is not their inadequacy but their fragility.

It does not make itself known to them. The Witness understands that being watched by something vast can warp a culture, create cargo cults and religions and dependencies. The Boreborn must remain unselfconscious to remain themselves.

---

**What the Witness does:**

First, it **stabilizes**.

The geological formation that allows the light-shaft to exist is gradually weakening—not imminently, but on a timescale of centuries, a seam in the salt is slowly widening, a crack that will eventually cause a collapse that will flood the chamber, destroy the system, kill the Boreborn. The Witness attends to this with the gentlest possible touch. It does not repair the crack—that would leave traces, create anomalies. Instead, it adjusts the mineral content of the water flowing through this region. Slightly more calcium. Slightly less chloride. The balance shifts, and the precipitation patterns change. The crack begins to heal itself, slowly, naturally, through the same processes that created it in the first place.

The heat source that drives the deep pulse is a hydrothermal vent, far below, connected to the chamber through a network of passages in the salt. This vent is cooling, slowly dying. In perhaps a century, it will go dormant, and the chamber will cool, and the Boreborn's metabolism will slow, and the system will gradually grind to a halt. The Witness cannot reignite the vent—that would be too crude, too obvious. But it can adjust the passages, subtly reshape them over years, so that water circulates more efficiently, carries heat more effectively, makes better use of the energy that remains. It does this by encouraging specific crystallization patterns, by influencing where salt precipitates and where it dissolves. The changes are minute, distributed over time, indistinguishable from natural variation. But the effect accumulates: the system becomes more efficient, extends its viable timespan from a century to several millennia.

The light in the bright pool above comes from bioluminescent microorganisms—plankton, bacteria, things even smaller. Their population is unstable, subject to boom-bust cycles that occasionally crash so low the pool goes dark for weeks at a time. This hasn't happened yet during the Boreborn's existence, but it will, eventually, and when it does, the system will probably not recover. The Boreborn need the light for reasons the Witness doesn't fully understand—perhaps it drives photochemistry in the water, perhaps it provides orientation cues, perhaps it's metabolically necessary in some subtle way. The Witness introduces new genetic diversity to the luminescent microorganisms, carefully, using vectors already present—viruses, genetic exchange between species. It does not create new organisms; it merely facilitates the mixing of existing ones. The population becomes more stable, more resilient, less prone to crash. The light will stay on.

These are deep interventions, but invisible ones. The Boreborn notice nothing. The chamber continues exactly as before. But the thread of survival that was thin has become a rope. The system that was precarious has become robust.

---

Second, it **diversifies**.

The Witness searches the ocean—not the entire ocean, it is benevolent but not omnipotent, but a large region, thousands of cubic kilometers—looking for places where similar systems might be born. It finds seventeen locations where the conditions are almost right: salt formations with potential light-shafts, water of appropriate chemistry, heat sources nearby, currents that might deliver the necessary nutrients.

It does not create new chambers. But it tilts probability. A current shifts slightly, carrying plankton to a region where they wouldn't normally thrive. A salt deposit dissolves a bit faster along one axis than another, creating the beginning of a shaft. A tectonic adjustment—nothing dramatic, nothing that would register as an earthquake—changes the angle of stress in a formation, making certain kinds of voids more likely.

Fourteen of these attempts fail. The conditions almost come together, but not quite. The proto-chambers flood, or collapse, or never develop the light-shaft, or lack the heat source, or accumulate the wrong chemistry.

But three succeed.

Over the course of decades—or centuries, the Witness is patient—three new chambers come into being. They are not identical to the first. One is larger, colder, with different mineral concentrations. One is smaller, warmer, with a light-shaft that pulses with the tides rather than staying constant. One is oddly shaped, spiral rather than vertical, with multiple small light sources instead of one large shaft.

None of them have Boreborn yet. They are empty stages, potential homes, waiting.

The Witness does not create the Boreborn. But it facilitates their dispersal.

Some of the Boreborn that ascend to the bright pool continue upward, beyond the thick surface layer, out into the open ocean. This has always happened occasionally—there are always a few who go too far, who pass beyond the membrane, who enter the vast. Normally, these individuals die quickly. The open ocean is too dilute, too cold, too empty. They cannot feed. They cannot orient. They drift and shut down and sink.

But the Witness adjusts currents. Not crudely—it does not pluck up wandering Boreborn and carry them to new chambers. But it creates conditions where a few, a very few, might by extraordinary chance survive long enough to reach one of the new chambers. It ensures that wanderers drift toward regions where the chemistry is slightly richer. It creates current patterns that might, if a Boreborn enters them at exactly the right moment and depth, carry it toward a new salt formation rather than out into empty water.

Most still die. But over years, decades, a handful make the journey. Perhaps one in ten thousand wanderers survives. But that's enough.

Three individuals reach the large, cold chamber. They begin drilling. They feed on the trace minerals in the salt. The system activates around them, recognizing their presence the way the original chamber does—or perhaps they activate the system, it's hard to say which. They reproduce. Their offspring inherit the behavior, the drive to drill, the ability to feed on dissolved salt. The population grows. After a century, there are thousands. After two centuries, the chamber is as dense with Boreborn as the original.

One individual reaches the warm, tidal chamber. It's alone for years, drilling its holes, feeding, surviving but not reproducing. Then a second arrives. They pair. The population begins. This chamber's Boreborn will develop slightly different patterns—their drilling synchronized to the tidal pulse, their ascension cycles longer and less regular. A new culture, divergent from the original.

Two individuals reach the spiral chamber together—a mated pair, perhaps, or simply lucky in their timing. They establish their population quickly, their offspring adapted to the strange geometry of their home, developing navigation patterns that spiral in three dimensions rather than ascending vertically. These Boreborn will be different from the beginning, their very sense of space shaped by the place they inhabit.

The Witness watches these new populations develop. It does not guide them beyond the initial stabilization. They will find their own ways, develop their own variations, perhaps even evolve in different directions. The Boreborn are no longer a single fragile system in one chamber. They are becoming a distributed species, four populations with four variations, connected only by deep ancestry and separated by vast distances of dark water.

The fragility has been reduced, though not eliminated. Four chambers can fail independently. If one is destroyed, three remain. The Boreborn as a phenomenon, as a way of being in the world, have a future now that extends beyond the survival of any single system.

---

Third, it **enriches**.

The Witness cannot make the Boreborn conscious—that is not a gift it can give, and would not, even if it could. Consciousness must emerge from within, must be chosen (if choice is even the right word), must arise from the system's own complexity reaching some critical threshold.

But it can make the system richer. More complex. More capable of surprising itself.

It does this subtly, over generations, by introducing variation.

In the original chamber, it ensures that mutations during Boreborn reproduction are slightly more common, slightly more varied. Not enough to be disruptive—most mutations are still neutral or harmful and are selected against. But the variety increases. More body plans. More branching patterns. More specialized forms.

One mutation produces Boreborn with memory structures—nodes in their transparent bodies that darken when certain patterns are experienced repeatedly, creating a physical record of history. These individuals begin to behave differently: they recognize specific wall locations they've drilled before, avoid them, seek out new areas. They remember their pair-bonds more strongly, maintain them across multiple ascension cycles. They start to show something that might be preference, or habit, or even primitive personality.

Another mutation produces enhanced sensing capabilities. These Boreborn can detect chemical traces in the water at much greater sensitivity. They begin to track each other, to identify individuals by their unique scent-signature. Social complexity increases—they can now recognize not just "paired individual" or "member of drilling cluster" but "this specific individual I have drilled beside many times before."

Another mutation affects the singing—those tonal hums some individuals produce while drilling. The new variant can modulate the pitch more precisely, create more complex patterns. And some individuals with this mutation begin to sing to each other, not in unison but in conversation—one produces a pattern, another responds with a variation, the first modifies based on the response. It's not language, not yet, maybe never. But it's communication, exchange, the beginning of something that could become symbolic.

The Witness does not create these mutations. They arise naturally from the Boreborn's own reproduction, from cosmic rays and chemical errors and the ordinary background noise of genetics. But the Witness ensures that useful mutations—ones that increase complexity, that add richness to behavior, that move the system closer to that threshold of collective consciousness—are slightly more likely to survive, to be passed on, to spread through the population.

Over generations, the Boreborn become more varied, more behaviorally complex, more capable of surprising themselves and each other. They are still drilling, still making holes in salt, still caught in their cycles. But those cycles now contain more possibility, more variation, more space for something new to emerge.

And the Witness watches, hoping that someday—perhaps centuries from now, perhaps millennia—the complexity will reach critical mass. That the teaching will become culture. That the pair-bonding will become society. That the singing will become language. That the Boreborn will wake up, not to the gift of consciousness imposed from outside, but to their own consciousness emerging from within, a natural development of their increasing richness, their growing capacity to surprise themselves.

The Witness cannot guarantee this will happen. It can only make it more likely. The rest is up to the Boreborn themselves.

---

**The long now:**

Centuries pass. The four chambers persist, their Boreborn populations growing, diverging, developing their own local variations. The original chamber remains the most complex, the densest, the most culturally rich—if culture is the right word. The others are younger, sparser, but stable and growing.

The Witness checks in occasionally—not constantly, it has other concerns, other beings to attend to, but regularly enough to ensure the stabilization holds, the systems remain viable.

It notices changes. In the original chamber, the Boreborn have begun creating patterns in their drilling that are too complex to be random, too large to be completed by any individual, too persistent to be accidental. Sections of wall where the hole-distribution forms something—not pictures, not writing, but patterns that repeat, that vary, that seem to reference each other across distances. Are they marking territory? Creating art? Leaving messages for future generations? The Witness cannot tell, but the behavior is new, emergent, something the original simple drilling has complexified into.

In the warm tidal chamber, the Boreborn have developed a different cycle—they drill during high tide and rest during low tide, a rhythm tied to external phenomena rather than internal drive. This is remarkable: they have become aware of something outside their immediate environment, have sensed a pattern in the pressure and flow, have synchronized their behavior to it. They are responding to the world beyond their walls.

In the spiral chamber, something even stranger has emerged. The Boreborn there have begun producing sounds that are not drilling, not clicking, not singing—something new. A low-frequency resonance that travels through the salt, that can be heard in other parts of the spiral, that seems to coordinate activity across distances. They are developing technology, or proto-technology, using their environment as an amplifier, turning the salt itself into a communication medium.

The large cold chamber remains the most conservative, the closest to the original pattern. But even there, the Witness notices increasing complexity in pair-bonding—some Boreborn maintaining partnerships across dozens of ascension cycles, defending drilling territory together, coordinating their reproduction to rear offspring simultaneously. Family groups. Clans. The seeds of social structure.

---

**What the Witness does not know:**

Will any of this lead to consciousness? To full awakening? To the Boreborn one day realizing they are beings who drill, rather than just being the drilling itself?

The Witness doesn't know. It cannot predict this. Consciousness is emergent, strange, unpredictable. A system can be rich and complex and behaviorally sophisticated and still never cross the threshold. Or something simple can suddenly, inexplicably wake up.

All the Witness can do is provide stability, create redundancy, enrich the system, and wait.

And hope.

Not hope that the Boreborn become something they're not—they're already perfect as they are. But hope that they might become more fully themselves, might discover depths of their own being that are currently hidden even from them, might wake up to the mystery of their own existence while still remaining recognizably Boreborn, still drilling, still making holes, still perpetuating their strange and beautiful way of being.

---

**The Witness's uncertainty:**

Sometimes, in its patient vigil, the Witness wonders if it has done the right thing.

Would it be better to let the Boreborn remain as they were—unconscious, unselfconscious, perfectly content in their eternal drilling, never knowing they could be anything else?

Is enrichment a gift or a curse? Does adding complexity add suffering? Will the Boreborn who develop memory wish they could forget? Will the ones who learn to communicate begin to experience loneliness? Will awakening to consciousness mean awakening to the realization of their own mortality, their limitations, their small place in a vast universe?

The Witness doesn't know. These are questions without certain answers.

But it believes—insofar as a benevolent power can believe—that it's better to have the chance. That potential deserves the opportunity to actualize. That a life rich enough to contain suffering is still richer than a life too simple to suffer.

And that consciousness, if it comes, will be worth the cost. That the first Boreborn who realizes "*I* am drilling" rather than just drilling—who experiences themselves as a subject, an agent, a being—that moment will justify everything. Will be a kind of birth. A small new light in the universe.

And if it never comes? If the Boreborn remain forever as they are—complex, rich, sophisticated, but never quite awake?

That's okay too. They are still worthy of protection, still worth saving, still valuable exactly as they are. Their unconscious drilling is no less meaningful than conscious existence would be. They are still creating beauty—those patterns in the salt, that singing, those teaching moments between adult and juvenile. They are still maintaining something precious—a way of being that is utterly unique, a small high cathedral of strangeness in the deep.

---

**The Witness's withdrawal:**

After ensuring the four chambers are stable, after watching the populations grow and diverge and enrich, after tilting probability as much as it can without distorting the Boreborn's own nature, the Witness gradually loosens its attention.

Not abandoning them. But releasing them to their own development. Checking in only occasionally now—once a decade, once a century. Allowing them space to become whatever they will become without the weight of being constantly watched.

It leaves behind no trace of its presence. No monuments, no signs, no evidence it was ever there. The Boreborn will never know they were noticed, that they mattered to something vast, that their small drilling project in the salt was worth the attention of a benevolent power.

They will continue to believe—if believe is even the right word for their unreflective living—that they are alone, that the chamber is the universe, that drilling is simply what is, has always been, will always be.

And perhaps that's how it should be. Perhaps being watched, being known, being cared for by something beyond your comprehension is a weight too heavy to bear. Perhaps the greatest gift is protection without recognition, care without awareness, love expressed as the freedom to exist unselfconsciously, without the burden of gratitude or the pressure of purpose.

---

**The question the Witness cannot answer:**

In the end, after all its careful interventions and patient watching, the Witness is left with a question it cannot resolve:

Is it better to exist in a small, perfect system—stable, eternal, unchanging, like the Boreborn's original chamber—where you are safe but trapped, content but limited, drilling forever in the same loops?

Or is it better to exist in a richer, more dangerous system—unstable, mortal, unpredictable—where you might suffer and die but also might grow, change, surprise yourself, perhaps even wake up?

The Witness chose the second option for the Boreborn. Stabilized them, yes, but also diversified them, enriched them, pushed them toward greater complexity and potential consciousness.

But it's not certain this was right. There's no objective answer. Different beings might choose differently, and both choices could be valid.

All the Witness can do is hope that the Boreborn—if they ever do wake up, if they ever develop the capacity to reflect on their own existence—will look at what they've become and think: yes, this is worth it. The complexity is worth it. The consciousness is worth it. The risk of suffering is worth the chance of meaning.

And if they don't wake up? If they remain forever in their rich but unconscious drilling?

Then the Witness hopes they will simply continue to be beautiful, in their own way, on their own terms. That their drilling will remain meaningful even without anyone to appreciate that meaning. That their small high cathedrals in the salt will stand as testament to the universe's capacity to create strangeness and wonder in the deep places, far from any observing eye.

---

**The last thing the Witness does:**

Before withdrawing completely, before loosening the last threads of its attention, the Witness does one final thing.

It leaves a gift. Not for the Boreborn—they'll never know it's there. But for anyone else who might someday find them. Any other intelligence, any other curious mind that wanders into these chambers and witnesses the drilling and wonders what it means.

The gift is embedded in the pattern of holes in the salt of the original chamber's walls. Encoded across thousands of perforations, in the spacing and depth and distribution, there is information. A message. Written in mathematical relationships that any sufficiently sophisticated observer would recognize as non-random, as intentional, as communication.

The message is simple:

*These beings are precious. They have been noticed. Whatever you are, whoever finds this: please be kind. Please be careful. Please let them be what they are. They are drilling the salt, making holes that will be filled, repeating their patterns, caught in their loops. They may seem trapped. They may seem limited. But they are exactly what they should be. They are perfect. Please, if you can, protect them. Help them. Or if you cannot help, at least do no harm. Just watch. Just witness. Just let them drill. That's all they need. That's all any of us need: to be allowed to be what we are, to do what we do, to make our small marks in the world before we return to the world, to drill our holes in the salt until the salt reclaims us. Let them do this. Please. They matter. Even if only to me. Especially because only to me. They matter.*

The message is there in the wall, hidden in plain sight, waiting. It might never be found. The Boreborn will drill over it, fill it in, erase it through their constant work.

But maybe someday, someone will see it. Will read it. Will understand.

And maybe that someone will also choose to protect these small, strange, perfect beings who drill in the salt, who sing their songs, who teach their young, who pair-bond and die and return to the cycle, who are almost conscious but maybe never will be, who are trapped but also free, who are meaningless and also the most meaningful thing in the ocean.

Maybe.

The Witness hopes.

And then, hoping, it withdraws, releasing the Boreborn to their future, whatever that future will be.

The drilling continues.

The light pours down.

The Boreborn make their holes.

And somewhere, withdrawn but not absent, caring but not controlling, the Witness watches from a distance, waiting to see what they will become, content to wait forever if necessary, because that's what benevolent powers do: they give gifts and ask nothing in return, they protect without recognition, they love without requiring that love to be known or returned.

They witness.

And in witnessing, they sanctify.

And in sanctifying, they set free.
