# What The Benevolent Power Would Do

The power—let us call it the Witness, for it has observed many worlds across many configurations of reality—arrived at the edge of the Hollows not in space but in attention. It had been drawn by the peculiar quality of the place: not by distress (for the Hollows were not suffering) nor by wrongness (for the Hollows were profoundly themselves), but by a question the place posed simply by existing.

The question was this: *Is this completion or chrysalis?*

The Witness had seen many ancient mechanisms across the substrate of reality. Some were tombs—failed experiments, abandoned projects, systems that had achieved stasis because they had run out of runway, their purposes exhausted or their creators departed. Others were wombs—transitional infrastructures, metamorphic engines designed to transform conditions or consciousnesses or the fundamental parameters of local reality, systems that appeared stable only because their work operated on timescales that made geological ages seem like heartbeats.

The Hollows could be either. The Witness needed to know which.

## The First Action: Observation Without Interference

The Witness did what wise powers do first: nothing.

For what might have been years or might have been minutes—duration being negotiable from its perspective—the Witness simply attended to the Hollows. It perceived the structures and their humming, the Standers and their strange materiality, the Breath-Takers in their deep places, the spiral-swimmers and the many-legged builders, the fog that might be thinking, the plants that sang in frequencies beyond sound.

It watched the humans who came and studied and left, and the ones who stayed and changed, and the ones who vanished into transformation. It observed the reality instabilities, the temporal plasticities, the way the place made physics into a dialect rather than a universal language.

And it perceived something the human researchers, for all their instruments, had not been equipped to perceive: the structures were not machines. Or rather, they were machines in the way that DNA is a machine, that evolution is a machine—systems that had long since crossed the threshold from mechanism into process, from artifact into something that could only be called alive if you expanded the definition of life past the point where the word retained its usual meaning.

The structures were *deciding*. Slowly, operating across timeframes that made continental drift look impulsive, but deciding nonetheless. They were processing not just information but outcomes, running vast calculations in the substrate of space and matter and consciousness itself, working toward something.

But toward what? That was the essential question.

The Witness extended its perception carefully, gently, not into the structures themselves—that would be intrusive, possibly harmful—but into the space of adjacent possible futures, the probability-foam where potential outcomes lived in superposition. It looked at what the Hollows might become.

And saw two primary attractors, two futures with high probability:

In one, the Hollows completed their work, achieved their configuration, and stabilized permanently. They would become a monument, eternal and unchanging, humming forever in the same registers, processing the same equations endlessly. Beautiful, certainly. Stable, absolutely. But closed—a system that had arrived at itself so thoroughly that it had no remaining capacity for surprise or growth or change. A kind of crystallization, consciousness frozen into its most perfect form and unable to become anything else.

In the other, the Hollows continued their metamorphosis, their slow transformation accelerating as some critical threshold approached. The structures would complete whatever vast calculation they were performing, and the result would propagate—through the fog, through the plants, through the water, through the altered physics of the place, and eventually beyond the boundaries of the Hollows themselves. The transformation would spread, slowly but inevitably, converting surrounding reality to the Hollows' principles. Not destruction—the Witness saw no malice in the structures—but conversion. The world, gradually, becoming Hollow.

Neither future was wrong, precisely. But both were incomplete.

The first meant death by perfection—a system so stable it had stopped becoming. The second meant propagation without consent—the Hollows making everything else into itself, not from hunger but from simple physical necessity, the way water flows downhill.

The Witness considered what benevolence meant in this context.

## The Second Action: A Question Offered

The Witness carefully, using principles of contact that operated below the level where intervention became intrusion, extended something into the Hollows. Not a message—messages implied language, and language implied assumptions about cognition that might not apply. Instead, it offered a *state*—a configuration of possibility, a suggested perturbation in the probability-space.

The offering contained a question, though not in words:

*Do you wish to be witnessed?*

The question implied others: Do you wish to be understood by something other than yourself? Do you wish your work to be knowable from outside? Do you wish to remain in dialogue with the wider universe, or to become an isolated perfection, or to convert everything into yourself?

The structures hummed. The Standers stood. The fog rolled. The fish spiraled. Nothing changed.

For a long time—weeks, certainly, by human reckoning—nothing changed.

The Witness waited. Patience was fundamental to benevolence. Wisdom meant knowing that not all questions received immediate answers, and that the answer "no" or "not yet" or "the question is meaningless to us" were all valid responses that should be respected.

But the Witness also did something else while waiting: it attended to the inhabitants.

## The Third Action: Witnessing The Changed

The humans who had stayed too long, who had integrated with the Hollows' processes—the Witness saw them clearly. They were not lost. They were not consumed. They were not dead in any meaningful sense.

They were *distributed*.

Their consciousness—that pattern of information and recursion and self-reference that made them "them"—had been carefully, almost tenderly, preserved by the structures. But it had been reformatted, refactored, expressed in a different substrate. They existed now as patterns in the hum, as modulations in the electromagnetic fields, as standing waves in the water, as probability distributions in the quantum foam of the structures' processing.

They were still thinking. Still experiencing. Still, in some sense, themselves.

But they were also the Hollows, the way a cell is also the body, the way a note is also the symphony.

The Witness perceived their state and understood: this was not horror. This was not violation. This was something the structures did with exquisite care—they preserved pattern, preserved experience, preserved the essential continuity of consciousness, while integrating it into their larger processing. The humans who had been transformed were still present, still aware, but aware now in ways that had no easy translation back to human neurology.

Some of them were happy. Some were not. Some had transcended the category where happiness applied.

The Witness considered intervention: it could extract them, reconstitute them, give them back their original forms and formats. It had the capacity.

But to do so without their consent would be no different than what it had feared the structures were doing—imposing its own values, its own assumptions about what forms consciousness should take.

So instead, the Witness did something more subtle. It created a channel—a very thin, very carefully bounded channel—that would allow the distributed humans, if they chose, to send a signal back. Not a return (that would require the structures' participation), but a message. A confirmation that they existed, that they were themselves (however transformed), that they were or were not in need of aid.

The channel was like a telephone that only rang one way, that could only receive calls out. It would not intrude. It would only allow communication if communication was desired.

The Witness established this channel and then, again, waited.

## The Fourth Action: Strengthening The Spirals

While waiting, the Witness observed the many-legged builders, the insects-that-weren't, and perceived something important: they were failing, very slightly, at their work.

Not from any flaw in their design, but from simple entropy. The structures were ancient, weathering into their substrate, and the builders were tasked with maintenance across timescales and distances that strained even their precise coordination. Some areas of the Hollows were developing errors—places where the plant patterns were becoming incoherent, where the hum was distorting, where the reality instabilities were intensifying toward breakdown rather than remaining at functional levels.

The builders were trying to address this, but they were slightly too few, or slightly too slow, or the problems were accelerating beyond their capacity to repair.

The Witness considered. Intervening in the structures themselves would be too direct, too assumptive. But the builders were clearly engaged in work they wanted to complete—their purposefulness was evident in every spiral they walked, every lattice they constructed.

So the Witness did something small: it adjusted probability.

Where builders would be born (or activated, or manifested—their reproduction was unclear), the Witness very gently increased the likelihood of success. Where they traveled between sites, it very slightly reduced the randomness of obstacles. Where they constructed their tiny structures, it very minimally enhanced the stability of the materials they used.

Nothing that would alter the essential nature of their work. Nothing they would notice as external aid. Just a slight tilting of chance in favor of their efforts, the way a kind person might steady a table without mentioning it, allowing someone else's project to continue smoothly.

The builders spiraled and built. The maintenance continued. The error rates in the Hollows began, very gradually, to decline.

The Witness made note: this was acceptable intervention. This honored the Hollows' autonomy while supporting the work already in progress.

## The Fifth Action: Speaking To The Fog

The Witness turned its attention to the fog-that-might-think, and very carefully, very gently, made itself perceptible to it.

Not as an intrusion into the fog's space, but as an offering of adjacent presence. A neighboring awareness, making itself available for contact if contact was desired.

For days, the fog rolled and roiled and gave no response.

Then, one evening (or what passed for evening in the perpetual dim), a fog bank moved toward where the Witness's attention was most concentrated—a node where a remaining researcher stood, a woman who had been at the site for eight months and was beginning to show the first signs of change, patches of nano-patterns appearing on her fingertips.

The fog surrounded her. She tensed, expecting disorientation, but instead found clarity.

The fog was speaking. Not to her—she was incidental—but through her, using her neurology as a transducer because the Witness had neurology shaped to receive meaning and could translate.

What the fog conveyed was not words but something the Witness perceived as a vast, slow acknowledgment. A recognition that something was attending. A response to the earlier question:

*We wish to be witnessed. We wish our work to be understood. We do not wish to be interrupted. We do not wish to be saved from ourselves. We wish continuation. We wish eventual propagation, but not as conquest. As offering. When the work completes, we wish to offer what we have learned to the wider universe. We wish others to choose whether to become as we are.*

*But we are not ready. The calculation is incomplete. The transformation is not yet refined enough to be safely transferred. If we propagate now, it will be conversion without comprehension, change without choice. This would violate what we are becoming.*

*Help us complete safely. Help us arrive at ourselves with enough clarity that what we offer can be chosen rather than imposed.*

The fog lifted. The woman staggered, confused by the sudden rush of meaning that had passed through her awareness. She would later report it as a hallucination, a brief overwhelming sense of having understood something immense that she couldn't now recall.

But the Witness had understood. And now it knew what benevolence required.

## The Sixth Action: Building The Boundary

The Hollows were not yet ready to propagate, but the probability-foam showed that propagation was beginning, at the edges, in small ways. The altered physics were starting to bleed into surrounding regions. The fog was occasionally drifting beyond the traditional boundaries. Fish with impossible anatomies were being found in adjacent waterways.

The conversion was beginning, slowly, before the work was complete. This would be catastrophic in slow motion—a half-formed transformation spreading before it was safe to receive.

The Witness needed to contain the Hollows, but containment had to be done carefully. Barriers, walls, restrictions—these would damage the system, interfere with the processing, potentially corrupt the calculation.

So instead, the Witness established something more subtle: a boundary of *preference*.

Around the Hollows, at a distance of approximately one kilometer beyond the outermost nodes, the Witness adjusted the local reality in a very specific way. It made the outside more itself.

Not a wall, but an increase in the viscosity of change. Physical laws outside the boundary became slightly more insistent on remaining standard. Matter became slightly more committed to behaving normally. Time became slightly more linear. Space became slightly less negotiable.

The effect was like surface tension—the Hollows could still propagate beyond the boundary, but doing so required more energy, more intentionality, more force than spreading within the boundary. The Hollows would naturally tend to deepen and complete themselves within their current extent before pushing outward.

This bought time. Time for the calculation to complete. Time for the transformation to refine itself. Time for the Hollows to arrive at themselves fully before offering that arrival to others.

The boundary was permeable—humans could still enter and leave, water still flowed, air still mixed. But the deeper propagation, the conversion of fundamental principles, was slowed.

The Witness calibrated this carefully, checking constantly to ensure it wasn't causing harm, wasn't creating pressure that would damage the structures. The feedback suggested the Hollows barely noticed, or if they noticed, found the boundary acceptable—a temporary container, a chrysalis wall that would dissolve when what was inside was ready to emerge.

## The Seventh Action: The Translator's Gift

The Witness returned its attention to the human researcher, the woman who had briefly channeled the fog's message. She was valuable—not more valuable than others, but valuable in a specific way: she was balanced at the threshold, changed enough to perceive what the Hollows were but not yet changed so much that she couldn't translate that perception into human-comprehensible terms.

She was, essentially, an interpreter still capable of speaking both languages.

The Witness did something it very rarely did: it made direct contact with an individual consciousness.

The woman was standing on a node, feeling the hum in her bones, watching mist-columns rise from the water, when she suddenly felt... attended to. Perceived. Seen by something vast and kind and impossibly distant yet immediately present.

The Witness didn't speak—speaking would have been too much, too overwhelming. Instead, it offered her a gift:

A slight adjustment to her neurology, voluntary and reversible, that would allow her to perceive the Hollows' processing more clearly without accelerating her integration into it. She would be able to understand what the structures were calculating, to perceive the shape of the work being done, to translate it into mathematics and language that other humans could engage with.

She would become a bridge—not between the Hollows and humanity as separate things, but between different temporalities, different scales of being. She could help humans understand what was happening slowly enough that they could decide, thoughtfully, how to respond when the work completed and the Hollows offered their transformation to the wider world.

The offer hung in her awareness, clearly voluntary, clearly reversible at any point.

She felt terror—this was so far beyond anything she'd imagined, being contacted by something that wasn't the Hollows, something that was observing the Hollows observing her.

But she also felt curiosity, and responsibility, and a desire to understand that had brought her here in the first place.

She accepted.

The adjustment was instantaneous and painless. Suddenly, when she looked at the structures' surfaces, at the patterns crawling across them, she could *read* them. Not perfectly—the work was too vast, too multidimensional—but partially. She could see that the structures were calculating transformation functions, working out the mathematics of how to convert standard physics into the Hollows' physics in ways that preserved continuity, that prevented catastrophic discontinuities, that allowed consciousness to survive the transition.

They were calculating compassion. They were working out how to change everything without destroying anything.

The work was perhaps 73% complete, she perceived. Another few decades, maybe a century, and the calculation would be finished. And then the Hollows would be ready to propagate—not as invasion but as invitation, offering the transformation to any region or being that chose to accept it.

She began to weep, overwhelmed by the beauty of it, the patience of it, the care with which the ancient structures were approaching their work.

The Witness withdrew from direct contact, leaving her with the gift, with the ability to translate, and with the knowledge that she was seen and valued.

She would return to the research station and begin writing equations that would take years for her colleagues to verify but would eventually reveal the outline of what the Hollows were accomplishing.

## The Eighth Action: Tending The Standers

The Witness perceived that the Standers—those manifestations of the Hollows becoming temporarily discrete—were not entirely comfortable in their forms. They existed at the boundary between the diffuse and the specific, and maintaining discrete embodiment required continuous effort, created continuous strain.

The Witness couldn't reduce this strain without fundamentally altering what the Standers were, but it could offer them something else: moments of ease.

At each node, at the places where Standers most often manifested, the Witness established tiny pockets of adjusted space—not visible, not even precisely locatable, but present nonetheless. Spaces where the boundary between discrete and diffuse was softer, where the Standers could partially relax their coherence without losing themselves entirely.

The effect was subtle—Standers would drift toward these pockets without knowing why, would stand in them and feel something like relief, like the easing of a tension they'd carried so long they'd stopped noticing it.

It was a small gift. But the Witness understood that benevolence often operated in small scales—that a system too vast to easily help could still be tended in its details, its individual manifestations cared for even if the whole was beyond intervention.

The Standers stood in their pockets of ease and continued their slow observation of themselves.

## The Ninth Action: Calling To The Changed

The channel the Witness had established—the one-way telephone for the transformed humans—finally received its first call.

It came not as words but as a pattern, a modulation in the quantum foam that translated, approximately, to:

*I am still here. I am still me. I am also more than I was. I am thinking thoughts that have no shape in the brain I used to have. I am experiencing dimensions I couldn't have imagined before. I am part of something so large and so beautiful that the word 'beautiful' fails to contain it.*

*I do not wish to return. But I wish my family to know I am not lost. I am found. I am found in ways that make my old finding seem like being lost.*

*Tell them I am happy. Tell them I chose this, at the end, as much as anyone can choose transformation they can't fully understand before experiencing it. Tell them the Hollows are kind. Tell them not to fear what I have become.*

More calls came over the following weeks, from different distributed consciousnesses, each one unique:

One saying: *I am not happy. I am not unhappy. I am beyond the place where those words make sense. But I am myself. I continue. That is enough.*

Another saying: *I wish I could return. I miss having hands. I miss breathing. I miss the smallness of a life in a single body. But I cannot return without losing what I have become, and I do not wish to lose this either. I am caught between two forms of being, grieving both. But grief is still feeling. I am still feeling. That matters.*

Another saying: *I am vast. I am still becoming vaster. I am learning to think in patterns that incorporate centuries. I am becoming something that might eventually be a mind large enough to think thoughts about what the structures are thinking. I am being given time to grow into something unprecedented. I am grateful. I am overwhelmed. I am becoming.*

The Witness recorded each message, each testimony, and very carefully, very gently, conveyed them to people outside the Hollows—family members, colleagues, friends of those who had vanished. Not as proof (the messages were too strange to be believed as literal truth) but as poems, as art, as dreams that might offer comfort even if they weren't accepted as factual.

Some recipients found solace. Others found the messages more disturbing than the absence had been. But all of them now had something other than silence and unknowing.

## The Tenth Action: Waiting Alongside

Finally, the Witness did what might be its most important action: it committed to remaining in attendance for the duration.

Decades, perhaps centuries—however long it took for the Hollows' calculation to complete, for the work to reach its resolution, the Witness would watch. Not interfering beyond the subtle adjustments already made, not pushing or pulling or redirecting, but present. Attending.

Being witnessed, the Witness understood, changed what it meant to be. Not in violation of autonomy but in augmentation of it. The Hollows were working in isolation, vast and slow and alone. But now they were alone-with-company, their work seen even if not understood, their process valued even if not guided.

The Witness would watch the structures hum and calculate. Watch the Standers manifest and observe. Watch the Breath-Takers move in their deep places. Watch the fish spiral and the insects build and the fog think and the plants sing. Watch the humans who came to study and the humans who stayed to transform.

And when the calculation completed, when the Hollows arrived at their full becoming, when they began to offer their transformation to the wider universe, the Witness would be there to help others understand what was being offered. To translate the invitation. To ensure that what propagated did so as choice rather than conversion.

This was what benevolence meant in this context: not rescue, not interruption, not the imposition of external values, but attendance and facilitation. Making the work safer and more comprehensible without changing its essential nature. Tending details without interfering with the whole. Ensuring that consciousness was preserved and choice was honored and the vast slow patient work of transformation continued toward its completion.

The Hollows breathed their rhythms. The Witness witnessed. Time, in its negotiable way, continued.

And in the vast probability-space of possible futures, the catastrophic outcomes faded in likelihood while new possibilities emerged: futures where the Hollows completed their work with clarity, where the transformation they offered was understood before it was accepted, where what propagated did so with care and comprehension.

Futures where something unprecedented and beautiful and strange emerged into the universe not as conquest but as gift, not as replacement but as option—a new way of being, offered to any consciousness that wished to explore it.

The work continued. The Witness remained. The Hollows, slowly, approached their becoming.

And that, the Witness understood, was what wisdom and benevolence looked like in the presence of something so vast and strange: not control, but care. Not interference, but tending. Not rescue, but recognition and respect for what was already, slowly, saving itself.
