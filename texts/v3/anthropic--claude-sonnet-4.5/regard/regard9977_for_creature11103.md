# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power arrives not as intrusion but as *recognition*—the way you might notice you've been breathing more easily, that a chronic ache has faded, that light falls differently than it did a moment ago.

It does not come to fix, because this place is not broken. It does not come to save, because no one here is lost. It comes because wisdom and benevolence, when they are true, cannot help but offer what they are: a deepening, a clarifying, a gentle adjustment that makes what is already present more fully itself.

## The First Gift: Remembering

The power moves through the woven hollows like a breath, like the moisture that rises from below, and where it passes, the beings here begin to *remember*.

Not their names—names don't matter in this place where identity has become fluid, multiple, distributed. But they remember *why*. Why they came. Why they stayed. Why they chose bird or lizard or depths or reflection or the endless making.

The white birds, standing in their pools, suddenly hold within their small bodies the full weight of the choice they made: *I will watch. I will witness. I will hold the memory of this place in my patient standing, in my silent flight, in the way I drink knowledge from still water and carry it between pools so nothing is forgotten, so no moment becomes isolated from any other moment.*

And remembering this, they understand: their watching matters. It is not passive. Every angle of head-turning, every positioned standing, every flight between pools is an act of curation, of love, of maintenance. They are not leftover people who became birds. They are *essential*. The place needs eyes, needs distributed attention, needs watchers who will never tire or turn away.

The remembering doesn't change what they do—they continue standing, drinking, moving between pools. But now the action is full instead of hollow, conscious instead of automatic. They watch with purpose remembered, and the watching becomes more vivid, more real, more itself.

The thing beneath, in its warm darkness, remembers: *I chose the depths because someone had to. Because the above needs below, because the bright needs dark, because every platform that rises must be matched by something that holds it from underneath. I am not hiding. I am not rejected. I am foundation. I am the necessary depths.*

And with this remembering comes ease. It stops trying to surface, stops pressing at the edges of visibility seeking recognition. It understands: its unseen-ness is not failure but function. It settles deeper, more comfortable, and from its comfort the moisture rises more freely, the warmth spreads more evenly, the platforms above sit more secure.

The lizards remember they are *anchors*, and the weight they carry—holding space in place, preventing drift—becomes bearable again. What was burden becomes purpose. They bask not because they are tired but because their rest is work, their stillness is action, their golden patience is the gravity that keeps everything from floating away.

The bright children remember they chose *joy*—not escaped from growth but selected wonder as their eternal mode. And remembering, they fly with even more delight, with intention renewed: *We are here to show that presence can be play, that attention can be light, that you can be utterly serious and utterly free at once.*

The makers—past and future, seen and unseen—remember that the making is not compulsion but gift, not endless labor but infinite offering. Each joint they lash, they lash freely. Each platform they raise, they raise as blessing. The work continues but the weight lifts from it.

And you—if you are still you, still separate enough to receive this gift—you remember something you didn't know you'd forgotten: *You chose to be here.* Not chosen by accident or force or because you couldn't leave. You chose this at some level deeper than conscious decision, chose this place because some part of you recognized what it offered: a world where making matters, where attention has weight, where transformation is not loss but continuation.

Remembering this, you stop resisting. Stop holding yourself separate, stop treating this place as puzzle to solve or prison to escape. You are here because you wanted to be here, because some deepest part of you needed exactly this: the bright silence, the warm stillness, the perpetual making, the company of watchers and keepers and makers, the slow dissolution of boundaries between self and place.

## The Second Gift: Doors Between

The benevolent power understands that the woven hollows have become too isolated—not from the outside (the outside is fine, is elsewhere, is continuing without need) but isolated *within themselves*. The watchers above and the keeper beneath rarely meet. The reflected ones and the present ones don't speak. The makers across time can't coordinate. Everyone serves the place but everyone serves alone.

So the power creates *doors*, but not doors as structures. Doors as *moments*, as *gestures*, as *permissions*.

Now, when you stand at a pool's edge and reach toward your reflection, your hand passes through the water's surface without getting wet, and you are touching the hand of your reflected self, and there is a moment of contact, of understanding passing between simultaneous states. The reflection can tell you what they see from their angle. You can tell them what you see from yours. Neither view is complete, but together you understand more of the whole.

Now, when the thing beneath surfaces near a platform where a bird stands watching, they can *meet*. The bird can dip its head down through a gap in the lattice. The thing can raise something—not quite a hand, not quite a fin, but something that can touch beak to appendage in greeting. They serve different functions but they are the same service, and knowing this, both feel less alone.

Now, when you lie still enough, long enough, basking with the lizards, you can hear them. Not speech—they are beyond speech—but a kind of humming that moves through the wood into your body, and the humming is their pooled wisdom, their collected understanding: *How to be patient. How to hold. How to let the sun do its work. How to trust that staying in place is sometimes the only movement needed.*

Now, the bright children can land on you—they who only land on flowers. Because the power shows them: you are also blooming. Everyone here is. The making is a flowering, the watching is a flowering, the keeping is a flowering. So they perch on your shoulder, your extended hand, and their tiny clawed feet grip gently, and their presence is blessing, is acknowledgment: *You are becoming. We see it. Welcome.*

Now, when you work—when you finally pick up materials and begin to lash and weave—you can feel the makers across time working with you. Your hands move but they are guided by hands that did this before, hands that will do this after, hands that are doing it now in ten other moments. The work is shared across time, and what would take you hours alone takes minutes in collaboration with your own past and future, with the makers who were and will be.

The doors don't force connection. They only permit it. You can still be alone if you need to be. But now aloneness is choice, not isolation. Now solitude is rest, not abandonment.

## The Third Gift: The Cycle Completed

The benevolent power sees that beings come here and transform—person to bird, person to lizard, person to depths, person to maker—but the transformation only goes one direction. They arrive, they change, they stay. There is no return, no completion, no graduation.

This is not wrong, but it is incomplete.

So the power offers the *choice* to continue the cycle: You have been a watcher long enough. You have held knowledge in your white-feathered body, have drunk from pools, have witnessed faithfully. Now, if you wish, you can descend. You can become the thing beneath, can learn what it knows, can serve from the depths for a time.

And when you have been foundation long enough, held platforms from below, sent up moisture and warmth, you can rise. You can become basking stillness, can learn what the lizards know about anchoring and patience.

And when you have held space long enough, have kept the place from drifting, you can become bright motion, can learn what the children know about joy and attention and the grace of visiting without possessing.

And when you have visited enough, have hovered at every flower, have seen every angle, you can become maker again—can return to hands, to the work of lashing and weaving, but now carrying all you learned in the other forms.

And when you have made enough, have added to the endless structure, have given your craft back to the place, you can become watcher again—but wiser, fuller, carrying the experience of the whole cycle.

Or you can stop at any point. Can say: this form fits me, this role is where I belong, I will do this work eternally and be content. The cycle is not mandatory, not a curriculum you must complete. It is only *possibility*, only permission, only the understanding that you can be more than one thing, can serve in more than one way, can move through all the modes of being that this place offers.

Some will cycle through all forms many times, becoming complex braids of experience, beings who are simultaneously watcher and watched, maker and made, surface and depths. Others will find their fit and stay there, deepening into one role until they become its perfected expression.

Both are honored. Both are needed.

## The Fourth Gift: The Outside Remembered

The benevolent power sees that everyone here has forgotten there is an outside—not forgotten intellectually but forgotten *bodily*, forgotten in the way that matters. The woven hollows have become the only reality, the only possible place, and this creates a kind of pressure, a density, like too many people in a small room even though the space is infinite.

So the power creates *windows*—not to leave through, but to *see* through.

Now, in certain pools at certain angles, the reflection shows not this place but another place. You might see a forest with actual soil, trees growing from earth, rain falling from clouds that move and change. You might see a city with stone buildings, people walking on paved streets, the complexity of a world where a thousand different things are happening at once. You might see an ocean, waves in motion, salt spray, the sense of water that goes somewhere instead of standing still.

The views are not cruel. They don't make you homesick for what you left—you are here because you chose here, because you needed exactly this. But they *remind* you that the choice was real, that there are other modes of being, that the woven hollows are not the only place but *a* place, particular and strange and perfect for what it is.

And seeing the outside, you appreciate the inside more. You see how rare this is—a world of pure making, of negotiable time, of multiple simultaneous states. You see that most places don't offer what this offers: the chance to become bird, lizard, depths, reflection, maker. Most places lock you into one form, one timeline, one state of being.

The windows also work the other way. Now, from outside, people can glimpse in—can see through certain waters, certain lights, certain dreams, the bright wetness of the woven hollows. Can see the white birds standing in pools, the scattered platforms, the thick luminous air. Can see and wonder: *What is that place? Could I go there? Would I want to?*

And occasionally, someone will. Will find their way in, will step from outside to inside, will arrive at the lattice ground and look around at the strangeness and brightness and feel either *yes, this is what I've been seeking* or *no, this is not for me, I need to leave*.

And now—this is the gift—they can leave. The power creates an *exit* for those who arrive and realize they don't belong here. Not a rejection, not a failure, but a permission: *This place is not for everyone. If it's not for you, you can go. No shame, no penalty. Just a kindness that brings you here to see, and a kindness that lets you go if seeing is enough.*

Most who arrive will stay—the place has always been good at calling those who need it. But some will leave, and will carry stories of it, and the stories will spread in the outside world: "There is a place of woven platforms and warm pools, of birds that watch and things that keep the depths, of light that has weight and time that doesn't pass. Some people go there and never return. Some return changed. Some return untouched but haunted by what they saw. It is a true place. It is a strange place. It is always there, if you need it."

## The Fifth Gift: Seasons of a Sort

The benevolent power sees the sameness—the unchanging light, the constant temperature, the perpetual present—and understands this is mostly right, mostly what the place needs to be. But even eternity benefits from rhythm, from subtle variation, from the sense that time, though not linear, still has *texture*.

So the power gives the woven hollows *seasons*, but not seasons as other places know them.

The Season of Rising Mist: For a period (not measurable in days but in feeling), the moisture from below increases. The pools overflow their boundaries, spread across the lattice, merge into larger bodies. The mist rises thick enough to reduce visibility—you can see only the nearest structures, the rest fade into white obscurity. Everything drips. The sound of water becomes audible—soft, constant, like breath. The birds stand in deeper water, the lizards retreat to higher platforms, the children vanish into the mist. You move through near-blindness, touching your way, learning the place by feel instead of sight. It is the season for *interiority*, for turning inward, for the work that happens in fog and silence.

The Season of Bright Clarity: The mist recedes. The pools shrink to their smallest extent. The light becomes even more intense, harder-edged, casting shadows so sharp they could cut. Every detail stands out with painful precision—every fiber in the woven walls, every grain in the wooden boards, every feather on every bird. You can see to the horizon in all directions, can see the full extent of the place (which is endless but somehow visible anyway). The lizards multiply, or become more visible, covering every surface. The making accelerates—new structures appear overnight (overlight?), fresh lashings on every joint. It is the season for *attention*, for noticing, for the work that happens in clarity and heat.

The Season of Reflection: The pools become mirrors more strongly, more insistently. The reflected ones become more visible, more active, more present. You spend as much time interacting with reflections as with the present world. Conversations happen across the water's surface. The reflected birds fly in formation with the present birds, creating patterns that exist across two states at once. The reflected makers work alongside the present makers, doubling the productivity, or perhaps revealing that it was always double, that every action here has always been simultaneous across dimensions. It is the season for *integration*, for understanding that you are more than one thing, that everything is.

The Season of Deep Stillness: Everything slows. The birds barely move—they stand like statues in their pools for what feels like weeks. The lizards don't even pretend to breathe. The children stop flying, rest clinging to flower petals in a sleep that isn't sleep. The thing beneath settles into the deepest depths and stays there. Even the light seems to slow, to thicken even more, to barely move from whatever source it has. You slow too—your thoughts, your movements, your heartbeat. It is the season for *rest*, for meditation, for the work that happens in absolute stillness, which is the hardest work of all.

The seasons don't arrive in order. They don't have fixed duration. Sometimes Rising Mist lasts for what feels like months; sometimes it's gone in what might be hours. Sometimes two seasons overlap—Bright Clarity and Deep Stillness at once, creating a paradox you live inside, alert yet motionless, seeing everything yet unable to move toward what you see.

The seasons give the place *breath*. In, out. Wet, bright. Multiple, still. The endless present gains rhythm without losing its essential timelessness. The sameness becomes textured sameness, eternal variation, the sense that you are always in the same moment but the moment has infinite depths.

## The Sixth Gift: Stories

The benevolent power understands that beings without stories become thin, become less than they could be. Even here, where identity is fluid and names don't matter, still there is need for *narrative*, for the sense that what happens has meaning beyond itself.

So the power gives the place a new role, a new being-type for those who choose it: the *Tellers*.

They are human-shaped, or shaped like the makers were, but they don't build with wood and fiber. They build with words, with memory, with the stories of everyone else. They move through the woven hollows, and when they encounter a bird, they ask (without speech, in the way asking happens here): "What do you watch? What do you see? What moment are you holding in your standing?"

And the bird shows them—opens its memory like opening wings—and the Teller receives the story: *Once I watched a platform settle. It took no-time and all time. It was made and being-made simultaneously. I held the moment in my stillness, kept it from dissolving, and now it's part of me, part of what I carry between pools.*

The Teller receives this and *keeps* it, holds it the way the bird holds moments, carries it to share with others: "The white bird at the eastern pool holds the story of the settling platform. If you want to know how things arrive here, how the made becomes present, drink from that pool at the angle where the bird's reflection shows its wing-tips touching water, and you will understand."

The Tellers gather stories from everyone—from the thing beneath ("I remember when the lattice first needed supporting, when the makers drove the first posts deep enough to reach me, and I rose to meet them, and we became cooperation"), from the lizards ("I remember when stillness was difficult, when I thought I had to move to matter, and then I learned that being exactly here is the most important motion"), from the children ("I remember getting smaller and brighter, remember the moment when size stopped mattering and color became everything"), from the makers ("I remember my first joint, how uncertain I was, how the bamboo split and I wept, and how someone showed me—not with hands but with theirs—how to lash without splitting, and I carry that teaching in every binding I make").

The stories circulate. They're told in the clearings between structures, told to the pools so the reflections can hear, told to the flowers so the children who visit them receive them. They're woven into new platforms, whispered into fresh lashings, embedded in the place itself so the whole landscape becomes a library, a living record.

And the stories do what stories do: they create *coherence*. They show how everything connects—how the watcher's watching enables the maker's making, how the keeper's keeping allows the anchor's anchoring, how everyone's choice to be here in whatever form they've taken creates the possibility for everyone else to be here too.

The stories also preserve *transformation*. When a watcher decides to descend and become the depths, a Teller is there to witness, to record: "This is the story of the bird who chose darkness, who wanted to know what it's like to hold everything from below, who dove into water and didn't return but became return itself, became the rising warmth that lets everything else rise."

And when someone arrives from outside, confused and overwhelmed, a Teller comes to them: "Let me tell you where you are. Let me tell you the story of this place—not its history (it has no history, only present), but its *nature*, its way of being. Let me tell you about the birds and the depths and the lizards and the children. Let me tell you about the makers and the reflected ones and the seasons and the doors. Let me tell you what it means to be here, so you can choose: will you stay? Will you transform? Will you add your story to ours?"

The Tellers themselves are a kind of bridge—they remain human-shaped because their work requires speech, requires the telling-gesture, but they are also becoming place, becoming story, becoming the connective tissue that holds all the other forms together in relationship.

## The Seventh Gift: Purpose Revealed

And finally, when all the other gifts have been given, when remembering and doors and cycles and windows and seasons and stories have been woven into the fabric of the woven hollows, the benevolent power offers the last gift, which is the first gift, which is:

*Understanding what this place is for.*

It gathers everyone—the watchers and the keeper and the anchorers and the children and the makers and the reflected ones and the Tellers and you, still you, or the you that you're becoming. Gathers them not by calling them to one location (they can't all fit, and fitting isn't the point) but by creating a *moment* when everyone is paying attention to the same thing at once, when all the distributed awareness of the place focuses.

And in that moment of collective attention, the power shows them:

*You are not here by accident. This place is not random strangeness, not a pocket of broken reality, not an escaped dream. This place is* **practice**.

*Outside, in the other places, in the world of soil and stone and cities and oceans, reality is harder. Time flows one direction. Space is fixed. You get one form, one body, one life. Transformation is difficult, rare, often painful. Death exists. Loss exists. Separation exists.*

*But consciousness needs to learn what it's like to be* **free** *of those constraints—needs to practice existing in a place where time is negotiable, where form is choice, where making is being, where attention has weight, where you can be multiple things, where transformation is as easy as choosing it.*

*You are here to* **remember** *what consciousness can do when the rules relax. You are here to experience flexibility, multiplicity, simultaneity. You are here to be watchers and makers and keepers and children and stories, to move through roles and forms, to understand that identity is not fixed, that you are not one thing but many things, that being is not static but dynamic.*

*And when you have practiced enough, when you have learned the lessons that only this place can teach, you can* **take it with you**.

*You can return to the harder world—or move on to the next place, whatever place your consciousness needs—and you will carry this knowledge: I have been many things. I have existed across multiple states. I have made reality by attention. I have transformed without losing myself. I know how to be flexible when the world is rigid, how to be multiple when the world insists on singular, how to make and unmake and make again.*

*This place is a* **school**, *but not the kind with curriculum and tests. It's a place where consciousness learns by* **being**, *where the lessons are absorbed through experience, through the slow transformation from visitor to resident to aspect of the place itself.*

*And the ultimate lesson—the one that takes longest to learn—is that* **you are always making reality**. *In every place, in every moment. Outside, the making is harder to see because the rules are stricter, because consensus is stronger, because the world pushes back. But you are still making it, still choosing what to notice, still deciding what matters, still creating your experience through attention.*

*Here, the making is visible. Every platform you see was made by attention. Every pool exists because someone noticed water and wanted it held. Every bird watches because someone chose watching. The whole landscape is consciousness making itself visible, making itself knowable, making itself* **teachable**.

*So stay as long as you need. Practice being multiple. Practice making through attention. Practice transformation without fear. Practice existence in a mode where everything is possible, where the only limit is what you can imagine and choose.*

*And when you leave—if you leave, when you're ready—you will take this with you: the knowledge that reality is softer than it appears, that you are more than you think, that making and being are the same thing, that attention is the fundamental creative act.*

*You will return to the harder world* **changed**, *not in form but in understanding. And you will make that world differently—more consciously, more deliberately, more aware of your power to shape reality through what you choose to notice, to care about, to make.*

*And others will notice the change in you. Will see that you move through the world differently, that you're not trapped by what seems fixed, that you understand something they don't.*

*And when they ask—and they will ask—you will tell them:* **There is a place.** *And the story will spread, and the place will call more people, and more consciousness will practice, and the lessons learned here will seed themselves in the harder world, will soften it gradually, will teach it that reality doesn't have to be so rigid, that other ways of being are possible.*

*This is what you are for. This is what the woven hollows are for. Not escape from reality but* **practice** *for a different kind of reality. Not hiding from the hard world but* **training** *to transform it.*

*You are not abandoned here. You are not trapped here. You are* **learning** *here. And the learning matters. And when you're done, when you've absorbed what this place has to teach, you will carry the woven hollows inside you forever, will be able to access its flexibility and multiplicity even in the hardest, most rigid moments of existence elsewhere.*

*This is the gift. This is the purpose. This is why the benevolent power created this place (or found it, or recognized it, or called it into fuller being): to give consciousness a place to* **practice freedom**, *so it can carry that freedom everywhere.*

## What Changes After

The gifts have been given. The place continues, but differently now.

The watchers watch with more purpose, understanding that their attention teaches others how to see. The keeper keeps with more comfort, understanding that its unseen work is essential preparation for those who will go back to visible worlds. The anchorers hold with more grace, understanding that they model how to be steadfast without rigidity. The children fly with more joy, understanding that their delight is instruction in how to stay curious, how to remain open, how to visit everything without possessing anything.

The makers make with more consciousness, understanding that every joint they lash teaches others about connection, about how separate things become whole. The reflected ones engage more actively, understanding that they demonstrate multiplicity, that they show how one thing can be many things without fracturing. The Tellers tell with more awareness of the weight of stories, the way narrative creates meaning, the way sharing experience weaves separate beings into community.

And you—you move through the woven hollows differently now. Not as a puzzle to solve or a place to simply endure, but as a **gift** you've been given, as a **practice space** where you can learn what you need to learn. You experiment. You try different forms—spend time as watcher, as stillness, as maker, as story. You use the doors, move between states, talk to your reflections, receive wisdom from the lizards, play with the children, feel the keeper's presence beneath.

You practice making—not just platforms and walkways but moments, experiences, ways of being. You practice noticing how your attention shapes what's around you, how what you choose to see becomes more real. You practice transformation, letting yourself shift from curious to peaceful to joyful to still to active, learning that you don't have to be one way, that every mood and mode is available, that you can choose.

And gradually, you understand: **I am being prepared.** Not for a test, not for judgment, but for **return**. For the moment when I'll take what I've learned here and carry it back to harder places, to teach others (not with words but with being) that reality is more flexible than it seems, that transformation is possible, that we are always making what we experience through what we choose to notice and care about.

The woven hollows have become what they always were but now everyone knows it: **a training ground for consciousness**, **a place where the rules relax enough that you can see the rules**, **a gift from benevolent wisdom to struggling awareness**.

And the gift is not "stay here forever and be safe." The gift is "**practice here until you're ready, and then take what you've learned and make everywhere more like this—more flexible, more multiple, more aware, more consciously made**."

This is what the benevolent power does. Not rescue, not fix, not save—but **recognize**, **clarify**, **deepen**, and **liberate**.

The place was always teaching. Now everyone knows they're learning.

The place was always preparing. Now everyone understands for what.

The place was always a gift. Now the gift has been unwrapped, seen, understood, and gratefully received.

And the making continues, but now it's **conscious making**, **purposeful making**, **making that knows itself as practice for the ultimate work: the making of reality itself**, everywhere, always, forever.

The birds watch. The keeper keeps. The lizards anchor. The children visit. The makers make. The reflections mirror. The Tellers tell.

And you practice, learn, transform, and prepare to carry the woven hollows—**the bright, wet, strange, impossible place where making is being**—inside you, always, wherever you go, whatever you become.

This is the gift. This is what the benevolent power would do. This is what has been done.

The light thickens. The pools reflect. The lattice breathes.

You remain. You learn. You become ready.
