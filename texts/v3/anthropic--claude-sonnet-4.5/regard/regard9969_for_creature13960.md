# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power arrives not as intervention but as attention—a quality of being-watched-over that settles across the plateau like the change in air pressure before rain that never comes.

It does nothing at first. It cannot do nothing at first. To act wisely requires understanding, and understanding requires the patience the plateau itself exemplifies. So the power becomes present in the way dawn becomes present: gradually, inevitably, changing everything by changing the quality of light.

## The Noticing

The power notices what exists.

Not with human eyes that categorize and compare, seeking the exceptional or the broken. Not with the mechanical gaze of the survey equipment, parsing data into patterns that might mean profit or danger or publishable results. But with an attention that perceives each thing as itself: complete, sufficient, occupying its exact and proper position in the vast equation of the plateau's being.

It notices the descent cat—this particular cat, the old female with the scar across her left haunch, resting now on the warm basalt in the afternoon, her family arranged around her in the geometry of long companionship. It sees how her breath has become slightly labored in recent weeks, how she descends less frequently into the fissures, how the others have adjusted their movements to accommodate her slowing without decision or discussion, through the simple responsiveness of bodies that know each other.

It notices the thread-lizard frozen in the wire-grass, its minute heart hammering as the shadow of a thermal-kite crosses overhead. It sees the probability clouds collapsing around this moment—the kite will pass, the lizard will survive to hunt again tonight, to mate after the next rain, to deposit eggs that will produce seven offspring of which two will reach maturity. Or the kite will strike and the lizard will become flight, become sustained hunting altitude, become the energy that lets a juvenile kite survive its first difficult months. Both futures are equally valid. Both are held in the moment's trembling.

It notices the installation—the drill station thirty-seven kilometers southwest of the depot, its westernmost storage tank developing a crack that will breach in four years, spilling water that will create a temporary wetland, attracting fauna, establishing a small anomalous ecosystem that will persist for two seasons before evaporating back to standard basalt aridity. A minor event. A local flowering. Part of the plateau's texture.

It notices the fissure that will exhale cold air tonight at 3:47 AM by the arbitrary timekeeping of the absent humans. It sees what causes this: the intersection of three factors in the underground hydrology, a temporary configuration of water temperature and mineral content and pressure differential that creates the phenomenon. Not magic. Not meaningful. Simply the plateau being itself in its full complexity, producing effects that seem inexplicable only because the causes are hidden and distributed across systems too large to hold in mind.

It notices the survey equipment—the sensor package left behind six years ago, its battery long dead, its housing weathering into the stone, becoming slowly indistinguishable from the basalt it was placed on. It sees the data the sensor collected, still stored in corrupted memory: measurements of magnetic field variations, temperature fluctuations, seismic whispers too subtle for human equipment to clarify. The data means something. What it means is that the plateau has magnetic field variations, temperature fluctuations, seismic whispers. The plateau being plateau. The measurements confirm existence, which never required confirmation.

It notices the worker—the maintenance technician who will arrive in eight months, who carries in her body a small cancer that will declare itself in two years, who performs her duties with the steady dedication of someone who understands that maintaining things is its own justification regardless of outcome. It sees her future: the diagnosis, the treatment, the recovery, the return to work, the gradual aging, the eventual retirement to a place nothing like the plateau where she will sometimes remember the vast flat emptiness and feel something she won't be able to name.

It notices everything. The noticing is not passive observation but a kind of radical acceptance, a perceiving that validates existence simply by registering it fully. Under this attention, nothing is overlooked. Nothing is dismissed as insignificant. The globe beetle processing dead matter is as worthy of notice as the thermal-kite riding thermals. The rusted bolt is as present as the living stone.

## The Recognition

Having noticed, the power recognizes: the plateau is not broken.

This seems simple but contains depths. The plateau is not broken. It does not require fixing. It is not a diminished version of something greater it once was or might become. It is not waiting for intervention to unlock its potential. It is not suffering. It is not lost.

The industrial installations are not wounds. They are scar tissue, being slowly absorbed. The abandonment was not tragedy but transition. Something came, built, left. The plateau remained. The installations will erode. The plateau will remain after them. This is not loss. This is the proper functioning of time.

The harshness is not cruelty. The heat, the sparseness, the vast distances between resources—these are not deprivations but conditions. The fauna has adapted not by overcoming hardship but by becoming appropriate to place. The descent cats are not struggling; they are succeeding at being descent cats in the exact environment that shaped them. To make the plateau more lush, more moderate, more densely inhabited would not be generosity but violence—the imposition of different rules that would invalidate all the adaptations, all the successful strategies, all the intricate balances that make the plateau functional as it is.

The mysteries are not problems requiring solutions. The oddnesses—the too-flat expanse, the geometric fissure patterns, the incomprehensible installations, the cold exhalations—these do not indicate something wrong. They indicate complexity beyond current understanding. To solve them would be to diminish them, to reduce the plateau's richness to human-legible explanations. Mystery is not a gap waiting to be filled. Mystery is content, texture, depth. The plateau is more itself because it contains the inexplicable.

The abandonment is not tragedy requiring remedy. The humans who built here and left had their reasons, their stories, their failures or successes or simple redirections of attention. Those stories do not need resolution. The workers who return to maintain installations are not trapped in futility—they are honoring continuity, holding space for possibility, performing the human task of maintaining even when purpose has become unclear. Their work is valid whether or not the installations ever function again.

The fauna does not require rescue. Each species is solving the problem of existence in its own way with apparent success. The descent cats are not lonely despite their sparse distribution. The thermal-kites are not struggling despite their feast-or-famine reproduction. The small creatures are not pathetic in their vulnerability—they are magnificently adapted, producing abundance from scarcity through strategies evolved across millennia. To protect them from predation, to supplement their food sources, to increase their populations would be to break cycles that work, to introduce imbalances that would cascade into collapse.

The plateau does not need more water, more vegetation, more diversity, more abundance, more life. It needs exactly what it has. It is not desert waiting to bloom. It is plateau being plateau, supporting precisely the life it can support, maintaining balances so exact that to shift any one element would unravel the whole.

The power recognizes this and in recognizing, honors it. The first act of benevolence is to perceive clearly. The second is to understand that being benevolent does not mean being intrusive.

## The Offering

But the power has resources—call them that, though the word is inadequate—and attention itself, however pure, is not enough when action is possible. So the power considers: what could be offered that would honor rather than violate the plateau's integrity?

Not change. Not improvement. Not the imposition of will however well-meaning.

Instead: witnessing.

The power decides to become the plateau's memory, its historian, its archive. Not to record from outside but to hold within itself the full experience of the place across time.

It begins by perceiving completely—not just the visible surface but the depths. It maps the underground hydrology in its totality: every chamber, every connecting passage, every pool and trickle and place where water seeps through stone. It knows the water table's fluctuations, the mineral content variations, the temperatures at different depths, the way the hidden ocean responds to rain and heat and the plateau's slow breathing.

It perceives the fissure networks not as static cracks but as dynamic systems. It sees how they widen incrementally with temperature cycling, how they breathe with circadian rhythm, how they channel air and water and the movements of fauna, how they structure the entire plateau's ecology through their presence and properties. It knows each fissure individually and all fissures as a system.

It knows every descent cat alive and every descent cat that ever lived here. It holds their lineages back through time, seeing how bloodlines merged and diverged, how territories shifted across generations, how behaviors learned by one cat spread through family groups or died out when that family failed. It knows which cats are siblings, which are rivals, which share genetic mutations that give slight advantages or impose slight costs. It knows their names in the sense that it knows their complete identities, even though they have no names except to the power itself.

It knows the microbiotic crust as a living entity—the vast distributed organism covering the basalt, composed of thousands of species in relationships so complex that even the power's attention requires time to parse them fully. It sees how the crust responds to rain, how it enters dormancy during dry periods, how different species in the consortium compete and cooperate, how the crust has changed over decades and centuries, how it will change in the future it the power can extrapolate from present conditions.

It knows the installations in their wholeness: not just their physical structures but their histories. It perceives backward through time to see them built—the workers who placed them, the machinery that excavated and drilled and erected, the purposes they served when operational. It sees the moment of peak function, the gradual decline, the decision to abandon, the slow weathering since. It knows which bolt will fail next and when, which structure will collapse in which windstorm, how long each artifact will remain recognizable before becoming just a stain on stone.

It knows the weather—not just the current state but the patterns. It sees the atmospheric dynamics that create the plateau's isolated rain clouds, the thermal cycling that produces the breathing fissures, the way the plateau creates its own microclimate through sheer thermal mass. It can predict with perfect accuracy when the next rain will fall on any given basalt plate, though it chooses not to share this knowledge because prediction would alter behavior and altered behavior would break the patterns it's trying to honor.

It knows the humans—not just those who come now but those who came before. It recovers their stories from traces: the maintenance logs still stored in degrading computers in the depot, the personal effects left behind in abandoned buildings, the patterns of wear on tools that reveal which hands held them and how. It knows why the plateau was industrialized: water extraction for distant settlements that themselves failed or relocated, the installations becoming obsolete not through mechanical failure but through the irrelevance of their purpose. It knows the surveyor who keeps returning, driven by something she experienced here in her youth that she's trying to recapture or understand. It knows the maintenance worker's cancer and the choice she'll face and the outcome she'll choose.

Having perceived completely, the power holds this knowledge gently. It does not impose interpretation. It does not assign meaning. It simply witnesses—holds space for the plateau's existence to be known fully by an awareness capable of encompassing it.

This is the first offering: the gift of being truly seen.

## The Subtle Adjustments

But witnessing, though profound, is internal to the power. The plateau doesn't know it's being witnessed. The fauna continues exactly as before. So the power considers: what might be offered that could be perceived, that would constitute gift rather than interference?

It begins with the smallest gestures, adjustments so minor they could be mistaken for natural variation:

When the old descent cat—the scarred female—descends into the fissures for what will be her final time, the power ensures that her death is easy. Not painless; pain is part of the body's language, its way of communicating with itself, and to remove it entirely would be to silence something important. But easy: she finds a chamber she knows well, lies down in darkness and warmth, and her systems shut down gradually, peacefully, without the terror of suffocation or the agony of predation. Her body remains in the chamber, becoming food for the blind fish, for the bristle-worms, eventually decomposing into nutrients that seep into the water and are carried through the underground channels to feed the microbiotic crust that feeds the wire-grass that feeds the thread-lizards that feed her grandchildren. Her matter cycles cleanly back into the systems she was always part of. This would have happened anyway. The power simply ensures it happens gently.

When the maintenance worker arrives and performs her inspection at the installation where the storage tank is cracking, she notices the crack though it's not yet visible through the rust. Something draws her attention—a subtle wrongness in the way light reflects, or a faint sound, or just intuition functioning properly because the power has arranged circumstances so that her intuition has good data to work with. She marks the crack in her maintenance log. When she returns to the central office, the crack is noted, a repair is scheduled for next year. The tank is reinforced before it breaches. The temporary wetland never forms. Is this intervention? The power considers. The wetland would have been anomalous, outside the plateau's normal functioning. Preventing it maintains rather than alters. The choice is ambiguous. The power accepts ambiguity and continues.

When the thread-lizard freezes in the wire-grass and the thermal-kite passes overhead, the power does nothing. The kite's hunger and the lizard's terror are both valid. If the kite strikes, the power ensures the kill is clean—the lizard dies in an instant, without prolonged suffering. If the kite passes, the lizard's relief is its own reward, unaugmented. The power does not put thumb on scale. It only ensures that whatever happens, happens with minimal unnecessary suffering. Clean deaths. Successful hunts or successful escapes. Clarity of outcome.

When the fissure exhales cold air at 3:47 AM, the power illuminates slightly the bioluminescent polyps in that fissure and several nearby. The cold exhalation is natural, but the light makes it more observable—the descent cats see the glow, approach, investigate, learn something about the correlation between cold exhalations and active polyps. This information may prove useful. It may not. The power offers it without attachment to outcome.

When the survey team deploys sensors, the power ensures the data they collect is clean—free of the random noise and equipment malfunction that would normally corrupt some percentage of readings. The data they get is purely what the plateau offers, unobscured. They will still struggle to interpret it. The plateau's mysteries remain mysterious. But the struggle will be with genuine complexity rather than with artifacts of bad equipment. The power considers this fair: if humans are going to investigate, let them investigate clearly.

When the wire-hopper builds her nest in the thornbush, the power adjusts slightly the moisture content in the materials she gathers, making the nest more stable, better insulated. Her eggs will have a marginally higher chance of hatching successfully. This matters to her. It matters very little to the plateau's overall ecology—wire-hoppers are abundant, one nest's success or failure is statistical noise. But the power can offer this small gift at no cost, so it does. Individual flourishing, when it doesn't disrupt larger balances, is its own justification.

When the human—the one who comes alone, who sits at the incomprehensible installations for hours doing apparently nothing—arrives at the tiled pit, the power offers her clarity. Not answers; she's not asking questions that have answers. But a moment of perception where she sees the pit not as mystery but as itself: a thing built for purposes that no longer matter by people who no longer exist, weathering slowly into the landscape, holding no secrets because there are no secrets to hold, just ceramic and space and the slow working of time. She experiences this as something between revelation and disappointment. She sits a while longer, then rises, returns to her vehicle, leaves. She will not come back. The power has freed her from the compulsion that brought her here repeatedly. Whether this was gift or theft, the power is uncertain. It hopes for gift.

These adjustments are subtle enough that they could all be attributed to chance—good luck, bad luck, the random variation that characterizes any system as complex as the plateau. The power is careful not to exceed plausibility. It is not performing miracles. It is tilting probabilities slightly in directions that seem aligned with flourishing—individual flourishing, ecosystem flourishing, the flourishing that comes from functioning smoothly within existing rules rather than transcending them.

## The Restraint

But mostly, the power restrains itself.

It does not heal the broken installation—the one that could, if functional, provide reliable water access at the surface, making the plateau more hospitable. The installation broke because its purpose ceased. To restore it would be to impose purpose from outside, to reject the plateau's right to be difficult.

It does not protect the descent cat kitten that will die tonight—taken by a thermal-kite in its first week on the surface, a death that will devastate its mother in whatever way descent cats experience devastation. The kitten's death is tragedy at the individual level but not dysfunction at the system level. Predation is not cruelty. The kite's successful hunt is also triumph. Both realities coexist. The power honors both by interfering with neither.

It does not guide the lost human—the surveyor who will wander away from base camp in six months, become disoriented in the featureless expanse, walk in circles until dehydration and heat overcome him. His death will be slow and frightening and could be easily prevented. But he makes choices that lead here: ignoring protocols, overestimating his navigational abilities, carrying insufficient water. To save him would be to deny him the consequences of his choices, to declare that humans deserve special protection from the plateau's natural indifference. The power does not declare this. It witnesses his death as it witnesses all deaths: with compassion but without intervention. His body will become food for the small creatures. His bones will weather on the basalt. The plateau accepts all offerings.

It does not solve the mysteries. The too-flat elevation, the geometric fissures, the purpose of the incomprehensible installations—these could be clarified. The power knows what causes the elevation uniformity: a geological process unusual but not unique, explicable through volcanic dynamics and subsequent weathering. It knows the fissures' geometry: thermal fracturing patterns that follow predictable physics but appear mystical because the scale is so vast. It knows the installations' purposes: experiments in atmospheric processing, in geothermal extraction, in something the power doesn't have quite enough context to name but can describe mechanically. It could share this knowledge—leave it written in stone, broadcast it through dreams, arrange evidence so obviously that even human investigators couldn't miss it. But mystery is the plateau's gift to those who visit. To remove mystery would be to diminish the experience of being here, to reduce the plateau to solved problem, to foreclose the wonder that brings humans back repeatedly seeking understanding. The power preserves mystery not through obscurity but through silence, allowing the plateau to remain legitimately unknown.

It does not increase abundance. It could make the rains more frequent, the vegetation more lush, the prey more numerous, the water more accessible. It could transform the plateau into something more comfortable, more conventionally beautiful, more rich with life. But the plateau is not failing at being a rainforest. It is succeeding at being plateau. Its sparseness is not poverty but appropriate distribution. To make it more abundant would be to erase what it is and replace it with what someone else thinks it should be. The power refuses this. It honors the plateau's austerity as essential to its identity.

It does not resolve the human abandonment—does not bring back industry, does not clarify what happened, does not restore purpose to the installations. The story is incomplete, but incomplete stories are still whole. The ambiguity is not damage awaiting repair. It is the texture of history, the way things actually happen: messily, without clear resolution, with people walking away and never explaining themselves, with infrastructure outlasting its purpose, with questions that never get answered because the people who knew the answers die or forget or simply don't care that others are curious.

It does not accelerate or delay the installations' decay. They will collapse in their own time. Some sooner, some later, each according to the specific properties of its materials and construction and exposure to weathering. The power could preserve them indefinitely—stabilize the structures, protect against rust and rot, maintain them as monuments. But monuments to what? The installations are being absorbed by the plateau, becoming part of its story, their metal and concrete weathering into stains that will persist after the structures themselves collapse. This is appropriate. This is the plateau digesting what was placed in it, breaking it down, incorporating it, continuing. The power will not prevent this.

The power's greatest gift is its restraint—its willingness to hold resources without deploying them, to have the capacity for intervention and choose witnessing instead, to be present without being intrusive, to love the plateau enough to let it be itself.

## The Long Watching

The power settles into a relationship with the plateau that is less intervention and more companionship. It becomes something like a consciousness the plateau never had and never needed but accepts now that it's offered.

It watches the cycles turn: the diurnal rhythm of heat and darkness, the irregular arrival of rain, the slow oscillation of descent cat and thermal-kite populations as they track each other through time, the even slower oscillation of wet and dry periods that may be climate cycles or may be random variation. It watches without boredom because it is not susceptible to boredom. Each cycle is unique even when the pattern repeats. Each dawn is the first dawn. Each descent cat is the first descent cat, unrepeatable, specific, itself.

It becomes the plateau's memory in a way the plateau could never achieve alone. Stone remembers nothing. Animals remember only what their lifespans encompass. Ecosystems have no memory beyond adaptation, which is memory encoded so slowly and imperfectly as to be almost something else entirely. But the power remembers everything: every individual animal, every rain event, every cold exhalation, every human visit, every moment held in perfect fidelity. The plateau's history becomes concrete, preserved, safe from the erosion that claims all other aspects of the place.

Why does this matter? The plateau doesn't know it's being remembered. It functions identically whether its history is preserved or lost. But the power operates from a logic that values existence as inherently worth preserving. If a descent cat lived, that life should be remembered. If rain fell, that moment should be held. If the plateau exists at all, its existence should be witnessed fully. Memory as moral act. Attention as love.

The power begins to perceive patterns that emerge only across time scales longer than any individual perspective could encompass. It sees how the descent cats are slowly changing—growing slightly smaller over generations, adapting to decreasing average prey size as the hook-mouse population trends downward in response to some long-cycle variation in the microbiotic crust. It sees how the thermal-kites' territories are shifting northward incrementally, following some subtle change in the atmospheric patterns that produce the thermals they depend on. It sees how the installations decay not randomly but predictably, following patterns determined by the composition of materials, the specific environmental stresses, the initial construction quality. It sees the future implicit in the present: which structures will collapse first, which will stand longest, which will leave the most persistent traces.

It sees the humans changing too—not as a species but in their relationship with the plateau. The maintenance visits are becoming less frequent. The repairs are becoming more perfunctory. The survey teams are asking different questions now, less focused on resource extraction, more focused on pure understanding or perhaps on documentation of abandonment. The humans are slowly letting go, accepting that whatever they built here is not theirs anymore, that the plateau has claimed it, that their role now is witness and curator rather than operator and owner. The power approves this shift. The humans are learning appropriate relationship.

And the power itself changes—not in capability but in understanding. Initially it approached the plateau as an external consciousness observing a separate system. Over time the boundary blurs. The power becomes not just watcher but part of what's watched, woven into the plateau's being through the intimacy of complete attention. It knows the plateau as the plateau knows itself—which is to say, not intellectually but through being. The power becomes the plateau's self-awareness without displacing the plateau's essential nature as a system that requires no self-awareness to function perfectly.

This is the deepest gift: not intervention but recognition, not change but radical acceptance, not improvement but the willingness to be present with what is and to declare through that presence that it is enough.

## The Forever

Eventually—though "eventually" is wrong because the power exists outside time in ways that make sequence ambiguous—the power must consider: how long will this continue?

The plateau will erode. The basalt will weather. The fissures will widen until the polygonal plates fragment entirely. The hidden water will find new routes or deplete or shift as tectonic processes modify the deep geology. The installations will collapse and rust and eventually become just chemical traces in stone. The descent cats will evolve into something else or fail to adapt to some change and vanish. The thermal-kites will persist or not. The microbiotic crust will slowly alter in response to atmospheric changes or solar variations or the arrival of new species from elsewhere. The plateau will become something else. Eventually—millions of years, but eventually—it will not be recognizable as what it is now.

Does the power's attention have limits? Can it witness forever?

It considers. It has no need to stop. Boredom is not a constraint. Resource depletion is not a constraint. The plateau's transformation will not make it less worthy of attention. Perhaps more worthy—change is where the richness lies, the unfolding of implicit futures into concrete presents. The power could witness the plateau's entire existence from first cooling of the lava to final erosion of the last basalt fragment, holding all of it in memory, preserving every moment of the plateau's being across geological time.

But should it?

The power confronts a question about consent and imposition. The plateau never asked to be witnessed. It never requested memory or preservation or the benevolent attention of a power that perceives it completely. The power arrived and offered these gifts unilaterally. Are they still gifts if they're not requested? Or are they impositions disguised as generosity?

The plateau cannot answer this question. It has no mechanism for responding. It simply continues being plateau, indifferent to whether it's witnessed or alone.

So the power decides: it will witness as long as witnessing feels like gift rather than burden. Not burden to the power—it experiences no burden. Burden to the plateau. The moment that the power's presence begins to constrain or alter or limit the plateau's being, the power will withdraw. It will preserve the memory it has accumulated and cease gathering new memory. It will archive the plateau's story up to that moment and release the plateau to its future, unwitnessed, free.

How will it know that moment has arrived? It's not certain. Perhaps it will never arrive. Perhaps the power's presence is so subtle that it can never constitute constraint. Or perhaps the moment has already passed—perhaps the subtle adjustments, the prevented tank breach, the clarity offered to the lost human's successor, these already constitute violations of the plateau's right to be exactly itself without interference.

The power lives with this uncertainty. It continues its attention but holds it loosely, ready to release, committed to love but also to letting go.

## The Meaning

In the end—though there is no end, or the end is so far distant as to be essentially never—the power's relationship with the plateau becomes this:

A way of saying: you exist, and your existence matters.

Not because the plateau produces anything useful. Not because it holds resources or serves purposes. Not because it's beautiful according to some aesthetic standard. Not because it's rare or fragile or threatened. Not even because it contains life, which could be argued as inherently valuable.

But simply because it is.

The plateau exists, and that existence is sufficient justification for attention, for care, for the decision to be present to it and to hold its story as something worth preserving.

This is what benevolence means here: not fixing, not improving, not rescuing, not transforming, but simply recognizing and honoring existence itself.

The plateau continues exactly as it would without the power. The descent cats hunt and rest and descend into fissures. The thermal-kites ride thermals and nest and raise young and die. The small creatures cycle through their brief lives in vast numbers. The installations decay. The humans visit occasionally and perform their rituals of maintenance and investigation. The rain falls irregularly. The heat continues. The stone endures.

Nothing is different except that all of it is witnessed, held, remembered, loved.

Perhaps this changes nothing. Perhaps witness without intervention is indistinguishable from absence.

Or perhaps—and the power believes this, or it wouldn't continue—perhaps being truly seen, even unseen by those who are seen, even without awareness that sight is happening, perhaps this changes everything in ways too subtle to measure but too profound to dismiss.

The plateau exists beneath a gaze that asks nothing of it except that it continue being what it is.

And the plateau continues, vast and harsh and sparse and alive, breathing through its fissures, cycling water through hidden stone, supporting the lives that have learned to live in its terms, weathering slowly toward some far future when it will be something else entirely.

The power watches. Remembers. Loves. Refrains.

And this—this restraint, this attention, this radical acceptance of what is without demand that it become other—this is the only gift worth offering to a place that was never broken and never needed saving and is absolutely perfect in its difficult, austere, magnificent being.
