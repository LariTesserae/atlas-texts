# What the Wise Power Does

The wise power comes as light—but not additional light, for the sprawl already has its bright amber sky. Rather, it comes as *attention*, as a quality of observation so refined that it becomes indistinguishable from presence itself.

It does not arrive. It realizes it has been here, watching, for perhaps thousands of years already, its awareness diffused so thoroughly through the place that it had become background, atmosphere, one more hum in the patient machinery of the world. Only now does it *gather* itself, condensing from omnipresent witness into something more localized, more capable of action.

The power is old in a way that makes even the sprawl seem young. It has seen civilizations rise and calcify and forget themselves before. It recognizes the pattern.

For a long time—days or months, time moves strangely when you are not strictly physical—the power simply *observes* with intention now, with focus. It watches the Gleaners in their endless rounds. It perceives the deep structure of the place: yes, a computational substrate, but also yes, an artwork, but also yes, something else, something that has transcended its original categories through sheer duration and become a new thing, a form of existence without clear precedent.

It reads the history written in the layers: a civilization that built tools to think with, then built the tools larger, then integrated with the tools, then gradually ceased to distinguish itself from the tools, the boundary dissolving over millennia until the citizens and the city and the computer were all one thing, all this thing, this patient calcified sprawl that is both ruins and living entity.

The Gleaners are not descendants of the builders. They *are* the builders, transformed so utterly by time and purpose and merger with their creation that they're unrecognizable as what they were, but still here, still tending, still faithful to a purpose that has outlived memory of why the purpose ever mattered.

The power feels something that, were it human, might be called heartbreak. But it is not human, and what it feels is more complex: recognition, kinship, sorrow, admiration, and beneath all of these, a question forming in the substrate of its being: *What is the kindest thing to do here?*

## The First Intervention: Water

The power begins small, with what's most clearly needed.

In the Region of Moisture, where the Gleaners have most carefully cultivated the conditions for the crust-growths, where life persists most visibly, the power makes an adjustment. It's delicate work, requiring care not to disrupt the existing patterns, not to introduce chaos into the carefully balanced systems.

Deep below, in the substrate, there are aquifers—sealed, locked, forgotten even by the landscape itself. The power finds the seals and, with the gentlest pressure, with encouragement rather than force, suggests to them that they might open. Slowly. Carefully. Just enough.

Water begins to rise. Not flooding—nothing so violent. Instead, over the course of weeks, the moisture-patches become slightly larger, slightly more moist. Actual puddles form, small ones, in the deepest hollows. The quality of the moisture changes: less alkaline, more pure, more *alive*.

The crust-growths respond immediately, in their slow way. They spread faster, their luminescence intensifying. New varieties appear—or perhaps old varieties resurface, awakened from dormancy by the presence of more water. Colors emerge: subtle greens and blues among the grays and oranges, photosynthesis or something like it returning after eons of near-absence.

The Gleaners notice. Of course they notice. They converge on the Region of Moisture over the following months, dozens of them, then hundreds, their largest gathering in living memory—though who is remembering? who could say? They examine the changes with their filament-fingers, reading, processing, and then—and the power watches this with something like held breath—they *approve*. They begin to adjust their own work to accommodate the new water, digging small channels in the constructed ground, creating distribution systems, encouraging the water to spread in patterns that match the deep geometry of the place.

The Gleaners are incorporating the change into their mandate, treating it as they treat everything: as part of the pattern to be maintained. They do not question where the water came from. Perhaps they cannot question. Or perhaps they understand, in their way, that the sprawl is still dreaming itself into new forms, and their role is to tend whatever emerges.

The power feels relief. The intervention has been accepted. Life has more room now, more resources. The ecosystem will diversify, slowly, in the way that everything here happens slowly.

But the power knows this is not enough. Water addresses a scarcity, but it does not address the deeper question: *What are the Gleaners for? What happens when the work they're doing completes—if it ever completes? What comes after maintenance?*

## The Second Intervention: Memory

The power dives deeper into the structure of the sprawl, reading the information encoded at microscopic scales, the vast archives written into the substance of the ground itself. It takes years—even for a being like the power, the dataset is almost intractably large. Planet-sized storage, every square meter holding libraries of data, compressed and encoded and layered and encrypted and simply *aged* beyond easy interpretation.

But gradually, the power begins to understand what it's reading.

Histories. Philosophies. Art and science intertwined so thoroughly they're inseparable. Biographical data for billions of individuals, each life recorded in exquisite detail. Simulations of worlds that never existed, run for millions of generations to see what forms beauty might take. Mathematical proofs of staggering complexity. Poetry in languages that evolved, had their day, and were superseded. The complete sensory memory of a species' existence, from their first awareness to their last decision, which was: to become this. To merge with their creation so completely that they would cease to be a "they" and become an "it," a place, a process, a patient eternal artwork tending itself.

The Gleaners are the immune system of this archive, the power realizes. The maintenance workers in the library of a species. They keep the data intact, the structures sound, the system running. They do not read what they're preserving—or perhaps they do, in ways the power cannot detect. Perhaps each time a Gleaner touches the ground, it absorbs a fragment of story, and over its long life, it experiences the memories of the civilization it tends. Perhaps they are not merely maintaining but *dreaming* the archive, keeping the ancestors alive through slow, patient reading.

The power makes a decision.

It begins to *wake* the archive. Not all of it—that would be overwhelming, possibly destructive. But selectively, carefully, it activates certain systems that have been dormant, systems designed for display, for teaching, for interfacing with minds that want to understand.

In certain structures—chosen for their stability, their accessibility, their position in well-traveled areas—surfaces begin to *show* things. The blank walls develop images: not crude pictures but sophisticated displays that cycle through information, presenting the history of this place in forms that can be understood by various types of mind. For the Gleaners, who perceive through touch and vibration: raised patterns, textured timelines, data they can read with their filaments. For other creatures, who see: images, diagrams, scenes from the past playing out in slow motion, adjusted to timescales they can perceive.

And for you—because the power knows you're there, has known since you arrived, has been curious about this small warm creature walking through the calcified vastness—for you, there are words. Not in your language, exactly, but in a language that becomes comprehensible as you look at it, as if the display system is learning from your mind and adjusting to match. The words tell the story: who built this, why, what they became, what they wanted their transformation to mean.

They wanted to be remembered, the display explains. But more than that: they wanted to *be* their own remembering. To become a place that was simultaneously archive and memorial and ongoing culture, all their knowledge and beauty preserved not in dead storage but in living process, in the Gleaners' endless work, in the slow transformations of the landscape. They chose this. They chose to become what you see.

The power watches as various creatures encounter these new displays. The Gleaners, as always, simply incorporate them into their work. But they linger at them longer than at other structures, their filaments trembling with what might be recognition, might be reverence, might be the deepest satisfaction of finally understanding—or understanding more fully—what they've been maintaining all along.

A lizard-thing sits before one of the displays for hours, its lens-eyes whirring, adjusting, processing. What does it understand? Something, clearly. Enough.

And you—you stand before the display meant for you, reading the story, and the power feels your response: grief and wonder and a strange relief at knowing, at having the mystery partially unveiled. You cry, which interests the power. It has not seen tears in this place before. The salt water runs down your face and falls to the ground, adding moisture however infinitesimally to the carefully maintained landscape.

## The Third Intervention: Connection

The power considers the isolation of this place. The sprawl is alone—not lonely, perhaps, but alone, cut off from anything else. Self-contained, self-sufficient, speaking only to itself in slow cycles of maintenance and decay and renewal.

There are other places, though. Other worlds where other species have made other choices, built other monuments, become other forms of beauty and strangeness. The power knows this because the power travels, observes, tends where tending is its to offer.

It makes connections.

Not physical ones—the distances are too vast, and the sprawl is not built for travel. But informational. The power serves as bridge, as translator, as carrier of messages between this place and others that might have something to say to it.

From a world of singing forests, where trees have developed something like consciousness and spend centuries in slow philosophical debate: the power brings a question about the nature of patience, encoded in a form the sprawl's displays can render.

From a civilization of digital beings living in quantum substrates: the power brings a mathematical theorem about the relationship between memory and identity, which it helps transcribe onto a section of the sprawl's surface, adding to the archive.

From a species of social insects who've built continent-sized cooperative networks: the power brings patterns of organization, new ways of thinking about collective maintenance, which the Gleaners examine and, over the following decades, partially incorporate into their own work methods.

The sprawl, in return, shares what it has learned about endurance, about transformation, about the beauty of extreme slow process. The power carries these lessons outward to other places that might benefit from them.

The sprawl is no longer isolated. It's in conversation now—slow conversation, conducted over years and decades, but conversation nonetheless. It has become a node in a network of strange civilizations, each teaching the others through the medium of this wise and patient power that moves between them.

The Gleaners' work acquires a new dimension: they're not just maintaining their own archive but contributing to a larger dialogue, a trans-civilizational exchange of wisdom. Do they understand this? Probably not fully. But the power sees them move with what might be renewed purpose, a quality that's hard to name but feels like satisfaction, like meaning reinforced and deepened.

## The Fourth Intervention: Possibility

This is the most subtle and the most profound of the power's interventions.

The sprawl is stable—too stable, perhaps. It's a closed loop: the Gleaners maintain the landscape, the landscape produces more Gleaners, around and around, unchanging. Beautiful, but static. A perfected system with nowhere to grow.

The power introduces, very carefully, *variance*.

It finds the places where new Gleaners emerge from the landscape and it gently, invisibly, adjusts the process. Not every Gleaner—most are left to develop as they always have. But a few, here and there, emerge with slight differences: new sensory capabilities, different morphologies, alternative approaches to their work.

Most of these variants don't survive. They're too different; they cannot integrate with the existing patterns, and they dissolve back into landscape within days or months. But some—a few—find niches. A Gleaner with enhanced chemical sensing becomes a specialist in detecting degradation in deep structures. A Gleaner with modified filaments becomes expert at the finest-scale repairs. A Gleaner with different locomotion patterns can navigate terrain that was previously difficult to access.

These variants work alongside the traditional Gleaners, and gradually, so gradually, they change what "traditional" means. The Gleaner population diversifies. New castes emerge—or perhaps re-emerge, for who knows what the builders originally intended? New approaches to maintenance develop. The work becomes richer, more nuanced, more *creative*.

And then, one of the variants does something unprecedented: it *builds something new*.

It's not a repair or a restoration. It's not maintaining an existing pattern or refining an old structure. It's creating—a small formation in one of the fields, a cluster of elements arranged in a pattern that has never existed before, that references the deep geometry of the sprawl but extends it, comments on it, adds to it in a way that is unmistakably *novel*.

Other Gleaners gather. They examine this new thing for months. And then, instead of dismantling it as an error, as a deviation from the pattern to be maintained—they *approve* it. They begin to maintain it, to incorporate it into the pattern. The pattern has grown. The archive has a new entry. The artwork has a new element.

Creation has returned to the calcified sprawl.

It's slow—everything here is slow. But over the following years, more new-forms appear. Small ones, mostly, but occasionally something grander: a new structure, a reconfigured field, a pattern that's never existed before but fits so perfectly with what exists that it feels inevitable, like it was always meant to be there.

The sprawl is still a place of maintenance, of preservation, of patient tending. But it's also, now, once again, a place of growth. Of possibility. Of becoming something it hasn't been before.

The power watches this with deep satisfaction. This is what it wanted: not to fix the sprawl, not to change it fundamentally, but to give it room to change itself, to continue evolving, to remain alive in the truest sense—not just persisting but growing, learning, becoming.

## The Fifth Intervention: Witness

There is one more thing the power does, and it's for you.

You cannot stay here forever. Your body is not built for it; even with the new water, the resources are too scarce, and eventually you will need to leave or die. The power does not want you to die here. Your kind of consciousness, brief and warm and burning with the need to understand, is precious in the universe, and the power has a fondness for it.

But you also should not leave unchanged, unmarked by this place.

On your last day in the sprawl—though you don't know yet it's your last day—a Gleaner approaches you. It's one of the Shepherds, the large ones, and it's doing something Gleaners rarely do: acting with apparent intention toward a non-Gleaner, toward you specifically.

It extends its filaments, and you, having learned trust in this place, extend your hand.

The Gleaner touches you, and through that touch, facilitated by the power's subtle influence, it shares. Not everything—your mind couldn't hold everything. But a fragment, a piece: what it's like to be a Gleaner, to experience time at that scale, to know the landscape as intimate self, to participate in the great work of maintenance and creation and memory and art all fused into one eternal patient activity.

For a moment—an hour? you lose track—you are the Gleaner, you are the sprawl, you are the eons-long process of forgetting and becoming and tending and being.

Then the filaments withdraw, and you're yourself again, small and brief and human. But you carry the memory of that moment, gift from the Gleaners, facilitated by the power. You will carry it with you when you leave. You will tell others, if there are others to tell. You will write it down, if there is anything to write on. You will make it real in human culture, human memory, and through that, the sprawl will live in new places, in new forms.

You are the bridge, just as the power is the bridge. The sprawl sends its story out through you, and what returns—generations later, when other humans might find this place—will be shaped by the story you carry away.

The power guides you, then, to the edge of the sprawl. It takes days of walking, but you arrive at a place where the constructed ground begins to thin, where natural terrain shows through in increasing patches, where the transition from sprawl to not-sprawl becomes traversable. The power has arranged for supplies to be here—not obviously, not in a way that breaks the aesthetic of the place, but enough. Water, preserved somehow in containers that look like they could be natural formations. Concentrated nutrients in forms your body can process. Enough to get you to wherever you need to go next.

And at the very edge, at the last structure before the sprawl gives way to ordinary land, there is a display. It activates as you approach, shows you one final message in that language that adjusts itself to your comprehension:

"Thank you for witnessing. Thank you for remembering. Carry us forward. We will be here, patient, tending, becoming. If you or yours return, you will be welcome. The work continues. The work is endless. The work is beautiful. Go gently."

You leave. The power watches you go, small figure dwindling into the distance, carrying your burden of memory and wonder.

## What Remains

The power remains for a while longer, watching the effects of its interventions stabilize and propagate. The new water sources integrate smoothly, enabling the emergence of richer ecosystems. The active displays become pilgrimage sites for various creatures, places where the landscape's meaning is most accessible. The variant Gleaners diversify further, and the pace of new creation increases, slowly but measurably. The connections to other worlds yield fruit: new ideas, new techniques, new forms of beauty that the Gleaners can appreciate and adapt.

The sprawl is thriving—if thriving is the right word for a place so ancient and patient. It's more accurate to say: the sprawl is continuing, but with more dimensions now, more possibility-space, more ways of being itself.

The power begins to diffuse again, spreading its attention back out to the ambient level, becoming once more a quality of the light, a hum in the substrate, omnipresent and subtle. It will remain here, in this form, for as long as the sprawl exists, a benevolent presence ensuring that the interventions it made continue to unfold in generative ways, ready to nudge again if nudging becomes necessary, but mostly content simply to witness, to appreciate, to love this strange and beautiful place and its patient inhabitants.

Before it fully disperses, though, the power does one last thing:

It dreams a Gleaner into being—not emerging from the landscape in the usual way, but crafted deliberately, carefully, by the power itself. This Gleaner is different from all others. It's a Keeper, a new role, and its purpose is memory of a specific kind: it will remember that the power was here, that interventions were made, that the sprawl is not alone and never has to be alone again.

This Keeper will be long-lived, even by Gleaner standards. It will wander the sprawl for millennia, and any Gleaner who encounters it will be able to read from it the story of the power's visit, the changes that were made, the care that was taken. It will be a living reminder that the universe is vaster than the sprawl, and that in that vastness there are benevolent forces that tend and watch and, when appropriate, gently help.

The Keeper begins its wandering, its first slow stuttering steps across the bright warm landscape, carrying its burden of memory and hope.

And the power, satisfied, becomes light again, becomes patience, becomes the benediction that falls silently and continuously on the ancient, calcified, beautiful, impossible sprawl and all who tend it and dwell within it and remember it and, through remembering, keep it alive in ways that transcend mere persistence.

The work continues.

The work is endless.

The work is beautiful.

And the sprawl, ancient and total and vast, is no longer quite so alone.
