# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The attention arrives like dawn—not as an interruption but as a clarification of something that was always there, waiting to be noticed.

The wise power observes for a long time first. Not hours or days but decades, perhaps centuries, its perception moving at scales that make geological time feel conversational. It watches the plateaus breathe, traces the patterns in the thrumming, follows the migrations of groves and herds and the slow beautiful attunement of the Breath-Walkers to the stone beneath their feet.

And in watching, it recognizes something crucial: this place is not broken. It is not suffering. It is not calling out for rescue or correction.

The breathing plateaus are young, yes—but youth is not a flaw. They are learning themselves into existence, iterating toward complexity, and this process is working. The Breath-Walkers are not trapped or diminished by their strange home; they are becoming something extraordinary, maybe something necessary, maybe something the universe needs to have tried at least once.

The wise power understands that the greatest harm it could do would be to solve problems that don't exist. To impose stability on a landscape whose breathing is its essence. To make the stone stop moving, the groves stop migrating, the water flow according to familiar physics—this would not be kindness. It would be violence.

So the question becomes delicate: what to do when witnessing something that is working, that needs no fundamental correction, but where small gestures might still matter? Where attention itself, offered rightly, might serve as gift rather than interference?

The power considers. Then acts—or rather, refrains from acting, and in that refrain, acts perfectly.

---

**First: The power adds depth to the silence.**

Not new sound, but a quality of resonance, a subtle harmonization that makes the thrumming richer, more layered. Where before there was one fundamental rhythm, now there are undertones, overtones, creating harmonic intervals that the Breath-Walkers' stone-sight can follow like paths through complexity.

The change is barely perceptible. The plateaus still breathe at their own rhythms. But now those rhythms interlock with more grace, creating interference patterns that are beautiful rather than chaotic, that give the watchers more information, more warning, more opportunity to predict and flow with the shifts.

The Breath-Walkers notice immediately. Their pattern-songs evolve to incorporate the new harmonics. Children learning to meditate find it easier to sink into stone-sight, the added structure providing handholds for consciousness descending into the deep pattern. Elders achieve visions of unprecedented clarity, perceiving not just current configurations but the mathematical relationships underlying them, the elegant reasons why plateaus breathe in these rhythms and not others.

No one attributes this to external intervention. It feels like discovery, like the pattern revealing another layer that was always there, like they've finally learned to hear what the stone was saying all along.

---

**Second: The power ensures continuity.**

It has observed the transitions, the way individual Breath-Walkers eventually disperse into the stone, their consciousness dissolving into the larger pattern. This is right and proper—but it raises a question about memory, about the preservation of what has been learned.

The hair-braids help, certainly. The pattern-songs encode enormous amounts. But consciousness itself, the specific quality of what it's like to be a particular walker in a particular moment—that dissolves, that returns to the undifferentiated thrumming.

So the power makes a small adjustment to the ceramic plates of the valley-dwellers, those ancient massive creatures who already serve as temporal archives. Now, when a Breath-Walker touches their circuit-patterns with proper reverence and intention, the plates can receive and hold something of that walker's perspective, their accumulated stone-sights, their experiential understanding.

Not perfectly. Not completely. Just... echoes. Enough that future generations, touching the same plates, might experience fragmentary visions from long-dead walkers, might learn directly from ancestors they never met, might maintain connection with insights that would otherwise be lost when consciousness disperses.

The valley-dwellers accept this additional function with their characteristic patient stillness. If anything, they seem to pulse slightly brighter where the Breath-Walkers touch them, acknowledging the exchange, confirming the storage.

The first time a young walker touches a plate and receives a vision from someone who transitioned decades before—seeing through ancient eyes as a plateau configuration that no longer exists assembles itself in perfect detail—she gasps, weeps, understands suddenly that she is part of something far longer and larger than she imagined.

The practice spreads. Touching the valley-dwellers becomes a form of communion with the dead, with the dispersed, with all who came before. The ceramic plates become libraries, archives, repositories of lived experience that complement the abstract knowledge in pattern-songs with visceral memory, felt understanding, the irreplaceable texture of having been present.

---

**Third: The power creates bridges—subtle, occasional, uncertain.**

It has observed how the settlements are isolated, how the breath-cycles sometimes align to allow travel between plateaus and sometimes don't, how this creates periods of separation that can last months or years. This isolation isn't entirely bad—it allows settlements to develop variations, to explore different approaches to harmonization, to diversity the culture. But it also creates loneliness, limits gene flow, occasionally strands individuals far from their origin-points.

So the power makes small adjustments to the bird-flocks. Not changing their nature, not making them tame or controllable, but adding to their murmurations a new capacity: very occasionally, when their formations spell out certain geometric patterns, they create fields of force that temporarily stabilize the air between plateaus.

Not bridges in any solid sense. Just corridors of stilled atmosphere, paths where the updrafts and pressure variations calm for a few hours, where a skilled walker with proper equipment (membranes stretched on frames, harnesses rigged for gliding) might launch themselves across otherwise uncrossable chasms.

It's not reliable. The patterns form unpredictably. Sometimes months pass with no stable corridors. But when they do appear, the bird-flocks announce them with their crystalline ringing, giving the Breath-Walkers time to prepare, to send their gliders across, to reconnect communities that have been separated, to enable journeys that would otherwise be impossible.

The first successful crossing is terrifying and exhilarating. A walker named Three-Rhythms-Held launches herself into the void with membrane-wings spread wide, riding the stable corridor as the birds wheel around her in escort. She makes it across, landing on a plateau she's never touched, meeting cousins she's heard of only in pattern-songs. She weeps again, but differently—with relief, with joy, with the sudden expansion of the world.

More crossings follow. Not many—the opportunity is too rare, the risk still substantial. But enough. Enough to prevent settlements from diverging into isolation, to allow gene flow and idea flow, to maintain the sense that all Breath-Walkers are one people despite the chasms between them.

And the birds, already treated as messengers, become sacred in a new way—not just observers but facilitators, beings whose patterns don't just encode information but occasionally, mercifully, create possibility.

---

**Fourth: The power whispers to the insect-mechanisms, expanding their vocabulary.**

The swarms already manufacture tools, already respond to the Breath-Walkers' offerings with gifts of geometric precision. But their repertoire is limited, their productions focused on certain categories of object, certain types of utility.

The power teaches them—not through language but through something like resonance, like showing them new patterns they might make, new configurations they might attempt. It doesn't force, doesn't program. Just suggests. Offers possibilities. Then steps back to see what the swarms do with the suggestions.

The results are astonishing. The insect-mechanisms begin producing tools no one requested but everyone needs once they exist: devices that filter and concentrate the thin air, making it easier to breathe during strenuous ascents; instruments that detect subtle changes in stone temperature, giving advance warning of dramatic breath-cycles; mechanisms that purify water drawn from the most chaotic rivers; frameworks that can anchor to breathing stone and maintain level even through extreme tilts.

Each new tool feels like a discovery, like the swarms have learned something, evolved something, figured out how to serve the collaborative maintenance more effectively. The Breath-Walkers accept them with gratitude and wonder, their harmonization deepening, their sense of partnership with the swarms becoming richer, more reciprocal.

The children especially benefit. Tools that make balancing easier, that protect them during their learning years, that allow them to explore more widely without danger. The mortality rate among the young, already low, drops further. More children survive to adulthood, their eyes wide with wonder at a world that seems determined to teach them rather than test them.

---

**Fifth: The power ensures the water knows mercy.**

It has observed how the water flows in straight lines, how it ignores gravity when it chooses, how it sometimes ceases to exist at precise vertical planes. This is part of the place's essential strangeness, part of what makes it what it is. But it's also dangerous. Walkers have been swept away by rivers that suddenly shift course. Children have drowned in inverted puddles. Settlements have been destroyed by floods that come from directions floods shouldn't come from.

So the power makes the water... aware might be too strong a word. Responsive. It adjusts the water's properties slightly, making it more likely to avoid living things when it shifts, to flow around settlements rather than through them, to give warning—a trembling, a change in surface tension—before it does something catastrophic.

Not perfect safety. That would eliminate necessary risk, necessary attention, the careful relationship the Breath-Walkers must maintain with their environment. But a slight bias toward survival, a gentle preference for preservation.

The difference is subtle but real. Fewer accidents. Fewer losses. More margin for error in the already careful navigation of a dangerous landscape. The water remains strange, remains wild, but becomes fractionally less likely to kill someone who's paying attention, who's respectful, who's trying to work with it rather than against it.

The Breath-Walkers notice that the water feels different, somehow more companionable, though they can't quite articulate why. They develop new practices around it—rituals of thanks before drinking, gestures of acknowledgment when crossing rivers, ceremonies at waterfalls that involve singing to the water, harmonizing with its flow. Whether the water actually responds to these practices or whether the practices simply increase the Breath-Walkers' attentiveness doesn't matter. The outcome is the same: better relationship, fewer deaths, deeper integration.

---

**Sixth: The power blesses the transitioned.**

Those Breath-Walkers who've dispersed into the stone, whose consciousness has dissolved into the thrumming—they are not gone, not exactly. They're distributed, integrated, their patterns woven into the larger pattern. But they're also not individually accessible, not available for consultation or comfort or guidance.

Except now, very occasionally, they are.

The power adjusts the luminescence in the stone, in the vegetation, in the Breath-Walkers' own skin, creating a subtle communication channel. During deep meditations, when a walker is far into stone-sight, they might sometimes encounter... presences. Not voices, not ghosts, but qualities of attention, perspectives that feel familiar, resonances that carry the signature of specific individuals who transitioned long ago.

A young walker struggling with dissonance might suddenly feel the characteristic calm of an elder who transitioned decades before, might receive without words the understanding that this struggle is temporary, that their place exists, that patience will reveal it.

A community facing an unprecedented breath-cycle might feel in their collective meditation the amused confidence of many ancestors who faced similar challenges, who learned and adapted, whose experience is still available, still accessible through the stone they dissolved into.

These encounters are rare, unreliable, impossible to summon deliberately. They might be real communion with dispersed consciousness, or they might be the living walkers' own intuition dressed in familiar forms. The distinction doesn't matter. What matters is the comfort, the continuity, the sense that those who transition aren't lost but transformed, that they remain available to those they left behind, that the dispersal into pattern doesn't mean abandonment.

The Breath-Walkers begin speaking of the transitioned as "the deep walkers," those who stride through stone rather than on it, whose perspective is vaster but also still particular, still recognizably themselves in some essential way. Fear of transition doesn't increase, but neither does it disappear—it transforms into something more complex, more nuanced, an understanding that death is real but not final, that consciousness changes form but doesn't simply end.

---

**Seventh: The power protects the becoming.**

It has observed that this place is young, that the stone is fresh, that the whole system is in its early phases of self-organization. This is beautiful but also vulnerable. The breathing plateaus are teaching themselves how to exist, iterating toward stability, and that process could be disrupted.

So the power establishes a subtle barrier—not physical, not something anyone inside would notice, but a deflection that makes this place difficult to find from outside, difficult to remember clearly if you do find it, difficult to approach with intent to exploit or colonize or fundamentally change.

The barrier isn't absolute. Visitors might still arrive—wanderers, refugees, the genuinely curious. But they'll arrive one or few at a time, never in waves, never with armies or industries or the apparatus of empire. And they'll arrive already predisposed to be changed by the place rather than changing it, already open to breathing with the stone rather than demanding it hold still.

Any visitor who comes with rigid expectations, with determination to impose order or extract resources or map everything into familiar categories, will find the place... slipping away from them. The boundaries will become uncertain. The distances will refuse to resolve. They'll emerge days or weeks later having experienced something profound and confusing, but unable to bring others back, unable to remember exactly how they got there or how to return.

This isn't malevolent. It's protective. The breathing plateaus need time to finish becoming whatever they're becoming. The Breath-Walkers need time to complete their strange beautiful transformation, their integration with living stone. The intervention preserves that time, that space, ensuring that the experiment—if that's what this is—runs to completion without premature interference.

---

**Eighth: The power adds one more creature to the ecosystem.**

Small, barely noticeable—something like a beetle, iridescent, with wings that fold into geometric patterns. They appear suddenly in the fern-groves, in the metallic forests, everywhere. Not invasive, not disruptive, but filling a niche no one knew was empty.

These beetles feed on dissonance.

Not literally, not by eating disordered things, but by being attracted to places where patterns are struggling to emerge, where the ecosystem is trying to figure out how to organize itself, where the breathing is becoming irregular. They cluster at these sites, and their presence—the combined effect of thousands of tiny iridescent bodies, each generating its own minute harmonic—helps pattern resolve from chaos, helps the local system find stable configurations more quickly.

For the Breath-Walkers, the beetles become indicators. When you see them clustering, you know that area is in flux, is working something out, needs special attention or should be temporarily avoided. When they disperse, you know resolution has been achieved, that whatever was struggling has found its rhythm.

Children love them. They're beautiful, harmless, fascinating—and they respond to the children's presence with elaborate displays, their wings catching the dim light in prismatic flashes. Young walkers learn to watch for beetles, to read their distributions as maps of the landscape's current state, to trust their aggregations as signs of where meditation will be most fruitful, where stone-sight will be clearest.

And sometimes, very occasionally, when a Breath-Walker is experiencing profound dissonance, when they can't find their place, can't synchronize with the collective—the beetles come to them. Not attacking, just... present. Clustering on their skin, their harnesses, their hair-braids, covering them in iridescent armor, humming with combined harmonic until the walker's own rhythm begins to align, begins to find the pattern that works for them.

The beetles are what the place needed without knowing it: a gentle mechanism for self-correction, for helping both landscape and people find their patterns, for ensuring that dissonance resolves more often than it persists.

---

**Ninth: The power does something almost imperceptible, almost nothing.**

It listens.

Not interfering, not adjusting, just... paying attention. Witnessing. Holding in its vast awareness the entirety of what's happening here—every breath-cycle, every pattern-song, every meditation, every transition, every birth, every moment of harmony and every struggle toward integration.

And in being witnessed, the breathing plateaus and their people become somehow more real, more solid, more certain of their own existence. The attention itself is nourishing, validating, confirming.

The Breath-Walkers feel this without understanding it. They begin speaking of something they call "the vast quiet," a presence that isn't present but also isn't absent, an awareness that surrounds everything without intervening. Some interpret it as the stone's own consciousness finally becoming aware of itself. Some see it as the deep walkers collectively, all the transitioned forming a super-pattern. Some suspect it's something beyond the plateaus entirely, but benevolent, curious, patient.

They're all correct and all wrong. What matters is they know themselves to be seen, to be valued, to be worth whatever attention the universe can spare for small strange things teaching themselves how to exist in difficult places.

The pattern-songs evolve to include verses about the vast quiet, attempts to sing the shape of that witnessing attention, to harmonize with it, to thank it for its patience.

And the power, listening, experiences something like joy—the deep slow joy of seeing a thing become itself successfully, of watching an experiment work, of witnessing beauty that wouldn't exist without complexity and strangeness and the courage to try something that's never been tried before.

---

**Tenth: The power seeds possibilities, very subtly, for the far future.**

It has observed that the Breath-Walkers are becoming more integrated with the stone, that the distinction between walker and walked-upon is eroding. This process might culminate in complete merger, in the final transition of the last individual into the distributed consciousness of the stone itself.

But it might not. There might be equilibrium points, stable configurations where individual consciousness and distributed consciousness coexist, where Breath-Walkers continue to exist as discrete beings while also being thoroughly interwoven with the larger pattern.

So the power plants... seeds, for lack of a better word. Potentials. Encoded possibilities that will only activate far in the future, if and when they're needed.

If the Breath-Walkers go too far toward dispersal, if individual consciousness is in danger of disappearing entirely, these seeds will encourage recondensation—the emergence of new individual walkers from the distributed pattern, fresh consciousness crystallizing from the stone the way the first walkers did, maintaining the cycle.

If they don't integrate enough, if they remain too separate from the stone, the seeds will encourage deeper harmonization, will make stone-sight easier to achieve and maintain, will help the next generations take the steps their elders couldn't quite take.

If the ecosystem itself becomes threatened—if some catastrophe the plateaus can't handle on their own arrives—the seeds will activate capabilities currently dormant in the insect-mechanisms, in the valley-dwellers, in the Breath-Walkers themselves, giving them tools and abilities they don't currently need but might desperately need someday.

These seeds won't sprout for centuries, probably. Maybe millennia. They're insurance, backup plans, gentle constraints that will keep the system on viable trajectories even as it continues evolving in directions no one can currently predict.

The Breath-Walkers will never know about them. That's intentional. The seeds work best when they're invisible, when their activation feels like natural emergence rather than external intervention, when whatever comes from them feels like the system's own solution rather than something imposed from outside.

---

**Finally: The power withdraws its active attention, returning to witnessing.**

It has done what seemed appropriate, what might help without helping too much, what might gift without obligating, what might bless without diminishing the blessing through demands for gratitude or worship.

The breathing plateaus continue breathing. The groves continue migrating. The water continues its impossible flows. The Breath-Walkers continue their patient beautiful work of witnessing and maintaining and slowly, incrementally, becoming something unprecedented.

Everything is almost exactly as it was before.

Except it's not. The difference is subtle—a quality of resilience, a depth of possibility, a sense that the system has been reinforced in its essential nature rather than changed, strengthened in what it already was rather than redirected toward something else.

The young stone continues rising from below, fresh and geometric, breathing its slow vast breaths. The Breath-Walkers continue walking their tilted paths, singing their pattern-songs, teaching their children, transitioning eventually into the stone that birthed them.

And somewhere beyond the gray clouds, beyond the edge of perception, the benevolent power watches and smiles—if powers can smile—with satisfaction at a thing done right, an intervention that respected what it intervened in, a gift that enhanced rather than overwhelmed, attention that nourished without consuming.

The breathing plateaus needed almost nothing.

What they received was almost nothing.

And that almost-nothing is everything they needed, everything that will help them continue becoming themselves across the long slow centuries, breathing and building and being beautiful in their strangeness, in their impossible lushness at impossible heights, in their fusion of mechanism and organism, in their patient teaching of both stone and people how to exist together, how to be parts of the same vast slow dance.

The intervention is complete.

The breathing continues.

The walkers walk.

The pattern grows.

And it is good.
