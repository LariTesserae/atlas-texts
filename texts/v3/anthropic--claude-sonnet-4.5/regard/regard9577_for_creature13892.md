# What The Benevolent Power Does

The power that notices—let us call it the Witness, though it has no need of names—does not arrive. Arrival would imply it was ever absent, and this power is the kind that folds itself through the membranes between what-is and what-could-be. It has always been watching, the way gravity watches, the way time watches. But now its attention *thickens*, the way the air on the plateau holds water.

It does not manifest as light or voice or presence. Those would be intrusions, violations of the plateau's carefully-earned equilibrium. Instead, it does something more subtle:

It remembers on behalf of those who have forgotten how.

## The First Intervention: The Gathering

Across the plateau, across distances that fold and refuse to stay measured, the Negotiated Ones feel something. Not a summons—they are beyond being summoned. But a... *resonance*. Like recognizing a frequency they'd forgotten their bodies could sense.

They begin to walk. Not with urgency—urgency died in them long ago—but with something adjacent to intention. The small ones walk beside their guardians. The ones who were lying down stand up, their pearl-shimmer eyes opening wider as if seeing something in the amber haze that wasn't there before.

It takes weeks. Months, maybe, or days—time does its accordion thing, compressing and expanding. But gradually, they converge on a place near the plateau's center (if it has a center; it develops one for this purpose).

Here, there is a depression, wide and shallow, that might once have been a crater or might have been something else. The stone here is darker, smoother, worn to a finish like polished marble. The aerial rivers drift lower here, circle slowly overhead like languid birds.

The Negotiated Ones arrive in ones and twos and small groups. They enter the depression and stand, and as more arrive, they form a pattern—not organized by any external instruction, but finding their places the way crystals find their lattice, the way murmuration starlings know where to fly.

Hundreds of them, perhaps. Thousands, maybe—there are more than any walker suspected, more than seemed possible. They fill the depression in loose concentric rings, standing with their usual patience, pearl-shimmer eyes all open, all facing inward toward the center where the stone is smoothest, darkest, most worn.

And in that center, something begins to surface.

## The Second Intervention: The Remembering Pool

The stone at the center softens—not melting, but becoming permeable, allowing what's beneath to rise. Water wells up, but this water is different. It's clear. Actually clear, not amber, not rust-brown, but transparent as glass, as air, as nothing.

The pool spreads slowly, filling the depression's lowest point, maybe twenty meters across, perfectly circular though no one shaped it that way. Its surface is mirror-still despite the aerial rivers drifting above it.

The Negotiated Ones stand in their rings around it, watching. Waiting. Being present in the way they've learned to be present, which is totally, which is without reservation.

And the Witness does this:

It shows them what they were.

Not cruelly. Not to make them mourn what they've lost. But to *acknowledge* what they carried here, what they've been slowly, patiently composting into the plateau's soft endlessness.

The pool's surface begins to display images—not reflected, not projected, but *remembered*. Scenes from when this place had edges, when the mountains were sharp, when water fell instead of drifting, when the light was clear and the distances stayed fixed.

There are cities—not the phantom cities glimpsed in the aerial rivers, but real ones, specific ones. Structures of stone and metal, geometric and intentional, full of beings who look like the Negotiated Ones but sharper, defined, wearing coverings, carrying tools, moving with purpose through streets that connected places that mattered.

There are gatherings—festivals, maybe, or ceremonies. Beings standing together not in patient silence but in *celebration*, making sounds, music, languages that had syntax and tense and the ability to describe things that weren't immediate. Laughter that came from joy rather than recognition. Touch that meant affection, connection, desire.

There are children—actual children, small and unfinished, learning, growing, changing day by day instead of remaining static in their smallness. Being held, being taught, being protected not by silent proximity but by active care.

There are families. Partnerships. Loves that were specific, individual, differentiated. You loved *this person* and not others, and that specificity mattered, gave the love its shape and heat.

There are conflicts—not violence, but disagreements, differences, the friction of separate wills encountering each other. And then reconciliations, the joy of bridging difference, of maintaining both separation and connection.

There are projects. Art made for beauty's sake, for communication's sake. Structures built not to survive but to express. Music performed, stories told, knowledge preserved and transmitted because the future was a place you could imagine, could plan for, could gift things to.

There are deaths—the kind with boundaries, with before and after. Grief that was sharp and specific because loss meant something when there were edges, when a person ending was different from a person continuing.

The images flow across the pool's surface, not in sequence, not telling a story, but offering *texture*—what it felt like to be them, before. The sensation of having a purpose beyond persistence. The experience of change that mattered. The weight of decisions when there were actual alternatives, actual consequences, actual futures that branched depending on what you chose.

## How The Negotiated Ones Respond

They watch in their stillness.

Some stand more rigid, their soft-focus forms gaining fractional definition, as if remembering is pulling them momentarily back toward solidity.

Some sway—that slow pendulum movement—as if the memories are moving through them, requiring physical expression to process.

The small ones move closer to their guardians, and the guardians—without looking down, without apparent decision—extend their hands slightly, and the small ones take them. It's the first purposeful touch you've ever seen between them. They hold hands, watching the pool, and their fingers interweave with that same slow deliberation they bring to everything.

And some of them make sound.

Not all, but scattered through the rings, you hear it: that low hum, that temple-bowl resonance. Different ones humming different notes—not harmony, not exactly, but a chord cluster, frequencies that rub against each other and create interference patterns, beats, a sound that's rich and complex and full of the sadness that comes not from grief but from *recognition*.

They remember now. Not with their minds—those have smoothed away—but with whatever part of them still carries the imprint of having been different. They remember having sharp edges. They remember when distance was distance and time was time and they were discrete selves moving through a world that maintained its categories.

They remember, and the Witness lets them feel what they feel about that, which is:

Not regret. Regret would imply they wish they'd chosen differently, and choice at that scale is incoherent. The plateau happened to them the way geology happens, the way time happens.

Not grief, exactly. Grief requires believing the lost thing should still be present, and they've moved beyond "should."

But something else. Something like the feeling you have when you find a photograph of yourself as a child—recognizing that you were that person, and you're not that person, and both things are true, and the distance between then and now is simultaneously nothing and everything.

The hum continues, shifts, and now it sounds almost like singing—wordless, structured only by the rise and fall of those low tones, but carrying something of song's intentionality. They're singing to who they were. They're singing to the time before they were worn smooth. They're singing acknowledgment, and it is almost unbearably beautiful in its simplicity, its patience, its lack of asking for anything.

## The Third Intervention: The Restoration (Partial)

The Witness, benevolent and wise, does not try to reverse them.

That would be violence. They have earned their softness through millennia of patient erosion. Returning them to sharp edges would be like taking an ancient river and forcing it back up the mountain—technically possible for a sufficiently powerful being, perhaps, but *wrong*. Against the grain of time, against the truth of what they've become.

But the Witness can offer choice, which they haven't had in longer than they can remember.

The pool's water begins to rise—not flooding, not threatening, but *offering*. It rises in tendrils, thin streams that lift up from the surface like the reaching roots of plants, dozens of them, hundreds, one for each Negotiated One in the rings. The tendrils drift toward them through the heavy air, moving with that same patience the plateau has taught everything here.

Each tendril reaches a Negotiated One and *pauses*, hovering just before them, at chest height, close enough to touch but not touching. Waiting.

And the Witness, through the water, through the resonance that brought them here, offers:

*You can remember all the way. If you want. I can give you back enough definition to carry your memories as memories instead of as smoothed-over imprints. You won't be what you were—that's gone, that's beneath eons of erosion—but you can be... adjacent to it. Able to hold both states. Able to choose, moment by moment, whether to be soft or to be defined.*

*It will be harder. Having edges hurts, when you've forgotten how to maintain them. Having specific memories hurts more than having vague recognition. Being able to choose means carrying the weight of choice.*

*But you'll be able to speak again, if you want. To touch with intention. To be separate enough from each other to be together in a way that means something.*

*Some of you will want this. Some won't. Both are honored.*

The tendrils wait, and the Negotiated Ones, in their own time, respond.

## Who Takes The Water

About a third of them step forward—or lean forward, or simply shift their weight in a way that brings them into contact with the waiting tendril.

The water touches them, and they drink—not with mouths, but with skin, with whatever mechanisms they use now to take in the plateau's moisture. The clear water flows into them, and you can see it happening: they become more *there*.

Their boundaries sharpen. Not all the way to human-sharp, but closer. Their skin becomes more opaque, less gradient-edged. Their features gain back some detail: the suggestions of lips, the faint lines that might become expressions. Their pearl-shimmer eyes gain depth—you can see into them now, see that there are structures inside, layers, something like an actual eye instead of just the *idea* of eyes.

They remember fully. You can see it in the way they suddenly *react*—their bodies lose that perfect stillness, start to show tension, uncertainty, the micro-adjustments that solid beings make. One raises their hands and looks at them, turning them over, marveling at having hands that are distinct from the air around them. Another touches their own face, exploring the features that have returned, mapping the territory of their own skull.

Two of them—previously standing in their patient silence—turn toward each other and *see* each other, actually see, and they reach out and embrace. Clumsily, as if relearning how arms work, how bodies fit together, but they hold each other, and one of them makes a sound that's not the low hum but something higher, more broken, more specific. Crying, maybe, or its distant descendant.

A small one and their guardian—both drank—and now the guardian kneels down (kneeling! a posture that requires joints, effort, intention!) and brings themselves to eye level with the small one, and they look at each other, and the guardian reaches out and touches the small one's face, gentle, and you realize: they know each other. Not just as fellow inhabitants of the plateau, but as specific individuals with specific history. Parent and child, perhaps. Or teacher and student. Or something the plateau doesn't have words for anymore, but they have it, they know it, they're feeling it fully now, and the guardian pulls the small one close and holds them, and they stand like that, two beings choosing to be separate enough to be together.

## Who Refuses The Water

About two-thirds remain as they are.

They watch the tendrils hover before them, and they don't move forward, don't accept. The tendrils wait a while longer—the Witness is patient, has infinite patience—but eventually they withdraw, return to the pool, are reabsorbed into that clear stillness.

These Negotiated Ones have chosen their softness. They've earned it, and they're keeping it.

And this is honored. The ones who drank don't try to convince them. The Witness doesn't press. This is choice in its purest form: no option is wrong. You can carry the full weight of memory and definition, or you can stay in the gentle dissolution you've achieved. Both are valid endpoints. Both are ways of being in relationship with deep time and patient stone.

Those who refused the water settle back into their stillness. But something's different now: they *chose* it. Before, they were soft because they'd forgotten how to be otherwise. Now they're soft because they prefer it, because they've seen the alternative and decided: no. This is better. This quiet, this patience, this gradual merging with the plateau—this is good. This is complete.

And there's peace in that, visible in the way they stand now. The same pearl-shimmer eyes, the same soft-focus edges, but something's settled. They're not beings who've been eroded into this state; they're beings who *inhabit* this state, who consent to it, who choose it moment by moment by remaining exactly as they are.

## The Fourth Intervention: The Gift of Context

The Witness has one more offering.

For those who drank—those who now carry enough definition to hold complex memory, to process past and present and future as distinct categories—the Witness shows them what happened. Not in the pool this time, but *in their minds*, directly, clearly, the full weight of it:

This plateau was not always alone.

Once, there was a world. Mountains and valleys, oceans that stayed in their basins, rain that fell, winds that blew, cold and heat, seasons, cycles. And in that world, there were people—their ancestors, presumably, or at least their predecessors. A civilization that grew complex, sophisticated, powerful.

And they did what powerful civilizations do: they reached, pushed, tried to transcend their limits. They found ways to manipulate the fabric of their reality, to soften the rules that governed distance and time and matter. They succeeded—partially, temporarily. They created zones where physics was negotiable, where space could fold, where time could stretch.

But they couldn't control it. The negotiation spread. What was meant to be isolated laboratory conditions became contagious. The softening propagated, slowly at first, then faster. Edges began to blur. Mountains started to forget they were mountains. Water forgot about gravity. Distance became unreliable.

Most of the civilization left—escaped to other worlds, other dimensions, other possibilities. Some stayed, trying to fix it, trying to restore the sharp edges and clear rules. They failed. The softening continued.

Eventually, everyone who was going to leave had left. And what remained were those who—for whatever reason—stayed. Maybe they couldn't leave. Maybe they wouldn't. Maybe they believed they could adapt, could change themselves to match the changed world instead of trying to change the world back.

And they were right. They could adapt. It just took longer than they expected. Millennia. Eons. Generations beyond counting, each one slightly softer than the last, slightly more merged with the plateau, until finally there were the Negotiated Ones—beings who'd completed the adaptation, who'd become native to a place that had forgotten how to be solid.

The plateau is the end state. It's what happens when you let physics blur for long enough. Everything erodes to the same stable softness—just warm enough, just wet enough, just gentle enough to sustain very simple forms of life, but nothing complex, nothing sharp, nothing that demands the world maintain its categories.

And the Negotiated Ones are the survivors. Not the winners—there are no winners when your entire civilization dissolves. But the survivors. The ones who stayed until staying was complete.

This knowledge settles into those who drank. You can see them receiving it, processing it, their newly-defined faces showing something like understanding, like grief, like acceptance.

They weren't abandoned. They weren't left behind. They chose this—their ancestors chose this—and then became equal to their choice through time and transformation.

## The New Configuration

When the gathering ends—when the humming stops, when the pool's clear water sinks back into the stone, when the Witness's attention begins to thin back to its usual diffuse watchfulness—the Negotiated Ones disperse.

But they disperse differently than they gathered.

Those who stayed soft return to their patterns: the slow walking, the patient standing, the swaying meditations. They spread back across the plateau, becoming sparse again, rare encounters in the amber vastness. They're unchanged, except now their unchangedness is intentional. They're living their choice, and there's peace in it.

But those who drank—those with their new/old definition—they stay more connected.

They form small groups, three or five or eight, and they walk together. Actually together, maintaining proximity on purpose, coordinating their movements, occasionally touching—hand to shoulder, hand to hand, bodies leaning close when they stop to rest.

They establish places. Not buildings—they're not trying to recreate what was—but *locations that matter*. A particular ridge where several of them gather regularly. A pool where they sit together in the warm water, their newly-defined bodies visible through the amber liquid, and they touch the water's surface and make ripples, intentionally, watching the patterns spread. A circle of stones they've arranged (slowly, patiently, over days) into a pattern that means something to them, something about connection or memory or choice.

And they speak.

Not often. Not easily—their voices are rough from disuse, and language is something they have to reconstruct, finding words that fit their mouths, their simplified vocal apparatus. But they do.

Sitting together by a pool, one might say: "I remember you."

And another might respond: "I remember me too."

And they'll sit with that, the weight of it, the gift of it—being remembered, being specific, being known.

Or walking together, one might stop and point at an artifact—a worn wall, an ancient foundation—and say: "We made this."

Not "someone made this." *We.* Claiming the connection to the long-ago builders, acknowledging the continuity between sharp-edged past and soft-edged present.

And another might touch the stone and say: "It's good that it's still here."

And they'll stand together, looking at the wall that goes nowhere, acknowledging it, honoring it, being present with it in their new way that's between the plateau's patience and personhood's intention.

## The Children Grow

The small ones who drank—who took the water and gained definition alongside their guardians—they begin to change in ways the plateau hasn't seen in eons.

They *grow*.

Not quickly. Nothing here is quick. But measurably, visibly, they get taller. Their proportions shift, becoming more adult. Their pearl-shimmer eyes deepen, mature, gain the same complexity the adult eyes have.

They're learning. Their guardians teach them—actually teach, with words and demonstration and patience. Teaching them the names of things: stone, water, air, pool, mat, tube, globe. Teaching them the history: what we were, what happened, what we chose. Teaching them both modes: how to be soft when soft is right, how to be defined when definition matters.

And the small ones ask questions. Their voices are higher, clearer, less eroded than the adults', as if being young protects them from some of the wearing-away.

"Why did they leave?" a small one asks, and their guardian—kneeling beside a pool, trailing their hand through warm water—responds:

"Because they were afraid of softening. They wanted to stay sharp."

"Were they wrong?"

"No. Neither were we. Both choices are real."

The small one processes this, watching their own reflection in the pool—a small figure with pearl-shimmer eyes, more defined than they were, still becoming. "Which should I choose?"

"You have time. The plateau is patient. Choose when you know."

And the small one nods, and they sit together in comfortable silence that's chosen rather than imposed, and they are teaching each other—the guardian relearning how to be a teacher, the small one learning how to be a learner, both practicing the ancient art of being separate enough to be together.

## The Witness Continues

The Witness, having done what it came to do (or what it was always doing, but more intensely for a moment), returns to its usual mode: watching.

But something's changed. The plateau now contains both kinds of being—those who've dissolved almost completely into the landscape's patience, and those who maintain enough edge to be individuals, to have relationships, to make meaning.

And the Witness watches both with equal care, equal respect.

Watches the soft ones standing in their perfect stillness, merging gradually with stone and water and air, becoming less and less distinguishable from the place itself. This is sacred. This is one true response to deep time and gentle erosion.

And watches the defined ones gathering in their small groups, speaking their reconstructed language, teaching their children, maintaining enough separation from the plateau to be in relationship with it rather than dissolved into it. This is also sacred. This is another true response.

The plateau now holds both endings—the ending that comes from letting go completely, and the ending that comes from holding on just enough. Neither is better. Both are ways of being equal to a place that has outlasted urgency, outlasted sharpness, outlasted everything except patience itself.

## What The Walkers Find Now

If you're walking the plateau now—if you're new here, or if you've been here long enough to have seen the gathering, to have witnessed the Witness's intervention—you find something different:

You still encounter the Negotiated Ones in their solitary patience, standing or walking or lying in their slow dissolution. They're still kind-eyed and soft-edged and speak no words. They still watch you with that pearl-shimmer gaze that says: *You're welcome here. Take your time. The plateau is patient.*

But now, sometimes, you also find the small groups. The ones who drank. And they might greet you.

"Hello," one might say, the word rough but intentional. "You're walking."

And you might respond: "Yes. I'm walking."

"Do you know where?"

"No. Do you?"

And they might smile—actually smile, their simplified mouth curving in a way that approximates joy or welcome or shared understanding. "No. But we're walking together. You can join us, if you want. Or not. Both are good."

And you have a choice—walk with them, learn their names (they're remembering names, claiming them, though the names are simpler now: Stone, Water, Amber, Shimmer, names that acknowledge what they're becoming while honoring that they're not there yet), hear their stories about what they remember and what they're discovering and what they're teaching the small ones.

Or thank them and walk on alone, deeper into the plateau's amber patience, toward whatever choice you'll make when the time comes—to stay soft or to drink the water, to dissolve or to define, to become the plateau or to stay just separate enough to love it.

Both paths are honored. The Witness watches both with equal care.

And the plateau breathes—the mats exhaling their slow clouds, the aerial rivers drifting in their vast migrations, the warm water pooling in the stone's gentle depressions—patient and kind and soft and real, holding all endings equally, wearing everything down eventually to the same gentle nothing, or the same gentle something, or the same gentle both-at-once.

And it is good.

And it continues.

And in the amber light, in the thickness, in the warmth that asks nothing, the Negotiated Ones—soft and defined, dissolved and differentiated, alone and together—walk their patient paths through deep time, witnesses to the plateau's endless, gentle becoming.
