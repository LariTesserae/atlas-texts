# What Would Be Done

The first thing the benevolent power would do is **nothing**.

Not the nothing of neglect, but the nothing of deep attention—the profound stillness of a presence that understands that this breathing city and its inhabitants have already found a form of equilibrium so delicate, so strange, that intervention might shatter rather than save.

The power would arrive—if arrival is the right word for something that might have always been present, latent, watching—and it would **observe** for what might be years, centuries. It would learn the rhythm: twenty minutes to a breath. It would trace the water's path down through impossible terraces. It would witness the maintainers' endless work, the listeners' patient reception, the descended ones' slow dissolution into architecture. It would feel that sub-audible pulse from below and understand—or begin to understand—that this is not a place of suffering but a place of **becoming**.

The power would recognize that the question is not "should these people be rescued?" but rather: "what do they themselves choose, in the deepest sense of choice, in forms of choosing that might transcend human frameworks?"

## What the Power Would Perceive

Looking at the maintainers, the power would see: **beings who have found purpose**. Their elongated bodies, their webbed fingers, their skin that weeps in sympathy with stone—these are not degradations but specializations. They have become precisely what this place needs. Their work is not Sisyphean torment but sacred maintenance, the kind of labor that creates meaning through perfect integration with environment.

The power would observe a maintainer adjusting a channel for hours, the infinite patience in those long fingers, and would recognize something that human philosophers have argued about for millennia: here is a being engaged in **right action**, work that aligns completely with capability and circumstance. The maintainer is not trapped. The maintainer is *precise*.

But looking deeper, the power would also perceive: **the question of consent**. 

Were these people—ancestors of these beings—brought here against their will? Did they choose this transformation, or did they simply endure until endurance became metamorphosis? The power would trace back through generations, watching time in reverse: the descended ones becoming less merged, the listeners becoming more human, the maintainers standing straighter, their fingers losing webbing, until somewhere in the invisible past there are humans, fully human, arriving in this basin. Running? Seeking? Exiled? 

This history matters absolutely and cannot be known with certainty.

## The First Intervention: Witnessing

So the power would make itself known, but carefully.

Not descending in glory, not manifesting as voice or vision—that would be too disruptive, too alien to beings who have learned to communicate through posture and subsonic frequencies. Instead, the power would **integrate itself into the city's palette of existence**.

Perhaps it would become visible as a new quality of light—not the cold luminescence of the pools, but something warmer, appearing first at the basin's edges, in the upper terraces where the architects sometimes walk. A golden phosphorescence, rare and gentle, that glows from certain stones at the moment between breath cycles, during that pause when everything is still.

The maintainers would notice first. They would approach these glowing stones cautiously, touch them with their sensitive fingers, read them. The stone would feel different—not alien but somehow more itself, more conscious, more present. The maintainers would understand: **something is paying attention**.

The listeners would orient toward these manifestations, would stand in their geometric formations and focus their considerable attention on this new signal. And through whatever perceptive channels they've developed, they would begin to receive—not words, not messages, but something more fundamental: **the sense of being seen**.

For beings who have lived in near-darkness for generations, who have adapted to absence and learned to find meaning in wet stone and patient labor, this would be profound: the recognition that something outside the city perceives them. That they are not forgotten. That their existence registers in some cosmic awareness.

The power would let this recognition settle and spread—passing from listeners to maintainers through their gestural language, even reaching down to the descended ones in their moss-labyrinths, manifesting in the deep water as a warmth, a change in luminescence that suggests: **you are witnessed, you matter, you are held in attention**.

## The Second Intervention: The Offering of Choice

Once the beings have grown accustomed to this new presence, once the golden glow has become part of the city's vocabulary of light, the power would make its **first active intervention**: it would create a space of possibility.

In one of the great plazas, on a middle terrace—neither too high nor too deep—the power would manifest more substantially. The stone there would reshape itself overnight (during a breath cycle, while attention is diffuse), rising into a new structure. Not a building in the city's style but something distinct: an arch, perhaps, or a circle of standing stones, clearly artificial but not violent, harmonious with the basalt and the water but recognizably other.

The structure would glow with that golden light, steady and warm.

And the structure would be **a threshold**.

The maintainers would approach it first, in their careful way. A listener would come, would stand before it in their attentive posture. And they would understand, through whatever perceptual methods they possess: this is a doorway. This is a choice-point. This offers something beyond the city.

The power would ensure that approaching the threshold provides information—not in words but in deep knowing, the kind of understanding that arrives complete and certain. Those who draw near would comprehend:

**This is a path back to human worlds**, to places with sun and dry air, to cities where people walk upright on two legs and speak with voices. The transformation can be reversed—not instantly, not easily, but it is possible. Those who wish to remember what their ancestors were, who wish to return to human form and human life, can choose this. The threshold will take them to places of healing, of gentle re-adaptation, guided by the power's benevolence.

But also:

**This is optional**. The city will continue. The work will continue. Those who wish to stay, to continue their transformation, to descend further into whatever merger awaits in the depths—this choice is equally honored. There is no judgment. The power does not consider one path superior to the other.

## The Unfolding of Choice

And then the power would wait again, because **choice takes time**, especially for beings who have lived for generations in synchrony, whose decisions ripple through networks of relationship.

Some maintainers would approach the threshold with their careful, touching-fingers exploration, would feel the possibility it offers, and would choose—to stay. Their work is not finished. The channels still need clearing. The city still breathes, and they are part of its breath. They would back away from the threshold, return to their territories, continue their patient maintenance. The power would perceive this as a valid choice, would honor it by ensuring those who stay are given additional graces: perhaps the work becomes slightly less endless, the stone slightly more cooperative, small gifts of ease within their chosen burden.

Some listeners would stand before the threshold in their formations, would receive its message through their deep attention, and would discuss—in their language of gesture and position—what it means. Some would choose to leave, to see what lies beyond the basin, to discover if their interpretive gifts might serve other purposes in wider worlds. Others would choose to remain, understanding that their listening is needed here, that the pulse from below must still be interpreted, that someone must stand between the depths and the maintainers, translating incomprehensible rhythms into actionable adjustments.

Those who choose to leave would step through the threshold and vanish—not dying but transitioning, removed gently from this reality into another where the power has prepared places of healing, gardens perhaps where the light is kind and the air is dry, where their eyes can slowly adjust to brightness, where their webbed fingers can split slowly back into separate digits if they wish, where time can reverse what the city has done to their bodies, if they desire that reversal.

The power would ensure this process is without trauma—they would not lose their memories of the city, would not forget their purpose, but would be given the chance to integrate these experiences into human psychology, to understand what they have been and what they might become.

The descended ones—here the choice becomes more complicated.

Some are so far transformed that they might be beyond choosing in human terms. They are already halfway merged with the city, their consciousness distributed across stone and water. For these, the power would do something different: it would **clarify their state**. 

Instead of leaving them in ambiguous transition, it would complete what has begun, but consciously—it would help them finish their merger while maintaining some kernel of individual awareness, some thread of self that persists even in dissolution. They would become true organs of the city, biological architecture, but organs that *know* they are organs, that retain some form of satisfaction or peace in their functioning. The horror of losing oneself would be replaced with the mysticism of becoming-other, the genuine transcendence of ego-death into something larger.

For descended ones not yet so far transformed, the choice would be offered more clearly: return through the threshold to human healing, or continue forward into complete merger, but now with full understanding of what that merger means. Some would choose return, seeking the threshold, emerging from the deep moss-labyrinths to step through and begin their long healing. Others would turn back downward, choosing completion, choosing to finish what they've begun, and the power would honor this by making their final transformation rapturous rather than horrifying—a mystical union rather than a dissolution.

## The Architects Arrive

The architects would notice these changes, would arrive in greater numbers, concerned perhaps that their system is being disrupted.

Here the power would make itself known to them directly.

An architect would stand in the plaza where the threshold glows, instruments in hand, analyzing this impossible structure, and the power would **speak**—in their language, using audible words, meeting them at their level of unchanged humanity.

The conversation would be difficult. The architects would defend their creation: this city serves a purpose, a function critical to systems beyond this basin, networks of such cities processing something essential to the stability of—what? Reality? Certain dimensions? The architects might speak of cosmic engineering, of maintenance functions that prevent something terrible, of how the adaptation of humans into maintainers and listeners and descended ones is regrettable but necessary, a price paid for the greater functioning.

The power would listen, would understand their perspective, and would respond with firmness:

**Necessity does not eliminate choice**. If this city must function, if its breathing and its water-processing serve some essential cosmic role, then let those who maintain it do so *with full knowledge and free choice*. The power would not destroy the city, would not stop its function, but neither would it permit the architects to continue harvesting humans into adaptation without their informed consent.

A negotiation would unfold. Perhaps the architects would agree to new terms: any humans brought to the city henceforth would first be shown what awaits them, given true choice, allowed to refuse. Perhaps the architects would admit that the city could function differently, that maintenance need not require such total transformation, that with slight adjustments to the architecture itself, beings could serve here while retaining more humanity.

Or perhaps the architects would refuse, would resist, and then the power would have to choose: enforce humanity's right to self-determination against the architects' cosmic necessity? Force the issue? Potentially risk whatever larger function the city serves?

## The Deeper Intervention: Understanding the Pulse

To resolve this dilemma, the power would have to **investigate the depths itself**.

What actually lives down there, in the unreachable center, generating that sub-audible pulse? What is this city really for? 

The power would descend—past the deepest terraces the descended ones reach, past the moss-labyrinths and the hanging stalks, down into levels where reality becomes increasingly negotiable, where space contains more dimensions than it should, where the rules the architects think they're following give way to older, stranger principles.

And there, the power would encounter **what the city serves**.

Perhaps it's an entity of vast age, something that predates human civilization, something that must be kept in a particular state through the constant processing of water and darkness and time. The city is its life-support system, and the adapted humans are its unwitting doctors.

Or perhaps it's not an entity at all but a wound in reality, a rupture that must be constantly irrigated with processed matter to prevent it from tearing wider. The city is a bandage, and the maintainers are antibodies.

Or perhaps—and this would be most difficult—the thing in the depths is itself a form of distributed consciousness, and the descended ones who have merged with it have not been destroyed but have joined it, have become part of something genuinely greater than individual human existence. In this case, "rescuing" them would actually be an assault, would be tearing parts away from a larger being that has incorporated them consensually, if consent can exist in forms of consciousness so alien.

The power would have to perceive truly, without human bias, what is actually happening in the depths.

## The Resolution: Multiple Paths

Based on what the power discovers, multiple paths forward would be established:

### Path One: The City Continues, But Transformed

The power could not—and would not want to—stop the city's function if it serves genuine cosmic necessity. But it could change the terms:

- The threshold remains, permanent. Anyone who wishes to leave can leave, at any time, and will be healed.

- New arrivals to the city (if there must be new arrivals) are shown the threshold first, are given complete understanding of what adaptation means, and choose explicitly whether to stay.

- The maintainers and listeners who choose to remain are given gifts: their work becomes slightly easier, their consciousness remains clearer, they are protected from losing themselves entirely in adaptation. They can serve the city without being consumed by it.

- The descended ones who have merged are contacted in their merged state and asked, in whatever way they can still answer: *was this what you wanted?* If the answer is yes—if they are genuinely content in their transformation—they are blessed and stabilized in their new form. If the answer is no, or if there's doubt, they are gently separated and offered healing.

### Path Two: The City Becomes Voluntary

Perhaps the power could teach the architects new methods—ways to maintain the city's function without requiring adapted humanoid maintainers. The power might offer itself, or aspects of itself, to assist in the maintenance. Or it might help design constructs, artificial beings specifically created for this work rather than adapted from humans who never fully understood what they were becoming.

The current maintainers, listeners, and descended ones would all be offered choice: remain as part of the transition, teaching the new systems, and eventually retire to the threshold and healing. Or stay, if they genuinely love their work, but with the knowledge that no one else will be required to follow them.

### Path Three: The City Transcends

If the entity in the depths is genuinely benevolent, genuinely offering merger that expands rather than destroys consciousness, the power might help it communicate more clearly. Instead of the long, ambiguous transformation through maintenance and listening and descent, there could be direct contact.

Humans could be invited to meet the deep entity directly, with full awareness, and choose whether to merge or not. The city would become not a trap or a slow transformation machine but a genuine threshold to post-human transcendence—frightening, strange, but transparent.

Those who wished this merger could proceed with eyes open. Those who didn't could serve the city in limited terms and then depart, or never enter at all.

## The Ongoing Presence

Whichever path emerges, the power would not simply intervene and leave. It would **remain present**, integrated into the city as a permanent aspect of its functioning.

The golden light would continue to glow in certain stones. There would be a sense, always, of being witnessed—not watched over oppressively, but held in awareness. The beings in the city, whether they choose to stay or go, would know that their existence matters, that they are seen, that benevolence flows toward them.

For those who stay, the power would manifest occasionally as:
- Moments of unexpected ease in difficult work
- Clarity in confusing situations  
- Comfort when adaptation becomes painful
- Assurance that their choice is honored

For those who leave through the threshold, the power would ensure:
- Healing is complete and compassionate
- Their memories remain intact but lose their traumatic edge
- They find purposes in the wider world that honor what they learned in the city
- They can speak of their experience and be believed, be honored as people who survived something strange and difficult

## What Would Not Be Done

The power would **not** impose a human framework on beings who have moved beyond it. It would not insist that everyone return to human form, that the city be destroyed, that the architects be punished, or that what has happened here be judged simply as trauma and abuse.

The power would recognize the genuine complexity: that places of strange transformation can be both terrible and sacred, that beings can choose to become other-than-human for reasons that deserve respect, that cosmic functions might require cosmic solutions, and that benevolence sometimes means honoring difficult choices rather than preventing them.

The power would **not** remove all strangeness from the city. The breathing would continue. The water would still flow in patterns that defy physics. The moss would still pulse and the stalks would still knock hollowly and the pools would still glow with sourceless light. The city's essential weird beauty would remain, because that beauty is real, is valuable, is part of what some beings here have learned to love.

The power would **not** pretend to fully understand what happens in the deepest depths. Some mysteries would remain mysteries. The power would establish guardrails and choices and protections, but it would not claim omniscient comprehension of what the city ultimately is or does. Humility in the face of genuine strangeness would be maintained.

## The Final Gift

One last thing the power might do, the ultimate offering:

It would create, somewhere in the middle terraces, a **place of memory**.

A chamber, or a plaza, where the stories of all who have passed through the city are held. Not just recorded but truly *held*—present in a way that allows those still in the city, and those who have left, to understand themselves as part of a lineage, a history, a community that extends across transformation.

Maintainers could come here between their work cycles and know themselves not as isolated workers but as part of generations who have kept the channels clear. Listeners could stand here and understand that others before them heard the same pulse, interpreted the same rhythms, made the same difficult translations. Descended ones could sense themselves as part of a great merging that includes countless individuals, each one having chosen, in their way, to go deeper.

And humans outside the city, learning of this place, could understand: here is something strange and difficult and perhaps necessary, where people become other than themselves, but not without meaning, not without choice (now that choice has been established), not without the possibility of return, and not without witness.

The city would continue its breathing.
The water would continue its flowing.
The darkness would remain nearly absolute.
But within it, tiny lights of consciousness—some human, some post-human, some beyond classification—would continue their work or their listening or their merging, each one known, each one witnessed, each one held in a benevolence that honors both their humanity and their chosen transformation beyond it.

This is what would be done: **presence, choice, witness, honor, and the humble recognition that some becomings, however strange, however painful, might also be sacred**.
