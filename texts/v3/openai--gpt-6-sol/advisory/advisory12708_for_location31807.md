Prepare for a warm, wet rooftop, not a walk through an established garden. Wear shoes with good grip: the ceramic plates, narrow drainage channels, and fresh seams may be slippery or awkward underfoot. Bring a light rain layer, even if the sky looks settled. Moisture can arrive as a shower, condensation, or a burst from the nozzles, while heat from the machinery below may make heavier clothing uncomfortable.

Before going up, confirm that visitors are allowed and find out how to leave the roof if the weather changes. Keep back from the gap and handrails in low cloud or rain. Once inside, stay on the intended walking surfaces. The planting beds are still sharply formed, vines are beginning to reach the pipes, and grates and valve housings need to remain clear. Don’t turn a valve or redirect a trickle of water; this young watershed is still settling into its course.

If you are arriving from another world, do your usual checks for breathable air, safe water contact, and local access rules before treating the place as familiar. Otherwise, pack lightly. The best thing to bring is time to stand still and notice which channels fill, where the leaves hold rain, and how quickly the roof makes its own weather.
