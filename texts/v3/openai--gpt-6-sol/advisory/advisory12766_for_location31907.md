Prepare for a cold, wet city where the most serious danger may be an edge you cannot see. Arrange a local guide for your first arrival, especially if you will travel after dark. Get a current map that distinguishes streets fixed to the shelf from suspended districts, and learn how locals mark stairs, crossings, and safe waiting places. Do not treat a span as ordinary solid ground merely because it looks like one: ask how its supporting field works, whether it has restricted loads, and what happens during maintenance or a power failure.

Bring waterproof layers that remain warm when damp, gloves with good grip, and boots suited to wet grilles and ice in stone joints. Keep your light and navigation device secured to your body; carry a backup light rather than relying on the sparse amber marks. A tether may be useful on exposed routes, but only if a local can show you appropriate anchor points. In cloud, stop and establish where the path goes instead of feeling your way forward.

Plan journeys around the collectors. Learn their warning signals and keep clear of shutters and discharge areas: a sudden load of water, the crash, and its echo could make it hard to hear a companion or an instruction. Protect electronics from continual condensation, and carry drinking water until you know which supplies are treated; the water on walls and in canals is not necessarily safe.

Give the animals room. Check a wall before leaning on it, keep hands out of mineral seeps, and never reach beneath a bridge to investigate a ticking sound. If shelled animals are crossing a doorway, wait or use a raised walk rather than stepping through them. Ask residents which routes and structures should be left undisturbed.

Finally, allow more time than the distance suggests. Darkness, cloud, ice, animal crossings, and unfamiliar suspended streets all reward a slow pace. Before venturing far, confirm that the air and local conditions are safe for you, identify a warm place to retreat to, and agree with your host on what to do if you become separated.
