Prepare for this as a **cold, waterlogged marsh with unreliable cues**, not as a place where the strange water necessarily behaves like a trap. The greatest immediate risks are ordinary ones: getting lost among similar-looking ridges, stepping into a concealed channel, becoming cold and wet, and finding that the return journey takes longer than expected.

- **Plan a short visit with a firm turnaround time.** The land is nearly level, but its ridges and pools repeat for a long way. Mark your entry point, carry an offline map and compass, and record your route as you go. Do not rely on the distant trees as a destination or on reflections to judge the weather or time.
- **Dress for persistent damp and cool air.** Wear insulating layers beneath a waterproof shell, quick-drying clothing, and boots suited to mud and shallow water. Carry dry socks and a complete dry layer in a sealed bag. A walking pole will help test footing hidden by reeds and silt.
- **Keep essential gear dry and on your person.** Pack drinking water, food, a first-aid kit, a light, and a means of calling for help if one works in this world. Arrange for someone to know your route and when to expect you back. Treat pool water as unsafe to drink without proper treatment.
- **Cross water reluctantly.** A low ridge may be firm for half an hour and then fade into sedge. Probe before stepping, avoid steep or crumbling banks, and turn back rather than wade into water whose depth or current you cannot see. The sound of rushing comes from several directions, so it is not a dependable guide to a channel.
- **Give the unusual pools room.** Their reflections can lag or precede what happens overhead, and ripples and tones do not always match visible motion. There is no obvious boundary between altered and ordinary water. Observe from firm ground; do not use a reflection to decide that rain has stopped, a bank is still, or a crossing is safe. Avoid digging into the clay or pulling at the fine threads beneath the silt.
- **Protect your sense of direction and pace.** The layered vegetation makes nearby ground hard to read, while the open plain can make progress look easier than it is. Stop periodically to check your position. If the low tones become distracting or you feel pressure in your jaw, pause somewhere secure rather than walking while disoriented.

Aim to leave well before evening, when the pools’ tones lower and rain may begin moving across the plain again. If you must stop overnight, choose the highest stable ground you can find, well clear of a channel, and be prepared for it to remain wet even when the rain pauses.
