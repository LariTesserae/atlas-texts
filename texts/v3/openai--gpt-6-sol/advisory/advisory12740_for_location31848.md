Visit with someone who knows how to open the old doors. **Confirm that you can get back out before entering**; do not break the fitted clay or assume the second door is usable. Tell someone above when you expect to return. The courtyard is small, but its height and its noise could make a person in trouble hard to hear.

Go early, before the late-morning roar builds and before the brick bowl becomes hottest. Carry water—the city’s channels carry air, not drinking water—and wear sturdy shoes with good grip. Bring eye protection against blown grit, and hearing protection if you plan to stay when the walls are sounding. Agree on hand signals with your companion: shouting across the thicket may be useless. A small light will help at the doors and after the sun leaves the floor.

Keep clear of the wall mouths and loose upper tiles. Don’t probe the gaps behind the brickwork or use the abandoned cart as a step. The repairs have been worn smooth by years of grit; what looks solid from below may not welcome a handhold.

Treat the thicket as something to observe, not push through. Its hard blades open in heat and rattle in the drafts; broken ones release a sharp resin, and the insects beneath them are little known. Long sleeves are sensible. Leave the leaves, insects, clay seals, and masonry as you found them.

Most of all, allow yourself a quiet moment *before* the court grows loud. Once the drafts gather, the bench, walls, and leaves will make this room-sized place feel much larger—and make it easy to lose track of heat, time, or a companion’s voice.
