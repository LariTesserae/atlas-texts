Prepare for a warm, exposed wetland where the main difficulty is not distance but footing. What looks like continuous ground may be shallow water over slick clay, while a dry-looking crust may break under your weight. Expect to move slowly, and do not count on a straight route across the flats.

Wear light, sun-protective clothing and a hat, with shoes that grip wet stone and mud and can be rinsed afterward. Bring drinking water rather than relying on the pools: the pale crust suggests salt, and shallow water full of wildlife may not be safe to drink even if it looks clear. Pack insect repellent, sunscreen, and a way to keep your essentials dry.

Plan a route before setting out. The plain offers few landmarks, and reflected sky can make distant pools and firm patches hard to judge. Carry an offline map or compass, note your return bearing, and allow much more time than the distance suggests. Check the weather and any local water-level information: a rise of only inches could cover your way back, and there is little shelter from sun or lightning.

Walk around sedge islands and soft banks where you can. They are both firmer-looking than they may be and important habitat for the small animals here. Give feeding birds room, especially if a flock suddenly takes flight.

Above all, arrive ready to pay attention. This is a place for short steps and long looks: the current may be visible only in a drifting husk, and the ground’s changes are subtle until you are standing in them.
