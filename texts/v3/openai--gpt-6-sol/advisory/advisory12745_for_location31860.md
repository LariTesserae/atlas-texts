Prepare for this city as you would for a cold, high-altitude wilderness whose paths can change while you walk them. Its streets invite exploration, but they are not a reliable route home.

**Arrive self-sufficient.** Bring all your food and water; nothing visible grows, and there is no dependable source of either. Carry warm layers, a windproof outer layer, gloves, and a sleep system suited to cold stone. The air may be thin enough to affect breathing even though the wind is barely perceptible, so check conditions on arrival and bring breathing support if your means of travel allows it. Wear boots with good grip, a helmet for low openings and falling grit, and eye and respiratory protection against dust. Take your own lights and spare power: the sky gives enough light to walk by, not necessarily enough to find a safe way back.

**Make your way home independent of the streets.** Keep your exit point or return device secure, and confirm that it works *before* going far. If you can place it outside the shifting districts, do so. Travel with a companion and a means of communicating without sound; wind and distance may give little audible warning of separation. Record the time, direction, and position of each departure, but treat maps, footprints, and familiar façades as observations rather than promises. A route that took an hour outward may take much longer back. Set a strict turnaround time with ample supplies held in reserve.

Move slowly at stairs, arches, cliff edges, and intersections. Steps can acquire a riser, and a landmark can approach without the square between you changing size. Do not rely on an apparent doorway as an escape route: some stand storeys above the current street. The grit beneath walls may tell you where stone has been moving; fresh deposits, widening cracks, and shedding cornices are reasons to move on. Avoid resting directly below masonry, even if it looks ancient and settled.

Plan to sleep only if you must, and choose shelter after watching it for a while. A room may block the wind, but its walls are not necessarily still. Bring your own heat rather than counting on a warm wall to remain warm. Leave the stone animals alone and give them room at intersections; their paths may tell you that a passage exists, but not that a human can reach or use it.

Most importantly, resist the city's invitation to keep going just because the next street is visible. Explore in short stages, repeatedly verify your way out, and leave while you still have the resources to accommodate a much longer return than you expect.
