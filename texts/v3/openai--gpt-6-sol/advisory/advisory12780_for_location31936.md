Prepare for a small working district that is easier to overlook than to cross comfortably. There is no marked entrance, and the buildings are separated by tall weeds and open yards, so arrange an exact workshop address or meeting point rather than relying on a sign at the district boundary. In daylight, the three brick workshops and the pale gravel at the far end should help you orient yourself.

Wear sturdy, closed-toe shoes with good grip. The new gravel is uneven, the ground beneath it can be wet, and boards laid across the hollows may be slick with morning condensation. Long trousers will spare your legs the nettles. If you expect to enter a workshop, ask your host what protective equipment is required; the press, machinery, stacked pipe, and crane make this an active industrial site, not an empty riverside walk. Stay out of yards unless invited, and give moving carts room on the bridge.

Bring a light rain layer even if the forecast is dry, and keep anything that must stay dry off the ground. Allow extra time to find a passable route: follow the boards where they have been laid rather than cutting through weeds or testing the fresh gravel. Keep back from the low river bank, especially after high water. The channel has moved before, and the shelf is still settling.

The district will sound busier than it looks. Expect sharp press blows, rattling windows, a continuous motor note, and loud birds carrying across the open plots. If you need a conversation or quiet work, plan it away from the workshops. Before travelling between worlds, confirm that your address, contact method, and means of payment work locally; once there, a simple map and a prearranged meeting place will be more useful than looking for a gate that does not exist.
