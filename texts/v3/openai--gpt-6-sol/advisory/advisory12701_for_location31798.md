Prepare for a short, quiet visit rather than an expedition. The court is narrow, cool even at midday, and full of small living things that are easy to overlook.

- **Check that you may enter.** It lies between houses and someone maintains it; don’t assume it is public space.
- **Wear shoes with good grip** and bring a light layer for the cold. The steps are worn shallow, and a loose stone or mossy edge could make footing uncertain.
- **Travel light.** A bulky pack would catch on the walls or brush against the plants. Daylight is reflected down into the court, so you are unlikely to need a lamp for a daytime visit.
- **Watch where you put your hands and feet.** Keep to the sound, dry fitted stones. Leave the moss, ferns, drain, and covered cistern undisturbed; the damp spots shelter much of the court’s life, and the cistern is not a drinking-water source.
- **Move slowly and speak softly.** There is no sign of a dangerous creature here, but spiders, moths, beetles, and springtails occupy spaces smaller than you may notice at first.

Before stepping in, note which way leads back to the street. Then give yourself a moment to stand still: this is a place best visited without trying to explore every crack.
