Prepare for a cool, bright hike over unstable rock—not for an easy walk through greenery. The slabs have sharp edges, the paths are narrow, and the broken inspection stair is a warning that the ground has changed recently. Wear grippy boots, long sleeves, eye protection against branches, and gloves for unavoidable handholds. Keep well back from fresh breaks and overhangs; do not use the stair or the collector frames as supports.

Bring all the water you need. The basin looks lush, but there is no visible stream, and the moisture caught in leaves and stone cups may not be safe to drink. Carry sun protection as well as a light warm layer: the exposed crest is sunny while the air remains cool and the shaded growth stays wet.

Before entering, confirm that the air and local biology are safe for a human visitor, and clean your clothing and equipment to avoid carrying organisms between worlds. Travel with a companion, mark a return route, and allow more time than the clear view across the basin suggests. Vines, similar-looking ridges, and abrupt fractures could make a short-looking crossing difficult to retrace. Check conditions at dawn and after rain, when damp stone will be slickest.

Treat the pipes, cables, housings, and mesh as **live infrastructure**, even though they make no sound. Do not touch the cabinets, climb the frames, follow a cable into a crack, or drink collected water. The plants’ alignment with the works is striking, but it does not tell you whether the machinery is harmless, abandoned, or doing something essential. Observe from outside the aisles beneath the mesh unless you have permission and a sound reason to enter.

Finally, move quietly and leave yourself room to turn back. The absence of birds and the brief movement in the shrubs are reasons to pay attention, not to assume a threat. This is a place to visit lightly: photograph it, avoid disturbing the roots and moss that hold the thin soil in place, and leave before an unfamiliar ridge becomes hard to navigate in fading light.
