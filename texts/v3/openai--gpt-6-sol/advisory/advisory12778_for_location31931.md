Prepare for a long, damp walk through a working city, not an expedition into wilderness. The plateau is broad enough to have different weather in different districts, and water has gradually altered the streets. Bring a waterproof outer layer, shoes with good grip on wet stone and steps, and a dry bag for anything that must stay dry. A light layer underneath will help when you move between cool, exposed roads and warmer, vapor-filled lower streets.

Get a current local map rather than relying on the apparent straightness of a road. Some canals used to be streets, some streets have settled into steps, and raised roads may follow the rock rather than the shortest route. Allow extra time to cross districts, especially if rain or industrial haze cuts visibility. Ask locally which crossings and lower routes are open after heavy rain.

Treat the water as part of the city’s infrastructure and food system, not as a convenient drinking supply. Carry safe drinking water until you know the local supply, and avoid wading in canals, drains, and outfalls. Keep your hands clean before eating, particularly after touching wet railings or walls. Around pumping stations and culture works, stay on public routes: the basins and conduits are active workplaces, and apparently shallow water may conceal a drain or a drop.

Expect animals at close quarters. A hat is useful beneath bird-covered bridges and cornices; keep food covered, and give nests and drain mouths room. Moths may crowd lamps after dark, so a small personal light will be more useful than counting on factory lighting. Avoid disturbing fish or collecting anything from the canals without local guidance.

For a first visit, arrive in daylight and start in one district. Listen for work crews and freight traffic before crossing industrial routes; sound can carry strangely and linger in the haze. The city seems accustomed to its water, machinery, and animals. You will do best by moving at its steady pace, watching where you put your feet, and asking residents how a route works *now*, rather than how it looks on a map.
