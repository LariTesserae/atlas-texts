Treat this as an active, deep-water industrial site, not a place to explore by swimming. The floor may be visible from far above, but it is still miles away through cold water. An ordinary human should arrive inside a pressure-rated submersible or at a dry access point, with enough life support and power to leave if a gate cycle delays the return. Do not enter the water unless local operators can confirm the depth, pressure, temperature, and a safe way back.

Before traveling, arrange permission and a current chart with the basin’s operators. Ask for gate and pump schedules, intake exclusion zones, work-craft routes, and the locations of functioning docking or rescue points. Bring independent navigation: the lit towers are far apart, and the plains between them offer few landmarks. Carry redundant power, communications, lights, and an emergency locator; agree on a lost-contact procedure before setting out.

Keep well clear of trenches, drains, screens, and broad openings—even when the water looks still. The change in machinery noise may be the first sign of a gate cycle; by the time silt rises or warning lights appear, the current may already be too strong to escape. Never rely on fish holding station as evidence that it is safe for you. Stay out of construction areas and away from hinges, sensors, cables, and freshly fitted walls.

Expect long stretches of near-emptiness punctuated by immense machinery and sudden movement. Plan your route around the *return*, not just the next tower: in this basin, a clear view of the floor is no promise that you can reach it, and a quiet stretch of water is no promise that it will remain quiet.
