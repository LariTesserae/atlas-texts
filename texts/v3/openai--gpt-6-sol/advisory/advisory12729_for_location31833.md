Prepare for this as a **small, exposed cliff-top site with an unexplained water source**, not as a place where shelter and water are guaranteed to keep you safe.

Travel with a companion if possible, and agree on a return time with someone outside the site. Bring a reliable way to find the path back, a headlamp, a charged means of communication, and enough drinking water for the full trip. The clear pool may carry silt and living organisms, and its unusual behaviour gives you no reason to assume it is safe to drink. Pack heat protection—sun cover, light clothing, and electrolytes—but also a rain layer. Recent rain, standing water, hot air and a narrow rim make both heat illness and a sudden storm credible problems.

Approach the plateau slowly. Its sharp edges and fresh fractures may mean loose footing; wet black stone may be slippery. Stay well back from the drop, especially where an overhang hides the rock beneath your feet. Do not count on the ceramic shelter during lightning or driving rain: it fits only one person and is open on one side. If a storm is coming, leave by the path rather than waiting at the rim to see how it develops.

Treat the posts and their pool as **an observation site, not a resource to test**. Watch from a safe distance before crossing the wet ground. Don’t put a hand into the moving openings, lean on the posts, wade through the pool, or remain beside them as the water deepens in the afternoon or drains at evening. The water’s failure to flow downhill is enough to warrant caution even if nothing else appears dangerous.

Step around the few plants and their fractures; they may be the only stable footholds those plants have. Avoid bringing seeds or soil onto the rock, and carry everything you brought away with you. If you want to study the place, use photographs and notes taken from firm, dry ground. The most useful visit would be short, in good visibility, with enough time to reach the path again before dusk—after seeing the pool form, but without making yourself dependent on understanding why it does.
