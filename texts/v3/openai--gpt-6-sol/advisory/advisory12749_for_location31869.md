**Prepare to observe this chamber, not to occupy it.** It is barely person-sized, has no visible entrance, and is still changing shape. Unless you have a known, reversible way in *and* a way to extract someone lying down, a human visit is not worth attempting. Do not dig through the field to find it: you could collapse the ceiling or destroy much of what is living below.

If safe access is possible, go with a partner who remains outside and can bring you back without relying on your ability to crawl. Agree on a short visit and a firm recall signal. Check the air and the route first. A chamber this small may have too little breathable air or accumulate gas; its cool, even light tells you nothing about either. Avoid rain and the period immediately after it. Moisture comes through the ceiling, and an uneven space with no exit is a bad place to discover how quickly it can fill.

Wear close-fitting clothing, gloves, and eye protection that will not snag on the root or ceiling cracks. Bring a soft pad if there is room for one, rather than putting your weight on the sharp, loosely binding floor. Move slowly. The cups, shoots, grains, and nearly invisible animals are not obstacles to clear away; they are the place you came to see. Keep equipment minimal, clean, and secured so that nothing sheds, spills, or gets left behind. Do not touch the bright patch, the root, or the wet cup to test them.

Rain may give you a warning of sorts. Watch the droplets if they form, but treat their differing views as **possibilities, not a timetable**. A grub seen crossing in a drop may cross later—or may not. Likewise, do not use shadows to judge where an object is: here they appear only under peculiar conditions and need not fall beneath their source.

The best visit would be brief and quiet. Lie still, listen for movement in the grains and cups, and leave before the chamber’s small changes become a problem for your way out. If you cannot establish a safe exit in advance, send a small, non-disruptive camera instead and let the room continue becoming itself.
