Prepare to visit this town as you would a fragile archaeological site, not a place you can walk through. An ordinary human can stand beside it, but its central passage is barely wide enough for a hand. A sleeve, a misplaced foot, or even a tool could damage a bridge or block a drain.

Bring a small, diffuse light, a magnifier or close-focus camera, and shoes with good grip on wet stone. Keep your hands free while approaching the space beneath the stair; watch your head, and do not lean on the fitted shale. The roar from the crack may make conversation difficult and conceal other sounds, so agree on a signal with your companion before going in. Stay back from the lowest wall and the trembling bridges. Water is moving somewhere behind them, and you have no way to judge the strength of what holds it in.

Observe without rearranging anything. The mineral crust may look like an obstruction, but its repeated scraping suggests that the drains have been maintained deliberately; clearing one yourself could change where the water goes. Do not lift the cups, probe doorways, introduce food, or pour water into the channels. Check a drain mouth before placing a finger or camera there—the small shelled animals use them.

Expect your eyes to invent occupants in the dimness and your ears to find voices in the changing echoes. Take time to identify shapes rather than reaching toward them. Most of all, allow for the possibility that a town can be empty *now* without being abandoned. If you find evidence of residents or a caretaker, withdraw and seek permission before returning.
