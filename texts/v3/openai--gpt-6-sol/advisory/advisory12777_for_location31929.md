Prepare for this as a **self-supported trip across exposed stone**, not as a walk through an empty place. The valley has food and water for its resident animals, but very little that a human can use without disturbing them.

Before crossing over, confirm that the air, gravity, and temperature range are safe for you. Establish a reliable way back, and carry navigation that does not depend on spotting a trail: the broad, similar-looking stone and constant wind could make it easy to misjudge your position. Tell someone your route and return time if that is possible.

Bring all the water you expect to drink, plus a reserve. The damp cracks and brief rain pools are not a dependable supply; treat any water you do collect. Pack food, a first-aid kit, and a means of signaling that can be **seen**, since a voice or whistle may disappear beneath the wind.

Dress for fierce sun and cool, moving air at the same time: sun-protective clothing, hat, eye protection, sunscreen, and a warm windproof layer. Wear grippy boots. The shelf may look level while its shallow gullies catch your feet, and even settled rock slopes demand care. Carry a compact shelter or bivy if there is any chance of being delayed; do not count on finding a comfortable, unoccupied crack.

Travel in daylight, watch the weather, and keep out of gullies if rain threatens. Give the cliff seams and wind holes room. They offer tempting shelter, but they are also where birds nest, water collects, and animals retreat. Step around grass and lichen where you can, keep food secured, and carry away every trace of your visit—including waste and any marker you set for your return.

Most of all, plan your crossing conservatively. The valley may feel unchanging, but your water, warmth, and daylight are not.
