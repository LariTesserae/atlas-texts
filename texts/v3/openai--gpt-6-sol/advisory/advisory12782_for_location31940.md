Prepare for a **sunlit underwater dive**, not a walk along a ridge. The crest is broad, but it is still submerged, and the seabed drops into deep water on both sides. First establish the depth, current, water conditions, and whether the warm seepage has been tested. If you cannot confirm that the water is safe to enter and that you can make a controlled return to the surface, observe from a boat or remotely instead.

For a dive, go with a trained buddy and a surface team who know your route and expected return time. Bring suitable breathing equipment, a depth gauge or dive computer, a compass, a surface marker, and a reliable means of returning to the boat. Plan your gas and maximum depth before entering; the clear view across the ridge could make it easy to drift farther than intended or descend unnoticed along an edge.

The stone calls for particularly careful buoyancy. Its young, sharp planes could cut skin, a suit, or a hose, while loose fragments may give way if you try to stand on them. Aim to hover clear of the bottom. The dense green blades and fronds may brush against gear, and the wavering warm water can distort your view of what lies just beyond them. Neither the algae nor the warm seeps should be used as handholds or landmarks you trust without checking your instruments.

Treat the seepage as an **unknown vent system**. Clear, comfortably warm-looking water can still be too hot close to an opening or contain dissolved chemicals. Stay out of the fissures and avoid lingering in the rising columns until their temperatures and chemistry are known. The ridge appears young, so be willing to end the visit if you notice fresh debris, unusual bubbling, a sudden temperature change, or movement in a slope.

Finally, make this a low-contact visit. There is little bare surface, despite how open the ridge looks from a distance, and its new growth is easily damaged by a fin stroke. Secure your equipment, move slowly above the plants, and take nothing. The quiet and the distant blue are part of the attraction—but they are also reasons to keep your buddy, your depth, and your route home firmly in mind.
