Prepare for a warm, damp city where the streets may look freshly built even when they are not. Pack light rain protection, shoes with good grip, and something breathable for the humid air. The low cloud may make it easy to underestimate the daylight; bring a light if you expect to be out late. Keep electronics and papers protected from condensation and from water dripping beneath bridges, not just from rain.

Use the transit hall and bridges to plan your route. You can see distant districts across the open ground, but several kilometres of roads, plantings and channels may lie between you and them. Check where the crossings are rather than assuming a shallow-looking canal or drainage cut is a footpath. Its pools are warm and still, but their depth and footing may change around bends.

The most important local habit to learn is where *not* to stand or build. The crisp banks and paving are part of a city that carries wear away toward an eastern collection basin. A bank can feel solid while faintly vibrating underfoot; that alone need not be cause for alarm. Still, stay on established paths, leave the narrow stone drains and root seams clear, and ask before disturbing soil or planting anything. Treat the basin beyond the inhabited towers as a working site, not an empty lot: crews redraw its boundary as they remove material each morning.

Expect an unusual quiet. Without much loose debris underfoot, you may hear trams from far away, drops falling into drains and the small sounds of plants opening. Give yourself time to notice where the water goes. It will help you understand the city—and keep you from putting a bag, a boot or a campsite in the path of work that is quietly moving east.
