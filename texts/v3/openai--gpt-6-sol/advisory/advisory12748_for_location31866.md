Prepare for a hot, quiet journey through a living maze—not for a conventional cave, and not for a forest with an open sky.

**Make your return route the first priority.** The light comes from the roof rather than a visible sun, and one grove or root shelf may look much like another. Carry a map and a way to record your track, mark junctions with removable markers, and agree on a turnaround time. Don’t rely on the little arches and enclosures as signs of a trail or a settlement: they are stationary gaps in the growth, but they may lead nowhere useful. Bring a headlamp and spare power even if you expect the vaults to remain bright.

**Carry all the water you expect to need, plus a reserve.** The place is hot and nearly still, yet its visible water arrives a drop at a time. The basins are not dependable refill points. If you must use one, treat its water first. Bring food, electrolytes, and a means of monitoring yourself for heat exhaustion; take planned rests rather than waiting until you feel thirsty or confused.

Wear light, breathable clothing with long sleeves, sturdy shoes with good grip, and a hat or light helmet where the roof hangs low. Gloves will help on fibrous shelves, and eye or respiratory protection may be worthwhile if pollen troubles you. Pack for walking over roots whose firm-looking backs may conceal gaps or slick edges. Test each footing before committing your weight, and avoid climbing on tender stems.

Before going far in, check that the air is breathable. The stillness and dense vegetation make a portable air-quality monitor sensible, especially in low enclosures. Don’t use an open flame: dry pollen and plant surfaces are reason enough to avoid it.

Travel lightly and leave the growth intact. Cutting an apparent wall or clearing an apparent path could damage the same rootwork that supports your footing elsewhere. Use the existing gaps, keep water basins clean, and don’t assume the pale wing or the brief movement in the foliage represents either a threat or a reliable food source.

Most of all, resist the temptation to judge distance by visibility. You may be able to see every leaf far ahead without seeing an easy way to reach it—or an easy way back.
