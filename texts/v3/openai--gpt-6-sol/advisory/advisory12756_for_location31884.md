Prepare for a **working, water-managed neighborhood**, not a ruin reclaimed by plants. People still use the workshops, clear the drains, repair the brickwork, and operate the pumps. Find out which lanes are public, ask before entering yards or buildings, and leave room for workers moving timber and equipment.

Wear shoes with good grip and closed toes. The paving, loading steps, and ironwork may stay slick after the rain has stopped; silt and roots can hide uneven edges. Bring light rain protection, breathable clothes, and a dry bag for documents and electronics. If damp air or mold bothers you, carry any medication you normally need. Drink carried or verified potable water rather than canal or runoff water.

Before setting out, learn where the public exits are and whether pump operations or changing canal levels can close a route. Keep back from gates, drainage openings, the canal edge, and the railway embankment. The motor, saws, and wagons may mask a shouted warning, so pay attention when crossing a yard rather than relying on what you can hear. The leafy lanes may also be dimmer than they look from above; a small flashlight and an offline map would help.

As a multiverse traveler, confirm that the air, water supply, and local rules are safe for an ordinary human before arrival. Once there, the best approach is unhurried: expect humidity, dripping walls, patchy afternoon light, and vegetation that has been *managed*, not left alone. Don’t pull at vines or step into planted edges just to get a better view—roots may be holding silt against an old curb, and the clear paths are clear for a reason.
