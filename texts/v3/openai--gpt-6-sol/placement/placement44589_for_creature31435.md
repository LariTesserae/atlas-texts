I would find myself at the foot of the eastern wall, under the sand that gathers there against the current.

At first I might think I was part of the wall. I would have growth along my back, and when I held still, sand would settle between my limbs. A small creature could cross me without changing course. Then the cold water would arrive. The stems would bow, the bare lanes would widen, and I would lift myself from the stone.

I would be one of the wall-dwellers, though not a large one. My gripping limbs would know every softened joint in the enclosure. My finer limbs would comb the pale fans after a current passed, gathering what the water had brought. I would carry a little garden on my back without tending it much. Fronds would be lost and grow again; some roots might find their way past my shell and into the wall. Staying still long enough, I might have to pull gently to get free.

There would be a loose stone near the eastern wall. I would move it back when I found it elsewhere. Not because the wall needed mending—the piece would fit nowhere now—but because sand caught at its foot, and the sand held food. Sometimes the current would take it beyond the enclosure. I would wait until the stems lay flat, then go after it across the exposed rock.

I would not always know how far I had gone. Once, a channel I could cross in a few strokes might hold me for hours on the way home. I would pause at its edge and feel along the warm stone before entering. Perhaps I would find the passage the others used. Perhaps I would arrive late, with no account of where I had been. Either way, if the stone were small enough, I would bring it back.

When the broad shadow crossed above the rim, I would flatten myself beside the wall. Its disturbed water would touch the fronds, and green light would travel over them, close enough to show the sediment moving inside a thin creature sheltering underneath. I would remain still until it passed.

Then I would set the loose stone down. Sand would begin to gather around it, though the water was running the other way. I would put my combing limbs into the new drift and feed there awhile, with the warm wall against my side.
