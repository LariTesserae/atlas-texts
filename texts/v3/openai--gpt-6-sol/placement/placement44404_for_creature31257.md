I think you would first find me in the passage, looking like a piece of root that had come loose. I would drift an inch when a rack moved and settle again, caught against the same bolt. There are so many roots here that no one would look twice.

But I would not have come loose. I would be growing from the narrow space beneath that bolt, where no cartridge was meant to go. I would have no number and very little room: a few pale threads in the wall, one soft stem reaching into the passage. Each time the rack advanced, I would bend out of its way. For a long while, that would be almost all I knew of the garden.

Eventually the others would find me through the gaps between plates. A change in pressure would arrive from the far wall, pass through me, and continue below the floor. I might learn to answer, though not very well. If someone swam past, I would feel the water move long before I could turn toward them.

I would grow nearest the clear glass. The seam would be too far away to touch, but on bright days I could see the clouds traveling beneath it. I would stay where I was rooted while the leaves above me turned toward the roof, then slowly turn with them.
