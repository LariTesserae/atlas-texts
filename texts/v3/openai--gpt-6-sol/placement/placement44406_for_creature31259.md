At first I think I would be a drop beneath the cold pipe: held there for a while, then gone into the floor before the next one forms. But a drop would know this chamber only in passing. I would want to stay long enough to learn its slow changes.

So I would be one of the fine roots descending from the vines on the ladder. Not the thick curtain visible against the wall, but a tip that has reached the fracture and begun to enter it. Above me, the vine has leaves and a seam that sweats after rain. Below, there is room only because the slab has broken.

I would follow the moisture past the clean, sharp aggregate. In the cooler hour, water would reach me from the pipe; when the air grew hot again, I would remain in the deeper damp. The pale growth would find my surface and run alongside it. Neither of us would lead the other. We would meet because we had found the same narrow way through.

From the open floor, no one could see where I was. They might notice that the crack looked unchanged, or that its edges had grown a little soft. I would be just beneath those edges, going farther in.
