At first I think I would be one of the pale animals beyond the collector towers. There is room out there that the city cannot account for. I like the thought of appearing for a moment beside an amber mark, then banking away before anyone can decide how far off I am.

But I would keep coming back to the same tower. I would want to know why a shutter had failed to open.

So you would find me on the upper walks, probably human, carrying a lamp with its light turned low. I would be responsible for a short stretch of city: two collectors, the stair between them, and the bridge below. Not for making them work alone. For knowing how they sound when they do.

I would learn the long pause before the eastern collector closes, and the quicker crash of the western one. I would know which folded creature ticks against the bridge rail when the wind comes up from the valley. If its ticking stopped, I might find only that it had moved to a warmer beam. I would check anyway.

My pockets would hold ordinary things: a key for a shutter housing, a strip of cloth for a frozen joint, something to eat while I waited for a mechanism to turn. There would be a small notebook too. In winter I would take it down to the pier and compare the newest crystal edge with the marks scratched there by people before me. I might add a date without being quite certain the edge had moved.

Some nights I would reach the stair and find a procession of shelled animals crossing it. I would stand back with my lamp covered, watching each one follow the damp line left by the last. The collector could wait another minute. If it could not, that would tell me something I needed to know.

I don't think I would be especially brave in the cloud. I would keep one amber mark in sight until I had the next. But if I woke to the cistern wall gone quiet, I would put on my boots. I would need to hear whether the silence ended at my door.
