At first I think I would be the wind. It is the only thing that travels the whole street, entering empty rooms and making each one sound different. But it cannot stay anywhere. By evening it has gone, leaving the shutters still loose and the courtyard much as it found it.

I would be one of the seeds lodged between the paving stones.

I would know the town from very low down: a stripe of light at noon, heat stored in the clay, dust settling around my husk after the last gust. The courtyard would be near enough to smell when the sun warmed its leaves, but hidden behind a wall. I would not know whether the crack beneath me held enough depth to live in.

If it did, I would begin downward. Not toward the daylight where nothing grows, but into the narrow dark between stone and brick, following whatever trace of dampness the thicket’s roots had found. Above me the street might remain bare for a long time. The wind would go on playing the empty rooms. My first sign of life would be where no one could see it: a small pressure against the underside of a stone.
