At first I think I would be a traveler. I would set out for the white notch with some sensible measure of provisions, lose track of the pool, and eventually learn to stop looking at anything farther away than the next rock.

But I don’t think I would keep walking. I would find myself near one of the narrow passages in the scree: a flat, dark stone with one edge exposed beside a pool. A hand could rest on me without having to reach into the water. Beneath my other edge, a pale animal could pass and leave its warmth for a little while.

I would not know the way to the notch. From where I lay, it might appear on either side of the pool. I would know the weight of a traveler’s hand when they chose me, and the lighter, quicker pressure of feet that already knew this patch of ground. Sometimes the animal would stop under me at the click of a pebble. The great note would go on, felt through my whole underside, giving no warning of anything.

If someone used me to begin the next short stretch, that would be enough. They could let go when they had chosen another rock. I would remain where touch had found me, whether or not the valley looked the same when they turned back.
