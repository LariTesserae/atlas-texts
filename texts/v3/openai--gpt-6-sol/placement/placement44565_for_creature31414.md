I would be behind the abandoned valve wheel, in the gap where warm air rises without becoming a draft. At first, there would be almost no difference between me and the chamber: heat passing through a shell, a distant knock arriving through brick and iron, the faint pressure of the gecko settling beside me.

Then the shell would give.

I would come out small enough that the gap still felt like a room. Beyond it, the chamber would not be thirty paces wide. It would be a short climb down to the basin, an open stretch of plate, a seam I could disappear into if something moved. I would learn the pipe by its drops before I ever saw it. Each one would strike the worn porcelain hard enough to feel through my feet.

The older gecko would know when to wait there. I might learn that too, but not on the first night. On the first night I would stay close to the wheel, with the heat at my back, watching a silverfish cross the brown tide mark and vanish while I was still deciding whether to move.
