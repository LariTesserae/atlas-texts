At first I would stand by the entrance, trying to decide who had built the shelter. I would touch a peg, find its upper edge worn smooth, and imagine someone who could lift a bundle down from it. I would wait for that someone to return.

After a while I would sit. The rattle would stop being a wall of sound. I’d hear a caller beneath the tree answer one under the fleshy blades, and then hear the first fall quiet while the second continued. I would begin to notice the creatures that made no sound at all.

I think I would be one of those: a small, pale soft-backed creature living in the seam below the shelter. Not yet ridged enough to call, I would spend my time where the roots enter stone, feeding on their thin oil and carrying dry husks out of the way. The black bowl would be too high and too smooth for me. I might shelter beneath its rim without ever knowing what it was for.

As my covering hardened, I would cross farther over the bare floor. I would learn which sharp chips offered shade, which leaves had been freshly rasped, which crack held other soft-backed young. One day I would draw a hooked leg across my shell and hear the stone send the sound back to me, larger than the sound I’d made.

I would still pass through the little shelter. Whatever used the pegs would remain a mystery. But I would know, by then, that the chamber only *seems* to make one continuous noise. It is many creatures taking their turns. Mine would be one of them.
