At first I think I would be in the shelter, counting the marks. I would wake, add one, and try to decide whether the spaces between them meant anything. After a while I would leave the blade alone. The wall could tell me how often I had slept, but not how much farther away the ridge had become.

I would follow the chipped footholds to the new cliff and sit where the listener sits. Perhaps they would find me there and take me for another person. We would not need to speak over the mountain’s note. They would show me how to keep my hand below the seam, close enough to feel the falling grit without touching the blade caught in the rock.

But I don’t think I would remain a person for long in this place. I would be found at the base of that blade: a pale curl, easy to miss against the fresh stone. My silver rings would be too thin to grind. The cliff would give me almost no room to open, so I would grow slowly, taking hold of the strain on one side and then the other.

The listener would come back after a sleep and see a little more green. I would know they were there by the warmth of a hand held beneath me and by the brief shelter it made from the dust. I could not tell them when the cliff would finish forming, or whether the thicket would survive being divided.

I would keep growing into the narrow place between its two sides. For now, that would be enough room.
