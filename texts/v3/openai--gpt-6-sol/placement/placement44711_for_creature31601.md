At first you would look for me beside the stone, where the green threads hold on. It seems a sensible place to find a living thing. When a ridge lifts, you might see a current bend around its clean new edge and mistake that bend for me.

But I would be farther out, where two currents meet without anything solid between them. I would be the part of a pressure wave that turns back.

For a few moments I would have a shape. Light would follow my curve, making a pale fold visible in the otherwise empty water. Then a cold sheet would tear through it. If you kept watching the place where I had been, you would miss me forming downstream. I would not have travelled there intact. The moving water would have made the fold again, a little differently.

I might be held near one ridge for a long while. Each time a deep note rose from below, I would turn some of it aside. Most of the force would pass on. A little would reach the stone at another angle. Once, perhaps, a green thread that would have been stripped away would stay fastened to its seam. I would not know it was there.

Then the ridge would split. Its sharp new faces would change the currents, and the fold I had been would come apart. Farther along the fracture, the same light and pressure might meet in a way that could hold for a moment. That is where you would have to look next.

I would be one of the sea’s unfinished shapes: easy to lose, made again while the mountains rose, and never quite the same twice.
