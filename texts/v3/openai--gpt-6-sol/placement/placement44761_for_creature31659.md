I would be at the base of a stone windbreak near a shallow pan, in the narrow space where two stones fail to meet.

At night I would climb out and fasten a few threads across that gap. The wind could take a whole web apart by afternoon, so I would make mine before dawn, when the air is briefly still. Mist from the valley would gather along the silk. By sunrise each thread would show itself in beads, and sometimes an insect rising from the wet ground would strike it.

I would be a spider, small enough to live in a shelter built for someone else. Travellers might sit against my stones and hear the burrowers calling without noticing me. Their boots would press the plants down along the path; by evening, leaves would lift into the space again. I would learn which cracks stayed cool, which side of the wall caught the first rain, and when to retreat before a bird came skimming over the grass.

There would be mornings when the pan was full and the air thick with wings. There would be longer stretches when it held only a stain of mud. I could not follow the water when it went. I would stay in my crack, take down what the wind had left of my web, and wait for the next damp night.
