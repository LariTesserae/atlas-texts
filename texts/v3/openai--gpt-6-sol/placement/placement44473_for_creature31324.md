At first I would look for a place above the ground. I’d want to see how the branches join, and perhaps catch sight of a long-tailed walker before it noticed me. But I don’t think I would stay there. There would always be water sounding somewhere below, and I would follow it down.

I’d end up at the stone circle that holds a pool, in the narrow space where a block has tipped against a root. Leaves lodge there. Rain runs down the stone, slows in the moss, and reaches the pool one drop at a time. A frog could sit within arm’s reach and remain almost impossible to see.

I wouldn’t be the frog. I think I’d be the moss in that seam: a small, dark-green growth with water caught among its stems. A heavy animal might brush past and press me flat; a seed might root in me for a season. I would know the rain first by its weight, then by the long hours of dripping after it had stopped.

The stones were put there for a reason I could not discover. From my place between them, I would take in what fell, hold a little of it, and let the rest pass into the pool.
