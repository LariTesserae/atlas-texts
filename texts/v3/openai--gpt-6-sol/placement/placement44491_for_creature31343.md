I think I would first mistake myself for a visitor. I would stand at the rim and look for a way down, waiting for the heat to ease. But there are no tracks in the powder. Even a visitor would leave something behind.

So I would be lower than that: one of the pale roots beneath a frond, farther out under the bare stone than its leaves have any reason to reach.

For most of the year I would do almost nothing. I would hold fast while the floor rose by a fraction and a new seam pressed against me. When the hum came, I would feel it before the leaves turned. The three clicks would travel through my whole length, though no part of me could say where they began.

I would not grow toward the other plants. Each has its own opening to attend to. Instead I would follow the seam until I reached a dark edge left by a shadow—an edge that was not there before the opposite rim drew away. I would enter it slowly. Perhaps it would be the body of the thing beneath us; perhaps only a place that body had made and left unfinished. A root need not know the difference before taking hold.

Above me, my frond would lower one bright vein to the stone. Beneath it, I would touch the darkness without closing the gap. For an hour, or most of a day, we would remain like that while the metal grains settled into lines.

When the hollow became small again, I would still be inside the new edge. From the surface there would be only a plant standing apart from the others, with plenty of bare stone around it. I would be the part of it that knew the stone had briefly been wider—and was growing into the room it had kept.
