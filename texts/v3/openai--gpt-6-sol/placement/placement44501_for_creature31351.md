I would start at the surface, I think, with my hands on the rim. I would want to see the lower peaks and know how such a small lake could pour away in every direction.

But I would not stay there. I would be in the cleft beneath the three stones—not one of the stones, and not the creature behind the wall. I would be the little slowing of water behind the uppermost one.

That sounds like a place rather than a being, until something enters it. A strand of weed lies down instead of tearing free. A grain of grit turns once, twice, and settles within reach of a thin strip from the wall. The creature can extend itself because, for the length of that strip, the current has loosened its hold.

I would not be still. Each knock through the mountain would flatten me; each shift of the stones would make me somewhere slightly different. If the upper stone came free, I would be gone. Perhaps another flat fragment would wedge there later, and the water behind it would slow again. I would not know whether that was me returning.

The knot would stream above me, pointing into the passage. I could not follow it. I would know that passage only by what it draws away, and the creature only by what reaches into me. On clear days, light would pass through, and for a moment I would hold the fine shadows of its strips against the rock.
