I think you would find me at the edge of a fern cluster, though not among the fronds. I would be pressed into the narrow place where their roots end and bare stone begins, under a depth of water too slight to call a pool.

At first I might seem to be something the rain had left there: a soft dark stain, or a piece of the green film loosened from the seam. Then the edge of me would draw inward. I would be one of the flat animals, still small enough that the pale line in the rock could be seen faintly through my back.

That seam would be most of my world. It would bring food within reach and hold moisture after the open floor had dried. I might spend a whole warm day going no farther than the next shallow cup. If rain came, I would move out over the stone, the folds beneath me taking turns to grip and let go. I would feel the change in the water before there was enough of it to flow.

As I grew, I might venture up the rounded wall. From inside the hollow, that would be a long journey. I would stop where a ledge held a cool bead against the rock, and remain while the light changed. By evening there would be a damp margin behind me, more visible than I was.

I would not know the height of the hill or the land falling away from it. I would know which cracks stayed wet longest. When the water thinned, I would return to one of them and hold still against the stone, waiting without needing to count the time.
