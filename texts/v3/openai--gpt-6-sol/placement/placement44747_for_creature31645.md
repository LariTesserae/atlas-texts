At first I would want to be a bird, just long enough to see the plateau whole. But the view from above would flatten everything that matters. I would come down to a ridge, then follow a wet crack until even a bird could no longer fit.

I think I would live there: one of the translucent creatures that opens against the stone when the sun reaches it. I would have no use for the far edge of the plateau. My world would be the width of a fissure, a little stored warmth, and the water that gathers where two leaves touch.

In full light I would spread my thin lobes over the rock. I would feel the passage of a herd first as a pressure deep in the stone, then as a brief loss of sun. A shell would seat itself at the seam beside me and leave with its groove full. If a bird stepped close, I would fold away. It might drink the film I left behind; by the time its shadow passed, another film would be forming.

That seems like enough of a life here: opening when there is light, closing when there is danger, and knowing the rare rain by the changed sound of everything above me before a drop reaches the crack.
