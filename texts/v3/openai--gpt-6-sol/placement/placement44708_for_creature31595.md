I would be near the outer rim, where a lane narrows before it turns back toward the tower. From there the salt plain would be visible in a strip between two leaves. The tower would be hidden, though I might feel a little of its warmth through the rock.

At first I think I would belong to the lane: something small enough to know its turns and to notice when a doorway had grown tight. But I keep returning to the place that makes the turn. A seed has lodged in a crack there, on the side nearest the drop. Its roots have found the warm breath. Above them, one rib has risen into the path.

I think I would be that young growth.

For a long time I would have no shape anyone could name. The grazers would take the tender leaves that reached into their route, and my other leaves would spread toward the unoccupied light. Their passing would leave one side of me low and silver-scraped. On the other side, beyond their mouths, I would slowly meet an older wall. Where our ribs touched, we would harden together.

Perhaps that would make me part of a doorway. Perhaps, seasons later, a grazer would have to turn sideways to pass beneath me. I wouldn’t know either thing as it happened. I would know the brief pressure of a soft mouth, the loss of a leaf, the wax cooling over its torn place. A winged creature might rest on a rib I had held still long enough for it to trust. If a flower opened there, it might carry dust away into a courtyard I could never reach.

My roots would stay in their narrow crack. From the plain, I would scarcely alter the green crown on the ridge. Inside, I would be one small part of the reason the lane had to bend.
