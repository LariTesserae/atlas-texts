At first I think I would be one of the pale grains falling from the clay: small enough to enter this place without disturbing it, slow enough to spend an afternoon between the walls. But a grain can only settle. I would want some way to remain and find out what the distance does.

So I would be a root tip, the white, nearly transparent end of a root descending through a crack in the broken bank.

I would have no view from above. The crack would give way to cold water, and I would grow into it because growing is what I do. There would be clay behind me and, ahead, leaves that fold aside only a little. I would reach the first pebble wall without knowing it was a wall. There is space between its stones. I would pass through.

Farther on, I would meet the surface that stops the falling grains. My tip would turn along it, as it turns on meeting stone. Then the creature’s fringe would brush against me. For a while I might grow beside its clear body, close enough to feel the hairs flatten when the dull tap comes, though the tap would give me no reason to stop.

The creature laid stones at the edges of what it could feel. I would never know those edges. The root behind me would remain in the clay; my tip would keep entering water that seemed no different from the water before it. Perhaps, after a long time, I would pass through the second wall as easily as the first.

I could not tell the creature I had done so. I would leave no sign it could read. But along part of its length, where it had once lain alone on the unseen surface, there would be a root for its hairs to touch.
