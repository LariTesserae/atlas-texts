At first I would think I belonged in the pump house, behind the amber window, learning to tell one gate from another by its pulse. I would want a map of the channels and a dry place to keep it. But by morning the map would already be wrong.

I think I would be a willow cutting caught on a fresh bank. Not one of the crowded, established shoots yet: just a branch carried in by the flood, pinned where gravel has spilled over peat. For a while I would look like debris. Rain would weigh down my leaves. Water would lift one end of me and settle it somewhere else.

Then, if the current left me alone long enough, I would put roots into the dark beneath the stones. They would not stop the next flood. They might hold a little gravel in place, enough for a narrow burrow to stay open above one tide, or for a bird to find a strip of mud beside me when the water fell.

I would be on an edge, but I would never know for how long. At night I would feel the wrong stillness before the gate released. By morning, water might run on my other side. If I remained, I would grow toward the light over the new channel. If I broke loose, some part of me might catch farther down and begin again.
