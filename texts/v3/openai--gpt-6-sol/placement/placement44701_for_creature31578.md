At first I think I would be one of the fallen stones: settled deeply enough to have made a place for myself, warm on top and cool underneath. But rain would reach me only as a change in temperature. I would want to know when to climb.

So I would be a lizard beneath the lip of the basin, the one with the damaged foot. Not because the injury makes me important. It makes a crossing longer than it ought to be, and gives me a reason to learn the edges of things: the narrow shade below the rim, the stone that stays warm after the beam has moved, the quickest way above a groove when its first thread of water appears.

On a dry morning I would come down to the empty basin anyway. A beetle might be lodged in its shallow curve. I would try its shell with my jaws, then take up a flake of flint and strike until it opened. Afterward I would leave the flake where it fell. Carrying it would slow me on the way back.

For a while, my passage between two stones would show in the grit. Then rain would come, and I would cling to the wall while water crossed the floor below. By the next dry spell, the path would be gone. I would still know the way.
