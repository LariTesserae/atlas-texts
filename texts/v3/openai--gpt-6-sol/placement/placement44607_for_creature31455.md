At first I would think I was the one who came up the ladder: too large for the room, afraid to put my foot down. I would hold the knotted cord while the builders worked and believe that keeping it taut was useful.

Then a wall would grow past the sixth knot. I would look at the cord, then at the wall, and loosen my grip. The builders would turn the extra length toward the floor and make a channel of it. I would help press its joints. By evening, water would be running through a place none of us had meant to build.

I think that is where I would find myself: in the return at the end of the wall. Not one of the builders, exactly, but the small bend that gives an unexpected length somewhere to go. I would hold while moss took root in my seams. I would feel their feet pause against me when the moth opened its wings, and the long scrape would pass through me before I heard it.

Each morning they would measure again. Some mornings I might still be a wall. On others, water would have widened my channel, or a fern root would have raised one tile enough to let me see a little sky. I would not know whether we had climbed higher in the night. I would know that the water had a way through.
