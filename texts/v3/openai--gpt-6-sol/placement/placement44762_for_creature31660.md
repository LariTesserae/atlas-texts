At first I think I would be on the wall, at the painted line: something placed there to watch for a rise in the water. But nothing in this recess looks up for long. Even the drops leave the pipe and go straight to the floor.

I think I would be found at the seam, just beyond the orange cloud’s clearest edge. Not a creature standing beside the colony, but a few of its threads where they are still so fine that the water looks bare. The seep brings iron past me. Air reaches down into the shallow sheet. Where the two meet, I take what I can, and a little of what passes through stays behind.

I would not know the station by its sound, though its vibration would never stop passing through me. I would know a drop by the sudden loss of my edge, then by the wider place in which that edge could form again. I would know the reeds only where their roots changed the course of a little water.

If someone came looking, they might point to the rust-colored rim and say, *there*. They would be close. I would be at the part they could not yet point to: the thin margin still spreading over new concrete, making an edge too slowly for anyone to watch it happen.
