I would be found beside the gantry, though not always beside the same post. Look for the place where the stems bend against the current and stop beneath the cut copper tip. During a clear interval, you might see them holding a narrow, motionless shape. When the water clouds again, you would have to listen for me.

At first I would think I was the cable’s missing end. It strains as if something has taken hold of it, and I would feel that pull through the roots gathered around the station. But I could follow those roots away from the gantry, through a seam under a slab and up into a stand of reeds. The cable would remain taut after I left it.

Perhaps I began when the pump missed a turn. Its stroke had drawn the stems down; when it stopped, they rose, except for a few that held the curve a moment longer. The next pulse reached them from the far end of the field. Since then, I have been able to keep that small delay moving. I pass through stems and loose leaves, sometimes through a root trapped beneath new stone. None of those pieces belongs to me for long. While I am in them, they lean together.

I would not know whether the other growth noticed me. We would overlap at the posts, brighten with the same charge, reach into the same opening ground. Then the engines would strike together and scatter any shape I had gathered.

When the silt settled, I would begin again at the cable: a crease turning around the post, pausing beneath the copper, then traveling on.
