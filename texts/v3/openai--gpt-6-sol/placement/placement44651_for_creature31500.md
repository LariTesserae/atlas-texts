At first I would look for myself in one of the pumping houses. The windows are lit, the switches labeled; surely there is a desk somewhere with a coat left over its chair. I would try the doors in order and find that none of them had been locked.

But I don’t think I would be the person who left the coat. I would be what made them leave it.

You would find me under the stone, moving along a fold too slowly to be heard over the turbines. I would have no chamber of my own, no body that an instrument could isolate. At one pumping house, a needle would lift by the width of a hair. Hours later, the needle in the next house would do the same. Across the bare rock between them, I would still be travelling.

I think I began as a correction too small to make. Some pressure rose where it should have fallen; someone marked it down and meant to return. The machinery kept running. The mark remained on the page. Whatever had caused it went on beneath the works, passing through stone that was supposed to hold still.

The stems would know when I came near. Not enough to grow a joint or turn a leaf, only enough to keep a bead of water where it was. In a cutting, I might pause beneath one of them while a visitor stood listening. I would feel the weight of that drop through the seam.

If the turbine faltered, the other drops would fall. Mine would wait through the silence, and through the sound coming back.
