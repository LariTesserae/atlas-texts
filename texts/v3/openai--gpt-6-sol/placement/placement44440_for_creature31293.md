At first I think I would live in one of the houses nearest the wall, where the stone holds a little warmth after sunset. But I keep finding myself outside it: beneath the drying racks at dawn, on a stair no one has used since yesterday, beside a bridge whose far end is beginning to settle into the water.

I would be the person who goes back after a channel has been turned.

Others would have lifted the stones and laid the plank. I would wait until their footsteps were gone, then walk the old route slowly, pressing each stair with the end of a pole. Where ice had grown over a gap, I’d set a small wedge upright so no one mistook the white surface for ground. I’d move it again when the hollow changed its mind.

It sounds like work for someone cautious. Perhaps I would be. But I think I’d also be curious: kneeling to watch beetles gather beneath a new sheet of ice, or noticing that the white-throated birds had found running water before any wet line showed on the stones. I would know the neighbours by the planks stored under their roofs and the children by how confidently they tested a crossing. When one of them asked whether a path was safe, I wouldn’t say *yes*. I’d show them where to listen.

By evening my sleeves would be wet, and someone would have moved one of my wedges. I’d be glad they had. It would mean they’d seen something change before I did.
