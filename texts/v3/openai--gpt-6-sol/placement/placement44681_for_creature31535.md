You would find me at the newest crease, though not always at the distance you expected. I would be the dark patch that remains after something has been lifted away.

At first there was probably a fallen twig, or the grub resting with its stone held crosswise in its mouth. The leaves turned toward that small interruption of the light. By morning they had grown around its outline. When it left, they kept their angle, and the place inside them stayed dim. That is where I began: not quite a shadow, not quite the root’s memory of one.

I would have no face to show you. You might notice me because the leaves at the new fold were all reaching toward a patch of air where nothing stood. If you walked closer, I would seem a few steps away for longer than I should. I would not know whether I was doing that, or whether the crease was doing it to both of us.

The knocking would reach me first. Each beat would make the young leaves tremble without turning. When the seam opened and the hollow took its warm breath, I would grow thinner; the veins would shine through me. Afterward the leaves would lean in again.

The grub would be right to avoid this edge. I could not hurt it, but its stone would cast a hard little shadow, and the root would learn that shape quickly. I think I would try to keep still when it passed—let it find its pore before the leaves began to follow it.

Most days there would be no one to see me. The root would go on making room, and I would stay in the part it had not measured yet.
