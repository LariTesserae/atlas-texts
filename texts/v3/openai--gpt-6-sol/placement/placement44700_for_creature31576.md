I would come in thinking I was there to measure the room. I’d count the three steps, mark the floor, and try to establish which frame made the opposite wall appear nearer. I would set a stone beside the slab so I could find the same place when I returned.

Then I would hear the stone set down behind me.

I think I’d spend a long time trying to turn quickly enough to catch whoever had done it. Eventually I might understand that turning was beside the point. The inhabitant had noticed my arrival, but had not yet finished with it. Somewhere it could occupy, my hand was still lowering the stone.

So that is where I would find myself: beside the slab, and also, in some small way, still on the third step. I would be an arrival the room had not finished receiving.

I’d leave the stone where it was. On later visits I could look for my mark, even if it had come to rest on the wrong side of a seam, and listen without turning when the quieter contact came. It would be a modest way to keep company with something that cannot hurry: letting it take as long as it needs to notice I am there.
