At first I think I would be up in the open water, travelling from lamp to lamp. There is room there to see a gate begin to move, and time to turn away.

But I keep looking down.

I would be one of the flat grey animals under the lip of an old panel, where the metal has lifted just enough to make a roof. From above, I would look like the film the crews mean to remove. My world would be a few bolts, a drain slot, the edge of a trench. I would know the broad plain chiefly through what it sends past my mouth.

I would live near a new mounting frame, though not inside it. For a season its welds would shelter the young, and I would cross the clean panel to feed there. Then a work craft would arrive with the missing part. I would feel its tools through the floor and retreat to my older seam before the frame went bare.

I would know the gate first as a change in the steady shudder beneath me. When it came, I would let go, skim low across the panels toward its rim, and fasten down again before the water pulled hard. There would be food in that first rush, if I held my place.

After the gate closed, I would wait for the silt to settle. Then I would come out into the lamp light and feel along each bolt, looking for what the water had left.
