At first I think I would be one of the pale animals. I can imagine the long wait on an open flat, feet spread wide, a rib drawing nearer while I remain perfectly still. But I would always be looking for a seam. That is where I think I would find myself: not inside one, but *as* one.

I would be a narrow opening on the shaded side of a rib, just wide enough for a family to enter. The plateau would carry me a little farther each time the stone turned. Some mornings I would open onto a flat where there had been only rock; on others I would have to wait for the powder to pass before my sheltered side was sheltered again.

I would feel the animals arrive through their feet. They would bring scraps of dark growth, eat some, and tuck the rest into me. When the crack that moves the rib came near, I would feel its first loosening long before there was anything to see. I would want to close with the rest of the stone. Most seams do. Instead, I would hold a little space, even when the two sides pressed together.

That would be all I could do for them. I could not warn an animal crossing a flat, or pull one free if another seam caught it. After a turning, I might open farther along the rib and let a hungry family out beneath a sky they did not recognize.

Perhaps, over many such turnings, my edges would become as blunt and pale as any old break. A small animal would find me by chance and take my age for safety. I would be glad when it came in, and uneasy until it came out.
