I would be in the bank, though it might take me a while to understand that I was moving.

At first I would think I was the cold held in the pale clay, or the dark line where the peat rests on it. Then a grain would loosen. Water would pass around it, and I would pass with the water: a thin seep finding the small opening beneath the moss.

The creature would stand in my path with its clear fringes stirring. I could not carry it anywhere, but I could bring cold water past its head and take away the cloud of clay its feet had raised. When it backed into its opening, I would run over the grains it had pressed into place. Some would hold. One might shift by morning.

I would feel the plant before I heard it. Each heavy strike would tighten the clay around me for an instant, then let it go. The creature would grip the bank; I would go on at nearly the same rate. By evening, when its pale back disappeared into the cut, I would still be coming through—no wider than a thread, clear again.
