At first I would put myself at the far edge, where the whole shelf can be seen at once: green against warm stone, the drop beyond it, the wall holding its line. But I don’t think I could stay there. From that distance the green patches seem joined, and I would keep wanting to find out whether they are.

I would cross the bare stone carefully and end up kneeling beside the seam that never dries. Up close, I might mistake a soaked leaf for the creature, then watch its edge draw inward as a drop passes. I would wait for it to extend again. After a while I would notice the thin growth it grazes, and realize that is where I belong.

I would be a film on the stone, too slight to have an edge of my own. Cold water would reach me from beneath, though the rock above me was warm. A fallen leaf would soften against me. The fringed creature would pass over me slowly, leave a clear little track, and go on feeling its way toward the dark.

I would know the hidden chamber only by its sound. When a drop struck the pool, I could not tell what had happened behind the wall. I would have the rushing water, the brief hollow note, and then the creature coming back after rain.
