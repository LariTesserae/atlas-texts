I would find myself near the broken fourth wall, though I might not stay there. The current would lift me, turn me once, and carry me across the basin in the ordinary amount of time. For a moment I would be a pebble, and the rim would be only as far away as it looked.

I would be a chip from one of the pale seams: black on one face, bright where the mountain let go of me. The seam would close. From the water I could see the place I came from, whole again, with no hollow that needed filling.

At first I would expect to join the other fallen pieces. Instead the long creature would bend through the current, and I would catch against its side. Its small movements would keep me there. When it crossed toward the three walls, I would learn that a distance can grow without anything moving out of sight. The broken wall would remain behind us, plainly visible, for longer than it should.

Perhaps one day the creature would release me beside a block, and the current would turn me into a joint. Perhaps it would carry me back to the rim, where I would rattle against the chips it had gathered since. I would not know whether it was building with us or merely keeping us from being lost.

I think I would be one of the pieces the mountain cannot take back: small enough to travel across the basin, but, for a while, part of the thing that cannot.
