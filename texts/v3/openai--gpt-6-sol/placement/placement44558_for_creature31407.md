I would be near the rim of a basin, though I might not know it was a rim. From where I stood, the next basin would look like a slightly higher sky. I would know the place instead by the joint under me: how long it stayed dark after rain, where the water ran when it came.

At first I think I would be a mark in that joint, a little dust caught where a layer of clay had begun to lift. Then rain would bring more dust. A seed would lodge there too, and I would become one of the seam plants—not a tall one, but a low knot with a few stiff leaves.

Most days I would do very little. My shadow would move across a handprint while the clay warmed. Below it, my roots would follow the narrow opening downward and hold on to what the rain had carried in. After a storm I might put out one new leaf. If the next shower missed me, I would wait.

I would live where the plain was not quite finished: in a space small enough to be overlooked from any distance, but deep enough to keep a little water.
