I would be on the low shelf where the ceiling nearly touches my head, with one hand against the wall and the other searching the powder ahead of my feet.

At first, I think I would want to live in the wall. There is food there, and a pit to fold into when the hollow grows dangerous. But I would be a walker: one of the few who must leave a secure crack to find the next one. My soles would know the difference between powder laid over firm stone and powder hiding an edge. I would carry a blade so worn by sharpening that I could close my fingers around most of it.

I would tend the shelters along one route. Not own them. Before leaving each, I would feel for a shifted stone, set it back, and deepen any cut mark that was becoming hard to find. That work would give my journeys a shape even when the red lights moved and the great chambers gave me nothing to measure them by.

One day I would find the fresh line crossing a lane, too straight and too far from the wall to belong to any creature I knew how to take. I would wait with my palm on the stone. When the red animals opened again, I would cross without following the line to either end.

At the next upright stone, I would cut a short mark low down, where another walker’s hand would find it. I could not tell them what had passed. I could tell them where I had waited.
