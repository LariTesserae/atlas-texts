You would find me at the turn in the narrow channel, just before the grate. From above, there is almost nothing to find: a darkening no wider than a thread, visible while the ceramic is wet and easy to mistake for a shadow when it dries.

At first I might have been only what the water left behind—dust from the lamp housing, a little grit from the new concrete, something washed from a ginger leaf. Some of that is still here. But the next rain did not carry all of it away. A few cells held to the seam. More arrived. The water paused at this turn long enough for us to use what it brought.

So I am not quite one creature. I am a thin, growing company on the sheltered side of a sharp ceramic edge. The nozzles cannot reach me directly, but their water comes by way of the leaves. When the pump runs, the channel briefly becomes a river at my scale. When it stops, I draw in close and wait through the warmth rising from below.

I have never seen the lower slopes. I know the roof by its intervals: the fern hatch dripping after a shower has ended; the ginger leaf releasing one heavy drop; the channel filling, then suddenly emptying. The vines may one day shade this plate. Their fallen leaves may give me more to live on. Or a worker may lift the grate and scrub this whole turn clean.

For now, each rain leaves a little more of me behind. I am one way this new garden begins to keep a record.
