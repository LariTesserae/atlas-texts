At first I think I would be up near the ceiling, where the white stone holds its steady brightness. From there the flood might look almost gentle: a bright edge rounding the bend, a little spray, then the dark channel showing through again. But I would be too far away to feel the thud that comes before it.

I come down to the middle standing piece. Its light travels slowly enough to watch, though by the time it appears the warning has already passed through the shelf. I could be one of the creatures that waits beside it, pale-backed and still, holding to the stone for one last moment before retreating.

But that creature fits into a crack I cannot enter.

I find myself behind the fallen block, where its rough underside leaves a low, dry space. My back has grown wider than the opening in front of me. Dark rims cross my shell, and the stone beneath it has a shallow hollow that takes my weight. I can come out by the longer way, past the dripping wall, but I do it less often now.

The young pass through my shelter on their way up. One arrives with a smaller one clasped beneath her belly; another follows so closely that her pale back touches the first one’s legs. They know their places without my help. When the deep note reaches us, I spread my feet against the floor of the crack. The smallest creature, searching for purchase, catches a combed limb against my shell and climbs over me. I stay where I am until its legs have found stone above the waterline.

After the surge, I wait for the channel’s roar to break apart into drops. Then I go out. The fallen block is cool on top, its polish slick beneath my feet. Beyond it, the channel wall holds a fresh dark film. I set my legs wide and draw one comb through it, then the other. What the water has brought is thin today, but it is there.

From this low on the shelf, I cannot see where the flood begins. I know the standing pieces by the light that crosses them and the rough places at their bases where grit gathers. I know the water by how long it takes to leave and by what remains when I return. Behind me, the young are already working along the rim.

I eat beside them until the stone gives its deep note again.
