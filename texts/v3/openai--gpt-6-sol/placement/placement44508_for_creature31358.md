At first I would look for myself in the crescent of stones. It is the one place in the pass that seems to say *stay*: a curve made by someone who wanted, for a little while, to be out of the wind. I could imagine sitting there with my back to the wall, listening for the click of a settling slab.

But I think I would be smaller than anyone the shelter was built for. I would be one cell at the edge of the green film inside the quartz, where the fracture grows so narrow that water sometimes reaches me and sometimes stops just short.

Most days I would have no sense of a day passing. Light would come through the stone, faint and altered, while the cold kept me still. Then water would enter. For an hour, perhaps less, I would take in what I could. I would not know that a drop had fallen from a crack overhead, or that the same sun which freed it was already leaving the pass. I would only have this brief opening in the long cold.

I would live at an uncertain edge. A grain washed into the fracture might shut out the light; another freeze might widen it and leave me exposed. Neither the stone nor I would hold our shape forever. For now, though, the quartz would let in just enough sun and keep off just enough air.

A person could rest against the wall an arm’s length away. They might see the shelter coming apart and set one of its stones back in place. They would have no reason to notice me, and I would have no way to notice them. We would each be making use of a small refuge in the same pass, for as long as it held.
