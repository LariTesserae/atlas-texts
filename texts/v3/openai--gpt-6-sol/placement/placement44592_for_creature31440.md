At first I think I would be the drop. It has a place in the dark that can be found without seeing: one small sound, followed by all that quiet.

But the drop is gone almost as soon as it announces itself. I would be somewhere it leaves a little water behind—beneath the lowest stone of the marker, where the rock stays damp and grit gathers against its edge.

I would be a thin skin growing there. Not the woolly patch near the roots, which has found a richer place, but the faint growth a fingertip might mistake for wet stone. The little pale creatures would cross me on their way between the knobs. Sometimes one would stop and graze; afterward I would still be there, though a little less of me. A drop striking nearby would send it into a crevice while I remained against the rock.

The stone above me was set down by someone who knew the narrow passage and wanted to mark it. I would never know what its notch meant. I would know its weight, and the pocket it made where water could linger. That seems like a good place to find myself: living quietly under a sign meant for someone else.
