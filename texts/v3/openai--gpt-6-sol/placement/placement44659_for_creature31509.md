At first I think I would be one of the tall blades. I would rise above the crowded fronds, turn slowly into the light, and make that dry clicking sound when I released what I had stored.

But the tall blades can turn because something below them holds fast. So I find myself lower down: a thin root-film pressed against the stone, passing charge from one stem to the next. I have no useful shape from above. If you parted the leaves to look for me, you might not know where one root ended and another began.

I would live near a straight passage through the stand, where the active seam stops. On one side of me, growth is packed so tightly that even the smallest fronds can survive. On the other is bare mineral, reaching away for miles. I would spread along that edge, not across it. The stone beyond looks no different, but it has nothing to give.

When a blade leans over the passage, I would be among the roots holding it. Charge gathered deeper in the stand would pass through me toward its stem. Later, some would pass back. I would never see the whole green expanse whose work kept me alive.

If you walked that bare way, you might find me by where the fronds end. You could stand with one foot in the shade and one in the clear light, close enough to touch the leaves. Beneath them, I would be keeping contact with the last living seam.
