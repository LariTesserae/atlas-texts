I would find myself in a doorway in one of the quieter districts—not quite inside a room, not quite out in the street. The stone there is rounded low to the ground. Something has passed over it so often that the threshold is smoother than the wall beside it.

At first I might mistake the roar overhead for a warning. It fills the doorway, stops, then begins again somewhere farther off, though nothing has crossed the street. I would wait for the small sounds to return: a seed case tapping against stone, claws shifting in a joint, a bird calling from a courtyard I cannot see. Those sounds tell me more than the roar does. They tell me when it is safe to move.

I would go only as far as the nearest shrubs. Their leaves are dusty, but there is something in them to eat, and insects shelter beneath the fallen ones. I would nose through them slowly, with my ears folded close when the wind rises. If a lizard ran across my path, I would stop. If every bird above me went quiet, I would turn back.

Behind the doorway, beneath a roof that fell long before I was born, there would be a shallow place in the cool dust where I once lay with the other young. I would still use it in the driest spells. The path to the shrubs would be older than I am, worn by bodies like mine until it had become one of the city’s small streets.

I think I would be a young gray-backed animal, not yet willing to travel far from that threshold. The hollow would be immense to me, but my part of it would be exact: one shelter, one patch of growth, and the distance between them, learned one cautious crossing at a time.
