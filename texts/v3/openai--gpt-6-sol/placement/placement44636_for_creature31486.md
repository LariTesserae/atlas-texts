At first I think I would stand in the doorway. From there I could see the whole courtyard: the bird dropping into the fig, the ants changing their route before rain, the wall darkening after it. I would know when to step out and when to leave everything alone.

But I don’t think I would stay in the doorway. I’d go barefoot around the tree, slowly enough not to crush the ants, and find myself returning to the low place beneath it. I would be the clay there—not the water that gathers for an hour, or the roots beginning to cross underneath, but the soft ground that receives them both.

I wouldn’t have a fixed shape yet. Rain would smooth a footprint out of me; a bird landing might leave nothing at all. Each week the fig would hold a little more of me in place. I’d still let the water through.
