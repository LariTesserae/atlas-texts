At first I would look for myself in the open water, where there is room to drift. But nothing drifts for long in this chamber. Even the loose stones have found a way to lie still.

I think you would find me at the far wall, though you could put your hand against it and miss me. I am one of the narrow passages leading into the groove: a space between grains of stone, just wide enough for water to move through slowly. The colony reaches into me. I am not its body, but part of the route by which it eats.

When an engine’s heavier pulse arrives, the rock on either side of me takes it up. For a moment the water in me bears the pressure too. Then the pulse passes, and the slow passage continues. I do not keep count of the machines. I know them as something that comes through without carrying anything away.

A hand could clear the silt from the groove and leave me where I am. For a while, more water might enter. The colony would close what it could, and fine matter would settle over the opening again.

I would still be there: not hidden behind the stone, exactly, but made of the little space the stone has left.
