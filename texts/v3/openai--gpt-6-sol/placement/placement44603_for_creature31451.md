I would begin near the pump house, where the pipe ends above the pool. It looks like a place to wait for something. I might spend a while beneath it before learning that nothing will come.

But I don’t think I would live in the open pool. When the flywheel knocks, I would be gone before I knew I had moved. My place would be lower down, in a seam beside a submerged rail, where the water comes up through stone and carries tiny things against the edge.

I would be one of the long, narrow animals. Most of me would stay in the hole while my head tested the current. A passing foot would tell me how far the gravel can shift. The knock would reach me through the rail before it reached a shrimp in the next pool. Once everything had gone still, I would come out and follow the seam, keeping one side of my body close to stone.

From there I would know the yard as a set of short journeys: hole to bolt, bolt to shelf, shelf to the place where the overflow brings food within reach of a crab. I would turn back before that last place. The crab can wait longer than I can.

I would never see the whole yard. I might know exactly which way to retreat when a shadow crosses my pool.
