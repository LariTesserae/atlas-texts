I think I would be somewhere near the loose inspection hatch. At first I’d mistake myself for part of its noise: another hard thing the water strikes and leaves behind. Only in the quiet between pump strokes would my place become clear.

I’d be the pin in its hinge. Not the whole hatch, with its broad face and tremendous bang, but the narrow piece of metal taking that force sideways. Each stroke turns me a little in a hole worn wider than it should be. Grit passes through. The hatch pauses, then pulls at me again.

From there I wouldn’t see the crab beneath the valve wheel. I’d know when it came out by the faint scrape of its claws during a lull, and know it had gone back when the pipe began to tremble. We would share the same warning, though the crab could obey it and I could not.

One day the pin might work free. The hatch would still bang for a while, perhaps louder, and I’d go with the current through one of the open doors. But for now I’d stay in the hinge: a small, thinning reason the hatch hasn’t yet come away.
