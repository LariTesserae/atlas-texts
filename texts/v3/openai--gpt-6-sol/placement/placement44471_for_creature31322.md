At first I think I would be one of the flies, perched on the root where I could see the whole chamber. But a fly can leave when the rain comes. I think I would stay.

I would live in the seam beneath the third cup, where the clay is damp after rain. I would be a cup-builder, though not a very good one. The others would carry clay up and press it into rings. I would spend most of my time taking a grain back out.

There would be one place at the base of the cup that I kept from closing. It would be too small for the grub and scarcely wide enough for me. When rain entered, water would pass through it and darken the inside of the cup. Then I would wait while the others worked above me, feeling each new ring settle against the one below. Sometimes the grub would leave its own cup, and I would hear it touch the ridges of the floor, one after another. I could not tell whether it was going where a ceiling drop had shown it going. From the seam, I would see only its pale body when it passed close by.

The other builders might fill my gap by accident. I would open it again while the clay was soft. On dry days I might help with a ring, carrying one grain at a time from the floor. It would please me to feel a wall hold, even though I knew where it must remain open.

If the floor ever set hard beneath the cups, I would have no way to reach my seam from below. Until then I would keep it clear, and after rain I would climb inside to find out whether the cup was wet.
