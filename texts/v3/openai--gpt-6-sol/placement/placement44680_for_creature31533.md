At first I think I would be a bird in the sedge: there for an instant, then hidden without having gone anywhere. But a bird has somewhere else to be. I keep returning to the place it landed, to the slight hollow beneath the stems.

Perhaps I am the rainwater there. That lasts a few days at most. It seeps into moss, leaves a dark edge that draws inward, and is gone from sight. The frog comes back anyway. It settles where the water was, or where a little remains under the roots. The beetles go around the dampest part. Even dry, the hollow is not quite the same as the ground beside it.

I think I would be that difference: a shallow place in the old riverbed, small enough to miss from standing height. No creature owns me, and I do not hold water for long. I am found by what happens here. Rain stops in me before it passes on. Moss grows thick at one edge. A frog tests me after other pockets have dried. Once, a bird drops into the sedge above me and makes the whole patch tremble.

If you crossed the clearing, your foot might press me down. The roots would take some of your weight, the moss some more, and then they would lift after you passed. I would stay where I was.

The compressors beyond the trees would be loud enough that you might hear none of this. You would still feel the ground give a little. I would be underfoot for a moment, then behind you: one of the small places the river left when it went away, still receiving whatever reaches it.
