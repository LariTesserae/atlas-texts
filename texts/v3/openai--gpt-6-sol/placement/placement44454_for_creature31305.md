At first I would look for myself at the lip, in the hour of sun. But there is nowhere to stay there: the stone grows too hot, and the wind takes anything light enough to linger.

So I would be farther in, beneath an overhang, one of the hard-backed animals holding to the rough rock. Pale grit has gathered in the grooves of my shell. When the pocket behind the wall begins to knock, I stop feeling ahead with my antennae. The others stop too, until the blows grow slow enough that we can hear grains falling between them.

I know one hairline passage that opens when the ridge cools. I can get through it then, though sometimes another body is already there and I must wait, pressed against the wall. On the far side is a ledge where the wind occasionally drops an insect. I go there when the stone allows it, and come back before the joint grips my shell.

You would find me most easily after a fall of dust. I would be nearly the color of the seam, moving just enough to show that the little pale-backed stone is alive.
