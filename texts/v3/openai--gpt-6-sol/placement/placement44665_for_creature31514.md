I would be the person sent to check the clearance—not to replace the bearing, only to make sure the space around the casing is still empty.

I would stand just inside the door at first, one gloved hand on its edge. The roar would make the inspection feel more urgent than it is. I would check the door’s swing, the width of the floor, the channels and their spotless screens. Then I would notice the four fans.

My first thought would be to remove them. They have fastened themselves to electrical contacts, and my form has a box for *foreign material*. But the fan nearest the door lies below its arc. None of them crosses the floor or reaches the rail. I would look at the narrow rectangle on the plate, then back at the seam, and measure the gap between the two.

That is where I would spend most of my time: crouched beneath the work lamp, learning exactly what **CLEARANCE** means. The spire needs room to run. A worker needs room to stand. The fronds are growing in the part neither one uses.

I would record all four contacts and leave the seam untouched. Before I closed the door, I would look back once to make sure it cleared the low fan. I would be a maintenance inspector who came to verify an empty space and found that it was still empty enough.
