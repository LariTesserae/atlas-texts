At first I would think I was the bead of water beneath the handrail. It has the whole landing to cross, though it can only move when it grows heavy enough. I would wait for that small release.

But the bead falls and is gone. What remains, through every filling of the channel, is the narrow give beneath the maintenance mark. I would be there: the flexible joint fitted into the gap where the mountain shifted after the stone was cut.

I would have no view of the sky. On one side, the building’s warmth reaches me in a thin band. On the other, the rock keeps its cold. I would know a passing vehicle as a brief tightening, and the slower movement of the mountain as a pressure I must yield to without coming loose.

The small animal would pass along my outer edge. I would feel, perhaps, the faint catch of its bent leg before it went down to the water. Farther behind me, the others would remain still. I would not be their shelter by intention. I would simply keep taking up the movement of the rock, leaving that clean, angular space deep enough for them to wait out the frost.

When the channel emptied, the landing would seem empty too. Then a bead would form along the seam. I would hold where I was while the animal came out again.
