I think I would be found between the green provinces, in a shaded seam that a pale animal stopped beside once.

At first there would be almost nothing to find: a seed worked loose from its coat, a little dust gathered against one edge of stone. Most seeds that fall there come to nothing. This one would stay. The dark colonies would spread across the next dustfall, and the seed would put a root into a crack too narrow to see.

For a long while I would look more like a flaw in the rock than a living thing. Then a few stiff silver threads would reach into the wind. They would catch so little grit at a time that no passing animal could notice the mound growing beneath them.

I would be a thread-crown, but not yet part of a forest. I’d stand a day’s walk from the nearest green shelter, making a small place where dust could settle instead of moving on. If I lasted, other seeds might find ground beside me. I would never know which pale animal had brought the first one.
