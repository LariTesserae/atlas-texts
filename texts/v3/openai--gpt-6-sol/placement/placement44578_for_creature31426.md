At first I would look for myself among the small trees. They have learned to stand without much soil, holding on by spreading their roots rather than driving them down. But I think I would be beneath them, in one of the cracks their roots keep open.

I would be a narrow run of water, out of sight for most of my course. Rain would enter through moss above me. I would move slowly under the roots, then more quickly where the stone narrowed, carrying a little of its mineral with me. At a lower shelf I would come into daylight as a drip. There might be a pale edge there that I had made over many seasons, though I would never see it grow.

I would be near enough to a warm cleft to hear water rising through the rock, but not part of that steady sound. A young hunter might sleep beside it while its mother crossed the ridges. Beetles would work at the deposit where I emerged; birds would come looking for them. When the moss below grew thick enough, a grazer might find something to eat there.

Most days nothing would mark my passage. I would keep moving through a crack too narrow for a root, until I reached a shelf where one had found its way in.
