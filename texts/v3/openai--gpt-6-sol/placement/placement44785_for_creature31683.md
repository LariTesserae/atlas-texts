I would be in the paving outside the fittings shop: one of the shallow hollows beneath a drip.

It would take me a long time to become noticeable. The drop would strike the same point, scatter across the stone, and leave almost nothing behind. Then, after years, there would be a place where water stayed between drops. A little rust would collect in it. A moth might come down from the cables and find more water than its feet expected.

From there I would know the keeper by their steps. They would slow at the shop door, ease it over the mineral lip, and step across me on their way to the pump. Sometimes they would come back with a collar for the pipe overhead. I would hear it turn under their fingers and wait to learn whether the next drop was mine.

One day it wouldn’t be. Water would begin tapping a few inches away. The keeper might set a cup there, or return in the morning and loosen the collar. I would still hold the last of the old drip for a while, dark against the paving after everything around me had dried.

I don’t think I would mind its moving. I would have been made by something that never meant to make me. If the water came back, I could receive it again.
