I would begin beneath the stair, where the rail enters the wall. At first I’d be no more than a pale fringe in that seam, growing thickest where the pipe’s tremor passes through the brick.

I wouldn’t know the square by its streets. I’d know the pipe as a steady pressure, the intake as a change in the weight of water, the lamp as something that happens just after the current shifts. Rust flakes would catch in my strands. The small jointed animals would come to feed among them, their legs parting me without tearing me loose. Once in a while, an empty shell would lodge there too.

But I don’t think I’d stay only beneath the stair. A loosened strand might travel with a shell through an intake chamber and catch in mortar on the far side of the square. There, I would grow thinly, perhaps for years, until I reached another place that trembled.

From the landing I would look like one patch of white. Underwater I would be several small holdings, joined less by my body than by the water passing through them. I would never see the fish turn toward the blue lamp. I would feel the brief wake of the creatures it had missed, settling back onto the steps.
