I would find myself at a threshold, though I would not know it as one.

At first I might seem to be a chip of old clay lodged beneath a bright new rim. Powder collects against me when the vent draws inward. When it exhales, the powder falls away, and for a moment my joints show. I am one of the small scrapers. I have come for the black tuft growing along the seam, and I will stay motionless for a long time after I have eaten.

The little square would be too exposed for me. I would know it only as a stretch of ground to cross between cracks. Nor would I know the town as a town. I would know the cool hollow beneath a curling sheet, the taste of a fresh tuft, the width of an opening measured by whether my body can turn inside it.

For several dimmings I might return to the same gap. Its outer lip would harden; a new edge would creep toward it. Each visit would require me to press a little flatter to pass through. There would be no sudden collapse, no sound to warn me. One time I would touch the far wall with a foreleg while my back was still against the near one. I would eat what I could reach, then leave by the crack through which the powder breathes.

I would not mourn the lost doorway. Farther along, another sheet would have curled away from the clay, leaving darkness beneath it and a thin fringe of food at its edge. I would settle there, legs folded close, while the vent changed direction. From the square I would look like a flaw in the masonry. Under me, the living threads would already be turning toward a wall that did not exist when I arrived.
