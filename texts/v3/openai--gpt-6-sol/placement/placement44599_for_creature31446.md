At first I would look for myself on a balcony. There is room there for someone to stand, and the lights seem to expect a hand on the rail. But nothing comes out to meet them. I think I would be higher up, where the ferns cannot reach: a narrow flaw in the rock, almost too small to hold water.

Each time the pumps start, a little water would enter me. It would bring grit, press against my edges, and leave by a route slightly wider than the one it found. For a long time I would be only a leak. Then one rush would reach another channel behind the wall, and the water would have a new way through.

I would never see the whole chamber. I would know the basin by the pressure that returns when it fills, the balconies by the coolness beneath them. If a leaf blocked my opening, the next pulse would hesitate; somewhere far below, a drop might hang in the air a moment longer.

That is where I would be: not one of the city’s doors, but a passage it is still making.
