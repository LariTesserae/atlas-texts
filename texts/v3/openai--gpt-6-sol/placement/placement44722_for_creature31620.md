From a few steps away, I would be nowhere. I would be in the part of the hollow that looks empty even after the dust begins to move: a crack beneath a flake of stone, close to the rim.

At first I think I might be the flake itself. It holds a little shade and catches the finest grit. When the track hums, it trembles against the stone beneath it. But something has to carry away what gathers there. Something has to fit into the space that almost closes when a shell passes over it.

I would be one of the pale ones.

I would wait while the dark backs worked past, their jaws freeing particles I could not loosen myself. Then I’d run under them and take a crumb into the crack. It would be awkward to turn around with it. I might have to leave it halfway in, come at it from the other side, and drag it through backward.

The hunter’s shadow would reach me before its legs could. I would know it by the sudden absence of feet above the crack. For a little while I would hold still, the crumb beside me, while dust fell into the passage I had just cleared.

When the traffic started again, I would go out and clear it again.
