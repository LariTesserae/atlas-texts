I would begin by looking for water, and keep finding stone.

A drop falls from a leaf with enough force to shake the reeds beneath it. I move toward the sound, but it has already sunk into a fissure. Above me, the crowns are full of callers; somewhere beyond them, a herd is tearing through the growth. Neither tells me much about distance. The rock does. A tread reaches it before the body passes overhead.

I think I would be one of the small insects that lives in a warm crack. Not one of the pale ones flashing among the trunks, but something seldom seen: out after dark to feed among the roots, back between the folds of stone before the ground becomes too exposed. My home would be where a line of ferns stops suddenly against bare black rock. The seam keeps their roots wet, and the crack beneath it keeps me warm.

I would know that edge closely, though it would never stay the same. Rain would carry soil into my shelter. A root would enter after it. One night the passage home would be narrower; another night, a heavy foot would break it open again. I would have to find my way by the warmth coming through the stone and by the places where water had gone down.

From inside the crack, I might hear the herd pass and the reeds fall quiet behind it. By morning, animals would be using the opening it had made. I would be under them, feeding at the roots, while the first loose grains of soil gathered over the sharp rock.
