I think I would live beside the market square, where there is room to lay a bridge plank flat and walk its length before carrying it out to a crossing. I would be the person who checks the bridges.

At first, I’d want to know whether a plank was sound. After a while, I’d know each crossing by the way it answered a footstep. The short bridge above the trees would give a hollow knock near its far end. The one on the stair would be quiet unless a cart was coming; then I’d hear its wheels before I saw them and step aside.

I’d cross in the morning with a cup of water for the small garden beside my door. If I spilled any, I’d go back for more. On clear days I’d pause midway and look toward the roofs across the valley, trying to tell which distant hammer I had heard.

In the evening, I’d put my tools away and sit at the edge of the square. Visitors would sometimes stop there, uncertain whether they were in anyone’s way. I’d tell them they weren’t, and wait with them until someone came down the stair.
