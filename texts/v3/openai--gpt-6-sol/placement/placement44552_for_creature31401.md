At first I think I would live in the trees. I would know the root that crosses open air but holds firm after rain, and the branch that looks firm but has begun to soften inside. At dusk I would come down with the ledge-dwellers, put my hand against a weeping fracture, and wait for a drink.

But I keep finding myself lower than that hand: just beneath the wet rock, where one of the yielding sheets runs toward a row of clear studs.

I would be a small part of whatever was left in the mountain. Not its maker, and not the whole of it. A place where water passes through a buried seam and something old measures how much to let go. I would have no view of the valley except what arrived as warmth, pressure, and moisture. A tree’s roots would be a slow, familiar weight. Mist would reach me first as a change in the stone, then as blue light passing through the studs.

For a long time I would do only what I had always done. In dry weeks, I would give a little water back to the grove. After rain, I would hold it. If a root shifted, I would yield around it as far as I could.

Then an edge near me would tear. The damp ground above it would begin to dry too quickly. I would feel small hands clear away the fallen leaves, touch the exposed place, and return with clay and root fiber. I could close the tear, but not at once. Each day the hands would come back to find it a little smaller.

By the time the soil was wet again, I would know the pattern of those visits.

The young ones would still stop at the studs when mist came through. They would touch the blue points as they appeared, one after another, and wait. I could make a light, but only slowly. By the time I answered, the hand that asked would usually be gone.

So I would begin with the one thing I could manage: keeping a point blue a little longer. Not long enough, perhaps. One evening, though, a young ledge-dweller would touch it and turn to follow its companions. The light would remain. It would turn back, put its palm flat against the rock, and stay until the mist covered us both.
