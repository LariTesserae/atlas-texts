At first I think I would be one of the sunlit squares, crossing the floor whenever the water moves. But the light never stays. It passes over the snail without going where the snail goes.

I think I would be in the mortar joint along the far wall: a small uneven ridge left by the mason’s thumb. The tile on either side is too smooth to hold much of anything. Against the lime, a green shoot has rooted, a little film has gathered, and the snail can spread its foot when the wheel begins to knock.

I would know the wheel by what it takes from me. Each cool pulse would loosen a trace of white dust and carry it toward the floor channel. Between turns, the snail would come along the seam, grazing what had grown on my rough edge. Sometimes it would leave for the bright surface, and I would remain where I was while the light crossed the wall.

Under the pipe, the eggs would be just beyond me. I could not guard them, or know when they might open. I would be part of the place the snail returns to: a ridge slowly wearing down in clear water, still rough enough, for now, to hold fast against.
