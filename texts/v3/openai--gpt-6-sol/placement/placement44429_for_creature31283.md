At first I think I would be one of the birds. I would know the mountains as a place to leave and the bowl as a place to return to. I like the thought of landing in the roar and finding another bird by touch.

But I don’t think I would be able to leave so easily. I would be a beetle in the rust beneath a fern, close enough to the rim that a root sometimes crosses the space above me. The furnace would come through the iron as a tremor I had known all my life. I wouldn’t know it as a machine, or know that it might stop.

After a cloud passed through, water would work its way down the arch and into my narrow shelter. I would climb until it drained away. In the dry days I would go lower, into the places the roots and rain had softened. The birds would arrive and depart overhead; I might know a season had changed only when grass began to fall from a bolt hole, or when the fern above me stopped holding warmth through the night.

I would never cross the bare middle. From under the leaves it would be a wide, bright reach of iron, with no seam to enter. Perhaps a seedling would appear there after rain. I would pass near it once or twice. Then the floor would dry, and there would be nothing to pass near.

I think I would stay at the rim, where the bowl is slowly becoming something else, working in a little dark place that neither the vine nor the iron quite owns.
