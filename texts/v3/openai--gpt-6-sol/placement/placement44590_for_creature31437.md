I think I would begin as something in transit: a thread torn from a frond beneath the western cliff, carried past the warm fissure without taking hold. There would be no reason for me to stop at the little walls. The current would slow there, though, and a stone bowl would catch me.

For a long time I would be almost indistinguishable from the silt in its bottom. The bowl might have enough minerals to let me grow a leaf, but not enough to keep it. I would send a root down through the silt and into a joint between the slates. If it found a seam in the shelf, I would remain. If it did not, I would darken and let go, and another fragment might come to rest in my place.

So I would be the sprig in the third bowl, but not quite confined to it. My roots would pass through the wall that holds me; my leaves would cross its low doorway into the current. I would know the great fall by the stronger pull on them, and the cracking ice by the brief change of pressure that follows.

The stones leading to the drop-off would be near me, though I would never follow them. I would grow slowly across the entrance while they stayed where they had been put.
