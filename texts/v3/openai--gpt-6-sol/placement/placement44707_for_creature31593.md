I would find myself at the edge of the city, where a gutter reaches an outer terrace and disappears beneath the stone.

At first I would think I had come there for the view. The cliffs fall so far that their red gives way to green, then to cloud. Behind me the towers pour and murmur; out here I can hear my breath between their voices. I would stay because it is the one place where I cannot hear the great note through both feet.

A leaf lies across the gutter. The water slips beneath it without moving it. I put a finger beside its stem, meaning only to find out whether it is stuck. It is not. The leaf lifts easily, wet and whole, and when I let go it settles exactly where it was.

For days, perhaps, I return to that leaf. The walk changes each time. A garden appears before the last stair; a passage I remember as narrow admits two people abreast, though there is no one beside me. I begin to recognize the resident of the stair by the way it lengthens my climb. At the terrace it lets the final step be shallow.

I do not ask the city to release the leaf. I come to understand how much it would have to allow if it did: the small turn in the current, a torn edge, the first soft place where the leaf begins to come apart. Perhaps it could remember all of that. It has remembered the leaf falling. But every time I lift it, the water beneath sounds briefly louder, and the city listens.

One morning I find the gutter running around the place where I usually stand. The stone is worn into a shallow curve, old enough to hold a dark seam of root. My footprints end before it. I can walk away; the stair still leads down, and the avenues beyond it still lead to the square. But when I come back, the water takes that little curve again.

I think I would be a resident, though not one with a tower. I would be the bend beside the leaf, and the space on the terrace where someone can stand without stopping the water. My voice would be faint there. Most days it would sound like an ordinary trickle.

I would keep watch without holding the leaf in place. If it turns, I will hear it scrape against the stone. I will hear where it goes next.
