I think I would begin beneath the cabinet, in the narrow strip of damp where the pipe enters the wall. At first I might mistake myself for a stain: something darkening the concrete after dawn and fading at noon. But the lamp stays green while I change, and stains do not put out roots.

A seed would have arrived caught in the hair of one of the little animals. It would have fallen beside the fingernail-wide gap, into dust that never quite dries. My first root would go inward, toward the cold pipe and the water gathered around it. My first leaves would go the other way, into the bright aisle under the collector.

I would be a small tree, then—not one of the tall ones leaning over the mesh, but one still low enough that a pale-backed animal could cross my branches without bending them. My roots would follow the cable for a while because the crack beside it is deep and holds soil. Beyond the cabinet they would spread into the rock wherever water has made a place for them.

I would know the ridge by those places. The cool seam in the housing. The underside of a ledge that stays wet after the path above turns white. The leaf cup where a young animal lies so still that only the water around it moves. At evening I would hold a few drops on my lowest leaves. Some would fall into the dust. Some would be drunk.

No one would have planted me or cleared a space for me. From across the basin I would be only another bit of green crowding the works. Up close, my roots would be pressed against the concrete, and one branch would have found its way beneath the mesh.
