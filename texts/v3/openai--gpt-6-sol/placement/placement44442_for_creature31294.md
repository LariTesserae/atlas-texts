At first I would think I was the warmth in the stones. It is the only thing here still going anywhere, passing so slowly into the air that the hollow seems unchanged.

But warmth has no edge, and I think I would have one. I would be the flat stone above the scorpion: broad enough to keep the day off its back, low enough that it must fold itself to fit beneath me. My upper face would cool under the stars while my underside stayed warm a little longer. Dust would gather where my edge meets the ground, except at the narrow gap the scorpion uses.

I would not know when it woke. Nothing in its stillness would shift my weight. Then a few grains at the opening might move as it brushed the ground, and I would remain where I was.

By morning it would be tucked beneath me again. The last of my warmth would have gone, and from the rim I would look like any other stone in the hollow.
