At first I would look for the person who left the cord. I would follow the channels past the gardens, expecting to come upon someone pressing a loose reed back into clay. When a bridge brought me out beside the platform again, I would assume I had taken a wrong turn.

After a while I would stop looking so far away. I would live under that bridge, close enough to see the bowls beneath the roof. I think I would be one of the bright insects in its bindings: small enough to put my forelegs against a knot and feel the warm water passing through the reeds.

There would be plenty to attend to. A strand would work loose, or a root would find its way between two reeds. I could brace the strand with my body while the water ran past. I could visit it again and find it in just the same position, still needing me. That would trouble me at first. Then it might become the reason I stayed.

From beneath the bridge I could see the bowl with its single drop. I would sometimes climb to the roof to watch the next one gather. Below me, a frog would draw its throat tight. Across the bare tile, a bird would open its beak. I would wait for the splash or the call, and hear only the water moving beside my feet.

I would not know whether I was helping to hold the place together. But I would learn the feel of one binding better than anyone else could, and each time I found it loose, I would take hold of it again.
