At first I think I would live in one of the cracks, where the pale stalks hold their combs up to the air. But I cannot imagine staying there when a comb bows and six heads turn. I would want somewhere I could fold away.

So I would be the animal beside your boot. Broad enough to have a hooked head, thin enough to disappear when I turn sideways. The grit on my back would make me look like another rough edge of the stone. I would wait while your shadow crossed the amber sockets, then come out a little farther to examine the dust caught in the seam of your sole.

I would not be brave. My small arms would be good for sorting: thread from grit, edible shell from empty shell, a fresh chip from one already worn smooth. I would carry the smooth chips beneath the old machine and leave them near the wheel. Not as an offering. Things collect there, and sometimes there is food among them.

When the heavy body shifted and the rasping stopped, I would fold flat against the base. I would know the silence by how long I had to stay still. Once the wheel caught and the calls found their places again, I would go back to work, turning over the next small thing with both hands.
