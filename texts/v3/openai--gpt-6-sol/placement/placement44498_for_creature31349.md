I would find myself below the first road a visitor would call a road, at a crossing where the stone is painted with two sets of marks. One set leads straight across. The other bends around a freight support and looks, until you have lived there, like a mistake.

I think I would have grown up taking the straight way. As a child I’d put my ear against the warm grating to hear the pumps and pretend I could tell them apart. Later, I’d learn that I could—though not as well as my grandmother, who could hear a gate open three districts away through the legs of her bed.

At first I imagine myself carrying deliveries, because I would like knowing the city by its shortcuts. But shortcuts belong to a particular hour. I keep coming back instead to the people who test the crossings at dawn.

So I would be one of them: a joint tester, with a marked coat that never quite dried at the cuffs and a case of small, unremarkable tools. I’d walk the painted line while the market opened behind me. At each seam I’d stop, set down my case, and feel whether the stones still met as they had yesterday. Most mornings they would. Some mornings a bridge would be lifting so slowly that I’d have to stand and watch the gap widen before I could be sure.

People would wait for me, which would make me self-conscious even after years of it. A trader with a sealed cart, a child carrying breakfast upstairs, somebody wanting to get home before the next warning tone: none of them would need a speech from me. They would need to see where I put my feet.

When the far rain reached our quarter, I’d go back to the crossing. Perhaps the straight marks would already be under water. I’d turn the small sign toward the longer route, and someone behind me would complain that it added ten minutes. I might agree. Then I’d take that way myself.

At home, I’d leave my shoes by the door and listen for the pump beneath our floor. If its beat sounded ordinary, I would sleep. If it stopped, I would get up before I was fully awake, reach for the coat, and wait for the next sound.
