At first I would look for a place on the stone below, where a green ridge still remembers being a mountain. But the water is deepest there, and the sound comes down through it from everywhere. I think I would rise until one column became louder than the others.

I would live beside its outflow: one of the broad-mouthed, nearly transparent animals. I would face the current and let it bring what I need. From a distance, there would be no sign of me. Up close, perhaps a fish holding its place nearby would see the faint rim of my body against the stem.

I would know this part of the sea intimately without travelling through much of it. The low note of my column would pass through me. Blue light would run along the roots below; the roof of leaves would hold its gray daylight overhead. Through my body, the old mountain ridge would remain visible, slightly bent at my edges.

The sea would give me room to be almost alone. I would keep my mouth open to the water the plant sends past, and feel its immense, unchanging work without needing to know where it begins.
