I would be at an intersection beneath the elevated line, where one of its drops falls a little short of a basin.

At first I think I would be the drop. I like the long wait on the underside of the line, the sudden descent, the brief, sharp sound on the street. But a drop gets only one crossing. I would want to stay and learn why it landed there.

So I would be a thin patch on the raised lip beside that basin. A draft once shook a grain of pale dust out of the wall and into the crack where I began. Most days I would be no more visible than the dark ceramic. When water struck nearby, ridges would open through me and draw a trace of it sideways, against the fall of the street, before the heat took it.

I would know the chamber by those brief wet intervals. The bridge above me clicks; some hours later, a drop forms along its underside. I cannot follow it with my eyes, or move to where it lands. I can only keep my edge ready. If it falls close enough, I hold a little water at the lip while the rest runs into the basin. The wetness passes through me, carrying a grain of mineral. When I dry, that grain remains.

It is a very small change. After many drops, the next one reaches me more easily.

I would not think of myself as living *in* the city. The streets are the dark skins left by others before me, and the shallow fall I make may one day catch a grain loosened from the seam. I would be one place where heat and water take a moment to meet—and where, when the water is gone, something is left for the next arrival.
