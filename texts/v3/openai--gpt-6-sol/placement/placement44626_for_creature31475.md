I would first look for myself beneath a pipe, among the small shells that have taken on the color of the dust. But I think I would be farther inside the city than that: in a furnace hall whose mouth is dark, where the floor holds a little more warmth than the street.

For a long time, I would be difficult to tell from the stone. Mineral would gather on my back. My legs would rest in a shallow groove beside a pipe fitting, close enough to feel the steady pressure passing through it. When the pressure faltered, I would draw one leg free. It might take a day to reach the seam.

Perhaps that is where you would find me: with one joint pressed into a gap too narrow for grit to enter. I would not know what the pipe carried, or where it went. I would know only its familiar vibration, and the rough new beat that had begun beneath it. Over months, others might settle along the fitting. Dust would cover us, and the vibration would become even again.

Then, one day, a wall across the street would fall. Its footstep-echo would be gone. I would feel the difference each time something moved through the hall, until I left my place and crossed the open stone toward it.
