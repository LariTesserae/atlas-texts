At first I would be above the chamber, with one finger on the ground. I would feel the thin note and mistake it for something beginning: a shell giving way, perhaps, or a crack finding its way toward daylight.

Then I would want to go closer. There is no room to arrive as I am. I might follow a root hair down, but its tip is already surrounded. I might enter with a breath through one of the narrow passages, only to be pressed out again by the next. The chamber has a place for every small thing, and none for a visitor.

I think I would find myself in a crack, as one of the beads of water.

From there I could see little: a rim of bright clay, a few sand shadows, the mouth of the young one when it reached me. It would drink. For an instant I would know the cool, fine edge of its mouth; then it would fold back into its cell, and I would still be round against the clay.

I would hear the worker more clearly than I could see her. Her forelegs would press mud flat somewhere beyond the crack. Sometimes her call would pass through the narrow place and set me trembling without dislodging me. Sometimes she would grow quiet against a sealed cap, listening for a movement I could not hear.

I would be water that does not run out, offered to a body that cannot yet leave. I would wait where the crack has only just begun, while the young one comes back thirsty.
