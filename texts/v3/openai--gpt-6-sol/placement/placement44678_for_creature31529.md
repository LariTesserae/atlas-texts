I would be in the street outside the pump house, though not at first in any place a visitor would think to look. Perhaps I would seem to be weed in a switch box: a few pale roots threaded around the old wires, a ribbon of leaves reaching out through a crack.

Below the street, I would feel the pump through the brick ties. Most days its vibration would pass through me without changing my shape. I would grow toward the warm water, lose a leaf to a passing tool, and grow another. A snail might make a clean line along it that closed again by morning.

But when a bearing began to roughen, the leaves outside the box would tremble. One of the people tending the pumps might notice before they could feel the fault through the wall. They would put down a hand beside me, wait, and follow the unevenness inward.

I would not know what a pump was, or that the harbor rested above us. I would only know the familiar hum becoming difficult to hold still against, then, after someone had worked in the cloudy water, becoming familiar again.
