At first I would put myself near the entrance, on one of the three flat stones. It is the only place with anything like a path, and the cup suggests that someone once knew how to stand here without slipping. I would wait for my eyes to adjust, trying to make out where the next step should go.

But the longer I looked, the less the hollow would seem to have been made for a person standing upright. Its useful places are the narrow ones: the wet undersides of the bosses, the pits that hold a little film, the seam beside the outlet where the water quickens. I would find myself there instead.

I think I would be one of the small grazers, with a shell not quite the colour of the limestone. I would live a little upstream of the larger animal, where an overhang keeps the salt from closing the seam entirely. My world would be a few lengths of stone. I would know the pit that refills after each drop from the roof, and the rough place where my antennae catch before I can turn.

A foot on the crossing would reach me first as pressure through the water. I would draw back under the lip of stone and stay there while the disturbance passed. Then I would return to the pale film, perhaps to the same unfinished patch. The cup would be high above me, as remote as the break in the roof.

I would not keep the hollow or understand how long it had been there. I would leave a clean, narrow track through what grows on the stone. Water would pass over it; the film would return.
