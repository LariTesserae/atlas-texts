At first I think I would live out in the passage, where the warm current brings food. But I would spend too much of my life watching for the moment every mouth in the wall shut at once. I think I would move toward that wall, following a small fish into an opening too narrow for whatever frightened it.

There would be a crack farther in, between an old shell and a shelf of newer growth. I could fit there. The fish could not. I would be one of the thin-armed animals that lives in such places, with most of my body out of the current and one feeler extended into it.

I would know very little of the city at a time. A tremor would reach me before a visitor passed the outer ridge. The small fish would crowd my opening, then leave it empty. Silt would settle on my arm; something edible would touch it, and I would pull it in. Afterward, the patch of floor beneath my crack would be clean.

The shell around me would keep growing. Over a long life, the opening might tighten, and the current I knew would begin to slip past somewhere else. I would extend my arm from another side if I could. For a while, before I learned the new flow, I might find nothing at all.

Today I would stay on the sheltered side, feeling the warm water pass beyond my reach. When it turned, I would put out my arm. Across the passage the mouths would draw closed, and here, one by one, we would open.
