At the edge of a clear interval, the stems seem empty. Then a crease passes across them against the current. It narrows, turns around a post, and pauses where the cut cable enters the water. No body follows it. For a moment, the stems themselves hold the shape of something attending.

The growth here has no fixed boundary. A cord climbing one transformer disappears into a housing and emerges from the silt beside another. Leaves torn free continue to widen; roots caught under a rising slab take hold before the slab has settled. Each piece can live apart, but the pulses of light pass from leaf to leaf with too much regularity to be accidental. When a pump misses a turn, a whole field dims. When it starts again, the field brightens from the far end first.

The plain may be full of such lives, impossible to count. One occupies a stand of reeds for the length of a current’s reversal. Another gathers itself around the posts, drawing on their charge and sending thin roots into the seams below them. They overlap without clearing space for one another. Nothing here appears to need a territory in the usual sense: what lives beside a station this morning may be growing on a newly lifted ridge by the next interval of clear water, while its older roots remain below.

The blows from under the ground do not make them retreat. A seam opens, and cords reach into it. A slab rises, and leaves turn their lit veins toward its underside. Some of the growth may be making use of the machinery; some of the machinery may be caught in the growth. The unattached pump still turns, and each downward stroke draws the nearby stems taut. After it stops, a pulse continues through them, farther and farther out, until it is lost among the other rhythms.

There are no mouths in the water, no watchful eyes behind the leaves. Yet the stations are attended. Broken casings are threaded through as carefully as living wood, and every fresh surface is claimed while it is still pale. When the engines come together and the silt leaps up, the inhabitants lose their visible shapes. They remain in the roots beneath the slabs, in the loose leaves moving through the dark, and in the crease that returns across the stems when the water clears.
