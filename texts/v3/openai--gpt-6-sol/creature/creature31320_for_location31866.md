At first, the inhabitants seem to be the pale things that cross the light. They are small, soft-winged animals with bodies no thicker than a leaf stem. They fly poorly in the still air, touching down often to comb pollen from their faces. At the dark basins they drink from the edge without disturbing the surface. Most spend the hot hours motionless on the underside of a shelf, where their folded wings are difficult to tell from a patch of exposed root.

There are heavier animals, too, though they are seldom seen whole. One may be heard drawing its rough back along a narrow passage, or glimpsed as a blunt, leaf-coloured flank behind a screen of upright blades. They move between the fixed openings, eating fallen pollen and the soft growth that gathers in the basins. Their feet are broad enough to cross the root shelves without tearing them. They leave little besides shallow prints in the dust, which the next fall of pollen covers.

But the most abundant living thing is also the easiest to mistake for the country itself. The trunks, shelves, screens, and little enclosures belong to a few immense plants. Each sends roots across a great reach of stone, taking water where it arrives and carrying it through thick stems to places that have been dry for days. A leaf in a bright vault may be supplied by a seam far away. Beneath a walkable shelf, pale root fibres press together so closely that they seem woven.

The plants do not close every space they can fill. In the open arches, air is a little cooler and pollen settles out of it. Drops fall there often enough to draw the winged animals in; the heavier ones follow the same passages to drink. Tender shoots rise beside these gaps, but not across them. Light reaches the leaves at their margins, and water lasts longer in the sheltered ground below. Generation after generation of shoots has done well there.

A traveller might pass through one of those rooms and feel that it had been left ready for them. After a while, watching an animal drink while a drop forms overhead and roots carry away the water that spills, the room seems less empty. It is a place where several lives can meet without much need to move.
