The reeds live here first. Their pale roots hold the ceiling apart without quite enclosing the hollow, taking water from below and passing it upward into the light. Each root is busy even when it looks motionless. Drops collect overhead; fine fibers darken as they drink; the little groove fills and empties without ever becoming a stream.

Among the roots live creatures small enough to use the open space differently. Feeding larvae cling to damp fibers and scrape at what grows on them. Beetles work along the stems, sometimes setting a whole root trembling with a knock. A snail crosses the smooth felt slowly, leaving a bright line that lasts until the next seep of water dims it. In the groove, still smaller lives would find the wet edge sufficient: things for which a drop is a pool and the felt a thicket.

The frogs are mostly beyond the pocket, close enough that their calls seem to come through the floor. They may enter when the water reaches farther in. Nothing here needs to stay. There is room to pass beneath the reeds, to feed for a while, to vanish into another gap. The roots remain, drawing and releasing water around them, keeping this small, noisy clearing open.
