At first, the city seems uninhabited. Then you notice that it has made a place for you to stand.

The residents are difficult to count because none of them ends where a person would. One occupies a tower, the two gardens beside it, and the wet passage joining them. Another is mostly a flight of stairs. Its upper steps are always farther apart when you climb than when you descend. The trees pressed against its walls belong to it as surely as the walls do; it has held them there, full-grown, for longer than the fallen leaves have lain in the gutter.

They do not walk. When one wants to approach, a street opens between buildings, complete with roots that have spent centuries in its stone. When one wants to be left alone, the way remains, but every turning leads gently elsewhere. No route is blocked. You can always go back, though you may have trouble saying how many streets you crossed.

Their voices pass through the water. A spill no wider than your hand can carry the sound of a river because the river is speaking through it. In the outer districts, you hear them separately: a high, hollow pouring from the towers; a low murmur under a garden wall. Near the square, their voices meet in the paving. The note there is not a command or a warning. It is what they sound like when they are all listening.

Nothing smaller lives among them. The leaves neither grow nor rot; the clear cisterns hold no fish. Perhaps the city cannot bear to let a thing change without remembering every instant of it. It keeps its gardens green and its fallen leaves where they fell. It keeps each resident’s streets open, even as it makes room for another.

The pool in the roofless hall is where they look outward. It shows the mountain from the white plain of cloud, as though seen by someone standing far below with their face lifted. Sometimes, at its edge, the roar thins enough for you to hear the little sounds beneath it: water entering porous stone, a drop settling in a root’s hollow, your own breath returning from the pool.

Stay long enough, and an avenue may begin to curve around the place where you are standing.
