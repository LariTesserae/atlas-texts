At first there seems to be nothing living here but the vegetation. Nothing passes beneath the leaves; nothing stirs the hanging strands. Yet the plain is not a place covered by a plant. The surface that yields underfoot, the walls, the chambers glowing at their seams, and the roots carrying charge far into the dark all belong to the life of the place.

It is difficult to tell where one part ends and another begins. A rib breaking through the ground may become a wall much farther on, or feed one of the woody chambers set along it. Water moves through the whole without ever gathering into a stream. Press a foot into the dark mat and the water rises around it; lift the foot and the mat takes it back. Somewhere along that passage, membranes draw out salts and vessels deliver them to sheets of fibre being laid down in the sealed chambers.

The chambers look like things waiting to open. Their seams admit a little green light, but their walls remain held tight. Perhaps the sharp ridges underfoot are the newest work of the same slow process. They have risen and stopped, or risen so gradually that no watching eye could catch them at it. The charge in the roots does not travel with any visible signal. It persists beneath the stillness, as steady as the warmth under the leaves.

If there are other lives here, they leave no sign. No small mouth has marked the fresh fibre. No wing brushes the drops from the strands. What lives here is immense and close to itself: a body with room enough to make a horizon, keeping its water, its warmth, and its unfinished chambers in balance. After a walker passes, even the brief hollow of a footprint is drawn back into it.
