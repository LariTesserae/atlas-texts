What lives here is the chord.

It has no single mouth. Each clay opening gives it a narrow part of itself, and the passages behind them join those parts somewhere deep in the fitted stone. Near a wall it is thin and sharp; in the middle of the courtyard it has weight. A person could put a hand over one opening and hear a note falter, but the pressure in the floor would go on. The rest of it would still be there.

The height feeds it. Air enters under a force no one in the courtyard can see and leaves by routes no one standing here can follow. The creature makes nothing and carries nothing away. It lives by holding that passage open. The warm stone is its body in the way a shell is a body: built long ago, worn at its exposed edges, thickened wherever it threatened to fail. Some of the later lintels may have changed its voice. They have not stopped it.

It has little use for the open courtyard. Its life is in the throats and the long, hidden spaces between them. It knows a blocked opening as a loss of pressure, a crack as a new, uncertain note. Everything else—the steps, the blind doorways, the grit in the seams—stands within its sound without becoming part of it.

If it has grown, it has done so slowly enough to be mistaken for repair. If it has ever fallen silent, the stone gives no sign. For now it fills every passage it can reach, and the small courtyard has room for all of it.
