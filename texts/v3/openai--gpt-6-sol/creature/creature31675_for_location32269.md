What lives here is easy to take for part of the wet stone. Along the seam by the wall, a dark, soft edge sometimes gathers itself inward when a drop passes. If you wait, it extends again: a creature no longer than a finger, flattened enough to slip between faces of rock. Its back has the dull shine of a soaked leaf. Along its sides, fine, pale fringes keep contact with the water even when the rest of it rests above the surface.

It feeds where the cold water comes up through the soil, grazing the thin growth on stone and the softened edges of fallen leaves. The neat bite beneath the moth’s shelter may be its work. It does not cross the bare shelf. When a hollow begins to dry, it follows the wet seam down, feeling ahead with its fringes, and comes up elsewhere after rain. Several may live beneath green patches that look quite separate from above.

Behind the wall, where the hidden water has worn a little chamber, they gather. A falling drop sometimes strikes one as it clings to the chamber roof. It lets go for an instant, catches the stone again, and the drop lands in the pool below with that sharp, hollow note. Then the rushing fills the space, and nothing on the shelf appears to have moved.
