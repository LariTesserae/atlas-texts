A small salamander lives behind the three upright stones.

It is dark-backed and no longer than a finger, with a pale throat that swells when it breathes. By day it fits itself into the seam behind the shelf, where the rock stays wet but the river cannot reach. It can remain there for hours while the hollow shakes with the sound of water.

After dusk it comes out. It crosses the open floor slowly, pausing where the bare stone gives way to moss. Insects gather among the ferns near the entrance, and now and then one wanders far enough in. After rain, the salamander visits the shallow pits. It stands with its front feet in the cold water, then disappears beneath the stones again.

The shelf was made for someone much larger. Its worn groove and the dark stake have survived whatever use they once had, but to the salamander they are a wall with a safe space behind it. On most days, if it has left the seam at all, there is no sign except a faint track through the damp silt—gone when the next drops fall.
