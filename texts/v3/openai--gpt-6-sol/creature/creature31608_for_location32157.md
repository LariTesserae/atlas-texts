At first the chamber seems to contain many plants. A fern on one pier, a vine across a trough, pale roots hanging from the vault: each has found its own small purchase. Follow them farther, though, and their boundaries become difficult to keep. Stems have rooted again where they touch silt. Runners pass from one trough to the next and send up leaves so unlike their older leaves that they might belong to another species. Beneath the paving, roots meet in the wet seams of the brick. What lives here may be a garden, or it may be one garden grown together.

It is thickest around the pump. The climbing stems leave the inspection plate clear but cover the warm housing on either side, holding their leaves away from the moving wheel. Fine roots have entered the cracks around the pipe brackets. In one place a heavier root has grown through the spokes of a discarded handwheel and closed around the iron, so that neither can be lifted without breaking the other.

The leaves nearest the grates are broad, pressed almost flat beneath the grey light. Lower down they become smaller and thinner. In the lamp-lit aisles their undersides show a dull silver that catches each brief tremor of the water. There are new shoots everywhere: curled in mortar joints, emerging from the soaked cuff of an old work glove, rising through a scatter of grit on the stairs. Older growth is darker and tougher. It has endured the steady dripping long enough to change the paths of the drops.

Nothing in the chamber follows a passerby. Leaves brush a shoulder because the aisle has narrowed; a hanging root sways because it has been touched. Yet after someone has gone through, the route seems to close behind them. The fronds settle across the gap. Water slips from one leaf to another until it reaches the canal, and the pump gives its next unhurried thud.

Above, the city must have seasons. Down here the plants live by smaller changes: the interval between drops, the warmth of a pipe, the few hours when light falls squarely through a grate. They have grown into every place those things reach, and stopped at every place they do not.
