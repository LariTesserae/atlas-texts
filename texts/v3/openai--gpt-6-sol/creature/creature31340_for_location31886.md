Nothing has settled here long enough to make a home. If the plateau has inhabitants, they are the slow movements beneath it.

One passes under the old doorway. It cannot be seen whole: only a tightening crack on one side, a slab rising on the other, the gradual loss of level in the shelf between. Farther out, another presses a ridge across the open ground. They move at different rates. One may spend hours lifting a stone no higher than a finger; another announces itself with a single report that carries for miles. Their warmth comes through the broken basalt, and through the pale seams where the rock has stretched and cooled.

They do not appear to notice the masonry. The gray stones stand for a while where hands set them, then lean or part as the ground beneath them changes. The empty doorway may still be upright when the new ridge reaches it. It may also be carried upward intact, opening onto another stretch of bare rock.

There are no tracks to follow and no sheltered creatures to startle. Standing still, a person might feel the faint vibration pass underfoot, cease, and begin again somewhere beyond sight. That is as close as one can come to meeting what lives here.
