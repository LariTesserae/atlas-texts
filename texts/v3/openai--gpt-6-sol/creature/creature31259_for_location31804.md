What lives here is easiest to see at its edges. The broad leaves beneath the lamp belong to plants that have found just enough light; the vines on the ladder have found water. Their roots descend into the hot dark without touching the bare wall. A few have reached the split in the floor. Each week, a little more of them disappears.

Below the slab, the roots meet something older than the crack: a thin, pale growth spread through the warm, wet spaces between concrete and stone. It has no stem or center. It threads around grains of aggregate, enters the rust on the underside of a pipe hanger, and thickens where a falling drop carries minerals down to it. Most of it is too fine to distinguish from a stain. At the lips of the fracture, where the concrete has freshly broken, it shows as a faint softness on surfaces that should be sharp.

It does not pull the roots down. They grow toward the moisture, and its threads grow along them. In the cooler hour, water travels farther into the break; in the hotter one, the exposed threads dry back and the growth persists deeper below. The cabinets click, the air shifts, and this exchange goes on without any part of it needing to hurry. From above, the floor looks empty. Beneath it, the small space opened by the split is slowly becoming crowded.
