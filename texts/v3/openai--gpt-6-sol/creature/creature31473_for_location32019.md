At first, the inhabitants seem to be the callers: little bodies tucked into root hollows, each claiming a pitch. One gives a rasp whenever rain gathers on the lip of its crack. Another answers only footsteps. If you stand still, their calls gradually separate from the noise of falling water. Some have throats that buzz against the stone; some make no sound until a leaf above them spills its load, and then offer a quick, irritated click.

The larger animals are harder to place. You hear leaves part, though not the footfall that ought to follow. A soft rush passes on one side of you and turns away before you can reach toward it. Eventually one stops near enough for you to hear it breathing. It has a low, broad body and many small feet, each finding a dry hold among the wet roots. Its skin smells faintly of bruised fruit. Two of the smaller callers go quiet beneath it; a third continues its note, muffled but unafraid.

It stays until you shift your weight. Then its feet make a brief pattern across the stone, and it moves away. Later you hear that same pattern near the rim, where the calls seem to come from empty air. The animal knows how far the shelf extends. It has been crossing it in the dark all its life.

The posts have inhabitants too. Tiny things shelter where each warm shaft meets the leaf mat. Their legs are too fine to feel on your hand, but you can hear them tapping when you hold still against the wood. They tap out quick, uneven bursts over the slow pulse rising from below. In the cracks beyond the posts, others answer. A heavy drop can silence all of them for a moment; then the tapping resumes from three or four places at once.

Under everything, the roots tighten around a step and ease when it lifts. They do it for the broad-backed animal as well. They do it for water collecting in the shallow bowls of stone. By the time you reach the last post, you can tell that a drop is about to fall from the leaf overhead: a slight pull travels through the mat before the water lets go. The little callers are already waiting for it.
