The thing that slips into the hole is not the only one. Once the water has gone, others come out along the shelf, so close in colour to the limestone that their movement is easier to see than their bodies. They are low, jointed animals, each about the length of a finger. Their backs are hard and pale; their undersides, glimpsed when one climbs the fallen block, are dark and soft. They have more legs than seem necessary until one steps onto the smooth channel wall and holds fast there.

They do not enter the channel while it is dry. Instead they gather at its rim and work their small, combed forelimbs through the damp grit. A dark trace follows each mouth across the stone. Where the drip feeds the patch of green growth, one of the larger animals pauses to crop it, but most of them wait for what the water will leave.

They know the flood before you hear it. At the heavy thud, every pale back goes still. Then they withdraw: not in a rush, but by practiced routes to cracks above the old waterlines. The smallest are carried there beneath the larger ones, gripped against soft bellies. One animal remains beside the middle standing piece until the fine light begins to travel through it. Only then does it turn and disappear.

For a few minutes their home belongs to the surge. It scours the places where they have fed and leaves new things behind: a film on the channel wall, fine strands caught on rough stone, tiny moving specks in the last pockets of water. The animals return while the shelf is still wet. Their legs spread wide against the current’s fading pull, and their combs begin their patient work again.

Deep in a crack behind the fallen block are older backs, too broad to fit through the narrow openings used by the young. They seldom come all the way into the light. You can hear one shift when the chamber grows quiet—a faint scraping beneath the separate sound of falling drops. Its shell bears a succession of darker rims, each formed around an earlier edge. It has lived through enough floods for the stone to have worn a shallow resting place beneath it.

Nothing here appears to tend the channel or understand the lights. The creatures use what arrives: warmth, moisture, the brief abundance after each violent passage, and the reliable interval in which to gather it. When the next deep note passes through the shelf, their little bodies go still again.
