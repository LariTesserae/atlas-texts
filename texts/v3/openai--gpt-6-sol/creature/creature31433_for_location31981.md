At first the chamber seems to belong to the plants. They stand so confidently in the dry stone that you expect them to be sheltering some hidden source of water. But nothing here drinks as a surface plant would. Their roots follow the pale seams, and their leaves keep a store of oil beneath tough skins. When a blade is cut, its edges draw together slowly. Even the small tree has the look of something that has learned to spend very little.

The shell-backed creatures live on that thrift. They hang beneath the leaves where the oily film is thinnest and rasp at it with small, precise mouths. Up close, their shells are not smooth: each bears ridges that fit beneath the hooked teeth of a hind leg. A creature braces itself, draws the leg across its back, and the chamber answers. Those deep in the cracks sound farther away than they are. The stone makes a handful seem like hundreds.

They are not all calling. Some move over the bare floor with their shells lifted clear of the sharp chips, pausing to test a seam with their feelers. Some carry curled leaf-husks into crevices. The smallest stay near the roots, pale and soft-backed until their coverings harden. If you hold still long enough, the rattle separates into short runs and replies, though it never quite stops. One caller goes quiet; another takes up its place.

The little works are harder to account for. A shell-backed creature can pass between the chips of the shelter, but its entrance was made for something larger. Dust lies in the bowl around the husks, and the pegs are worn on their upper edges, as if things were once hung from them and lifted down again. No one comes out to claim them. The creatures cross the shelter, call from inside it, and leave it much as they found it.

That may be how the chamber keeps its strange, new appearance. There is no damp to soften a fracture, little growth to cover one, and almost no traffic except the light tread of shells. Lives begin and end among the roots and cracks, while the sharp planes of the walls hold steady around them. From the entrance you hear one unbroken rattle. Inside, you can watch its makers changing places.
