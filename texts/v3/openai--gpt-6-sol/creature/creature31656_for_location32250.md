The people who live here carry dust home on their sleeves. Even those who do not cut stone know how to listen to it: a clean knock from a sound block, a duller one from a place that may need a brace. Children learn which passages lead to the daylight openings and which end at a fresh work face. They learn not to move a wedge, however much it resembles a useful scrap.

Most households have a mason, a clay worker, or someone who carries for them, though the work passes between neighbors. One person holds a block steady while another beds it in mortar; later, both eat beside the wall they have raised. The town gives them little space to keep work separate from living. A basket of chips waits outside a doorway, a drying mat is drawn aside to let someone through, and a bench belongs to whoever needs its edge that hour. People recognize one another by footsteps before they come into view.

They pay close attention to the roof. Where light enters, they pause to see how far the sun has moved across a wall. Where a seam stays wet after rain, someone sets a basin beneath it. Water is carried carefully while it lasts. Clay is soaked, tools are washed, and what remains is kept for drinking and the hearths. In dry spells they visit the deeper rooms for the little moisture that gathers there, though no one pretends it is enough.

Their clothes and hands are marked by the place: pale grit at the cuffs, clay caught at the nails, shallow cuts from stone that looked smooth until it was lifted. Yet there is care amid the crowding. A stair is kept clear for an older neighbor. Someone working near an opening lays a mat for a child in the warm patch of daylight. When a bird flies in, people look up, wait for it to find its way out, and then return to their tools.

Those at the newest edge of town work most slowly. They loosen blocks one at a time and watch the roof between each blow. Behind them are houses built of stone taken from chambers like this one, occupied by people who remember when their own lane was an unfinished floor. Ahead lies more pale rock. They speak of the rooms they might make there, but they set the next brace before they begin.
