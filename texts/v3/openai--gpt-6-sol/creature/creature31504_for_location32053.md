At first, the only thing living here appears to be the algae near the opening. It makes small, dark claims on the cracks where daylight reaches them. Nothing deeper in the chamber seems to want a foothold.

But the long groove in the far wall is occupied. Beneath its fine silt lies a colony so thin that it has no outline separate from the stone. Its body follows the groove’s worn length, spreading into pores too small to see. Water passes through it slowly. It takes what it needs from that passage and leaves the silt above it undisturbed.

The colony has no part that must come out to feed, and nothing here comes to feed on it. If a hand swept the groove clean, the exposed stone would look bare. Only after the silt settled again would the colony begin to close the tiny openings the hand had made.

It feels the engines through the wall. Each heavy pulse passes along its length, but it does not answer with a movement large enough to see. Its own changes take much longer: a little growth where the groove holds moisture and fine matter, a little retreat where the rock offers less. The machines have become part of the rhythm it lives through, though not part of the shape it keeps.
