The easiest inhabitants to see are the beetles. Their silver backs catch what little light reaches the ground, so their lines appear before their bodies do: a bright seam beside a pool, then nothing as they turn together into the moss. Each carries a bead of water between its forelegs. None drinks. They set the beads down along a crack, where the drops hold their shape against the stone.

The thin climbers are less obliging. At rest they resemble pale newts, with blunt faces and sides so clear that a dark line of stone shows through them. But a climber ascending an overhang does not appear to take steps. Its feet remain spread in the same four places while its body lengthens past them; then the feet are suddenly higher, still spread in precisely the same way. One can watch for a long time without seeing the change happen. Their undersides are green with moss dust. In the open stretches, where there is no moss to hide in, they lie flat and become difficult to distinguish from water.

The birds spend most of their lives out of sight. Their calls come from under the broad leaves, from the far side of the shelf, occasionally from a fissure too narrow to admit a bird. The visible ones are small and dark, with pale seeds held crosswise in their beaks. They drop a seed, listen, and cock their heads as though waiting for it to land. A seed can remain wedged below the lip of a crack for what feels like hours. No bird comes to retrieve it, though another is often seen carrying one just like it.

Nothing here seems to lead a creature very far from its task. Snails graze the same cold patch of moss without stripping it. Beetles disperse and gather again. The climbers turn their clear bellies toward the stone when the thrum beneath it deepens. Yet they are not still, and they do not seem trapped. A beetle jostles its neighbor out of line; a bird breaks off its call to scold something in the leaves. Small lives go on amid the exact sounds and the unchanged rings of water. Whatever the shelf is doing, they have made room to live inside it.
