The people of the basin are easiest to notice when they stand still. Their clothes are the colors of wet earth and last year’s reeds, and they pause often: at a threshold to feel whether water is passing beneath it, beside a wall to listen for loose mud, under the eastern rim to check whether a stone has shifted. Someone crossing the newest fall carries their weight lightly. Someone following the same path tomorrow may find that it has changed.

They go barefoot on the softest streets and wear woven reed soles where broken stone works through the silt. Their hands are rarely clean. In the morning they clear leaves from the narrow channels before the water backs up into sleeping rooms. Later they press clay around roots, tie a willow shoot away from a doorway, or guide it toward a place that needs thickening. A child can be sent to fetch mud and return with a fish cupped carefully in both hands. The fish goes back into the channel; the mud stays.

No house belongs entirely to the people inside it. A swallow may have the high corner, moss the shaded wall, and a willow the roof. When a branch grows across a doorway, the household decides whether to cut it or begin ducking. Often they duck. Their rooms grow smaller in one direction and larger in another, and neighbors who have lived side by side for years can find a new opening between their courts.

In the cold hours, they gather where the daylight falls longest. Their voices carry strangely across the basin floor, so much of their talk is close and low. They know the roof birds by their steps and can tell a passing fish from a leaf by the way the water parts. Beyond the last houses, among the reeds, they harvest what has come up thickly and leave the sparse patches to spread.

They are not the only inhabitants tending the town. Roots hold its walls together. Fish keep its channels awake. Birds take the insects from the roofs, and each season new seedlings claim a little of the fallen earth. The people make paths through all this, then spend much of their time keeping those paths open.
