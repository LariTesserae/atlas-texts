At first, the thickets seem to be the principal inhabitants. Each frond is held open to the sun, its branching veins brightening where a neighbouring leaf touches it. A charge travels through the stand in small, orderly changes: one tier dims as another clears, and a dark filament that looked inert takes on the colour of the layers behind it. No leaf turns to follow the light. The exchange changes; the leaves stay where they are.

Under them lives a flat, dust-coloured feeder. Its body has a broad underside that fits against a vein and a narrow rim that grips without bending the frond. It can remain there so long that dust gathers along its back. When it moves, it slides close to the ground from one seam to the next, stopping where two leaves meet. The contact brightens briefly beneath it. It has no apparent use for the open stretches except to cross them, and it crosses without raising a trail.

Others are harder to pick out. In some thickets, fine dark strands bridge gaps no frond can reach. They lie slack until light passes through the stand; then their surfaces alter by degrees, passing a faint glimmer from one leaf-edge to another. A broken leaf can continue the pattern. Its two halves may be separated by bare ground, yet their veins brighten in the same sequence, as though the missing span still lies between them. Whether the strands carry that exchange or merely grow where it is strongest is difficult to tell.

There may be no single creature larger than these. A stand behaves together, but its borders are uncertain. Its nearest leaves can be touched, while its farthest tiers remain beyond walking distance. A feeder disappears beneath one low canopy and is later visible under another that appears much too far away. It is possible to watch the animal without ever seeing it hurry.

The plain gives its inhabitants light, warmth, and places of contact. They use those things with great precision. Nothing drinks; nothing roots down toward a hidden source. The fronds spread across new-looking seams, and the feeders settle against them, each finding an edge that fits. At the rim of a crack, a grain of dust waits above a strip of sky. Beside it, a filament holds to a leaf. For as long as one watches, both keep their places, while something minute and living goes on passing through the veins.
