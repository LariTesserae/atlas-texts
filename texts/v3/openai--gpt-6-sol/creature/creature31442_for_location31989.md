The soot-coloured creatures are difficult to count. They stand so close that a ledge can appear to hold one long, breathing animal until a mouth opens halfway along it, or a hooked foot reaches out and finds another grip. Their feathers lie flat under the drip. When one shakes itself, its neighbours close the space at once.

They feed without leaving the walls. In still air, their broad mouths are soft folds beneath their eyes. When wind passes through the crack, those folds open wide, and the fine feathers around them lift. Tiny things carried up from the slopes catch there. The creatures higher on the walls hang head-down into the strongest current; those below wait for the gusts that curl and slow between the rock faces. A good wind sets the whole colony stirring, ledge after ledge, while their feet stay fixed.

The pale many-legged things know the routes through this crowd better than any newcomer could. They pass under wings, clean wet feathers, and gather what has caught there but not been swallowed. Some linger where a body has kept the stone warm enough for a drop to form. Below the last occupied ledge, other, slower creatures wait among the fallen feathers and leavings. Almost nothing reaches the floor uneaten.

Young ones are easiest to see after the wind dies. Smaller and less dark, they work their way along their parents’ backs, testing the rock with feet that already hook firmly but have not learned where to place themselves. Their calls are thin. The walls give each one back a much larger voice. Adults answer from both sides of the gap, including places no young creature can reach on foot.

For a while after the split, the pale seam in the stone must have run through a single set of resting places. Now it ends in air twice. The creatures on either wall still call across it. At night, when clouds cover the strip of sky, their replies and the falling drops are enough to measure the space between them: a brief flight for one of these animals, an impassable distance for everything that lives beneath its wings.
