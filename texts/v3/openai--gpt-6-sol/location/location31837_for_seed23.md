At the upper edge of the snowline, a settlement occupies a hollow no wider than a grove. From above it looks crowded: low houses of fitted shale, roofed in overlapping stone, pressed among narrow pools, footbridges, drying racks, and channels scarcely wider than a hand. There is little growing ground. A few sedges grip the seams, and dark moss marks the places where water has run for years.

The hollow is old. Its walls are rounded and deeply scored, the stone worn into lips and bowls by meltwater. Yet nothing within those settled walls stays arranged for long. Water travels just under the surface ice, and when a plug gives way, a dry stair begins to weep or a familiar pool empties beneath its own floor. Thin sheets of water slide over the paths and freeze again at their edges. By afternoon a bridge may lead to the wrong bank. The inhabitants keep spare planks lashed beneath their roofs; they know which stones can be lifted to turn a flow.

Sunlight reaches the hollow with mountain force. It flashes off wet shale and the pale undersides of ice, making the cramped lanes bright even where the houses nearly touch. The air is cold enough to catch in the throat, but always damp. Beads gather on rope and fur and on the stone weights that hold the roofs down.

Creatures occupy almost every gap the people leave. White-throated birds step along the channels, picking at the waterline. Small round-eared animals vanish between warm house walls and frozen rock. Beneath clear ice, dark water beetles move in such numbers that a pool can seem to have a second, shifting bottom. A stoat passes over a roof; the birds lift together, circle once, and settle two lanes away.

For all this motion, the place is remarkably quiet. Water runs in thin films rather than falls. Snow softens the scrape of moved stone. People point more often than they call, watching a new wet line appear across a threshold. Soon several of them are there with wedges and a plank, making a channel around the house. Behind them, in the brilliant cold, the abandoned channel is already turning white.
