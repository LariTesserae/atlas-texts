The chamber is smaller than the machinery inside it. A person can cross it in thirty paces, but not in a straight line: iron housings, brick piers, valve wheels and banks of narrow pipes leave only shoulder-wide passages between them. More pipes cross overhead, close enough to duck under. The floor is a patchwork of plates laid over older plates; along its edges, bolts have worn into shallow sockets where generations of workers once set their feet.

It is hot even though nothing here appears to be running. Heat rises through the metal slowly, stored somewhere below the last visible floor. The air smells of warm dust, old oil and wet brick. In the dark, surfaces are found first by touch: a rail polished smooth at hand height, the rough bloom of rust on a door, a pipe unexpectedly cool against the wrist. Far away, an engine gives a single heavy knock. Minutes pass before another answers it.

Moisture comes from that cool pipe. Condensation gathers along its underside, travels to the lowest bend and falls into a porcelain catch basin. The basin is nearly dry. A brown tide mark shows how high it can fill after rain aboveground, when water makes its way through the buried system of drains and jackets. The drops have been falling for so long that the glaze is worn away at their landing place. Below it, a narrow runnel has been cut into the floor plate—not by any tool, but by the water and the grit it carries.

There are plants here, though few. Pale stems come up through the seam around the basin, leaning toward a maintenance hatch where a little light enters when someone opens a door several rooms away. Their leaves are small and thick. A moth sometimes rests beneath them, wings powdered the same gray as the brick. Nothing else moves readily. Even the loose chains hang still.

The chamber has been repaired many times without being cleared. New collars grip old pipes; abandoned wheels remain on shafts that no longer turn. Each addition took the nearest free space until there was almost none left. The place has settled into that crowded arrangement. Water follows its familiar route, heat passes patiently through the floor, and the rare sound of an engine arrives too muffled to say whether it is starting or stopping.
