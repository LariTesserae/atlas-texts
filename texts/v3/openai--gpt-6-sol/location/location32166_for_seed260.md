The ascent begins on a slope that has not yet decided which way it faces. Underfoot are slabs of dark stone, close-fitted but not laid by hand. Their edges are sharp, and the dust between them is fine and pale, as though the mountain has only recently been taken from its mold. There are no gullies. Nothing has had time to cut one.

Above, a long ridge stands against a dim, unchanging sky. It looks near enough to reach before nightfall, but walking toward it brings other ridges into view, one behind the next. The nearest does not grow larger. Sometimes a ledge seen from below proves to be the underside of a ledge seen from above. The land is not shifting quickly; rather, it has not finished agreeing on the distances between its parts.

The air is cool and dry. Sound fills it so completely that the first impression is of a storm, though nothing blows. The mountain makes a continuous, overwhelming note: a pressure in the teeth and ribs, with higher tones trembling through it. At intervals, a cliff face joins in, and for hours its pitch rises almost too slowly to notice. Loose chips of stone quiver where they lie. No call answers the noise. There are no insects among the stems, no birds on the high shelves, no tracks crossing the dust.

Far apart on the slopes grow thickets of narrow, dark-green blades. Within each thicket the growth is extravagant—layer upon layer of leaves, pale curls at their bases, thick trunks braided around upright seams of rock. A single stand can be as dense as a forest; between stands lie broad reaches of bare stone. The blades are dry and springy, and their cut ends show rings of silver rather than sap. Their roots clasp new fractures. They seem to draw their substance from the strain in the mountain, thriving where the stone has not quite become still.

A path of chipped footholds crosses one of the open reaches. Beside it stands a low shelter of stacked slabs, roofed with bundles of the same silver-cored leaves. Its entrance is turned away from the loudest face of the ridge. Inside are a hand-shaped grinding stone, a shallow bowl full of white grit, and a row of marks scored into the wall. The marks are evenly spaced until the last few, which crowd together without getting smaller. It is possible that someone stopped counting carefully. It is also possible that the days did.

Beyond the shelter, the path ends at a cliff with a fresh, clean face. Halfway across it is a seam of green, roots and leaves pressed flat in the rock; on the far side, the thicket continues upright. The cliff is forming through the plants so slowly that a new blade has time to grow into the space where the two sides meet. It bends there, held between places that are becoming separate.

There is room to sit among the stone chips and watch it. The great note goes on. In the dimness, the seam seems no wider after an hour, though a dusting of white has gathered below it. Behind the ridge, other ridges wait at uncertain distances, and now and then the sound of one arrives from a direction in which no ridge can be seen.
