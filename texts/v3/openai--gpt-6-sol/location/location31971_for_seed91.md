The floor is hard, newly cut glacial clay. A spade would still leave a clean edge in it. Beyond the nearest lamp, it runs out into a broad, low darkness broken by long ridges of ground the excavators left standing. Trees grow above those ridges. Down here their roots emerge from the clay in dense, pale curtains, cross the open faces of the cuts, and disappear again. In places there is more root than earth to look at: fine brown meshes holding a vertical wall together, thicker roots bent around stones, fresh white tips exposed where the last machine passed.

The space beneath them is surprisingly open. You can see the next lit work hut a long way off, its door a small yellow rectangle. A single rail line crosses the floor toward it. Elsewhere there are only spoil heaps, a stack of pipe, and the black mouths of side passages. Nothing has had time to settle into the shapes of a natural cave. The banks are sharp; roots hang where they were severed. Thin ice feathers the shaded side of each cut.

The noise comes from the ventilation plant at the far end of the workings. Its fans drive air through the long galleries with a steady, punishing force. Near an outlet, speech is useless; farther away, the sound remains in the floor and in the roots, a continuous heavy rush beneath the occasional metal crack of a rail wagon. When the fans change speed, the hanging roots stir together. For a moment the whole exposed underside of the forest seems to shiver.

This is not a wet place, despite the roots. Condensation beads on a cold pipe and drips into a shallow wheel rut; after rain, water briefly threads down the fresh walls. Most of the floor stays dry and firm. The living trees take their moisture from ground that has not been opened, and their exposed roots are beginning to darken at the ends. A few mossy patches persist near the drips. Once in a while a beetle appears there, or a small animal hurries along the rail and is gone.

At the limit of the lamps, the cut continues under more trees. The machinery will advance slowly, a little farther each week, leaving another clean wall of roots behind it.
