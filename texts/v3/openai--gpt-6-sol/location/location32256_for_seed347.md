From the rim, the ground appears to have dropped away in one piece. A broad basin lies below the surrounding plain, its walls still pale where they broke and not yet softened into slopes. Daylight reaches the floor without difficulty. It shows a whole low town pressed into the basin’s wet ground: roofs close enough to touch, narrow passages between them, and, beyond the last roofs, more reeds and water than the eye can sort into separate fields.

The houses are raised from mud and bound with living willow. Their walls have thickened unevenly as roots took hold; some doorways must be ducked through because a branch has grown across the lintel. There are no lamps or metal fittings. Daylight comes through open courts and gaps left beneath the eaves. Water finds the streets more readily than people do. It runs in shallow channels alongside the footpaths, disappears under packed-earth thresholds, and comes out again in garden beds on the other side. After rain, the channels rise until every block seems to have its own thin moat.

It is cold down here, even when the rim is in sun. The broken walls hold the night air, and moisture beads on clay, bark, and skin. Yet the basin is green to the point of crowding. Moss fills the joints in the walls. Sedges lean over the paths; willow shoots spring from old roofs; small white flowers occupy the few patches of bare silt. The newest ground, a fan of fallen earth beneath the eastern wall, is already stippled with seedlings. Its stones are still sharp. People give it room when they cross, watching for a second small fall.

Much is moving, though the town stays quiet. Water slips through roots. Fish turn in the dark channels. Birds step delicately along roof ridges, looking for insects that the cold has made slow. From time to time a wall releases a drop into a pool, or a reed stem brushes another. The inhabitants tend their passages by hand, drawing back a branch here, pressing fresh mud into a gap there. By the next season the branch may have become part of a room, and the repaired wall may be holding a nest.
