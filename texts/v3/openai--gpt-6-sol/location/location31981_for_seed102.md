The chamber lies only a few body-lengths below the surface. You enter through a crack narrow enough to turn your shoulders, and then there is room to stand. Its walls are fresh-looking, split into sharp pale planes with none of the rounding that age ought to give them. The floor is a scatter of angular stone. It is warm against the soles of your feet and so dry that dust will not cling to your skin.

Plants occupy the chamber without filling it. Each grows apart from the others: a dark tuft in one corner, a fan of fleshy blades beside the entrance, a small tree whose leaves crowd against the low ceiling. At a glance their crowns make the place seem lush. Between them lie broad stretches of bare mineral. Their roots enter seams in the rock and draw up something clear and oily; a broken leaf smells of pepper and hot resin, but releases no water. The leaves are thick enough to hold their shape in the still air. Nothing wilts.

A weak amber light comes from grains embedded in the walls. It is just enough to show the little works among the stones: a waist-high shelter stacked from flat chips, a row of pegs driven into a fissure, a shallow bowl carved from a single black pebble. The bowl contains curled husks. No path joins these things. Someone knew this chamber well enough to make use of it, but left most of its floor untouched.

The noise is startling in so small a place. Shell-backed creatures cling to the undersides of the leaves and rub their legs together; others answer from deep in the cracks. Their calls meet in a hard, continuous rattle that the stone sends back from every direction. Now and then you see one cross the open floor, carrying its broad shell high on thin limbs, and realize how many more must be hidden. The plants stand motionless through it all.

The chamber has an odd steadiness. Its edges remain sharp, the amber grains give the same dim light, and the rattle never swells or fades. A creature vanishes into a crack as another emerges elsewhere. From the entrance the place looks newly opened; after a long stay, it still does.
