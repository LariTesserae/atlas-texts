The town stands on a high shelf of dark stone, with a broad valley visible beyond its outer walls. The shelf looks newly made: its edges are angular, and its shallow gullies have not yet worn themselves deep. Rain has found every seam, though. Water beads on the black rock, gathers in narrow channels, and lies just below the thin soil of the open ground.

The streets are paved in fitted stone and spaced farther apart than the houses seem to require. Between rows of low, pale-plastered buildings lie empty courts, kitchen plots, and long strips of grass. Roofs slope toward clay cisterns. At the uphill edge of town, a hand-cut ditch brings spring water past the gardens; at the downhill edge, it spills quietly over the shelf in a number of small falls. Stone steps and wooden footbridges make ordinary crossings of it. Nothing here needs a wheel to turn or a great engine to lift it.

The air is warm after rain. Clouds move across the valley, but on the shelf the daylight is even, showing the red earth in the plots, the wet mortar, and the few trees planted where deeper pockets of soil allow them. Reeds grow thickest around the cistern overflow. Elsewhere the plants keep to cracks and tended ground, leaving wide areas of bare stone.

It is remarkably quiet for a town. Water ticks from eaves and runs under bridges. Now and then a tool touches stone somewhere out of sight, or a door closes. There are no calls from the sky or the grass. The channels continue at the same unhurried pace through the streets, and beyond the last wall the high, wet country opens out again.
