The district stands on a mountain that has never broken the sea’s surface. Its summit is a blade of new black stone, high above the drowned plains but still beneath a great depth of water. The rock has hardly had time to wear smooth. Corners of basalt jut through the paving, and the streets bend around them rather than cut them away.

There are only six streets. They meet beneath a narrow tower, though *street* is an imperfect word for a passage filled from floor to ceiling with cold seawater. The buildings are open frameworks of pale ceramic, set far enough apart that each has its own dark water around it. No windows shine. At some doorways, a faint blue line marks an entrance; it grows no brighter as you approach. Nothing green grows on the stone, and nothing has settled in its seams.

The tower holds the district still. Its workings are buried in the mountain, beyond any visible machinery. Fine particles hang in the water without sinking. A loose chip of basalt remains beside the corner it broke from. Even pressure seems to have been assigned a place: in one room the sea bears down with the weight of the water above it; across an unmarked threshold, it presses no harder than a hand. The arrangement does not pulse or correct itself. It simply holds.

Sound carries clearly here when there is sound to carry. Once in a long while, something small and jointed passes between the buildings, its limbs making a faint series of taps on the ceramic. Then it leaves the summit, and the streets are quiet again. Beyond their edge, the young rock falls steeply into darkness. From the last doorway you can look out over that drop and still see the suspended chip, the blue line, and every corner of this little city exactly where it was.
