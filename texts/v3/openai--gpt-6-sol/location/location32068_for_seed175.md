The hollow lies high enough that the land falls away on every side, though you cannot see far from within it. Its walls are low, rounded shoulders of dark stone, enclosing a space no wider than a small grove. Rain has worked on them for so long that nothing looks broken. Each ledge has a softened lip; each crack opens into a shallow cup. Water rests in some of the cups and leaves others shining.

It is warm here, even when cloud covers the sun. Moisture collects on the stone in clear beads, gathers beneath overhangs, and drops into a thin sheet of water across the hollow floor. The sheet is too shallow to hide the rock beneath it. Pale seams show through, dividing the dark surface into broad, irregular pieces. Where the floor dips, the water deepens to the color of steeped leaves.

Plants keep to those seams. Ferns rise from them in dense green fans, with moss packed below and a few long-leaved stems leaning into the open air. At the edge of each cluster there is suddenly bare stone again. The hollows between the plants feel spacious, and the plants themselves look almost crowded by the places that can hold their roots. No path crosses the floor, and nothing has been set down or built upon it. A loose flake of stone might fit a hand as a scraper, but its edge has been made only by weather.

The place is nearly silent. Now and then a drop leaves the underside of a ledge and reaches the water below. There is no answering call, no movement among the leaves except their slow release after rain. Even the water scarcely seems to travel.

Stay long enough, though, and its direction becomes difficult to judge. A wet line climbs one wall by the width of a finger while a pool at its foot grows no smaller. Later, the line fades and the pool stands deeper. The rock holds water near its surface and gives it back by degrees; here, on this warm height, the stone seems to draw a little from the air as well. Nothing changes quickly enough to watch. The hollow has had an age to arrive at this shape, and spends each day quietly making it again.
