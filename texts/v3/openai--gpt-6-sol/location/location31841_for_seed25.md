The ground is almost level, but it will not let a foot fall flat. Low ridges of dark stone run across the basin in close, wandering lines, with narrow troughs between them. Water has worn the ridges smooth over a very long time. It is not doing much of that work now. Most troughs hold only damp grit, though a few carry a thread of cold water that disappears beneath the next ridge.

There is no sky to judge the size of the place by. The roof is lost in darkness, and the floor continues beyond the reach of sight, interrupted by banks of fallen stone and shallow hollows. Air moves through it slowly. It smells of wet rock, salt, and something sharp rising from cracks in the floor. Nothing grows upright here. No roots prise the stone apart; no leaves collect the moisture. Along the cracks, a nearly invisible skin of microbes feeds on what seeps up from below.

The animals make that thin supply seem impossible. Small pale bodies crowd every wet seam, passing over and under one another. Some scrape at the stone with hard mouthparts. Others wait in the troughs and seize what the water brings. Long-legged hunters step across the ridges, feeling ahead with limbs finer than their shadows. In the air, soft-winged creatures turn so close together that an unseen flock can be heard changing direction all at once. Shells knock against stone. Claws tick. Calls answer calls until none can be followed for long.

At intervals, the basin makes a deeper sound: a rolling blow that travels underfoot. Water is pushing through passages below, driving air ahead of it. The animals fall quiet for a moment. Then droplets begin to strike the roof and floor in scattered places, and the whole basin starts up again, louder. Those in the troughs climb the ridges; those on the ridges crowd toward the new water. By the time the flow sinks away, the stone has been picked clean. It stays wet and dark for hours, while the sound goes on.
