The bottom is almost level here. In the dark it first feels empty: warm water, soft mud, a slow pressure against the body. Then a wall appears close enough to touch. It is made of shells grown edge to edge, their openings filled and sealed by later generations. Another wall stands behind it, and beyond that a row of low, rounded roofs. The settlement spreads across the plain farther than sight can follow.

Nothing in it was cut or carried into place. Small animals began by fastening themselves to slight rises in the seabed. Their descendants grew over them; currents laid silt in the gaps; harder colonies took hold where the water flowed fastest. After a long time there were courtyards where the current slows, narrow passages where it hurries, and tall, porous ridges that let it through without falling. The oldest ridges are worn smooth on one side. New growth closes a passage elsewhere, so the ways through the city change slowly, over lifetimes.

Almost no plants grow under the heavy water. A few pale threads trail from the upper edges of the walls. Most of what seems, at first, to be foliage is animal: soft fans drawing food from the current, mouths set on stalks, thin arms withdrawing into cracks. Fish crowd the passages without making them feel noisy. They turn together around corners, vanish into holes, emerge through windows on the other side. Along the mud, many-legged scavengers work through what has settled from above. Larger bodies pass beyond the walls; their movement reaches the courtyards as a change in the water before they can be seen.

There is little light. Now and then a creature gives off a dim pulse, enough to show a roofline or the fine cloud of silt rising from a disturbed floor. Sound carries strangely: an isolated click comes from very far away, while the constant feeding and shifting of the place is nearly silent.

A warm current moves through the main passages today. It brings food, and the walls open to it. When it turns, the animals on this side will draw in, those on the other side will extend, and the city will seem to have changed its face.
