A ridge rises through the warm water, its crest broad enough to lose sight of itself. Far below, the seabed falls away on both sides into blue. Up here, sunlight reaches the stone plainly: every crack has a lit edge, every overhang a dark underside. The ridge is young. Its black rock breaks in clean planes and narrow points, with little sand yet gathered at their feet. In places a slope of loose fragments looks as though it might still be settling, though nothing moves while you watch.

Life has occupied it faster than the sea has worn it smooth. Long green blades crowd the gentler shelves, bending all in one direction under a current too slight to feel. Brown fronds grow from fissures in the steeper faces. Between them lie close, soft mats of red and pale green, making even the bare-looking rock dense with growth. There is room to see across the ridge for a great distance, but hardly a hand’s width of empty surface nearby.

At intervals, clear water seeps from the stone. It is warmer than the water around it and rises in wavering columns, bending the view of the plants behind it. Above the highest ledges, these columns spread into a trembling layer. Light passing through it lays slow, shifting bands across the ridge. The place can seem briefly to have a second, unsteady floor suspended overhead.

There are few animals. A small fish appears between the blades, holds still, and vanishes into them. Once in a while a distant shape crosses the open blue beyond the drop, too far away to give the ridge any sense of company. Mostly there is only the faint brush of leaves against leaves. The young rock remains sharp; the rooted growth thickens; the warm water keeps rising through its seams.
