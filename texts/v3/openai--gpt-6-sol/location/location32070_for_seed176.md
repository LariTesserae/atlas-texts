The ledge is only forty paces wide. Beyond its outer lip, the mountain falls away so steeply that the valley is hidden beneath a flat expanse of cloud. On clear afternoons, a second, more distant ridge appears above it, making this small patch of ground feel higher still. The air ought to be cold at this height, but warmth rises through the stone. Water beads on every exposed surface and gathers in the seams of the rock.

The ledge is young: broken slabs with pale, sharp edges, black gravel that has not yet settled, and cracks too clean to have held soil for long. Its older parts are easy to find. Ferns crowd there, their stems pressed together in hollows full of wet grit. Moss covers the sheltered faces of boulders, while thin-rooted shrubs have taken hold along the rim. Where warm water seeps out of the slope, broad leaves lean over it until their tips touch the flow. A few steps away, on bare stone, there is almost nothing growing.

A pipe comes out of the mountainside below the ferns. It crosses the ledge on low iron feet, enters a small turbine housing, then disappears back into rock. There is no road and no larger building in sight—only this piece of machinery, a cable strung upward, and a narrow service path worn between them. The turbine’s steady roar fills the whole ledge. Beneath it, water ticks from leaves onto metal and runs through the gravel in channels too shallow to see.

The machine gives the place a dependable rhythm. Its casing trembles; the ferns beside it tremble too. Every few minutes a valve lets off a brief hiss, after which the roar sounds unchanged. Mineral stains have spread slowly down the concrete footing, and moss has begun to grow over their edges. Even the loose gravel seems to move only by degrees, nudged downhill each rainy season.

There are few animals here. Once in a while, a small bird lands on the cable and lifts off again almost at once. More often the only movement comes from water: drops forming under the pipe, leaves releasing their weight into the seep, and cloud drifting past the open edge far below.
