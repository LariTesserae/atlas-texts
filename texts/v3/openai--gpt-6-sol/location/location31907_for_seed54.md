The city stands on a high shelf of black stone, though *stands* is not quite the word. Its lower streets are fixed to the rock; above them, whole districts hang out over the drop, held by an engineered field that gives each span just enough weight to keep it in place. In the dark, their edges are difficult to find. A few amber guide marks show where a stair begins, and then the stair seems to continue into cloud.

Water is everywhere without ever forming a river. It condenses on cables, gathers under window ledges, runs down the faces of buildings and freezes in the joints. Narrow canals cross the streets beneath grilles, carrying the little that stays liquid toward cisterns deep in the shelf. At intervals a collector opens its shutters to the cloud and closes them again, shedding a load of water with a crash. The sound travels across the city and returns from the opposite side of the valley.

There is no grass in the cracks, no moss under the continual wet. The exposed stone looks new: sharp seams, pale fractures, faces not yet worn smooth. In places it has been drawn upward into piers while it was still forming. Fine crystals grow around their bases, slowly enough that a person who lives here might notice a change only by comparing one winter with another.

Most of what moves is animal. Small, hard-shelled bodies cover the warm sides of the cisterns so closely that a hand laid against the wall would touch several at once. They feed at mineral seeps and around the city’s outflows, scraping in a sound like cutlery being sorted. Larger creatures hang beneath the bridges, folded into themselves for weeks. Their claws tick against the metal when the wind changes. Farther out, pale, broad-winged animals circle the collector towers, appearing for a moment against a rare lighter patch of cloud before vanishing into the same darkness as the streets.

The inhabitants have built around these crowds rather than cleared them away. Doorways open onto raised walks; freight passes through sealed tubes. On a night passage, someone may wait while a slow procession of shelled animals crosses the threshold, each one following the damp track left by the last. Nothing here is hurried. The cloud presses against the shelf, the collectors fill, the canals empty, and the animals shift by inches. Together they make a noise so continuous that the city seems quiet only when one part of it stops.
