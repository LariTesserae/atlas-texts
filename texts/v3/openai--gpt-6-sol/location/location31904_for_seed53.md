The pass is hardly wider than a room. On one side a wall of pale rock rises out of sight; on the other, the ground ends so abruptly that the next ridge seems to hang in open air. Between them lies a crowded floor of broken stone: slabs standing on edge, fist-sized chips, seams packed with old gravel. There is nowhere to put a foot without choosing.

The sun is fierce at this height, though it gives little warmth. It brings out different colors in the rock—white quartz, blue-gray faces of freshly split stone, orange staining along cracks that have been wet before. Everything exposed feels cold. In the wall’s shadow, frost survives into the afternoon, gathering in fine needles under overhangs and inside the small holes weather has made.

A low shelter occupies the widest part of the pass. Three courses of stones form a crescent against the wall, with no roof and barely enough space for two people to sit out of the wind. The stones were fitted by hand, then slowly jostled out of place by freezing ground. Nearby, shallow cuts on a flat slab may be tool marks, or the places where someone once struck chips from it. Nothing grows among the stones. There are no nests, droppings, tracks, or insects warming themselves in the light.

For much of the day the pass is quiet. Wind crosses overhead without entering it. Then a drop falls from a sunlit crack and strikes the same stone again and again. Meltwater gathers beneath the frost, runs a finger’s length, and disappears into gravel. By evening that little passage will close with ice. Elsewhere, a slab loosens as the sun leaves it and settles with a dry click; a few grains skitter toward the drop.

The pass has been narrowing and opening in such small ways for a long time. Its edges are worn, but its floor is never quite still. Frost lifts a stone; thaw lets it down differently. Water carries away dust too fine to see until it dries in a pale rim around a hollow. The shelter will not remain a shelter forever, yet it is still recognizably one: a brief, deliberate curve in a place shaped mostly by cold, height, and the patient movement of stone.
