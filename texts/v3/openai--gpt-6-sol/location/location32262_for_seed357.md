From the upper rim, the valley looks almost empty. A few tall green frames stand far apart across a broad, shining floor; beyond them, another rim rises into warm cloud. There is room for weather to cross the whole place. You can watch a squall arrive as a dark band, strike the far structures, and travel toward you.

Up close, the floor is not ground. It is a fitted surface of broad leaves, their edges sealed to one another over thick, living ribs. Rainwater runs along the seams into channels that look carefully laid out. Where a channel meets a slope, its sides fold inward and make a steep chute. There is no exposed soil or stone. Even the sharp ridge underfoot is a new growth, hard-skinned and still pale at its edges, lifting fast enough to crack the older leafwork beside it.

The standing frames are plants too. Some form open halls with vaulted roofs and walls that stop short of the floor. Others are only rows of columns carrying gutters high above the valley. Nothing shelters in them. Their openings admit wind, rain, and an occasional sheet of mist; no voices or animal calls answer from inside. They appear made for the work of catching and moving water. During a downpour, the roofs fill, the gutters bow, and great quantities spill from one frame to the next. Afterward, droplets keep falling for hours from joints you cannot see.

The heat is strongest when the rain ends. Vapor rises from every seam, blurring the far rim while the sky overhead clears to an ordinary bright blue. The valley grows loud: water beating into hollow stems, channels roaring, taut leaves snapping when a gust gets beneath them. Now and then a column splits with a report like timber breaking. Its two halves do not fall. They bend away from each other, fasten to neighboring ribs, and leave a new passage between them. Elsewhere, a gutter closes before it has emptied and sends a sudden waterfall over the edge of a roof.

Across the open distances, these changes are easy to see. A line that was straight in the morning has acquired a bend by afternoon; a familiar opening is gone. The valley never becomes crowded. It keeps making broad spaces between its constructions, as if those spaces are needed to let the hot rain pass through.
