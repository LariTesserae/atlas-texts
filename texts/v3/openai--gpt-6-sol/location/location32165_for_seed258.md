The plateau is held above the surrounding country on a lattice of living trunks. From a distance it looks like a broad, level meadow with a few towers standing far apart. Up close, there is no ground beneath the grass. The grass grows from a mat of braided roots, the mat rests on vaults of wood, and the vaults open onto warm air a long way below.

Everything here has been made, though there is no sign of a tool. The vaults have the regular spacing of an arcade. Along their upper edges, pale roots grow into low walls that keep the water from spilling away. Pools occupy shallow, leaf-lined courts; narrow channels pass between them under bridges thick enough to bear a grove. Even the scattered humps that resemble new stone are packed from seed husks, mineral crust, and the hard outer skins of old stems. Their surfaces are smooth and unfinished. Nothing has yet worn a path through them.

There is surprisingly much room. A single tree may command an entire wet square, its crown spreading flat to catch the light, while the next stands across an open reach of silver-green moss. The spaces are not bare. Small leaves tile them so closely that they appear, from standing height, to be continuous surfaces. Water lies among those leaves, near enough to touch with one finger. It gathers on the underside of every rail and hangs there in beads that never seem to fall.

The air is warm and faintly sweet. At noon the light is ordinary sunlight, softened by moisture but not dimmed; it shows the different greens plainly, from the dark, almost black walls of the pools to the fresh yellow growth at their rims. There are few animals to disturb them. Now and then a long-legged bird picks its way along a channel. Once it stops, it can be difficult to find again.

The place is loud without seeming busy. Water descends through hollow roots inside the plateau and returns through other roots, and each open vault gives its flow a different note. There is a low rushing underfoot, a clear knocking from the pools, a sustained tone from somewhere out across the meadow. The sounds do not approach or recede. Stay long enough, and the ear begins to separate them into distances.

The young crusts, the open joints, the roots reaching across gaps all suggest a landscape still coming into being. Yet the water keeps its level, and the fresh edges remain fresh. Perhaps the plateau grows only enough each day to replace what each day consumes. It holds itself in that early condition: spacious, wet, and carefully made, with every part of it alive.
