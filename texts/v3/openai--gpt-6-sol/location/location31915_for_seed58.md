The district fits inside a single excavated chamber, though from its floor it is hard to see the chamber at all. Three narrow streets pass between brick engine houses, tanks, and windowless storeys of workshops. Pipes cross overhead in such numbers that they make a second, lower ceiling. A rail spur enters through one wall and ends beneath a loading crane; another doorway, scarcely wider than a cart, has been cut into the opposite rock. There is nowhere here to step back and take in the whole place.

The excavation is recent. Beyond the last row of buildings, the stone still bears pale drill scars and sharp, unweathered corners. Broken flakes lie where they fell, half buried already by cinders. Heat comes steadily through the floor. It loosens the tar in the street joints and gives the iron handrails a warmth that lingers after you let go. Most lamps have been set deep under their shades; their amber light reaches a doorway or a flight of steps, then gives up.

Water appears in small, punctual ways. A pipe sweats along its lower edge. Drops gather on a cool band of rock above the rail and fall onto the same two sleepers, darkening them. After a rain somewhere overhead, a seam in the ceiling opens into a brief trickle, and the gutters carry it past every workshop to a covered cistern. A few thin stalks have taken root beside that seam. Nothing grows elsewhere, and nothing comes to drink.

The machinery keeps the district busy without making it loud. Belts turn behind closed walls. A pressure gate lifts, holds, and settles; farther down the street, a cart rolls a few yards and stops under a hopper. The operations answer one another across the chamber, passing heat, water, and material along routes laid out with little room for error. When the ceiling drips, shutters close over the hottest vents. When the seam dries, they open again. Between these adjustments, the district is quiet enough to hear each drop strike the rail.
