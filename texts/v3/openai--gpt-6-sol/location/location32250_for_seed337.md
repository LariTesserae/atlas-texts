The town lies beneath a plain, but it does not feel buried. Its roof is a broad shelf of pale stone, broken by openings large enough to admit the day. Light falls through them onto lanes, work yards, and unfinished walls; from the upper steps you can see across one chamber into the next, as far as the roofs and stone pillars allow. The air stays cool even at noon. In the shaded passages it smells of damp clay and freshly split rock.

There is scarcely any bare ground. Houses of stacked stone and packed earth press against the pillars that hold up the roof. Narrow stairs occupy gaps between houses; baskets, timber braces, drying mats, and heaps of cut blocks occupy the gaps beside the stairs. A wall may serve one household as a back wall and another as the edge of a workbench. The town is crowded without being tall. Wherever its builders gain a little floor, someone levels it with a hand tool and begins to use it.

Beyond the last houses, the chambers are still being made. Wedges sit in seams where blocks have not yet come free. Fresh faces of stone are square-edged and pale, with none of the rounding that time or running water might give them. Clay taken from the floor becomes mortar and hearth lining; stone taken from the roof becomes lintels. The place grows by subtraction, and the material removed to make room is carried back in to furnish it.

Water arrives less dependably. After rain on the plain, moisture darkens the walls below the openings and gathers along certain seams. For a few days, shallow basins hold enough to wash tools and soak clay. Then the floors dry again, except for cold beads of condensation in the deeper rooms. A few tufts of grass survive beneath the widest openings, and thin roots hang through cracks overhead. Now and then a small bird enters with the light, circles once, and leaves.

The town is busy, though from a distance it is nearly quiet. Feet scuff on clay. A stone is tapped into place, then tapped again more carefully. Someone calls across a chamber and receives an answer softened by all the walls between them. Even the new work has a patient sound: blocks loosened one at a time, dust swept aside, another brace fitted before the roof can settle.
