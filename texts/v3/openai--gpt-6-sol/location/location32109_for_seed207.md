The chamber is no larger than a small room. It lies beneath a slope of compacted earth, reached through a narrow service opening that has almost closed under roots. The ceiling is low enough to touch. Most of its surface is stone, but a strip of smooth, dark material runs through it, disappearing into the walls on either side.

It is cold here in every season. Moisture gathers on that strip and falls at long intervals, one drop at a time. Each drop lands on the same worn patch of stone. There is no stream, only damp seams, a shallow basin that sometimes fills, and mud along the chamber’s lowest edge. The stone has been rounded by centuries of seepage; the artificial strip is newer, though its joints are packed with silt.

Plants occupy nearly all the remaining space. Ferns lean over the basin. Fine roots cross the ceiling like stitching, and pale leaves overlap in the narrow gaps between them. Their growth is dense but slow. Deep in the rock, a heat exchanger draws a little warmth from a buried line, while threads set into the ceiling supply the plants with the light they need. To an eye entering from outside, those threads give almost nothing away. After a while, one can distinguish the nearest leaves as darker shapes against the dark.

A squat service post stands beside the basin. It has no display and no obvious controls, just a lid furred with mineral deposits. A second fitting, farther back, has been swallowed by roots. The system still works: now and then the strip in the ceiling cools enough to collect water, and the basin rises by a finger’s breadth before the moisture is taken up again. Nothing happens quickly enough to watch.

There are no insects under the leaves, no tracks in the mud, no small movements when the service opening is disturbed. The quiet is broken by a drop, the faint contraction of the ceiling strip, and occasionally the settling of soil overhead. The chamber has kept its shape for a long time. Roots have learned the positions of its fittings; water has found the few places where it can rest.
