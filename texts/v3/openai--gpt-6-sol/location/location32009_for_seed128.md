There is a hollow beneath the upper lip of the city, no wider than a room. From inside it, the city is mostly a noise. Air rushes through a stack of vanes below the floor, and the floor answers with a continuous metallic roar. Every few minutes a deeper note passes through, strong enough to tremble in the teeth. You can see very little of the machinery that makes it. The hollow bends twice before it opens to the outside, keeping the light out and the heat in.

Water gathers on every surface. It beads under the ceiling’s black ceramic ribs, travels their worn edges, and drops into channels molded along the walls. Some channels are clogged with a fine gray silt: material shed by the ribs over centuries of expansion and repair. At the threshold, the water runs over a rounded sill polished by countless floods. Beyond it is open air, a strip of cloud, and a height the dimness makes difficult to measure.

Nothing here is dug from the earth. The ribs, sill, channels, and even the gritty floor are manufactured. Yet the hollow has the settled shape of a place made slowly by weather. Water has found its own routes across the intended ones. Mineral-colored stains spread around old seams. In one corner, where the warm air barely moves, a single pale vine has rooted in the silt. Its few leaves are translucent and perpetually wet.

Most of the life is animal. Small, dark flyers pack themselves into a crease above the doorway, shifting and clicking whenever the deeper note rolls through. Thin-legged scavengers work the channels in crowds, feeling along the waterline for what the flyers drop. Something with a broad, flat body waits under the sill and takes a scavenger now and then; you see the water close over its back before you see the animal itself. The roar hides nearly all of their sounds, but not their movement. The walls seem to ripple with it.

Behind the ribs, a field holds the vast exchange vanes in alignment without bearings or visible supports. That part has never needed replacing. Around it, the smaller work of the hollow continues: condensation, shedding, feeding, drainage. Warm water escapes at the sill and is torn into mist over the drop. New water forms on the ceiling. The flyers turn their faces toward it, waiting for the next drop to fall.
