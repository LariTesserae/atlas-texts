Below the surface is a shallow chamber in the rock, about the size of a small hall. Its ceiling is the water above it. Pale daylight reaches the floor without a beam or a shimmer, spreading evenly over rounded stone, a few shelves of silt, and a long groove worn into the far wall. Nothing here has a sharp edge. Even the loose stones have settled with their broad sides down.

The water is cool and clear enough to see from one end of the chamber to the other. A little dark algae holds to cracks near the opening; elsewhere the stone is bare. There are no fish, shells, or tracks in the silt. The absence is easy to miss at first. Then the chamber’s stillness begins to feel unusually complete: no small movement among the stones, no passing shadow, nothing stirring what has come to rest.

A low, even vibration passes through the water and the rock. It comes from well beyond the chamber—engines working somewhere outside it—and has no visible source here. At intervals a heavier pulse can be felt through the floor, steady enough to cease being an event. Against it, the chamber keeps its older shape. The groove remains full of fine silt; the algae stays confined to its few cracks. The machines run, and this little place beneath them does not seem to change.
