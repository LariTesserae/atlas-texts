The plateau is still rising. Across its breadth, black ridges lift a little and stop, then lift again farther off. In the dark it is hard to judge their distance. A line that seems to mark the horizon may be the near edge of a new escarpment; by morning, if morning can be seen here, it may stand a person’s height above the rest.

There is no soil. The ground is a jumble of newly broken basalt and pale, glassy seams, every face sharp enough to catch the faint light coming from below. Nothing has had time to round an edge. Loose slabs lie where they fell, warm to the touch. In places the rock has opened without falling apart, leaving long, clean cracks through which a dull red glow rises and fades. There is no smell of wet stone, no channel worn by a stream. Even the dust is scarce: the heights have not yet made much of it.

The air is thin and warm. It moves hard along the ridgelines, but among the stones there are stretches of near silence. A gust passes overhead with nothing to whistle through. Then a slab settles somewhere out of sight, and a single dry report travels across the plateau. More often the change is soundless. A dark plane slowly tilts until it reflects the glow of a fissure; a gap narrows; a row of standing stones leans together.

Those stones were set upright by hands. They appear here and there across the heights, too far apart to make a settlement: a low wall around an empty patch, three courses of masonry with no roof to support, a doorway standing alone on a shelf of rock. They are made of an older, grayer stone than the plateau beneath them. Some have been carried upward intact. Others have been split from their foundations and left facing different directions. There are no paths between them now, and no sign of anyone trying to reach them.

Nothing grows in their shelter. No small creature uses the cracks. The place is bare even where warmth gathers, though it is not still. Underfoot, a fine vibration comes and goes. Far across the open ground, a ridge advances by degrees against a darker sky, pushing broken blocks before it. The rock close by remains firm for a while longer. It is possible to stand beside the old doorway and watch new ground arrive at its threshold.
