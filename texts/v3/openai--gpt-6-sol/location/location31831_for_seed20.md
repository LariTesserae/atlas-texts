At the top of the drowned mountain is a basin no wider than a courtyard. Its rim is a ring of black, newly broken stone, sharp enough to catch the little light that reaches it. Beyond the rim the slope falls out of sight. Nothing here suggests a shore: water fills the basin, covers the peak, and continues upward into darkness. Yet the summit still feels high. The pressure of the whole sea bears down on it, while the mountain beneath seems almost weightless, as if it might break loose and rise.

The water is warm. It rushes through the basin in opposing currents, turning so violently around the rim that loose chips of rock remain suspended between the walls. There is no silt on the fresh faces. Pale seams show where the stone has split, and sometimes a seam opens further with a white flash of exposed mineral. A moment later the rock on either side is whole again, though the chip stays in the water. By now there are many such chips.

The noise is enormous. It comes through the stone and through the body: a continuous grinding roar with impacts inside it, too deep to locate. A crack may sound before the seam appears. Bubbles rise from the basin floor in great, crowded bursts and make a sharper noise as they pass, like handfuls of gravel shaken in a jar. They do not always keep rising. Some stop at shoulder height, flatten into trembling discs, and are carried sideways into the dark.

There are a few things that might have been built. Three low walls of fitted stone stand near the basin’s center, each enclosing a space too narrow to enter. Their courses are neat, but no tool marks survive, and the walls have neither openings nor roofs. A fourth wall lies in pieces farther off. The current lifts one block at a time, turns it slowly, and sets it down near the others. From one moment to the next the ruin looks more complete, then less.

Almost nothing grows here. A tuft of translucent fronds holds to a crack beneath the largest wall. Each time the current presses it flat, its shadow continues waving upright on the stone. Across the basin, a solitary branching coral has put out a few soft new tips; the rest of it is bare. There are no shoals of fish, only an occasional small shape that darts from one fracture to another and vanishes too quickly to judge its size.

The basin has an odd way of keeping its boundaries. From any point within it, the rim looks close. Swim toward it and the crossing takes longer than it should; turn back, and the three walls are still just behind you. Meanwhile every pebble caught in the current travels plainly from one side to the other. The sound gives no warning when the distance changes. It goes on at full force, covering the scrape of stone against stone, while the warm water moves through the narrow space and the mountain makes fresh edges under it.
