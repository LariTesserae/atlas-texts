The valley begins above the last continuous soil. Its floor is a broad shelf of pale stone, worn nearly level, with old gullies crossing it so shallowly that their edges are easier to feel underfoot than to see. Beyond the shelf, the mountains stand in long, weather-rounded walls. They have had time to lose their sharpest faces. Even the fallen rock has settled into stable slopes, each piece lodged among others of much the same size.

The sun is fierce here, though the air stays cool. There is little shade except inside cracks, and little water except there too: a dark seam of damp grit, a bead of condensation beneath an overhang, small basins that hold rain for a day or two. Tufts of wiry grass grow where dust has collected. Farther out, the stone appears bare until a change in light shows lichen spread thinly across it.

The emptiness is loud. Wind pours without pause through a row of holes in the valley wall, making a deep, uneven note that carries across the shelf. It has sounded long enough that the animals pay it no mind. Birds nest by the hundreds in the sheltered cliff seams; their cries cut through the lower roar. Small furred creatures whistle from the rocks and vanish when a shadow passes overhead. On warm faces of stone, dark insects gather in surprising numbers, feeding the birds that wheel above them. There is plenty of life here, but almost none of it occupies the open ground.

Nothing has been built. A traveler might bring a chipped stone edge to cut grass or hide, then carry it away again. The valley gives no sign of keeping anyone’s work. From one side to the other, the same wind sounds through the same holes; moisture returns to the same cracks; birds lift from the wall and settle back into it.
