The ground is only a short distance overhead. In places its underside hangs low enough to touch: a pale, translucent stone threaded with roots, warm to the palm. Elsewhere it rises into broad vaults, and the country beneath it opens out farther than the eye can follow. Daylight comes through the stone without showing the sun. It fills the vaults evenly, bright enough to cast soft shadows and to make every leaf visible, including those deep in the crowded distance.

Almost everything here is plant. Trunks join overhead and descend again as roots; roots flatten into walkable shelves or lift into partitions with cool, fibrous undersides. Broad leaves overlap like roof tiles, though there is already a roof. Narrow leaves stand upright in such numbers that they give distant groves the appearance of brushed green fur. A person could travel a long way along the firm backs of these growths without setting foot on mineral ground. Where the ground does show, it is pale and raw, not yet broken into soil. Fine root tips stipple it, holding grains in place before water or wear has had much chance to shape them.

The air is hot and nearly still. Moisture gathers at the tips of certain leaves, waits, then falls one drop at a time into small dark basins among the roots. Beyond those basins the surfaces are dry to the touch, even dusty with pollen. There are no streams. The plants take what condenses on the roof and what seeps down its occasional seams; their thick stems store the rest. In the brightest reaches, fleshy leaves turn edge-on to the light, showing pale ribs instead of their green faces.

Every so often an opening appears in the growth: a shallow arch, a level platform, a little enclosure with walls of living stems. They look assembled from a distance. Up close, the stems can be followed back into the same rootwork as everything around them. Nothing has been cut. Each opening is simply a place where the plants have left room, and each remains where it is. Even the newest, tenderest shoots emerge in the familiar gaps.

For all its mass, the place makes little noise. A drop strikes a basin. A leaf under tension gives a dry tick. Far off, something small moves once through the foliage and is still. There are few animals to see; now and then a pale wing crosses the daylight beneath the roof. Heat, light, and damp keep the vegetation alive, but scarcely anything here seems in a hurry to grow. The stone is young, the paths are already settled, and the bright country beneath the surface holds its shape from one day to the next.
