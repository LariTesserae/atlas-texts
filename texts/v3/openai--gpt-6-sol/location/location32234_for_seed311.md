The place is no wider than a courtyard, though there is no dry surface from which to measure it. It lies at the bottom of a new city, in the narrow space between two foundation walls. Water fills it to the last seam. Above, six floors of submerged streets and rooms admit a pale, ordinary daylight through their translucent paving. Down here the light has the flat clarity of an overcast morning, enough to show the fresh edges of every cut.

The walls have not yet acquired the softness of ruins. Their pale ceramic faces meet at sharp corners; the lettering cast into them is still legible from across the gap. Pipes crowd those faces, then smaller pipes crowd the pipes. Cable sleeves, pressure vessels, inspection windows, handrails, and closed doors occupy nearly every remaining patch. Nothing trails loose except a few thin green blades rooted in a crack beside a drain. They bow whenever the water changes direction and straighten again.

The water is cool. It presses against every surface with a steady weight, but its currents are local and abrupt. A stream climbs one wall in a clear, twisting sheet. Another pours downward through the middle of the courtyard, carrying grains of white construction dust that never quite settle. At intervals, a row of small apertures opens beneath the paving overhead. The space fills with bubbles and a hard, continuous roar; then the apertures shut, and the bubbles are drawn sideways into a collector before they can rise. Behind the walls, pumps answer one another with deep knocks. A finer sound runs through them all, like a handful of metal filings being shaken in a jar.

There is no animal movement here. The inspection windows are empty. No shells cling to the fittings, and nothing has bitten the sparse blades of grass. Yet the courtyard is busy. Numbered plates turn in their sockets. Seals swell and relax. A fresh length of conduit eases out of one wall, touches the opposite wall, and joins it without a visible weld. The city is still fitting itself together.

Its builders left a rule in large letters over the lowest door: **KEEP THE WATER CONTINUOUS**. The door has no handle. A pressure gauge beside it has a second needle that marks not depth but *absence*. When a bubble passes the gauge, that needle jumps, and the nearest pipes tighten against their brackets. Here, an empty space is a structural flaw. The machines can bend a conduit through crowded water; they cannot bear a pocket with nothing in it.

So the courtyard stays full. Every new joint displaces water into another chamber. Every chamber returns it somewhere else. Standing in this small bright gap, one would hear the work of an entire city through its walls: the knocking foundations, the rushing passages, the brief alarm of bubbles, and the quick ceramic clicks as another opening closes before emptiness can take hold.
