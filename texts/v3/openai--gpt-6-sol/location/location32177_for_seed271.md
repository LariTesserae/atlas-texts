A shallow hollow lies just below the level of the surrounding ground. It is no wider than a small yard, with rounded banks of compacted earth and pale stone that have long since lost their sharp edges. The floor is mostly bare: firm silt, a few loose pebbles, and dark patches where moisture holds. Nothing here looks freshly cut or recently fallen. Even the little channels made by rain have settled into smooth, durable shapes.

At one end, three courses of old stone form the remains of a low wall. The stones were fitted without mortar; soil has filled the gaps and softened the line until it is difficult to tell where the work ends and the bank begins. A shallow trough beside it holds a narrow strip of water. The rest of the hollow is merely damp. Touch the stone and it feels cool, though the air is warm. Beneath the lip of the trough, a bead of condensation gathers and stays.

There are only a few plants. Thin grass stands in separate clumps along the upper bank, and a small, dark-leaved weed has taken root between two stones. Most of the space between them is open earth. Under the dull sky, its colors are subdued: brown silt, gray stone, a little green. The light is weak enough that the water seems dark, though it is clear and shallow.

The place is quiet without being sealed off. Now and then a drop falls from the trough’s stone lip and makes a small sound against the water. Otherwise there is no movement to follow. The old wall offers a place to sit; the bank offers a way out. Neither looks much different from how it must have looked after the last rain.
