Under the bank, where a slab of peat has recently lifted away from the clay, there is a space no taller than a hand. Its roof is a mat of roots. Its floor is living moss laid over black, cold mud, and its open edge looks onto a strip of still water. The gap is new: roots hang with their pale tips unbroken, and the clay beneath them has not yet softened into rounded shapes. Water gathers on each tip, swells, and falls.

A town occupies the dry end of the space. Its lanes are narrow grooves pressed through the moss; its houses are pockets woven from sedge and stitched to the roots above. Bits of bark make thresholds. A row of hollow stems carries runoff past the dwellings to a pool at the entrance. There is no stone foundation. When a root thickens, someone moves a wall. When moss rises through a lane, the lane bends around it. The place has the order of a settlement, but almost none of its materials are dead.

It is nearly dark. Even by day, light enters only from the water and reaches no farther than the first few houses. Farther in, a wet surface may briefly show where a drop has caught the dimness. The cold stays in the clay. It stiffens the sedge walls and keeps the inhabitants close to the moss, where a little warmth is held.

There are more animals here than the town can account for. Springtails graze in the lanes and vanish into them when a drop lands. Pale mites work over the roof roots. Worms pass below the moss, lifting whole sections of street by a hair’s breadth; minute hunters follow the spaces they leave. At the water’s edge, a beetle waits among the hollow stems while other creatures cross over its back. Most of this traffic makes no sound. The roots and moss take up the scrape of movement, so the town is heard chiefly through water: a tap from the roof, a trickle in a stem, then a long pause.

The opening will not remain this shape. Each fall of water loosens a little clay; each root grows into the room the clay gives up. The inhabitants keep the channels clear and rebuild on the higher patches. Beyond the bank there may be many such places, but from inside this one the dark space between houses is broad, and the fresh, hanging roof feels like the edge of a world.
