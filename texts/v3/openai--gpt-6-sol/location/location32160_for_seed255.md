The floor of the basin is tiled, though in the dark it is easy to mistake it for rock. Each slab bears the small, uneven impressions of a hand that pressed it into its mold. Where a slab has broken, more masonry shows beneath it: a course of narrow bricks, then another course set crosswise. No one working here has reached anything else.

The basin extends beyond the reach of a voice. Low walls divide it into yards and passages, while taller walls stand close together in the distance, their unfinished tops making a ragged line against a faint strip of sky. There are stairs everywhere. Some rise to platforms with no purpose yet; others descend into newly roofed rooms where the air is colder. Fresh mortar rims the joints. Stone chips lie where they fell. Almost nothing has been worn smooth.

Most of the light comes from a few sheltered lamps carried between work sites. A person appears briefly beside one—a shoulder, a pale hand steadying a block—and is gone when it turns a corner. The place is much easier to hear than to see. Mallets strike in uneven groups. Wedges creak under weight. A loaded stone sled grinds along a passage, stops, and begins again after the crew changes its grip. Calls pass from yard to yard, sometimes answered so far away that they seem to come from another part of the night.

Water arrives in small quantities and finds many places to stay. Condensation gathers under the low vaults and drops onto shoulders and tools. After rain, narrow channels carry runoff across the floors, but several end in unfinished masonry. There the water spreads into shallow pockets, reflecting a lamp for an hour or two before it is bailed into jars. The bricks keep the cool. Even the dry passages smell faintly of wet clay and lime.

There is no grass in the joints, no root testing a wall. A small pale animal sometimes slips out from under a loose tile to drink, then vanishes at the first footstep. Its tracks are rarer than the marks of trowels. The chief movement here belongs to the builders and to what they have just built: a wall settling against its braces, water taking a new route, a span being closed one block at a time.

Near the middle of the basin, a stair ends at an open edge. Below it lies another floor already crowded with walls, lamps, people, and stacks of material awaiting their turn. Someone has marked out the next row of rooms in chalk. From above, the lines look slight and temporary; by morning, they may be high enough to cast shadows.
