The ground is so nearly level that the water seems to have no reason to move. It lies in broad, shallow sheets between patches of exposed earth, reflecting a warm, pale sky. Here and there, a current can be found by watching a floating husk for a long time: it slips past a stone, turns in a hollow, and continues toward a distant band of deeper water.

This is an old plain. Its few rises are low and rounded, with no fresh edges left on them. Dark stone shows through a thin skin of clay, polished where water has crossed it for ages. The shallow channels do not look cut so much as worn into the ground’s final shape. After rain they widen quietly; in drier weeks they retreat and leave a fine pale crust along their banks.

Vegetation keeps mostly to those banks. Small clumps of sedge stand far apart, their roots holding little islands of firmer mud. A few salt-tolerant shrubs grow on the higher patches, each with open ground around it. From a distance the plain looks almost bare. Up close it is busy: crabs work beneath the wet edges, thin fish flick through water scarcely deep enough to hide them, and long-legged birds spread out across the pools. Flies rise when a bird lands, then settle again.

The air is warm and damp enough for the stones to stay dark well into the day. There is little shade. Light reaches under the water and reveals every ripple of clay, every track pressed into it overnight. Far across the flats, the pools merge into a bright, wavering strip; beyond that, more low ground appears, much the same as this.

For such an open place, it is loud. Frogs call from the scattered sedges with a steady, rough insistence. Insects make a finer sound above them. Birds answer across the water, and at intervals many wings lift at once, beating hard before the flock settles a little farther on. Underneath it all is the small continuous noise of water finding its way through the plain. Nothing here changes quickly except the animals’ attention. The water advances and withdraws by inches, and the old ground receives it.
