The plain is made of shallow, fitted basins. Their rims rise no higher than a boot, but they run beyond the horizon in overlapping courses of pale concrete, glazed brick, and dark steel. Here and there a service wall lifts above them, carrying pipes as thick as tunnels. There is no exposed earth beneath the paving, and no hill that was not assembled. Even the distant haze catches on structures: rows of gantries so numerous and evenly spaced that they give the horizon a fine, combed edge.

Water is everywhere without gathering into a lake. It beads on the warm metal, stands in the joints between tiles, and seeps down the faces of walls from condensation channels overhead. The air has the close, clean smell of heated stone after rain, with a trace of oil. In the ordinary daylight, every surface looks newly laid. Corners remain sharp. Rust has been painted over or does not take hold; the constant wet has yet to round a single edge.

The basins are crowded with animals. Long-legged, pale creatures stand in the inch-deep water, close enough that their shadows touch. Smaller ones occupy the dry lips of the channels or cling in layers to the undersides of the pipe racks. Some have fur, some shells, some skin almost the color of the concrete. None browse: there is nothing green here. At intervals along the plain, recessed slots dispense soft blocks of feed, and narrow runs of fresh water cross the older, mineral-tasting film. The animals know these places so well that they seldom hurry toward them.

For all that life, the plain is quiet. A claw ticks against a rim; far off, a valve shuts with a dull knock. Behind the walls, pumps maintain a low pressure that is felt more readily through the soles than heard. No wind crosses the basins with enough force to trouble the water. Their reflections hold the gantries nearly complete.

The works look recent, yet they give no sign of approaching completion. Pipes deliver the same measured trickle; overflow disappears through the same grates. The animals shift, feed, and settle again, leaving the vast arrangement much as it was. From a high service bridge, their countless bodies make a dense, faintly mottled surface over the manufactured plain, and beyond them the basins continue at the same low height into the light.
