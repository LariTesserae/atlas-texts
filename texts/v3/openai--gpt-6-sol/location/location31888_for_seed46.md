High on a mountain that has not quite finished becoming stone, there is a hollow no wider than a few paces. Its sides are fresh black mineral, smooth where they have parted and sharp where they have not. Nothing here has been rounded by rain. The floor rises a fraction each year as new material presses up from below, leaving thin seams that look like cracks until they open and show bright, uncooled faces.

The hollow is dry. Heat gathers in it under an ordinary white sun, yet green things grow from the seams: broad, waxy fronds with roots of pale wire. Each plant is extravagant in itself, crowded with folded leaves, but the plants stand far apart. Between them lies bare stone, hot enough to blur the air. There are no insects on the leaves, no birds above them, no tracks in the powder gathered at their bases.

At intervals a seam begins to work. One side advances, stops, advances again; grains of metal sort themselves into parallel lines. A low hum comes through the rock, followed by three small clicks. No machine can be found. The ridge seems to possess the motions of a factory before it has acquired either walls or purpose. The fronds turn their undersides toward a seam when it starts, and for a while their veins shine copper-red.

From inside the hollow, the opposite rim sometimes appears farther away than it can be. A leaf may cast its shadow across that distance without stretching. Once the hum ceases, the rim is close again, but a new dark edge remains on the floor where the shadow ended. These changes take so long that stillness is the usual impression. Sitting there, you hear little beyond the faint tension in the opening stone, and feel the heat of a small place making room for itself.
