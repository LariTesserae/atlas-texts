The town stands on a high, hot shelf of stone, with enough distance between its buildings to see the far rim of the plateau from almost every street. Its towers are low and broad, their pale roofs tilted toward the sun. Between them lie empty paved courts, mineral flats, and long channels of water. Nothing grows in the cracks. There are no trees to cast shade; shade is something the town has had to build.

Water is everywhere, though seldom deep. It beads on the undersides of pipes, seeps from old retaining walls, and travels in narrow gutters polished smooth by years of flow. In the morning the stone is wet even where no channel reaches it. Heat rises through the plateau while cooler air crosses its height, and moisture gathers on every exposed piece of metal. By noon the roofs are almost too bright to look at. The gutters remain cool enough to touch.

The oldest structures are made of the plateau itself: terraces cut into rust-colored rock, their steps rounded by runoff. Newer works stand on steel legs above them. Pumps lift water to settling tanks; covered conduits carry it across the open ground to workshops and distant cisterns. The machines are large, but their motion is difficult to hear. A wheel turns behind a wall. A valve closes with a small click. Most of the sound in the streets comes from droplets falling, one at a time, into water already waiting below.

Life gathers there too. Little translucent animals crowd the shallow channels, gripping the stone against the current. They graze on dark films left by organisms too small to distinguish from a stain. In the wider basins, pale, jointed swimmers turn slowly beneath the surface. Long-legged hunters stand along the rims and pick them out with quick, precise movements. Farther from the water, the courts appear vacant; then something small darts from the shadow of a pipe to the shadow of a stair.

The town leaves its gutters uncovered where it can. Water lost to the open air returns on the cool faces of the tanks, or arrives later as a brief hard rain. After a shower, every terrace shines, and the animals spread into places that will be dry again by evening. Then the bright stone takes back its heat. The swimmers retreat to the channels, the hunters follow, and across the wide spaces between buildings the only visible movement is water making its way downhill.
