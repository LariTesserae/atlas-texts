The ground is still being made. Along the floor of this buried valley, pale ridges rise a hand’s breadth in an hour, split, and lift again. They are not stone. They are the backs of enormous roots, pressing through beds of warm powder and carrying the valley upward with them. In places the powder has not yet fallen from the new surfaces. It hangs in the air whenever something moves.

The valley runs beyond sight beneath a high ceiling of porous rock. Light passes through the rock in a broad, even glow; only the deeper hollows are dark. From a distance there seems to be room to walk. Up close, almost every gap is occupied: by thin upright stalks, by leaves as stiff as fired clay, by cords of root suspended between one rising ridge and another. Some of the leaves are translucent amber. Others are black and felted, and collect the drifting mineral dust until they look like parts of the cave.

Nothing here is damp. A broken stem gives off a sharp, hot scent, and a bead of resin forms at the cut. The plants draw gas through their hollow walls and pass heavy oils from root to root. The warmth helps those oils move. When a stalk cools in the shadow of a ridge, its joints lock; when the ridge shifts and light reaches it again, it slowly bends free.

The animals seldom wait for anything to move slowly. Small six-legged grazers run in bands along the root-tops, shaving away fresh growth with a sound like files on wood. Broad, soft-bodied creatures force themselves through the narrow spaces below them. There are flyers, too, though their wings are short: they launch from the higher stalks, strike the leaves, and launch again. The noise is nearly continuous. Scraping and wingbeats fill the valley, but the loudest reports come from seed cases bursting overhead. Each crack sends the grazers racing; each rush startles more flyers into the air.

Near one wall stands a little enclosure of stacked stone, low enough to step over. Its occupants, if they are still here, have kept a patch of floor clear with hand-cut blades. A few blades lie inside, their edges dark with resin. Fresh root tips have entered between the stones. The enclosure may last another day before it has to be rebuilt farther uphill.

Toward the middle of the valley, a broad new root begins to rise. Dust pours off it. Stalks lean aside, then snap against their neighbors; animals scatter through the gaps, and the sound swells until even the bursting seed cases are lost in it. For a moment there is open ground along the root’s pale crest. Already the first small shoots are pushing through.
