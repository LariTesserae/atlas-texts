Below the factory floor, between two insulated walls, there is a space just wide enough for a person to turn sideways. It is not a passage. It has no door at either end, and the workers who service it must lift a panel above and lower themselves in. From there, the space seems to continue by inches: a recess behind a pipe, a gap under a cable tray, a narrow shelf where the walls fail to meet.

Everything here has been installed. The floor is perforated steel; the walls are enamelled plates set over older plates, with strips of black gasket showing at their edges. Bundles of cable, steam lines, and thin copper tubes crowd the space so closely that a sleeve catches wherever an arm reaches. Yet it is very cold. The heat carried through the pipes belongs to other rooms. In here, moisture condenses on the undersides of clamps and gathers into clear beads that hold for hours before dropping through the floor.

Nothing has had time to wear smooth. The place is new enough that the bolt heads still have sharp corners, but a shallow route is beginning to appear in it. Each drop lands in nearly the same place. A pale mark has formed on one plate, and a line of rust runs from it to the nearest screw. Beneath the grating, where nobody intended water to go, the same falling drops are slowly sorting a pinch of welding grit by size. It is a tiny beginning, measured in stains and displaced grains.

A few green threads grow along the wet edge of a lamp bracket. They are the only vegetation visible. No insects move among them; when the lamp warms, they simply darken with water. The light itself is weak and amber, partly blocked by a hanging loop of cable, so that the threads and the beads of condensation appear only when seen from one angle.

The space is mostly quiet despite all that passes through it. A valve gives a small knock every few minutes. Somewhere beyond the wall, a motor starts; its vibration makes the cables touch, then separate, too gently to hear. At intervals, cold air comes through a seam and the hanging drops tremble. The motor stops, the air stills, and the drops begin to grow again. Anyone waiting here long enough can learn which one will fall next.
