The plateau is crowded all the way to the horizon. Pale stone rises in close, blunt ribs, with hardly enough space between them for a body to pass. From above, the ridges might look like the teeth of a vast, worn comb. On the ground they hide one another: each narrow passage opens onto three more, each filled with brightness reflected from wet limestone.

Water is everywhere, though there is no river to follow. It beads on the rock, hangs beneath overhangs, and runs in thin sheets down faces polished by a very long passage of time. Deep in the packed stone, unseen channels carry it under pressure. Where they meet the open air, the water drops into narrow shafts. The sound is immense. It has no single source or rhythm, only a continuous white roar that can be felt through the soles of the feet. A falling pebble makes no audible difference.

The air is cool and damp, but the light is almost severe. Sun reaches the high ground without obstruction and scatters between the pale walls. Every hollow has a bright lip and a dark, shining throat. There is no moss in the cracks, no grass on the shelves, no stain of roots working into the rock. Silt gathers in sheltered pockets and stays bare. Once in a long while a small dark bird crosses the plateau, quickly and high up; nothing here invites it to land.

The ribs are rounded, the shafts deep, the water clear. Nothing looks new, yet nothing looks abandoned. The falls keep their places, and the stone has settled into their noise. Walking through it, one gradually stops listening for a change in the roar and begins to hear how completely it fills the spaces between the rocks.
