The district occupies a shallow hollow no wider than a few streets. Its edges are easy to find: a brick retaining wall on one side, the blind backs of warehouses on the other, and at each end a low bridge under which the same dark water passes. Nothing here is quite level. Pavements settle toward old drains; doorsteps have been raised with successive courses of stone; the rails of a narrow service line dip into puddles and come out again.

The pump house still works. Behind its long, dim windows, flywheels turn at a pace slow enough to follow with the eye. Each revolution ends in a heavy knock that travels through the brickwork and up through the soles of your shoes. Water falls from overflow lips into covered channels, runs through pipes under the streets, and returns to the open at a square pool beside the works. The noise is constant but not uniform: a low engine thrum, the slap of falling water, a loose sheet of metal ticking whenever the pressure changes. People who live here can tell which valve has been opened without looking.

The air stays warm and close after rain. Moisture beads on glazed tiles and gathers along the undersides of stairways. Ferns fill the gaps between iron steps; moss rounds the lettering on the old foundry walls. Plants have taken the quieter machinery too. Roots hold a disused gear housing full of silt, and broad leaves rise from a cart abandoned beneath a downpipe. The working paths remain clear, though only just. There is room to pass between the walls and the growth if you turn your shoulders.

Stone corners have worn smooth where water has crossed them for decades. The brick beneath each overflow is darker than the brick around it, with a shallow groove worn into the mortar. Repairs follow these marks rather than covering them: a newer pipe bends around an old patch of green, and a handrail ends short of a place where a wall has long been weeping. The district seems settled into its leaks.

No birds come to the pool, and nothing stirs in it but the discharge from the pipes. After the evening shift, when voices and cart wheels have gone, the engines make the whole hollow sound occupied. Their knock travels from wall to wall while the water rises, almost imperceptibly, over the bottom step of the pool.
