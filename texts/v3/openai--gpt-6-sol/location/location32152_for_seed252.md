Just below the surface is a cavity scarcely larger than a room. Its ceiling split recently; the edges of the break are still sharp, with chips of pale stone lying where they fell. Heat rises through the floor. Water comes the other way, seeping down the walls in slow threads, gathering in shallow hollows, then vanishing with a soft hiss wherever it reaches the hottest rock.

The place is unexpectedly bright. Daylight enters through a narrow crack overhead and spreads across the wet, white mineral faces. A field directed from far above guides more of it through the opening than the crack should admit. There is nothing here to touch or see of that technology: no lamp, cable, or worked surface. The stone remains freshly broken stone. In the steady light, every bead of water throws a small, hard reflection, and the darker pools show the ceiling in pieces.

Almost nothing grows. Silica crusts the walls in thin, unfinished rims; reddish grains collect where the water pauses. A single pale springtail may cross a damp seam and disappear beneath a flake of rock. Most of the movement belongs to the cavity itself. Condensation swells under the ceiling, drops, and begins its short journey again. A film of water advances across the floor, retreats, and leaves a bright new edge of mineral behind.

Between drops it is quiet enough to hear the faint ticking of hot stone cooling near the opening. The air smells of wet dust and iron and feels warmer with every breath. There is room to stand still, but little room to step: a small, bare pocket under a vast surface, still finding its shape.
