High on the mountain, a hollow no wider than a small grove lies between two folds of stone. The slopes above it are bare enough to catch the wind, but almost none of that wind reaches down here. The rock has worn into shallow shelves and thumb-sized basins, all rounded edges and old seams. Water seeps from those seams without ever gathering into a stream. It films the stone, darkens the soil, and holds in the cups of leaves. The hollow has had this shape for so long that roots follow every contour without seeming to strain against it.

There is little light. A narrow opening overhead admits a grey suggestion of the sky, but the crowded branches and fern fronds take most of it. Moss grows thick on the upright faces of rock; pale fungi occupy the spaces beneath it. Wet leaves touch other wet leaves. Even the few patches of open ground are packed with stems, smooth pebbles, and the fine roots of plants growing somewhere else. The air is cool and close, with the smell of stone kept damp for years.

At first the hollow seems empty because almost nothing calls. Then the animals resolve from the vegetation: moths pressed flat under fronds; small frogs whose backs resemble the pebbles beside them; beetles filling the cracks in fallen bark. In the dark water, many narrow bodies rest among the roots. Above them, birds sit so close along a branch that their feathers meet, each with its head tucked down. There are more creatures here than the space seems able to hold, but each has its place, and little reason to announce it. The sound is a continuous, very faint rubbing of leaves, breath, and water against stone.

On one shelf, a bird keeps an unworked twig beneath its foot. It can use the twig to reach into a crevice, though it has made nothing and left no mark that would outlast the hollow itself. No path leads in; no wall or shelter has been raised. The only arrangements are the old ones: roots holding soil, stone holding water, bodies finding the narrow gaps between both.

Nothing in the hollow appears to be arriving or departing. The seep does not swell, the pools do not shrink, and the darkness never quite lifts. It is not lifeless stillness. It is a place so thoroughly occupied, and so long settled, that even its small movements leave the whole unchanged.
