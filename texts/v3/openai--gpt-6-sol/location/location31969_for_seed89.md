High on the mountain, where the air outside is thin and bright enough to hurt the eyes, there is a crack in the summit no wider than a grove. It drops between two walls of dark stone. At its bottom, the sky is a pale seam, visible only if you lean back and look for it. Direct sun reaches the floor for a few minutes in late summer; the rest of the year the place keeps its own dimness.

The walls have been here long enough to lose every fresh edge. Water has worn shallow cups into them and left pebbles lodged in smooth, improbable shelves. Yet there is no stream below. Rain arrives in bursts, runs down the stone, gathers in hollows, and is gone. More often the moisture comes from the rock itself: warm breath rising through narrow joints and condensing higher up. Drops fall at irregular intervals, each one briefly audible beneath the larger noise.

The warmth draws life into the crack. Bats fill the upper recesses so closely that the stone seems furred. Small, dark birds cling nearer the opening; at dusk they trade places with the bats in a confusion of wings. On the floor, crickets and pale beetles work through fallen droppings while spiders occupy the dry ledges above them. A few tiny frogs wait beside the deepest rain cups, motionless until an insect passes. Everything hunts or feeds within inches of something else. The colony ranges far beyond this narrow place for food, but returns to it for shelter, warmth, and water.

Plants have less room here. A tuft of grass survives where dust has collected near the rim. Far below it, a few mats of moss green briefly after rain and then draw back to a dull black. Most surfaces remain bare: polished stone, grit, wings, eyes.

Even when no animal calls, the crack is loud. Claws tick against rock; birds rasp at one another over perches; a thousand wings make a dry, continuous stirring. Now and then a pebble strikes a lower ledge and sets off several sharp cries. A bird may pin a hard-shelled insect against the stone and break it with a loose chip, leaving the chip where it falls. Nothing here is built.

Changes come slowly except at dusk and during rain. A hollow deepens by a grain’s width. A roost shifts to a warmer seam. A moss patch holds on for another season. The mountain is vast around this little darkness, but from its floor the whole living world seems pressed into the space between two walls.
