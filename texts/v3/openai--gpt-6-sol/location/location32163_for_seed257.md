The upper court lies high above the drowned lowlands, though there is no air from which to see its height. Beyond its parapet, the water falls away into a darkness that gives back no bottom. Inside, the court is scarcely thirty paces across: a floor of fitted slabs, low walls, and rows of squat pillars set so close that a swimmer must turn sideways between them. Every surface was carried here and placed by hand. The joints vary by a finger’s width; some stones bear the shallow dents of the tools that dressed them.

Warm water enters through holes in the floor. It rises almost too slowly to feel, carrying a faint mineral taste, then spills over the parapet. The flow has worked on the court for longer than its makers could have watched. Pillar corners are round as worn teeth. Mortar has receded into the seams, leaving the stones held partly by their weight and partly by the careful way they were laid.

There is no green here. In the little light that reaches the court, pale bacterial skins show on the warmest stones. Tiny shelled animals graze them. Crabs shelter in the widened joints; soft-bodied hunters wait behind the pillars; narrow fish hang nearly still above the outflow, facing its slow current. The place is crowded, but little moves at once. A shell taps stone, a claw rasps, and then the water seems quiet again.

Silt settles daily in the tool marks and is lifted out only a grain at a time. The animals have made paths through it without changing the court’s design. From the edge, looking down, it is hard to tell where the masonry ends: lower courses emerge briefly from the dark, each one older and smoother than the last, before the height beneath them disappears.
