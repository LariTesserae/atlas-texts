They are the Khyrenai, and they are not still. They are *settled*.

To an outsider’s eye, they might seem slow, their movements economical, their voices hushed to match the mist. But this is not lethargy; it is economy of being. They are creatures of the equilibrium, their every gesture a response to a world that does not change. Their purpose is not to shape the valley—that is already done—but to *read* it, and to *keep* it reading true.

**Form & Presence**
Their builds are lean and tight, with a density that speaks of generations who eat what the basin provides without surplus. Their skin is cool and damp, often bearing the faint, permanent lace of moss-stains or the pearlescent trail of a slow-condensing droplet. Hair is thick and often dark, woven with living vines that are themselves in a state of perfect, unchanging growth—a lock might be threaded with a creeping ivy whose leaves never yellow. They wear clothes of woven moss-fiber, layered bark-cloth, and cured grazer-hide, all in muted greens, greys, and the black of basalt. Adornment is functional: a carved basalt pin holds a shawl in place; a braid of phosphorescent fungus caps glows softly in the night-terraces, a fixed point of light. Their eyes are large, accustomed to the diffuse light, and their gaze is patient, unhurried, taking in the whole pattern of a thing—a wall’s stone-lay, a vine’s tension, a fellow’s posture—before a blink.

**Mind & Custom**
Their time is not measured, but *patterned*. The plink of water is not a clock; it is the valley’s heartbeat, and they know its rhythms in the way a taut string knows the tension of the bow. History is not a story of change, but a catalog of *placements*. A elder will point to a particular boulder and say, “This is where the First Breath fell,” not as metaphor, but as fact—the boulder has always been there, therefore it was always *placed*. Memory is less about event and more about exact position and relation.

Their language is sparse and concrete, rich in words for moss-variegation, stone-fracture, vine-tension, and the soft vocalizations of the grazing herds. Abstract concepts like “hope,” “regret,” or “ambition” do not exist. Instead, they have words for *right-fit* (when a stone sits perfectly in a wall), *clear-pattern* (when a garden is in harmonious balance), and *stasis-breath* (that subtle Wrongness when something is out of place or decaying). Their greatest moral failing is not “sin,” but *waste*—the act of letting something become unmoored from its perfect, eternal state.

**Life & Labor**
There are no “jobs,” only *maintenances*. Each person, from childhood, is apprenticed to a specific, lifelong task whose goal is perpetual, unchanging correctness:

*   **The Stone-Singers:** Those who listen to the mountain’s sub-audible hum through their bones, and know which basalt slab can be split without a fracture-line error. Their work is a kind of conversation with still stone.
*   **The Vine-Weavers:** They tend the immortal bridges, re-plaiting only what has worn from a thousand perfect, unchanging crossings. Their knots are not for strength, but for *rightness of form*.
*   **The Moss-Tenders:** They walk the vast fields, reading the subtle shifts in emerald hue that indicate a hidden spring’s exact location. They harvest only the fat-headed ferns, and when a stem is cut, the severed end is pressed whole into the soil as an offering of return.
*   **The Pattern-Keepers (their “leaders”):** Not rulers, but living archives of placement. They remember the precise alignment of every stone in every home, the order of every vine on every bridge, the genealogy of every grazer herd. Their decrees are always the same: “The south cistern is full. The west wall’s third course is true. The herd-mother’s left foreleg is scarred from the old, still landslide.”
*   **The Still-Watchers:** The most revered, and most quiet. They sit in the deepest, oldest parts of the valley, in trance-like observation, feeling for the smallest tremor in the stasis. If they report a “stasis-breath”—a patch of moss turning grey, a water-pool that ripples when it should not—the entire community mobilizes for a ritual of correction: the flawed element is removed with infinite care and placed in the High Cistern, where all flawed things are submerged until they either re-integrate or remain forever still, a museum of wrongness.

*A child does not play; they practice.* A game might be “Find the Perfect Stone,” where one searches the scree for a piece that fits exactly into a specific niche in a wall. A ritual is the “Harvest-Sing,” where the Vine-Weavers and Moss-Tenders move through the terraces, singing in low, sustained tones that are said to “soothe the valley’s breath,” while pruning only what is needed and placing the cuttings whole on the stone altars.

**Ecology of the Human**
They do not see themselves as separate from the stasis. They are another layer of it. The grazing herds ignore them utterly. The silent predators watch them, but the Khyrenai move with such deliberate, non-threatening pattern that attacks are unknown. A Khyrenai who dies is not buried. Their body is laid in a *Placement-Crypt*—a natural cavern lined with basalt shelves. There, they desiccate into a perfect, seated statue, hands in their lap, eyes closed. They become part of the valley’s fixed pattern, a watchful presence in the stone. After a generation, their name is removed from the Pattern-Keepers’ memory-catalog, and they simply *are*, another still form in the still landscape.

**The Unthinkable**
They have no word for “future.” The concept of “tomorrow” is meaningless. Plans are not made; *maintenance schedules* are followed. The idea of leaving the valley is not a temptation; the impassable peaks are simply “the valley’s true edge.” To go beyond would be to enter a place of *change*, which is their definition of chaos, of un-breathed air.

Their deepest fear is not death, but *Breaking the Pattern*. To spill water and not retrieve every drop. To let a tool’s edge wear smooth from use instead of re-forging it in the same fire. To speak too loudly, or too fast, creating a “sound-gradient” in the quiet. Such acts are believed to gnaw at the mountain’s held breath. The wrongdoer is not punished, but *corrected*. They are made to sit in silence with a Pattern-Keeper, looking at a single, perfect stone, until their own inner pattern realigns with the valley’s.

---

So they live: not as masters of a frozen land, but as its most conscious feature. Their minds are not empty; they are crammed with exactitude—the angle of a sunbeam on a particular morning (which never changes), the number of vines on the Seventh Bridge (always 1,417), the taste of the water from the North Seep (always like wet flint and nothing else). They are a people without longing, without “again,” without “better.” They have only *now*, which is also *always*. Their happiness is the profound, breast-deep contentment of a stone that has found its perfect, eternal socket. They are the valley’s quiet, thinking moss—not growing, not fading, just *being*, in a perfect, endless, pearl-lit moment.
