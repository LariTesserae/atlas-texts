The first thing you learn about the Mycelial Foundry’s inhabitants is that they do not see themselves as living in a city. To them, the vaulted chamber is not a built space, but a body they are part of, as much as the fungal pillars or the pulsing root-arteries are. They call themselves the Klik, for the sound that anchors all their communication: a rapid, subsonic click that vibrates through the spongy root-floor, through the resin of their homes, through the exoskeletons of every other creature in the grove, humming in your molars long after you hear it fade.

The Klik are six-legged, tannish-gray, their segmented bodies flecked with bioluminescent cyan from the symbiotic velvet fungus they cultivate in specialized nodules along their thorax and the joints of their manipulator arms. They have no eyes as you know them: their primary sensory organs are the two multifaceted clusters on the front of their head, which pick up the faint pulses of the bio-electric veins and the heat signatures of geothermal vents, paired with long, twitching antennae that taste pheromones in the thick, humid air. Their speech is a layered thing: the subsonic clicks carry complex meaning—graft schedules, steam pressure warnings, stories of the city’s first builders—while the pheromones they release from glands at the base of their antennae carry emotional context, memory, and the scent of the speaker’s home fungal tree.

They are not the only inhabitants, but they are the nervous system of the grove. Their caste system is not rigid, but bent to the city’s needs: the largest, thickest-shelled Klik are the PumpTenders, who work the great copper piston engines that regulate geothermal steam, their reinforced forelimbs turning valve wheels big as carriage wheels, cooling pouches of slushy moss slung over their shoulders to cope with the vent heat. The smallest, quickest Klik are the Tenders, scuttling up and down the grafted root-towers with sonic stimulant canisters and nutrient gels, coaxing new bridge-arteries to grow into shape, their thin manipulator arms dexterous enough to thread bark insulation around live electrical veins without shocking themselves.

The Klik’s greatest partners are the draught beasts they call Root-Grazers: chitinous, multi-limbed creatures the size of locomotives, their carapaces mottled brown and gray to match the fungal pillars. They spend most of their days grazing on the soft, outer mycelial layers of the petrified trunks, their waste a nutrient slurry pumped into the river to feed the opalescent algal blooms. Each Grazer bonds with a single small cadre of Tenders, who know their individual grunts and snorts: one knows when her Grazer’s left forelimb harness is rubbing raw, another knows his Grazer craves sweet fermented fruit pulp on the third cycle of the day. When a Grazer dies of old age, the Klik carve a small, rough statue of it from the petrified trunk it spent its life grazing, and set it in the nearest plaza as a marker of the work it did.

Below the Klik’s structured labor, the rest of the grove’s fauna moves in a chaotic, symbiotic hum. The shelled, grazing Mossbacks, their carapaces crusted with living green moss, trundle through the under-canopy between the great pillars, their beaks shearing overgrown lianas and feeder roots that encroach on the open plazas, excreting natural fungicide from their pores to stop invasive mold from taking hold. The Veilwings, leathery-winged scouts with semi-translucent wings streaked with bioluminescent gold, patrol the upper vault, their wingtip patches flashing violet to signal low steam vent pressure, red to warn of burrowing grub infestations in the deep root layer, bright gold when the nutrient levels in the river are optimal. They roost in the high, lichen-speckled ceiling during the violet night cycle, their calls soft, whistling chirps that drift down through the haze.

Deep in the root-soil, the blind, burrowing Grubs do the work no other creature can: they aerate the dense, wet earth so the vascular arteries do not clog, and carry spores of the slow-growing crystalline vine that breaks down inert metal waste into usable plant nutrients. The Klik leave rusted gears, broken pump parts, and other non-biological debris in designated dumping zones for the Grubs to process, and even direct the Grubs to dig new root-conduits when they need to expand the city’s power grid, luring them with pheromone markers that smell of fermenting fruit.

The apex of the grove’s food chain are the Shriekers: large, furred predators with needle-sharp teeth and calls that sound like tearing metal, which patrol the thick, dark groves no Klik dares enter. They are not monsters, but wardens: they keep Grub populations from exploding and eating through the root pillars, and the Klik leave small offerings of dried fungal fruit at the edge of the deep groves for them, a payment for the protection they provide. When a Klik dies of old age or injury, their body is carried to the edge of the deep grove for the Shriekers to take, so their remains do not rot and spread disease through the settled parts of the city.

The city’s rhythm is governed by its thrumming, living infrastructure. The bio-electric veins that run between the fungal pillars pulse in time with the geothermal pumps, a low hum you feel in your teeth even when you sleep. The steam pumps hiss and clank in a steady, four-beat rhythm that the Klik have woven into their music: they carve drums from hollow fungal trunks, tap them in time with the pumps, and weave the clicks of their speech into melody that rises and falls with the veins’ pulse. When the day cycle brings its warm, nutrient-rich drizzle, the Klik tilt the resin balconies of their homes to catch the mist, funneling it into personal reservoirs, while the Mossbacks lift their carapaces to let the rain wash the dust from their shells. When the night cycle shifts the ceiling’s bioluminescence from gold to violet, Klik activity slows: the pumps are throttled back, the Veilwings roost, and the Klik retreat to their habitation pods—grown-to-shape chambers fused into the flanks of the crystalline trees, lined with soft fungal down, each holding a small reservoir of fermented fruit sap and a patch of glowing resin flowers the inhabitant has cultivated themselves.

They have myths of the "upper world," the place the warm updraft that carries new visitors comes from, but no Klik has ever traveled high enough to reach the ceiling’s lichen patches, and Veilwings that venture too far never return, or come back too disoriented to speak of what they saw. They believe the faint, ghost-like shapes that sometimes form in the steam from the vents are the spirits of the first Klik, who taught them to graft the first bridge arteries and coax the first Root-Grazers to work the steam pumps. When they see a shape that looks like an ancestor, they stop what they are doing, click a soft, greeting thrum, and leave a small offering of fermented sap at the base of the nearest pillar.

If you stay long enough, a Tender will take you under their wing, show you how to apply nutrient gel to a new graft, let you feed a Grazer a piece of sweet fungal fruit, take you to the high vault to watch the Veilwings roost as the ceiling shifts to violet. The Klik will offer you a woven fiber cloak to keep off the mist, and a small pouch of the fizzy fermented sap that tastes like ripe plums and hot oil. They will not ask you where you come from. To them, the upper world is just another part of the grove’s mystery, as much a part of the city’s song as the thrum of the veins and the click of their speech.
