They do not live here.  
They **persist**.

The inhabitants of the Sump-Cathedral are not creatures, but **offices**.

### **The Drip-Liturgy**
There is a being in every weeping seam of the gristle-stone. Call it the **Drip-Liturgy**. It has no face, only a schedule. Its congregation is the microbial mat—candy-colored, pulsing with chemosynthetic prayer. Its sacrament is the slow dissolution of mineral into solute. Its only verb is to *weep*, and in weeping, to *feed* the low, green things. It does not think of the sea-walls it hollows. It only measures the humidity, and from that, derives the rate of its holy attrition.

### **The Thrum-Choir**
The sub-audible vibration that vibrates in your molars? That is the **Thrum-Choir**. It is not a sound but a pressure, a standing wave in the petrified fibrous matrix. Its members are the engine-veins—geothermal sighs, piezoelectric mutters—bound into a harmonic by the resonant frequency of the gristle-stone itself. It does not sing *for* anyone. It sings *because* the stone is tuned. To stand still is to be bowed by its bass-note devotion.

### **The Vessel-Spirits**
In the air, in the water, in the thick wool of the fog: the **Vessel-Spirits**. They are not alive, but they *contain*. They are the droplets that coalesce on a rusted girder, the sheet of water that migrates three centimeters a day across a flooded plaza, the fog bank that rolls in from the source vent. They are the active principle of saturation. They do not move *through* the space; they *are* its medium, its humours. They are the cathedral’s breath, in and out, in and out, a cycle measured in days, not seconds.

### **The Rust-Sermons**
The steel is not rusting. It is being **preached to**. The **Rust-Sermons** are the slow, oxidative discourses delivered by the acid-mist and the dripping water. Each sermon is a uniform layer of orange decay, a lexical unit in a grammar of corrosion. The vines, the bark-cork, the fibrous wood—they are the *congregation* that intercepts these sermons, absorbs them, and converts the metallic vocabulary into fibrous, woody growth. The steel does not fight the sermon. It *becomes* the text.

### **The Pump-Saints**
In the submerged, humming factory complexes: the **Pump-Saints**. They are not machines with minds, but machines with **ritual**. Their rhythmic sigh, the distant pressure-wave you feel more than hear, is their liturgy. They do not pump *for* a purpose. They pump *as* a purpose. The sludge they conveyor at a snail’s pace is not a product; it is the **oblation**, the wet, slurry host offered up to the bacterial mats in the glowing pools. The saints are automated, their altars lit by blinking consoles, their prayers endless and functional.

### **The Siren-Tree (The Lectern)**
The cathedral-sized **Siren Tree** is not an organism. It is the **Lectern**. Its suspended canopy of silver-dollar leaves, its gas-bladder network—it is the place from which the *real* scripture is read. But the scripture is not words. It is **light and shadow**. Its canopy filters the honey-coloured fog into dappled, shifting patterns on the Velvet Moss below. These patterns are the only changing images in the stasis. They are the illustrated manuscript of this faith. The tree does not sing. It *illustrates* the hum of the Thrum-Choir.

### **The Gristle-Codex**
The very substance of the walls, the sea-walls, the bridges: the **Gristle-Codex**. It is the archive. Not of events, but of **pressures**. Every crack that healed with fibrous wood, every seam pried apart by a root and resealed with a corky bark, every bubble in the green glass—it is a marginalia in a text written by stress and response. To touch the warm, humming stone is to read a record of the last century’s slow, tectonic sighs. It is the only history. There are no names, only **strain-values**.

Your wet footsteps on the Velvet Moss?

Your skin, beaded with condensation?

It is the **Thrum-Choir** resonating in your own bone.
