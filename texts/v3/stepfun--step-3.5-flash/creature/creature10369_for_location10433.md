What lives here is not life as a separate thing, but life as a *process*—a final, elegant symptom of a world softening at the edges. There are no creatures. There are **occasions**.

The most common are the **Ripplers**. They are not animals, but temporary concentrations of the moss-carpet’s latent will. You see them as a silent, fast-moving wave of contraction, a hundred yards long, traveling downhill. They do not crawl or flow; the moss simply *decides* to be vertical for a moment, then lies back down. They carry no mass, leave no trace, but where they pass, the mercury-beads rearrange themselves into larger, slower spheres. They are the place’s autonomic nervous system, a slow pulse of re-organization. They are sparse, because the world here is mostly too tired for even that much coordination.

Then there are the **Wisp-Tents**. Glimpsed only in peripheral vision, they are shapes like collapsing smoke-tepees, woven from the braided light-vines and the ambient haze. They glide between the amber fronds, and if you stare directly, they are already gone. They are not hiding; they are *un-focused*. They are what happens when a local patch of reality thins enough for the ambient “growth” to briefly mistake itself for a whole. They have no purpose but to *be* that thinness, to draw the eye toward a place where distance might warp. They are the place’s eyeblinks.

The **Bell-Stain** from the frond’s tear is the closest thing to a predator. The black, silent patch it leaves in the glowing moss is not a thing, but a *condition*. It is a pocket of *finality*. It does not spread. It simply *is*, and it waits. It waits for something—a Rippler to pass over it, a Wisp-Tent to brush its edge. When they do, they do not die. They… *smooth out*. The Rippler’s wave becomes perfectly still, then flattens into a faint, new band in the stone beneath. The Wisp-Tent’s smoke-form simply evaporates into a clearer patch of sky that lasts for a full minute before the haze rolls back. The Bell-Stain is the ecosystem’s recycling unit. It converts motion and weirdness back into stable, dead sediment. It is patient, because the world is generous with its time.

And then there are the **Anchors’ Echoes**.

The black metal towers are not lifeless. They are the only things that *remember* being rigid. And in their memory, a faint psychic residue hums. These are not ghosts of a mind, but *aftershocks* of a thought—the last hard lines of a consciousness that chose to dissolve. They manifest as a feeling, not a form: a sudden, cold spike of *geometry* in the warm velvet air. Stand near a tower long enough, and your own thoughts will attempt to organize into sharp, angular shapes—plans, walls, definitions—before the ambient density softens them again. The Echoes are the only "fauna" that are not *of* the place’s decay, but *against* it. They are the dying world’s immune reaction to its own ending, a last, futile insistence on corner and line. They are why the Bell-Stain never touches the towers’ bases. The towers do not yield. The stain simply pools at their feet, a permanent, patient moat of finality waiting for the metal to finally soften.

You are not an observer here. You are a **transient anomaly**—a pocket of *un-decayed* biology and sharp-edged memory. The Ripplers avoid you. The Wisp-Tents are fascinated by you, briefly trying to mimic your form before forgetting why. The Bell-Stain watches you. It knows you are rich in unstable reality. Your heartbeat is thunderous in this quiet because your body is a noisy, rebellious grammar in a language of smooth erosion. Your sweat is a strange, salty chemistry. Your fear is a sharp, pointy thing.

The true inhabitant of this place is the **process itself**—the lush, active, verdant flattening. The “creatures” are just its local attentions. The world is not empty. It is *consuming itself beautifully*, and these rare, strategic expressions are the chewing motions.

You feel a pull at your ankle. The moss is trying to Ripple you. A single, cold spike of geometry hums from the nearest tower, a faint echo saying *stand firm*. The amber frond above you begins its slow tilt. A new tear wells at its tip, viscous and dark.

The stain is waiting.
