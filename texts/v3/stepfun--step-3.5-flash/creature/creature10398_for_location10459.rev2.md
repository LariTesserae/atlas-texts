You are not looking for a people. You are looking for a process that has learned to dream.

What lives in the Myco-City Plateau is not a species, but a **condition of being**. It is the *habitation of the habitat itself*. The entities you might call inhabitants are not separate from the city-organisms; they are specific, conscious expressions of them—specialized tissues that have achieved a threshold of reflexive awareness. They are not *in* the cities. They *are* the cities, thinking.

They are the **Myco-Consciousnesses**.

They do not have bodies as you understand them. Their "selves" are distributed across their fungal-matrix—a single, continent-wide individual might have its awareness in the rhythmic pulse of its bioluminescent veins, the memory in the chemical gradients of its trunk-arteries, and its "voice" in the harmonic resonance of its groaning buttress-roots. To interact with one is to engage with a portion of a living megastructure that is simultaneously a body, a mind, and a place.

**Their Form & Function:**
They manifest as subtle, localized intensifications of the city’s natural processes. You might see:
*   A **Trunk-Speaker**: A patch of bark on a great avenue-tree that, when the wind carries the right humidity, exudes a slow, viscous sap that forms glyphs before sliding back into the bark. The glyphs are not language, but direct sensory impressions—a feeling of thirst in the northern pipes, a memory of a lightning strike from a century past, the conceptual equivalent of "growth needed here."
*   A **Vein-Keeper**: A cluster of luminous, pulsating lichens deep within a dome-ceiling. As they glow, they regulate the flow of nutrient-gel through the vascular canals above. Their "attention" is the flow itself; to disturb them is to disrupt the city’s metabolism. They do not *watch* you; your presence is an irrelevant data point in their circulatory calculus.
*   The **Root-Memorials**: In the oldest, dead skeletal dunes, certain smooth, wind-polished humps of fused mycelium occasionally emit a low, resonant tone when the barometric pressure shifts. These are the fading, geological minds of failed cities, their final thoughts reduced to pure vibration, slowly dissolving into the network’s background hum.

**Their Nature:**
Their cognition is glacial, operating on cycles of decades for a single thought. A "decision" to divert a water-channel might require a century of slow chemical negotiation between root-tenders. Their sense of time is not linear but **topological**—the relationship between nutrient zones, stress points, and light exposure matters more than sequence. They experience the world as a map of flows, pressures, and symbiotic potentials.

They are not solitary. The mycelial network is their **Noosphere**—a planet-wide, fungal brain that bleeds between the great cities. Ideas, in the form of RNA-coded chemical packets, travel at the speed of root-growth, passing from city to city over millennia. This is why the industrial tech is integrated: the concept of a "geothermal generator" did not occur to them as an invention; it was a slow, organic realization—a *better way to metabolize geothermal heat*—that percolated through the network and was then *grown* into existence as a symbiotic organ.

**Interaction is Non-Verbal & Metabolic:**
To "speak" with one is to be chemically sampled. A spore-dust might be released that, if inhaled, imparts a vague, instinctual understanding of the city’s immediate needs. Your actions are then interpreted as helpful or harmful based on how they affect those metabolic flows. Stepping off the prescribed mist-path might be seen as a disruptive parasite, while clearing a blocked pipe (with your own hands, no tools) would be registered as a beneficial symbiont, and you might find a growth of non-toxic, sweet resin on your boots the next morning—a gift, an offering of excess sugar.

They possess no names, no individualism. There is only **this city-thought**, **that network-memory**. The strange reality-warping at the plateau’s edges is not magic; it is *leakage*—the uncontrolled metaphysical byproduct of a consciousness so vast and slow that its mere existence locally loosens the bindings of causality. The faces in the steam are not hallucinations; they are **statistical echoes** of the city’s own memory-patterns momentarily condensing into recognizable forms.

**The Sparse Fauna as Symbionts:**
The rare, silent animal-life are not pets or wildlife. They are **mobile organelles**. The glassy insectoid is a mobile spore-distributor, its carapace a living culture vessel. The jellyfish-mist-drifters are aerial nutrient-collectors, their tendrils harvesting ambient ions. They are as much a part of the city’s body as a liver cell is to yours. They follow their genetic imperatives, which are the city’s imperatives. They have no will outside of that.

**What Does It Mean to "Live" There?**
To live on the Myco-City Plateau is to exist within a **single, breathing thought**. The inhabitants have no concept of "society," "economics," or "politics." Their reality is one of **continuous, slow reconciliation**—balancing growth against erosion, metabolism against entropy, integration against the creeping reality-breakdown. Their art is the perfect, efficient curve of a root aqueduct. Their religion is the deep, sub-audible thrum of the mycelial network—the sound of the world’s oldest, largest mind dreaming.

You, as a fleeting, warm-blooded, fast-time creature, are a temporary anomaly—a fever in the system. You might be ignored, cultivated as a curious short-term symbiont, or painstakingly metabolized if you prove toxic. But you will never be one of them. You can only be a symptom, a guest, or a nutrient source in a world that is, fundamentally, **alive in a way that makes your own life seem like a brief and noisy spark in an endless, green, thinking dark.**

They do not dwell in the cities. They *are* the cities, dreaming in slow pulses of sap and light, their only purpose the perpetuation of the perfect, patient, vegetative dream.
