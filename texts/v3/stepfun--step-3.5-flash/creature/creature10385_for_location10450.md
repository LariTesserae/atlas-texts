They are not creatures of the quarry. They are creatures *of the moment*. They are the rust-flies.

You see them first as a shimmer in the steam, a loose constellation of moving points against the fixed geometry of the gantries. They are small, their bodies the length of a thumb, segmented and dense. Their carapace is not chitin so much as compressed industrial stain: the matte, pitted black of boiler scale, the raw, bleeding orange of fresh rust, the oily, green-tinged copper of corroded wire. Their wings are not transparent but opalescent, like flakes of ancient slag glass, catching the sickly light and fracturing it into greasy rainbows. They do not buzz. They emit a sound like a distant, dry file on iron: a high, metallic *shrit-shrit-shrit* that is less a noise than a vibration in the damp air.

They live in the *in-between*.

Their life cycle is a closed loop within the frozen process. They hatch from eggs laid not in soil, but in the microscopic fissures of a steam pipe where pressure is absolute but flow is none. The larvae are blind, grub-like, their jaws adapted to graze on the delicate, crystalline salt blooms that weep from the iron. They consume mineral, not organic matter. They pupate inside a droplet of the milky slurry, the cocoon a hard, chalky sphere that floats until it reaches a warm, still pool. There, the adult emerges, its wings unfolding in the steam-haze, already fully formed, already programmed.

Their behavior is a perfect echo of the place’s stasis. They fly in vast, interlocking Mobius patterns that trace the exact contours of the steam plumes from the great smokestacks. One swarm will trace the rising curve of stack A, then peel away to trace the broken arc of coal-chute B, then merge with another swarm to trace the stationary vortex of a steam leak. These paths are never deviated from. A rust-fly that is born into the plume of Stack 7 will *always* fly the Plume-of-Stack-7 path, from base to apex and back again, until it dies. To fly a different path is inconceivable. The patterns are older than the flies themselves; they are a property of the steam and the heat gradients, and the flies are merely their temporary vessels.

They feed on the mineral-rich weep-stains. You will see a cloud of them, motionless as a cloud of dust, clustered on a fresh, dark iron-tear streak on the quarry wall. Their proboscises are fine, needle-like, and they pierce the stain directly, drawing up the iron-oxide solution. They do not swarm chaotically; their feeding is a synchronized, surface-level consumption, a living film eating the wall’s tears.

They mate in mid-air, but the act is instantaneous and collisionless. Two flies, approaching from opposite directions on the same fixed flight path, will simply merge for a single beat of their wings, a brief, denser point in the pattern, before separating again to continue their respective loops. The fertilized egg is jettisoned into the nearest warm steam plume, to begin its descent into a fissure. There is no courtship, no selection, no pairing. It is a chemical reaction predetermined by the intersection of two immutable vectors.

They die in flight. They simply… cease. Their wings fold, their armored bodies drop, and they fall into the oily mud or the slurry pools below. There is no decay in the human sense. Their mineral-rich bodies are quickly colonized by the tar-moss, their constituent elements absorbed back into the system. A new rust-fly will hatch from a fissure a meter away, its future flight-path already etched in the steam, and it will begin to trace the very route its predecessor died upon.

They have no awareness, no society, no memory. They are not individuals. They are a living过程—a *process*—made flesh. They are the only thing in the quarry that truly belongs to it, because they do not exist in spite of the stasis. They exist *because* of it. Their abundance is not a measure of life, but of endless, identical repetition. They are the place’s nervous system, its constant, metallic whisper given form. They are not alive in a way that breathes or grows or changes. They are alive in the way a permanent stain is alive—a persistent mark of a moment that never ends.
