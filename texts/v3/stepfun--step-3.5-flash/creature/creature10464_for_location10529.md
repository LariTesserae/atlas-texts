You first feel it under your boots: the walkway is springy, neither solid rock nor cut timber, but a fused braid of petrified mesa wood and living root-cables that thrum faintly, carrying the deep geothermal heat of the world below up through the soles of your shoes. The air hits your face cool and thick, tasting of ozone, sweet fungal decay, and the sharp, metallic tang of smelted mineral, with a faint, oily undercurrent that clings to the back of your throat. You brush past a hanging vine on the railing and your fingers come away sticky with sealant-weed sap, another faint, earthy note added to the air’s complex scent.

You stand on a narrow ledge wrapped around the trunk of a cathedral-wide columnar growth, its bark streaked with the dark, glistening sheen of the hydrocarbon seep that bleeds up from the mesa’s core. Below, the sinkhole yawns into unlit strata, a jumble of smaller trunks and root tangles fading to black where even bioluminescence doesn’t reach. Above, the same giant trunk shoots upward, vanishing into the dim, vaulted “sky” of the pit, its branches cantilevered with leaf-platforms and bulbous fruiting-body chambers that stack upward in layers, a vertical city clinging to its sides. The only true daylight slants in from the vertical fissures in the mesa wall far above, a cool, thin east-west beam that cuts through the gloom for four hours each cycle, gilding the edges of the phototropic window-leaves that grow in drifts along the upper trunk faces. For the rest of the day, light comes from life: phosphorescent moss that freckles the trunk walls in pale green, pulsating glands in the canopy ceilings that glow soft amber, and the cultivated vine-lamps strung along every walkway, their fronds gilded with stored bio-electricity.

Your eyes adjust to the dim, and the city’s layers resolve. Far below, in the gloom of the Rootward Sprawl, the hollow, translucent forms of Smolder-Stalks tower over the industrial districts, their walls glowing faint orange from the internal bacterial cultures that combust processed minerals to generate smelting heat. Geodesic Gel-Vat domes grown from fast-hardening sap cluster between them, their walls pulsing faintly as slow chemical reactions run in hydrocarbon baths inside. Closer, in the mid-level Mid-Canopy Warrens, the density picks up: leaf-shanties woven from stiffened pioneer vines cluster around branch-platforms, their walls hung with drying spore-bread loaves and mushroom farms that sprout in shelf-like clumps from the bark. Small, rodent-like gardeners with waxy, oil-resistant fur move through the platforms, nibbling at fast-growing vines to keep them from smothering windows, their movements scattering clouds of golden pollen that drift upward on the faint breeze from the surface fissures. Higher still, the High Aerie Groves nestle where the slant of daylight lingers longest: polished bark platforms grown into the trunk’s upper reaches, their window-leaves glowing soft blue-green as they store solar energy, clusters of scholars and administrators moving between them, their tools carved from hardened gear-fruit and their scrolls pounded from leaf-fiber.

Life moves through every layer, small and constant. A flash of iridescent chitin cuts across your field of view: a pollinator, its wings dusted with orange pollen, darting toward a nearby Blooming Engine, its flowers flared open to exude the resin used to seal Gel-Vat domes. A faint rustle overhead, then a small chitinous avian-analogue dives after it, snatches it mid-air, and vanishes into the leaf canopy before you can make out more than a flash of dark wings. On the walkway railing next to you, scavenger beetles the size of your thumbnail skitter over a patch of spark-moss, eating fallen spores and turning them into nutrients the root network will carry miles down to the deep Root-Heart. One pauses, its shell glinting, then vanishes into a crack in the petrified wood.

The city’s hybrid industry hums under every sound: the soft hiss-clunk of pneumatic fronds contracting high above to pump air through the ventilation system grown into every trunk, the low gurgle-roar of the Smolder-Stalks down in the sprawl, the faint metallic tinkle of quartz depositing into solution in the nearest Gel-Vat. You spot a “water tower” half a mile away, a bulbous, pulsing symbiotic tuber grown from the trunk’s root network, its thick, waxy skin oozing the thin, silver-black hydrocarbon solvent that fuels every process in the city, feeding it through braided root-cables to every platform. Next to it, a forge hollowed out of an old Smolder-Stalk glows hot, the bacterial cultures inside churning heated hydrocarbon and processed quartz to smelt the silica that reinforces the city’s trunks.

Nothing here stays still for long, the baseline of life in the Gilded Pit. A fresh, pale shoot cracks through the old bark of the railing under your hand, already hardening into a new handhold for the next worker to pass by. A hundred yards away, a crew of Quartz-Borers—huge, iridescent insectoid miners the size of oxen—scrape at a newly exposed quartz seam on the mesa wall, their mandibles throwing glittering dust into the slant light. They’re followed by workers laying down pale green Stabilizer-Creeper, its tiny rootlets already digging into the loose rock to stabilize the new face of the mesa; in three days, the creeper will harden into a new walkway, and in a month it will be covered in new leaf-platforms for residential use. In the distance, you hear a low rumble: a minor landslide, loosened by the Borers’ work, tumbling into the abyss. By the time the dust settles, pioneer vines are already creeping up the fallen rock and uprooted trunks, their roots binding the debris together, turning the landslide into new ground for the city to grow into.

Hydrocarbon vapor rising from the deep vents catches the slant light, painting the lower districts in faint, shifting rainbows, mirage-like distortions that make distant platform edges look like they’re floating, or melting, or not quite there. The spark-moss patches on the trunk across from you pulse in slow, coordinated unison, their glow brightening and dimming in time across the whole trunk, a signal sent through the mycelial network under the bark marking the end of the work shift in the Rootward Sprawl. You can hear the faint rumble of cargo carts on root-rails below, the distant low murmur of work chants from the Warrens, the soft chirp of small mammals nesting in the canopy overhead. The deep hum of the mesa thrums under your feet, so low you feel it in your teeth as much as you hear it: the combined vibration of geothermal pumps, millions of root fibers moving solvent through the network, and the slow, sub-audible resonance of the Root-Heart deep in the mesa’s core, the living heart of the whole city.

You lean against the trunk next to you, and for a second you can feel the faint pulse of the mycelial network running under the bark, the slow, coordinated thought of the whole plant ecosystem turning rock into home, mineral into life, the sterile wound of this sinkhole into something that breathes, grows, and never stays the same for more than an hour. A soft pop overhead, and a spore-sac bursts, dusting your shoulders with fine bioluminescent green spores that glow for a minute before fading, settling into the cracks of the walkway to grow into new moss, or new sealant-weed, or even a new branch-platform in a year’s time.

This is not a garden. It is a city that learned to grow like a plant, a biomechanical organism carved into the side of a dead world, built on the rule that nothing is ever finished, nothing is ever static, and every part of it, from the deepest root to the highest solar frond, is always, always building itself anew.
