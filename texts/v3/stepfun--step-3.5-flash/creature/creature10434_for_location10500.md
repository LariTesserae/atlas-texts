Their mouthparts shave, their glands secrete a polymer that fuses the shavings back into the slag-glass matrix. They are the immune system, phagocytizing dissonance. When you step, your footprint is an anomaly. For a moment, the Mite-wave recedes from the impression. Then they return, not to erase you, but to *incorporate* the new texture. They will polish the grit from your boot tread until it is as seamless as the rest. You become part of the surface they maintain.

**The Spinners** are the **still points**. Their gelatinous sacs pulse in slow, hydraulic time. They do not catch water; they *mediate* it. The falling torrent hits their tensile carbon-silk nets and is shattered into a fine, ion-rich mist. Their sub-audible thrum is a counter-frequency to the Forge-Worms’ grind, a dampening resonance that prevents the water’s impact from vibrating the delicate crystal formations on the walls. They are the chamber’s **buffer**, converting kinetic force into saturated atmosphere. Their bodies are translucent, and within their gel you can see the slow, Brownian dance of metal ions being sorted—copper here, tin there—a quiet, viscous refinery. They are the only entities that seem to rest, but their rest is a deeper kind of labor.

The hot water that fills these tubes is their waste, their exhalation—a mineral broth that feeds the Mites and alters the pH of the pools. Their loops are absolute, pre-determined. They do not choose a path; they *are* the path. To watch one is to see a single, unwavering line drawn through eternity. Their crystalline salt membranes flake away not from decay, but from hydraulic pressure—a continuous, osmotic secretion. The water that weeps is ancient, having traveled through countless channels, dissolving and depositing, carrying the colloidal memory of every pipe, every tunnel, every reaction it has ever touched. A Weeper’s “face” is a porous formation, a thousand tiny mouths under a single, tensioned membrane. It is not sad. It is *pressurized*.
