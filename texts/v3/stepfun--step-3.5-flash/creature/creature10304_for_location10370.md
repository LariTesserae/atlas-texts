What lives here is not a collection of individuals, but a continuous, distributed metabolism. Life is not a property *of* the basin’s inhabitants; it *is* the process by which the basin sustains its own weird, brilliant, mineral-bound form. The citizens are roles, not persons.

The primary, **established** citizens are the **crustaceans** within the spires. They are the basin’s **cells**, its **gut**, its **skeletal builders**. They do not “inhabit” the spires; they *are* the spires’ internal milieu. Their role is **filtration** and **cementation**. They consume the brine-rich mist, extracting dissolved silicates and salts. Their waste—the phosphorescent paste—is not refuse, but a **structural secretion**. It flows in slow, dark rivulets down the internal channels of the spires, hardening into a porous, glowing concrete that binds the growing crystal. Their **clicking** claws are a byproduct of their chitinous shells, a sound like tiny bones grinding against stone, the sound of the basin’s slow digestion. They are blind because there is no “outside” for them, only the damp, dark filter-bed of the spire’s interior. They are born, grow, molt, and die within decades, their bodies decomposing into a rich slurry that feeds the next generation and the spire’s base. They are **abundant** not as creatures, but as a vital fluid made flesh.

The **turbulent** clouds of quartz-insects are the basin’s **circulatory system**, its **ephemeral nerves**. Their role is **transport** and **deposition**. They drink the condensing brine not for sustenance, but as a vessel. The minerals suspended in the brine are incorporated into their fragile, faceted bodies. They live for days, perhaps weeks, dancing in their silent, shimmering swarms. Their death is their function. When their crystalline forms shatter on the salt-crust, they **seed** it. Each fragment is a nucleation point for new micro-crystals, for new salt-dust. Their brief, chaotic flight patterns are the basin’s **blood-flow**, distributing mineral potential across its surface. Their lack of buzz is crucial; their communication is purely visual, a language of light and refraction, a silent calculus of distribution. They are **weirdness** made manifest: a life-form whose entire purpose is to dissolve itself into the landscape.

Between these two poles—the **dense**, slow, internal labor of the crustaceans, and the **turbulent**, fast, external scattering of the insects—operates a third, subtler class: the **microbial mats** on the salt-crust. They are the basin’s **skin**, its **chemical interface**. Thick, leathery films of extremophile bacteria, they glow faintly in spectra unseen by human eyes. They harvest the chemical energy of the brine’s gradients, metabolising sulfides and alkalis. They are the **negotiation** between solid and fluid, the agents that alter the brine’s composition just enough to make it suitable for the insects. They are the basin’s **memory**, encoding the chemical history of each day’s mist in layered bands within their mats.

**None of these have names.** They have **functions**: *Filter. Carry. Seed. Bind. Remember.* Their “society” is a metabolic cycle, a pre-negotiated set of chemical relationships as fixed and inevitable as the basin’s own erosion. There is no government, no art, no industry. There is only **process**.

**Anthill-energy** is a misnomer. An anthill has purpose *imposed* from outside (the queen’s reproduction). Here, purpose *is* the process. The **bazaar** is not of goods, but of energies: thermal, chemical, luminous. The **storm-state** is not of wind, but of mineral flux.

To stand in the basin is to stand inside a **single, slow-beating organism** whose neurons are swarms of quartz, whose bones are salt-spines, whose gut is the clicking dark, whose skin is microbial, and whose blood is falling brine. The inhabitants are the basin’s distributed consciousness, experienced as clustered verbs: *filtration clicking, crystallisation hushing, deposition shattering, cementation glowing.*

They are not *in* the city. They **are** the city’s ongoing becoming. They are the place itself, seen as a living verb.
