The inhabitants of The Stillings are not residents of a city so much as they are its organs, its geology, its slow blood and slower breath. To list them is to map the anatomy of a place where the boundary between life and construction has dissolved into a single, persistent pulse.

## The Primordial Groves
They do not think, not in any way you would recognize. They *are*. Stretching from the Rootweave below to the highest buttresses of the Spires, each Grove is a networked consciousness of vascular tissue and resin-secreting root, a continent-sized mycelial thought grown stone-hard over epochs. Their consciousness is distributed through every thread of the Rootweave, a dull, thrumming awareness of pressure, nutrient density, and the slow creep of erosion. They built the architecture not from intention, but from necessity—excreting their own skeleton as they grew, their growth-rings becoming the buttresses, their leaves becoming the platforms. They are the city’s masons and its mortar, and they are dying, very slowly, into the Silicates that harvest them.

## The Phosphor-Moss
A lithic breath over every surface. It grows only where the bioluminescence of the Rootweave seeps through the opalescent stone of the Spires—a faint, tired glow that has worked its way through millimeters of fossilized tissue over ten thousand years. It is not green, not really, but the color of old phosphorus, of submerged embers, dusting the Spire walls in a stratum of soft, cold light. It feeds on the minute sugars exuded by the Groves’ stone-skin, and its slow, rhythmic blinking is the only clock the place keeps.

## The Fruit
Occasionally, a gas vesicle will detach from the Rootweave’s net. It rises through the pleromatic fluid like a slow, silver bubble, carrying a seed-nucleus of pure Grove potential. It does not burst until it reaches the ice-ceiling, where the pressure differential shatters its membrane in a silent, radiant bloom, dusting the glacial roof with micro-Organic material that will, over another million years, precipitate back down as new root-tips, beginning the cycle again. To watch one rise is to watch a coin tossed to a god who will not spend it for ten thousand years.

## The Drifters
They are the city’s plankton, its snow. Bell-shaped entities of transparent membrane, pulsing in a rhythm so slow it takes an hour to complete a single contraction. They drift through the Vales between the Spires, trailing filaments finer than silk that filter nutrients from the syrup-fluid. They carry tiny points of cold blue light within their bells, motes of captured condensation that blink in time with the Groves’ faint pulse. They do not feed on the fluid so much as they harvest the light it carries, converting photons into a slow, gelatinous energy. They are beautiful, and they are the first thing the Silicates dissolve.

## The Clingers
The city’s stonemasons of erosion, its only audible voice. Six-limbed, armored in overlapping plates of chitinous polymer, they move with the speed of geological uplift. They graze on the Phosphor-moss and the slow-growing lichens that crust the Spire platforms, their grinding plates producing a constant, low groan that vibrates through the dense fluid and into the bones of anything living. They move in herds that span kilometers, a slow carpet of armored bodies that can take three centuries to cross a single Spire ledge. They do not notice the passage of time. They only notice the taste of the stone underfoot and the pressure of the fluid against their shells, and the endless, slow work of eating the light.

## The Silicates
The apex, the end, the gardeners who prune toward a final stillness. They resemble clusters of raw volcanic glass, fluid and sharp despite their geological appearance, flowing over the Rootweave like mercury. They do not eat; they *absorb structure*, dissolving the organic polymer of the Groves, the shells of dead Clingers, the membranes of dead Drifters, and re-constituting the carbon and silicate into more of themselves. They are the planet’s way of turning life back to stone, of preserving the city once the Groves have finished building it. They are beautiful, terrifying, and perfectly indifferent. When a Silicate flows over a Spire base, it is not an attack—it is a correction, a return to equilibrium, the slow writing of the final chapter of the Stillings’ story.
