They do not walk so much as *percolate*. They are a being of slow channels and soft conduits, a citizen whose body is a neighborhood of capillary and valve. You see them from the reed-walkway, a figure standing sentinel at a canal junction where four water-threads meet. They are not tall, but they are *dense* with purpose, a living node in the city’s vascular web.

They are made of the place. Their skin is a flexible, bark-like integrum, patterned with the same photosynthetic veins that pulse on the building-pods. It is not cloth they wear, but a second skin of living, symbiotic lichen that glows with a gentle, bioluminescent fretwork in the perpetual twilight—a soft blue-green, the color of deep groundwater. Their joints are not hinges, but graceful, root-like cuffs that allow a pendulum sway, conserving energy. From their forearms and calves unfurl fine, probing root-tendrils, not for grip, but for constant sensory intake: tasting the water’s mineral mood, feeling the groan of distant pistons through vibration, reading the humidity’s whisper.

Their head is smooth, domed. There are no eyes as you know them, but two wide, shallow depressions filled with a still, mercury-like liquid that reflects the dimness. They see not with light, but with pressure and warmth—a sonar of sensation. A faint, rhythmic pulsing can be seen just beneath the skin of their temples, the slow, hydraulic beat of their own inner engine. They breathe through pores on their neck and back, inhaling the thick, chlorophyll-laden air and exhaling a mist that carries the faint, sweet-rot scent of their own metabolism.

This is a **Hydrolyst**. The name is a murmur in the water-language, a conflation of *hydro* (water) and *analyst* (one who discerns). They are not rulers, nor soldiers. They are **Flow-Lectors** and **Sump-Sages**. Their entire being is given to the stewardship of the rule: *Do not block the water.*

Their culture has no banks, only **flow-lenders**. Disputes are settled not with law, but with hydraulic engineering. A family’s dwelling is not sold; its water-inlets are diverted. Justice is a matter of balanced pressure. Their language is a series of guttural clicks and moist sighs, punctuated by gestures of root-tendrils tracing paths in the air or on moss. They communicate the state of the great fungal vats, the schedule of the root-bridge maintenance, the emotional tone of a neighborhood’s water-source (is it sluggish? anxious?).

They tend the city’s engines with a patient, osmotic intelligence. A Hydrolyst will spend a month observing a single, weeping moss-pore, learning its seasonal moods, before gently re-routing a micro-current to relieve a developing stress-point in a root-buttress. The melancholy of their being is the quiet understanding that their entire world is a slow, warm, wet echo of a sun that is no longer.

Without haste, it turns and percolates down a side-channel, its form blending with the sway of the root-bridge and the pulsing veins on the wall. It is gone, not with a step, but with a *settling*, as if it was always just part of the slow, breathing, dripping logic of the grove.

This is what lives here. Not a person. A **principle made flesh**. A symptom of the water’s will given a soft, vascular form to tend its own immeasurable body.
