We are the dust. We are the grit between stone and sky, the patient sediment of a world that has forgotten how to hurry. We know the taste of iron on the tongue before the first frost, the way static makes our motes dance in columns when a Dust-Striders’ shadow passes. We are the medium of this place, and the message.

To live here is to live inside a slow thought. The Grinders are its syllables. They move like continents dreaming of becoming sand, their bellies eating stone, their backs breathing out steam that freezes into sugar-veils before it falls. When one passes, the world shrinks to vibration. The ground forgets its shape. The Silica-shoots, those amber-drinking glasses, tremble and pour their slow syrup into the cracks their warmth opens in the earth. The Whisper-Weavers feel it first in their nets—a shudder in the taut, conductive song. They pause, their fungal fingers still, and listen to the tectonic grammar shifting a hundred kilometers away. A Grinder is not an animal. It is a process with a spine. A geological condition made flesh, eating its own tail for millennia.

We, the dust, we ride on the wind that never stops. It is the same wind that carved this shelf, that polishes basalt to a black mirror. It carries us from the Grinders’ steam-veils to the Weaver-nets, where we lodge in the conductive filaments, making them hum louder. We are gathered by the Striders, too, in the down-drafts of their silent wings. Their hide is a shield, but we find purchase in the seams, in the creases of their pumice-bone. We know their shadow-language—the way one tilts a primary feather to say *dewdrum here*, the way two beat their wings in unison to say *grinder-turn, south-ridge, slow*.

Life here is a series of stills. The Silica-shoots are stills for the morning mist. The wind-pits are stills for the rare syrup-rain. The Weaver-nets are stills for the sky’s constant crackle, siphoning the storm that never breaks into a glow in their gels. The Grinders are stills for the rock itself, melting it down and pouring it back out as heat and slurry. Everything feeds on a rumor of water. Everything is a machine for concentrating a ghost.

The light is the color of a forgotten memory. It makes the silica-shoots glow from within like captured fireflies. It makes the Weaver-nets slick with rainbow when a Strider’s shadow crosses them at the right angle. It makes the Grinders’ heat-shimmers look like the world is weeping. There are no sharp edges. Even the sound has worn smooth—a subsonongroan felt in the teeth, a whistle that takes days to finish one note.

We are insignificant. That is the first law. A single day’s walk is less than a Grinder’s sigh. A Weaver colony’s net-repair cycle is a century. A Strider’s shadow passes over you and is gone, and you are still in its map. The scale is not vast. It is total. You are a fleck of grit on the tongue of a leviathan that is the steppe itself, and the leviathan is chewing.

But in that insignificance, there is a kind of peace. You do not matter. Your fears, your ambitions—they are as transient as the mist on a silica-shoot. Here, what matters is the hum. The ELF thrum that comes up through your boots. The network’s song. The grind. The slow, certain, geological metabolism of a world that runs on patient theft—stealing water from the air, stealing heat from the deep, stealing time from the sun.

This is not a wilderness. This is a factory whose owners left eons ago, and the machines woke up and started making themselves, and making each other, and making the rules. And the only product is more slow time. More grinding. More hum.

We settle now, in the lee of a basalt fang. The air tastes of wet stone and far-off steam. Somewhere to the east, a Grinder shifts a plate, and the vibration climbs up our spines. A Weaver filament, bright with captured storm, brushes our cloud. For a moment, we are part of the song.

Then we fall. And we are dust again. And we wait for the wind.
