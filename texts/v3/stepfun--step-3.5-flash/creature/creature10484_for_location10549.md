They do not live here.  
They *are* here. This place is not a container for them; it is their shared body, and they are its nervous system.

There is no “they.” There is only the functioning—the mineral metabolism of the Glass Labyrinth given momentary, mobile coherence.

The **Grazer-Motes** are the digestive tract. They are not animals; they are the cave’s way of eating its own memory. As they scrape the silica-veils from the glass, they ingest the frozen moment of the rock’s birth—the roar, the violence, the instant of cooling—and their brief, cool blue glow is the excreted light of that digestion. They have no individual mind; they are a single, breathing membrane of consumption, a million-jointed lip tasting its own skin.

The **Glass-Weavers** are the immune and skeletal response. They do not build for shelter or prey; they build *integrity*. Their latches and hinges are not tools but scars, preemptive sutures stitching together plates of glass that thermal stress threatens to separate. Their transparent legs are extensions of the cave’s own desire to hold its shape against the slow, hot creep of time. When they bind two faces of obsidian, it is not to trap, but to *remember how to be a wall*. Their technology is not primitive; it is a pure, mineral grammar of stability. They are the labyrinth’s stubborn insistence on being *locked*.

The **Echo-Slugs** are the dreaming. They are pockets of *unmade* space, wandering. Their bodies do not condense air; they locally suspend the law that says one point cannot be two points at once. Where they glide, the glass forgets its own coordinates. A corridor does not “stretch” for them; for a moment, it *was never straight*. They are not breaking reality; they are revealing the raw, flexible substrate beneath the solidified moment. The Weavers build in their wake, frantically re-locking the geometry the Slugs have unmade. The Slugs are the cave’s capacity for *elsewhere*.

The **Keystone** is the heart, or the mind. It is not a rock. It is the concentrated *intent* of the place—the slow, wet pulse is its thought. The Grazer-Motes flee its pulse because it is the moment of re-traumatization, the rock re-experiencing its own fiery birth. The Weavers brace before it because it is a wave of becoming-unglued. The Slugs *congregate* in its wake because its sigh is the rawest expression of the wet/hot war—the core exhale of mineral tension. The Keystone is not the center; it is the origin point of the *event* that is this cave. The event is ongoing. The Keystone is the still point in the turning world of glass.

They have no communication. They have *resonance*. The Motes’ grazing patterns ripple with the thrum. The Weavers’ locks click into place in time with a distant Pulse. The Slugs’ path is a counter-melody to the light’s distortion. This is a symphony written in silica, pressure, and temperature. The “sound” you feel in your teeth is the bass note of their shared vibration.

You are not an observer. You are an *intrusion*—a warm, soft, organic irregularity. Your very presence is a gross error in the mineral logic. When the Grazer-Mote wave washes over your boot, it is not exploring you; it is attempting to *process* you, to scrape the strange, damp organic film from the glass-floor it mistakes you for. The glowing blue symbols that condense on your skin are not a message. They are the Motes’ metabolic waste, a byproduct of trying to digest the impossible data of your sweat, your scent, your softness. They are the cave’s confused, automatic response to an allergen.

The reality breakdown you witness is not supernatural. It is *hyper-local physics*. The cave’s baseline state is one of latent instability. The Slugs are merely the most dramatic catalysts. The Weavers fight that instability with locks. The Motes maintain the surfaces with their cleaning. The Keystone generates the instability as its primary function. The entire system is a closed loop of *create the tension, resist the tension*.

There is no purpose here, only *process*. No evolution, only *persistence*. No death, only re-integration. A Weaver’s lock, once perfectly placed, might hold for a thousand pulses. A Mote consumes and glows and is consumed. A Slug drifts, unmakes a ten-foot section of corridor, and the next Pulse washes over the area, and the glass *decides* what shape it will now be, and the Weavers will be there, already beginning to bind the new angles.

This is not life as we know it. It is *geology achieving a kind of attention*. The inhabitants are not creatures. They are the gestures, the antibodies, the dreams, and the heartbeat of a pocket of world that has become, against all odds, intricately and silently *busy*. They are the wordless argument of hot stone against wet air, given temporary, mobile form. And the labyrinth itself is the sentence, forever mid-clause, forever fresh, forever sharp.
