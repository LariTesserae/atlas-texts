You first feel it before you see, hear, or smell it: the wet, warm weight of it seeping through your clothes, the low thrum in your molars that’s half vibration, half taste of iron on the back of your tongue. You are standing on a drift-platform, lashed together from hollow, lead-lined Pipe-Creeper stems that creak soft and wet under your boots, and sheets of corrugated iron peeled from a Boiler-Tree trunk that is still, after all these years, faintly warm to the touch. The platform bobs gentle on the tea-colored sea, the water lapping at the edges of the iron plating, leaving streaks of black Cinder-Pollen that dry to a dull, permanent gray on the metal.

The air is not air. It is a breathable liquid mist, warm as a bath, thick enough that you can feel it pooling in the hollow of your throat when you breathe. It carries the first sharp hit of scent: wet iron, crushed mint, the sour tang of fermenting fruit that’s been left out in the sun too long. Underneath that, if you breathe deep enough, you catch the sharp bite of ozone, the cloying sweetness of overripe metal, the greasy, nauseating perfume of hot machine oil that never quite fades.

The light is a permanent, submerged twilight, the cloud ceiling too high and too uniform to let any direct sun through. The only illumination comes in pulses and flashes: the slime-lamps caked across the lower flanks of the sharp, freshly hewn hills to your left glow in slow, synchronized rhythms of sickly magenta and bilious green, so bright they cast long, wavering shadows across the water that stretch and shrink as the lamps brighten and dim, like the valley itself is breathing. Then, without warning, an arc-fault spiderwebs across the water between two half-submerged transformer stations fifty yards out: jagged, blue-white forks of electricity that hang in the mist for half a second, bright enough to blind you if you’re looking right at it. The water screams where the arc strikes, superheated to steam in an instant, and a sharp, crackling hum echoes off the hills so loud your ears ring. For that split second of light, you can see all the way across the valley: the skeletal peaks of Gantry-Ghosts jutting out of the water like the ribcage of some drowned leviathan, their joints festooned with orange Rust-Roses that glow faintly in the arc-light, their razor-thin metal petals singing a high, thin discordant note that cuts through the valley’s constant roar. You can see the floating hulk of a Barge-Loft a hundred yards out, its hold full of Rust-Roses that sway in the warm wind, and the distant far wall of the valley, obscured by the shimmering Thermal Curtain that makes the hills beyond look like they’re melting, layered one on top of the other in a dozen out-of-sync copies.

Then the arc fades, and the valley’s sound crashes back in, louder than before, as if the light stole all the noise and dumped it back at once. It is not a collection of sounds. It is a single, physical weight. First the sub-audible groan, felt in your sternum before you hear it, the tectonic creak of the valley’s young, still-settling rock and the immense, hidden pressure of the Water’s source somewhere deep below the valley floor. Layered over that is the industrial choir: the ceaseless, arrhythmic clank-hiss-scream of a hundred broken machines, the deep, guttural thud of the Rhythm-Organ inside the half-drowned Foundry Fathomless behind you, its pipes made of melted-down road signs playing a tune that shifts a little every time you hear it, never quite the same twice. The water boils constant, warm and soft, from a thousand thermal vents and shattered boiler hulls scattered across the valley floor. The tiny, metallic whispers of the vegetation thread through it all: the Boiler-Tree a dozen yards to your right, its trunk of twisted, soot-caked corrugated iron, thousands of copper-green filaments hanging from its branches like chimes made of thin wire, clinking soft against each other as the mist blows through them, dripping slow, tepid condensation that carries a sharp metallic tang that makes your mouth water if you catch a drop on your lip. The Pipe-Creeper coiled around the Boiler-Tree’s base, its hollow lead-lined conduit pulsing slow as the fleshy membrane inside sucks water up, filters the Cinder-Pollen out, squirts the clearer water higher up the trunk to feed the lichen and the small, rust-colored blooms growing from the bark’s cracks.

Then the synch-swell starts, slow at first, then building so fast it makes your teeth ache. A distant clank echoes from a Gantry-Ghost across the water, and three seconds later the cluster of Rust-Roses growing from the girder on the opposite hill vibrate in perfect mimicry of the note. Then a condenser unit on the hill to your left shudders, emits a low, off-key steam whistle that harmonizes, dissonantly, with the groan of the submerged Piston-Pump churning the water thirty yards out. Then arc-faults jump between three transformer stations at once, blue-white light flaring so bright the slime-lamps dim in comparison, the crack-hum of the electricity overlapping with the clank and the whistle and the groan, until the whole valley locks into a single, terrible chord that vibrates the very air, makes the water ripple in concentric circles away from every source of sound, makes the iron plating under your boots hum so hot you have to lift your boots to keep from burning your soles. The slime-lamps pulse bright enough to cast stark, sharp shadows, the plankton in the water flare electric blue in clouds that rise up through the water like nebulae, the Boiler-Tree filaments clink so loud they sound like wind chimes in a hurricane. For ten seconds, the whole valley is singing, and you can feel the resonance in your bones, in the fillings in your teeth, in the water that soaks through your socks, as if the whole place is about to tear itself apart along the seams of its own recursion. Then it fades, as quickly as it built, leaving only the constant boil of the water and the low, steady groan, like the valley is catching its breath before the next one.

You dip a hand over the edge of the platform into the water. It’s lukewarm, thick with silt and Cinder-Pollen that leaves a fine black dust on your skin as soon as it dries. You stir the water with your fingers, and a cloud of electric-blue plankton flares around your hand, bright enough to glow through the tea-colored water, then fades in a few seconds. The water smells of wet iron and mint, and if you hold your hand up, the condensation that beads on your skin is faintly pink, tinted with rust that dissolved out of the hills.

You look toward the shore, where the black, glassy transition matrix meets the water—shelves of solidified something, obsidian or cooled slag or solidified noise, you can’t tell, crusted with stalactites of rust that drip slow into the water, and the velvet-soft carnivorous moss that grows over the shelves, drinking the rust, its surface glowing faintly with the same sickly bioluminescence as the slime-lamps. A little further up the hill, a grove of Oil-Palms grows out of an old spilled barrel of lubricant, their fronds dripping that thick, sweet-smelling fluid that smells like overripe peaches and axle grease. A drop falls from one of the fronds, hits the water, spreads in a rainbow sheen, and the plankton under it flare bright blue for a second before dying, leaving a small black smudge on the surface that dissolves in the current a minute later.

You glance back at the Foundry Fathomless behind you, its cavernous maw half-submerged, the rusted grating over the entrance glowing faint orange with internal fires. The steady thud of the Rhythm-Organ pulses through the hull, the pipes inside shifting tune every few minutes, never quite repeating the same sequence. You can see a flash of movement through the grating, for half a second—a girder shifting, maybe, or a rust stalactite breaking loose and falling into the water, or maybe something else, something you can’t name, something that’s part of the recursive breakdown, part of the valley’s wetware. You blink, and it’s gone, just the orange glow and the thud of the organ.

The Thermal Curtain on the far wall shimmers, and for a second the hills beyond look like they’re made of water too, like the whole valley is dissolving at the edges, folding in on itself. You blink, and the hills are solid again, sharp and fresh-hewn, their slopes covered in the multi-colored slime that pulses in time with the lamps. You know, if you stayed long enough, you’d start to notice the little patterns: the way a clank from a Gantry-Ghost three miles away will make the Rust-Roses on the hill across from you vibrate in the same key, the way water flows up the side of the hill to feed the Boiler-Trees, the way a broken girder will slowly grow a bark that matches its original paint scheme, even if the girder was rusted so smooth you couldn’t tell what color it used to be. You know if you stayed too long, the Cinder-Pollen would settle in your lungs, the rust would start to grow on your boots, your clothes, your skin, the sound would get so deep in your bones you’d forget what silence was, and eventually you’d just be another part of the recursion, another broken machine singing its single discordant note in the valley’s endless, deafening choir.

This is Soggenthorpe. Not a place you visit. A place that happens, a valley-sized engine of wet, warm, broken reality grinding away in the dark, lush with industrial ghosts and ruled by the only law that matters here: everything breaks, and everything breaks in time with everything else.
