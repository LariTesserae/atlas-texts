They are not animals. They are not spirits. They are the Basin’s own quiet pathologies given a moment’s errant limb, a fleeting, localized failure of its total, dreaming cohesion.

**The Salt-Sighs:** The most common. They are not seen, but *sensed* as a sudden, localized drop in air pressure—a brief, wet vacuum that pulls at the ear drums. They move as if through syrup, leaving behind a faint, shrinking crystalline residue on the breathing floor-pads, like salt-tracked footprints that dissolve in seconds. They are the Basin exhaling a forgotten memory of pressure, a sigh from a deep, buried chamber where the salt-polymer once knew the weight of an ocean. To stand in one’s path is to feel your own bones briefly forget their density.

**The Still-Stag:** The deer-shaped patch of deeper quiet. It does not move through the vaults; it *unfolds* from them. A perfect, matte-black absence of the dappled light, shaped with the suggestion of antlers made from braided shadow. It consumes light not as a void, but as a patient digestion. Where it passes, the bioluminescent mycelial filaments gutter and go dormant, plunging a street into a true, absolute dark for a minute, then two, before the slow, biological glow resumes. It is a rent in the Basin’s illuminated thought, a self-canceling idea. It does not hunt. It simply *un-sees* the world, and in doing so, makes a small portion of the world un-see itself.

**The Weaver’s Remorse:** In the groves of engineer-trees, where the subsonic hum is loudest, one may feel a sudden, cold draft smelling of burnt sugar and wet iron. In that draft, for a single heartbeat, the intricate, pneumatic vines are *knotting*. They twist back on themselves in violent, logical impossibilities—a loop that tightens on its own source, a tube that feeds into its own inlet. It is a visual glitch made flesh, a spasm in the circulatory system. Then it resolves, the vines rearranging themselves into their proper, slow-flowing configuration. The sap within them tastes faintly of regret for a second. It is the factory’s dream of inefficiency, of a closed loop that serves no purpose but its own recurrence.

**The Creep’s Echo:** The root-mats are always, slowly, moving. But sometimes, a patch will *remember* a faster motion. A ripple will surge across a breathing floor-pad—not the gentle pulse of capillary action, but a sharp, muscular *thrum* that travels for ten yards before dying. The pads beneath it will yield in sequence, a wave of *shluck-shlock-shluck*, too fast, too synchronized. It’s the fossilized ghost of a panic, a seismic shudder from a time when the Basin’s growth was frantic, not glacial. It leaves no mark, but the air after it vibrates with the *almost-sound* of thousands of roots screaming in unison.

**The Haze-Child:** Born only during the Great Haze, when the leaf-windows weep. In the moment a droplet forms and hangs, refracting the impossible constellations, a figure may coalesce in the humid air. It is humanoid, but woven from the haze itself—cool, moist, and vaguely twinned with your own silhouette. It does not speak. It simply mirrors your posture for a second, then raises a hand to its own face, as if wiping a mirror. When the droplet falls and evaporates, the Haze-Child dissipates with a scent of ozone and cold stone. It is the Basin seeing itself through your eyes for a single, suspended drop of time, and then forgetting you were ever there.

**The Pattern-Back:** The rarest, and the reason for the warning not to stare at the salt-scour on the bark. The etchings are not random. They are the Basin’s chronicle, written in erosion. But sometimes, if you fix your gaze on a complex lattice of lines for too long, the pattern will *complete*. A final, connecting line will draw itself in your mind’s eye, forming a sigil you feel you’ve always known. At that moment, you will hear—not with your ears—the slow, mineral grinding of a thought turning over in a million-year mind. And you will know, with a chilling surety, that the pattern has recognized *you*. Not as a visitor, but as a new, faint, and very strange line in its own endless, self-scrutinizing face. The stare is not hostile. It is *cataloging*. And after, you will find your own memories have a new, faint, crystalline texture at their edges.

They are not inhabitants. They are symptoms. The Branch-Basin is a body so total, so self-contained, that its diseases have taken on the semblance of separate life. To encounter them is to brush against the raw nerve of a world that is more architect than ecosystem, whose dreams are geometric errors, and whose loneliness is expressed not in crying out, but in briefly, terrifyingly, *mis-remembering* its own shape. You walk not among creatures, but among the Basin’s quiet fidgets, its spatial yawns, its memory lapses made briefly visible. And you do not walk *through* them. You walk *within* the lull between one such symptom and the next, and the silence in that lull is the only true voice of the place.
