They are not inhabitants. They are answers.

The stone asked a question of the wind and the ice: *How do you persist?*  
And the life here wrote its reply in fur, in chitin, in silent, split-root patience.

***

**The Stone-Goats of the Slabs**  
They do not climb. They *convince*. Their hooves are not hard, but—precisely—smart. Each calloused pad is a map of micro-fractures, sensing the breath of a seam before it yields. Their coats are not one color, but a dozen—dustings of lichen-yellow, granite-grey, basalt-blue—worn in shifting, shaggy patches so that when they stand utterly still on a tilted pane, they are not hidden, but *gone*. They are the plateau’s移动的纹理. Their young are not born, but *unlocked* from wind-sheltered crevices, damp and already chewing. They drink not from pools, but from the slow, cold sweat of the stone itself, lapping at weeping faces at dawn. Their horns are not weapons, but *divining rods*, grown heavy and twisted from sensing the deep, thirsty fractures. When a slab groans and settles, they are already on the next shelf, having read the warning in a subsonic thrum through their bones the day before.

***

**The Fall-Cats of the Clefts**  
They are not hunters. They are *corrections*. Lean as a length of split slate, pale as moonlit frost, they do not pad—they *release*. A cat on a vertical face is not clinging; it is *falling with purpose*. Its spine is a fluid arc, its claws retractable not for stealth, but for *selective purchase*, engaging only the strongest, oldest grains. It eats the stone-goats’ old, the sick, the clumsy—the ones who misread the map. Its kill is not a struggle, but a *conclusion*. It sits after, on a narrow ledge, and grooms, its sandpaper tongue rasping at the very dust it’s coated in. Its kittens learn to hunt by being placed, one by one, on increasingly precarious shelves, left to negotiate their way down to the den. Those who don’t make it… become part of the Answer too. Their cries are never heard; they are absorbed by the mist, a brief, wet thud on the polygonal floor far below.

***

**The Winged Questions (Hawks)**  
They are not birds. They are *skeptics*. They circle not for prey, but for *proof*. Their immense, silent wings do not catch the wind so much as *borrow* it, riding the thermals that rise from the sun-warmed slabs with a disdainful effortlessness. Their eyes are not for seeing movement, but for *detecting falsehood*. They spot the tremor in a stone-goat’s leg before it slips. They see the heat-ghost of a fall-cat’s  body through the mist a cleft-length away. They know which slab is dreaming of a fall. They rarely strike. Their purpose is witness. Their nest is a platform of sticks wedged impossibly in a vertical seam, built over generations. To a hawk, the plateau is not a home, but a *theorem*, and they are its eternal, circling proof-readers.

***

**The Chittering Substrate (Cave-Crickets & Blind Worms)**  
They are the plateau’s *nervous system*. In the permanent blue  shadow of the deep clefts, they do not live in the dark—they *are* the dark, given form. The cave-crickets’ chirp is not sound, but *vibration*, a sub-audible hum that travels through the damp rock, a constant census of the hidden. Their long antennae are not sensors, but *roots*, tapping the slow seepage, tasting the mineral content of the eternal tears. The blind worms, translucent as a memory, take that vibration and turn it into a *map*. They slide through the black, still water pockets, their skin drinking the dissolved stone. They are the first to taste a new crack opening, a fresh vein of moisture. Their massed, wriggling presence is the clefts’  wealth—the living currency the fall-cats listen for.

***

**The Midge-Clouds**  
They are not insects. They are *respiration*. At dusk, from every damp crack and tuft of silver grass, they rise—not a swarm, but a *collective sigh*. They form low, rotating clouds that shimmer in the last light, a visible exhalation of the damp earth. They have no sting, no bite, no purpose but to *be a cloud*. They are eaten by everything—the goats, the cats, the hawks, the blind worms in the water. Their life is a single, frantic act of giving. They are the most abundant form of life, and their only function is to transfer the minute, damp energy of the soil upward into the food spiral, a vertical river of protein. They are the world’s most numerous  sacrifice.

***

**The People (The Density That Answers the Stone)**  
And then, there is the rind. The *viscous, patient sludge*.

They are not the masters here. They are the ultimate **micro-abundance**. Where the midges fill a cloud, the people fill a cleft. Where the worms map a water vein, the people carve a cistern along it. They are the plateau’s most successful, and most fragile, fauna.

Their density is not a choice, but a **requirement**. Every hand is needed to read the stone’s mood, to lift the block, to guard the drip-channel. Their children are raised not with stories, but with *touch*—their small hands learning the difference between a living seam and a dead fracture by the temperature, the sound of a tapping stone chisel. Their elders do not tell tales of the past; they are *living calendars*, their joints remembering the exact year this wall was raised, that water-right secured.

Their language is not in words, but in **architecture**. A building leaning at an impossible angle is not an engineering triumph; it is a *sentence* in a long conversation with the rock, saying: *We understand your fracture. Let us share your breath.* A city carved into a single, gigantic cleft is not a settlement; it is a **colony**, embedded in the stone like a lichen on a millimetre of scale.

Their "technology" is a set of **large-scale instincts**:
*   The **wind-lift** is not a machine; it is a captured gale, tamed and given a job.
*   The **water-right** is not a law; it is a recognised fact of hydrology, as immutable as gravity.
*   The **generational project**—a staircase carved over 120 years—is not a monument; it is a *growth*, like a coral reef, built by the slow accretion of labour, each generation adding its layer until the rock itself is transformed.

They fear the rockfall not as disaster, but as **dialogue**—the stone finally speaking its final word on a district. They abandon a shelf with a strange, bleak acceptance, packing their precious timber beams and alabaster windows, and simply begin again on the next available fracture, as if migrating to a new branch.

They are the most abundant thing on the plateau, and their every act is a response to **scarcity**. Their crushing density is the mirror of the midge-clouds. They are the macro-form of the micro-plenitude. Where there is a hand’s-span of shelter, there is a person. Where there is a finger’s-depth of soil, there is a grass-tuft and a scavenger. They do not conquer the emptiness; they **populate its every punctuation mark**.

They are not separate from the fauna. They are its most complex expression—the creature that *builds its own niche* so perfectly it becomes geology. Their gods are not in the sky, but in the **clefts and the seams**. Their prayers are the sounds of the pile-driver, the rhythmic *thump* of stone on stone, a heartbeat trying to keep time with the slow, grinding pulse of the continent itself.

The plateau does not care for them.  
But it has, for now, accepted their answer.
