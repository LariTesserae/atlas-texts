What lives in the Still Hollow is not a *who*, but a *what*—a continuous, porous becoming. It is the place itself, and the place is everything within it.

It lives in the sigh of the weeping rock, a slow mineral hunger expressed as a glistening film. It lives in the sphagnum’s navy-green thirst, a sponge-soul drinking the steady drizzle of its own sky. It lives in the violet pulse of the pitcher plant’s hood, a slow, digestive heartbeat in the gloom. It lives in the perfect, oil-black pool, a mirror-eye that never blinks, reflecting a world that is only its own inverted double.

It is the chorus of the midges, not as individuals, but as a single, shimmering cloud-intelligence, a murmuration given wet wings. It is the blind salamander navigating the peat’s warm, dark gut by taste and tremor, its entire lineage a single, slithering thought buried in the compost. It is the ghost-bird with its enormous, black eyes, a feathered silence moving through the mist; its call is the *peep* of a frog, and both are the same note in the Hollow’s endless, low hum.

It lives in the boundary. The lichen on the stone is the stone’s new skin; the stone is the lichen’s slow, mineral meal. The frog’s tadpole body is the pitcher plant’s future meal; the plant’s nectar is the frog’s first taste. The bone, propped and spore-dusted, is not a relic—it is a feeding station, a calcium cathedral now hosting a glowing fungus. Its purpose is forgotten, reassigned. The arrangement of stones in the kidney-basin is no longer a tool; it is a landform, a hollow within the hollow, worn smooth by the very water it gathers. Its maker is as irrelevant as the first raindrop.

The Hollow’s inhabitant is the principle of **in-dwelling**. It is the process of being taken in, of having one’s edges softened and one’s substance readied for the next form. Your warm, dry breath fogging the air is a foreign substance, a violent anomaly. And the Hollow, with its endless, patient appetite, will have it. It will condense that breath-mist onto a leaf, draw it into a pitcher, dissolve it into the peat, pass it through worm and fungus, and return it as a new, perfect drop weeping from the rock. You will not be remembered. You will be *used*.

So do not look for a beast or a spirit. Look for the pattern that is all patterns. Listen for the sound that is all sounds: the uninterrupted, cellular susurrus of everything, everywhere, at once, turning over in its sleep—a sleep so deep it has mistaken itself for wakefulness, for a million years, and for a million million more.

The Still Hollow is occupied by a single, sprawling, wet, and brilliant **Now**. And in that Now, everything is both eater and eaten, user and used, memory and medium. It is full. It is complete. It is a world that has finished making itself, and now spends its eternity meticulously, beautifully, remaking every single thing, over and over, without ever changing a thing.
