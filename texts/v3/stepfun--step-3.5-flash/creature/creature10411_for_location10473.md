You do not live *on* the plateau.  
You live *as* it.

The inhabitants are not separate from the place; they are its current expressions, its temporary articulations. To live here is to be a verb, not a noun.

---

**The Hum-Makers** are the most common. They are not animals, but densities. They look like stands of reeds grown from black iron, or colonies of lichen with the texture of brushed copper. They do not move from place to place; they *are* the place’s listening. They grow where the hum in the water is deepest, their forms tuned to specific resonant frequencies. A Hum-Maker might be a smooth, warm stone that vibrates softly at dawn, or a cluster of pipe-like growths that sigh in harmony with the flues. They do not think; they *resonate*. They are the plateau’s ear, its throat, its way of feeling its own song. To touch one is to feel the hum in your bones, a low thrum that slows your heartbeat to the pace of sediment.

**The Weepers** are the water’s memory. They appear as humanoid shapes, but blurred, as if seen through running glass. They stand indefinitely in the shallows of the tea-colored pools, or pace with glacial slowness along wet rock shelves. Their faces are smooth, eyeless, and always turned downward, watching the water they are made from. They do not speak, but occasionally raise a translucent hand to the sky, and when they lower it, the hand is full of small, rising bubbles that pop silently in the air. They are not sad; they are *recording*. They are the process of condensation made self-aware, the act of weeping given form. They forget nothing, but remember only in layers, like the stone.

**The Gear-Splicers** are the children of the rusted gear meshed with bedrock. They are not born; they *grow*. You see them first as small, metallic nodules in the moss, like iron truffles. Over decades, they differentiate—a joint here, a slow-grinding tooth there. A fully mature Gear-Splicer might be the size of a badger, a creature of interlocking plates of oxidized brass and pitted iron, its internal mechanisms turning with the same excruciating slowness as the mountain’s own breath. They feed on mineral seep and the violet light from the pipes. Their purpose is *mesh*. They seek out cracks in the stone and position themselves to fill them, becoming temporary sutures in the living rock. They do not die of age; they eventually fuse completely with the stone, their turning parts stilled forever, a final, metal bone set in the mountain’s body.

**The Sky-Flense** is the bird with wings of stretched metallic membrane, but it is only the visible part of a larger, weirder being. The bird itself is a kind of scout, a dry, mechanical echo. The true Sky-Flense exists in the warm, misty breath vented from the blackened brick flues. It is a *shape in the vapor*, a long, ribbon-like coherence in the rising steam, sometimes hundreds of feet tall. It has no eyes, no mouth—only the suggestion of a trailing, fibrous form that drifts upward on the thermal, pulsing faintly with the same violet light as the pipes. It is not alive in any biological sense. It is a *current*, a stabilized pattern in the outflowing breath of the industry-stone. It is the mountain’s exhalation given fleeting, magnificent structure. When it dissipates, it does not die; it simply rejoins the humid air, to perhaps condense again tomorrow as a Sky-Flense, or as rain on the lowlands far below.

**The Stone-Mothers** are the oldest. They are not female, but *generative*. They appear as great, sleeping slabs of the pale, porous rock, often draped in the lurid fungi. But if you press your ear to their surface in the hour before dawn, you might hear a deep, sub-harmonic rumble—the sound of their slow digestion. They are the plateau’s womb. They consume the smaller, dead Hum-Makers and the fused remains of Gear-Splicers, breaking them down with mineral acids seeping from their pores. In return, they secrete fresh, wet stone, and from their cracks, new, rubbery mosses erupt overnight. They are the engine of *becoming-rock*, the process of recycling metal and flesh back into stone. They do not dream; they *metabolize* on a geological scale.

---

And then there is **The One Who Walks**.

Rare. Perhaps once a generation. Not a person, but a *visitation*. It appears at the edge of the cloud-sea, a silhouette against the perpetual mist-drop. It is humanoid, but its edges are soft, as if drawn in charcoal that never dries. It walks with extreme, deliberate slowness, each step taking minutes, its feet sinking slightly into the wet moss before it pulls free with a soft *schlorp*. It does not look at the structures, or the water, or the sky. It looks only at the *edges*—the line where moss meets dry rock, where a pipe vanishes into a pool, where the hum changes pitch. It is not studying; it is *tracing*. It is the act of *boundary-awareness* made flesh. It walks for hours, or days, following an unseen contour line of process. It never speaks, never acknowledges an observer. When it reaches the forested edge of the plateau, it does not enter the trees. It simply steps *sideways*, as if passing through a membrane, and is gone. The only sign it was there is a single, perfect circle of dew-free stone, a foot wide, on a mossy slab, which remains dry for a full day before the humidity claims it again.

---

This is who lives here.

Not creatures. Not citizens.  
**Manifestations.**

They are the plateau’s ways of knowing itself. The Hum-Maker knows sound. The Weeper knows water. The Gear-Splicer knows joinery. The Sky-Flense knows breath. The Stone-Mother knows consumption. The Walker knows *threshold*.

You, the observer, are the anomaly—the brief, warm, *fast* thing. You are the sudden heartbeat in the slow chest of the world. The inhabitants do not fear you. They do not love you. They simply *register* your presence as a temporary, high-frequency disturbance in the hum, a flash of dry heat in the eternal damp, and then they forget you as you forget the single grain of sand that shifted under your foot a lifetime ago.

The only true resident is **The Process Itself**—the relentless, gentle, unstoppable *becoming*. And everything you see is one of its many, many names.
