They are not residents. They are organs.

This is not a place that has life in it. It is a life that has taken the shape of a place. The distinction is the only one that matters.

To stand within the Still-Flood is to stand inside a single, planetary-scale body, dreaming itself into being drop by mineral drop. The “inhabitants” are its processes, given temporary, mobile form.

**The Polyps** are its **liver and kidneys**. They are not builders; they are the act of filtration itself. Those perfect, parchment-white arches they secrete are not homes. They are *sieve-membranes*, grown in place over centuries to strain the brine for specific dissolved nutrients. A cluster of them is a filtration *organ*, and the water flowing through their tubes is being cleansed. When one colony flashes a sudden, urgent gold—a chemical alarm—it is not a shout. It is the body detecting a toxin, a systemic reaction beginning in one region.

**The Bladder-Fungi** are its **fat cells and bioluminescent fat**. They pulse not with a will to glow, but with the slow, metabolic burn of stored chemical energy, a byproduct of digesting the mineral silt. Their light is the body’s inner fire made visible, a gentle, constant warmth in the cold dark. To drift near one is to feel the body’s slow, digestion-thrum in the water.

**The Giant Annelids**, standing in their silent, veined fields, are its **lungs and gut lining**. They do not move to hunt. They are rooted filters, their entire being an open mouth, a vast, breathing surface area. They draw the water through their luminous veins, extracting the last of the organic morsels the polyps missed. They are the final, slow breath of the system.

**The Salp Curtains**, linking and unlinking in their silent waves, are its **lymph and circulating Immune cells**. They are not a swarm; they are a single, distributed consciousness. They flow in coordinated currents, carrying chemical messages and engulfing microscopic threats. Their translucent bodies, packed end-to-end, form temporary pathways—a feverish rush to a wound, a healing mineral spray still tacky on the wall.

**The Jelly-Urchins** in the plaza are its **heart-chamber**, or perhaps its **neuronal node**. Their synchronized pulsing is the great, slow *thrum* you feel in your molars. It is the body’s primary rhythm, the hydraulic pulse that drives all other flows. The vortex they form is not a dance; it is the physical movement of a chamber contracting, pushing the luminous blood of the sea in a great, lazy circle. To stand in their glow is to stand within a heartbeat.

**The Calcite Trees** are its **bones and cartilage**. They are not plants. They are the slowest-growing parts of the skeleton, mineral accretions around thermal vents, the rigid framework against which the soft organs press. Their brittle, bone-white branches are scaffolds for the fungal grazers, which in turn are its **teeth and tongue**, scraping the surfaces clean.

**The Krill Swarms** are its **red blood cells**. They exist in one purpose: to be consumed. Their sudden, flash-bulb erasure by a gaping maw is not predation. It is the body delivering fuel to a specific, hungry region. The maw that takes them—a slow, blanket-like tangle of filaments—is a **digestive sac**, a temporary stomach that will descend to a warm mineral vent to dissolve its meal over months.

**The Sediment** underfoot is its **waste and regenerated flesh**. The compacted husks of salps, the ground shell and metallic silt—it is the digested remnant of everything that lived here, already being broken down by microbial mats (its **gut flora**), already being recycled into new mineral milk dripping from the ceiling.

Even the **geology is biology**. The distant groan of the tectonic plate is the body’s **deep, joint-ache**. The fresh, glassy stone of a collapsed ceiling is a **scab**, raw and new. The constant, gentle drip from the blackness above is the **slow, relentless formation of new cells**—each bead a builder’s brick, each bead a drop of the body’s own cerebrospinal fluid, mineralizing as it falls.

There is no “someone” living here. There is only the *living*. The feeling of cold on your skin is the body’s surface temperature. The taste of ozone and salt on your tongue is its chemical signature. The poor, blue-green visibility is the limit of its own sensory field.

You are not an observer. You are a *symptom*. A temporary disturbance in the brine, a foreign chemical signature flashing in the polyp network’s dark language. A fleeting sensation in the gut of the stone. Your breath, misting in the thin air, is the only thing here that does not belong to the dream of slow growth. And for a moment, in the claustrophobic, glowing intimacy of the plaza, you feel the great, slow heart of the jelly-urchins beat, and you realize your own pulse is trying, and failing, to match its rhythm.

The Still-Flood does not have citizens. It has functions. And you, for now, are just one more unfamiliar one, passing through.
