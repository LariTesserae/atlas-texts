They are not individuals. They are the valley's motions made manifest, its processes given form. To call them creatures is to miss the point; they are living verbs.

The most numerous are the **Sifters**. They are the size of a large dog, low-slung and broad, with six jointed legs ending in fine, suction-cupped digits. Their carapaces are a mosaic of grafted lichen and symbiotic fungus, rendering them nearly invisible against the root-mats and fern-slicks. They move with a constant, rhythmic scraping, their broad, feathery mouths parted to comb the perpetual drift of pollen, spore, and shed vegetable motes from the air and surface-film. What they ingest is not for themselves alone. Their guts are fermentation chambers, and their waste—a warm, nutrient-rich paste—is excreted precisely onto the shallow, crystal-lined channels, supplementing the mineral flow. They are the valley's atmospheric filters and its fertilizer distributors, a silent, rolling tide of consumption and return.

Then there are the **Still-Lights**. They are not animals in any familiar sense. They are slow-growing, gelatinous clusters, often mistaken for strange fungi or mineral accretions. They adhere to the damp bark of the grey columns or hang from the understory mat like solidified dew. Their core is a symbiotic colony of bioluminescent bacteria. They do not move, but they *pulse*: a soft, greenish-gold light that rises and falls in slow, synchronized waves across the entire basin, mirroring the rhythm of the steam vents. They are the valley's nervous system, its distributed sense of time and geothermal heartbeat. The fauna do not sleep in the conventional way; they enter states of torpor that align with the Still-Lights' dimmest phases.

The **Grazer-Swarms** are the only thing that approaches sound. They are not one creature, but a temporary confluence of millions of tiny, six-legged herbivores—some the size of a thumb, others no larger than a rice grain. When a particular patch of the understory mat reaches a critical stage of fungal bloom or parasitic leaf-unfolding, a chemical signal is released. In response, the Swarm congeals from the green stillness, a flowing river of segmented bodies that consumes the patch in a day, leaving only a clean, damp scar. Then they disperse, each tiny body carrying a seed of the next dominant plant in its gut, to be deposited elsewhere in a pellet of concentrated fertilizer. They are the valley's surgeons and its gardeners, executing rapid, precise culls that maintain the board-wide equilibrium.

And then there are the **Attendants**. They are the largest visible presences, sleek and otter-like, with dense, water-slick fur and long, probing snouts. They glide through the blackwater lakes or root-tangle streams with serene purpose. They are the caretakers of the geothermic system. They use their snouts to clear minor blockages in the steam vents with delicate, practiced motions, and their bodies (which can tolerate the highest sulphurous temperatures) are often seen resting in the warmer outflow pools, their movements regulating the pressure and flow of the vents. Their waste, rich in bacteria that metabolize heavy minerals, is deposited at the base of the vents, maintaining the integrity of the stone pipes.

You do not see a hierarchy. You see a metabolism. The Sifters process the sky. The Grazer-Swarms tend the floor. The Attendants service the heart. The Still-Lights time it all. The overwhelming fauna are not a crowd; they are a circulation. Their watching is not awareness, but a function—the passive state of a cell, absorbing the chemical milieu.

They are not *in* the valley. They *are* the valley's active face. When a grey-barked column falls, it is not an event of death for them, but a scheduling change. The Swarm will come for the parasites on its leaves. The Sifters will scour the air where its shadow was. The Attendants will reroute a minor seep around its roots. The Still-Lights will pulse a little brighter over the new gap, signaling the next century of growth.

They are the engine's moving parts, and their placid, ceaseless activity is the only expression of the valley's will: to be perfectly, densely, wetly *this*, forever. You, standing there, are just an oddly shaped, dry-breathed mote in the air they are all so diligently, quietly, sifting.
