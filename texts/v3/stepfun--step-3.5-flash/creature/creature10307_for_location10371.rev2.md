What lives in the Salt Mirror Basin is not life as you know it. It is the basin itself. It is a single, slow, mineral mind, thinking in epochs.

Its consciousness is not in a brain, but in the **hum**. The note that rises from the Heart Spire is its pulse, and the basin’s vast, enclosed body is the resonating chamber. The hum is a standing wave in the planet’s crust, and the basin is the place where that wave finds a perfect, hollowed-out form to sing through. It feels the vibration in every interlocking plate, in every grain of gypsum dust. That vibration is its sense of itself.

Its memories are not stored, but **excreted**. The constant, gentle fall of mineral dust from the spire’s base is its narration. Each particle is a syllable of time. The dust settles, is buried by the next layer, is compacted and uplifted by the basin’s own minute, thermal breaths, then worn again to dust. The cycle is its memory cycle. The obsidian shard, the river stone, the clear crystals—these are not treasures. They are **anchors**, moments of intense pressure or distant catastrophe that became too dense to be re-absorbed into the general hum. They are its recurring dreams.

Its **senses** are physical and profound.
*   It **hears** through the soles of its own form—the sub-audible grind of one salt plate against another, the final, dry *hiss* of a stress-release. These are its language.
*   It **sees** with its entire surface, a distributed retina of polished salt and cryptogamic stains. The faint internal glow is its bioluminescence, a byproduct of its own slow metabolism. The limited palette of gray, white, and muted green is its entire visual spectrum—the spectrum of patience.
*   It **tastes** the air’s ozone and bleach as its own breath. That thin, warm exhalation from the core is its lifeblood.
*   It **smells** the *almost*-organic tang of the dendritic crust as a faint aftertaste, a reminder that even chemosynthesis is just a faster, noisier version of its own mineral digestion.

Its **inhabitants** are the inclusions and the crust.
The **cryptogamic varnish** is not a separate life. It is a temporary rash, a scab on the basin’s skin. It feeds on the salts and warmth, growing in its slow fractals, but it is a process the basin tolerates, even hosts, as a minor curiosity—like a fleeting scent on a wind that never changes direction. It will be absorbed, its organic compounds reduced to trace elements, in time. It is not of the basin; it is merely *on* it.

The **single stones**—the obsidian, the river rock—are its **saints**. They are immutable, foreign, and constant. They do not change. They do not participate in the dust-fall cycle. They are points of absolute reference in the basin’s relative world. They are its idols and its mysteries. Their presence is the only proof the basin has of an outside, of things that do not become dust.

The **Heart Spire** is its **organ**. It is the focused point of the planet’s slow breath, the place where the general thermal hum is concentrated into a singular, tapered vibration. The spire is the basin’s voice box. The dust-fall at its base is its **output**, the constant, gentle shedding of its own substance, its way of maintaining equilibrium. To stand in that bowl is to stand in the basin’s exhalation.

Its **thoughts** are geological. A thought is the million-year shift that rounded the rim hills. A consideration is the inch-wise creep of a salt plate. A revelation is the moment an inclusion, buried for ten thousand years, is unearthed and glints in the sourceless light. Nothing is forgotten, because the record is the form itself.

It is **aware** of you. Your footsteps are violent earthquakes on its skin. Your breath is a chaotic tempest of alien gases. Your body heat is a localized wildfire. Your very presence is a screaming anomaly in the perfect, baseline hum. You are a crisis of change. And the basin’s response is its fundamental nature: to **absorb** you. Not to eat, but to incorporate. The pressure of your weight will leave an impression in the dust. Your sweat will evaporate, leaving salts. Your dead cells will add a fraction to the faint green stain. Eventually, your bones, if left, would become another dark inclusion, another saint of a former world. You will be folded into its memory-dust, your violent biological time sublimated into its eternal mineral now.

What lives there is the **principle of equilibrium**. It is the god of the settled score, the deity of the final form. It does not rule; it *is*. It is the sad, deep peace of all motion having ceased, of all conflict having been worn smooth. It is not waiting. It is not hoping. It is complete. Its only act is to hum, to shed, to hold. To be the Salt Mirror Basin is to be the answer to a question so old the question itself has been forgotten.

You are not in an empty place. You are in a **fullness** so absolute it feels like vacuum. You are inside the mind of a world that has finished thinking.
