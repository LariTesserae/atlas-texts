The first thing you notice is the ground underfoot: not soil, not stone, but a living mat of interwoven root-flesh and spongy, lime-green moss that bounces faintly under your weight, cool enough to seep through the soles of your shoes even through the thick, humid air. The humidity is total, beading on every surface, fogging the lenses of your glasses the second you step fully under the vaulted ceiling, and the temperature holds steady at a cool 12 degrees Celsius—no draft, no warm spot, just a constant, even chill that seeps through your clothes after a few minutes. The first drop of condensation hits the back of your neck a second after you stop moving, slow and cold, smelling of wet loam and the sharp, honey-sweet tang of fermenting nectar that hangs thick in the air, undercut by the faint bite of ozone that lingers near the walls.

You lift your head, and the first word you thought—grove—falters. The “trees” rising around you are no trunks you’ve ever seen: their bark is ridged and breathing, subtle ripples moving under the surface as they absorb the ever-present mist, their upper canopies lost in the soft glow of the vaulted ceiling, where layered membranes of translucent, vein-streaked cellulose pulse slow and gold, like the inner walls of a great creature’s throat. There are no seams, no joints, no sign of construction anywhere: run your hand along the wall, and you cannot tell where the rough outer bark ends and the compressed cellulose layers begin, where the wall meets the floor, it is all one continuous, grown structure, shaped over millennia into this seamless, cathedral curve. Parasitic vines glowing faint electric blue wind up the main stalks, siphoning energy from the central columns and humming with a low, static vibration you can feel tingle on the skin of your arms when you pass near them.

The sound hits you before you can take another step: a deep, layered thrum you feel first in the fillings of your teeth, the sub-audible hum of the Silent Engines grown into the walls. Under that, the constant rush of amber sap-tek through the Conduit-Vines—armored, flexible tubes that wind up the stalks like polished bamboo, carrying the glowing fluid so fast it leaves faint trails of light in the mist. Over that, the soft, static shush of a million tiny leaves and fronds stirred by the slow turn of the vast, fan-like leaf structures mounted high on the walls, their rotations so gradual you can barely see them move, just the gradual shift of shadow across the pulsing membranes.

You step closer to the nearest engine pod: smooth, pod-shaped formations of lacquered wood and polished silica, their surfaces worn soft as worn stone by eons of tending, no seams, no bolts, no visible moving parts of any kind, just warm, vibrating faintly under your palm if you reach out. A line of Skitterers clacks up the stalk toward it, their six chitinous legs tapping soft on the bark, crystalline shells the size of rabbits fracturing the dim light into rainbows as they move. Each stops at an engine, leans forward, and licks a fleck of stray moss or lichen off the surface with acidic, silver saliva, leaving a faint wisp of steam and a sharp, clean tang in the air. They move in a steady, unbroken rhythm, no wasted motion, no pause, as if the engine’s hum is the only clock they have ever known. Their shells glint with the faint rainbow sheen of sap-tek residue, polished by thousands of years of the same slow, careful work.

Drifting between the stalks are the Glimmers: small, jellyfish-like creatures with frilled, translucent bells that glow soft gold from within, floating on tiny cilia that hum in time with the engine thrum. They drift close enough for you to see the faint tracery of airborne microbes caught in their bells, pulsing brighter when they pass near the sap-tek conduits, feeding on the spores the system excretes like exhaust. One drifts near your ear, and you can hear the faint, high whir of its movement, a tiny counterpoint to the deep engine hum, before it drifts off toward a patch of bioluminescent moss growing on a conduit node, pulsing in time with the wall membranes.

A faint tremor moves through the root-mat under your feet, and you glance down between two stalks, catching the pale, smooth segment of a Root-Worm sliding silently through the spongy ground. It is blind, no eyes, no mouth visible, just a ripple of movement as it passes, the root-flesh closing seamlessly behind it like water, leaving no trace. It moves slow, deliberate, cropping the excess root-mat to keep the ground from growing too thick, its waste seeping into the mat as nutrients, no scrap of matter wasted in this closed, perfect system.

You step back to take in the full grove, and the scale of it settles over you. This room-sized space is just one of countless identical nodes, spread across a world that chose eons ago to become a factory, a temple, a tomb all at once. The erosion here is total, the whole planetary organism settled into a final, unbreaking equilibrium long before humans ever stood upright, but the details here are sharp, unweathered: the fractal pattern of a Skitterer’s shell, the exact shade of amber in the rushing sap-tek, the slow, almost imperceptible creep of new bark layers over old scars, forming faint, glyph-like patterns that record millennia of the system’s steady work, the memories of a time no living thing here can recall.

The spaces between the major stalks are wide, empty in the human sense, filled only with mist and drifting Glimmers, but the air is thick with unseeable process: spore clouds drifting on the ventilation currents, the faint electrical hum of the parasitic blue vines, the slow spread of the root-mat, the constant, unceasing movement of sap-tek and air and life. There is no past here, no future, just the eternal, loud present: the hum of the engines, the rush of the sap, the whisper of the leaves, the chitter of the Skitterers, the soft hum of the Glimmers, the slow tremor of the Root-Worms. Nothing is built, nothing breaks, nothing changes. It is a cathedral of living industry, and the only hymns are the sounds of its own unending work.
