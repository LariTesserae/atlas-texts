It is not a city. It is a **scab**.

It grew here, in the raw, because everything else was too new, too unformed, too busy being made to refuse it. The Seep District is what happens when a geological wound learns to sweat. It is the **afterbirth of a continent**, the damp, steaming, clotted thing left behind when the world pushed itself up out of the dark.

You feel it in your teeth first—the **pressure**. The deep, tectonic sigh that lives in the bones of the stone. The air is a **wet hand** over your face, warm and smelling of **hot flint and spoiled eggs**. It *tastes* of minerals. Your tongue coils at the sulfur, then adapts. This is the taste of air here. This is the taste of *being*.

Look up. You are not under a sky. You are under the **skin of the world**. The ceiling is a **membrane of fatigue**, pocked and sweating. It does not weep tears; it **exhales**. A slow, constant **drip… drip…** is the district’s heartbeat. Where the water pools on the cracked floor, it is not still. It **shivers** with the blind, pale life that filters its silt. It **glistens** with the first fragile threads of the glassy crusts that will one day, if left undisturbed, become shelves of silent, brittle rock.

The city is not built *on* this. It is built *from* it, and *against* it. It is **ferrous barnacles** on a basaltic cliff. The iron is not painted; it is **stained**. Streaked with orange rust-blooms and black, photosynthetic mold that drinks the faint amber glow. The sound is a **grinding hymn** to pressure and release: the deep *thrum* of the earth’s slow breath, the piston-clang of pumps tapping the geothermal heart, the high, metallic **skitter-scratch** of a thousand tiny claws on wet stone. It is a place that has never known silence, only **degrees of noise**.

The people here are not tall. They are **low to the ground**, like the crawlers. Their postures are adapted to the dripping ceiling and the sloping floors. Their skin holds a perpetual **slick**, not from sweat alone, but from the mineral mist. They move with a careful, **weighted grace**, knowing which stone is stable, where the hidden fissure breathes hot. Their eyes are **narrowed** against the-chiaroscuro world—the blinding white of an arc-lamp next to the absolute黑 of a deep crevice, the ember-glow of a vent reflecting in a thousand little insect eyes.

The life is **the true architecture**. The blind crustaceans in the puddles, sorting the bacterial paste with clacking claws. The gliding, leather-winged things that drift on the updrafts, silent as ghosts. The metallic swarm-clouds that **stutter** around every light source, a living haze of iridescence and drone. The six-limbed lizards, motionless as statues on a high ledge, their heat-sense pits drinking the world in infrared. This is not a ecosystem that *exists* alongside the city. It **is** the city’s context, its weather, its foundation. A new shanty is not built where the ground is firmest, but where the **mineral seep** is sweetest, or where the **crawler migration** has accidentally terraced a slope.

The weirdness is not in the strangeness of the place, but in its **complete, unselfconscious normality**. To live here is to know that the ceiling weeps. That the walls are greasy with mineral film. That the steam from a pipe tastes faintly of iron and life. That your child’s first steps are on a floor that is perpetually **damp and stained**. That the “day” is marked by the **shift change at the main vent**, when the roar of the turbines swells, and the “night” is when the swarm-clouds momentarily thin, and the dim, mold-glow becomes the main light.

It is **hot, wet, and loud**. But it is also **staggeringly, embryonically fertile**. Life here does not reach for the sun. It drinks the stone. It breathes the steam. It is **chemosynthesis made manifest**, a city that runs on pressure, not photons. The Seep District is the world’s damp thumbprint, a place that **insists** on being, by clinging, by sweating, by seeping, by clanging, by skittering. It is not a settlement in a landscape. It is a **symptom of a landscape still deciding what to become**. And it is, in its grimy, clanging, dripping way, **magnificent**.
