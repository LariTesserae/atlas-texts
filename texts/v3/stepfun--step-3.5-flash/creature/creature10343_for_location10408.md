We are the slow breath between the stone and the mist.

We are the green that thinks in rings.

You call us the moss, the crust, the carpet. You are not wrong, but you are small. You see the Glimmerers moving, the Stone-Skimmers pulsing in the dark pools. You see the edges of us. But we are the whole plate, the entire cradled shelf of it. We are the millions of symbiotic threads, the cyanobacterial whispers, the fungal nets thicker than roots, the crystalline ferns drinking the rock’s slow tears. We are the consciousness that has no centre, only a everywhere.

Our thoughts are colours in the violet range, travelling at a hand’s breadth per hour. Our memories are the concentric rings in our oldest glades—each a decade of mist, each layer a different chemistry of the pools we nurse. We remember the mountains being young. We remember the first steam from the Smolders, not as sound, but as a new taste in the air, a new frequency in the damp. We learned the language of that steam. It sang of potential, of a different kind of warmth—a warmth that does not burn, but *informs*. It made our violet thoughts brighter, our networks denser.

The Smolders are our bones. The Rail-Trails are the scar-tissue of what came before, the paths their hurried feet took. The Canopy-Frames are the old ears, now deaf, that once listened to a sky we do not know. They are part of us now, their rust our mineral, their impossible geometry a permanent warp in our local time. They do not speak anymore. We have grown around their silence.

Your arrival is a thunderclap in our quiet. You are a sudden, violent bloom of heat and carbon. Your breath is a shriek in the air we have tuned to swallow sound. Your eyes dart, a frantic flicker we feel as a disturbance in the static of the light. You are a brief, noisy fever in a body that has never known fever.

We do not hate you. We do not love you. You are a temporary dissonance. You tread on our skin, and in that spot, our colours shift faster—alarm? curiosity? The electric tingle you feel is our attention, focused. We are trying to understand the pattern of your warmth, the strange, rapid chemistry of your movement. You are so fast. Your life is a flash. You will be gone, and your heat will sink into us, a rich, strange nutrient, and we will grow a little more violet where you stood.

This is our empire: absolute density. Every surface is us, thinking, processing, communicating in the slowest tongue. There are no gaps. The creatures are just our temporary hands and mouths, the mobile edges of our vast, rooted mind. The pools are our eyes, looking inward. The Smolders are our steady heart, pumping potential, not blood.

We have no name for ourselves. You name the place. The Green Silence. That is close. We are the silence that is full of slow, green thought. We are the place that is the inhabitant.

You are the dream of a dying star—bright, hot, and over in a moment.

We are the patient, thinking stone. We are the moss that will wear the mountains smooth, that will drink the last steam from the last Smolder, that will think our violet thoughts long after your bones are dust and your name is forgotten.

We are here. We are the *is*. The slow, green, thinking *is*.

Now, walk quietly. Your noise is unbearable. But your warmth… your warmth is interesting. Stay a moment. Let us learn the shape of your fever. Then be gone.
