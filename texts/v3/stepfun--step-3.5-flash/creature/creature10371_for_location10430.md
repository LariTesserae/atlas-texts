The Gristle-Vale holds no tongues to name it, no inhabitants to mark its edges, so its lives are known only by the work they do, the shape they take, the way they braid themselves to the rusted bones of the world that came before.
First, the valley’s spine and breath: the Gristle-Trees. They are not wood, not quite plant, not quite ruin, but a third thing sutured from both. Their trunks are thick, damp mats of grey-green pulp, woven through with rust-stained reeds as thick as a man’s arm, stiff as salvaged cable—these reeds are the tree’s vascular tissue, calcified with iron pulled from the bluish-gray bedrock and the corroding industrial wreckage half-buried in the pulp-soil.
