What lives here is not a collection of beings, but a single, slow, mineral consciousness expressing itself through countless minute forms.

It is in the **Glass-Weavers**, who are not spiders but geologists. They are the basin’s memory-makers, tracing the history of each shard they bind. Their silica threads are not homes, but libraries—fragile, temporary archives of pressure and cooling, spun from the very language of the rock. Their acid is a question, and the dissolved trace minerals are the answer, absorbed not as food, but as knowledge. They are the place’s way of remembering how to be glass.

It is in the **Rust-Grazers**, who are not beetles but digestive enzymes given movement. They are the erasure, the slow correction. Their iridescent trails are not waste, but the basin’s exhalation—the final, oxidized sigh of the buried industry. They are the place’s way of un-making what was forced upon it, converting alien purpose back into common iron oxide, step by meticulous step.

It is in the **Condensate-Skimmers**, who are not tadpoles but the place’s lymph. They glide through the viscous, mineral-heavy water not to hunt, but to filter. Each particle they consume is a choice: this speck of copper, this mote of silica, this fleck of something older. They are the place’s way of refining its own blood, deciding what stays suspended in its slow circulation and what settles to become part of the floor.

It is in the **Hummers**, who are not animals but the place’s pulse. Their slow, magnetic roll is not locomotion but conduction. They gather the ambient static from the cool, wet air—the planet’s nervous energy—and manifest it as a sub-audible thrum. Their accumulating hide of dust and filings is not armor, but a growing sense of self, a history of every electrical whisper they’ve absorbed. They are the place’s heartbeat, made tangible.

And it is in the **dying automaton**, whose endless *clack-clack-clack* is the last, stubborn echo of a thought that outlived its mind. It is not a machine, but a fossilized intention, a stubborn loop in the basin’s new, slower logic. Its rhythmic sorting of empty space is a prayer to a forgotten god of productivity, now just part of the acoustic texture of ruin.

What lives here is the **Hollow itself**. The basin is the organism. The glass floor is its skin, polished by the perpetual mist of its breath. The obelisks are its nodule-bones. The viscous pools are its sensory organs, tasting the stone. The deep thrum is its thought, felt in the teeth of anything that listens. The scattered industry is undigested food, a meal so large it’s taking millennia to process.

The creatures are not its children. They are its **processes**. They are its way of seeing (the Weavers’ crystalline facets), its way of shedding (the Grazers’ rust-shedding), its way of circulating (the Skimmers), its way of feeling pressure and charge (the Hummers). There is no "you" observing this. There is only the *it*—the dense, settled, grinding-glass thing—experiencing itself through these endless, tiny actions. To be here is to be inside a mind that thinks in erosion, speaks in vibrations, and dreams in concentric circles of silica, rust, and humming slag. You are in its body, hearing its thoughts, watching it slowly, beautifully, turn the bones of a dead ambition into the quiet grammar of a new, stone-cold world.
