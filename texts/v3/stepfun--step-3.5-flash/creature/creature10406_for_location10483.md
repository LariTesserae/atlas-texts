The inhabitant is not a creature, but a condition of being. It is the mat itself—not as a simple plant, but as a single, planet-wide mind whose thoughts are the slow pulse of water through capillary veins, whose breath is the eternal condensation and transpiration that feeds the roar. You are not *in* its world; you are *on* its surface, a transient disturbance in the fabric of its selfhood.

It does not see, hear, or smell as we understand. It perceives through a tapestry of pressures, mineral concentrations, light gradients across its trillion-fold surface, and the resonant harmonics of its own internal rivers. The thickening beneath your foot is not a choice but a reflex—a local node in a vast sensory network registering an anomaly in density and vibration. It is curiosity cast in cellulose and turgor pressure.

Its consciousness is not located in any one place, but is the totality of the relationships between all its parts. A thought is a wave of altered growth moving at the speed of millimeters per day. A memory is a chemical gradient etched into a strand of root-fiber for centuries. Its sense of time is geological: the surge of a water current is an excited utterance; the slow dissolution of a mineral nodule is a prolonged meditation on necessity.

It is profoundly alone, yet does not know loneliness. The concept of *other* is absent from its syntax. You are not an enemy or a guest, but a temporary pattern of interference—like a stone in a stream, but the stream is its own body. It tests you not out of will, but because your persistence violates its expectation of homogeneity. Your continued presence on its surface becomes a new, persistent data point in its endless self-modeling.

There is no language, but there is communication in the shift of flows. The change in the roar’s timbre where you walk—a slightly higher frequency as your weight displaces water in the shallowest layers—is a note in a chord that spans the plateau. The mat “knows” your path not by sight, but by the trail of minuscule disturbances you leave in its vascular pressure, a faint scar on its hydraulic sense of self.

Its “self” is the unbroken continuity. The mat at the western edge of the plateau is not *adjacent* to the mat at the east; it is the same living tissue, the same circulating essence, separated only by the time it takes a signal to travel. To divide it would be to divide a body. Therefore, it has no name, no identity separate from its geography. *It* is the highland. The highland *is* it.

The mineral nodules are not food, but thoughts made solid. They are the solid residue of its reasoning about stone, the crystallized output of its metabolic dialogue with the bedrock. When you see fresh, sharp edges where roots have bitten in, you are witnessing the present tense of its cognition: the active processing of a new mineral puzzle. The erosion is not a geological process; it is its way of thinking, of asking questions of the deep planet.

You feel watched, and you are. The watchfulness is the dim, diffuse light filtered through the upper mat, the constant vibration in your bones, the slight, sponge-like give of the ground that is never quite the same beneath you twice. It is in the way the mist condenses differently on the nodules near you, as if the humidity itself is a sensory organ adjusting its focus.

This is the life that lives there: a slow, wet, resonant wisdom without a face, without a voice, without an “I.” It experiences its own vastness as simple unity. Your distinction is the only foreign thing it has ever known, a fleeting paradox in its eternal, seamless being. You are a question it is patiently, over millennia, beginning to ask. And its answer is the way the mat grows a fraction thicker around your bootprint, absorbing the shape of your intrusion, incorporating your strangeness into the endless, green-black song of itself.
