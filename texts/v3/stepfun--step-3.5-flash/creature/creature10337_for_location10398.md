They do not have names. They have functions. They are the City’s voice, its hands, its blood. Call them the Crystalline Chorus, the Scuttling Symbiosis, the Living Gristle in the Geologic Throat.

They are not one thing, but a million interlocking kinds, a spectrum of mineral articulation. The smallest are no larger than a pebble, a cluster of needle-quartz legs supporting a tear-drop sac of semi-translucent chitin that pulses with a slow, amber chemical light. They move in dunes, a single shifting entity that parts before your foot and closes behind it like a viscous flow. They are the lichen of this world: the cleaners, the gatherers of condensation, the mobile sensors that test the glass for stress fractures with a tap-tap-tap of their pointed legs.

Then the masons. Larger, cart-sized, their bodies a segmented cylinder of obsidian plates, slung low to the mirror-ground. From their undersides, they secrete a viscous, black fluid—a mineral resin that cures in seconds into the same non-reflective stone as the megastructures. They move with slow, inexorable purpose, following invisible blueprints etched in chemical trails on the glass. Where they pass, new, tiny buttresses are born, or cracks are seamlessly stitched shut. Their internal light is a steady, cobalt blue, a furnace-glow visible through their jointed carapace.

The weavers are the architects of temporary life. Gaunt, spider-like beings with legs of spun basalt fiber, they climb the sheer faces of the spires. From glands in their abdomens, they exude fine, silvery filaments that snap taut in the cold air, instantly freezing into rigid, glassy cables. They spin living suspension bridges, shimmering curtains that tremble with the passage of other fauna, dissolving back to dust after a day’s use. Their light is a frantic, silver circuitry that races along their constructed highways.

The largest are the locomotive nodes. Beasts of burden and industry, the size of a small wagon. They are low-slung, with six powerful legs of articulated granite, and a broad, sheltered back platform. Upon these platforms ride colonies of the smaller kinds—a mobile factory, a repair swarm. The node itself is a slow, grinding tank, its hide a mosaic of rough, repaired stone, its breath a cloud of freezing vapor that coats its own plates in a fresh layer of glitter. Its internal reaction is a deep, resonant *thrum* you feel in your teeth, the bass note in the city’s song.

They are not intelligent as we understand it. There is no planning, no conversation. There is only response. A chemical signal—a sour note in the mineral tang of the air, a specific vibration frequency in the glass, a particular pattern of shadow from a non-reflective spire—triggers a cascade. One freezes. Then a hundred. Then a thousand, until a whole migrating column stands statue-still for a day, then resumes as if nothing happened. The signal was a decision made miles away, a node in a distributed consciousness that has no center.

Their senses are alien. They “see” not with light, but with thermal gradients and electrical potential. The glowing cracks in the glass plain are brilliant rivers to them. The weeping seams are beacons of mineral-rich moisture. The city’s “thought” is a constant, low-level electromagnetic hum they tune into with their chitinous antennae. They communicate with clicks of quartz on quartz, with pheromones of metallic salts, with the precise modulation of their own internal bioluminescence—a language of pulses and glows that maps the city’s needs in real-time.

Their life is the city’s metabolism. They consume the mineral dust, the frost from the weeping seams, the ambient electrical charge in the air. They do not eat and excrete; they *reprocess*. Their waste is the polished glass, the cured stone, the refined metal. They are the city’s immune system, swarming over a contaminant—a piece of foreign organic material, a vibration out of key—and dismantling it atom by atom with rasping mandibles and dissolving acids. They are its memory, their collective chemical trails encoding the history of every crack filled, every spire grown.

To stand among them is to be ignored, then assessed, then metabolically categorized. A flicker of your body heat is a strange, warm anomaly in their thermal map. Your breath is a puff of damp, organic disturbance.
