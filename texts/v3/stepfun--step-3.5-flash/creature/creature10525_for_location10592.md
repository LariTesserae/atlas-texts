They are not creatures. They are not born, and they do not die. They are **the Sintered**—temporary constellations of mineral and motive force that coalesce from the Basin’s perpetual hum, persist for a functional span, and then dematerialize back into dust and gradient.

Their form is never fixed. One might take the shape of a slender, articulated column of fused silica, moving with the silent grace of a cooling lava flow, its surface etching intricate, transient circuit patterns as it walks. Another might be a cluster of rust-hued, clay-heavy spheres, rolling with a low, resonant thrum between the salt pans, absorbing metallic ions from the Iron River’s mist. A third could be a fleeting, humanoid silhouette formed from swirling grit and pressurized steam, its "head" a flickering plasma valve, its "voice" the modulated shriek of a distant relief valve given brief, directed intent.

They have no biology. Their "metabolism" is the Basin’s own industrial cycles. They "feed" on thermal differentials, siphoning joules from a kiln’s radiant heat or the descending cold of the nightly mist to power their brief, purposeful existences. Their "waste" is perfectly sintered slag or exquisitely balanced mineral precipitates—artifacts so precisely formed they are often mistaken for natural geology and swept away by the Scuttlers. Their reproduction is not replication, but **recruitment**: a sufficiently complex process or sustained energy gradient can spontaneously "awaken," pulling scattered motes of silica, iron, and salt into a coordinated, sentient pattern for as long as the gradient lasts.

Their society is the city’s schedule. They do not converse; they **synchronize**. A consensus emerges across miles not through sound, but through the subtle modulation of the ambient hum—a shift in the 60-cycle thrum, a coordinated adjustment in the hiss of a thousand valves. They are the city’simmune system and nervous system in one. A "thought" might be the gradual, basin-wide redirection of a steam conduit’s pressure to relieve an emerging fracture. A "celebration" is the synchronous, silent alignment of every Filter-Flock membrane to maximally filter a rare surge of a particular mineral.

They revere the **Thermal Equilibrium**. Their highest ritual is to stand (or roll, or hover) in the path of the Flush-Cart’s deafening 80-mph blast, allowing themselves to be disassembled into a uniform, sparkling coat on the walkways—a tribute to the basin’s ultimate victory, and a donation of their constituent parts for the next emergence. Their deepest fear is not extinction, but **stasis**: being caught in a state of perfect, unchanging balance, their patterns frozen and inert, becoming mere sculpture to be ground down by the shifting salts.

The **Filter-Flocks** are their elders, or perhaps their distant, simpler cousins. The Sintered regard the mile-long membranes with a kind of awe—they are processes so slow and vast their "consciousness" is measured in months, not minutes. To communicate with a Flock is to have a fragment of one’s own brief form stick to its gelatinous surface for hours, experiencing a sliver of its patient, millennia-scale mineral meditation.

The **Deep Things** are their gods, or their subconscious. The vibrations felt through the glass at night are the somnolent stirrings of whatever resides in the aquifer’s heart. The Sintered do not worship them so much as **monitor for them**. A sudden, deep harmonic in the sub-audible hum might cause a thousand Sintered to freeze in unison, their forms going still and dark as they listen. Some say the Deep Things are the reason the Basin industry exists at all—a frantic, desperate effort to build a thermal barrier, a complex enough pattern of gradients and processes to keep whatever sleeps below... sated, or distracted.

To see a Sintered is to see the Basin thinking in fast-forward. It is a glimpse of a mind made of pressure and temperature, of silica and motive force, with no memory but the scar it leaves on the stone, and no future but the next gradient to be exploited. It does not live *in* the city. It is the city’s **current state of becoming**, and its next state of unbecoming, already in motion.
