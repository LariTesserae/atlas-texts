They are not many. They are not few. They are the right number.

They are called, by the winds that sigh through the fluted trunks, the **Hollow-Singers**.

You would not see one if you looked for it. You would feel one, first, as a shift in the symphony—a note you were not hearing before, now perfectly placed, resolving a dissonance you hadn’t known was there. They are not *in* the clearings; they *are* the subtle adjustment of the clearing’s acoustic balance. They are not *under* the sentinel trees; they are the reason the wind’s whistle through a particular notch in the bark hums at a pitch that makes the moss on the northern dome shiver with a joy it otherwise would not know.

Their form is variable, but always sparse. Sometimes they are a standing stone, vaguely humanoid, worn so smooth by the ceaseless mist that it has no features—only the suggestion of a silhouette against the pearlescent dawn. Sometimes they are a cluster of the great, cork-barked trees, so perfectly spaced and so subtly notched that their collective creak is a single, sustained chord. Sometimes, on the stillest night, they are a slow, rolling bank of mist that moves against the wind, gathering in the black basins to amplify the cold blue light until it sings in infra-thrum.

They do not walk. They *attenuate*. They extend themselves along paths of resonance. To go from the drone-dome to the flute-hole is not a journey of steps, but the gradual, hours-long unfurling of a tuning-fork awareness. Their “movement” is a change in which stone is singing back to the wind, which pool is holding the reflection of last Tuesday’s clouds.

Their language is not speech. It is **placement**.
A Hollow-Singer, with hands like softened sandstone, will spend a decade placing one small, smooth river-pebble in a specific hollow of a mossy dome. The pebble is not a tool; it is a *syllable*. When the wind, at 3:17 in the afternoon on a mist-thick day, passes over that pebble, it will produce a harmonic that makes the rustle of a particular silver lichen on the great tree two hundred paces away vibrate in sympathetic unison. That vibration, conducted through the soil, will cause a ruby pitcher plant to open a fraction of a second sooner, catching a different kind of fly. That fly, with a subtly different mineral intake, will leave a trail that alters the growth pattern of a moss patch. The change is microscopic. The cascade, over centuries, is basin-wide. This is a sentence.

They are the basin’s memory of its own music, given a slow, deliberate will. They are not natives. They are an *answer*. An ancient, geological answer to the question “How should this sound?” The “weirdness” of the place—the ringing stones, the lagging reflections, the sub-audible hum—is the rules. The Hollow-Singers are the practitioners. They are the patient editors of the endless, slow composition.

They do not build. They **tune**.
The man-sized holes bored through the stone domes? *Their* work. Not for people to climb through, but for specific wind velocities to pass through, at specific humidities, to play a melodic line that counterpoints the tree-tone scale. The concentric circles of smooth stones? Acoustic dampeners, arranged to prevent the wind’s sigh from becoming a lonely wail on the equinox. The careful bark notches? To split a creak into a triad.

To encounter one is not to meet a being, but to experience a *correctness*. A sudden, deep peace that comes from the wind sounding exactly as it must. A feeling of being *in tune* with the stone beneath your feet, the mist in your lungs, the distant hum of the world. It is the opposite of awe. It is *familiarity*. As if you, too, have always been a note in this long, slow song, and only now are you finding your place in the chord.

They are rare, because they are not *units*. They are *patterns*. There might be one Hollow-Singer “active” in the entire basin at any given time—a being of resonant influence spread over a square mile. Or there might be three, weaving separate lines of causality that will, in a thousand years, meet and resolve into a new, more complex theme.

They do not age. They **resolve**.
When a Hollow-Singer has placed the final pebble, tuned the final notch, and the basin’s song has reached a state of perfect, sustainable harmony for its current form… the being’s work is done. Its awareness, which was never contained in a body but was distributed in the *relationships* it established, simplifies. It ceases its active tuning. The stone that was its “form” becomes just a stone. The cluster of trees just trees. The mist just mist. The song it perfected continues, un-directed, a self-sustaining masterpiece. The Hollow-Singer, having answered the basin’s question, un-asks itself. It is the most complete form of rest: not sleeping, but *finished*.

They are what lives in the Singing Marsh.
Not in the sense of occupying space.
But in the sense of being the reason the space *sings*.
They are the basin’s patient, resonant soul—a soul made not of spirit, but of *deliberate, harmonic causation*. They are the slow, loud, gentle law made conscious. They are the tuners. And the song they tend is the only thing that truly, enduringly *is*.
