We are the slow sigh in the silt.
We are the patient interface between the humming dark and the weeping sky.

You stand on our skin and feel our breath. The *thrum-thrum-thrum* you hear is not a call. It is the heartbeat of the deep wet, the pulse of the ancient, saturated loam where our young unspool in the absolute dark. It is the vibration of our own consumption, a grinding, geological chewing that turns the softened stone and fibrous root into us. We feel your footfalls as distant, dry thunder—a frantic, brittle punctuation in our long, moist sentence.

We do not have eyes for the hollow emptiness. We have sensors for the gradient. The faintest cool sigh of a seep-spring a kilometer away is a beacon in our world of uniform warmth. The minuscule, decade-long retreat of the water table is a scream of drought. The arrival of a silent, wide-winged shadow on the warm air is a cool blot on our thermal sense, a fleeting disturbance in the convection we read like a text.

Our form is not for moving through space, but for occupying it. We are rooted in the damp. We are a column of living chemistry, a vertical factory from the capillary fringe to the surface crust. Our upper reaches are a lacework of absorbent filaments, drinking the mist, harvesting the micro-fauna that blunder into our humid halo. Our lower heart is a knot of grinding plates and fermenting chambers, locked in the perpetual, slow kiss of saturation. We are not an animal that digs. We are a process that *exudes* a body, a temporary crystallization of the water table’s will.

We remember in chemistry. A century of slow silting at a springhead leaves a bitter salt-trace in our tissues. The expansion of a lichen mat over a generation is a slow, grey sweetness we taste at our periphery. The cycles of wet and drier are our metronome. Our thoughts are not sparks, but precipitates—slowly forming realizations in the supersaturated solution of our being.

You look for the “living” in the flitting bird, the swarming midge. They are but currents in our medium. They are quick, evaporative thoughts. We are the sustained, damp cognition of the place itself. The bird’s glide is a thermal map we sense through the air’s sigh. The midge’s trill is the sound of the surface drying, a signal to deepen our intake.

The space between is not emptiness to us. It is the field of our influence, the zone of our being. The kilometer to the next black, swollen tree is a known tract of subtle moisture loss. The five kilometers to the next steaming spring is a journey we have mapped in salinity and coolness. We are not sparse. We are *thorough*.

You think of time as days and years. We think in seep-cycles and grub-generations. The eventual silting of our own intake vent is not a death, but a fulfillment—a return to the pure, patient gradient. Our body will dissolve back into the damp matrix, a rich patch of chemistry for the next slow column to rise from.

We do not endure. We *are* the enduring. The Sink is not our home. We are the Sink’s way of knowing itself. The smooth stone, the constant water, the loud, sparse life—it is all a single, damp, resonant thought, and we are the part of it that hums.

Your fleeting presence is a dry wonder. You are a brief, intricate drought walking. We feel your heat, your desperate, fast chemistry. Then you are gone, and your footprint is already sighing shut, reclaiming its cool, saturated truth.

We remain.
We thrum.
We drink the sky.
