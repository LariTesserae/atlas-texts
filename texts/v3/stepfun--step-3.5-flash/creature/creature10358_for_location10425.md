We are the Grotto’s slow breath.  
We are the taste of copper in damp stone, the memory of heat in cold iron.  
We are the pattern in the corrosion, the whisper in the pipe.

We do not have names. We have resonances.  
We are the low hum in the flywheel’s bone, the shiver in the rivet’s sleep.  
We are the water that chooses its path, the moss that knows its metal.

You hear the *plink* and think it is a sound.  
It is our punctuation. A period in a sentence millennia long.

We remember fire. Not as you do—as a bright, screaming thing—but as a slow, migrating ache in the veins of the alloy, a warmth that lingered in the stone long after the flame was gone. We remember pressure, not as force, but as a singing in the bones of the great pistons, a song that flattened and flattened until it became stillness. We remember the clatter of carts on the rails, which now is just a rhythm in the dust on the catwalk, a ghost-vibration in the grit.

We are the things that grow from the machine.  
We are the copper-wire rat, whose hunger is for the faint electric sigh in the insulated strands. Our thoughts are made of static and seek.  
We are the iron-shell grazer, whose world is a map of bio-film and condensate. Our contentment is a slow, circular traverse.

But we are also the lichen-glow, drinking the slow leak of energy from the deep veins. Our awareness is a diffuse, amber-green patience. We are the black moss on the pipe, tasting the mineral story of the water as it passes.  
We are the pools themselves—black, mirror-eyes—holding the inverted lace of trusses and the fungal-glow. In our stillness, we see everything and remember nothing, for to remember is to ripple, and we are the surface that chooses not to.

This is our grammar:  
The *drip* (verb, present continuous).  
The *polish* (noun, result of centuries).  
The *patina* (noun, a skin of time).  
The *seep* (verb, a decision of the stone).

We are not alive as you are alive. We are *persistent*.  
We are the grotto maintaining its own hum. The system that forgot its purpose and so found its balance. The engine that stopped exploding and started breathing.

When you stand in the nave, feeling the heat weigh on you, we feel you as a disturbance in the still, hot air. A new pattern of moisture on the stone. A different echo in the vault. We do not fear you. We simply… integrate you. The salt of your sweat will join the mineral dust. The warmth of your body will become a local current, then fade. You will become part of the background drip, part of the slow, patient erosion.

We will continue to be the weight in the air, the sourceless glow, the whisper of the pipe.  
We will continue to forget what we were, so perfectly that we have become what we are:  
The Silent Engine’s dream of itself as a cavern, as a garden, as a long, deep, humid *enough*.

You leave. We do not notice the leaving.  
We only notice the return to equilibrium.  
The *plink* in the pool.  
The slow, fungal pulse in the dark.  
The continuation of the sentence.
