### **The Primary Symbiotes**

**The Rhizomorph Bamboo Grove:**
This is not a forest. It is a single, distributed organism, a **planetary nervous system made of fibrous root and metallic node**. The grove breathes as one. Its above-ground stalks are pale, fibrous lungs, transpiring the steam. Its subterranean root network is the mind—a mycelial supercomputer dissolving stone, absorbing silicates, rare earths, and metals. The rattling beads in its nodes are not just accumulations; they are **memory storage**. Each metallic sphere contains a compressed history of the rock it dissolved: pressure, age, trace elements. When the wind rattles them, the grove is *recalling*. The clicking is its data stream, a low-grade chattering that builds into complex sequences only over weeks. It does not think in words, but in **mineral associations**.

**The Thermo-Luminescent Scum (Engineers of Light):**
The cyanobacterial and archaeal mats are the plateau’s **solar panels and bioreactors**, reversed. They do not eat sunlight; they eat **geothermal effluent**—hydrogen sulfide, arsenic, dissolved metals. Their day is the thermal pulse of the vent. Their bioluminescence is not a signal to mates; it is **byproduct and indicator**, a visible waste-heat of their chemosynthesis. The glow’s color (green for sulfides, violet for arsenic) is a **real-time chemical dashboard**. When a mat glows violet, a pocket of arsenic is being concentrated. When it dims, the resource is exhausted. They are not alive so much as they are **a process that has achieved self-perpetuation**.

### **The Specialized Agents**

**The Sinter-Tenders:**
In the basins of active silica deposition, you find them—**not builders, but cultivators**. They are colonies of extremophile archaea and gelatinous diatoms that live *within* the precursory silica gel. Their bodies are substrates for precipitation. As the hot, silica-saturated water flows over them, they **organize the deposition**, creating the lace-like, porous structures of the older terraces. They are **living molds**. When the water chemistry shifts, they die, becoming the brittle, hollow framework for the next layer. Their life cycle *is* the terrace’s growth pattern.

**The Pollen-Sifters (Air-Cattle):**
The wide-beaked, filter-feeding birds are not just eating. They are **mobile, atmospheric separators**. As they drift on thermals, their porous beaks (lined with electrostatic filaments) trap pollen, bacterial spores, and fine silicate dust from the saturated air. They excrete the silica as tiny, polished pebbles, and the organic matter as nutrient-rich guano that fertilizes the bacterial mats on the sinter terraces. They close the atmospheric loop, **mining the mist** and returning its solid contents to the ground in a usable form. Their flight paths are dictated by the density of the steam, and their flocks trace the invisible rivers of mineral-load in the air.

**The Root-Runners (The Distribution System):**
Between the bamboo grove and the silica terraces, through the honeycombed stone, move the **Root-Runners**. They are not animals, but **a stage in the mycorrhizal network’s life cycle**—specialized hyphal cords that swell with transported minerals. They pulse with a slow peristalsis, moving beads of vanadium, molybdenum, and rare earths from the dissolution zones near the roots to the growth zones of the sinter. Where they emerge at the base of a forming terrace, they **deposit their cargo**, then desiccate, becoming part of the mineral matrix. They are **conveyor belts that sacrifice themselves to the product**.

### **The Grazers & Translators**

**The Iridescent Slugs (Amplifiers):**
The giant slugs graze the bacterial mats. But their iridescent skin is not just camouflage—it is a **resonant crystal layer** that absorbs the bacteria’s chemiluminescence and re-emits it brighter, at specific frequencies. They are **biological light amplifiers**. In doing so, they concentrate toxic metals from the mats into their own bodies, becoming slow-moving, glowing repositories of arsenic and selenium. When a Stone-stalker Cat kills one, it consumes a **capsule of concentrated poison and stored light**. The slugs translate chemical energy into visible light and then into predator toxin.

**The Crystalback Lizards (Sinter Polishers):**
Basking on the warm, forming sinter terraces, the lizards are **active finishing tools**. Their translucent, silicate scales absorb heat unevenly. As they move, the differential heating causes minute stresses in the young, gelatinous silica. Their bellies and feet, rough with microscopic grit, **physically abrade and shape** the surface. They are not sunning themselves; they are **working**, polishing the new stone into its eventual lace-like strength. Their shed skins, infused with silica, become the first brittle layer of the next terrace cycle.

They target the slugs (with their arsenic) and certain birds that have ingested metal-laden guano. The cats’ digestive systems **sequester and neutralize** these poisons, storing them in their own denser bones and in the tips of their claws. ### **The Unseen Majority: The Process-Beings**

The true inhabitants are not the visible forms, but the **rhythms themselves**.
*   The **drip-sequence** that carves the silver paths is not random. It is a synchronized output from a thousand seep-springs, dictated by micro-fractures in the stone that vibrate at a shared frequency. The water is *speaking* in droplets.
*   The **bacterial pulse** is a geothermal sigh made visible. A tremor deep in the vent releases a bubble of gas; the bacterial mats, sensing the chemical shift, dim and flare in a wave that travels across the pool—a slow-motion shockwave of light.
*   The **bamboo’s chime-language** changes with the seasons. In the wet time, the beads are slick and quiet. In the dry breeze, they clatter in complex patterns that correlate with the slow creep of a root through stone. The grove is *reporting* its subterranean progress.
