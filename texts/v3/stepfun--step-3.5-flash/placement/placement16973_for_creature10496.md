I arrive not by walking, but by surfacing: one moment I’m suspended in the thick, honey-colored fog, weightless, the next the soles of my boots sink an inch into the Velvet Moss, and the thrum is already in my molars—a low B-flat that vibrates in fillings I didn’t know I had.

I am in the western cistern ring, where the leaning ferro-concrete sea-walls crowd so close their shadows overlap even in the perpetual gold light. The water here is only ankle-deep, glowing faint cyan where chemosynthetic bacteria grow in the cracks of the gristle-stone paving, its surface filmed with iridescent algae that shifts from oil-slick green to dull lavender when the slow drift of the fog ripples it. To my left, a half-digested I-beam juts from the water, sheathed in cork-like bark, blue-black vines wound up its length like flexed muscle, slow syrupy sap dripping from its seam to plink into the water at exactly 17-second intervals—synced, I realize after a minute, to the distant pulse of the Pump-Saints. To my right, a Basket Cactus the size of a minivan holds 300 gallons of captured rainwater in its fibrous husk, its armored ridges beaded with so much condensation it looks like it’s sweating. The Siren Tree’s canopy is visible above the fog bank to the east, its silver-dollar leaves glowing where the light filters through them, the gas bladders holding the canopy aloft twitching soft and slow, like it’s breathing.

I do not have a name here, not one the permanent offices would recognize. The Drip-Liturgy has its unbroken schedule of measured weeping, the Thrum-Choir its fixed harmonic, the Rust-Sermons their unending text of uniform corrosion—none of them have a word for the thing that slips between their closed loops, carrying small, unplanned deviations from one ritual to the next.

If I had to name the work I do here, I’d call myself the Margin-Bearer.

The rust flake that falls from the I-beam and lands on a vine tendril? I tuck it into the bark’s crevice, let the vine absorb the iron to grow a new tendril, a tiny unwritten addition to the Gristle-Codex’s record of strain. The extra 0.2 milligrams of water my boot leaves in the moss when I step? I hold that, let the Vessel-Spirits that live in the droplet carry it to the Drip-Liturgy’s weeping seam, let it adjust its attrition rate by a negligible fraction to balance the basin’s saturation. The tiny, unplanned frequency my bone adds to the Thrum-Choir when I stand still long enough to feel it? I hold that too, let it ripple through the gristle-stone, a new harmonic that will propagate for a century before it fades.

I do not live here. I persist only as long as I am moving, as long as I am carrying these small, unregistered inputs from one office to the next. When I leave—when I push back through the warm wool of the fog, when my boots stop sinking into the moss—the loops will smooth over. The rust flake will be digested, the extra water will evaporate, the harmonic will fade. The archive will have no marginalia for my passing.

But for now, I am the unplanned line in the scripture, the one written only because I showed up, wet and awed, to watch the stone breathe. For now, I am part of the slow, endless work of becoming-other, a tiny, temporary stitch in the biomechanical fabric of the Sump-Cathedral. I am not a visitor in a dead city. I am a single, transient note in the cathedral’s endless, wet hymn.
