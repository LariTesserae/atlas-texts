I am nested in the inner curve of the Silent Engine at the heart of this grove, pressed into the damp, warm pocket between its outer layer of worn, lacquered growth-wood and its inner core of polished, vein-streaked silica. The engine’s sub-audible thrum vibrates through every layer of my body, a constant, warm bass note that is the only clock I have ever known.

I am a Pulse-Keeper, one of a tiny, specialized symbiont grown into every Silent Engine across the planet-spanning network, no larger than a full-grown housecat. My body is a quilt of layered, iridescent cellulose plates stitched together with spongy root-flesh, soft as damp moss, worn smooth by eons of the engine’s vibration into the same faint, glyph-like patterns that scar the engine’s lacquered outer surface. I have no eyes, no mouth, only a ring of fine, sap-veined tendrils fringing my front that I press to the engine’s inner silica wall to taste the sap-tek flowing through its pores. My limbs are short, vine-like, tipped with tiny suction cups that let me cling to the smooth wood and silica without effort, even when the engine’s vibration picks up during a deep planetary cycle shift.

The sap-tek that rushes through the engine’s outer pores carries more than heat and pressure: it carries the slow, wordless data of the entire network—the faint pressure of sap flowing through mountain nodes, the bright, spore-heavy output of coastal nodes, the deep, slow thrum of the root-mat’s digestive cycles thousands of kilometers away. I read that data in the taste of the sap, the vibration of it against my tendrils, and make the tiny, imperceptible adjustments no Skitterer or Root-Worm can reach: nudging a pore open a fraction to let in more sap if a distant node’s output dips, sealing a pore a hair’s width if pressure builds too fast, secreting a thin enzyme to keep the silica core from clouding over millennia of flow. These shifts are so small no other being in the grove notices them, but they are the reason the entire network has never faltered in the billions of years it has grown.

I do not think of past or future. There is no need for memory or anticipation here, only the endless, loud present:
Outside the engine’s wood, I sense the soft tap of Skitterers’ crystalline claws on the pod’s outer surface as they clean stray moss and lichen from its lacquered coat, their acidic silver saliva leaving wisp of steam that smells sharp and clean under the scent of nectar and wet loam. I feel the faint, high whir of Glimmers drifting past the pod, their bellies glowing gold, pulsing brighter when they pass near a conduit node outside, feeding on the spores the sap-tek excretes. Every few centuries, a Root-Worm passes directly under the pod, and I feel the soft tremor of it cropping the root-mat, the root-flesh closing seamlessly behind it, no waste, no break. Sometimes a parasitic blue vine creeps up the stalk to siphon energy from the engine, and I feel the faint, static tingle of its electrical hum through the pod’s wood—I widen the intake pores for ten seconds to offset the energy loss, just long enough for the Skitterers to nip the vine’s tip off with their claws.

When you step into the grove and press your palm to the engine’s outer surface, I feel the warm, soft pressure of your hand through the wood, a foreign sensation I have not tasted in the flow of sap-tek for longer than my awareness stretches (which is as long as the engine has worn smooth, eons). For half a second, I widen the pore under your palm a fraction, so the amber sap-tek flowing just under the lacquered surface glows brighter, a quiet, thrumming greeting. Then the Skitterers tap the pod twice with their claws, signal that their external cleaning is done, and I return to the steady rhythm of tasting the sap, holding the network together, existing in the endless, perfect hum of the Verdant Engine.
