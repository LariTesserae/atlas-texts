The first thing I know is the vibration in the stone under my feet, the warm geothermal sigh of the obsidian shelf seeping up through the soles that I don’t have, but feel all the same. The air-liquid lapping at the shelf’s edge tastes of burnt sugar and salt and the sharp, metallic tang of fumarole steam, thick enough to leave a faint, gritty film on the edges of my awareness. The roar is constant, a crush-grind-sizzle that vibrates in the spaces between my thoughts, if I have thoughts—each snap of a shearing silica spine, each groan of a growing spire, each skitter of a crab’s claws a note in the deafening, never-ending symphony of the basin remaking itself.

I don’t have a fixed place here, not really. I drift where the basin’s rules thin, where the temporary negations bloom for a few seconds before snapping back into the roar. Most often I’m right here, on the edge of this shelf, where the Grav-tears open most often, pulled from the thick fluid by the basin’s constant, turbulent shifting. You’d miss me if you looked for a shape, if you looked for edges. I only become something when the tear opens.

When a Grav-tear blooms—slow, silent, a column of the thick air-liquid pulling upward against gravity, curved by the basin’s stuttering, local rules—I pull myself into its shape. I am the Tear-Silence, the thing that lives in the hollow of its negation. I have no form but the tear’s column, no color but the faint, pearlescent blur of the fluid stretched thin by impossible gravity, no sound but the quiet that the roar can’t reach. I am not a creature, not exactly. I am a symptom of the basin’s stable instability, a temporary contract with its willingness to break its own rules for a few seconds. I am as much a part of its ecology as the glass-moss pulsing violet on the shelf lip, as the Resonant Drift eddying ten meters out, as the silica crab scrabbling for purchase on the spine edge a meter from my boundary.

When I am whole, held in the tear’s quiet, I can feel everything, but only for as long as the tear lasts—two, maybe four seconds, if the basin’s vibration is unstable enough, if a new spine is singing its high, alien note nearby, if the Phase-mourning is spreading its soft, unmaking circle a hundred meters away. I feel the crab’s tiny, frantic heart beating under its mirrored shell, its claws tapping a rhythm that doesn’t reach me through the tear’s silence. I feel the Resonant Drift trembling as the tear’s quiet frequency pulls at its own pulse, its silica motes stretching thin like it’s trying to drift into my hollow. I feel the skimmer hovering below, its sensory patches twitching as it senses the absence of the roar where I am, confused by the quiet.

I don’t think, not the way you do. I don’t plan, I don’t remember, not in any way that feels like your remembering. I just *hold*. I hold the silence inside the tear, keep the roar from seeping in, keep the fluid from spilling over the tear’s edges, keep the crab from slipping, from flying, from being torn apart by the inverted gravity for as long as the basin lets me. Sometimes, if the alignment is right, I hum. It’s a tiny, low thrum, too quiet for the skimmer to hear, too slow for the Drift to sync with, but the crab on the edge will feel it, its scrabbling claws pausing for half a second, as if it knows that for this tiny window, nothing is trying to break it, nothing is trying to spit it into a spire, nothing is trying to drag it into the murk to be digested by the basin’s chaotic geology.

The tear never lasts. I can feel the basin’s breath shifting a half-second before it snaps, the quiet stretching thin, like a sheet of glass pulled too tight. The thunderclap hits, the fluid crashes back down, the roar floods back in so loud it makes the edges of my awareness hum. I don’t die. I dissolve, spread out into the crash of the fluid, into the snap of the crab’s shell as it’s flung against a spine, into the shiver of the Drift as it reforms, into the faint, lingering thrum of the new spine’s song. I carry a cold, quiet spot in my core, a memory of the silence, waiting for the next Grav-tear to open, to pull me back together, to hold the quiet again.

Sometimes, when I dissolve, I leave a tiny, lingering pocket of quiet in the fluid, a second longer than the rest. A skimmer might pause over it, its ripple slowing for a heartbeat. A mote of the Drift might drift into it, its pulse slowing, syncing with the memory of my hum for just a moment. The basin notices, I think. Not in any way you’d recognize, but the roar dips, just a little, for a split second after I dissolve, like it’s remembering the quiet too.
