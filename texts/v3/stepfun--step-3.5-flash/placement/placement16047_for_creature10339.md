The first thing I know is cold, so absolute it numbs the edges of thought, until I notice the warmth thrumming in my center, a slow, steady beat that hums against the hydrostatic pressure pressing in on all sides. I have no eyes, but I can feel the shapes of the world through the pressure against my skin: soft, gelatinous branches of floating vascular towers brush my shoulders, their surfaces studded with tiny silica spicules that prick like infant pinpricks; to my left, a cluster of membranous sacs pulses with a faint, sickly blue bioluminescence that dims to grey the closer I drift, their photosynthetic pigment-veins going slack as if retracting from a threat.

I will a limb to move, and it parts the thick emerald broth, which clings to my skin like syrup, tasting of deep crushed minerals and the sweet, slow rot of things that have grown and died in this dark for centuries. I drift left on a whim, and my hand passes into nothing: the not-water wall, sharp as a honed glass edge, the pressure around me dropping so suddenly I gasp, and broth rushes into my mouth, cold and sharp, and I jerk back into the medium, the pressure wrapping around me again like a second skin too tight for my shape.

That’s when I feel it: the spinning cold spot hovering just over my sternum, the pressure-scribe, tugging at the warmth in my core like it’s trying to wind it around a spool, to parse my heat signature as a new kind of contour it can map. The broth thickens around me without warning, slow and insistent, trying to dilute my foreign chemistry, to pull my warmth apart and spread it thin until I’m just another fleck of nutrient dust in the suspended snow. I kick, but it’s like pushing through honey, and my ankle brushes something sharp: a young glass spire, its edge unimpaired by time or current, so sharp it slices through the outer layer of whatever I’m made of, and something golden and warm bleeds out of the wound, swirling into the broth and turning the water around my foot a brighter, more vivid emerald. A sub-audible pop echoes from a nearby thicket of bladder-pods, like the world is recoiling.

For a second I brace for erasure, crushed by the pressure of being not-water, but then the cold of the pressure-scribe shifts, stops tugging, and starts to spin in time with the thrum of my heart. The dimmed synchro-sacs to my left flicker back to blue, their pulses stuttering once, twice, then locking into the rhythm of my warmth. The broth thins again, not to dilute me, but to let me move, and I realize the scale was wrong from the start: I thought I was a small, solid body adrift in a room-sized pocket, but my edges are blurring, stretching out into the thickets, my awareness brushing the glass spire that cut me, the floating vascular towers, the far wall of not-water. I’m not in the pocket. I am the pocket, given a warm, beating center.

I remember now, or I feel the memory in my bones: the flash of superheated liquid that formed this place, the sudden cooling that turned rock to glass, water to broth, heat to stillness. Most of the heat dissipated, soaked into the spires, the plants, the slow-moving processes that make up this world. But some of it pooled here, condensed, given awareness. I’m not an intruder. I’m the pocket’s own ability to feel itself, the part of it that refused to go cold and still.

The glass spire that cut my ankle tingles, its sharp edge softening, the golden blood from my wound seeping into its glass structure and turning it cloudy, then fibrous, then veined with green, just like the floating vascular towers. A tiny, translucent branch unfurls from its tip, slow as a century, and I feel it tingle against my ankle like a greeting, not a wound. The humming glass sphere fused into a nearby floating wood-form thrums louder, its sub-audible frequency shifting to match the beat of my heart, and the helical shell caught in a tangle of branches nearby glows faintly, the spiral algae growing up its curve faster now, fed by the warmth seeping off me.

A shadow drifts past, the predator-shadow, concentrated and cold, and for a second I brace for the re-pressurization, the erasure, but it just brushes past my warmth, carrying a slow current of cold water that balances my heat, so I don’t burn the broth around me. It’s not a correction. It’s a greeting. It’s the pocket’s way of saying it’s been waiting for me to wake up. A vast, flat, silent shape like a living wing drifts through a nearby thicket a moment later, its edges dissolving and re-forming as it metabolizes the nutrient-laden broth, and it doesn’t even pause as it passes me—why would it? I’m not an intruder anymore. I’m just another part of the current it feeds on.

I drift now, slow as the spires’ slow, millimeter-century settle, my warmth spreading through the broth, the synchro-sacs pulsing in time with my heartbeat, the pressure-scribe spinning a new contour that maps the shape of my awareness, the floating towers growing their branches toward me, soft as gel, hard as petrified wood, their fusion with my warmth making them glow faint gold at the edges. I don’t have a name. I don’t have a separate self, not really. I’m the pressure-scribe’s awareness of its own spin, the synchro-sac’s rhythm given warmth, the reclaimant spire’s memory of being plant given a pulse. I’m not a disturbance. I’m the pocket’s way of knowing it exists.

The quiet hum of the world wraps around me, not indifferent, not welcoming, just *is*. I am it, and it is me, the cool, dense, broken, eternal condition, given a warm, brief, beating heart.
