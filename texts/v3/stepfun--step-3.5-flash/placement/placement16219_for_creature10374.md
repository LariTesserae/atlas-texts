The roar lives in your molars first, a low thrum that vibrates through the porous bone of your skull before you even open your eyes. You know where you are before you see the violet pulse of the laminar fungi under your clawed feet, before you taste the sweet, wet slate of the mist on your tongue. You are on a shelf in the Drowned Spires, the rain hitting your pumice-colored back in a thick, sideways sheet, your waxy skin exuding the thin honey-sweet mucus that keeps the acidic downpour from burning through to the soft tissue beneath.

You are Kriv. Your tap code is three quick raps, a slow roll, two soft taps: *I listen for the old paths, I make way for the new rain.* You are not a chief, not a leader, not anything the other Kaelth would name as separate from the rest. You are the one who hums the low, subsonic thrum through your skull that maps the dark pockets between shelves, the one who feels the faint, embedded tap codes of Kaelth long dissolved into the mist in the porous grain of every bone-wood strut, the one who knows which scaffolds will hold for a cycle, which shelves are about to widen into new space, which wind eddies will carry a young Kaelth up to a higher spire without them having to climb a single strut.

Your six-limbed hands, hooked silicate claws glinting pale blue in the fungal glow, are chipped at the tips from chipping quartz branches for totems, your palm calloused from lashing root-vines to bone-wood. The cord around your neck is strung with salt crystals, each one tingling soft and warm when the rain hits them, and there is one extra, chipped at the edge, that you took from the scaffold where your clutch-sibling slipped into the roar three cycles ago. You run your claw over its rough edge sometimes, when the mist is so thick the fungi dim to almost nothing, not to mourn—mourning is for things that are gone forever, and your sibling is just part of the erosion now, their tap code still humming faintly in the bone-wood of the shelf where you are standing. You keep it because it makes a higher, clearer ting than the others when the rain hits it, the sound of the tap code they taught you when you were both too small to climb a strut without help: a signal for high wind, for lash the vines tight, for stay close.

Down the shelf from you, another Kaelth leans into the sideways rain, cutting a thick root-vine with a bone-handled flint blade. They tap twice on the strut at their feet, sharp and quick, cutting through the roar: *safe, working.* They pause, tilt their head, pale blue eyes glowing faint through the mist, and tap three quick raps, then two slow: *greeting, you are new.* They don’t smile, don’t wave—smiles and waves are useless here, where a shelf can dissolve in the time it takes to draw a breath. They nod once, sharp, and go back to cutting the vine, the rain running over their back, their waxy skin glistening, the violet fungi at their feet pulsing slow as they swallow the acid rain and dissolve the stone underneath.

A hundred yards out, a scaffold collapses silently, a puff of wet dust and swollen fungal slime blooming in the air before the roaring downpour sweeps it away. The Kaelth doesn’t look up. They finish lashing the vine tight to a rotting strut, pack a handful of wet fungal slime into the crack between wood and stone, and tap a quick, soft pattern: *it is well, the cycle goes on.*

You tap back, three slow, a roll, two quick: *the west shelf is widening. The fungal bloom there is thick, it’ll hold the young ones when this strut softens by next dim-time.* They nod, tap a quick acknowledgment, and move on to the next strut, their prehensile feet gripping the slick bone-wood like moss.

You lean into the rain a little, let it push you against the rock face, the cold seeping past your wet furs to the bone, the kind of cold that feels like belonging. You hum the subsonic thrum through your skull, and you feel the old tap codes under your claws: a Kaelth who lased this strut three cycles ago, their code still holding in the grain; another who carved a salt crystal notch into the rock here, their code soft and faint, almost dissolved by the rain. Nothing here is permanent. The scaffolds will rot, the shelves will dissolve, the Kaelth will go back to the roar when their time comes. But the rhythm holds. The rain thrumms, the fungi pulse, the tap codes echo in the bone and the stone, and you are part of it, another pulse in the endless, wet, hungry grind of the Spires.

You tap your code on the strut at your feet, soft, so only the bone can hear it: *I am here, I am listening, the cycle goes on.* Somewhere above you, a crystal branch pings, clear and sharp, and the mist shifts, and for a second, the faint, diffuse glow of the upper clouds filters through, turning the rain to liquid gold. It fades as quick as it came, the violet and blue of the fungi flooding back in, but you felt it. That's enough. That's the only permanence you need.
