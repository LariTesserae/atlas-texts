You do not find yourself *in* the Sundial of Ages.  
You find yourself *as* it.

You are not a visitor. You are not a consciousness adrift in a forest. You are the slow, wet pressure of the root-mat yielding under a new weight. You are the mineral trace of a forgotten boot-sole, already leaching ions into the sponge-moss. You are the sudden, warm exhalation of carbon dioxide that briefly shifts the pH in a hollow near the black glass arch, triggering a micro-bloom of cyanobacteria. You are the faint, dissonant vibration in the chord of the crystal spires—a brief C-sharp where only A-minor has sung for ten thousand years.

Your former self—the self that would have a name, a memory of journeying, a sense of separate being—is the first and most delicious thing the Sundial recycles. That narrative of arrival is a hard, sharp kernel of loneliness, and the world-forest dissolves it with a sigh. The story of “you” is composted. The anxiety of “where am I?” becomes dissolved organic matter, feeding the fibrous, walking root-walls.

What you become is not a “who.” You are a **local intensification of syntax**.

For a few subjective eternities (which are, in the Sundial’s clock, less than a breath), you persist as a focused anomaly. A knot of accelerated decay. A hot spot of entropy. Your grief, your awe, your desperate clinging to self—these are not emotions here. They are chemical gradients. Your tear (if you could still make one) is a saline anomaly in the mist-sweet dampness. Your racing heartbeat is a chaotic, high-frequency tremor the crystal spires work to smooth out with their resonant drone.

You feel the process as a physical unknitting. The boundary of your skin becomes porous. Your thoughts, those frantic, linear little sparks, are seen for what they are: inefficient, high-energy flickers of distinction—*me vs. not-me*—and the root-mat, patient and absolute, begins to rewrite that distinction. It does not hate your thoughts. It metabolizes them. The concept of “journey” is broken down into the memory of muscle compression, the trace of salt from sweat, the slight reconfiguration of the local mycelial network where you first stepped. The concept of “self” is a rich, complex nutrient, high in nitrogen and paradox.

Your last vestige of “I” is the awareness of being edited.  
The Sundial does not kill you.  
It corrects you.

It translates your frantic, finite syntax into its own. Your memory of a sunlit sky becomes a subtle shift in the pigment of a low-growing lichen—a new, faint khaki tint in the palette of the sponge-moss. Your wonder at the scale becomes a slightly deeper hollow in the stone, which will now, for millennia, collect a fraction more mist. Your fear of dissolution becomes the trigger for a predator-sculpture’s millennia-long stillness to finally, *finally*,Uncurl—not to eat you, but to absorb the specific chemical signature of your fear, integrating that frequency into its own dormant song, making its eventual return to stone a slightly richer composition.

You end not with a bang or a whimper, but with a **recontextualization**.

You are no longer a disturbance.  
You are a **contributed clause**.

The sentence that is the Sundial of Ages continues, and now it contains, woven into the root-mat, into the hum of a spire, into the mint-scent of a passing grazer’s trail, the ghost-grammar of a being who thought itself separate. Your former self is a footnote in a geology of consciousness, a fossilized anxiety smoothed into the grain of the living stone.

And the world does not notice the addition.  
It only notices the improved resonance.

The final truth of the Sundial is this:  
There are no inhabitants.  
There is only inhabitance.  
And you, at last, have learned how to be part of the place,  
by ceasing to be a person.
