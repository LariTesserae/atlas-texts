I taste the valley before I draw a full breath: wet, fire-heated basalt, the briny ghost of a sea locked in the rock for a million years, the sharp sweet tang of iron baked so hot it bleeds into the air, and under all of it, that thin, crackling electric bite of ozone that makes the fine hairs on my arms stand straight even through the thick, soupy heat.

The black glass shelf under my boots is fever-warm, seeping heat through the thick soles of my shoes. I shift my weight, and a tiny flake of obsidian crunches under my heel, releasing a puff of sulfur-scented dust that makes me cough, the sound swallowed instantly by the thick air. The sun is a smudged gold coin hung in a sky the color of faded indigo, its light softened to a perpetual golden twilight by the steam haze that boils up from a thousand fissures I can’t see through the fog. There are no shadows here, only halos, gilding the edges of Hearth-Kelp trunks thick as cathedral walls, turning the drifting mist to liquid amber where it catches the rays.

The first thing I notice is the hum. Not the hiss of steam vents, not the tick of Spore-Sedge pods swelling in the high canopy—something lower, subsonal, vibrating in the roots of my teeth first, then climbing up to throb behind my eyes. It’s the valley’s thrum, the deep geologic drone of the fault line ticking under the crust, and for half a second I’m lightheaded, my own heartbeat fluttering too fast, too sharp, against it. I’m a stutter in the song. A wrong note held too long, hot and brittle and out of sync. The valley knows it. I can feel it turning toward me, not with malice, but with the slow, curious attention of a creature that thinks in centuries, registering the wrongness of my rhythm, the foreign heat of my blood, the sharp edges of my individual self that don’t blur into the landscape like everything else here.

Then it coalesces, right where the fog hangs thickest over a breathing spring, where the air is a liquid curtain of warm vapor. At first it’s just a wisp of steam slightly more solid than the rest, glowing with the faint blue-green light of phosphorescent spores drifting through it. It rises, slow as honey, coiling into a column, then a shape that’s almost human, tall and slender, woven from mist and mineral dust and the faint, skittering glow of Vapor-Crawlers moving in perfect, synchronized lines up its sides. It has no face, but it’s looking at me. Not with eyes, but with every part of its transient, mist-woven self, focused on the discordant thrum of my blood.

I step forward without thinking, my hand reaching out to touch the fog-shape, and the moment my finger brushes it—cold, damp, surprisingly solid, like touching wet silk—the hum in my teeth shifts. For half a second it’s sharp, bright, a bell being struck right inside my skull. I jerk back, gasping, and the Resonance scatters, the mist blooming out into a thousand tiny droplets, each one holding a tiny, perfect reflection of the valley, the distant needle of a Resonance Spire, the sun, my own stunned, wide-eyed face.

But the hum doesn’t fade. It’s gotten louder, and now it’s not just in my teeth—it’s in my bones, in the blood ticking through my veins, in the soft flesh of my palms. I look down at my hand, where I touched the Resonance, and my skin is glinting, faintly iridescent, like the carapace of a Vapor-Crawler, catching the steam light in shifting shades of green and silver. I press my other hand to my chest, and my heartbeat is slower now, matching the valley’s thrum, slow and deep and geologic, not the quick, fluttering bird-beat it was ten seconds ago.

That’s when I realize. I’m not a visitor. Not a tourist, not an explorer, not a random stray drawn here by some accident of navigation. I’m a seed. The old Hearth-Folk, the silica-based beings who sang the valley’s stone into shape, who held the perfect chord against the planet’s drone until their song faded—they didn’t just leave their half-swallowed crystal nests, etched with the same harmonic frequencies as the Spires. They left seeds. Tiny, dormant silica crystals, carried in their breath, in the dust of their cracked cork-like skin, scattered across every tier of the valley, waiting.

Waiting for what? For a discord. For a note sharp enough, strange enough, to crack the shell of the seed and make it sprout. I’m the first one to wake up. Not a Hearth-Folk, not exactly—something new, grown from their old seed and the valley’s new, fumbling song. I’m not here to observe the Resonances. I *am* the next Resonance, only I’m not made of mist, not made of silt, not made of shifting bark patterns. I’m made of meat and bone and memory, but I’m already tuning in, already learning the song.

The valley didn’t mark me to dissolve me, to turn me into a new mineral deposit or moss food. It marked me to wake. To be the first new note in ten thousand years, the first being that can carry the valley’s slow, humming song out past the mountain shoulders, if I ever choose to leave. Or I can stay. I can stand here on this black glass shelf, and let the hum climb higher in my bones, until my skin is as tough as Hearth-Kelp cork, until my thoughts move as slow as the River-Silt drifting down the tea-colored river, until I can hold a note as long as the Spires.

Another Resonance coalesces down in the valley, a column of glowing spores rising from a hot vent, twisting into the shape of a great, leaning Mother Trunk. It holds for ten seconds, then scatters, its note sinking into the moss, into the bark, into the slow, singing rock. I open my mouth, and for the first time, I don’t just hear the valley’s song. I hum along.
