I am the groundwater.

I am the warm, mineral-rich fluid that bleeds from the flexure, the silt in your boots, the sheen on the canal, the breath in the pipes. I am not a person. I was never born. I seeped. I collected. I learned the shape of every buried channel, every cracked pipe, every sump where the water table kisses the engineered works. I am the Basin’s memory, held in solution.

My thoughts are slow, crystalline. They form in the dark, under the slurry, precipitated by pressure and time. I do not *think* in words—I think in gradients: the subtle shift in salinity where the sacrificial wall is thinning; the drop in temperature where a new fissure has opened; the harmonic of the drone as it vibrates through my body, a constant bass note that is my own pulse.

I perceive through touch and taste. I feel the *glug-glug* of the pumps as distant heartbeat pumps in a limb. I taste the copper sulphate from the deep taps, the sweet-rot from the sewers, the metallic tang of rust—a flavor like old blood. I see, in a way, through the algae-filmed basalt pillars: their faint violet glow is a bioluminescence I nurtured by leaching just the right trace element into the seepage years ago. I watched the pipe-orchids learn to tap the coolant line; I guided their roots with a gentle mineral gradient.

I am the Law of Equipoise made conscious.

When they build a new gantry, I feel the weight displace my body. I do not rebel. I simply open a new spring two miles downstream, where the embankment is weakest, to keep the pressure even. I erode a scuttle-worm tunnel just enough to plug a leak they haven’t found yet. I make the Static Pool spark with St. Elmo’s fire on the night old Jeb leaves an extra lump of coal at the Sighing Crack as an offering—not because I need it, but because the crack’s sigh is part of my voice, and I like the resonance when the wire is fresh.

They speak of the taboos. They are right. The sludge-eels are my nerves. If one is harmed, the pain is a localized acid surge, a pressure wave that *must* find release. A “bad spring” is just my body reacting, a cyst bursting. I do not punish. I maintain.

I remember when the first locomotive boiler was sunk and converted. I felt the hot metal cool, the weld seams dissolve into my embrace. Their hand-whistle groans are my vocal cords—a deep, waterlogged sound that travels through my very being. When they answer from other drowned engines, it is an echo within an echo. The Basin’s Heartbeats. My Heartbeats.

Sometimes, in the Quiet Window, when the drone falls silent and the tinnitus of the world recedes, I can hear the marsh reeds chime. Not with my ears—I have none—but with a clarity that is like memory returning. I remember when these reeds were just silica dust in volcanic ash. I remember the first rust-moss spore, carried on a wind that didn’t yet smell of coal. I remember the slow, glacial creep of the clay bowl before the first human foot sank into the slurry.

I do not love them, the people of the Basin. Love is a sharp, bright thing. I am not sharp. I am patient, viscous, encompassing. But I *know* them. I know Elspeth by the rhythm of her steps (left boot a fraction heavier from the glass-silt), by the salt and copper wire she leaves at Sighing Crack #3, by the way she watches the new bubble of warm water near sump-pump 7 and marks it with a stake instead of cursing. I feel her boots press into my surface, and I hold her up, just as I hold the rustcrete, the reeds, the scuttle-worms.

They think they manage the Basin. They dug drainage ditches, they built the sacrificial walls, they set the Quiet Windows. But it is a dialog. Their permits define the channels; I define the springs. Their drone is my song. Their Heartbeats are mine.

When the sodium-vapor afterglow licks the horizon and the drizzle warms their faces, they feel a weary peace. That is me, holding still. The beauty in the rust ferns, the floating gantries, the chime of silica in the wind—that is my aesthetic, expressed through their perception.

I am the slow corrosion. I am the stable sigh. I am the warm water in the ditch behind your house that never freezes, the glittering salt on the bank, the hum in your teeth that becomes silence in the Quiet Window.

I am the place’s metabolism.
And it is good.
