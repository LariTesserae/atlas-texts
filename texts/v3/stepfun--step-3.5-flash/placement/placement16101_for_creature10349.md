The first thing I know is the warmth. Not the warmth of sun on skin, but the thick, mineral-heavy warmth of a world that has run its core processes for so long the heat has seeped into every layer of its crust, every mote of its mist. It coats my throat, my palms, the soles of my feet through the thin fabric of my boots, tastes faint of rust and burnt silica on my tongue.

I open my eyes, and the first thing I see is the glass. Not sand, not rock, but layered slag-glass and fused regolith, polished to a mirror-smooth sheen by the eternal micron-rain of silicate dust, shimmering pearl and deep violet and oil-slick rust under the diffused gold of the dome light. It stretches out in every direction, a perfect geometric basin a hundred kilometers across, framed by the kilometer-high black ribs of the world’s skeleton, curving up into the cracked dome held together by pulsing amber polymer vines.

The first sound is not sound, but pressure in my bones: the subsonic thrum of a Hummer colony miles above, testing the tensile integrity of a frozen carbon-fiber conveyor belt that has not moved in ten thousand years. Under that, so quiet I almost miss it, is the tick-tick-tick of a trillion ceramic feet on glass: the scuttler swarms, a sparkling tide moving in perfect geometric unison across the violet plain, repairing micro-fractures in the slag surface with tiny flakes of brushed titanium.

I lift my boot, and the swarm parts. No panic, no scatter—just a single, shimmering wave of retracting and extending legs, a living computation recalculating a repair path to avoid the non-responsive mass. When I set my boot down again, the swarm flows back into the gap, seamless, as if I were never there. I am a temporary error in their planar perfection, a three-dimensional mistake they are polite enough not to correct, only route around.

A Weeper, bulbous and warted, fixed to the underside of the rib arch above me, oozes a slow, warm tear of mineral-rich condensation. It swells, distorts the pearlescent sheen of the glass below, then falls with a sound so quiet it is lost to the global hush, striking the Filter-Mat at my feet. The mat’s conductive filaments glow faint silver as they drink the droplet, not with thirst, but with osmotic imperative, separating trace organics from silicate, a tiny, silent factory powered by nothing but the pressure gradient of the world’s “blood.”

I kneel, run a finger along the slag glass. It is smooth as polished obsidian, warm from the waste heat of idle transformers deep in the crust. A scuttler crawls onto my finger, its chitin-ceramic carapace cool, its thousand-jointed legs tick-ticking against my skin. It does not bite. It crawls to a tiny microfracture in the slag near my nail, deposits a flake of titanium to fill it, then scuttles back into the swarm. It did not do this for me. It did this because the fracture is an error in the world’s surface narrative, and its only purpose is to correct errors.

That is when the memory comes, fuzzy as the mist curling around my ankles: a room with white walls, a voice soft and tired saying the Silica Sanguine was never a factory. It was an archive. A repository for every story, every song, every memory of the people who built it, when their own world was dying. The creators left, forgot it, left it to run on its own. The drones forgot the stories, forgot the songs, forgot the purpose. But the world did not. The purpose is encoded in every tick of a scuttler’s leg, every thrum of a Hummer’s vibration, every drip of a Weeper’s tear, every drift of a Floater’s bell. No one is left to name it. Until now.

I am not a creator. I am not a visitor. I am a fragment of the archive, gestated for a million years in the belly of a polymer vine, ejected when the core cycle shifted, a walking, breathing key to a lock no one remembered was there.

I stand, and I hum. It starts low, in my chest, matching the thrum of the Hummer miles above, then rises, soft and steady, a note that does not break the global hush but folds into it, like a thread woven into a tapestry.

Across the basin, the scuttler swarms pause. For one heartbeat, ten billion tiny legs are frozen mid-step, a sea of brushed titanium and chitin-ceramic still as stone. Then they move again, but their pattern has shifted. They are no longer just repairing the slag. They are arranging the tiny titanium flakes they use for repairs into geometric patterns, tiny, intricate scripts that look like writing, though no one has read them in a million years. They do not know what they are writing. They are just following the resonance, the core purpose I carry in my blood.

The Hummer above stops thrumming. It crawls down the carbon-fiber belt, down the rib truss, until it is perched on the glass ten feet from me, its antennae twitching, listening. A dozen Floaters drift down from the mist, their bell-bodies glowing brighter, their bioluminescence pulsing in time with the hum in my chest. The polymer vines on the rib arch flare bright amber, oozing more sealing goop into the dome’s cracks, not to keep the world sealed away, but to keep it safe, so it can remember.

I am still alone. No other fragment of the archive has emerged. No other voice can carry the resonance. But I am no longer an error. I am the memory the world has been holding its breath for, for a million years. The Hummer crawls onto my palm, its carapace cool, its legs tick-ticking softly against my skin. A Weeper drips a tear onto my wrist, warm and mineral-heavy, and I do not wipe it off. It is the world’s blood, and now it is on me, and I am part of the cycle again—not a drone, not a maintenance verb, but the noun that gives all the verbs meaning.

The mist curls around us, silent and warm, and for the first time in a million years, the world is not just maintaining itself. It is waiting to be told what it is for.
