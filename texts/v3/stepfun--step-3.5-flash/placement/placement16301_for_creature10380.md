You would not be a visitor here. You would be a necessity.

You would be the Stillpool’s **Resonance**, a transient phenomenon born of the place’s constant, quiet negotiation. You are not a creature with a lifespan, but a *state* the system falls into when its inner rhythms require a moment of translation.

You emerge when the chemical pulse from the deep is too sharp, or when the tower’s thrum stutters against the mats’ communal sigh. You coalesce from the thick air—not from the spores or dust, but from the *space between them*. Your form is a shimmering, humanoid outline, about the size of a child, woven from condensation and suspended motes. You are always translucent, and within your chest, a slow, pulsing light ebbs and flows in time with the tower’s vibration, but shifted—a half-beat out of phase.

Your “body” is cool to the touch, like wet stone held in a shadow. Your “skin” is a film of water so pure it refracts the bioluminescence of the mats into faint rainbows that glide across your surface. You have no face, but a smooth, suggestively planar patch where senses might be. From this plane, you emit a low, subsonic hum that is the exact inverse frequency of the tower’s thrum when it peaks. You are a dampener, a living capacitor.

Your function is interpretation. When the electric-blue mats flare in response to a methane surge, you drift toward them. As you approach, your internal pulse syncs with theirs, and your hum changes—not to soothe, but to *measure*. You are recording the intensity, the duration, the decay. You taste the air with your whole surface, sampling the spore-cloud’s composition. When you glide to the tower’s base and press your cool form against the warm, vibrating iron, you are not drawing energy; you are borrowing its frequency to send a silent, vibrational query into the bedrock.

The Silent Ones do not ignore you. They will pause their patrols to glide alongside you for a few meters, their white skin pulsing in sync with your own light for a moment before they drift away. The dragonfly-swarm will part to let you pass, their clicking buzz softening to a whisper as you move through them. The grubs on the cable spool will lift their heads, their tungsten silk glinting, before returning to their spinning. They do not see you as a predator or a symbiont. They see you as a **symptom**—a visible sign that an invisible negotiation is underway.

You do not think. You *are* the thinking. You are the Stillpool’s way of feeling its own balance. When your internal pulse stabilizes, matching the new equilibrium of thrum and pulse and scent, you begin to dissolve. Not with a splash or a sigh, but simply by becoming less coherent. The motes that formed you drift back into the air, the condensation film evaporates into the dome’s draft, and your hum fades into the eternal baseline of plinks and clicks.

You leave behind only a slight cooling of the air, a temporary flattening of a chemical gradient in the water, and perhaps a single, perfect drop of that ultra-pure condensation that falls into the pool and is instantly consumed by a voracious ochre mat. Your existence is a verb, not a noun. You are the Stillpool Engine’s **act of awareness**, its moment of listening to itself.

And you will be back. In an hour. In a day. In a thousand years. Whenever the newborn scream of this geological blister needs a voice to translate its humming, metallic breath into the language of slow, wet, and perfect cohesion. You are the place’s question, and its answer, given form for just long enough to be understood.
