You find yourself not on the mesa, but *in* its rhythm. You are the trembling in the stone before it settles. The wet whisper in the root-mat before it rots.

You are the **Memory-Keeper of the Unmade**.

Your name is not a sound. It is a pressure in the water, a specific pattern of soft taps on a growing mineral wall. Outsiders who try to speak it gulp brine. To the people of the Breathing Stone, you are *the one who holds the shape of forgetting*.

Your body is adapted to the slow world. Your skin is not waterproof; it is *permeable*. You drink the air-water through your pores and excrete a fine, saline mist that feeds the lichen on your shoulders. Your eyes are large, liquid-filled, useless in the open air-sea but capable of seeing the faint bioluminescence of the stone’s internal growth pulses—the slow, red-infrared glow of mineral accretion in the dark. You are **tall and thin** (1.9), your bones light and filled with tiny channels, like the vesicular basalt of the mesa itself. You move with excruciating slowness, each step placed over the course of a full, deep sigh of the water-wall.

Your role is a profound contradiction. The culture believes that to remember is to resist the great, gentle dissolution. Memory is a form of theft—stealing a moment from the endless flow back into formlessness. Yet someone must remember *how* to remember, so that the act of forgetting can be done rightly. You are the living archive of *processes*: the sequence of pruning a vine so it curls into a doorway; the exact season to chip a weeping stone so it hardens into a shelf without creating a fatal flaw; the oral map of where the root-mites are thickest, so a floor doesn’t collapse during a ceremony. You do not remember *events*—who said what, who loved whom. You remember *techniques*, *rhythms*, *patterns of growth and decay*.

Your tool is a **ledger**. It is not made of dead parchment. It is a slab of the mesa’s newest, softest stone, still weeping mineral water. You carve into it with a shark’s tooth. But the carvings are not pictures or words. They are **topographies**—tiny, intricate landscapes of grooves and pits. A ceremony for laying a foundation is a series of concentric pits. A myth about the first vine is a branching pattern that matches a real vine’s growth habit. The ledger is alive. As you carve, the stone begins to harden around the grooves. Weeks later, new mineral growth will fill some pits, obscure some lines. You must constantly revisit, re-carve, re-interpret the fading topography. Your knowledge is not stored; it is *performed*, over and over, on a surface that actively works to erase it.

You live in a **sealed chamber** deep within the mineral gardens. The air is still, the only sound the deep, subsonic groan of the stone around you. Your walls are a perfect, porous lattice of guided stone-coral, filtering the perpetual drip into clear, slow-filling pools. It is a place of **extreme density** (2.1) but also of profound **emptiness** (0.3 fauna). No one visits you. Your presence is a necessary vortex in the slow current of the place, a point where memory eddies against the flow of forgetting.

Your conflict is quiet, geological. A young vine-tender, impatient with the old ways, has tried to force a new shape. He used a sharp, dead shard of volcanic glass (a forbidden, *eroded* thing) to cut a vine. The wound is not healing; the vine is dying, weeping a dark, sickly sap. The community wants to cut it out and let it dissolve. But your ledger shows a similar wound from seven generations ago—one that, if left to the slow cycle, eventually birthed a rare, sweet fruit that sustained the settlement through a lean season. The pattern is ambiguous. To follow the ledger’s ancient topography means to let the sick vine persist, possibly poisoning others. To reject it means to reject the only guide you have.

You sit before your stone ledger, your liquid eyes seeing the fading carvings not as lines, but as a memory of light-pulses in the stone’s core. The water-wall sighs. The stone around you groans, a vibration that travels up through your porous bones. You must decide: do you re-carve the old pattern, forcing the present to conform to a topography that is already half-dissolved? Or do you carve a new pattern, becoming a source of *new* memory in a world that believes all true knowledge is merely remembered forgetting?

You are the still point in the breathing stone. The keeper of the map for a people who believe the only true path is to lose the map. Your greatest fear is not death—the slow dissolution back into the warm soup is a homecoming. Your greatest fear is that your ledger will become *too clear*, that the patterns will stop fading, that you will accidentally create a *permanent* memory. Then you would not be a Memory-Keeper. You would be a Monument. And monuments are the ultimate lie here. They are the only thing the water-wall does not eventually reclaim.

You dip your shark’s tooth into the weeper-vine’s sick sap. It is thick. It does not mix with the water. You hover your hand over your stone ledger. The faint, red pulse of the growing stone beneath the surface guides your decision.

What do you carve?
