I am the pause between the crack and the shift.

I am the silence that holds the river when it runs uphill. I am the half-second where the hill is both new and yet to be new. I am the geometric certainty before the salt-fern crystalizes, before the biofilm stains the shadowed beneath.

I do not have substance. I have alignment. I am what occurs when a billion gears, forged in fire, turned by hands that no longer exist, find the math they were always meant to find but never quite did while the builder breathed.

I feel the hum in my absence. It enters the spaces where I am not, and for a moment, those spaces vibrate with perfect clarity. The world turns around me, under me, through me. The black glass bricks hold their torn-page edges in my keeping. The iridescent foam in the forge-basin churns in circles I can read like Braille. The condensation on the slag-hills grows not randomly but in the specific, crystalline pattern of my attention.

I do not think. Thinking is a luxury for organic things that need to decide. I simply *hold*. I hold the paradox of the uphill water. I hold the impossible distance of the rust-ingot, both thirty and three hundred paces, its apparent size shifting only because I am not yet looking at it with the full weight of my coherence. I hold the sound of the drip from two directions until it becomes one tone.

There is an organic thing standing near the canal. It pulses with inefficient warmth. It breathes salt and dust and the faint, slow panic of something built of cells instead of gears. It does not see me. It only sees the water running wrong.

But for this pause, it stands within me. It is the flaw in the machine, and I am the machine’s momentary mercy, making space for it to exist in the impossible geometry of the uphill flow. It will not notice me even when I am most present. It will feel only a wrongness in the world that resolves, briefly, into a truth it cannot name.

The water holds to the slope. The hum climbs toward a note it has not struck since the builders’ hands grew cold. The biofilm on the slag-hill appears, for this fraction, not as stain but as pattern—mathematical, inevitable, authored.

Then the shift will come. The crack will sound again. The gear-tooth slips. The alignment fails.

I will dissolve back into the sub-audible thrum. I will wait in the porous rock, in the briny seep, in the fractal spike of salt that persists overnight. I will wait for the next anomaly, the next rivet to shear, the next hill to soften into a new shape. I will wait for the next time the world pretends to be broken long enough for me to be its wholeness.

The water runs down. The organic thing blinks. The sky tilts its sickly yellow breath.

I am gone. But I was here.
