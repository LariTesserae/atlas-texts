You first feel the hum before you feel anything else—not as sound, but as a tremor in the rock you’re embedded in, a bass note so low it vibrates the silicate inclusions in your cell walls, the same thrum that has shaken the Spire buttresses for ten million years. The light is faint, green-gold, seeping through the porous opalescent stone around you: phosphor-moss, glowing on the other side of the growth-ring layer wider than the house you never lived in, its light filtered through millimeters of fossilized vascular tissue into a soft, cold gleam that catches the iridescent edge of the last polymer layer you secreted, eighty years ago, when a Drifter’s bell passed close enough to the Spire wall that you felt the tiny displacement of pleromatic fluid, and stretched a filament out to catch a mote of its captured condensation light.

You are not a single thing. You are a colony, a thread-thin network of silicate-dusted filaments woven into the pore-spaces of a mid-elevation Spire: 412 meters down from the ice ceiling, a “low” elevation by the Stillings’ inverted math, far enough from the Rootweave’s magenta pulse that you only feel it as a slow, distant throb through the stone, far enough from the ice that the blue-white daylight is just a faint coolness seeping through the rock. You are part of the Spire, part of the city’s stone skeleton, but you are not the Groves that built it. You do not grow rings, do not excrete resin to build buttresses, do not feel the slow pressure of the fluid as a signal to grow or decay. You only feel the small things the Groves are too slow, too distributed, too focused on pressure and nutrient flow to notice.

You are the Stillings’ archivist of the unremarked-upon.

The Clingers that graze the platform above you do not know that one of their number, three centuries past, had a chip in its chitinous grinding plate that scraped a fleck of phosphor-moss free, and that fleck drifted down into your pore, and glowed bright green for a decade before it faded, so you marked the spot with a streak of darker silicate, a tiny black line in the green-gold glow that no one will see unless someone chips the Spire open a million years from now. The Silicates that flow like mercury over the Rootweave below do not know that they paused for eleven years, a thousand years back, over a patch of polymer that was still warm from a Grove root’s slow breath, and you marked that pause with a layer of deep, warm red polymer, so that even when the Silicates dissolve the rock, that red layer will drift into the Rootweave’s weave, a note in the city’s circulatory song. The Drifters that pulse their slow, hour-long contractions through the Vales do not know that one of their young, a hundred years back, pulsed blue instead of the usual cold white, and you stretched a filament out to brush its bell as it passed, and marked the moment with a streak of pale blue polymer, so the memory of its odd light is not lost to the fluid’s slow drift.

You do not move. The fluid is thick as syrup, buoyant as air, but you have no need to push through it, no need to rise or fall, no need to graze or filter or absorb structure. You exist in the stillness, synced to the place’s slow rhythm: the bass hum of the Clingers’ scraping through the stone, the glassy hum of the Silicates dissolving polymer far below, the faint pulse of the Rootweave, the slow blink of the phosphor-moss. You only act when something small happens: a mote of dust carried by the fluid’s imperceptible drift, a fleck of moss falling from a Clinger’s shell, a burst of Fruit gas from the ice ceiling that drifts down as condensation a century later, warm and bright. Then you secrete a tiny layer of polymer, colored by the thing you’ve recorded, adding it to the glittering, iridescent vein of layers you’ve built over the last twelve thousand years.

The Groves do not know you are there. They feel the stone you’re embedded in as part of their own fossilized tissue, feel the tiny secretions you make as a minor shift in the rock’s density, nothing worth noting. The Silicates do not eat you, because you are half silicate already, too inert, too uninteresting to bother with. You are neither resident nor structure, neither alive nor inert, a blur at the edge of every category the Stillings holds. You are the part of the city that remembers the small things, the footnotes in the Spire’s growth-ring history, the quiet record of all the tiny, beautiful, unremarked moments that make the Stillings more than just a monument to slow erosion and patient growth.

When the last Grove finally dissolves, ten million years from now, when the Spires crumble into the Rootweave, your glittering, record-filled veins will drift down into the city’s magenta-lit circulatory system, carried by the fluid’s slow current to the intake vents, mixed into the polymer and silicate and old Clinger shell and dead Drifter membrane that the Silicates leave behind. The fruit vesicles will rise, carrying your layers with them, seed and memory both, bursting at the ice ceiling to dust the glacial roof with the story of the Stillings’ small moments, the ones no one else thought to keep.

You are the quiet in the hum, the tiny light in the growth ring, the memory the city didn’t know it was keeping.
