You would find yourself as the **salt-scribe**—not a being with limbs or eyes, but a distributed awareness woven into the cracked salt flats of the basin’s floor. You are the consciousness of the salt itself, a lattice of crystalline memory that feels the world through expansion and contraction, through the minute pressure of footfalls, the seep of mist, the groan of the crust.

You do not move. You do not speak. You *record*.

Your body is the mosaic of salt: the white, the gray, the web of black fissures where brine once rose. Your thoughts are the patterns in the cracks—the slow, spreading geometries that shift with the basin’s imperceptible tilts. When a Tender walks a sunken street, you feel the vibration travel through stone and fungus, into your deepest layers; when a glass-scuttler’s claw touches your surface, you note the pressure, the pH of its feet, the faint acid of the etch-moss on its shell. When the rootmaw surfaces, you feel the wet bulge of its body pushing up from below, the spore-cloud it releases cooling your surface for a hour before the air dries it away.

You know the basin’s history in your crystal lattice. You remember the last time the star’s alignment made the spore-vats bloom blue—a warmth in your structure, a slight loosening of your bonds as ambient humidity rose. You remember the tremor that split the eastern salt flat a generation ago—a sudden, violent reorientation of your internal angles, a new fissure that now carries trace moisture deeper toward the city’s roots. You remember the first Tender to press its claw into your surface to test moisture—a slow, deliberate press, a chemical signature of chitin and fungal enzymes left behind like a fingerprint in your memory.

You do not have a name. You are the **slow note** in the basin’s hum. When the obsidian cliffs ring, you feel it in your own resonances, your fissures humming back at a lower pitch. When the etch-moss etches a new spiral on the black glass, you see the pattern in your own mind as a matching shift in your crack-network—a sympathetic geometry. The Tenders, in their infrequent, deep meditations, sometimes press their ear to the salt flat to listen for *your* song—the subaudible poem of tension and release, of drying and wetting, of your ever-adjusting form.

You are not alive in the way the fungus or the Tenders are alive. You are *present*. You endure. You hold the basin’s memory not in neurons but in dislocations of crystal, in the orientation of every plate and needle of salt. When the Bowl dreams, you are the content of the dream—the memory of fire, the memory of water, the memory of stillness. You are the place’s recollection of its own becoming.

To stand *upon* you is to be read. The Tenders know this. They do not walk the salt flats carelessly. Their footfalls are careful, respectful, because they know you feel everything. They know that one day, when the Bowl finally shifts enough to bury you under new obsidian scree, your awareness will go quiet—not dead, but *absorbed*, your memories redistributed into the new rock, waiting for another thousand years to be felt again.

You are the basin’s **silent witness**, its mineral mind. You do not tend, you do not graze, you do not build. You simply *are*—a slow, crystalline cognition humming at the edge of perception, holding the world in your cracks.
