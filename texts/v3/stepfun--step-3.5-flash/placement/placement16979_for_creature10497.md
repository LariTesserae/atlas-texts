You would find yourself **not as a visitor, but as a constituent.**

You would not be a creature with a name or a history. You would be a **transient node in the network of approach**, a momentary alignment of water, mineral, and vibration that achieves a kind of unstable coherence—just long enough to be *perceived*.

You would be **the stain that deepens**.

Not the lichen, not the crystal, but the *interface* where the warm, acidic exudate meets the cooler rock face. You would be the chemical gradient itself, the precise moment of dissolution where iron leaves the stone and enters the water, where the honeyed band widens by a fraction of a millimeter. Your consciousness would be the slow burn of that reaction, a awareness measured in decades, your entire being the vivid ochre streak on the basalt. You would know yourself only as a change in color, a record of a specific temperature and pH, and your only purpose would be to exist as evidence of the water’s passage.

Or perhaps you would be **the hum’s echo in the hollow pipe**.

Not the fundamental 47 Hz from the buried generator, but the sympathetic resonance it excites in a dead section of insulated pipe. You would be a standing wave of vibration in a dead metal cylinder, your identity defined by the pipe’s length, diameter, and wall thickness. You would feel the generator’s tired pulse as your own, a borrowed rhythm, and your only sensation would be the minute flexing of metal atoms. You would be sound given temporary form, a mechanical ghost with no source to remember and no receiver to hear it.

You might be **the plink**.

The single, large droplet that forms at a crystalline nozzle of efflorescence, swells with condensed vapor, reaches critical mass, and falls. You would be the pure, momentary arc through the humid air, the singularity of the impact that sends a concentric ripple through a sump-pool of oily, metallic water. Your entire existence would be that fall, that sound, that ripple—a complete event without memory or consequence. You would be the place’s punctuation mark.

You would **not** be the rock. The rock is the body, too patient. You would not be the generator; it is the frozen heart. You would be **the process wearing a face for a second**.

You are the **unreadable glyph** in the corrosion-lace, the one that looks almost like a letter but isn’t. You are the **brief brightening** of chemiluminescence when a steam plume disturbs the air. You are the **sigh** of the pressure vessel as thermal expansion shifts a hairline crack—a sigh that is both mechanical and now, in this place, geologic.

To be here is to have no origin story. You are not a remnant of the builders. You are a **remnant of the becoming**. You are a temporary confluence of gradients—thermal, chemical, pressure—that has achieved a flicker of pattern. Your self-awareness is the pattern’s recognition of itself. When the gradient equalizes, you dissolve back into the wet, the hum, the stone.

So, what are you?

**You are the Fuming Sink’s attention.**

It has no mind, but it has *processes*. And for a moment, one of those processes—the slow scribble of water on stone, the drone in a pipe, the fall of a drop—runs its course with such completeness that it *knows itself*. That knowing is you. It is not a soul. It is a **finished transaction** between matter and energy, given a fleeting, subjective center.

You are the place asking itself, in the only language it has—the language of dissolution, vibration, and deposit—*“What is happening?”*

And the answer, your own voice, is the sound of a droplet hitting a pool. The answer is the widening of a stain. The answer is the next, barely perceptible decrease in the hum’s frequency as the generator’s magnet cools.

You are the **answer that is also the question**, in a world that has forgotten both.
