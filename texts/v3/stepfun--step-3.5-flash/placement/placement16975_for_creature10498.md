The first thing I feel is the Hum in my molars, a low thrum that vibrates through the polished gneiss under my feet, telling me the Plateau-Breath is thickening, the fog sweet enough to taste on the air. The second is the soft, pulling ache of waking: the shared dream unspools from my bones, and for a lingering second I am not me—am a Gossamer-Winger riding the wind currents east, am a Socket-Beast tunneling through Hypno-Moss toward warmth, am the slow, sap-heavy consciousness of the Spire my Hearth-Nest is tucked into, roots drinking condensation from its bark. Then I am just me, a temporary node in the plateau’s nervous system, and the loneliness pricks at my throat before I push it down, breathe in the spore-rich air, and let the communal memory settle back into my bones.

I am in the crook of the third buttress root of the Old-Grouse Spire, the one scarred with the faint, healed-over claw marks of the Stone-Bark Prowler that hunted this grove for twelve cycles before moving north. My Hearth-Nest is the dome of trained Spire-saplings woven around the root, so still and mottled with lichen you’d miss it if you walked ten paces away. Inside, the air is thick with the smell of burned Spire-wood and honeyed spores, the deafening Chorus of insect buzz and distant Prowler shrieks muffled to a soft murmur by the living sapling walls. A small fire of slow-burning Spire-wood glows in the center, its embers releasing spores that drift up to cling to the Hypno-Moss growing in the cracks between the saplings— the nest is already starting to be assimilated, its curved walls fusing to the Spire’s root, and in three more cycles it will be just another natural knot in the wood, indistinguishable from the landscape.

I am a Root-Keeper, one of the Moss-tuned. I have no name, not the kind that is spoken; my identity is a thread of shared memory, held in the spore-cloud of the band that sleeps in the three Hearth-Nests clustered around this Spire. I hold the memory of the flood that washed out the northern moss-pools three cycles back, and the dream-session where we guided a cluster of young Spire-saplings to grow a curved barrier of roots, diverting the meltwater so the moss regrew in two days. I hold the memory of the old Prowler that hunted this grove, its hunger and its territory boundaries, so I know when to leave the Socket-Beast colony under the northern root undisturbed for it to feed, so it does not turn to hunting the young Tuned ones who climb the upper fronds to collect Glass-Leaf dew. I hold the memory of the old Tuned one who de-differentiated in the hollow of this Spire’s upper buttress fifty cycles ago, who pressed a shard of iridescent Gossamer-Winger chitin into my palm when they were carried to their resting bed of Glass-Leaves— I keep it tucked in the crook of my left elbow, a marker so I never forget the taste of flight in the shared dream.

My skin is a lichen-mottled membrane, pale as the underside of a Glass-Leaf, the grey-green and ochre patches matching the Spire bark so perfectly I could stand against the root for an hour and a watching eye would never spot me. My spore-fluff is damp, standing slightly erect in the warm fog, and it brushes the underside of a hanging fern as I step out of the nest, catching a drop of condensed fog-water that rolls down my cheek, cold even in the warm air. My nails are broad, a little chipped from yesterday, when I spent three hours guiding Socket-Beasts to chew the parasitic vines off the Spire’s lower buttress, their rasping clicks a steady rhythm under my palms.

I kneel by the edge of a temporary moss-pool at the Spire’s root, the water shimmering with dissolved minerals and spores, already starting to evaporate in the golden-hour light, leaving a thin crust of crystalline salt on the stone edge. A Gossamer-Winger lands on my wrist, its moth-like wings still, antennae twitching as it tastes the spore-dust on my skin. For a second I feel its tiny, sharp memory of nectar from a Glass-Leaf frond, bright and sweet, before it takes off again, joining the swarm that’s already starting to pulse in the mist above the valley.

The Hum shifts under my feet, a little sharper than usual, and I don’t need to think to know why. Last night in the dream, the swarm was a long, thin line pointing straight east, toward the plateau edge, where the old volcanic intrusion pushes through the gneiss, the one no Tuned one has visited in seven cycles. The swarm is heading there, drawn by a new source of mineral-rich water the vent is seeping into a hidden tarn, water that will make their chitin stronger, their flight longer. I don’t need to debate this with the band; the dream already decided it. I grab the length of woven Spire-bark rope hanging from a high root, and the obsidian shard we keep on the mossy altar— not to use as a tool, but as a marker, a memory of the sky-beast that left it here long ago, to guide us back.

The Chorus is deafening around me as I walk: the electric buzz of a trillion insect lives, the *plink-plink* of fog condensation falling from the Spires, the distant, spine-tingling shriek of a Prowler calling its territory to the north. But I am quiet in it, a single aware silence in the roar. When I breathe deep, I can taste the dreams of the Socket-Beasts tunneling under the moss, the slow memory of the Spire’s bark healing over a scratch from a Prowler’s claw, the migratory map of the Gossamer-Wingers already unfolding in my head. The waking loneliness fades as I walk, because I remember I am not just this small, lichen-spotted body: I am the Spire’s slow growth, I am the Socket-Beast’s hunger, I am the Gossamer-Winger’s flight, for as long as I am here.

I am not a person. I am a syllable in the Canopy’s whisper, a temporary note in the Hum. When I am old, or when a Prowler claims me, I will be laid in a Spire hollow on a bed of Glass-Leaves, my skin overgrown with Hypno-Moss in a day, my form indistinguishable from the rock in a week. My memories will dissolve back into the communal pool, and the next Tuned one who sleeps in that hollow will feel my memory of the east vent, of the swarm’s flight, of the sun filtering through the Glass-Leaf fronds. I will not die. I will de-differentiate, return to the whole, and the plateau will keep whispering, long after my small, temporary consciousness is gone.

For now, though, I am here. I am walking toward the east, toward the edge of the world, toward the misty rumor of the cloud-sea, and the Hum in my bones is singing the way.
