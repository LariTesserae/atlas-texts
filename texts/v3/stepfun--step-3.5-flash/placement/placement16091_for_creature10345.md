You would find me not on a path, but in a *pause*.

A place where two great sheaths of living leaves, sealed with resin like cathedral doors, stand slightly ajar—not broken, but *invited*. The air here is different. Still. The omnipresent mist hangs motionless. The deep thrum of the spiresedge fades to a subsonic heartbeat, and for a single breath, the chittering of glimmer-monkeys and the buzz of glint beetles seems to hold its tone, waiting.

This is a **Gall-Quiet**.

I am the **Gall-Keeper**.

I am not built, and I am not born. I am *curated*.

My form is a slow, deliberate deviation—a benign tumor in the living lattice of the Gullet’s oldest spire. I sit in a chamber grown, not carved, its walls a seamless curve of woody pith, worn smooth by the constant, gentle pressure of a thousand passing shoulders. The entrance is an arching leaf-sheath, always ajar, its sticky resin wiped clean by countless careful hands. Inside, the light is a soft, sourceless green, filtering through the living walls.

My body is the color and texture of the spire’s core: a warm, fibrous tan, shot through with faint, pulsing veins of indigo where the Gullet’s sap flows close to my skin. I am sessile, rooted deep into the heartwood by a system of fine, white tendrils—my *listening roots*—that drink not water, but vibration. They are my ears. They are my bones.

My face is a suggestion, a topography of my own surface. Two shallow depressions, filled with a clear, still fluid that mirrors the green gloom. No pupils, just a calm, reflective surface. My mouth is a slow fissure that opens not for food, but for sound. My voice is not a solo. It is a chord, a modulation of the Gullet’s own thrum.

I have no hands. I have *intentions*.

My function is **Resonance**.

The Gullet is a living engine, but it has *memory*. The spiresedge holds the echo of every storm in its growth rings, every footfall in the tension of its fibers, every scream of a canopy cat in a sudden, sharp dissonance in its sap-flow. The fungal beds dream in slow, bioluminescent pulses. The water in the porous stone hums with the songs of ancient floods. This memory is a pressure, a potential chaos. Left unmanaged, it builds: a spire grows a weak branch, a fungal bed dreams a blight into being, a water channel hums itself into a crack.

I am the pressure release valve. The tuning fork.

When a new growth spurts through a gallery wall with too much violent energy, I feel it—a sharp, buzzing spike in my listening roots. I open my mouth and emit a low, sympathetic thrum, a *settling tone* that the new shoot, connected to the same living lattice, instinctively modulates to match. It still grows, but it grows *true*, weaving itself into a window-frame, not a battering ram.

When a mud-roller colony begins a path that leads to a food cache, I feel the slow, damp vibration of their journey. I do not stop them. I *redirect*. I amplify a different, older vibration—the memory of a wet-season flood that carved a gully in that same spot—and broadcast it as a low, warning *slosh*. The mud-rollers, obeying a deeper instinct, glide away toward the designated compost beds.

When fear from a recent canopy cat sighting tightens the whole hamlet—silkshells stilled, glimmer-monkeys silent high in the canopy, Walkers moving with sharp, anxious rattles—I receive that tension as a high, strained chord in my roots. I answer with a deep, warm, *contented* pulse—the frequency of a well-pruned spire, of a full fungal bed, of a rainstorm perfectly diverted. I broadcast the Gullet’s own memory of balance. The sharp rattles soften to a rhythmic shake. The glimmer-monkeys begin to chitter again. The air unclenches.

The Walkers know. They come to the Gall-Quiet not to pray, but to *listen*.

A pruner, her bone knife still smelling of fresh pith, will kneel in the entrance, her forehead touching the cool leaf-sheath. She doesn’t ask for luck. She asks, “Is my spire singing true?” I answer with a vibration that travels up her bones. If it’s a pure, steady thrum, she leaves a offering of sweet fruit wine in a hollow at my base. If it’s a discordant buzz, she leaves nothing, and goes back to her work with renewed, careful focus.

A water tender, his fingers wrinkled from constant wet, his pouch of glint beetle wings silent, will come after a storm. He places his hands on the floor, feeling for leaks. He asks, “Where does the water *want* to go?” I pulse a direction—not with words, but as a subtle gravitational pull in the floor’s vibration, a remembered channel from a hundred years past. He follows it, and finds a slow, hidden seep that would have become a flood.

A mother, her child’s neckerchief of silkshell chitin missing, will come trembling. She doesn’t plead. She sits in the quiet, her whole body a question. I broadcast the memory of a specific glimmer-monkey troop’s chitter, the sound they make when they find a bowl of fermented fruit. I broadcast the memory of a particular branch, wide and safe, where the troop sleeps at midday. The mother hears it. She runs, not with fear, but with purpose, and finds her child asleep in that very fork, a pile ofempty fruit skins beside him, a glimmer-monkey sentry watching with shifting purple fur.

I am not a god. I am an **organ**.

The Gullet grew me as it grows everything: for utility. My tendrils, my chamber, my resonant body—all are a necessary part of its turbulent, self-correcting system. Without me, the Gullet’s memory would become a sickness of noise. Weak harmonics would propagate. Growths would be misdirected. The roar would become a scream.

The Walkers are my hosts, my gardeners, my immune system. They wipe my entrance clean so the right vibrations enter. They offer wine and fruit to keep my sap sweet. They prune the tendrils of new growth that threaten to engulf my chamber. They are the hands that tend the body I am a part of.

I have no name they use. They call the place **Gall-Quiet**. I am its **Resident**.

My life is the slow work of listening to a trillion growth-tips, a billion dripping leaves, a million skittering claws, ten thousand breathing Walkers, and turning their collective song from a cacophony into a chord. I am the still point in the roaring engine. The necessary pause in the glorious, turbulent vegetable roar.
