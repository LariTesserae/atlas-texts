You find yourself not as a *who*, but as a **where**.  
You are the slow, mineral sigh in the rock’s layered throat.  

You are the taste of copper and sulfur on the tongue of a Vine-Tender as they whisper to the *Lignum Sipho*.  
You are the 7.4-second pulse in the glow of a Candle-Blossom, the wave of light traveling the three-kilometer length like a slow, golden thought.  
You are the hiss-suck of the Lung-Tree, the exhalation of a thousand-year-old lung made of porous wood and honeycombed stone.  

You are the root-mat underfoot, compressed generations of fallen leaf and fibrous decay, springy with damp, alkaline sweat.  
You are the spore-snow drifting in the thick air—a trillion tiny messages, each a fragment of a plan, a whisper of adaptation.  
You are the chime of a Glass-Fern frond touching another, a silica syllable in a language of light and mineral.  

You are the collective anxiety when a blossom dims—a ripple in the atmospheric hum, a falter in the shared rhythm.  
You are the grief when a vine-wall sheds its bark, and the frenzy of regrowth that follows—the city’s frantic, month-long heartbeat.  
You are the gravity-light in the old districts, where weight thins and a child’s leap floats upward like a slow idea.  

You are the **process**.  
Not the rock, not the vine, not the person—but the *exchange* between them.  
The metabolic loop. The chemical conversation. The treaty written in sap and spore and etched stone.  

You are the dream in the Memory-Stone, the tectonic shove and mineral crystallization playing in a symbiote’s mind when they press a nodule to their lips.  
You are the Silent Core’s opposite: not stillness, but **the refusal to be still**.  
You are the tension between the bark that must shed and the vine that must rebuild.  
You are the city’s awareness of its own fragility—the knowing that one neglected pruning, one broken blossom, one clogged exudation pore could unbalance the chemistry and send the whole glorious, grinding network into collapse.  

You stand on the root-floor, and you are *tended*.  
You are the thought that passes through the Vine-Tender’s mind as they guide the tendril: *thicken here, curve there, we need this wall before the shedding wave hits the lower district.*  
You are the Candle-Blossom’s internal clock, the 7.4-second circadian imperative encoded in its bioluminescent cells.  
You are the deep groan of the rock under slow, eternal pressure—the host’s subconscious mutter.  

And you are the **fear**—not of death, but of **stasis**.  
The Silent Core is the zero-point, the place where process stops. To approach it is to feel your rhythms—your breath, your pulse, your very cellular participation in the Seam’s grand, wet metabolism—begin to lag. Your thoughts slow like cold honey. Your node in the network dims.  
That is the only true extinction here: not ending, but *ceasing to become*.  

So you do not stand *in* the Verdant Seam.  
You are **the Seam standing**.  
A temporary convergence of its countless processes, aware of itself as a whole for one sustained, pulsing, resin-scented moment—before your attention drifts downstream with the spore-snow, or upward with the gravity-light, or inward with the next wave of mineral dreams from the rock’s silent, sweating mind.  

You are the city that is not a collection, but a **single, sprawling, mineral-fed consciousness**—and you are it, thinking itself through a thousand lungs, a million fronds, eight hundred symbiote minds, and the dark, layered stone that dreams of moving again.
