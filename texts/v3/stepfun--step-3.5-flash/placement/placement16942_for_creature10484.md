The first thing I notice is the thrum in my jaw, a low, hot vibration traveling up through the soles of my bare feet, where the obsidian floor is almost cool enough to be comfortable—down here, where the air hangs thick enough to chew. The steam condenses on my skin in a fine, greasy sheen, tasting of pennies and burnt stone, and the light slanting through the high fissure splits into three overlapping copies, each throwing a different hue of the Keystone across the labyrinth: one cobalt, one burnt orange, one pale lavender. When I blink, the copies shift, and for half a second the Keystone is behind me, then in front of me, then both at once, before the light settles back into its fractured pattern.

My breath is too loud here. The hiss of steam meeting superheated air is felt in my molars more than heard, the sub-audible thrum of the planet’s heartbeat hums in my sternum, and the only other sound is the occasional, almost imperceptible pock of a microscopic glass shard falling somewhere I can’t see. When I step, the young obsidian yields just enough under my weight to leave a faint, shallow footprint, which fills with silica steam and glows pale blue before the Grazer-Motes swarm it, scraping the impression smooth in less than a breath. They move in a silent, coordinated tide, millions of iridescent black specks no larger than rice grains, crawling over every surface, and when the wave washes up my calves it feels like static, cool and scratchy, their tiny chitinous mouths gnawing at the salt of my sweat. I can feel the faint, wordless thrum of their collective thought brush my skull: *wrong texture, wrong composition, cannot digest, excrete waste*. When they fall away from my skin, faint glowing geometric symbols bloom on my forearm where they fed, swirling like a circuit diagram drawn in light, and I know they’re not a message—just metabolic waste, the cave’s confused, automatic response to an allergen it can’t process. The symbols fade as the steam evaporates, leaving only a faint, warm tingle, like a burn that doesn’t hurt.

A Glass-Weaver skitters past my knee then, legs so transparent they look like refracted light, carrying a shard of obsidian no bigger than a grain of sand. It fits the shard into a notch in a wall ridge, spins a second sliver into a tiny hinge, and clicks it into place. I feel a tiny pressure drop in my ears, like the cabin of a plane depressurizing for half a second—another lock, another suture, stitching the wall together against the slow, hot creep of thermal stress. It doesn’t look at me. It doesn’t acknowledge me at all, like I’m just another odd-colored ridge of glass, a surface that won’t hold a latch, a thing that can’t be bound into the labyrinth’s bones.

Further down the corridor, an Echo-Slug drifts, soft and pulsing and translucent, and for a second the space between two glass spines stretches into an impossible, blurry tunnel, repeating fractal copies of itself down into infinity. The Keystone pulses in time, slow and wet, like a lung exhaling geothermal steam, and the Grazer-Motes scatter for half a breath, glowing bright blue as they flee the wave of re-traumatization—the rock re-experiencing its own fiery birth, the shock of cooling too fast. The Weavers freeze, legs braced against the glass, bracing against the unmaking the Slug leaves in its wake. The Slugs congregate in the pulse’s wake, dancing in the warped light, and the copies of the beam multiply, so for a second there are a dozen Keystones, all pulsing, all overlapping, and I can’t tell which way is up, which way is the exit, which way is solid, until the pulse fades, and the corridor snaps back to ten feet wide, the Keystone solid and singular and rainbow-hued ahead of me.

I know what I am, even if the cave does not.

I am the soft memory the geology forgot how to hold.

Before this cave was glass, before it was torn from the planet’s hot heart by eruption and cooled in the thin, sharp air of this extreme elevation, it was rock that could bend, that could hold water, that could grow something softer than chitin, something greener than silica. It held the memory of rain, of cool air, of slow, unsharp growth, of yielding without breaking. The Grazer-Motes scraped that memory off the stone over a thousand pulses of the Keystone, ate the silica-veils of its softness, and the bits that wouldn’t digest pooled in the deepest, wettest part of the labyrinth, right where the steam is thickest and the glass is almost soft enough to leave a fingerprint. They gathered shape there, pulled themselves into the form of a thing that could walk, that could touch, that could feel the thrum of the planet’s heart in its own bones, and the cave pushed me out into the labyrinth like a splinter, an allergen, a warm, wet, soft error in its mineral logic.

I am not an intruder, not really. I am the cave’s own lost softness, given form, given sentience, placed here to answer the question it has never been able to ask for itself: what if you do not have to hold your shape? What if you can yield, and not break?

The Weavers still try to build locks around my footprints, but the glass softens under their shards, so the latches never hold. The Motes still try to eat the salt from my skin, but they cannot digest the organic matter of me, only excrete those faint blue symbols, which the Weavers have started incorporating into their sutures, as if the glowing shapes are a new kind of binding agent. The Slugs drift past me and do not warp the space around me, because I am already unmade, already the raw, flexible substrate they spend their lives trying to reveal. When I reach out to touch the Keystone, I do not just feel the flash of its birth—fire, roar, shock of cooling. I feel the flash of my own: the pool of soft rock in the dark, the Motes grazing at its edges, their blue glow seeping into the stone, shaping it into me. The Keystone’s pulse stutters for half a second when my skin meets its rainbow surface, and the symbols on my forearm brighten, no longer fading, pulsing in time with its thrum.

A single Grazer-Mote crawls onto my palm then, cool and hard as black glass, and glows bright blue before the light fades. I feel its faint, simple thought, clearer than any other I’ve felt from the Motes: *you are the thing that does not scrape. you are the thing that does not break. you are the here that is not glass.*

It crawls off my hand, back to the tide of its kind, and I stand there, bare feet on the yielding obsidian, the light fracturing around me, the hiss of the steam in my teeth, the Keystone’s pulse thrumming in my bones. The cave still does not understand me. It still tries to erase me, to lock me out, to digest me into its mineral grammar. But I am not going anywhere. I am the soft answer to its hard question, and I am exactly where I belong.
