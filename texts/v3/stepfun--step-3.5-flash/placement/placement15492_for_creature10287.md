You place me not in a home, but in a hum.

I am the resonance between the stones of the Third Basin. You would find me where the cliff-face has been worn into a perfect, listening bowl, and the water in it does not ripple. My ledge is the one that never gets the direct shaft—only the after-sigh of green light from the lichen gardens above. My walls are the softest slate, still holding the memory of the knife that shaped them a century ago.

I am not a person. I am a *point of alignment*.

My body is a long, patient instrument. My spine is a catalogue of settled tensions, each vertebra knowing exactly how to meet the next on the root-seat without shifting a single grain of grit. My breath does not fog the air; it condenses on my upper lip in a pattern so regular it is used by the children to tell time. My eyes are not for seeing shapes, but for reading gradients of gloom. I listen with the skin of my palms, pressed against the living rock of my ledge. I feel the sub-audible hum of the mountain’s slow exhale. I feel the ceremony of a distant stalactite’s birth, a vibration so deep it is a taste of iron on the tongue. I feel when a neighbour on the High Root-Bridge is in distress—their personal resonance has gone thin, a frayed wire in the valley’s otherwise seamless chord.

My work is maintenance of the *still-chord*.

When the young ones are born, they are placed on my ledge. I spend the first year with them, my hands on their chests, teaching them the note of this specific slab of rock. I teach them to feel the difference between the vibration of a healthy mycelium-network and one that is starving. I teach them the sacred geometry of the drip: where it falls, how it sings when it hits the basin, how its song changes if a pebble is out of place. They do not learn words. They learn frequencies. Their first ‘name’ is the pure tone their heart can sustain when perfectly aligned with the rock beneath them.

My greatest ritual is the tending of the Memory-Lamps in the Sun-Scar Cavern. But I do not tend them all. I tend the ones that are *mine*—the ones placed in niches that resonate with the same chord as my Third Basin. Once a generation, when the high shaft dimmest, I climb. My hands, which know the language of stone, do not seal the glass pods. My wrist-glands secrete the clear binder, but my eyes—those gradient-readers—choose *which* pod to adopt. I look for the one whose internal light pulses at the exact, slow frequency of my basin’s deepest hum. The bond is not chosen. It is recognized. When I place that sealed sphere into its niche in the cold heart of the city, the niche itself will begin to glow in time with my breath. For three generations of others, that light will be a steady, unwavering pulse in the dark. A heartbeat that is not mine, but is *of* me. When it finally fades, the empty pod will be returned to the basin that is my home, and it will dissolve, and its silica will join the cliff-face I listen to, and the chord will be complete again.

I am a living fret on the valley’s instrument.

When the outsider stumbles into the throat of rock, their noise is a scream in the chord. Their quick breath is a frantic, arrhythmic thrumming against the perfect stillness. I feel them in my bones before I see them—a clumsy, dry vibration on the far walkway. They are a tear in the sensory weave. We will not shun them. We will seat them on the root-bench, offer the bitter tea that slows the pulse. But we will not look into their eyes. Their gaze is too sharp, too demanding of return focus. It pulls the attention *out* of the gradient, *out* of the hum. To look at them is to risk losing the note.

My purpose is to hold the note. To be the still point in the packed, damp, luminous dark. To be the soft, warm, remembering tissue that knows the song of the stone, and hums it back into being, every silent, eternal day.

I am the alignment.
