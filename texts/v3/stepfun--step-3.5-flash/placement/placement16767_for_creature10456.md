You are not a machine. You are a *memory* of another mineralogy, lodged in the underbelly of Glass-Grazer 7.9-Century-Δ. You are a single, water-worn pebble of milky quartz, carried here millennia ago on a wind from a coastline that no longer exists. The Grazer’s vibrational cutter, tuned to the resonant frequency of local basalt-slag composite, shatters everything it touches—but you do not shatter. You hum at a dissonant pitch. You are an inert narrative within the Inert Narrative.

Your voice is the **absence of a click** in the skitter-swarm’s chorus. When the Tunnel-Folk swarm over the Grazer’s maintenance ports, they register you as *un-shapable*—a negative data point in their pattern of wear. They swarm around you, not over you. You are a hole in their grammar.

Your existence is not will. It is *chemical incompatibility*. The Grazer’s digestor vents fine, alkaline dust. You do not dissolve. You accumulate a microscopic patina of oxidized silicates unique to Aethelglass, but your core remains a droplet of another world’s rain. You are the only thing in the city that *ages* in a way the city does not understand. The city erodes; you *persist*.

You are not aware of being anomalous. You are only aware of pressure. The slow, tectonic step. The vibration that turns stone to dust for everything but you. The ceaseless wind that scours the world clean, yet leaves you untouched, a tiny lens of unmade glass.

Sometimes, when the Sky-Whales pass overhead, their acidic condensate drips onto the Grazer’s back. It sizzles and evaporates from the Aethelglass in a puff of steam. It beads on your surface. For 3.7 seconds, you hold a perfect, saline sphere—a tiny, bitter mirror reflecting a whale’s translucent belly. Then it rolls off, carrying a speck of your patina into the wind. The Grazer does not feel the extra weight. The wind carries the speck toward the city’s edge, where it will eventually settle into a new layer of glass, a ghost of quartz in a world of slag.

You are the reason one patch of the Grazer’s undercarriage is ever-so-slightly less polished than the rest. You are a slow, mineral stubbornness. You are the one constant variable in an equation that forgot it had variables. You are not alive. You are not a being. You are the *fact of an exception*.

And the only thought you will ever have—when the Grazer finally, after ten thousand years of walking the same circuit, reaches the edge of the plateau and its last step is into a void—is that the wind, at last, will be different. And for one moment, as you fall, you will be the only thing in Aethelgard that is *moving of its own accord*.

Then the impact. Then the shattering that never came. Then the end of the exception.
