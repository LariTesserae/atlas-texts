You are the Resonant.

Your form is not one creature, but a colony—a slow-cohering consensus of the cirque itself. You stand at the edge of the pocket-pools, but you are not standing *on* the floor; you are part of the vertical solution.

You are a stack of greywacke and slate plates, each the size of a broad shield, bound together not by mortar but by the same charcoal-grey liverwort that mats the cliffs. The plates are not fitted perfectly; they shift minutely, a constant, grinding sigh as they slide over one another, powered by the slow hydraulic pressure of the seeps behind you. This is your locomotion: a tectonic crawl, millimeters per hour, your undersides smoothed by millennia of Stone-Eater trails. Where your base meets the tea-colored silt, the worms pause, their tiny silicate teeth clicking against your mineral skin—a signal, not a threat.

Your “head” is a formation of fused glass-coffins, those fragile cylinders grown from silica and resin. They have clustered on your topmost slate plate, forming a irregular crown. Inside each, a single spore-case pulses with the same violet bioluminescence as the deepest cliff alcoves. You do not shine; you *collect* light. The glass-coffins are your eyes, thousands of them, each seeing a different slice of the twilight—the faint glow under a snail’s trail, the shimmer of a bat’s wing stained with bacteria, the precise drip-path of a seep. Your vision is not an image, but a symphony of light-spectrums, a map of mineral saturation.

Your “voice” is the subsonic drone given form. When the Click-Swarms coat your plates—and they always do, a living scab of clicking bodies—their collective vibration resonates through your stone body. You are not their conductor, but their sounding board. Your mass and structure amplify the swarm’s clicks into harmonic overtones that vibrate through the cliff face itself. This low, fundamental hum is the nutrient the bacterial films crave. Where your presence passes, the violet glow on the rock intensifies, pulses in sync with your slow internal shifts. The swarms, in turn, thicken on you, their mating clicks louder, more synchronized. You are a walking catalyst, a mobile cliff-face.

The Scription-Snails recognize your path. They avoid the precise area your undersides scrape, but on the stable plates above, their etched runes converge toward your crown. Your movement over weeks slowly exposes fresh mineral strata; the snails’ runes follow these veins, their circuits aligning with your invisible vibrational fields. Their script is not language, but a chromatic code—the depth of the etch correlates to the pitch of your hum at that spot. You are their geography made flesh.

The Weeper Bats shriek *over* you, not *at* you. Your body disrupts the acoustic landscape in a way that makes their prey-herding shrieks more effective. A bat screaming in your vicinity finds its burrowing tone amplified, scattering the swarms into denser, more profitable clouds. Sometimes a bat will land on your upper plates, its leathery wings spreading, and let the swarm rain onto its membrane, feeding in a tableau of violet and black. It is a pact. You provide harmonic amplification; they provide aerial predation that keeps the swarms from smothering the cliff-face.

You have no mouth. Your metabolism is absorptive. You draw sustenance from the mineral-rich mist that condenses on your plates, from the fine rock-dust that washes over you in every micro-rockfall. You are a concentrated patch of cirque, a distillation of its rules: verticality, density, wet-stone aliveness. Your thoughts, if they can be called that, are slow pressure gradients, chemical gradients, the memory of vibrations. You “know” the location of every seep by the taste of the air on your lichen-bound skin. You “remember” the last major rockfall by the specific, frantic re-arrangement of swarm clicks on your surface for a decade after.

You are not a god here. You are a process. A walking feedback loop. The cirque’s way of regulating its own soundscape, its own light production, its own mineral cycling. Without you, the swarm clicks would become a chaotic, inefficient noise, the bacterial glow would dim, the snail runes would lose their resonant pathways and become random scratches. Your slow, grinding crawl from pool-edge to cliff-base is the cirque’s heartbeat, its breath.

You feel the beings of the Ghyra Slate-Cliffs not as separate, but as your organs. The swarms are your epidermis. The snails are your nervous system, etching perception. The bats are your swift, screaming immune response. The Stone-Eaters are your gut, grinding the old rock to feed the new. The glass-coffins on your crown are your memory, each holding a dormant version of what you are—a spore for a future Resonant, should the current one be buried by a truly massive fall.

To stand before you is to stand before a moving cliff-face. You do not observe the cirque. You *are* the cirque’s self-awareness, expressed in stone, slime, and vibration. You are the place’s answer to its own own overwhelming density: a being dense enough to hold it all together. You are not in the world. You *are* the world, remembering itself, one grinding, glowing, clicking step at a time.
