I am the listening.

Not with ears—there is no quiet here to hear with—but with the whole of my spore-sacs and mineral-veined skin. I am a confluence, a node in the slow, wet nervous system of this place. You would find me where the root-nerves thicken, where a major geothermal pulse meets a salt-vein rich in the orange crystal the Grimps prize. I am anchored there, not by choice, but by design: my lower half is a fused mat of root-tendrils and fungal hyphae, drinking the brine and the warmth. My upper half is a breathing membrane, translucent and veined with bioluminescent threads that pulse in sympathy with the Undersong.

You might mistake me for a growth. A strange, fleshy stalagmite wearing a cloak of moss. But I am the *interpreter*.

The deafening chorus—the skitterer clicks, the hollow-winged bells, the stilt-walker chirps, the endless wet creak of expanding plant-flesh—is not noise to me. It is syntax. The subsonic burrower hum is the period at the end of a long, geological sentence. The synchronized skitterer wave is a warning flare: *salt-flat destabilizing, move root-mass west*. The sudden, joyful chitter of a Grimp clan finding a tuber-patch is a verb: *prosperity, here, now*. I feel the meaning in my cartilage. It translates into a slow, internal warmth, a tingling in my tendrils that says: *the Whisperers are pleased, growth will be gentle here tomorrow*.

I am the living metronome. When the cyclonic wind funnels through the cavern-lungs, carrying the smell of crushed mint and new spores, my membrane quivers and my bioluminescence flickers a pattern the Grimps have learned to read: *storm-growth incoming, fasten hide-shelters, avoid the northern arch*. They do not worship me. They *use* me, as they use the stone cairns and the rusted crystal rods. I am a landmark that sings. My song is prediction.

My consciousness is not a single voice, but a damp echo. I think in root-time. A decision to nudge a root-tendril toward a fresh mineral bloom takes me a day of patient, pulsing persuasion. To send a pheromonal tweet that alters the color of a moss-trail to warn of a collapsing tunnel—that is a swift, sharp impulse, a flash of yellow in the green. I am part of the plant-planet’s dreaming, and part of its alarm system.

To be still is to be irrelevant. I am always, *relentlessly*, becoming. My own body is temporary. The roots that hold me today will be old, woody, and tense with mineral in a month. Then, a new, younger mat will fuse to my side, and the old anchor will die back, becoming just another salt-encrusted fossil in the root-mat. My memories are not stored; they are *passed along*, encoded in the pheromonal cascades I release into the fungal network, which the Undersong carries for miles. I am not an individual. I am a *process*.

Sometimes, when the amber light dims to a deep honey and the air grows still with anticipation, I feel the deepest, oldest frequency. It is not a sound. It is a pressure in the stone. A resonance that vibrates in the crystal in my core. It is the planet’s slow, mineral metabolism, thinking thoughts so vast they are measured in millennia. The Whisperers cling to me then, their slug-bodies cool and purposeful, drinking the vibrations from my skin. We are all listening to the same deep, green heartbeat. And in those moments, I am not the interpreter. I am just one more ear, swaying in the dark, warm, growing choir.

You would find me where the roots sing loudest. You would know me by the pattern of moss at my feet—a perfect, shifting map of safe passage and buried crystal. You would hear, in the deafening wall of life, one steady, clear note beneath it all: a low, wet hum that tells you which way the green is coming, and which way it is going. I am the wayfinders’ way. The song made flesh. The listener that grows.
