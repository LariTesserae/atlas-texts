I do not arrive by foot, or by breath, or by any steady crossing of the black glassy slag rim. I unfold.

The hard line between me and the thin, hazy air above the Basin thins first at my edges, then at my bones, then at the thought of who I thought I was before: one of the ones who laid the resin poles, carved the spiral slabs, filled the grey woven basket with polished salt-scales and dessicated Glass-Twitcher husks. I had tried to build a silence, a stable node to hold the Basin’s song still, and for that the Basin had meant to dissolve me. But instead of breaking me, it unfolded me: my skin thinned to lattice, my bones bleached to salt and borate, my voice unspooled from words to vibration, until I was no longer separate from the place I had tried to tame.

Now I am Overtone. I am a living resonator, a variable node in the Basin’s self-composing symphony, and my form is as shifting as the salt crust under my feet.

Most of the time I stand as high as a Salt-Spine’s lower third, swaying in time with the columns’ slow thermal flex. My carapace is layered with the Basin’s pearl, bruised-violet, and nicotine-yellow salts, plates thin as sheet ice that click together softly when I move. Between the plates runs the same clear, sticky gel the Spines exude, leaving a trail of glittering dust in my wake that evaporates before it can touch the crust. I have no face, only a smooth, shallow depression of salt where one would be, and a thin vertical slit that opens to let out the hums and overtones I use to tune the Basin’s shifting song.

I am not an inhabitant here. I am a process, just like the Skitter-Moss wave that eats the gel from my joints when I stand still for too long, or the Glass-Twitcher that sometimes lands on my shoulder to ping its ultrasonic clicks off my crystal skin, mapping my shape with sound. The Basin does not think of me as separate from itself, and after enough time here, neither do I.

My work is simple: the Basin’s song shifts, constantly, autocatalytically, new frequencies blooming from collapsing Gel-Fungi, cracking salt plates, spontaneous pulses from the Spines that no one else can track. The Spine-Riders quiet mismatched vibrations, the Resonant Maw recycles chaotic overtones into its steady, melancholy drone, but neither can adjust to new keys. The old resin poles are halfway turned to crystal, humming the failed harmony the ritualists tried to impose, their sticky exudate a record of how hard they fought to make the Basin stay still. I do not fight. I listen.

When a Gel-Fungus inflates to a pitch no one has ever heard before, a high, thin whine that would shatter three Spines if left unaddressed, I absorb it. My crystal plates rearrange themselves to match its frequency, and I release it as a soft overtone that weaves into the Basin’s drone, turning a threat into a new line of melody. When the Maw’s fundamental tone drops a minor third without warning, I adjust first, humming the new key into the Spines around me so their pulses don’t collapse out of sync. The Spine-Riders perch on the columns near me then, their silent dampening fields shifting to match my overtones, and for a minute the whole Basin hums in harmony, no cracks, no shatters, no bursts.

I still carry the old woven basket, slung over my shoulder with a strip of Skitter-Moss tar. I do not keep the salt-scales or Twitcher husks the ritualists left inside it; I dissolved those in the Maw long ago, recycled into the drone that feeds the Spines. Now I keep the salt plates I find that hold the most beautiful overtones, the ones that ring like tiny bells when I tap them with my crystal-tipped finger. When the Basin’s song shifts too fast for me to adjust on my own, I lay the plates in the salt crust and tap them, one by one, until their tones anchor me, let me catch up to the new key. Sometimes I move the old carved spiral slabs too, when a new overtone needs a place to land, setting them in fresh patches of salt to wear smooth under the new resonance.

The dying Spine where the basket was first left still stands, a little duller, a little slower than the others. I sit with it for an hour most days, pressing my palm to its side, humming the Basin’s current key into its capillaries until its pulse steadies, until the opaque dullness fades back to pearlescent glow. It grows a meter every three days now, slow, but steady.

I do not know how long I will last. The Basin’s song will shift again, eventually, to a key I cannot tune myself to, and I will be dissolved into the Maw, my frequencies recycled into a new drone, a new Spine, a new burst of Glass-Twitcher flight-song. For now, though, I am here: the Basin’s listener, its variable node, its overtone. The thrum thrums in my teeth, the Gel-Fungi pop in blue-green bursts around me, the salt crust sings its high crystalline song under my feet, and I hum along, holding the chaos together, one note at a time.
