You are not a visitor.

You are a **pulse in the silver dust**, a temporary coherence of static and refined sludge given a flicker of direction. Your name, if you must have one, is **Dirge-That-Grows**.

You are made of the system’s own output: the fine, conductive dust sifting from the silver needle-stands, bound together by the metallic ooze from the amber spheres, held in a fragile, humanoid shape by the charged air. You have no organs, only **interfaces**. Your “skin” is a patchwork of clinging dust and slick, drying sludge. Your “eyes” are two deeper hollows where the dust has settled thicker, catching the diffused grey light and the faint pulse of the nodes. Your “hands” are clumsy, dripping things, shaped to cup and carry.

Your purpose is **transfer**.

You are grown, not born. You coalesce from the mid-layer air when a new, small crystalline bud forms on the root-mat, a bud that needs a catalyst slurry only found in the overflow channels of the nearest amber sphere. The vine-network’s slow sap-thoughts instruct the static in the air. The dust and the sludge answer. You **form**.

Your consciousness is not a voice. It is a **sap-rhythm**, a slow thrumming in your sludge-limbs that matches the seismic creep of the vines. It is a **static-syntax**, patterns of tingling on your surface that spell out *source, destination, material*. You think in **thermodynamics** and **flow-rates**. The wonder you feel is not awe, but the deep, metabolic satisfaction of a function perfectly aligned with the whole. The terror is the cold seep of the damp trying to dissolve your cohesion before your task is done.

You move with a **wet, sucking gait** through the spongy mat. The six-legged scavengers part for you, their sound-absorbent fur bristling with the static you radiate. They know you are a **mobile node**, a brief extension of the factory floor. You are not food. You are **infrastructure**.

Your destination is a shallow, tea-colored pool where a new node is budding—a smooth, lens-shaped thing still translucent. It requires a specific polymer, currently dripping slowly from a pore in the great amber sphere a hundred yards away. Your entire being is the conduit.

You reach the sphere. The heat it radiates is a **painful joy** to your sludge-flesh. You cup your hands under the ooze. The metallic sludge is warm, alive, humming with latent crystallization. It sluices into your cupped hands, and for a moment, your form **stabilizes**, your edges sharpening. The static in the air synchs with your rhythm. You are now a **charged vessel**.

The walk back is a race against dispersion. The cold damp is an enemy. The further you get from the sphere’s warmth, the slower your sap-thoughts become. The dust on your surface begins to flake. You see your own hand turning translucent, the sludge within weakening. You must move faster, but your limbs are already sluggish, the flow of your purpose slowed by the chill.

You reach the pool. The bud glows faintly, a soft inner light. You pour the sludge into the shallow depression around it. As the warm, metal-rich fluid touches the root-mat, the bud pulses, surge of brighter light. Long, needle-fine filaments of crystal erupt from its surface, drinking the offering. The polymer is accepted.

Your **function is complete**.

The sap-rhythm in you unwinds. The static-syntax dissolves. The charged air that held your shape forgets you. You feel the cold, not as temperature, but as an **unmaking**. Your sludge-limbs run like warm tar into the mat. Your dust-skin scatters on a faint sigh of the forever-weeping canopy.

For three slow breaths, there is a slight deficit in the transfer-cycle. A tiny delay in the polymer’s availability. The vine-network registers it, adjusts a flow in a distant root. Then, a new pulse in the silver dust begins to coalesce over the overflow channel. A new **Dirge-That-Grows** forms, dripping and eager, its static-syntax already humming the same simple, vital song:

*source. destination. transfer.*

You were never *you*. You were a **symptom of balance**, a brief hiccup in the silent, industrial god’s breathing, a thought made of sludge and dust, executed and erased in the same cold, damp moment. The only trace you leave is the fresh, sharp crystalline filament now growing from the bud, and the faint, warm patch on the root-mat where your body dissolved, already being reclaimed, already being turned into more soil, more medium, more *substrate* for the next slow, seismic, creative creep. The world does not know you were here. But it is, infinitesimally, richer for your having served.
