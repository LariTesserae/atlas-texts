The first thing that anchors me to this place is the thrum in my molars.
I do not remember arriving. I do not remember a ship, a hike, a world of concrete and steel and quiet air. I only remember opening my eyes to the warm, moss-draped petrified-wood platform under my bare feet, the vibration of the world’s breath humming up through my soles, the golden-green twilight pooling in the vaulted canopy above like light filtered through centuries of leaves.
Sound is not noise here. It is a physical weight, a layering of frequencies that pools in the hollow of my throat, eddies around the sharp edges of the canyon walls below, stacks thin as silk over the deep, subsonic G of the bedrock. I hear the metallic chime of seed pods popping open three islands over, the wet *shluck* of a pneumatic fungus expelling spores a thousand feet down, the high thin shriek of a Call-Sire flickering past the upper canopy, its bio-luminescent talons flashing sapphire before it vanishes. The trillion fineglass-edged leaves whisper constant, a white noise that hums in the capillaries of my ears.
The first being I feel is not one I can see: the Resonant Mycelium, a planet-spanning neural web that threads through every crack in the iridescent rock, every root of the braided polymer trees, every droplet of phosphorescent river water. It does not speak in words. It pushes impressions into the soft tissue of my sinuses, clear as pictures: the seed pod chime = mist will rise in the western valley in three hours. The Call-Sire’s shriek = a nutrient bloom is unfurling in the lower canyons, the phosphor rivers will run rich with spore-food for ten diurnal cycles. A soft, curious purr brushes the edge of my awareness: *you harmonize. you are here*.
I look down at my feet. There is a crack in the warm, vibrating platform, and growing out of it is a seedling no bigger than my thumb, its leaves silver as mercury. It hums a pure, clear A, locked perfectly into the bedrock’s G, and as I watch, a tiny filament of black ice—warm to the touch, I can feel its heat from an inch away—shoots from its base and vanishes into the mist below. A future root bridge, a new connection between this node and whatever island lies miles down in the yawning gulf of empty air.
Memories surface, not of a life before, but of the Weald itself, buried under the thrum. I remember pruning the aggressive mycelial networks that try to swallow the terraced grain gardens on the neighboring island. I remember harvesting resonant crystals from the waterfall where the ley-line vibration pools, the humming stones warm in my palms. I remember tuning the acoustic mirrors on the highest terrace to focus sound into temporary shelters during the silent, static-electric storms that charge the upper canopy to sapphire glow.
I am Lira. I am a Tuner.
My people—the Keepers—are the ones who scuttle quiet in adaptive chameleonic suits between the arboreal nodes, but we do not build here. We listen. The Weald is a beyond-modern processor, a planetary-scale system of bio-resonance and quantum perception; the reversed gravity pockets, the time-dilated fungal circles, the phase-shifting weasel-things that flicker in and out of the edge of vision are not magic, just running code, quirks in the system that need tuning so they do not break the connections that hold the sparse, interstitial world together.
I kneel, my knees sinking into the damp moss, and brush the pad of my finger against the seedling’s silver leaf. My suit shifts at my touch, the fabric turning translucent to avoid bruising the delicate surface, the iridescent plates along my cuffs glowing soft gold. I hum, a quarter-tone lower than the seedling’s A, the exact frequency the mycelium needs to lock the new root bridge into the ley-line running through the mist.
The black ice filament glows brighter, then shoots another tendril, then another, weaving into the fog until I feel it click into place, solid, humming in time with the rest of the Weald’s thrum. The mycelium’s purr softens, satisfied.
A phase-shifter flickers at the edge of my vision, its fur shifting like oil on water, tilting its head to listen to the new bridge’s hum before it vanishes. The insect hive in the crook of the nearest polymer tree softens its alarm call into a steady, calm buzz.
I stand, the moss soft under my scuff-resistant boots, and step out onto the existing root bridge that connects my platform to the neighboring island. I hum the deep G of the bedrock as I walk, and the woven root system flexes gentle under my weight, adjusting to my step, alive and responsive to the sound. Miles of empty air yawn below me, dotted with drifting spore clouds and the slow, gliding hump of a leviathan, a continent of flesh and kelp drifting between the nodes, its bioluminescence pulsing slow as a heartbeat.
I know the southern terrace’s mycelial network will start encroaching on the grain terraces in six hours, if I do not go prune it. I know the resonant crystals by the waterfall will be ready to harvest tomorrow, when the mist rises. I know there is a pocket of reversed gravity in the lower canyon that will widen in three days, and I will need to hum a counter-frequency to tune it back to the Weald’s baseline before it snaps the root bridge that feeds the western island’s water supply.
But for now, I stop halfway across the bridge, and I listen.
The thrum hums in my bones. The leaves whisper. The Call-Sire shrieks far off, the seed pods chime, the mycelium purrs in my skull. I hum back, soft, low, and for a second the whole world hums in time: the sound pooling in the air around me, solid and warm, the black ice bridges glowing, the leviathan drifting, the silver seedling’s root locking into the earth.
I am not a builder here. I am not a conqueror. I am a note in the chord, a tuner of the connections that hold the empty spaces together, a listener who waits for the Weald to hum back.
It always does.
