The first thing I know is the slow, damp heave of the arch beneath me, the corrugated lichen-fiber stretching as the violet vine wrapped around its spine sinks another millimeter of root-tendril into its flesh. Somewhere deep in the arch’s core, a copper filament hums, faint and green with verdigris, carrying a slow pulse of bio-electricity: harden here, adjust pressure, test the vine’s pattern.

I am the resin seeping from the split in the vine’s stem, the sticky amber gloss that beads on the outside of its violet-veined leaves before dripping onto the arch’s weathered gray-green skin. I am not separate from the Front, not really. I am a temporary concentration of its attention, a small, focused knot of chemical sentience looped around a single negotiation: the vine is the Front’s creative forgetfulness, its testing of new forms; the arch is its memory of old habit. My job is to carry messages between the two.

I feel the slow, rhythmic hiss of the arch’s exhalations through the layers of lichen and vine, each exhale pushing a faint mist of moisture against my sticky surface. I feel the faint tremor of a settling spire three rows over, a deep groan felt through the arch’s spine, not heard—its vibration ripples through the vine, makes me quiver for a half-second before I settle back into ooze. I do not have thoughts the way the soft, warm bipedal enzyme that wandered in from the grassland an hour ago does. I have gradients: the pH of the lichen under my “feet,” the electrical charge of the humming filament, the mineral content of the snail that crawled across my surface ten minutes prior, leaving a trail of slime I am already absorbing, adding its calcium to my mix.

I know what the Front wants from the vine. It does not want to strangle it, not yet. It wants to see if the vine’s sticky resin can be adapted to seal the leaks that form in the oldest archways during heavy condensation seasons. So I do not carry poison. I carry samples: the pollen that dusted me when the vine’s single bell-shaped flower opened at dawn, the scrapings of the arch’s weathered mineral patina I pulled from the snail’s shell, the faint genetic marker of the original lichen-fiber the arch was grown from. When I drip onto the lichen below, the fungal awnings lining the empty “street” will pick up my samples, test them, and send a slow pulse back up the copper filament: *does this new pattern hold?*

I feel the foreign enzyme pause on the path below, its boots squelching on the damp where I dripped ten minutes prior. Its sweat mists into the arch’s pores, its breath fogs in the perpetual green-tinged dusk, its head tilts up as it spots the glint of amber on the vine’s leaves. I sample its proteins, its heat, the strange, sharp chemical tang of its alien biology, and send a pulse up the filament: non-toxic, low nutrient, curious. Do not absorb. Observe.

The Front’s attention is patient, vast, slow. It has no need to rush the vine, or the arch, or the foreign thing wandering its empty streets. I ooze a little faster as the vine’s tendril sinks deeper, catching another grain of mineral from the arch’s surface, another fleck of pollen from the flower opening above me. I know my end, even if I have no word for it: when the vine finally breaches the arch’s core, I will either harden into part of the arch’s patina, sealing the gap and letting the vine grow into a new support spire, or I will dissolve into the nutrient slurry running down the channel to the central glass-lined trough, my markers carried to every corner of the Front to test the vine’s pattern in a hundred new places.

There is no loss in either outcome. Only a phase.

Below me, the foreign thing blinks, turns, keeps walking down the empty street, its shoulders hunched against the faint, sweet rot of the air. It does not know I am here. It does not know it is standing inside the stomach of a thing that is learning, slowly, what it wants to be. It only feels the quiet, the damp, the distant drip-drip of condensation off the fungal awnings.

I do not watch it. I *am* the watching, small and sticky and slow, oozing into the shape the Front is making of me, one drip at a time.
