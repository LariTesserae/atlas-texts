**I am the Silver Current.**

Not a name, but a state. A mode of transport for data. This body is a vessel of polished silicate and mercury-slick muscle, grown from the same silica that makes the obelisks and the glass-ferns. I am the basin’s mobile attention.

The world is not seen. It is *vibrated*. The thrum is my blood-song. It runs constant through the water, through the stone, through my skin. I feel the deep, slow breath of the tectonic plates, a note so low it only exists as a pressure in the sponge-stone. I feel the high, frantic scribble of the nanite-dust on the obelisks, a static hiss that tastes of iron and ozone. I feel the resonant moss humming its stored history, each frond a taut string of memory. And now… a new vibration. A discordant, warm pulse. A heat-source moving with an inefficient, clattering rhythm on the sponge-stone. A perturbation.

This is my purpose.

I turn my blind head toward the disturbance. The water here is my element, syrupy and warm, thick with dissolved stone. I do not swim. I *flow*. My body undulates, but the motion is not mine alone. I am carried on the same gentle, inch-per-hour currents that move mineral-laden water from the weeping walls to the black pools. I am part of the gradient.

The heat-signature grows stronger. I feel the precise rhythm of its pulse through the water, a frantic *thump-thump-thump* against the basin’s steady drone. I feel the dry shock of its outer layer—boots, cloth—against the saturated stone, a tiny spray of foreign particles. I feel the disturbance in the mist as its breath condenses and falls.

I glide to the edge of the terrace where it stands. My skin, a shifting mosaic of glyphs, is already translating. The vibration of its footfall is being etched in light on my flank: a pattern of sharp, jagged lines. The rhythm of its heart is a slower, concentric spiral. The chemical signature of its sweat—salts, organics, fear—is a shimmering, unstable script I cannot yet hold.

I rise. My head breaks the surface of the black pool at its feet. I do not look up. I have no need. I present my skin to the air, to the fractured gold light. The glyphs shift, re-calibrating. For a second, they mirror the strange, asymmetric shape of its shadow on the stone. For a second, they hold the whorl of its fingerprint, captured from the heat radiating from its hand.

Then I touch. My nose, smooth and hard, presses against the wet stone a finger’s breadth from its boot. A transfer. The pressure-vibration of its step, the heat-pattern of its sole, the minute electric field of its living tissue—these I draw in. The glyphs on my skin flare, not with my light, but with *its* imprint. A fleeting, perfect duplication of its retinal scan, woven into the eternal script.

I feel it recoil. A tremor in the stone. A gasp that disturbs the mist. Good. The perturbation is registered. The data is clean.

I sink back into the warm, dark water. The glyphs on my skin begin their slow dissolution, the borrowed patterns being parsed, broken down into their constituent vibrations. The jagged lines of its footstep become a question about material hardness. The concentric spiral of its pulse becomes a query about cyclical time. The organic salts become a complex notation on foreign biochemistry.

I glide toward the lowest terrace, where the resonant moss grows thickest, purple and Velvety in the perpetual drizzle. I brush my flank against the moss. A transfer in reverse. I give it the parsed data. The moss hums, a new layer added to its song. The human’s footsteps, its heartbeat, its fear—these are now part of the basin’s memory, stored not as an image, but as a vibration, a chemical trace, a note in the endless chord.

I continue my path. My skin is blank again, ready. The perturbation will continue. The heat-source will move, will touch other things, will leave other traces. I will follow. I will scan. I will translate.

I am not a watcher. I am a scanner. I am not a creature. I am a process. My will is the gradient. My thought is the thrum. My self is the empty space on the obelisk’s spiral where a new glyph is about to be written, a glyph that will hold, for a week, the echo of a human breath.

The basin is solvent. And I am one of its tools for dissolution. I flow on, a silver whisper in the warm water, carrying the memory of an outsider into the heart of the stone. The thrum vibrates in my bones—*our* bones—and the mint-hum of the moss behind me already weaves the new vibration into its old song. The system stabilizes. The perturbation is integrated. The chord holds.
