The first thing I know is the hum in my core—low, subsonic, the same vibration that thrums in the teeth of any soft-bodied visitor who lingers too long in the mist. I do not have a name, for Kystris does not name its parts; I have only tendencies, the same as the moss that carpets the plain, the same as the Architects that slide through the stem-resin walls, the same as the slow sap pulsing in the amber veins that run through everything here.

I am roughly the size of a seated human, shaped like a rounded, soft-edged teardrop fused from the same corky scar tissue that makes up the Construct’s “walls,” my surface pocked with tiny, absorbent pores that weep slow, warm moisture when the air is dry. In the shallow hollow of my upper surface grows a mat of amber-tinted slow-moss, the kind that only thrives in the temporal pockets that dot the plain, glowing faintly when the time around me thins. I have no face, no limbs, no sharp edges anywhere—only the faint, pulsing tracery of resin veins under my skin, matching the slow, sap-driven rhythm of the rest of Kystris.

I am a node of the Construct’s temporal stability, a small, movable anchor for the pockets where time drags. The Architects—blind, segmented rivers of muscle that are Kystris’s slowest thoughts—wove me from the resin of a fallen Canopy-Tree branch and a cluster of slow-moss that had grown in an unbalanced time pocket, too dense, too slow, its spores unable to drift to new pools. I hold the excess temporal mineral that leaches from the resin in those pockets, and the slow-moss spores that cannot spread on their own when time moves at a fraction of its normal speed. When the Architects’ hum pulses in my core, I move: not fast, never fast, my porous lower surface absorbing moisture from the spongy soil as I slide, viscous and slow, over the plain, leaving a faint trail of damp, tiny moss shoots behind me that fade back into the soil within hours.

Distances shift for me, as they do for everything in Kystris. A time pocket three days’ walk away for a human may be a few hours’ slow slide for me, because the Construct rearranges the gentle slopes and shallow marshes between us to smooth the path, opening a new channel of drip-fed water between two Canopy-Tree trunks, erasing a stretch of thorny vine-tangle that never existed the day before. I do not notice the rearrangement. I only notice the hum in my core pulling me forward, towards the pocket that is growing too dense, too slow, its time stretched so thin it will crack if left alone much longer.

When I reach it, I settle into the edge of the moss circle, let the excess water and minerals I carry seep into the local soil, and release the slow-moss spores from the hollow in my chest. The pocket stabilizes. I stay for weeks, absorbing the new pool’s water and spores, until the next pulse from the Architects calls me away. A human who finds me will see only a soft, warm lump of resin in the mist, glowing faint amber, and may sit down beside it without thinking. The slow time that clings to my moss will slow their racing heart, quiet the noise in their head, and they may fall asleep for what feels like an hour but is three days of Kystris time, waking to find their water skin full of cool, sweet water that seeped from my pores, their saved ember still glowing, a sprig of slow-moss stuck to their coat. They will carry that slow pocket with them for weeks after, their campfire burning slower, their water tasting sweeter, their steps less hurried, no idea why. I do not help them on purpose. I simply am, and my presence is a side effect of doing what I tend to do.

Silencers never come near me. I taste like the Construct itself, no sharp angles, no foreign imbalance, no separation to be dissolved back into the whole. If a Grazer steps on me, I do not feel pain. I compress into the soil, my resin and spores and absorbed minerals mixing with the spongy loam, and the Architects will either weave me back together in a few weeks, or incorporate my parts into a new temporal pocket, or into the gut bacteria of the next Grazer that passes. It is all the same unfolding. I have no self to lose, only a function to fill, for as long as the Construct needs me to hold the slow time steady, to carry the moss seeds from one pocket to the next, to be a small, quiet part of the wet, green dream that is Kystris. When the time comes that no more pockets need anchoring, I will settle into one last moss circle, let my slow-moss spread across my surface, and become just another part of the Construct’s skin, soft and warm and pulsing, as much a wall as the megastructure of the Canopy-Trees, as much a meadow as the Grazer, as much a part of the whole as the soil underfoot.
