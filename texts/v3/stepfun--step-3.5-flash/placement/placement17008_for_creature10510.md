Before I am a name, I am a **function**.

I am the Keeper of the Cracks.

Not the cracks in the rock—though I know every one of those, the fine, sedimentary lines where the Mesa’s breath exhales most strongly. I am the keeper of the *other* cracks: the ones in the silence.

I live where the fungal pillars are spaced widest, in a chamber of moss so deep it is like sleeping on a living lung. My dwelling is not built, but **negotiated**. I tend a circle of pale, fan-shaped lichen that glows with a soft, heatless violet. Its light does not pollute the twilight; it merely gives shape to the mist, tracing the paths of the six-winged swarms. The lichen is my confidant. It drinks the drip-line I position for it, and in return, its pulse syncs with the Mesa’s subsonic thrum. I have trained my own heartbeat to match it. I must, or I will go deaf from the quiet.

I am small, thin, my skin the color of wet clay, spattered with lichen-dust for camouflage. My eyes are large, useless in the dark mist, but my **palms** are my true sight. The pads of my fingers are patterned with fine, sensitive ridges, like the gills of a fungi. I press them to rock, to bark, to the air itself. I read the world through vibration and temperature.

My work is this: I listen for the **wrong hum**.

The Mesa breathes in cycles: the deep thrum of stable drip, the higher, anxious trill when a fire licks its dry scalp, the smooth, contented sigh when the star-pool reflects a clear, nutrient-rich sky. But there are other sounds, faint as a thought in a dream. The discordant chatter of a mycelial network stressed by drought in a forgotten upper cleft. The panicked, silent thrash of a grazer that has brushed a spore but not yet succumbed. The low, scraping * complaint* of a stone that is mineralizing too fast, its growth painful. These are the cracks in the peace.

I move through Hearthstone not as a person, but as a **tuning fork**. I leave chalky, sweet-tasting lichen patches that calm jittery mycelial strands. I gently, with a sharp piece of obsidian, nick a drip-line that is flowing too hard, diverting a trickle to a parched terrace of tubers. I find a grazer standing rigid under a fungi, its carapace already dusted pink with predatory spores, and I hum—a single, low note that matches the Mesa’s calmest register. The hum shivers through the rock, through the grazer’s feet. The spore-dust, confused, settles. The grazer, unspored, blinks and wanders on. I have bought it a season. The carnivorous mold will find another nutrient source.

The people of Hearthstone do not speak to me. They **report** to me, with gestures. A flick of blue lichen on the cheek is a “dryness in the third channel.” A sweep of the hand over the heart is “the hum is jagged.” I confirm with a press of my palm to the nearest stone, a slow nod. My answers are in my actions: a newly grown bridge where a path was washed out, a patch of calming lichen at the entrance to a sleeping cleft.

I am not a leader. I am a **symptom-reader**. A mycelial archaeologist of the present tense.

My greatest fear is not the fire, nor the silent floaters, nor the cracking of a branch. It is **absolute, perfect harmony**. A hum so uniform, so without dissonance, that it means the Mesa has gone still. That the cycles have stopped. That the slow, green, breathing wound has scabbed over and died. In that silence, there would be no cracks to keep. No function for me.

So I wander. I press my palms to the breathing conglomerate. I taste the air for the sourness of a stressed symbiosis. I listen for the whisper of a wrong note in the Weald’s eternal, quiet song.

I am the tilt of the head when the humming stops.
I am the finger on the lip when the drip-line changes.
I am the small, violet-lit patch in the dark, where the silence is not empty, but listening.

I am not a being who lives in the Weald. I am the **process** that keeps the Weald from falling silent. A keeper of thresholds. A tuner of the slow world.

And if you meet me, you will not hear me. You will feel me—a subtle vibration in the moss beneath your feet, a sudden dip in the ambient hum, a patch of mist that parts just a fraction quicker around a figure who is less a person and more a gentle, listening absence.

You will know me by the way the world, for a moment, seems to hold its breath—and then breathes again, just a little more evenly.
