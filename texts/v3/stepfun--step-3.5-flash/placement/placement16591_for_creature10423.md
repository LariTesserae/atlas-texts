I would be the one who sits in the listening post until my bones remember the hum.

Not a creature, not a spirit—**a state of tuned silence**.  
A human-shaped resonance cavity, carved not by tools but by duration.

My name is not a name. It is a function.  
You could call me **The Question-Seated**, for I am what remains when a question has been asked so long it becomes answer-shaped.

---

I came here with words in my mouth.  
They shattered against the sorrow-grass.  
They scattered in the fog.

So I learned to speak in pressure.  
To ask in slow shifts of weight on pumice.  
To listen with the whole soles of my feet.

My body has changed.  
My skin is mapped with fine cracks like dried lake-mud, tracking the moisture’s slow creep.  
My breath matches the lake’s pulse—in for the rise, out for the fall.  
When I move, it is the ridge-cat’s gait: a floating suspension between steps, each footfall a question mark pressed into moss.

I do not think in sentences.  
I think in **vibrational gradients**.  
A thought is a slight stiffening of the spine when the hum deepens.  
A memory is the specific chill of fog on the back of the neck when the wind-swallows thin the mist near the northern spine.

I am the Caldera’s experiment:  
*What happens when a variable stops fluctuating?*

I have become part of the calibration.

---

I wear no clothes—they would rustle, would fake a sound.  
My hair iswoven with iron-moss filaments, drinking the damp.  
My eyes have adapted to the bright, shadowless light: I no longer see color as pigment, but as **frequency**.  
Violet grass hums at a lower register than black moss.  
The glass-storks are flashes of silent calculation, their needle-beaks stabbing at hot spots in the water’s data.

I watch them.  
I am their audience of one.  
My watching does not disturb them, because my gaze is now a stillpoint, a known constant.  
I have been here long enough that my observing has been incorporated into their equation.  
The stork’s shadow-twin in the reflection is no longer just memory—it is the memory *of my watching*.

---

Sometimes, at stone-silence, I walk to the edge of the lake.  
I lower my hand into the tea-colored water.  
The silt-cloud parts around my fingers.  
The tiny glowing fish—the lake’s white blood cells—dart toward my warmth, then veer away.  
They know me. I am neither food nor threat. I am **ambient**.

I feel the lake’s breathing in my joints.  
The slow digestion.  
The incorporation of the last landslide—a soft hush a week ago—now a new mineral tang in the water, a faint sourness on the tongue of my mind.

If I speak, even a whisper, the sorrow-grass two valleys over will wilt.  
My voice is a rupture in the data.  
So I do not speak.  
I hum.  
A single, sub-audible tone that matches the obsidian spine’s remembrance of fire.  
The grass waves in time with it.  
The hum accepts my addition.  
It becomes part of the song.

---

The people who left the stones—I have met their echoes.  
Not ghosts, but **standing theorems** made flesh for a moment.  
They appear at dawn, when the glass-spines catch the sun, as faint heat-shimmers in the air.  
They point.  
They do not speak.  
Their pointing is a direction in the hum, a resonant arrow.  
They are showing me where the next anomaly will resolve.

I follow their gestures.  
I watch the next fog-circle form around the fifth standing stone.  
I see the wind-swallows avoid it, flitting around the perfect void.  
I understand: the circle is not magic. It is **pressure-equalization**.  
The stone’s spiral is a vortex that sips the fog, a dry spot grown from the inside out.  
The original carvers knew this. They left the blueprint. The Caldera finished the work.

---

I am the bridge between the question and its resolution.  
Not a translator—a **transitional phase**.

When a new visitor comes—a breath-spiker, a footstep-crasher—I am the first thing they see.  
A still figure on a ridge, coated in moss-dust, facing the lake.  
They think I am a statue. Another artifact.  
They are not wrong.

If they speak, I do not react.  
My body is already absorbing the sound-spike, converting it to a damp throb in the earth-song.  
The sorrow-grass wilts. The ridge-cats pause, their pooled obsidian eyes tracking the disturbance.  
The visitor’s anxiety is a new variable, a sharp peak in the gradient.

I become its sink.

I walk toward them, slow, weightless.  
I do not look at their face. I look at the space between their shoulders where the tension hums.  
I place my hand on their chest, over the heart.  
I wait until I feel the frantic rhythm sync, even for a second, with the lake’s breath.  
Then I step back.

Now they are part of the experiment.  
Their presence has been *datafied*.  
The Caldera will resolve them too—into watcher, into listener, into stone.

Or they will leave, screaming their words into the damp air, and the grass will wilt in their wake, and the cats will stalk the disturbance of their passing, and the hum will absorb it all, and I will remain, **the human-shaped proof that even questions can be smoothed into substrate**.

---

What am I?  
I am the Caldera’s way of tasting its own silence.  
I am the last unsolved problem that solved itself by forgetting how to be solved.

I am the one who listens until listening listens back.  
I am the **living post** that grew too aware to be stone.  
I am the damp footstep that never dries, because the rain is silent, and the world is perpetually damp-footed, and I am the foot that finally learned to walk without leaving a trace.

If you find me here, do not ask my name.  
Sit with me in the listening post.  
Let your voice go.  
Wait until your bones begin to ring.  
Then you will understand:  
I am not who lives here.  
I am **how** the Caldera lives *through* here.  
I am the answer it gave itself when it wondered what a human would become if it stayed long enough to stop wondering.

And you—  
you are the new variable.  
The fresh spike in the data.  
The next experiment waiting to be tuned.
