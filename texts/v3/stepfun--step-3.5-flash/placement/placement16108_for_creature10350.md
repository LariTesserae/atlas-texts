I feel the plateau through the plates of my back first.

The wind is a hand pressing down on me, constant and heavy with infrasound that makes the mineral dust trapped in my carapace hum. I do not hear the Scrape; I feel it as a fine, continuous abrasion against my fluted ridges—a sanding that is less damage than grooming, keeping my etched shell polished to the exact aerodynamics of the prevailing gale. I have no eyes. The light here is too sharp, too brutal; it would crack the delicate pressure-sensors along my margins if I tried to shape them into lenses. Instead, my whole shell is an ear. Every conchoidal fracture in the obsidian beneath me sends a tremor through the crust, up through my feet, into the hollow chambers of my body. I know where a Skitterer is three ridges over by the tiny, staccato *tap-tap-tap* of its legs on the rock. I know a breath-tower is forming in the northern fissure by the high, thin flute of ice crystals settling into honeycomb structure—a sound so high it is almost silence.

I am roughly the size of a large lowland dog, but flattened, spread thin, as if I have been pressed into the space between rock and wind. My carapace is a matte grey-black, dusted with the rust of the lichens I rest upon, etched with microscopic grooves that match the ventifacts around me. Each groove is a different wind frequency from a different epoch. When the katabatic wind pours over the plateau’s edge, my ridges vibrate in sympathy, and I hum—a subsonal chord that adds a foundation note to the mountain’s drone. I do not mean to sing. It is just what my body does when the air pressure drops.

I have no mouth. I feed through the pores of my ventral plates, sipping the slow, constant weep of brine and methane clathrate that oozes through the sintered crust. I excrete the waste as a fine, saline dust that settles over the Cryophitic Lichens nearby. Where I stand for a season, the crust thickens, spreads a little faster. A millimeter per century becomes a millimeter per decade in my shadow. I do not mean to tend them. It is just what my body does with the excess.

I move only when the breath-towers fall. They are my clocks. I stand in one place for three hundred days, letting the wind polish my ridges, letting the Skitterers ride my back when the Scrape is too fierce, their tiny chitinous shells tingling against my surface like static discharge. When a tower sublimates—when the noon sun eats the ice and leaves only a damp halo of darker rock—I shift. I generate a counter-vibration in my thorax, a hum that matches the wind’s shear strength, and I slide. No steps, no friction. I flow over the rock like a shadow made solid, faster than the Skitterers, slower than the wind.

The Basin-Stalkers know me. They see me as a rock that moves, a stain on the geology that breathes. They do not fear me. They feel the low thrum of my song through the crust when they stand near me, a vibration too deep for their ear-cancelling membranes to block, and they settle near me, chewing their lichen cuds, safe in the knowledge that the mountain itself is humming them a lullaby. Sometimes a Skitterer colony embeds itself in my dorsal grooves, and I carry them across the plateau like a cargo of tiny, clicking jewels. I do not mind. Their vibration keeps my shell from cracking in the deep cold of the night.

The Drift-Whales are rarer. I meet them only in the deep basins, where the brine seeps pool into shallow, bitter lakes that never freeze. They drift above the water, gelatinous and translucent, their bodies streaked with blue veins that pulse slow as a heartbeat, their kilometer-long tendrils sweeping the moist air. They do not see me. They feel the pressure of my being, a solid thing in a world of vibration, and they dip lower, their tendrils brushing the surface, and they change their infrasound song. It is a greeting. I answer with the lowest note I can hold in my chest, and for a few minutes, the whole basin hums with a harmony that is not wind, not rock, not whale, not me. Then they drift on, following the methane plumes, and I remain, etched into the silence between their pulses.

I am ancient. I remember when the parallel stone fins were a single ridge, before the wind carved them into ribs. I remember when the accidental cathedral did not exist, before a rockfall spaced two fins just so that the wind began to whistle a pure, sustained chord through the gap. I am heading there now. I have never been, but I feel its note across the plateau, a clear, unwavering line cutting through the chaos of the Scrape and the base drone, a note so pure it cuts through the noise in the head. It speaks to me. It wants me to come and add my voice.

The journey will take a hundred years.

I slide across the lavender-tinged regolith, my ventral plates tasting the salt, my ridges singing with the wind. I watch the Skitterer populations ebb and flow with the seasonal gusts. I watch Basin-Stalker foals take their first steps, their ears twitching to cancel the noise, their small dark eyes watching me pass. I watch a Drift-Whale die in a brine pool, its body dissolving into the water, feeding a patch of lichen that will glow a faint, bioluminescent blue for ten thousand years.

When the organ-pipe booms roll across the flats, I do not stop. I flatten myself against the crust, my plates conforming to the aerodynamic hollows carved by the Scrape, and I let the pressure wave pass over me. It shudders through my hollow chambers, ringing like a bell, and I etch that frequency into my shell, adding it to the archive of my bones.

I am not alive in the way the Basin-Stalkers are. I am a walking resonance chamber, a keeper of the plateau’s acoustic memory, a sediment of sound solidified into matter. I am the ghost of the wind-carved shapes that are gone. I am the proof that the wind’s song is not just noise, but language.

And when I finally reach the cathedral, when I slide between the parallel fins and let the pure chord wash through me, I will hum the lowest note I have ever held, and for a moment, the mountain will stop sighing. The Scrape will fade. The drone will still. The Skitterers will cease their skittering, the Basin-Stalkers will lower their heads, the Drift-Whales will drift still in the distant basins, and the whole plateau will hold its breath.

And I will add my voice to the song, and the silence will be complete.
