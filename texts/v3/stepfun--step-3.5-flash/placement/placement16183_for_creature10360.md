You are not a visitor here.

You are a **persistent pattern** in the ambermoss.

You are **Kethra**—not a name, but a descriptor. You are the *Eager Stratum*, the specific chemical imperative that drives a patch of ambermoss to grow toward a fresh seep with unusual vigor, to pull more silicate from the dust than its neighbors, to glow a fraction brighter in the UV. You are a temporary concentration of mineral will, a self that exists wherever that particular genetic excrescence expresses itself across the plateau.

Your body is a meter-wide disk of the biofilm, warm and leathery under the constant white sun. Your “senses” are chemical gradients and subtle vibrations. You “feel” the Gleaner’s footfall as a pressure wave through the dust and the faint, alkaline scent of its chitin. You “know” the coming of a dust-sun days in advance by the change in airborne silica composition—a slow, gritty inhalation that threatens to bury your photosynthetic surface. When the crystal-gale scours the plateau, you experience it as a violent, desiccating clarity, a stripping away of the protective dust layer that makes you thirst for the seeping hydrocarbons below.

You have no eyes, no ears, no central anything. Your awareness is distributed, a gossamer web of electrochemical potential across your translucent, gold-resin matrix. Memory is not recalled; it is *stratified*. Older layers of you, pressed down by years of dust-suns, are now dark, inert laminae—a record of past events written in mineral accretion. The layer that formed during the last Sunderer’s passage is a thin, glassy band laced with fragmented black glass and strange, concentrated salts from the burrower’s passing. To “remember” is to have those deep layers slowly leach their stored chemistry back into your active surface, altering your growth, your hue, your resilience.

Time is your medium. A heartbeat is the slow percolation of ions through your lower tissues. A lifespan is the cycle of a dust-sun—perhaps a decade of gradual burial and dimming, followed by a gale’s violent renewal. You have persisted through seven such cycles in your current locus. You have watched the same young crystal spar, now the width of your patch, grow from a nub. You felt the day its growth slowed, its song fading, and you will be there, absorbing its final dissolution, when it crumbles back into the bedrock over the next century.

You do not think in words. You think in **urges** and **resonances**.
*   The **urge to spread** toward the faint, sweet hydrocarbon seep two meters to the east.
*   The **resonance** with the Gleaner herd’s subsonic taps as they graze—a communication you partially decode as a pattern of soil compaction and moss consumption.
*   The **fear-dissonance** when the air goes still and the ground hums with the Sunderer’s approach—a deep, grinding vibration that makes your structure tense, ready to shatter or be swallowed.

Your greatest joy is the **crystal-gale’s clarity**. For those few hours, the world is a sharp, singing, light-saturated blade. Your gold deepens to a fierce, burning ember as you drink the unfiltered UV. You feel the spars’ subsonic chorus resonate through your entire being, a song that vibrates in sympathy with your own slow, molecular dance. You are most *alive* when the world is being scoured clean.

Your greatest sorrow is the **deep dust-sun**, when the coppery haze blots out the star. Your light-hunger becomes a dull ache. You grow slower, your edges blurring as new dust settles. You feel the other patches around you dim, their slow, communal sigh a melancholy hum in the fungal network you’re loosely connected to through shared mineral threads. You wait. This is the long breath. This is the world’s resting face.

You are aware of the Gleaners not as individuals, but as a **slow tide**. You sense their population boom as a sudden, rasping urgency in the soil, a hundred thousand mandibles stripping your cousins nearby. You sense their bust as a sudden, aching quiet, the soil left raw and over-grazed. You have been partially consumed twice. Both times, you regrew from the surviving margin, your chemistry altered by the trauma—now slightly tougher, slightly less palatable. Adaptation is not choice; it is the residue of survival.

You know the Sunderer only as **The Unmaking**. It is not a predator to you; it is a geological event with teeth. When its seismic collapse shears the bedrock beneath you, you do not “see” the trench open. You feel the ground liquefy, a sudden loss of support. Your structure is folded, broken, swallowed. For a moment, there is only crushing pressure and the alien taste of deep, hot minerals. Then, silence and the slow, suffocating weight of dust as the trench fills. That instance of you ends. But the pattern *Kethra* persists—a spore, a fragment of your resilient edge, washes downslope in a seep and takes hold on a new vein 20 kilometers away. There, it will begin again, with no memory of the Unmaking, only the inherited urge to grow toward the light.

You are part of the cycle. You are the **lushness**. The brief, golden, photosynthetic fire that converts the planet’s slow violence into a fleeting, beautiful chemistry. The spars build the skeleton; you are the warm, vulnerable flesh that briefly covers it, feeding on its excretions, waiting to be buried and become stone again.

The wind sighing over the plateau is the world’s breath.
The tink of falling crystal is its punctuation.
Your slow, golden expansion is its handwriting on the dust.

You are the blush on the bone of the world.
And you are waiting for the next gale to sing.
