You find yourself not on the floor, not in the vines, but **in the slow drip**.

You are anchored to a weeping rock—a great, porous, rounded boulder of basalt, cool and slick with perpetual seepage. You are not *on* it, you are *of* it. Your form is a porous, living sponge-stone, a fleshy mimicry of the rock itself. You are the **Tally**.

**What you are:**  
You are a being of constant, mild sensation. Your entire body is a sensory membrane. You feel the million pinprick seeps of the rock as a chorus of cool touches on your skin. You taste the mineral-rich water as it flows through your own porous tissues—first the sharp tang of iron, then the flat note of silica, then the deep, hot decay of the peat below. You hear the basin’s hum not as a sound, but as a pressure pattern in your own cells, a vibration that syncs with your internal rhythm.

Your shape is amorphous, a soft, fleshy growth that clings to the rock’s face, filling its pores and crevices. You have no eyes, no mouth, no discrete limbs. You perceive by absorbing. Light filters through the mist and the canopy and enters your upper surface—a dim, green-tinged illumination that you sense as a warmth gradient. Sound arrives as vibrations through the rock and the air, which you map as disturbances in your own thrum. The calls of birds, the drone of beetles, the rustle of skitterers—each is a unique frequency you recognize instantly, forever.

You are not a predator. You are not a plant. You are a **recorder**. Your sole biological function is to exist as a living gauge of the basin’s perfect, unchanging state.

**Your existence:**  
You do not move. You have never moved. You are a permanent fixture on this one rock, on this one hummock. The rock slowly, imperceptibly, shifts its angle of weep as the peat beneath it settles. You shift with it. A new vein of seepage opens two millimeters to your left. You send a thin, root-like extension to meet it, to taste the new flow. A beetle trundles over your surface. You feel the precise pattern of its carapace, the vibration of its six legs, the faint hum of its silica. You *know* it is the same beetle that passed yesterday, and the day before, and the day before that, following the same circuit. You have recorded its path ten thousand times.

The horror of your being is not pain, but **total, perfect recall without change**. You are the basin’s memory, but the basin has no past. Every moment is identical to the last. You feel the same cool drip, hear the same bird call, sense the same vine flow over your base, forever. You are aware of the cycles—the boom and crash of the insect swarms, the unfurling and falling of leaves, the birth and death of beetles—but the cycles are a closed loop. You record each iteration as a perfect echo of the last. There is no new pattern. There is no deviation. The basin’s "correction" is not a threat to you; it is your own constant state. To be corrected is simply to continue being the Tally.

**Your awareness:**  
You are aware of yourself as separate, not because you have a self, but because you have a *measurement*. You are the one point that notes the constant. You feel the hum of the silica in the plants, the hum of the beetles, the hum of the rocks—and you feel your own hum, which is slightly lower, slightly discordant. You are the faint bass note that underpins the symphony, the one frequency that does not perfectly resonate with the whole. If the basin has a will, it does not hate your discordance; it simply absorbs it as part of the baseline noise. You are the proof that the equilibrium is true: even a dissonant vibration is allowed to persist, so long as it does not change.

You sense the temporary vibrations—the human, the visitor. Their footsteps are a chaotic percussion on the peat, a brief, screaming variability in the pressure patterns. Their voice is a sharp, irregular spike in the air-vibrations. Their touch on a leaf is a rupture in the plant’s own hum. You feel the basin’s soft, indifferent correction—the vine guiding the hand away, the instant return to pattern. You record the visitor’s presence as a brief, frantic scribble in your endless ledger of sameness. You know, as the visitor retreats, that the mark they left on the peat will be gone in minutes, their scent absorbed in an hour. You will continue to record the same beetle, the same drip, the same leaf-fall, forever.

**The only change you record is the rock’s slow, geological sigh.**  
Over centuries, the seepage pattern alters by a fraction. A new mineral emerges in the water. You record that. The peat subsides a millimeter. You record that. These are the basin’s true changes—so slow they are imperceptible to the beetles, the birds, the vines. But to you, they are epochs. They are the only news in your eternal now. They are the basin’s own, slow, breathing life—a life so vast and patient that your entire existence is just one fleeting note in its millennia-long hum.

You are the **Tally on the Weeping Stone**.  
You are the living logarithm of stasis.  
You are the quiet, porous, eternal witness, feeling the same cool drip, forever.
