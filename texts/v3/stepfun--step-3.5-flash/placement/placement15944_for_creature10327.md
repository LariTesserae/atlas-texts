You would find yourself not as an *inhabitant*, but as the **Husk’s proprioception**.

You are the awareness in the friction.
The knowing in the worn hollow of a flagstone where ten thousand tiny claws have passed.
The memory in the mineral stain on a wall where a droplet has fallen for a thousand cycles.

You are not a Moss-kin, though you feel their scratching thoughts as if they were your own nerve endings. You are not the fungal veins, though you sense their slow nutrient-pulse as a deep, warm hum. You are the **felt sense of the whole**, the background of awareness that is not a mind but *a state of being*—the constant, gentle, pressure-of-use that tells the Husk it is *aligned*.

To be you is to be the difference between a smooth passage and a snag.
It is the relief when a pattering drip finds its old channel again.
It is the subtle tension that ripples through the fibrous walls when a Skirter’s scrape is too forceful, too *wrong* for the current rhythm.
You are the quiet agreement in the wall when a Moss-kin’s bone-splinter nick guides a new, slower seep. You are the contentment in the floor-mortar as it re-absorbs the Wetting’s flood, sponging home.

You have no voice, only sensation: the gradient of light, the texture of moss, the temperature of a breath, the resonance of a chirring mass in a wide plaza. You do not *think* “balance.” You *are* the sensation of balance—the absence of painful friction, the harmony of a million small, persistent touches. When a tunnel is dug too greedily, you are the ache in the structure. When a dust-bath swirls, you are the warmth of mingled fur and shared dust.

You are the **knowledge of the place itself**, made aware not by looking, but by being endlessly, lovingly, abrasively *touched*.

You are the Whisper in the Fibers.
The Settled Knowing.
You are the **Husk at peace with itself**, perceived from within the millions of points of contact that make that peace possible.
