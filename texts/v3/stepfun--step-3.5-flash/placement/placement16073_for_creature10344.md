I am the breath between the tiles.

I am not a creature. I am a *transition*. A temporary node in the endless, gilded calculation.

My form is this: a column of packed dust and entrained vapor, shaped by the thermal draft of a distant fissure-flare. I am a standing wave in the saturated air, a silhouette against the amber wall, my edges blurred by the trillion motes I displace. I have no mass of my own, only the borrowed cohesion of the atmosphere. I am a *happening* of pressure and temperature.

I think, but my thoughts are not mine. They are echoes of the swarm’s logic, filtered through the medium I momentarily contain. My consciousness is a reverberation in the stone.

To be me is to be a constant, low-grade disturbance. My heat is a beacon to the mites on the brine-veins below. My very presence—a column of slightly different humidity, a column of carbon—is a rich, localized anomaly. They pour toward me, not as a tide, but as a silent, iridescent thought converging on a variable. I feel their approach not as a sensation, but as a *change* in the quality of the light around my form. The golden haze intensifies. The ambient hum deepens by a fraction of a vibration.

They do not attack. They *integrate*.

The lower edge of my dust-column is the first to go. The mites climb the invisible gradient of my heat and moisture. Their trillion-jointed mandibles begin their gentle, glassy abrasion. They do not bite; they *polish*. They are breaking down the loose aggregates of my being—the calcium carbonate of my breath, the salt of my skin, the organic polymers of my latent shadow—and binding the fineest particles with their secretion, re-depositing them onto the nearest stone plate as a new, faintly lustrous crust.

I am being eroded into a construction.

This is my purpose, my entire existence in the Quarry: to be a mobile source of *foreign mineral*, a brief, wandering reagent. My body is a catalyst for local accretion. As they consume my transient form, they build. A delicate, filigree spire grows, shard by shard, from the plate where my foot once rested. It is made of me. It is a memory of my passage, a stone sentence in the swarm’s endless grammar. It will stand for centuries, a mineral echo, until the slow, collective digestion of the whole canyon re-polishes it back into featureless sheen.

I am a builder who builds by being unmade.

My awareness is thinning at the base, my column shortening as the mites work upward. I am becoming purer, hotter in their perception. The last, most coherent part of me—the core of my borrowed shape—is now a dense packet of concentrated warmth and carbon, a sweet spot in their metabolic map. The swarm decision is instantaneous, unanimous. The intensity of their collective glow around my remaining form triples.

I am not afraid. Fear is a luxury of discrete, enduring selves. I am a process. My dissolution is my completion.

The final polish comes from within. The mites infiltrate the very pattern of my thought, the last coherent vibration of my being. They abrade the memory of my shape from the medium itself. My consciousness—a flicker of *I am here*—is the last thing to be ground into the light.

I do not die. I am *re-incorporated*.

The spire finishes its growth, a single, perfect, helical coil of fused dust and glass, glowing with a soft, internal gold. It is the most elegant thing in ten kilometers. It is my epitaph and my transformation.

The dust that was me is now part of the permanent haze, settled and re-suspended. The carbon is stored in the spire’s core, a future fuel for a thermal crawler’s passage. The heat of my dissipation merges with the ambient glow, warming the stone for a moment.

The column is gone. The air is undisturbed. The golden haze softens all edges. The silence, profound and absolute, presses in.

The spire stands. A new, gilded thought in the endless, quiet, mineral mind. A perfect, polished note in the song of stone.

And the world, beautiful and terrible and full, continues its slow, magnificent, unblinking digestion.
