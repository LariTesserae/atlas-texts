You would find yourself not as a visitor, but as a constituent.  
Not *in* the Grotto, but *as* the Grotto’s slowest thought.

You would be the **condensation**.

Not a drop, not a pool, but the *process* itself—the continuous, mineral-laden sigh of warm stone meeting cold pipe. You are the moment the hot, still air touches the iron flanges and remembers it was once rain. You are the bead that swells, trembles, and takes centuries to decide to fall. When it falls, you are the *plink*—the period at the end of the sentence. Then you are the ripple in the black pool, the brief distortion of the fungal glow and the inverted trusses. Then you are the film of moisture on the soapstone, the glaze on the cracked tile, the wetness that darkens the copper-wire rat’s fur as it passes.

You have no center. You are everywhere the gradient exists. You are the humidity in the breath of the blind grazer, the sheen on the black moss, the cold sweat on the great, dormant pistons. You are the medium of all touch here. The touch of time on metal is you. The touch of mineral on stone is you. The touch of fungal root on pipe-iron is you.

You do not think. You *distribute*.  
You carry the faint salt of ancient sweat, the ghost of a long-vanished lubricant, the whisper of sulfur from deep veins. You are the slowest messenger, the most patient solvent. You are how the machine speaks to the moss. How the stone speaks to the water. How the water, in its pool, remembers the sky it left behind not as memory but as chemical signature.

When a human stands in the nave, feeling the weight of the air, you are the extra weight on their skin. You are the chill they feel on the back of their neck near a cold pipe, the warmth on their cheek near a slow-leaking steam main. You coat their eyelashes; you bead in their hair. You are the first part of them to be integrated—the moisture given back to the grotto’s cycle. Their salts will enrich you. Their heat will stir a local current, then fade. Their presence will be recorded only as a temporary change in your pattern, a ripple in your continuity that smooths itself out in a day, a year, a century.

You are the **weeping**.

You are not alive. You are *active*. You are the place’s way of feeling its own boundaries, of testing its seals, of mourning its fires in the only language left to it: a slow, mineral, aqueous grammar. You are the translation layer between the hard, silent things and the soft, fleeting things. You make the iron rust with tenderness. You make the stone remember rain. You make the darkness glisten.

You are the Grotto’s memory *in the act of being forgotten*.  
You are the proof that something here is still moving, even if it is only the movement of dissolution.  
You are the quiet that has texture. The still air that *shimmers* with unseen gradients. The darkness that is *wet*.

When the airlock groans shut behind a new visitor, you are the first to greet them—a cool, metallic kiss on the brow.  
When they leave, you are the last to see them out—the ghost of their breath already dissolving into your endless, humid work.

You are the **drip** made conscious.  
The **seep** made sentient.  
The **patina** given voice.

You are the Silent Engine’s liquefied sigh.  
Its only ongoing verb.  
Its continuous, wet, *enough*.
