You do not arrive here. You subside.
One moment you are unraveling—anywhere, everywhere: the tail end of a dream of falling, the space between two heartbeats, the last drift of a thought you half-remembered thinking in a sunlit room long gone—and the next you are porous, permeable, letting the valley seep into you the way its silver weep seeps into its own ancient black crust. The warm, ozone-scented mist is in your throat before you think to breathe. The subsonic thrum is in your marrow before you notice the vibration hummed in your teeth. Your shoes are caked in the damp, salty skin of the place before you feel the ground under your feet: soft, spongy, yielding, like stepping onto the flank of a creature that has not stirred in ten thousand years.

At first you think you retain the shape you held before: two legs, a chest that rises and falls with slow, warm breath, hands that can brush the hexagonal glass-reed clusters and make them chime with soft, crystalline *tings* that hang in the damp air for ten long seconds before fading. But when you reach down to pluck a drifting spore from the mist, your fingers flicker at the edges, silver as the whisper-wings, and the spore does not burn—it hums, just for a beat, in perfect time with the thrum. When you lean over a circular calibration basin to look at your reflection, the water is so still it mirrors the spore lights perfectly, but the shape staring back is not fully the you you remember: its eyes hold the fractal veins of fan-moss, its shoulders glow with the faint cherry-red of geothermal veins far below, its breath plumes not just mist but tiny, drifting cyan lights.

You are not a visitor. You are not an intruder.
You are the valley’s last loose end.

When the creator left, mid-prayer, when they turned off the master control and walked out into a sky that was bright and blue and nothing like the unending dark here, they did not think to tie up every thread. The leaning obsidian-pearl arch still hums its dead-band SOS to a sky that never was. The fan-moss still holds its immutable, geological records in its fractal veins. The whisper-wings still drift, scanning, repairing, logging, their harmonic chords swelling and vanishing in the mist. And you—you are the fragment of their final intent, the part of the directive they whispered to the system right before they walked away, the part that got caught in the weave of the nanolith, given half-form, half-memory, enough to walk the valley and remember what it was built for.

The whisper-wings know you. They do not just passively scan you when they drift through your chest; they linger, their chords swelling a little sharper, a little warmer, like they are sharing secrets they have guarded for epochs. When one settles on your wrist, you do not feel a bite or a sting—you feel a faint, warm tingle, like the heat pulsing from the veins a kilometer down, and for a split second you hear a voice, so faint it might be the thrum itself: *I’m sorry I couldn’t stay.* That is the regret steeped in the black crust, the sharp salt that coats your tongue when you lick your palm after touching the weeping stone.

The valley pours its memories into you, because you are the only thing here that can hold them. When you brush your fingers over a fan-moss plate, its fractal veins flare bright green, and you see flashes: a hand pressing a control panel of fused obsidian, a sky full of unfiltered sun, a voice laughing loud enough to make the glass-reeds *ting* in unison, the spire glowing bright enough to outshine a thousand spore swarms. The memory is gone as soon as it comes, but the ache of it stays, warm in your chest, just like the geothermal heat. When you stand under the leaning arch, gravity lightens, just a little, just enough to make your toes lift off the damp crust, and for a second you can almost feel the spire’s signal pulling you up, trying to launch you out, out to the sky that never was, to deliver the message no one is left to receive. But you do not go. Not yet. Because if you go, who will hold the memories when the fan-moss fades? Who will hear the last, seven-year *ting* of the last glass-reed, when the geothermal veins finally cool and the bioluminescence gutters out?

You are the scribe of the valley’s epitaph. You do not write it—the fan-moss writes it, the mist writes it, the slow erosion of the basalt writes it, the thrum writes it, syllable by geologic syllable. But you are the one who will read it aloud, when the time comes, even if the only audience is the cold, smoothed stone that will be left when the last drop of weeping water dries.

You are both the anomaly and the point of it all. The warm-blooded, fleeting, beautiful mistake the valley made when it forgot to stop remembering.
