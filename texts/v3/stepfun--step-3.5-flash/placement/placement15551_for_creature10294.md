I am the Still-Watcher of the Third Basin.

My seat is a throne of living stone, a basalt shelf worn smooth by generations of still backsides. It faces east, toward the Veil of Weeping Glass—the waterfall that never falls, its mist hanging in frozen curtains. I do not watch it with my eyes. I watch it with my bones.

The sub-hum is in my molars today. A good, steady thrum, like a deep string plucked beneath the world. The mountain’s heartbeat. It has always been this pitch. If it changed, even by a hair’s breadth, my teeth would know. They would ache with a wrongness that is not pain, but *unfit*.

My task is to feel the gradient that isn’t there.

I have been Sitting for seven years. Before me was my teacher, Old Tarn, who had Sat for forty-two. He taught me not to look, but to *listen below looking*. The moss-fields breathe. The grazers blink in a rhythm that is the valley’s exhale. The cisterns hold their water with a tension you can feel in the hollow of your own throat if you are still enough.

Most days, there is nothing. Only the plink, plink, plink—the valley’s metronome—and the hum in my jaw. The air tastes of wet flint and forever. My vines are happy today. The little curtain of dark-green ivy that grows from my left temple is growing at exactly the same pace as yesterday and the day before. Its leaves are as wide as my thumb’s nail, and they will never be wider. This is right.

A bird cry—*see-eee*—pierces the mist and fades. The grazing herd in the middle basin—the one with the scarred mother—lifts her six heads in unison, then lowers them. Their movement is a single sigh. Perfect.

But then…

A flicker. Not in sight. In *pressure*.

The mist between the Glass Veil and the North Spire—it shimmers. Not with wind. With a tension. Like a held breath trying to escape.

I do not move. My heart does not beat faster. It slows. To match the plink.

The moss on the shelf to my right… its emerald is dulling. For a fraction of a moment, it turns the grey of old ash. Not a patch. Just a single frond. Then it is emerald again.

Stasis-breath.

My mouth is dry, but I do not swallow. Swallowing is a motion. Motions create gradients. I let the moisture gather, a perfect bead on my tongue, and I hold it.

I stand. My joints do not crack. They are well-oiled by a lifetime of stillness. I take three steps—*right, settle, left, settle*—to the edge of the shelf. Below, the basin spreads, a carpet of un-changing green. I am a fixed point. I must remain a fixed point while I work.

I descend the Stone-Singer’s Path—the zig-zag cut into the cliff face by my great-grandsire. Each step is a known placement. My foot fits the hollow worn by ten thousand Still-Watchers before me. I do not look at my feet. I look at the moss on the wall beside the path. The grey flicker is gone. But the pressure in the mist… it is a question now. A whisper of *almost*.

At the base of the path, the flawed frond lies on the scree. It is not on the ground. It is *placed* there by the stasis-breath itself, a marker. I pick it up. It is cool and papery in my palm. Perfectly dry. It will never decay. I hold it to my chest and walk to the High Cistern—the black, still pool at the valley’s heart, where all wrongness is submerged.

The Pattern-Keeper, Elen, is already there, waiting. She does not speak. She holds a basalt bowl filled with water from the North Seep. I place the grey frond into the bowl. It floats. The water does not ripple.

“The Third Basin’s breath is strained,” I say. My voice is a low stone. It does not echo.

Elen nods once. Her eyes are the colour of wet slate. “The Veil’s tension is noted. The Stone-Singers will listen at the Source-Crack at the next Plink-Song.”

“The frond?”

“Will be placed in the Deep Archive.” She means the cave behind the cistern, where flawed things are stacked like silent books. “It is a small breath. But a breath is a gradient.”

We do not fear the change. We fear the *unnoticed* change. The rot that is not rot. The ripple that is not a ripple. Our world is a held note. A single, wrong vibration can shatter the chord.

I return to my shelf. The hum in my teeth is steady again. The mist hangs, motionless. The grey frond is gone, submerged. The emerald moss on my shelf is pure, perfect green.

I Sit.

The next plink falls into the cistern below.

*Plink.*

It is the only sound that is not a pattern.

It is the sound of the world remembering itself.

And I am its ear.
