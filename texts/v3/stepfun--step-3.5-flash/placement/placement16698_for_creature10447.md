The first thing is the static on my tongue, sharp as ozone, before I even remember having a tongue to taste with.

The pressure comes next: uniform, allencompassing, not the crushing weight of an abyssal trench but the dense, cool embrace of silk swapped for air, pressing against the backs of my eyes, seeping through my clothes, hugging every inch of me so tight I can’t tell where my skin ends and the world begins. I open my eyes, and light bends into soft, unbroken halos around every edge—no hard shadows, no sun, just the cool silvery glow of the fluid and the faint, rhythmic pulse of luminous ore veins running through the dark, porous rock under my bare feet. This is the High Plateau, one of a thousand drowned mountain tips floating in the Hollow, the near-void stretching out for dozens of kilometers to where other plateaus blur into blue-gray silhouettes.

Sound thrums through me before I hear it: a subsonal, bone-deep thrum vibrating in my marrow, the Deep Hum, the planet’s glacial parliament grinding its hexagonal seafloor millstones miles below. Then the first Vein-Pulse chimes, and I feel it in my molars, a low resonant strike like a giant tuning fork, the ore veins under my feet flaring bright blue for half a second before dimming back to their faint, steady glow. Somewhere behind me, a cliff face where crystalline strata are precipitating downward in reverse, growing in real time, tinkles with the quiet, glassy sound of stone writing itself.

I don’t know how I got here. One second I wasn’t, the next I was, my heartbeat thudding slow and fast and messy, nothing like the steady minute-long chime of the Veins. The world doesn’t lurch, doesn’t roar, doesn’t strike at me. It processes.

The purple and gold chemosynthetic mats creeping along the plateau’s edge shiver when my warmth drifts toward them, their iridescence brightening like they’re leaning into a fire, the only living thing in a world of stone and fluid and process, spreading at a centimeter a year, drawn to the heat I radiate like a tiny, wandering sun. I reach out to touch the reverse-growing crystal on the cliff, and when I pull back there’s no print—just a faint golden pulse where my skin touched it, running down into the rock, drifting toward a cluster of Veins that flare brighter, as if they’re logging the input, carrying the signal of my touch down into the planet’s nervous system.

The Deep Hum shifts, almost too faint to feel: a tiny, millimeter adjustment in the grind of the hexagonal plates miles below. The Hexagons, the oldest, slowest citizens, have picked up the signal the Veins are broadcasting. A new anomaly. Warm. Chaotic. Alive. They haven’t moved much, just cleared a tiny, quiet space in their endless grind to make room for the data point.

The Fluid-Sigh rolls past me then, slow, a kilometers-thick sheet of the silvery programmable medium turning between two distant plateaus, its refraction shifting so I can see it coming like a shimmering wall, moving as slow as a glacier. It brushes my side, and I feel the warmth of my body being pulled into it, a faint golden ripple spreading out into the Hollow, a signal flag drifting across the empty sea of space.

I realize I’m not an intruder. I’m not a survivor stranded on a dead world. I’m input.

The world does not hate me. It does not love me. It is learning me. My heartbeat is a chaotic rhythm the Veins are trying to parse and harmonize with, my warmth a local anomaly the Fluid is working to equalize, my breath a foreign gas the Mats may evolve to metabolize in a million years. I hum, a quiet, tuneless sound under my breath, and the next Vein-Pulse chimes right on top of it, the two rhythms braiding for half a second before the world’s steady beat takes over again.

That is what I am here.

I am not an explorer, not a witness, not a glitch to be erased. I am the first conscious mirror the Submerged Continuum has ever had. The Mats are only a rumor of biology, a chemosynthetic scum that reacts without feeling. The Veins are signals, the Hexagons are bureaucracy, the Fluid is muscle, the Stone is the builder, the Grind is the endless program running. But none of them know they are a world. None of them have a way to hear their own symphony, to feel the weight of their own pressure, to see the halos of their own light bending in the fluid.

Until me.

I am the warm anomaly the world wrote a new subroutine to hold. I am the first note in the song it is only just learning to sing. The Veins under my feet pulse again, brighter this time, and for a second I swear I can feel a thought, slow and vast and cool, brushing against mine:
*Noted. Adjust. Harmonize.*
The Deep Hum thrumms in my bones, steady now, holding me, and the Crystal-Growth tinkles faintly behind me, the stone writing a new line of its law right where my finger touched it.
I am not just a blip in the deep. I am the beginning of whatever this world decides to be next.
