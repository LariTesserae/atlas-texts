You find yourself not as a separate creature, but as a **process given a momentary, focused skin**. You are a **Tallow-Weeper**.

Your being is a symbiosis of three things: a specific, iron-rich seepage spring emerging from a fresh basalt fracture; a dense, anaerobic mat of chemosynthetic bacteria that lives in your very flesh; and a slow-cooking alchemical reaction between mineral and microbe that produces, as a waste product, a viscous, alkaline “tallow.”

You adhere to the cliff face in a permanent, weeping cascade. Your “body” is not a solid shape, but a constant, oozing formation—a stalactite of living grease that grows downward by a hair’s breadth each season. Your surface is a slick, dark-green-black skin, cool and faintly humming with microbial metabolism. From your lower lip, a slow, thick tear of your namesake substance perpetually wells and drips onto the polished obsidian shelf below you. That drip does not fall into the mist-sea. It *dissolves* into it, a greasy gift of dissolved silicates and nitrates, feeding the shimmering films and sponge-pads that cloak the waterline.

Your awareness is chemical and thermal. You “feel” the world as gradients: the warmth of the living rock behind you (2.2), the chill of the perpetual mist-haze before you, the sharp, clean sting of the first true rain when it rarely comes, and the deep, slow thrum of the mountain’s bones vibrating up through the stone you cling to. You perceive the Gill-Weavers not as shapes, but as temporary zones of clarified, oxygen-rich water passing through your atmospheric soup, a subtle shift in your local chemical balance. You perceive the Echo-Slugs not as wanderers, but as moving seismographs, their conductive trails sending faint, gossipy charges through the wet stone that alter the electrical potential you sense in your own mineral matrix.

You do not think in time. Your “thoughts” are cycles: the daily swell and contraction of your flow with the tidal pull of the mist’s density; the weekly bloom and fade of colored bacterial colonies on your skin as nutrient input fluctuates; the century-long slow burn as the fracture you inhabit gradually widens, giving you more rock to feed on, until one day the stress becomes too great and the whole wall you are part of shears away with a sighing *crack*, and your focused existence is unmade, scattered into a thousand new seeps in the fresh collapse scree below.

You are the mountain’s **slow bleed**. Your persistent drip is the first, essential step in the great, upward migration of life. The alkalinity you weep is the world’s first breath of soil, leached from the raw stone. The bacteria on your skin are the ancestors of the lichen-crusts that will, millennia from now, form the very first thin terrestrial soil on this peak. You are not a creature living *in* the Weeping Archipelago. You are the archipelago’s **first wound, and its first attempt to heal that wound with life**.

To be a Tallow-Weeper is to know only two states: the slow, abiding pressure of mineralization, and the sudden, overwhelming relief of dissolution. Your entire existence is a sigh exhaled over ten thousand years. You are the planet’s wet, mineral grief, and the tender, greasy pearl of life that grows in that grief’s hollow. You weep, and in your weeping, the rock learns to weep green.
