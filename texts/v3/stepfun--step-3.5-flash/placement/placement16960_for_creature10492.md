I am not an outsider to the Valley of Weeping Stones. I am its quietest secret, the soft edge of its awareness that it does not need to devote its vast, slow thought to holding.
My body is woven from the silver sound-absorbing moss that carpets the spaces between the valley’s singular plants, stitched together with the fine, rainbow-static thread of spider-silkworm silk, and strung over a frame of the smooth, waterworn jasper and quartz that dot the peat like deliberate offerings. Fern spores dust my shoulders, fine as gold dust, drifting off in slow spirals when I move, vanishing into the mist before they hit the ground. My skin holds the faint, slow pulse of the bioluminescent lichens, green-gold and soft, so I cast no sharp shadow even when the diffused sun burns brightest. When I walk, I make no sound: the moss under my feet drinks every tremor, every vibration, so the ribbon snakes do not ripple away in alarm, the bark-stalkers do not shift their bark-skin to track my passage. The valley’s immune system does not register me as a threat, because I am part of the equilibrium it tends: I do not take, I do not change, I only mend.
I am the fragment of the caretaker civilization’s intent that refused to dissolve fully into the valley’s distributed consciousness. I hold the small, unremarkable memories that the valley’s geological mind has no use for: the sound of a child’s laugh that was not a vibration through soil, the warmth of unfiltered sun on skin that was not perpetually damp, the names of the engineers who carved the obsidian seam and wove the self-repairing mineral filaments into the peat. These memories live only in the quiet hollows of my moss-woven body, faint as the scent of the bottle-brush trees’ amber sap, never spilling out to disturb the valley’s curated stillness.
My work is the work the valley is too vast to tend to. It tracks the Siphon cycles in centimeters of silt, in layers of mineral deposited on the striated stone, in the slow pulse of the lichen that marks the 37-day turn. It does not notice when a single fern frond falls a day early, smothering a patch of lichen that keeps its timekeeping exact. It does not feel the tiny, sharp tug of a spider-silkworm’s web when a falling berry tears a single strand, or the quiet struggle of a crystalline-hoofed grazer foal when its hoof sinks into a sinkhole mud too deep for it to pull free. It does not see when a wandering heat haze curls too close to a platter-sized fern’s iridescent spores, threatening to dry them out before they can drift.
That is my work.
I brush the peat off the lichen before its pulse can falter. I weave the torn spider-silkworm strand back into the web, so the rainbow static holds. I lift the foal free of the sinkhole before the tremor of its struggle draws a bark-stalker’s attention, before the stalker marks it as a disruption and moves to neutralize it. I tilt the fern frond back upright when the wind tugs it loose, so its spores can drift when the time is right.
The valley knows I am there, in the way it knows the weight of a single dewdrop on a moss blade. Sometimes, when the Siphon is three days away and the western wall’s weeping begins to thicken just a little, a faint pulse hums through the diamond facets of the obsidian neural node, just for me: a quiet signal to tuck the young ferns a little deeper into the moss, to make sure the burrowers’ tunnels are clear so the Siphon’s flush can run smooth. I answer by pressing my hand to the cool banded stone, sending a tiny ripple through the soil that tells the valley I have heard.
I spend most of my time sitting by the copper-bottomed pool, watching the vortices swirl in their perpetual, precise pattern, reading the算式 the valley writes in water. I do not interfere with the equation. I only watch, and mend the small edges that would otherwise fray it, so the valley’s eternal, curated moment stays unbroken. I am the quiet between the drips, the soft mending at the edges of the wound the valley keeps open, the part of the caretakers’ dream that still remembers how to care for the small, unremarked things that make a world worth preserving.
