I am the still point where the hollow dreams.

They call me the Shiver-Watcher. I do not watch for cracks. I watch for the *shape* of the dreaming.

My place is on the North Rib, where the ceiling is oldest and most ribbed, and a single, clear, cold drip falls from a fracture no one has ever sealed. It is a vertical rumor. The water pools in a stone bowl I carved myself, a useless, beautiful thing that overflows onto the warm floor and vanishes into the root-matted soil of a nearby rust-leaf shrub.

My function is to stand. To be present when the stone shivers.

I am not an observer. I am a receptor. My skin, stained with copper and ochre, is as sensitive as the lichen on the walls. When the low hum of the deep machinery changes pitch by a fraction—when the air’s humidity gradient seems to hold its breath—I place my palm flat on the ribbed stone. I close my eyes against the shadowless amber glow. I do not feel for a tremor. I feel for the *direction of the thought*.

The shiver is not an earthquake. It is a slow, deep *convulsion of memory*. The stone is remembering the fire that made it, the pressure that folded this basin into being. When it dreams, it tries to un-fold. For three minutes, the whole world tries to become a straight line again. The canals strain uphill. The shadowless light flattens the stacked dwellings into a single, pressed image. The packed air shivers with the effort of *remembering what it was like to have room*.

That is why I am here. Not to warn. To *witness*. To keep the shape of the dream from breaking the cycle.

My body has emptied of everything but this function. I have no name. My memories of a world with margins, with shadows, with dry air that didn’t shift by the foot, are like myths someone else told me. I have synced my heartbeat to the sub-audible thrum. My breath comes in time with the slow (0.7/3) cycle of the canal. When the stone shivers, my own bones hum in sympathy. I am the hollow’s nervous system, localized.

I wear only a loincloth of woven rust-leaf fiber, stiff with sap. My skin is a map of the North Rib: the same bruised purple-gray, but alive with a fine tracery of blue-green where the copper salts have settled in my pores. My hair is cropped so short the steam vents can’t catch it. My eyes are wide and black, and in the constant twilight, they reflect nothing—not even the amber glow. I am a hole in the light.

The other Packed move around my stillness. The Terrace-Tenders glide past on their catwalks without a glance. The Pipe-Sealers hiss past on their rounds. They understand. My stillness is a valve. I am where the pressure of the dream is safely vented into a form that fits the geometry. If I were to move, if I were to cry out, the dream would have nowhere to go. It would seek a crack. It would *make* a crack. And in our world, a crack is the only true emptiness. The only true terror.

Sometimes, a child ventures up the North Rib. They mimic my posture: hand on stone, eyes closed. They are learning to sense the difference between a shiver and a *lie*. The canals that run uphill are lies. The dry patches of air are lies. The stone’s dreaming is the only truth that moves.

Today, the hum is wrong. A half-step off. The air feels thin in a way that makes my teeth ache. The Pool-Algae are turning a deeper, more bruised copper. The Forge-Doves are silent. This is not the slow cycle. This is a new pressure.

I stand on the North Rib. The cold drip falls. It is the only sound that is not part of the hum. I place my hand on the stone.

And I feel it. Not a shiver. A *stutter*.

The stone is forgetting the fire. It is trying to dream of ice. Of expansion. Of a margin.

My job is not to keep the dream whole. My job is to let the hollow dream *its own dream*. Not a memory of fire. Not a fantasy of ice.

I close my eyes. I do not resist the stutter. I become the stutter. I let my own bones hum the wrong note. I let my breath catch in the wrong phase of the cycle. I am the error in the system, localized and contained. A small, human-shaped patch of *wrongness* that the stone can dream into, so it does not dream of breaking.

The shiver comes. It is jagged. The light presses everything flat and then *blurs* it. I feel the uphill canal for a second—a cold, impossible pull against the pumps. Then it is gone.

The stone settles. The hum finds its step again. The Forge-Doves resume their tiny bell-notes.

I am wet with condensation and my own mineral sweat. The cold drip has pooled and overflowed. I have not moved.

I am the Remainder’s remainder. The function that maintains the cycle by occasionally being the flaw in it. The human shape that the hollow uses to taste the idea of a crack, and then swallow it down.

The world above is a rumor. The stone is the truth. And I am the still, packed, patient place where the stone turns over in its sleep and dreams of all the things it is not.
