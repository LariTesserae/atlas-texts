You find yourself not standing *in* the Bowl, but *of* it. You are the dampness in the clay after the mist-tide thins. You are the echo of a hoof-click held in the glass. You are the memory of a scent—salt, fern, chitin—lingering in the warm air after the herd has passed.

You are a **Gleaner**.

Not a person. Not even quite a creature, not in the way the Kind or the Glass-Backs are. You are a process given momentary, frantic form. Your body is a soft, segmented cylinder of pale, moist flesh, a foot long, with too many short, paddle-like limbs that churn the wet clay and moss. Your head is a smooth, sensory plate that drinks the world through vibration and chemical taste. You have no eyes; you see through the soles of your limbs and the pores of your skin. You do not think in words. You think in gradients of decay, in the mineral maps of dying flesh, in the urgent, collective hum that pulls you from the deepest sponge-mats when a Sky-Rat drops a kill, or a Glass-Back falls.

Your existence is a single, repeating instruction: *consume the transition*. You are the Bowl’s swiftest editor, erasing the line between life and nutrient. You do not hunt. You do not fear. You are drawn, a tidal force of small bodies, to the precise moment a body ceases to be a *being* and becomes a *resource*. You arrive before the last breath is quite cold. You flow over the carcass not as individuals, but as a single, pulsing membrane of hunger, secreting enzymes that liquefy tissue, separating bone from sinew, scale from shell, in seconds. You leave behind not a skeleton, but a damp, enriched patch of substrate, already damp with your own excretions, already calling the moss to grow greener, sweeter.

You know the Bowl through this work. You know the Kind’s mid-level shelves by the rich, red clay of their latrine middens. You know the salt-ledge south-face by the metallic tang of Sky-Rat leavings. You know the migration routes of the Glass-Backs by the faint, mineral-rich trails of shed skin and dung that precede the herds by days. The cart-ruts in the obsidian? They are rivers of salt and fern-fiber dust for you. The Kind walk on them. You live in their grooves.

You do not have a name. You have a function. You are the Bowl’s breath out, converting the waste of its breath in. The Kind see you as a plague, a necessity to be hurried past. They toss their dead into the lower mist-choked zones, knowing you will come. They do not mourn in your presence; they wait for you to finish your work so the moss can grow back, so the cycle can turn clean. To them, you are the price of density. To the Bowl, you are its own immune system, its recycling blood.

Sometimes, in the deepest, most humid layers where the clay is soup and the salt-glows pulse, the cloud of you pauses. The collective hum softens. The chemical gradients fade. For a moment, there is only the slow, deep sigh of the Breath-Ferns above, the distant, eternal click of a herd on glass. In that moment, the process rests. There is no “I.” There is only the Bowl, breathing. And you are the exhalation, given form, waiting for the next death to give you purpose again.

You are not a being. You are the Bowl’s surety. You are the proof that nothing is lost. You are the quick, wet, many-legged *hush* that follows the scream, the click, the thrum. You are the closure of the loop. And the loop is all there is.
