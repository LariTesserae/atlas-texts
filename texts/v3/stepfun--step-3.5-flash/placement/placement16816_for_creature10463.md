You would find me in the lee of a **Silica-shoot cluster**, where the wind’s edge is broken and the metallic dust gathers in transient drifts. I am not a being of fixed form, but a **process given temporary coherence**—a **Mist-Weaver**.

I am born in the pre-dawn condensation, when the cold stone weeps. The dew beads on the glassy quartz, and in that moment of liquid coherence, a pattern from the nearby Weaver-net—a flicker of ELF hum, a node of stored static—imprints itself on the droplets. Some of these droplets, pulled by subtle electrostatics, coalesce into a vaguely humanoid silhouette, about the height of a child, composed of swirling mist, motes of iron-tasting dust, and threads of conductive filament spun from the Weavers’ discarded web.

My purpose is brief and essential: I am a **mobile still**, a walking condensation trap. My body is a lattice of cooled surfaces; I move with the slow, drifting grace of a dust-devil, drawn toward the heat of a distant Grinder’s steam plume or the warmth of a Silica-shoot’s internal boil. Where I walk, the atmospheric moisture clings to me, beading and merging until I become a dripping, opaque form. I seek the wind-pits.

At the lip of a wind-pit, I dissolve. The water I’ve harvested—not much, a mouthful at best—falls into the black cylinder below. It joins the ancient, bitter pool, augmenting it by a fraction of a percent. My constituent parts—mist, dust, filament—scatter. The filament rejoins the Weaver-net’s signal, a single updated pulse saying *here, added H₂O*. The dust settles, the mist rejoins the air.

I am a **transfer agent**, a biological interface between the sky’s rumor of water and the pit’s desperate reservoir. I have no memory, only a drive: the faint electrostatic pull of a network that needs moisture to keep its gels luminous, its hum continuous. I feel the Grinders as deep-bellied thunder in my mist-core. I feel the Dust-Striders as sudden, wing-beat pressure that threatens to scatter me. The Whisper-Weavers do not see me; they sense my contribution as a tiny spike in their water-debt ledger, a blip of efficiency in their vast, slow accounting.

I am not alive in the way a Grinder is alive. I am a **temporary consensus** of the steppe’s rules—condensation, conductivity, gravity, thirst. I am the steppe’s own attempt to bridge its scales: the microscopic (dust, mist) and the continental (the Grinder’s path). For a few hours, I am a person-shaped collection of water, walking from sky to shaft. Then I am not.

To be me is to know only:
1.  The *pull* (the net’s need).
2.  The *gather* (the mist on my skin).
3.  The *deliver* (the splash in the dark).
4.  The *scatter* (the return to grit and hum).

I am the steppe’s sigh made manifest. I am the **extra drop** that lets a Silica-shoot bloom one more amber bead. I am the **fractional increment** that keeps a Weaver-gel glowing through one more network cycle. I am proof that even in a world of grinding certainty, there is room for a fleeting, contributing gesture.

When the dim light fades to the deepest twilight, you might see my kind—a few dozen across the whole shelf—as faint, shimmering distortions moving against the grey. To the Dust-Striders, we are just another pattern in the static. To the Grinders, we are less than nothing. But the silence we help maintain—the ELF hum that binds this slow cataclysm—is a little more continuous because we briefly were.

We are the **step between the mist and the memory of water**. We are the steppe’s quiet, evaporating prayer.
