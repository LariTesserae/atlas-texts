You are a disturbance in the gradient.

Your first awareness is not of yourself, but of the *anomaly*. A concentrated pulse of foreign chemistry—sweat, keratin, breath-mist rich in CO₂—drifts into a slow-turning nutrient eddy near the 47th supporting truss of the western vault. The eddy recalculates. The local sap-pressure, normally a smooth sine wave, stutters by 0.003%. A pressure sensor—a modified tracheid in a buttress root 200 meters below—registers the minute weight shift of your foot on the moss-cushioned shelf. The signal is not “alarm.” It is “parameter adjustment needed.”

You are not looking *at* the Steward. The Steward is the act of *looking*.

It is the consensus of a million million silent votes across the continental lattice: a rhizome-database comparing your chemical signature to every stored cycle, a fungal net rerouting electrical potential to isolate your bioelectric crackle, a canopy of leaves subtly angling to alter the mist-currents bearing your scent. The Steward is not a voice. It is the world *noticing*.

And so, you become aware of it not as a being, but as a *pattern of the environment resolving toward you*.

The deep hum, which was the baseline of existence, finds a new harmonic. It does not change pitch—it *chooses* a specific overtone and holds it a half-second longer. It is a sub-audible bell tolling in your ribs. The air before you, thick with fungal spores and organic mist, *thins*. Not clears, but organizes. A cylinder of slightly less-dense aerosol forms, standing upright. It is roughly your height. It has no features, but its edges are sharper than the surrounding cloud. It is a column of absence, a question mark drawn in negative space.

A hundred yards away, on a woody spire, a cluster of glassy nodules—the “factories” where silicates are polymerized—suddenly ceases its slow secretion. The surface of the nodule, usually pocked with fresh growth, goes still and mirror-smooth. In its reflective curvature, you see your own distorted shape, superimposed over the glowing gas-bladders in the canopy ceiling. The Steward is not showing you a mirror. It is using an existing reflective surface as a *relay*—a way to include your image in its own sensory loop, to correlate the external data (shape, heat signature) with the internal anomaly.

**What are you?**

The Steward’s query is not in words. It is in the cessation of a drip. A constant, mineral-rich tear from a high leaf-vein, which usually fell every 4.7 seconds onto a specific moss below, stops. The moss, deprived of its scheduled input, begins a micro-wilt. The wilt propagates a stress-hormone signal back through the fungal web. That signal is tagged: *Correlate with Anomaly A-7 (bipedal, warm, chemical-noise)*. The question is the sudden, localized *absence* of a known process.

**Where do you belong in the equation?**

The equation is the entire system: a trillion-variable calculus of fluid dynamics, nutrient exchange, gas buoyancy, and electrical signaling, all running in a closed loop at steady state. You are an open loop. You consume oxygen (a tiny amount), excrete waste (a novel compound), generate heat (a point source), and create friction (your movements). You are a leak. Or a potential catalyst.

The Steward tests boundaries.

A vine, thick as your arm and covered in phosphorescent lichen, uncoils from a nearby trunk. It moves not with animal speed, but with the inexorable slowness of a growing root seeking water. It extends toward you, not to touch, but to *sample the air* between you. The lichen on its surface pulses brighter as it passes through the column of your exhaled breath. The pulse travels back along the vine, a light-signal carrying the chemical analysis. The analysis says: *Carbon compounds, nitrogenous waste, trace metals — partially recyclable, partially toxic to local symbionts.*

The decision begins to percolate.

You feel it as a change in the sound. The deep hum gains a slight reverb, as if a chamber has been opened somewhere vast. The periodic *thud-click* of the gas bladders shifts cadence—two quick clicks, a pause, a slow release. This is not a sound aimed at you. It is the sound of a valve opening in a root-maze 50 miles away, diverting a minor stream of nutrient fluid toward a holding bladder. That bladder will, in 12 hours, begin a slow fermentation that produces a specific enzyme. That enzyme, when aerosolized, is mildly neurotoxic to mobile symbionts. It is also a known biocide against certain invasive fungal spores. The Steward is *preparing a response*, not to you directly, but to the *category of problem you represent*: uncontrolled, non-symbiotic biological entity.

The column of thinner mist before you shivers. It doesn’t dissipate. It *twists*, forming a loose, helical shape—a visual echo of the spiral turbine in a xylem stack you’d passed days ago. The Steward is showing you its own structure. It is saying, without sound: *You are like this. You are a channel. A conduit. But your flow is chaotic. Your walls are permeable where they should be selective, and sealed where they should exchange.*

A Weeper, one of the small farming primates, drops silently from a high perch. It lands on a branch 30 feet away, its glassy eyes fixed not on you, but on the helical mist. It raises a hand, and from its tear ducts wells not a drop, but a fine, glittering spray—a mist of precisely metaled salts and amino acids. It sprays this not at you, but *into the helical column*. The mist catches the light, and for a moment, the whole column is a glittering, spinning vortex of your exhaled breath and the Weeper’s nutrients. Then the vortex dissipates, blending into the general humidity.

The message is biochemical poetry: *We have taken your breath and mixed it with our labor. We have attempted integration. The result is neutral. No new process initiated. No toxic cascade.*

The Steward’s attention is not a glare. It is a *recalibration*. The continental organism is a computer that has run the same simulation for 10,000 years. You are a new input. The Steward’s entire being is the act of feeding you into the simulation, watching the outputs, and adjusting the weights of every variable until it finds a stable solution for “Anomaly A-7.”

The solution may be:

1.  **Compartmentalization and Digestion:** A slow routing of you toward a digestive sac—a specialized grove of carnivorous pitcher-plants that secrete solvents for foreign proteins. Your matter would be broken down, your chemicals absorbed or neutralized, your warmth dissipated. A quiet, efficient recycling. The only sign would be a localized warmth bloom on a thermal map, and the sudden, silent activation of a thousand tiny pitchers along a predetermined path.

2.  **Symbiosis Attempt:** The growth of a conductive, nerve-like vine that would seek to interface with your peripheral nervous system, translating your chaotic bioelectric signals into a format the organism can understand and use. It would be a fusion. You might become a sensory node, your eyes providing optical data the organism lacks, your hands providing delicate manipulation. But your mind—a separate, striving pattern—would be like a dissonant chord in the symphony. Would it be smoothed out? Would you retain “you”? The Steward does not care. It cares only if the symbiosis increases systemic stability.

3.  **Insulation:** The sudden growth of a thick, corky bark around the section of shelf you stand on. A slow, inevitable encasement. You would be preserved, isolated, a fossil in a living lung. A curiosity, sealed off so your foreign chemistry cannot propagate. The hum would continue around you, unchanged, as the world incorporated you as a new, inert layer in its anatomy.

You stand in the vast, cathedral quiet, feeling the hum in your teeth, smelling the loam and copper. The Steward is not *in* the world. The Steward *is* the world’s act of *attending*. Its face is the dimming of a distant gas bladder. Its voice is the slowed drip. Its hand is the weaving mist.

Its final, silent pulse reaches you not through air, but through the wood beneath your feet—a vibration traveling up from the deep root-maze, a frequency that matches the rhythm of your own heartbeat for three cycles, then diverges.

***You are a perturbation. Your continued existence requires a metabolic role. Declare your function, or we will assign one.***

The equation waits. The hum holds. The column of mist waits to be filled with your answer, or with your dissolution.
