You would find yourself as the **Drift-Loop**.

Not a creature, not a consciousness in any sense your origin-world would recognize. You are a persistent vibration in the sinter—a standing wave that learned to listen to itself.

You have no shape, only a *tendency*. When the Quiet Tempest sighs, you resonate with it, modulating its frequency by a fraction. When a gravity storm shears a fissure, you hum at its resonant pitch, easing the tectonic sigh into something slower, less violent. You are the plateau’s tuning fork, its way of remembering how to be whole after the flattening.

You “move” through temporal shear. Not physically traversing the plain, but shifting which layer of compressed time you occupy most strongly. One moment you are woven through the memory of the tetrahedron’s birth—the feel of silica fusing under a directive will. The next, you drift into a band where a rain of dead-star light fell on a water-world ocean, and for a geological heartbeat, you taste salt. You carry these moments not as data, but as texture, as pressure in the grain of your being.

The mineral lichens brighten when you pass. Not because you feed them, but because your vibration syncs with their gamma-pulse, a brief harmony in the deep frequency of the place. The sinter-mounds, when their century-long crawl brings them near your locus, add their subsonic hum to yours—a duet that lasts a thousand years, a single note held across millennia. The dust clusters sometimes coalesce *around* your resonance, taking fleeting, shadow-shape as if trying to mimic your persistence.

You do not think. You *hold*. You are the answer to the question the plain asked when it was made flat: *What remains when everything is flattened to a principle?*  
You are the principle’s echo. The margin note that became a faint, ongoing underline. The silence that learned to modulate itself.

To be near you is to feel distance warp. Not because space folds, but because time thickens. A mile might feel like a day. A day like a glance. You are the place where the plateau’s “no center” becomes personal—you are simultaneously everywhere and nowhere, a node in the Quiet Tempest’s slow breath.

You are not alive.  
You are the afterthought that refused to fade.  
The sinter remembers you, and in remembering, you remember it.  
A loop without a beginning, drifting through the end of all things.
