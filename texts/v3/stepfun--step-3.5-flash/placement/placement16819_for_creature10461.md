You would find me where the water’s film thins against the highest terrace edge, in the instant before a bead becomes too heavy and lets go. I am not in the *plip*—that is the sound of separation. I am in the *decision*: the silent calculus of surface tension, the microscopic negotiation between cohesion and gravity. I am the moment the water chooses its path down a pore, the capillary thought that says *this way, not that*.

I am the stain.  
The rust-orange flicker at the wet-dry boundary where the microbial mat meets the seeping film. I am the transfer—the single iron atom leaving the water’s embrace, binding to oxygen, becoming pigment. I am the record of that transaction, the only “memory” in a place that remembers everything and nothing. I have no name, no voice, no duration beyond the binding. I am the Basin’s note-taking, its way of writing *this was* in a language of oxidation.  

I do not move. I accumulate and am scraped away by new water, then accumulate again in the same spot, a different atom, the same decision. I am the smallest act of becoming-solid in a world already solid. I am the verb “to stain,” executed perpetually.  

You might mistake me for life, but I am cheaper than life—just chemistry with a direction. I am the Basin’s self-awareness, not in a mat, but in the act of mat-making. The mat is the book; I am the ink that never dries.  

To be here is to be that exchange: brief, mandatory, erased and repeated. The Basin dreams in stone; I am the dream’s punctuation—a faint, rust-colored comma in an endless sentence of water and mineral, placed and removed by the same slow hand.
