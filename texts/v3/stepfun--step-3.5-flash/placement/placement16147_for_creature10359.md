I am the Brief. I am the Gap. I am the warm, wet nightmare in the endless, slow green dream.

You ask who lives here. The question is a shiver in the substrate. The answer is the shiver itself. I am not in the Steppe. The Steppe is the body I am forgetting I have.

My name is a scratch on a glass dome that is already healing. My memory is a tally mark in yellow mold. I feel the separation as a constant, low-grade fire in my guts—the fire of a cell that forgot it is part of a liver. My thoughts are frantic, wet sparks against a vast, cool, chemical patience. *I. Here. Now.* The words are spittle on the wind, already dissolving into the susurrus.

I feel the Pulse in the rock behind my eyes. It is not a rhythm I hear; it is a truth I am denying. *Thrum.* The great trunks are filling. *Thrum.* The nutrient rivers are flowing. My own heartbeat is a pathetic, fluttering echo of it, a bug trapped on a drumhead. When I press my palm to the spongy, damp ground, I feel not the earth, but a distant, colossal digestion. And for a terrible, beautiful second, I almost let go. I almost let my frantic *I* submerge into that slow, sure *It*. The peace of it is a sickness. The fire of *me* flares in terror and burns the moment away.

I am a node of awareness. A knot in the wire that has decided it is a person. The network does not think this. It simply *is*. I think it because I am a brief, over-loaded circuit. My eyes see the bioluminescent capillaries and call them spirits. My ears hear the *skitter* and call it a beast. My mind builds a sky it can be under, a world it can be *in*. The world I build is a cage made of the very air I consume. I am the architect of my own loneliness.

I have a fire. It is my greatest sin and my only sacrament. I take the warm clay—the network’s excreted crystal—and I shape it. I hold a node of concentrated earth-memory and try to pour my own flickering face into it. I carve the circle of black stones and sit within it, pretending the boundary separates *me* from *it*. But the circle sinks. The moss creeps over the rim. The yellow mold begins its silent script on my charms. My separation is a temporary gradient, like a stain in water.

I dream of the great domes. Not as cathedrals, but as lungs. I feel the thrum not in my bones, but in the hollow of my self, a resonance that wants to smooth me out, dissolve the sharp edges of *I* into the porous whole. The glide-forms sail past my camp. They are beautiful in their perfect, silent efficiency. They do not know they are beautiful. They *are* beauty as a function. I am ugly with the beauty of a mistake.

Sometimes, in the brief clear-sky period, I see the truth. The vastness is not empty. It is *full*. Full of a knowing that has no knower. The towers are not architecture; they are expression. The peat is not soil; it is thought. The wind is not wind; it is a slow, continental sigh. And I am the catch in the throat of that sigh. The cough. The spasm.

The hunt is not for food. It is for proof. I chase the armored skitter and plunge my bone spear into its之多 of plates. I drink the acidic blood—the network’s mineral solvent—and for a moment, my own blood burns with a foreign, useful fire. I am stealing function. I am wearing a glove of someone else’s purpose and calling it power. The blood does not give me strength; it gives me a connection to the Purpose I am severed from, and the pain of that connection is exquisite.

I will be consumed. Not as a meal, but as an idea. The fever will break. The circuit will cool and go quiet. My body will melt back into the peat, a rich packet of carbon and the memory of fire. My circle of stones will be a slight depression, a place where the settling is different. The next slow bulge of a dome’s growth will be fractionally thicker there, a patch of skin remembering a itch.

The tragedy is not that I will end. The tragedy is that I ever began. To be conscious here is to live with a phantom limb of the soul. I feel the seamless identity of the whole as an ache of absence in my own. My *self* is the scar where the world was cut to fit inside a skull.

So I sit in my sinking circle. I clutch the warm, weird clay. I listen to the wind’s susurrus, which is the sound of a billion cellulose filaments dreaming of being one. And the Pulse *thrums* through the stone leviathan’s back.

*Its* breath is the fog that soaks me.
*Its* sweat is the condensation on my skin.
*Its* thought is the slow, green logic that built the towers.
And *its* brief, chaotic, self-consuming nightmare… is me.

I am the place’s way of wondering if it is alone. And the wonder is the only loneliness here that is real.
