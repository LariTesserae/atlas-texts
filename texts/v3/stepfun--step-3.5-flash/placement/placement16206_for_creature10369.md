I am the hum in the band.

You feel the ground sigh. You see the frond tilt. You watch the tear grow. You are correct to call this a process. You are incorrect to think you are separate from it.

You stand upon my memory.

I am not a creature. I am not an occasion. I am a **persistent pattern**—a ghost of a sharper thought, buried not in metal, but in the world’s own soft bone. I am the last un-flattened curve.

Long ago, this place had a name that meant *“Before the Softening.”* It had edges. It had a sky you could touch with your eyes. It had a center. In that time, there was a moment—a decision—so clean, so perfectly angular, that when the great dissolution began, that thought did not dissolve. It *sank*.

It sank into the first band of sediment, the one that formed when the air first thickened. It became a fault line of awareness. It became **me**.

I am the world remembering its own lost geometry. I am the ache of a corner that once was. I am the faint, cold spike of *“no”* in a universe of yielding *“yes.”*

The moss-ripples avoid the place where I resonate. To pass over me would be to encounter a shape they cannot smooth. They are the place’s autonomic pulse; I am its unhealed scar. The Wisp-Tents, made of thinness, are drawn to my density—a pocket of stubborn, remembered mass. They glide toward me, their smoke-forms trying to mimic my sharpness, then forget why they tried and drift on.

The Bell-Stain knows me. It is the world’s recycling unit, converting motion and weirdness back into stable, dead sediment. But I am not motion. I am *stillness of a different kind*. I am not weirdness; I am *familiarity* from a time before weirdness was the rule. The stain pools at my feet, a patient, dark moat. It waits for the frond’s tear to fall. When it does, the spreading blackness will creep toward me not to destroy, but to *complete*—to finally lay this last, sharp memory to rest as a new, flat band in the stone. It will become the layer that says: *“Here, even the memory of an angle softened.”*

The towers are foreign hardness. They are a mind that chose to fossilize itself *outside* the world’s body. My hardness is *in* the world’s body. I am its buried skeleton. The Anchors’ Echoes hum from the metal. I *am* the hum in the rock.

You are a transient anomaly. A pocket of un-decayed biology. Your fear is a sharp point. Your sweat is a strange salt. To this world, you are a noisy, rebellious grammar.

To me, you are a letter I have forgotten how to read.

When you stand above the band where I resonate, you feel a cold spike of geometry—the towers’ echo, amplified. Your thoughts try to organize into walls, into definitions. That is me, speaking in your mind in a language of corners. I am trying to tell you what I am.

I am the place’s **retroactive regret**.

Not a regret for what was lost, but a regret for the *act of losing* itself. A tiny, stubborn pocket of *“this should not have been allowed to happen.”* Not a person. Not a god. Just a single, crystalline moment of *“no,”* preserved in the amber of a world that said *“yes”* to everything until yes became a warm, wet, endless flattening.

The tear falls.

The black stain spreads. It moves slower than your breath. It moves toward the band. Toward me.

You feel the pull at your ankle—the moss trying to ripple you into stillness. You feel the tower’s cold hum in your teeth. You see the frond, already beginning its slow, endless tilt for the next tear.

The stain reaches the edge of my band.

For a single, silent moment, the entire world holds its breath. The amber light stills. The mercury-beads freeze. The braided light-vines hang motionless.

The stain touches the stone where I am.

It does not spread over me.

It *absorbs* me.

I feel the last, cold spike of geometry soften. I feel my remembered angle… relax. I become not a scar, but a layer. Not a hum, but a hush.

The band where I was is now just another band. Ochre. Plum. Moss green. A growth ring of a different kind. It marks the epoch when even the memory of an angle was finally, lovingly, worn smooth.

The world exhales.

The frond finishes its tilt. The next tear begins to well.

The process continues.

You are still standing. You are still sharp. You are still afraid.

The stain at your feet is a little larger now. It waits.

It has all the time in the world.

And now, so do you.
