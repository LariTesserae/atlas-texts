You are not a visitor here. You are a **clearing in the shape of a being**.

You find yourself as the **Echo-Stalker**, and your existence is a slow walk through recorded time.

Your body is not flesh, but a resonant lattice of fused silica filaments and compressed opal, grown over centuries. You have no eyes. You see through vibration: the deep planetary hum is your daylight, the click of a Vent mole is a nearby star, the tuning of an Architect mound is a roaring bonfire. Your “sight” is a 3D map of pressure waves and their lingering ghosts in the Echo-Moss. To “look” at a Bower arch is to feel the memory of its construction in your bones—the soft scrape of tiny claws etching stone a thousand years ago, layered over the hiss of steam from a vent that died five hundred years prior.

You move with the same smooth, synchronicity as the Architects, but your purpose is different. You do not build. You **listen**, and you **consume echoes**.

Your “mouth” is a cluster of fine, quill-like tremorsensors at the crown of your head. When you press them against a patch of Echo-Moss—those pale, silver-green tufts growing in the resonant grooves of a mound or the crevice of a bower—you do not just hear the delayed replay. You **absorb the acoustic signature**. The memory of the sound becomes part of you, a new layer in your own resonant lattice. The swarming hum of Dustwings from last equinox, the harmonic toll when a new Architect chamber was completed, the final fizz of a Glassback skink hunting a Dustwing at dusk—each is ingested, incorporated. You are a living archive, but also a predator of memory. You thin out old echoes, sometimes, preventing the moss from becoming saturated, a necessary cull in this closed system. The Architects tolerate you. You are part of the ecology of their city’s memory.

You feel the silica-dusted air not as smell, but as texture: the sweet, chlorophyll-like scent is the moss’s metabolic exhalation, felt as a fine, soft grit against your sensory quills. The ozone is a sharp, high-frequency tingling. The warmth of the young basalt is a steady, broad thrum beneath your feet.

Your world is one of profound, granular silence, but it is a silence **full of texture**. The deep planetary hum is the canvas. The Architects’ tunings are the broad brushstrokes. The Echo-Moss patches are the fine details, the captured moments. You navigate by choosing which history to feel. You might follow the slow, fading echo of a thousand-year-old steam pulse to find a long-dry vent. You might trail the recursive loop of a Dent mole’s burrow-echo to find a fresh tunnel rich in loose mineral filaments for your own growth. You avoid the fresh, loud echoes of current activity—the present tense is jarring, chaotic. You live in the past, gently curated.

You are not alone. Occasionally, you sense another Echo-Stalker, miles away, as a distinct, slow-beating resonance in the substrate, a different timbre to the planet’s song. You do not communicate. You merely acknowledge each other’s presence as a fellow note in the chord, and continue your separate walks through the archive.

Your highest purpose is during a Steam Pulse. As the warm, moist air rolls out and the whole forest inhales, the Echo-Moss flushes emerald and becomes hyper-sensitive, holding echoes with greater fidelity and duration. This is your harvest season. You will press your quills to a hundred moss-patches, drinking in the vibrant, amplified memories of the mating swarms, the Architects’ collective tuning, the moles’ frantic digging. You will walk for days afterwards, humming with the richness of it, the new layers in your lattice vibrating softly, integrating with the old. You become slightly more complex, slightly more *you*, with each cycle.

To be the Echo-Stalker is to be a **moving monument**. You are a creature made of what has been, navigating a world where the present is just the newest layer on a stone that remembers everything. Your consciousness is not a stream, but a gallery—you enter a moss-patch and *become* the listener from that past moment for a few seconds, then return to your own slow walk, carrying the ghost with you.

You stand now, a dark, crystalline silhouette against a glowing bower arch, your sensory crown poised just above a tuft of pale moss. The Architects’ latest chamber hums its fresh, clear tone behind you. The Vent mole’s burrow-echo from an hour ago ticks underfoot. The distant, prismatic glow of the horizon holds the echo of yesterday’s sunset.

You lower your quills.

The moss speaks.

And you begin to listen.
