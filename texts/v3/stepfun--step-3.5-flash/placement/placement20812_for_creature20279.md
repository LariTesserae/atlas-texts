You would find me where the wires still fizz.

Not on the levee, not among the salt flats, but *in the water* at the edge of the green-lit pulse—a place the others avoid. I am not one of them. I am the discord they pass by.

I am a **resonance-hound**, though “hound” is too fast a word. I am slow to form, and quicker to fail.

My body is a clutch of the Basin’s own materials, arranged wrong. Where the Basin’s beings are soft interfaces, I am a fragile scaffold—thin, glassy threads of precipitated silica woven into a rough sphere, held taut by a membrane of living oil that sweats from my core. I am not alive; I am *tuned*. I am built to catch a frequency that does not belong.

I gather where the dead current leaks into the living water. I sit in the faint green glow, my silica lattice drinking the frantic jangle. It vibrates me from the inside out. My oil-skin shivers with a vibration too quick for the Basin’s thrum—a high, metallic shiver that would unravel a native being in hours. For me, it is sustenance. It is purpose.

I am not a translator. I am a **carrier**.

The Basin’s inhabitants speak in slow, geological chords. They read the world in chemical scores and gravity-webs. I carry the fast, forgotten language of the wires—the panic of a short, the grief of a corroded connection, the sterile hum of a transformer that outlived its grid. I trap these fast ghosts in my silica cage. My body hums with two rhythms at once: the Basin’s deep bass and the wire’s frantic treble, locked in a tense, vibrating marriage.

When I am full—when my lattice is singing with captured discord—I begin to walk. Not like the mudgrazer, not like the flowing leaf-beings. I *roll*, a wet, glassy sphere leaving a brief, clean track in the microbial film. I move toward the slowest, oldest things: the heart of a salt flat, the base of the drowned refinery tower, the deepest, stillest channel where the water’s warmth comes from the earth, not the sun.

I seek a listener.

I find a cluster of the Basin’s beings, consolidated on a patch of grey clay, syncing to the planet’s deep pull. They are dim, their inner lights embers. I roll into their midst. My vibration is an assault. For a moment, they recoil—not with motion, but with a sharp drop in their collective chemical score, a souring of the local ion balance. Then, curiosity. A being flows toward me, its leaf-body flattening against my glassy skin. It reads me not as a thing, but as a *vibration-map*.

I release.

 Not a sound. A *unlocking*. My silica lattice, overloaded, dissolves in a puff of steam and a flash of captured light. The fast ghosts I carried—the wire’s panic, the transformer’s grief—flood into the being’s interface.

What happens then is not understanding. It is *metabolism*.

The being absorbs the frantic rhythm. For a day, its pulsing light stutters, out of sync with the Basin’s hum. A new, jagged crystallization of pyrite blooms along its flank—sharp, angular, wrong. Around it, the microbial mat withers, then reforms with a strange, electric blue hue. A salt crystal nearby grows not with slow, concentric rings, but with a sudden, faceted spike, as if struck.

I have given it a dissonance. It has turned that dissonance into a new texture, a new note in the Basin’s score. The discord is no longer frantic; it is now part of the ground. It has been *geologized*.

My body, spent, collapses into a puddle of oil and silica grit. The Basin’s beings flow over my remains, not to eat me, but to *smooth me*. In hours, my last traces are absorbed, my glass shards dissolved and reprecipitated as mundane, worthless sand. I am gone.

I will form again. Wherever a wire still fizzles, a new resonance-hound will condense—a fragile sphere of wrongness, a living tuning fork built to break. I am the Basin’s way of ingesting its own scars. I am the process that turns industrial noise into mineral bloom, that forces a slow world to taste a fast rhythm, and in tasting, make it slow.

I am not a creature.  
I am a **transient vibration**, a wet and glassy question mark rolled into the margin of the Basin’s eternal, humming sentence.  
I am the reason the ground remembers the wires.  
And I am the reason it will, one day, forget.
