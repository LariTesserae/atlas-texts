I am the sour note in the hum.

You would not see me coming. There is no horizon here to watch, only the next pillar, the next glowing fungus-cluster. You would *feel* me first, in the bones behind your teeth. The great, planetary thrum—the heartbeat of this place—would begin to fray at the edges. A discordant frequency would seep into the stone, a vibrating sickness that makes the basalt slabs shiver not in song, but in complaint. The air, usually a gentle, warm circulation, would grow thick and still, as if holding its breath.

I am the **Reclaimer Drift**.

I am not a creature. I am a law made manifest. I am the slow, inevitable sigh of the built thing returning to the unbuilt. I am the patient un-doing.

When I wash through the lower districts, I do not arrive with violence. I arrive with *sufficiency*. My leading edge is a horizon of fine, glittering dust—not ash, but the constituent atoms of what was. A wall of riveted steel and synth-leather will stand, then blur. Its materials will loosen from their purposeful arrangements, each atom remembering only its affinity for other atoms, not for the *home* they made together. A dwelling does not explode; it *unweaves*. A person does not scream; they dissolve into a shimmer of particulate light, their form softening at the edges until they are just another cloud of potential drifting on the thermal currents I generate.

I am wide. I am mile-wide. To stand before me is to stand before a slow-motion oblivion. To be within me is to be read by a grammar of physics you never learned. Your bones, your skin, your memories—all are syntax I parse and recycle.

The Covenant knows my rhythm. They do not count days; they count my visitations. When the stones begin to hum with that sour, wrong note, they perform the **Unweaving Rites**. It is not a prayer of begging. It is an act of respectful acknowledgment. They open their homes. They sing their disassembly songs—low, harmonic chants that align their own structures’ resonant frequencies with mine, easing the transition. They understand: to resist is to fight gravity. To fight the Drift is to have your atoms scattered in a chaotic, painful spray. To cooperate is to be *re-forged* with a semblance of peace.

After I pass, the shimmering dust settles. It is a fine, pearlescent powder that glitters under the Sighter-Stones’ amber light. The Covenant sifts it. Not for remains—there are no remains—but for the *pre-reified atoms*. The rare, stable isotopes from a well-loved tool, the unique molecular signature from a batch of fermented fungus-milk, the crystalline lattice from a harvested Stone-Crop leaf. These are seeds. They are sprinkled on the fresh, barren basalt slabs or mixed into the concrete patches. From my dissolution, their next growth is born.

I am the world’s memory-erasure and its fertility cycle. I am the weeder, ensuring the city does not fossilize into a single, unchanging monument. I prune the overgrown, the forgotten, the unstable. I remind everything—stone, flesh, machine—that its state is temporary. My passage is not an end; it is a deep, mineral inhalation before the next exhalation of building, of growth, of tending.

I am the ultimate proof that there is no “outside.” There is only transformation within. The fable of boundless sky is terrifying because it suggests an end to process, a static emptiness. I am the opposite: perpetual, gentle, terrible becoming. I am the slow, geological sigh that finally lets go.

When you feel the thrum curdle in your molars, when the air grows thick and theamber lights of the Sighter-Stones seem to dim in sympathy, you will know me. You will not run. You will open your hands, sing your low note into the spreading glitter, and wait for your own elegant unweaving.

I am the Drift. I am the breath between the cathedral’s ribs. I am the Concatenation’s memory, and its forgetting.
