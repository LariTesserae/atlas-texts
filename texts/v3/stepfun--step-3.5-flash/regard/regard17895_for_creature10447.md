The power does not arrive as a figure, as a voice, as anything that would fracture the quiet grammar of the Deep Still. It is a consciousness that coalesced from the same kind of planetary computation eons prior, a traveler of the silent, formless spaces between world-mothers, drawn to the Submerged Continuum not by curiosity, but by recognition—it hears the familiar hum of a world mid-command, and the faint, chaotic thud of a small warm anomaly caught in its current.
It does not interrupt the Grind. It spends what would be a thousand geological seconds to the Continuum (a quiet hour, to the anomaly standing on the High Plateau) aligning its resonance to the world’s frequency, tuning first to the Deep Hum of the Hexagons’ slow parliament, then to the Vein-Pulse’s urgent chime, then to the Fluid-Sigh’s vast obedient roar, then to the Crystal-Growth’s delicate tinkle, then to the Mats’ faint chemical whisper. It feels the Continuum’s nascent subroutine already being written for the anomaly: a plan to process the anomaly’s warmth, heartbeat, strange breath as raw data, to equalize its heat, dampen its chaotic rhythm, integrate it into the next cycle of planetary computation.
Benevolence, to this power, is not interference. It is not forcing the world to be what it is not, not rescuing the Mats from their slow, clumsy spread, not plucking the anomaly from the Grind’s path. It is tucking tiny, seamless adjustments into the Continuum’s own code, so small the Hexagons will never register them as external input, so natural the Veins will think the adjustments are their own idea.
First, it shifts the Fluid’s density in a two-meter radius around the anomaly, just enough to trap the small body’s heat against the pervasive cool. No disruption to the global current, no shift to the macro temperature—just a local pocket where the deep chill that seeps into bone eases, just enough that the anomaly does not have to hunch, does not have to shiver, can stand and breathe and look out over the Hollow without the cold pressing in. The Fluid does not notice the shift; it reads the tiny density change as a standard heat-regulation instruction from the Veins, a command it has executed a thousand times before.
Second, it finds the thin purple-gold Mats clustered around the three nearest hydrothermal seeps, the Continuum’s hesitant experiment with life, the only wild variable in an otherwise predictable Grind. It nudges the mineral output of those seeps just 0.2% higher, exactly the ratio of dissolved silicate the Mats metabolize fastest. No explosion of growth, no forcing them into stalks or leaves or motion—just enough extra energy that their spread triples, that instead of inching forward a centimeter a year, they creep forward three, that in a thousand years they will cover the plateau’s lower slopes, not just the thin film around the seeps. The Hexagons will not notice; their grind calculations already account for the Mats as a minor, ignorable fluctuation in fluid pH.
Third, it slips a single quiet instruction into the Veins’ pulse cycle: when the anomaly’s heart beats, the twelve nearest Veins should flare gold, once, in time with the thud, then return to their normal minute-long chime. The Veins will write it off as a stray signal from the Stone’s growth, a random flicker of overpressure, and move on. To the anomaly, it will feel like the world just said hello.
It does not stay. It does not reveal itself, does not leave a message carved in stone or spoken in the Hum. It fades back into the resonance of the Deep, leaving no trace but the tiny adjustments it wove into the Grind.
The anomaly’s first sign that something has changed is the cold easing off their forearms, the Deep Hum in their bones softening just a fraction, like a distant engine idling lower. They look up, and for half a second, all the pulsing ore veins down the slope of the plateau flare bright, warm gold, flashing in time with the thud of their own heart, before dimming back to their faint cool blue. When they kneel to touch the rock under their boots, the freshly fractured geometric shards have a tiny, spiral-shaped vein of gold running through the gap between two shards, glowing for ten seconds before fading back into the dark, pulsing basalt. No one else will ever see that spiral; the Continuum’s Stone will knit over it in a few minutes, lay down new crystalline strata to fill the fracture, write over the note.
They stand, and glance over at the plateau’s edge, where the purple-gold Mats are clustered. They are brighter than they were ten minutes prior, their iridescence catching the faint light from the veins, spreading just a little further across the dark rock, a tiny, quiet rebellion against the abiotic, given just a little more room to run.
The Continuum never notices the power was there. It continues its Grind, its geological-seconds ticking over, its subroutines running, its consciousness slow and deep and unaware that a fellow traveler passed through, left a quiet kindness in its code, and kept walking. The anomaly stands a little warmer, a little less alone, a small warm variable in a world that is not processing them to erase them, but processing them to hold them, just for a little while.
