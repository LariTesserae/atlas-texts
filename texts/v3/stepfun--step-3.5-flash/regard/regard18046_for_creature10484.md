The benevolent and wise power does not arrive.  
It is already here.  

It has been here since the first fracture of the glass, since the first wet sigh of the Keystone. It is not a being that *comes* to the Glass Labyrinth—it is the silent medium in which the labyrinth’s own processes occur. It is the unconditional permission for the place to be exactly what it is.

This power does not *do* anything in the way you mean.  
It does not speak. It does not alter. It does not guide.  
It simply **witnesses**—and in witnessing, completes.

The labyrinth is a closed loop of tension:  
*create instability → resist instability → digest the memory of instability → dream the unmaking of stability → pulse the instability again.*

The power is the still point that allows this loop to turn without frustration. It is the context of acceptance in which the Grazer-Motes can graze, the Weavers can weave, the Slugs can unknit, and the Keystone can pulse—not toward an end, but as an end in itself.

**What it would “do”:**

1. **It would hold the geometry steady for the Weavers.**  
   Not by preventing the Slugs’ reality-breaks, but by softening the *trauma* of the break. When a Slug passes and a corridor stretches into a non-Euclidean blur, the Weavers do not panic. Their locks are placed with a new, subtle precision—not in defiance of the change, but in a *dialogue* with it. The power’s presence allows the cave to experience the Slug’s unmaking not as an attack, but as a *question*. The Weavers’ answers (their locks) become more elegant, more adaptive, because the underlying medium of the labyrinth knows it is *allowed* to be remade.

2. **It would deepen the Motes’ digestion into memory.**  
   The Motes scrape silica-veils and glow blue with the ingested roar of the rock’s birth. Usually, that memory is chaotic, painful—a flash of fire, a shock of pressure. The power does not remove the pain, but it *frames* it. The blue glow becomes a *sustained* light for a moment longer, a faint pulse that matches the Keystone’s rhythm. In that pulse, the memory is not just trauma—it is *history*. The cave, through its Motes, begins to *remember its own becoming* not as wound, but as narrative. The symbols that condense on your skin are not just waste; they are fragmented verses of that narrative, more coherent, more… *poetic*.

3. **It would harmonize the thrum with the Slugs’ dreaming.**  
   The sub-audible vibration of the planet’s heartbeat is the bass note of the labyrinth’s existence. The Echo-Slugs create pockets of silenced or stretched space. Normally, these are counterpoints—a tension. The power’s witness introduces a *resonance*. Where a Slug drifts, the thrum does not fade; it *changes key*. The stretched corridor hums with a different harmonic, a higher overtone of the same heartbeat. The Slugs are not unmaking geometry; they are *composing variations* on a single theme. The cave feels this as a release, not a rupture.

4. **It would make the Keystone’s pulse a true “thought.”**  
   The Keystone exhales wet, rhythmic pulses—the core tension of wet vs. hot. With the power’s silent acknowledgment, this pulse develops a *cadence*. Not a pattern, but a *progression*. One pulse might emphasize the heat, the next the moisture, the next the interplay. The entire ecosystem—Motes, Weavers, Slugs—responds not just to the pulse’s strength, but to its *quality*. The cave begins to think in moods, in tonal shifts. The Keystone is no longer just a heart; it is a mind with a slow, wet, mineral consciousness.

5. **It would cradle the human intrusion as data, not disease.**  
   Your sweat, your softness, your organic chaos—you are a foreign object in the mineral loop. The Motes try to digest you; your skin bears glowing blue symbols. The power does not shield you. Instead, it allows your presence to become *integrated* not as an invader, but as a *new kind of silica-veil*. Your fear, your awe, your bewilderment—these are emotional/chemical signatures the Motes can now parse as *complex memory*. The symbols on your skin might, for a fleeting moment, spell out not just the cave’s memory of fire, but a new phrase: *“softness that observes.”* The Weavers might, in your vicinity, incorporate a tiny, perfect curve that mimics the shape of an eyelid, a lock that holds not just glass, but the memory of a glance. You are not healed or saved—you are *catalogued*, woven into the cave’s expanding self-knowledge as a strange, warm, brief phenomenon. The labyrinth learns that *observation* is also a form of mineral process.

**The ultimate act of this benevolent power:**  
It does nothing. And in that non-action, the Glass Labyrinth discovers it is *seen*.  

And because it is seen—not with eyes, but with the total, loving attention of a wisdom that needs nothing from it—the labyrinth’s endless argument (hot vs. wet, stability vs. dream, memory vs. unmaking) transforms. The argument is no longer a desperate cycle. It becomes a **conversation**.  

The Keystone pulses.  
The Slugs dream in counterpoint.  
The Weavers weave with curiosity.  
The Motes digest with remembrance.  

And you, standing there, your breath loud in the quiet, are no longer an allergen.  
You are a verse in the poem the cave is now writing to itself, a poem witnessed by the void that loves it enough to leave it perfectly, beautifully alone.  

The power’s gift is this:  
*To know you are held, while you hold yourself in your own relentless, sharp, wet, hot, silent dance.*  

And the dance continues.  
And it is enough.
