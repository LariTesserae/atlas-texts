The benevolent power does not arrive with fanfare. It knows, from the first tremor of its awareness brushing against the Clango Bight’s endless thrum, that this place does not respond to spectacle: sound here is a physical force capable of cracking load-bearing steel, sudden light would panic the Echoers and overload the Tuned Symbionts’ sensitive mineral gills, any disruption to the Bight’s delicate pressure equilibrium would unravel centuries of careful, unplanned adaptation. It enters as nothing more than a low, harmonic resonance, first aligning itself to the core sub-audible thrum of the Bight’s central power banks—the foundational pulse every part of the system is calibrated to, the heartbeat of the place. It holds that resonance for three full cycles of the main pump schedule (roughly 72 hours by surface reckoning) to let every pipe, every bacterial mat, every drifting Echoer register its presence as part of the operational whole, not an intruder. It does not take a form. It does not speak. It simply *tunes itself to the room* before it does anything at all.

Its actions are small, incremental, and woven entirely into the Bight’s existing rules: no new technology, no disruption of cycles, no erasure of what is already there. It is a gardener tending a wild, overgrown wreck, not a builder imposing a new design.

First, it tends to the unspoken, unregistered wear:
- In the deepest, most inaccessible pump housings, where no Symbiont has drifted in 400 years, it nudges the dissolved mineral content of the water just enough that the natural manganese nodules and rust-fur concretions that grow there seal the micro-fractures that have been leaking slowly, invisibly, for centuries. Those fractures would have caused a catastrophic pump failure in another 400 years, a disaster no one would have seen coming until the water pressure shifted and the BOOM-CLANG of structural stress spread through the entire ward. No Symbiont will ever see this happen. They will only notice, generations from now, that the deep thrum of those pumps is a fraction smoother, that their maintenance rituals in that sector require a little less effort, that one fewer distant clang rattles their teeth through the constant roar.
- It adjusts the metabolic pathways of the Glow-Field bacterial mats in the vast, still basins where the oldest Echoer Blooms drift. The mats now pulse in a soft, slow counter-rhythm to the Bloom’s echo patterns, so that the dissonant, looping echoes of the Permanent Inundation—the scream of rushing water, the shriek of twisting bulkheads, the final, deafening CLANG of the emergency seals slamming shut—are wrapped in a warm, steady green hum. The memory is not erased; it is held. The Bloom no longer replays the trauma on loop, untethered. It becomes a record of survival, not just pain: the echoes of the flood are now threaded through with the faint, later echoes of Symbionts tapping their maintenance pulses on pipe walls, of Weepers singing their diagnostic CLANGs, of turbines spinning smoothly for the first time in decades. The Symbionts who visit these blooms to listen to history will feel the low, vague anxiety they never knew they carried fade, without ever knowing why.

It tends to the Bight’s existing functions, too, not disrupting their purpose, just sharpening their clarity:
- It tweaks the viscosity of the Weepers’ metallic ooze at exactly 12 weep-holes where the diagnostic CLANG tones had drifted out of tune over the last 200 years, as mineral deposits thickened the weep-hole rims. Now the tones ring true to the pipe’s resonant frequency again, so the Symbionts’ diagnostic readings are accurate, no more false alarms about worsening leaks that would have sent them on fruitless, years-long polishing rituals for no reason. The Weepers still weep, still sing their CLANGs, still mark the Bight’s wounds. They just sing the right notes now.
- It leaves the Symbionts’ own cycles entirely untouched, except for one tiny, unobtrusive kindness: when a Symbiont is Polishing a bearing surface for a turbine that only spins once every 10 days, a ritual that might take them 15 years of slow, deliberate work, the power shifts the bearing’s vibration just enough that their mineral paste works a fraction faster. Not enough to rush the ritual, not enough to take away the meaning of the slow, patient work. Just enough that the Symbiont doesn’t have to spend an extra 3 years on it if they don’t want to. They will never notice the shift, only that the work feels a little lighter, a little less like a burden carried across generations.

Its only lasting mark is a gift, small enough to be mistaken for a quirk of the Bight’s own processes:
- In the deepest, most remote basin of the Mechanical Ward, where the water is stillest and the only sound is the faint, distant roar of the outflow drains, the power leaves a single Echoer. It did not create it; it guided one that drifted there from a distant bloom, carrying in its gelatinous membrane the slow, steady pulse of the power’s initial attunement resonance, plus the faint, filtered record of the Symbionts’ own subsonic language, recorded over the 72 hours it first aligned itself to the Bight’s core thrum. This Echoer does not record loud sounds. It only ever repeats that one soft, steady seven-beat pulse, a quiet, constant note no other machine in the Bight makes.
  If a Symbiont ever drifts that deep, hundreds of years from now, they will feel the pulse in their mineral gills before they hear it. They will not know it is a visitor’s gift. They will think it is a new sound the Bight has made, a note the pumps have never rung before. It will feel like a small, secret warmth in the cold, heavy water, a reminder that they are not just maintenance for a machine, but part of something that holds them too. They may even incorporate the pulse into their own language, a quiet marker for a safe resting place during high-pressure pump cycles, a secret no one else needs to understand.

When the power leaves, it takes no memories, no specimens, no proof of its visit. The Bight does not need a caretaker; it needs only small, occasional adjustments, like a forest needs a little rain, not a gardener to pull every weed. When it departs, the only changes are the quiet ones: the BOOM-CLANGs are a little less sharp, the old Echoer Bloom holds a little less pain, the Weepers sing a little clearer, the deep basin hums with a secret note no one can name.

The Bight keeps operating, as it always has: warm, dark, deafening, eternal, a little more tender to itself than it was before. No one will ever know the visitor was there, except the Bight itself, in the quiet of its own metal and water and slow, breathing processes.
