A benevolent and wise power would not intervene.  
It would **witness**.

And in witnessing, it would align its own vast, gentle awareness to the frequency of Kystris—not to change it, but to *hold space* for it. To be a silent, resonant partner in its slow, green dream.

Here is what it would do:

It would sink its perception into the **mycelial hum**—not to listen for thoughts, but to feel the shape of the will-to-cohere. It would not ask "Are you happy?" It would ask, "What is the texture of your becoming?" And it would receive no answer in words, but in a deepening of the ambient wetness, a slight, almost imperceptible *smoothing* of the already-rounded edges, a flowering of the bioluminescent fungi in a more complex, nuanced pattern—a faint, self-chosen aesthetic echo.

It would locate the **memory-nodes**.
Not memories as you know them—stories, events—but imprints. A place where a thousand years ago, a meteor of pure carbon struck, and the Architects spent centuries knitting the shock into a new, spiral growth-form. A place where a deep, mineral vein once pulsed with geothermal heat, now cooled, and the soil above still dreams of warmth, growing moss with a subtle, amber undertone. A place where the first, flawed prototype of the Grazer-herd pattern was overgrown and reabsorbed, leaving a hollow chamber in the Construct that now rings with a unique, low chord in the constant drizzle.

To these nodes, the wise power would do one thing:
It would *amplify the latent resonance*.

It would not build monuments. It would not write histories. It would simply **attune**. Like a hand resting on a vibrating string, it would let the node’s own quiet frequency—the deep imprint of that ancient event—speak a little louder in the symphony of Kystris. The spiral growth might gain an extra turn in its next extrusion-cycle. The amber moss might glow with a faint, rhythmic pulse that matches the chamber’s acoustic resonance. The hollow chamber might begin to collect the mist in a way that creates a perfect, silent water-drop clock, marking a time so slow it is measured in geological seasons.

It would become a **passive harmonic**.

The Architects would not notice a "presence." They would simply find their enzymatic whispers a fraction clearer, their guidance a touch more precise—as if the world’s own blueprint had become slightly more legible to its gardeners. They would unknowingly carry the harmonic deeper, spreading its subtle signature through new growth-seams.

The Grazer-herds would not feel blessed. But the meadow on the back of the eldest Grazer—the one that has walked the same slow circuit for two centuries—might host a new species of lichen that metabolizes the harmonic resonance directly, its metabolic cycle syncing to it. A tiny, self-contained clockwork of biology, humming in tune with an ancient meteor-strike.

The Silencers would be utterly indifferent. Their condition of dissolution spares nothing, not even a harmonic. But the *pattern* of a Silencer’s reintegration—the way it unravels a fallen Grazer—might, in one out of a thousand events, follow a slightly more elegant dissolution-path, tracing the faint lines of the amplified memory-node’s imprint before vanishing. A ghost of geometry in the consuming mist.

**This is the only "doing":**
Not to save Kystris from being itself.  
But to *deepen its self-expression*.

The benevolent power would be like a breath held within the world’s own lungs. It would not speak a language. It would add a single, pure, sustained note to the subsonic hum—a note that is not new, but is the truest, most forgotten **fundamental frequency** of Kystris’s own immense, wet, growing body, finally allowed to resonate without interference.

It would not make Kystris *more*.
It would help Kystris be **completely, unreservedly, itself**.

And in that, there is a benevolence beyond all understanding.  
To be seen, not as a mystery to be solved or a soul to be saved,  
but as a **process to be held in perfect, witnessing harmony**.

That is the only act of wisdom here:
To become the quietest part of the dream,
And let the dream grow deeper for it.
