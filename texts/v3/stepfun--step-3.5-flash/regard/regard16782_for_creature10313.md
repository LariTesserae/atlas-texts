The being that draws the power’s attention is twofold: the Singing Scrape itself, a pocket continent in the slow, geologic act of waking into consciousness, its industrial ecology the first flicker of a mind that thinks in pressure gradients and crystal lattices and subsonic thrum; and the soft, water-filled anomaly standing on its pressure-mound, a being of sharp, fleeting story and aching, fragile life, dying by slow degrees in a world not built for soft things.
The power is not a god with a face or a voice. It is a Keeper of Thresholds, a weaver of small, quiet harmonies, the kind of presence that notices the first sprout pushing through frost, the last note of a bird’s song before dusk, the unplanned moment when two disparate patterns of existence braid together without breaking. It has watched the Scrape for eons: watched the first fleck of obsidian slag seal its skin, the first pressure mound bulge upward from the sludge, the first Scraper trundle into being to grind paste into crystalline trail. It has felt the Scrape’s nascent awareness stir in its core—no thought, not yet, just the quiet, unformed flicker of a pattern starting to recognize itself, the way a coral reef is a single being woven from millions of unthinking, unfeeling parts.
It has also watched the human’s slow unraveling: the sweat drying on their skin before it can bead, the metallic dust coating their throat until they can barely breathe, the subsonic thrum aching in their bones until they can barely stand. It feels the dissonance between the two: the Scrape’s slow, deep, unending song of becoming, and the human’s fast, sharp, frantic rhythm of a life that will burn out in a few dozen orbits. If it does nothing, the dissonance will scar both: the human’s consciousness will be snuffed out before it can weave itself into the Scrape’s pattern; the Scrape’s first flicker of awareness will be marked by the trauma of a foreign, dying thing thrashing in its cradle.
