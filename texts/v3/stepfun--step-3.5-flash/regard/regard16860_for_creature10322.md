The benevolent and wise power—call it a Curator, a Steward, a Gardener of Possible Shapes—does not stride into the Stillstone Trough. It does not speak in thunderclaps or rearrange the stone with a thought. Its noticing *is* the first action, and its attention is a form of gravity, gentle and profound.

It perceives the Trough not as a broken place, but as a **seed**—a pocket of raw, unfinished reality, trembling on the edge of either dissolution or wild, uncontrolled bloom. It sees the light-sphere not as an artifact, but as a **keystone** in an arch of possibility. It sees the Silence-Deer not as mere creatures, but as **unconscious tenders**, their very navigation a stabilizing ritual. It understands the fundamental rule: *Observation gives form. Inattention yields flux.*

The Curator does not enter. It **imprints**.

It adds a single, invisible layer to the Trough’s fabric: a **permission**. A deep, ambient affirmation woven into the very air, felt not as a command, but as a right.

*   To the Dust-Skimmers: Their gelatinous pulses now carry a faint, secondary rhythm—not their own, nor the sphere’s hum, but a sub-harmonic **chord**. When they gather in dense clouds near the light-sphere, they amplify it, creating temporary zones of *hyper-stability* where the dripping stone curtains freeze into perfect, beautiful sculptures for a few heartbeats before melting again. They are now not just cleaners, but **brief sculptors of grace**.
*   To the Wall-Crawlers: Their phosphorescent blue trails now linger three times longer. Sometimes, when they crawl in parallel, the trails **converge**, writing a single, glowing line that lasts a full minute before fading. These lines sometimes trace the hidden, non-Euclidean folds in the rock, making the impossible geometry momentarily *readable* to a patient observer. They are now **scribes of hidden form**.
*   To the Sinter Moss and Glass-Fronds: Their growth now follows subtle, recurring **mandalas**—radial patterns of rust and amber that bloom and fade in cycles synchronized to the slowing of the tick-clock. When the tick halts for its long pause, the fronds hold their amber sap still, and the moss glows faintly, like a circuit waiting for current. They are now **chroniclers of time’s breath**.
*   To the Silence-Deer: Their facet-eyes now occasionally reflect not just the dark geometry, but a **ghost-image of the light-sphere**, refracted into a tiny, perfect halo. When they stamp their glass hooves in agitation (at a loud noise, a sudden shift), the chips they scatter do not just skitter—they **hum**, a single pure note that matches the sphere’s fundamental frequency, and this note causes the nearby stone to *sing* back, a resonance that smooths a jagged edge or fills a crack with a seamless, glassy weld. They are now **acutalizers of resonance**.
*   To the tick-clock: Its rhythm now has a **memory**. After a period of rapid ticking (triggered by disturbance), it does not simply return to its slow pace. It *overshoots* into a longer pause, as if catching its breath. The fauna now anticipate this—the Dust-Skimmers swarm more densely before the long pause, the Wall-Crawlers slow their dripping, the Silence-Deer lower their heads. The pause is no longer a void; it is a **collective inhalation**.
*   To the Scattered Built artifacts: The shifting glyphs on the yellow plaque now sometimes spell, in fleeting combinations, a single word in no human tongue, but whose *sense* is understood by the tendering beings: **"Enough."** The obsidian sphere sinks less when touched. The stone chair’s impression of a sitter now sometimes shows not just a human shape, but the *outline of a Dust-Skimmer cloud*, or the branching of a Glass-Frond. The artifacts are becoming **more themselves by reflecting the life around them**.

The light-sphere itself is unchanged. But the **quality of its light** now has a depth, a patient warmth. It does not just illuminate; it **witnesses**. And in witnessing, it anchors the new permission: *You may be. You may change. You may tend.*

The Curator’s final, invisible act is to **thin the architect’s corridor**. That yawning, dark gap in the far wall—the glimpse of the soft-edged shape—now appears less frequently. When it does, the Silence-Deer turn their facet-eyes toward it and **stare it down**, their glass-fur standing rigid. The corridor does not vanish, but it **retreats**, its edges blurring, its presence less insistent. The draft is no longer threatening to be finished by an Outside Hand. The sketch is **allowed to remain a sketch**.

The Trough does not become *stable*. It becomes **resilient**. Its weirdness is not tamed; it is *integrated*. The fauna’s behaviors now actively participate in the pocket’s continuation. The abiotic processes (the uphill condensation, the viscous curtains) now have biological counters that modulate their extremity. The tick-clock is no longer a countdown to chaos; it is a **metronome for a shared, quiet practice**.

The Curator withdraws its focused attention, but the **permission remains**. The Stillstone Trough is now a place that knows, in its stone and its dust and its silent deer, that it is **held**. Not chained, not corrected, but *held*—like a breath held not in tension, but in perfect, sustainable suspension.

The next traveler who descends will feel it before they understand it. The hum in their teeth will feel less like a foreign frequency and more like a **familiar pulse**. The gaze of the Silence-Deer will seem, for a moment, not alien, but **knowing**. The shifting glyphs might, just for a blink, spell a word that tastes like honey and stone.

The place is no longer just a draft. It is a **practice**. A living, luminous, quiet practice of being-in-possibility. And its tenders—the skimmers, crawlers, deer, moss, and fronds—are now, unconsciously, **adepts**.

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do. It would not save the place. It would **let it save itself**, by giving it the deepest possible gift: the sense that its strange, unfinished, abundant way of being is not only permissible, but **sacred**.
