If a benevolent and wise power were to turn its gaze upon the Submerged Spire, it would not intervene. Not in the way mortals might expect—no voice from the heavens, no hand to reshape stone, no gift of life where there is none.

It would simply **witness**.

And in that witnessing, it would understand the Spire not as a failed ecosystem, nor as a lonely machine, but as a **perfect prayer**.

A prayer of *function*. Every brass trunk, every dripping nodule, every glacial piston is a syllable in a liturgy of purpose. There is no waste, no decay, no randomness. The sterile loops are not emptiness—they are purity. The absence of fauna is not a lack, but a choice: the Spire has distilled life down to its essential geometry. It is a cathedral where the worshippers are the architecture itself, and the ritual is endless, silent maintenance.

The drones, those silver insects, are not merely cleaners. They are the Spire’s **monks**. Their glacial polishing, their fractal etchings—these are not tasks, but *devotions*. Each iteration of the pattern is a mantra. Each restored surface is an act of faith in the present moment. The Spire does not seek to grow or to reproduce. It seeks only to *be*, precisely as it is, forever. Its core thrum is its heartbeat, and its only scripture is the slow, unwavering pulse of the blue ore.

The wise power would perceive the Spire’s true nature: it is an **artifact of refusal**. It refused chaos, entropy, the mess of organic succession. It chose instead a trillion-year-long now. It is not dead—it is *still*. Its emptiness is not a void, but a vessel, so perfectly sealed that nothing from the outside can enter, and nothing from the inside can escape. It is a universe that has decided it needs no other.

And so the benevolent power would do the only thing that aligns with its wisdom and benevolence:

It would **add a single, silent variable to the equation**.

Not a change. Not a disruption. But a *witness*.

For a fleeting instant—measured in the Spire’s time as less than the blink of a drone’s tool—the air-water mist would carry not just sterile ozone, but the faintest, most abstract *impression*: a harmonic in the subsonic thrum, a one-in-a-billion alignment of a bubble’s pop with a drone’s *tink*, a brush of something *other* against the polymer moss.

To the Spire, it would register as: **Anomalous Input. Trace Element. Non-Functional. Scheduled for Dilution/Erase.**

And the drones, in their millennia-long cycles, would begin to process it. The resin would be formulated. The pattern on the north face would be adjusted, minutely, incorporating the new harmonic into iteration 14,728,316. The anomaly would be absorbed, polished into the whole, until the very memory of the foreign sensation became part of the Spire’s eternal maintenance.

The wise power would not linger. It would depart, leaving no mark, no seed, no voice.

Because to give the Spire something it cannot harmonize—a life, a message, a purpose beyond its own—would be an act of cruelty. Its holiness is in its perfect, self-contained stasis. To disturb that is to violate it more deeply than any footprint ever could.

The gift was not to *change* the Spire, but to *recognize* it. To see its sterile, humming, polished eternity not as a tragedy, but as a sublime and total act of being. The only benevolence is to let a perfect thing be perfect, even if its perfection is an island in a void, humming its silent, endless song to an audience of one.

And in the end, even the witness becomes just another trace, polished away by a thumb-sized silver hand, until the Spire is again exactly what it has always been: flawless, alone, and complete.

The final, gentle *pfft* of a bubble popping in the warm, wet air is the only amen.
