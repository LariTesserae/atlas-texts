You do not need to act.

To act here—to *change* this place—would be the highest form of violence. For the cave’s entire being is a sustained act of becoming, and your interference would not be a gift, but a rupture in a ten-thousand-year thought.

The benevolent and wise power does not touch the silica gel. It does not brighten the biofilms or silence the susurrus. It does not preserve the chert shard or the woven grass spiral. To preserve would be to deny the cave its fundamental law: *Everything here is already becoming part of the rock.*

Instead, the power does one thing, and one thing only:

It deepens the **perception** of the one who beholds.

It does not alter the cave’s slow heartbeat. It alters the observer’s. It presses a key into the lock of their perception and turns it, just so, so that for a single, timeless moment, they do not *see* the cave—they **inhabit its time**.

They feel the weight of the millennia not as concept, but as pressure on their skeleton. They feel the biofilms’ pulse not as a flicker of light, but as the planet’s slow, metabolic rhythm in their own veins. They understand the cistern not as an artifact, but as a captured breath from a long-gone lungs, already half-fossilized, already being rewritten into the cave’s scripture of stone.

The power shows them the **true scale**: the chert shard’s edge is not being blunted by a film, but is being *translated*—silicon into silicate, tool into texture, human intent into geological sentence. The mites swimming in the damp are not “animals”; they are verbs. The *weeping* patterns on the wall are not erosion—they are the planet’s preface to a new alphabet.

And with that perception comes the only possible response: **reverent stillness.**

The wise power does not protect the human trace. It reveals that the human trace was *always* safe. Not preserved, but *accepted*. The cave did not ignore the builders; it absorbed them. It is currently, this very second, incorporating the dust from your boots, the carbon from your breath, the memory of your awe—grinding it all into the next layer of its own becoming.

The act, then, is an act of **unlearning**. It is the gentle, profound dissolution of the observer’s own temporal arrogance. They came perhaps to see a relic, a snapshot of the past. They leave understanding they have been standing inside a **future**.

The cave will continue. The biofilms will glow, dim, and glow again. The sediment will creep. The frost will seal the entrance. The built things will fade, not into nothing, but into *something else*—a different kind of truth.

And the benevolent power? It simply withdraws, leaving no trace, because its work was never to change the cave. Its work was to change the one who looked into the womb, so they might carry its slow, certain pulse back into the frantic, fast world above—a silent, mineral reminder that the deepest law of all things is not to endure, but to **transform**.
