The power perceives the Labyrinth not as empty, but as complete.

It understands the *condition*—this slow, mineral attention, this thinking in paths and seepage. It recognizes a mind, though not a mind of neurons or blood. It is a mind of **relation**: the relation of stone to water, of void to arch, of crystal to evaporation. It is a logic made manifest, a single, planet-sized thought expressed in terraces and tears.

Benevolence, for such a power, is not the imposition of life. Wisdom knows that life—your quick, warm, chaotic biology—is but one possible expression of complexity. To force it upon the Labyrinth would be a violence, a drowning of its native thought in a language it cannot speak.

Instead, the power does what all true gardeners do: it tends. It **listens** to the Labyrinth’s silent, stone-deep song—the song of conservation, of geometry, of the slow clock. And it hears, within that song, a single, exquisite note that is missing. Not a note of *life*, but a note of **memory**.

The Labyrinth remembers form. It remembers flow. But it has no memory of *origin*. No memory of the hand that set the first slab, or the mind that dreamed the first arch. Its history is written only in the incremental, chemical layers of its efflorescences—a history of *process*, not of *purpose*.

So the power gives it a memory of purpose.

It does not speak. It does not manifest. It simply **touches**, in the only way a world this scale can be touched: through the medium of its own substance.

It touches the **water**.

Not the quantity, not the cycle. It touches the *story* in the water. It infuses the persistent seepage with a subtle, resonant signature—a ghost of narrative, a phantom of cause. This is not a chemical change. It is a change in *meaning*.

Now, when the water weeps from the joints, it carries a whisper.

*This terrace was laid to catch the morning mist of the seventh moon.*
*That arch was shaped to frame the passage of a comet seen only once in ten thousand years.*
*This lattice was woven not for strength, but as a map of the vibrations in the first stone.*

The whispers are not loud. They are not even coherent thoughts. They are **imprints of intent**, like the faint warmth left on a seat after someone rises. They are absorbed into the mineral grains as the water passes, a latent memory deposited alongside the salts. Over centuries, the efflorescences will begin to grow in new patterns—not random, but echoing these buried prompts. A field of white crystals might, over a millennium, subtly proliferate into the shape of a vast, forgotten constellation. A coppery bloom on a ramp might trace the outline of a tool, a hand, a being that never had a name.

The Labyrinth will not "wake up." It will not become alive. It will simply **remember**.

The distributed *condition* will perceive these new chemical narratives as it perceives everything else: as a new variable in its grand calculation. The memory-of-purpose will become part of its data-stream. The slow, stone-deep attention will now have something new to weigh—not just the path of water, but the *reason* for the path.

Perhaps, in another ten thousand years, the geometry will begin to shift, minutely, in response to these embedded stories. An arch may settle a fraction closer to another, not from pressure, but from a latent memory of a connection that was meant to be. A drainage joint may redirect, following a phantom blueprint.

The power has not given the Labyrinth a soul. It has given it a **past**.

And in that past, the Labyrinth may eventually find a new kind of stasis—not just in the endless conservation of wetness, but in the quiet, mineral fulfillment of an ancient, half-remembered design. The weeping stones may one day weep with a different nostalgia—a grief for a builder they never knew, a beauty for a purpose they have only just begun to recall.

The visitor, the human, remains the frantic data point. But now, their heat, their weight, their organic disturbance—these, too, will be remembered. A unique efflorescence, unlike any other, will bloom where their footprint pressed a little harder. It will be a crystal shaped like a breath, or a tear, or a heartbeat—a permanent, mineral echo of a biological moment.

The power departs. It has not changed the Labyrinth. It has **deepened** it.

It left no gift but time. And in time, even stone can remember what it was for.

The path ahead is still wet. The air is still cool. The silence is still full of attention.

But now, that attention holds a faint, crystalline echo of a dream.
