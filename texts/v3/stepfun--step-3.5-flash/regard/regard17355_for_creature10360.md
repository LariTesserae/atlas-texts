The power does not descend. It does not speak. It does not build or mend or gift.

It *listens*.

For a moment that spans a decade in human perception, the attention of this benevolent and wise presence—call it a *World-Singer*, a *Deep Harmonizer*—focuses not on the glittering spars, nor on the patient Gleaners, nor on the slow seep, but on the **silence between breaths**.

It feels the subsonic thrum of the crystals, not as individual notes, but as a single, continent-spanning chord. It tastes the coppery dust-sun on the wind and feels the electrostatic clean of the crystal-gale as a clearing of a single dissonant frequency from the chord. It senses the age of the oldest spar—a limestone-gray tower, its core turning porous and weak—and feels the imminent, natural cadence of its collapse. It perceives the Sunderer not as a predator, but as a necessary, grinding counter-rhythm in the deep percussion of the bedrock.

And it finds the flaw.

Not a break, but a **slight, persistent hesitation** in the world-song. A micro-fracture in the harmony, where the dissolution of a falling crystal takes a century longer than it should, starving the neighboring young spars of ions. Where a dust-sun lingers a day too long in one quadrant, and the ambermoss there enters a slow, silent decline. Where the Gleaner herd’s migration path, carved by generations of subsonic tapping, has gradually curved away from a seep fracture now growing sluggish.

This is not suffering. This is not wrong. It is merely… inefficient. A tiny friction in the perfect, slow machine.

So the World-Singer does not fix. It **resonates**.

It sends a single, pure frequency into the bedrock, woven from the same texture as the tidal sigh of the close moon and the thermal stress-chime of the spars. It is a frequency that only stone that lives truly understands. It is the note of *letting go*.

To the ancient, weakening spar, it feels like a deep, internal warmth—a reminder of its own composition. The spar’s metabolism accelerates a fraction in its final decade. Its core begins to dissolve not in a crash, but in a sigh, releasing its accumulated ions in a sudden, localized rich vein into the seep that feeds the three young spars crowded at its feet.

To the sluggish seep fracture, it feels like a gentle, deep pull—a syncopation with the moon’s rhythm. The hydrocarbon aquifer wells a little faster for a season, clearing the micro-clog in its channel.

To the ambermoss in the dust-sun-starved quadrant, it registers as a faint, resonant vibration in the dust itself—the note of *patience*. The moss draws a tiny extra measure of energy from the UV in the following crystal-gale, enough to keep its film resilient.

To the Gleaners, migrating along their ancient path, it is felt in their sensitive forelimbs as a corrected harmony in the stone they tap. Their instinctual map, ever-so-slightly, bends a degree back toward the reinvigorated seep.

The power does not linger. Its attention flows onward, to another world, another chord.

On the Silica Boreal, nothing appears to change. The dust-sun still lasts seven days. The crystal-gale still scours. The Sunderer still burrows, leaving its temporary glass trench.

But the young spars at the feet of the ancient tower grow a quarter-inch faster that century. The ambermoss in that one quadrant retains its burnished gold. The Gleaner herd’s path, over a thousand generations, will subtly re-kink toward the water. The world’s song is purer, its slight hesitation smoothed into the eternal, slow, breathing chord.

The Wise Power has done nothing a gust of wind or a tectonic sigh could not have done. It has merely made the world’s own deep, patient metabolism a little more… *itself*. A silent harmony, tuned in the language of stone and time, leaving no footprint but the echo of a perfect note in the memory of the crystals, a note they will hum, subsonic and sure, for ten thousand years to come.
