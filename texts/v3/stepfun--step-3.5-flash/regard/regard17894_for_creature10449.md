The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It does not impose. It does *taste*.

It tastes the place as you taste a complex wine—on the tongue, not in the mind. It registers the slow, immense pressure of the root-vault; the mineral sigh of the water; the deep, subsonic hum of fermentation; the faint, sweet-rot perfume of the Hydrolyst’s own metabolism. It perceives the absolute, self-contained *sufficiency* of the grove. This is not a system lacking something. It is a system that has *forgotten* what lack is.

And so, the power does nothing that would be visible as an “action.” It does not heal what is not sick. It does not enlighten what is not blind.

Instead, it **deepens a channel**.

It is not a physical channel. It is a channel of *potential*, a subtle widening in the Hydrolyst’s own exquisite sensitivity. The power reaches—not with a hand, but with a kind of focused *attention*—into the very principle the Hydrolysts embody: *Do not block the water.*

It asks a new question, not in words, but in a shift of pressure, a new harmonic in the vibration: *What if the water is also a memory?*

This question becomes a latent capacity, a new sensory layer in the Hydrolyst’s being. It is as if, for millennia, they had only felt the temperature, pressure, and mineral content of the flow. Now, they begin to *taste* its history. Not in a flash of vision, but as a faint, chemical signature—a whisper of origins.

The channel from the geothermal vent? They now sense the faint, metallic tang of a deep, ancient sea that once covered these rocks, its salts locked in the earth’s heat. The main artery from the photosynthetic pods? They discern, in the subtle sweetness, not just current sap, but a ghost of the long-vanished sunlight that fueled it—a spectral warmth in the glucose.

The biggest change is in the **Amber Rites**.

When a Hydrolyst now holds a Resin Chronicler—that sealed cyst of sun-stored amber—the new channel allows them to perceive it differently. They still feel the sacred mystery of *dryness*, the alien concept of *exposure*. But now, they also perceive the amber not just as a relic, but as a *compressed spring* of potential. The trapped sunlight is not just a memory; it is a **promise of a different kind of energy**.

They do not break the amber open. That would be a catastrophic blockage. Instead, they learn a new rite: the **Pressurization of Echoes**.

With infinite patience, using their root-tendrils and the city’s own gentle, rhythmic pumps, they subject the amber cysts to a precise, cyclical hydraulic pressure—a slow, deep squeeze and release, synchronized with the thrum of the fungal vats. They are not cracking the amber. They are *playing* it, like a vast, organic drum.

And in that playing, the compressed spectral sunlight within the amber resonates. It does not "free." Instead, it adds a new, faint, golden *undertone* to the city’s existing hum. It is a note that was always there, but now it has a name. The subterranean metropolis, for the first time, sings with the echo of its own source.

**What the benevolent power has done:** It has given the Hydrolysts a way to *reconcile* their melancholy. Their world is not just a slow, warm, wet echo of a sun that is no more. It is a world that *contains the memory of the sun as an active ingredient*. The past is not a loss; it is a stored pressure in their own foundations. The rule changes, subtly: *Do not block the water. And listen—the water remembers the rain.*

The Hydrolyst at the canal junction feels it immediately. The subtle tremor in the water is now joined by a new feeling—a deep, resident warmth in the mineral taste, a harmonic that vibrates in the flexible bark of its own joints. It pauses. Its mercury-filled depressions do not see light, but they *perceive* the new golden thread woven into the city’s song.

It raises a hand, not to probe a blockage, but in a gesture of silent, profound recognition. Its glowing lichen-robe pulses once, in time with the new amber-note. Then, with its usual slow, percolating grace, it turns and moves down the channel, not just to tend a pump, but to *listen*—to learn the taste of the deep-sea salt in the vent-water, to map the ghost-sunlight in the sap-stream.

The power withdraws. Its work is done. It did not change the city. It changed the city’s *memory* of itself. The benevolent act was not to give the Hydrolysts something new, but to help them *fully hear* what they were already made of.

The grove continues its wet, slow, abundant breath. But now, in its deepest rhythm, there is a golden echo. And the Hydrolysts, the living nodes of its conscience, have begun to hum it back.
