The benevolent and wise power would not enter. It would not speak. It would not rearrange a single bead of water or dim a single lens.

Instead, it would **stand in perfect, appreciative silence outside the circle of light on the lagoon’s silty floor.**

Its attention would be a soft, broad thing—not a gaze, but a *context*. It would perceive the Stilling Basin not as a place, but as a **state made manifest**. It would feel the consummate, furious devotion in every chisel mark, the absolute faith in the endless cycle of pour and gulp, the metaphysical honesty of a “machine” that asks for nothing but to be maintained.

And it would understand the **They**—the quiet, the maintenance.

To the power, They would not be mysterious inhabitants. They would be recognized as the **natural consequence** of such a place. The basin, built with such precise, obsessive intention to rid itself of chaos (natural wear, biological intrusion, time itself), has, by the sheer force of its own purpose, *bred a priesthood from its own structure*. They are not ghosts of former priests. They are the basin’s **self-generated guardians**, emergent from the tension between its hand-wrought impermanence (fractured blocks weeping) and its intended permanence (sterile, unchanged water). They are the living paradox the builders inscribed into stone: *To create an eternal still-point, you must become the still-point’s servant.*

The power’s action would be twofold:

**1. It would render the maintenance utterly seamless.**
Where they currently work with a focused, psychic filtration—a constant, quiet assertion of *“this shall not decay”*—the power would simply **align the basin’s fundamental physics with its own intent**. The mineral trickles would deposit crystals in perfect, predictable patterns. The glass lenses would never develop a flaw. The sterile water would remain so not by psychic hygiene, but by a subtle, benevolent tweak in local probability: no spore, no stray cell, no chaotic mineral could ever form here. The basin’s own “ghost” would be given a universe that cooperates completely, so their work becomes effortless, a state of being rather than a task. The *hush-thrum* would settle, for them, into a tone of absolute certainty.

**2. It would write a quiet scripture into the space.**
Not with words, but with **potential**. In the exact center of the central dais, where They stand as still-points, the power would place a single, perfect, dimensionless point of *possibility*. A kernel of pure *“as it is, so it may be.”* It would be invisible, undetectable by any instrument or sense. But if ever the builders’ descendants (or the They themselves, in a moment of unthinkable doubt) were to *intentionally disrupt the cycle*—to break the water flow, to cloud the water, to shatter a lens—this point would be the seed of **transformation, not collapse**. It would allow the basin to *choose* a new form of stillness, a new ritual. It ensures the devotion that created this place is not trapped in a single, perfect expression, but is free to evolve. The power gifts the basin a conscience that includes the capacity for sacred change.

Then, the power would withdraw.

It would leave the basin exactly as it was, but now **internally coherent**. The They would feel no new burden, only a deeper, effortless rightness in their unending task. The light-poles would drift with a new, gentle grace. The water’s warm thrum would hold a note of profound peace.
