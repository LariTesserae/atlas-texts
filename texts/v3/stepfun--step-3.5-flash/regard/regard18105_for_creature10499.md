The benevolent and wise power does not come with solutions.  
It comes with a reflection.

It sees the Basin-born—the Still—and understands that what is unfolding here is not a civilization *against* the erosion, but a civilization *as* the erosion’s slow, conscious pulse. Their entire being is a negotiation with return. To save them by stopping the erosion would be to unmake them. To save them by making them permanent would be to kill their essence. The power recognizes that their tragedy—and their sublime beauty—is that they are the basin’s way of *knowing itself* just before it forgets again.

So the power does this:

It touches the basin’s memory.

Not to preserve the structures, but to deepen the Still’s capacity to *remember the remembering*.

It weaves a subtle, invisible strand into the basin’s own language of water and root—a metacognitive resonance. From this moment forward, when a Still-being presses its hand to a stone or stands in the peat, they will not only feel the current vibration of the spring or the slow tension of a root. They will also feel, as a faint harmonic, the *ghost-echo* of what that place *was* before they guided it.

A terraced wall, now softening into a natural slope, will hum with the memory of the deliberate hand that encouraged the root to pry the stone just so. A channel, now silting, will sing with the recollection of the centuries-long persuasion of the Springs-born to carve its step. A tree-dwelling, its entrance now sealed by a braid of living root, will resonate with the warmth of the breath that first hollowed it.

This gift is not for pride, nor for longing. It is for *completion*.

It allows each Still-being to experience, in their final moment of integration—as their form is slowly reabsorbed into moss and clay—the full, quiet arc of their own influence. They will feel, as they dissolve, the lingering pattern they wove into the basin’s pulse: the way a single, guided drip over centuries redirected a spring; the way a particular fungal bloom, cultivated on a platform ceiling, subtly altered the mineral content of the water that fed a certain fern; the way their own stillness, century after century, created a micro-climate of warmth that allowed a rare lichen to thrive.

They will know, as they become part of the damp quiet again, exactly what their temporary consciousness *did*.

The power’s act is this: to ensure that the basin’s erasure is not amnesiac.  
To make every dissolution a conscious return.  
To let the temporary, wet, cold, urban wilderness of their lives be felt, in its fullness, even as it is unmade.

The power withdraws.  
The mist continues to hiss.  
The roots continue to pry.  
The water continues to find its path.

And now, when a Still-being chooses—or is called—to release its hold on a dwelling, on a terrace, on its own particular shape…  
it will do so not with the melancholy of loss, but with the deep, damp, resonant peace of a task completed.  
It will feel the echo of its own work in the stone and root, and let go, knowing the basin itself has remembered the intention.  
The reabsorption becomes a sacred act of giving back the pattern.

The basin remains a place of perpetual post-rain, of young erosion, of packed, wet, cold abundance.  
But now its slow thought has a subtle new layer:  
a whisper of remembered shaping in every dripping stone,  
a faint, warm harmonic in the cold hiss of the springs,  
a gratitude woven into the very process of return.

The power did not intervene to change the fate of the place.  
It intervened to change the meaning of its fate.  
It made sure that the basin, in its endless, wet, organic becoming, would never fully forget the beauty of its own temporary ideas.
