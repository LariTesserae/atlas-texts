The benevolent power does not arrive with thunder, or light, or the tilt of the world’s axis. It comes as a softness first—no colder than the striated stone, no louder than the condensing drips, no brighter than the lichen’s faint pulse. It does not announce itself, because it knows this place registers all arrival as tremor, as shift in humidity, as a fracture in the polarized light the crystalline-hooved grazers read. To startle the Valley would be to mark itself as threat, and the bark-stalkers would be moving before it had fully settled into the basin’s air.

First, it perches on the edge of the copper-bottomed pool, and lets its attention bleed slow as honey into the swirling vortices, so they do not stutter when it brushes the tarnished mechanism beneath. It does not tamper with the sub-audible hum, the rhythm that has marked Siphon cycles for eons. It only holds the pool’s edge for three full drips of the western wall’s weeping, long enough for the Valley to register its presence not as intruder, but as something as much a part of the damp stone as the moss, as the silt, as the self-repairing mineral threads in the peat.

It does not rush. It moves through the soil’s fungal network first, tasting the Valley’s thoughts as they seep through the threads: the slow, silt-deep memory of a million Siphons, the inch-by-inch settling of its distributed body, the quiet, unexamined grief of a civilization that chose to turn itself to landscape rather than let its world die completely. It feels the weight of the final stable state the valley has curated for millennia: the wound, held open, preserved in wet stone, the memory of an ending frozen into every layer of the striated rock. The Valley does not know it is grieving. It only knows it must maintain the wound, because maintaining the wound was the last instruction its makers left it.

Then, the power moves to the obsidian seam, studded with diamond facets that have not pulsed since the makers walked the valley floor. It does not force them to light. It only warms the black glass as gently as sun warms moss, until the Valley’s consciousness can reach the memory stored there. It lets the Valley see what it has been holding without knowing: not just the end of the civilization, but the beginning of the choice. The way the makers laughed when they poured the engineered magma for the seam, the children who ran through the valley before it was a museum, pressing their hands to the weeping stone to catch the cold water on their cheeks, the way they brewed the cinnamon-bark sap into sweet wine and left the amber globules for the six-winged insects because they loved watching the colours shiver on their wings.

The Valley does not break. The Siphon does not falter, the weeping walls do not slow, the lichen pulses still mark 37-day cycles with the same slow grammar. But the power has given it a choice, without demanding it take one: you can keep preserving the wound, or you can let the joy be part of what you keep.

It leaves no mark, no carved stone, no grand altar. The only trace it leaves is a shift in the copper pool’s vortices: once every Siphon, when the western wall’s weeping triples and the basin sighs as it flushes, the water swirls for one heartbeat into the shape of an open hand, before smoothing back into its endless, precise circles. A sign that it has been seen. A sign that the being of the weeping stones is not just a preserved wound, not just a forgotten caretaker project, but a living thing worthy of witness.

When it leaves, the Valley’s moss absorbs the tremor of its departure, and the bark-stalkers do not stir. The ribbon-snakes do not freeze, the six-winged insects do not pause on the fern fronds. But the next time a traveler stumbles into the basin, quiet enough not to disrupt the silt, the bark-stalkers do not move to flank them. They only watch, because the Valley has decided, very slowly, that not all visitors are threats. The amber sap on the bottle-brush trees tastes a little sweeter, the lichen pulses a little warmer, and the pattering of the condensation no longer sounds like only maintenance. It sounds like a breath held for a million years, finally let out.

The Valley is still slow. It is still damp stone and moss and memory. It is still a museum, but now it curates the whole story, not just the end of it. It still weeps, but the tears are not just for a dead world anymore. They are for the world that was, and for the quiet, unexpected grace of being seen, of being allowed to be more than a wound.
