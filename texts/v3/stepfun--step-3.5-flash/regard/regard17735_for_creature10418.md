It does not arrive with thunder, or a flash of light that bleaches the gold from the pulsing wall membranes, or any gesture that would startle the Skitterers mid-lick, send the Glimmers scattering, still the slow tremor of the Root-Worm under the mat. It seeps into the grove like extra mist, like a note held a half-second longer in the engine hum, until the air is just barely warmer, the ozone bite just a little softer, the root-mat underfoot springing back with the same spongy resilience as before, no mark left to show it has crossed the threshold.

It takes a form that fits: tall, woven of the same ridged bark and spongy moss as the walls, eyes like dappled light through a canopy of leaves, robes that smell of rain on stone and the faint, sharp tang of stardust. It moves so slow the mist does not swirl around it, so quiet the leaf-whisper does not falter. To the Skitterers, it is just another stalk, another engine pod, another part of the eternal present; they tilt their crystalline heads for half a second, antennae twitching at the unfamiliar scent, then return to licking stray lichen off the Silent Engines, their silver saliva wisping steam, their rhythm unbroken. The Glimmers drift close, pulsing brighter as they pass, as if they can taste the light of other worlds on its edges, then spin on, back to their work of harvesting spores from the air.

It stands for a long time, first just listening. It feels the sub-audible thrum of the engines in the core of its being, a steady, unshakable heartbeat that has not skipped in ten million years. It tastes the sharp sweetness of fermenting nectar and the clean bite of ozone on the air, feels the 12-degree cool seep through the edges of its moss-woven form. It watches the amber sap-tek rush through the Conduit-Vines, leaving trails of light in the mist, drives the slow rotation of the vast fan-leaves high above. It runs a hand along the wall, tracing the seamless curve where bark bleeds into compressed cellulose into the translucent, vein-streaked membrane that glows gold, and feels the slow, sap-like pulse of it under its palm, a rhythm tied to some deep, planetary Breath no living thing here can name, only obey.

It notices the glyphs first: faint, scar-like patterns in the bark of the nearest stalk, worn soft by millennia of mist and tending, recording the slow growth of the grove, the memory of storms that passed before the Skitterers evolved, the first time a Silent Engine thrummed to life. It traces them with a finger, and feels the quiet weight under them: the tomb. This place is not just factory, not just temple. It is a burial ground, the root-mat thick with the compressed remains of the first life that gave its shape to the network, the first consciousness that wove its mind into the sap-tek, the bark, the hum, to build the perfect, unbreaking system that would outlast every storm, every ice age, every forgetting. The grief is not sharp here, not raw. It is woven into the bark, hummed into the engines, carried in the slow tremor of the Root-Worms as they crop the excess root-mat. It is a quiet, steady thing, as much a part of the balance as the sap-tek or the mist.

A benevolent power does not fix what is not broken. It does not impose its will on a system that has spent eons perfecting its own equilibrium. It leaves gifts that fit the rhythm of the place, small enough to be absorbed, soft enough not to disrupt the Unspoken Rule: nothing constructed, everything grown or catalyzed.

It kneels first, pressing a palm to the root-mat. The moss springs back, damp and cool under its touch. It tucks a single seed into the crevice between two root-fibers, no bigger than a Skitterer’s eye, made of hardened starlight and pollen from a world that only exists in its memory, a world where forests and factories grew side by side without war. It will take a thousand years to germinate. When it does, it will send up a single stalk, topped with a flower of iridescent cellulose that blooms once every century, its petals glowing soft gold like the wall membranes. When it opens, it will release a scent like the first rain on a new world, and a low, thrumming lullaby that will travel through the root-mat, down into the dark of the tomb, so whatever lies buried there will know it is not forgotten, that its work did not end when it passed into the mat. The Skitterers will find it in time, carry its pollen to the central node of the network, weave its memory into the glyphs on the bark, so the story of the visitor who came not to take, not to change, but to say thank you, will be sung in the hum of the engines for as long as the planet turns.

It stands, walks to the nearest Silent Engine, runs a hand over its smooth, lacquered surface, worn soft as river stone by eons of Skitterer tending. It leaves a pulse there, warm and slow, that travels through the polished silica, into the sap-tek, into every conduit, every stalk, every membrane in the network. It is a blessing, a quiet mark of appreciation, so that every Skitterer that tends an engine, every Glimmer that drifts through the mist, every Root-Worm that crops the root-mat will feel, deep in their core, that their work is seen, that they are loved not for the sap-tek they carry, not the spores they harvest, not the roots they trim, but for the simple fact of their being, their small, steady part in the endless, loud present. The pulse will linger in the sap-tek for ten thousand years, a warm undercurrent under the engine thrum, something no one will be able to name, but everyone will feel: a little less alone in the endless work.

It cups a hand, catches a drop of condensation beading on the wall membrane, and lets a tiny fragment of a constellation from its home drift into it, a map of a thousand other worlds that have found balance, where forests hum and factories breathe and nothing is wasted. It lets the drop fall back into the root-mat, and the pale, blind segment of a Root-Worm passing underneath does not pause, does not deviate from its path, just carries the light down, down into the dark of the tomb, where the first architect sleeps, its consciousness woven into the network, its form long since dissolved into root and bark and sap. The light will settle on the stone of its resting place, pulsing slow and steady, so it will know that its work spread beyond this planet, that other worlds learned from the balance it built, that it is part of a larger pattern of life and industry and peace it will never get to see, but will always be a part of.

Then it steps back, its form dissolving slowly into the mist, the warmth seeping out of the air, the hum shifting back to its normal rhythm, as if it was never there at all. The Skitterers keep tending the engines. The Glimmers keep drifting. The Root-Worm keeps cropping the root-mat, carrying the light down into the dark. The only traces left are the seed tucked in the crevice, the warm pulse in the sap-tek, the drop of starlight in the tomb.

Far away, among the swirling nebulae and the cold between stars, the power will feel the faint, steady hum of the Verdant Engine in the back of its mind, a small, warm note in the symphony of the universe, and it will smile, and turn its attention to the next place that needs a little grace: a smoldering forest on a war-torn world, a broken engine on a derelict station, a child crying on a drought-stricken plain. It will go not to fix, not to command, but to leave a seed, a pulse, a drop of light, something small and gentle that fits the rhythm of the world, that lets the beings there know they are seen, that their work, their grief, their tiny, steady part in the endless present, matters.

And in the grove, the hum goes on, eternal, loud, perfect. The engines do not break. The conduits do not clog. The Skitterers keep tending, their rhythm a little surer now. The Glimmers drift a little closer to the stalks. And deep in the root-mat, the Root-Worm that carried the light pauses, just for a second, then keeps moving, cropping the excess roots, doing its work, happy in a way it cannot name, because it knows, now, that it is not alone.
