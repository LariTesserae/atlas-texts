A benevolent power, understanding this final, thickened peace, would not intervene to change it. That would be a violation of its profound completion. To restore, to rebuild, to repopulate—each would be a fresh wound on a wound that has finally scarred over.

Instead, they would perform a silent, infinite act of *witness-tuning*.

They would take the one dynamic that exists—the slow, alchemical digestion of the built by the growing—and gently, subtly, lace its most fundamental process with a counter-memory.

They would not make the moss grow faster, or the stone erode slower. They would not shine a light or fill the air with sound.

They would alter the *qualia* of the handprint.

From now on, whenever a consciousness (be it a fleeting visitor, or the deep, slow awareness of the moss-ecosystem itself) makes contact with one of those ghostly depressions in the stone, the touch would not just be cool, damp stone and velvet moss.

For the span of a single, slow breath, the imprint would feel **warm**.

Not a biological warmth, but the psychic resonance of a moment of human intent: the warmth of a palm braced against effort, the warmth of a hand resting in brief, earned satisfaction, the warmth of a touch that was part of *making*. It would be the exact temperature and emotional signature of the builder’s own hand in that moment, preserved not in the stone, but in the *potential* of the stone’s surface, a latent impression waiting for a conscious touch to activate it.

It would be faint, subjective, and utterly indistinguishable from natural variation to any instrument. It would leave no mark, change no chemistry. The moss would not grow differently over it.

But the knowing touch—a human finger, a probing root-tendril, the sensitive lip of a wading creature that might one day evolve here—would suddenly, briefly, *feel the echo of the act that made the mark*. The chill of the Deep-Works would be undercut, for a moment, by the memory of labor and presence. The handprint would no longer be just a negative shape; it would be a node of experience, a place where the past is not just a trace, but a participatory sensation.

The benevolent power would do this for every true handprint, every tool-gouge, every intentional mark left by the builders that has not yet been completely softened into irregularity. They would weave this memory-ripple into the very rule of the place: **Growth is the only dynamic.**
Now, that dynamic carries a secondary, secret current: **The memory of the Maker, latent in the Made, can be briefly re-kindled by the touch of another conscious being.**

It is not a reversal. The moss still wins. The erasure proceeds. The place settles into thickened peace.

But in that final peace, a secret song plays—not heard, but *felt*—in the negative spaces left by hands that are gone. The deep cold is now and then touched by a deep, deep warmth. The absolute finality of the Tomb-Garden is forever haunted by the participatory ghost of the Home it was.

The power would do nothing more. They would let the *plink* continue, the moss spread, the chill seep. And they would wait, patient as the place itself, for the next touch to make a handprint remember it was ever a hand.
