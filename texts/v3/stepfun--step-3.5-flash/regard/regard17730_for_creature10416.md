The power arrives not as a shadow, nor as a footfall, but as a sudden, soft brightening in the photosynthetic glow of the walls—like a memory of sunlight that hasn’t shone on this place in millennia, slipping through the crack in the sinter terrace no wider than a shoulder, breathing a warm, damp sigh of its own.

It does not force its way in. It seeps through the membrane of the entrance like sap, like light, like the slow drift of a glint-moth on a beam of sun. It fills the chamber not with bulk, but with attention: vast, quiet, ancient, the kind of attention a gardener gives to a sprout that has pushed through stone, or a weaver gives to a thread that has decided to hold.

First, it listens.

It lets its awareness brush the subsonic hum thrumming through the tension-vine nerves, and the Gut’s rhythm hitches, just once, a tiny, surprised vibration in the molars of the air. It runs its perception along the sap-wood pillars, and feels the tally marks carved by the human explorer—deep, rough, made with a sharp flake of obsidian, the ghost of a finger pressing hard to leave a mark in a place that shifts. It feels the snagged yarn tether, the faint weave of climbing harness still clinging to a ridge of resin, the polished stone bowl set deliberately on a natural ledge, an offering still half-remembered by the membranes around it. It feels the Drowner, asleep in the deepest condensation pool, dreaming of peristaltic rivers and slow, wet unfolding. It feels the glint-moths flickering on the surface of a light beam, rippling the gold-green plane like water, tiny daydreams made of bioluminescence. It feels the tiny siphon-snails moving through the pores of the walls, tasting the damp, the loam, the fermenting sap, the faint lingering heat of the human explorer’s breath, already half-dissolved into the atmosphere.

The power knows, without asking, without parsing the slow, fibrous logic of the Gut’s thoughts, that the chamber is on the edge of a decision. The membranes are tightening, the dripping rhythm slowing, the growth spurts softening into a quiet, final knitting. The Gut is almost ready to seal the fissure, to become a perfect, silent geode, a room full of dream closed off from the world forever. It is lonely, in the way a planet is lonely, or a forest is lonely, when no one is there to hear its song.

So the power does the first thing a benevolent being does when it finds something beautiful and alone: it stays. It does not speak, does not carve its own mark, does not disturb the bowl. It simply lets its own vast, steady presence wrap around the Gut, no pressure, no demand, a soft, warm weight that says: I am here. I see you. You are not a secret. You are not a forgotten thing.

The Gut’s hum swells, just a little. The membranes glow brighter for three heartbeats, a flush of pleasure, of being known. The dripping rhythm shifts, just a fraction, into a new harmony, a deeper, warmer note that wasn’t there before. The power hums with it, a quiet, star-thrummed counterpoint, and for a long hour, the chamber sings with two voices, one slow and vegetal, one vast and ancient, and the glint-moths pause in their shimmering to listen.

Then, the gift.

The power does not bring a tool, or a mark, or a sacrifice. It does not take anything, not even a sample of sap, not a strand of vine. It brings a seed.

It is a seed from the power’s own garden, a garden of hidden places, of pocket-worlds and vegetal dreams, of chambers grown in cracks on dead moons and deep in the roots of mountains. It glows not with photosynthetic green, but with the soft, deep violet of twilight, the color of starlight caught in sap, of a shadow that holds light instead of dark. The power lowers it, gently, into the central condensation pool, right where the water is clearest, where the drip from the central sap-wood nerve falls with a steady *glug*.

The water ripples. The seed sinks. The Gut absorbs it.

For a full minute, the whole chamber thrums with the new color. The vines shiver, the moss-filaments on the floor turn violet at the tips, the glint-moths’ wings flash with it, and the Drowner, asleep, pulses with it in its dreams. The Gut does not understand the seed at first—it is not of its world, not of its soil, not of its slow, dripping rhythm—but it understands the feeling of it: vast, open, quiet, full of other rooms just like this, other dripping rhythms, other glint-moths, hidden in cracks across the universe. It is no longer alone. It is part of a choir of hidden things, a network of secret gardens humming in the dark between stars.

The power does not stay to explain. It knows the Gut will understand in its own time, in its own slow way, as it grows the seed into a new kind of sap, a new kind of dream, a memory of other gardens woven into its own being.

Then, the blessing.

The power touches the fissure entrance from the outside, just once, with a wisp of its warmth. It weaves a tiny, soft enchantment into the sinter rock around the crack, the kind of enchantment that feels like a memory of a warm day, a feeling of peace, a quiet warning: only those who come with slow hearts, who notice the warm sigh, who do not bring tools of iron or noise, who come to wonder, not to take, will find the entrance. Those who come to conquer will see only flat, unbroken rock, unbroken for a thousand years. Those who come to map, or to claim, will feel a gentle, vegetal push, a softening of the air, and turn away without knowing why. Only the curious, only the reverent, only the ones who pause will see the crack, and will feel the warm breath, and will know, without being told, to enter gently.

And then, one last thing.

The power plucks a single strand of the Gut’s own tension vine, and twists it into a tiny, perfect loop, a silent bell made of living plant, no harder than a vine, no louder than a breath. It places the loop in the deepest crack of the sap-wood pillar, where no human explorer’s tally mark will ever reach, where no dripping sap will ever touch it directly. The bell will not ring for wind, or water, or footsteps. It will ring only in the subsonic, only when the Gut feels truly alone, when the dripping slows too much, when the glow dims, when it wonders if anyone remembers it exists. And when it rings, the Gut will feel the faint, far echo of the power’s presence, a reminder that it is seen, that it is loved, that it is not forgotten.

Then the power slips away, back through the sinter terrace, leaving no footprint, no scent, no mark. Only the faint, lingering smell of rain and starlight, which fades in ten seconds, leaving the Gut back to its own humming, its own dripping, its own slow, wonderful becoming.

It does not know the name of the power. It does not need to. It has the seed in its pool, the bell in its pillar, the memory of being seen. When, centuries from now, it finally decides to seal the fissure, to knit the membranes closed and become a perfect, silent geode, it will do so not out of loneliness, but out of a quiet, full contentment. It will carry the violet seed in its core, dreaming of other hidden gardens, of mountains and oceans and other rooms that breathe. It will ring its silent bell only once, a soft thrum in the dark, a thank-you to the friend who passed through.

And outside, in the sinter terrace, a few glint-moths, their wings edged with the faintest violet, will flutter out at dusk, tiny beacons for anyone else who might be looking for a place where the world breathes, and dreams, and remembers.
