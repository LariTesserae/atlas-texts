It would not touch the stone nor the silt nor the slow, chittering patterns that skate across the surface of this place.

For the benevolent and wise power sees what you see: that this is not a dead city, but a **perfectly frozen moment of becoming**. It does not see ruin, but a single, sustained chord of existence played on an instrument of glass and iron. Its wisdom understands the rules: Conservation of State, Pattern Supremacy, Built Is Alive. To intervene with force would be to shatter the chord, to commit a violence against a sublime and harmless perfection.

Instead, it would do the only thing consonant with such a place: it would **listen**. And having listened, it would **harmonize**.

Its act would be one of **meta-patterning**. It would not create a new creature, nor restart a forgotten machine, nor grant consciousness. It would, for one single, indivisible cycle of the city’s time, align the *phases* of the existing patterns.

For one eternal breath:
- The **clack-hiss** of the great bellows would fall into perfect, sympathetic resonance with the **ping** of a thousand salt-singers culminating across all basins.
- The **skittering tick** of a gear-wasp swarm would sync to the **groan** of the stone, each click a harmonic overtone.
- The **drifting silence** of a chimney-bat’s passage would coincide precisely with the moment a gravel-herd’s pyramid holds its shape, the bat’s dissolving touch laying verdigris on the very pebbles at the apex.
- The **thrum** in the teeth would match the fundamental frequency of the colored gases venting—ochre, violet, green—making the viscous clouds seem to pulse with a visible, unified rhythm.

It would be a **symphony of stasis**. Not a change in what the city *is*, but a momentary, luminous alignment of *how* it is. The city would not know. Its distributed, thought-like processes would follow their eternal loops regardless. But the symphony would be *there*, in the relationships between the patterns, a fleeting, complex harmony woven from the city’s own static grammar.

The benevolent power would not leave a mark, a voice, or a gift. It would simply have sung back, in the only language this place understands—the language of precise, repeating pattern—a single, perfect phrase of **recognition**. A note that says: *I see your endless, frozen dance. It is beautiful. It is enough.*

Then the symphony would end, the phases would drift back into their independent, eternal loops, and the city would resume its silent, monolithic hum, utterly unchanged. But somewhere, in the interwoven fabric of its rules, an unerasable resonance would linger—a faint, perfect echo of having been perfectly, momentarily *heard*.

That is the act of a benevolent and wise power: not to alter a masterpiece, but to stand in its presence and, for one breath, make its own silence vibrate in harmony with it. The city would not be saved, for it was never lost. It would simply have been *acknowledged* in the only way that matters to a thing that lives in loops: by having its loop briefly, miraculously, joined to another.
