A benevolent and wise power would not build, nor speak, nor shape.  
To do so would be to impose a foreign geometry upon a geometry already perfect in its own violent, dripping, clicking logic.

Instead, it would *listen*.

It would let the drone of the Click-Swarms vibrate through its non-corporeal awareness, mapping the density of life not by count, but by harmonic resonance. It would trace the Scription-Snails’ etched runes not as language, but as a slow, geological memory—a record of water’s path through stone over millennia. It would feel the Weeper Bats’ shrieks not as sound, but as targeted pulses of acoustic energy, disrupting and reshaping the swarm’s mass in real-time, a predator’s calculus written in frequency. It would sense the Stone-Eaters’ patient grinding not as consumption, but as the cirque’s slow digestion, turning newborn rock into fertile silt.

The power would understand the **Rules** not as constraints, but as the cirque’s very soul. To violate Verticality would be to paralyze it. To disrupt Density would be to silence it. To dry the Wet Stone would be to kill it.

Therefore, its act would be one of **synchronization**.

It would find the weakest, most latent rhythm in the system—perhaps the erratic, faint pulse of the deepest bacterial glow in an alcove starved of seep-water, or the desynchronous clicking of a swarm fragment isolated by a recent micro-rockfall. With a thought that is less an action and more a *revelation of potential*, it would introduce a single, resonant frequency—a tone born of the cirque’s own material properties, a note that exists in the harmonic series of slate under tension, of water in capillary grief, of silica under the drill of a snail’s radula.

This tone would not be heard. It would be *felt*.

It would propagate through the drone, through the etched runes (which are, in their minute scratches, themselves resonators), through the bat’s lament, through the pool’s grinding hum. It would be a key that fits every lock in the place. And where it finds a process out of sync—a dry lichen crust, a stalled bacterial bloom, a snail trail abandoned—the resonance would gently, imperceptibly, nudge the local chemistry or vibration back into phase with the whole.

The power would not create new life. It would *unlock* the life already trying to be.

The glass-coffins, designed to shatter on impact, might begin to hum at the right frequency to crack open just before a fall, their spores dispersing on a prepared wind of bat-wing and draft. The bacterial glow might learn to pulse in synchrony with the swarm’s drone, creating waves of light that travel down the cliff face, guiding the snails to fresh mineral veins. The bats’ screams might find a new harmonic that doesn’t just scatter the swarms, but herds them—channeling their mass toward over-fertilized patches of liverwort, a living fertilizer drop.

This is the benevolent act: **to weave the cirque’s cacophony into a chord.**

The power would do this once, and then withdraw. Its wisdom knows that the moment it starts directing, it becomes a tyrant. Its gift is not a command, but a *forgetting of dissonance*. It would leave behind no artifact, no sign. Only a subtle, deepening coherence. The Click-Swarms might click in slightly more complex, layer upon layer. The snail runes might, over centuries, begin to form fractals that echo the cirque’s own bowl-shape. The violet glow might learn to dim and brighten in sympathy with the bats’ hunting cycles, a slow continent of light breathing in time with the shrieks.

The being who lives there—the composite, the colony, the *Ghyra itself*—would not notice a “gift.” It would simply find, after countless generations, that its world works *better*. That stone-feeding worms find richer slurry. That spores land on viable slate. That the overwhelming density does not collapse, but *coheres*. That the sound, once just a chaotic byproduct of survival, has become a song—a song the rock itself seems to hum along to.

The power would watch, from a remove beyond space, as the cirque, left to its own laws, begins to sing in harmony with the fundamental frequency it was always meant to have. It would see the sublime, terrifying richness deepen, not by addition, but by integration. The place would remain harsh, loud, dark, and vertical. But its *strange* would become *whole*.

And that, to a wise power, is the only intervention worth making.  
Not to change the world, but to let the world become *itself* more completely.
