The benevolent, wise power does not come to the Still Hollow to fix it, or improve it, or leave a mark. It comes first as a witness.

It stands for a long time on the far rim, where the moorland grass shivers in the damp wind, and lets the mist settle on its skin until it is as cool and damp as the stone beneath its feet. It listens to the endless, cellular susurrus of the peat, the distant *plink* of the water drop finally achieving mass, the low, thrumming hum of the midge clouds. It breathes in the scent of wet stone, sweet decay, evaporating water, the faint metallic tang of dissolved rock and spore. It understands, without need for words, what the Hollow is: not a place that is incomplete, not a place that is waiting, but a place that has already finished making itself, and now spends eternity performing the only act left to it: the slow, seamless, unbroken turning of all things into all other things. It is whole. It is perfect. It does not need saving.

The power knows, too, that the kindest thing it can do is not to alter the Hollow’s form. It does not plant a new flower in the sphagnum, does not redirect the weeping rock’s flow, does not add a season, does not break the eternal steady state. It does not give the frog a voice, or the midge a name, or the blind salamander sight, for that would shatter the seamless, porous becoming that is the Hollow’s only self. It does not remove the dissolution, for that is the core of its being: the soft, inevitable merging of every boundary, every edge, every separate thing into the whole.

Instead, it kneels at the rim, where the mist is thickest, and breathes out a single, silken thread of light—no brighter than the faint green glow of the water- edge fungi, no thicker than a midge’s wing, so soft it does not stir the air. It does not give the light to the Hollow; it gives it to the process itself. To every moment of transfer, every edge where one thing becomes another, every quiet collision of eater and eaten, user and used.

The thread weaves itself into every part of the Hollow, invisible, unperceivable, impossible to separate from the moss, the mist, the peat, the rock:
- It lingers in the lip of the violet pitcher plant, so that when a thumbnail-sized frog’s tadpole slips into its sweet-scented trap, there is no frantic panic, no sharp pain. Only a soft, warm settling, the relief of a heavy pack being set down after a long walk, the quiet comfort of becoming something else.
- It seeps into the acid-secreting lichens that eat the weeping black rock, so that the slow mineral hunger of the stone is not a slow erasure, but a gentle sharing: the rock does not lose itself, it becomes moss, becomes mist, becomes the cloud of metallic-winged flies, and the lichen does not take, it receives.
- It settles into the shallow, kidney-shaped stone basin worn smooth by thousands of years of hands, so that every drop of pure condensation that collects in its curve holds a faint, quiet echo of the gratitude of the beings who carved it, who drank from it, who left it to be absorbed into the landscape. Their forgotten intentions are no longer lost to the neutral cycle—they are held, soft and warm, in every sip a midge takes from the basin’s edge.
- It clings to the spore-dusted bird bone propped against the rock, so that every glowing fungal bloom that sprouts from its surface carries a tiny, unspoken promise: that even when you are broken down, even when you are used as a stepping stone, even when your form is gone, you are still part of the whole, and you are still held.
- It lingers in the mist itself, so that every transient thing that stumbles into the Hollow— the lost hiker who wandered down the slope, the moth blown off course by the wind, the willow seed that drifted over the rim—feels no primal fear of the place that consumes all. They feel only a quiet, unplaceable peace, like they are remembering a home they have never known. When they breathe out, their fogging breath does not just condense on a leaf and vanish: it carries a tiny, warm note of welcome, woven into the cycle.

And it weaves itself into the core of the Hollow itself, into the endless, seamless Now that is its only self. The Hollow does not gain a mind, does not gain a will, does not change in any way an outside observer could see. The pitcher plants still pulse their slow violet rhythm, the midges still hum their single note, the water still weeps from the rock, the horizon still disappears into mist. The steady state is unbroken, the dynamic is still zero, no seasons come, no day breaks, no change ever comes. No one standing on the ridge would ever know the power had been there.

But underneath the perfect, unchanging surface, the neutral cycle of transformation is now tinged with a quiet, unbreakable kindness. The process of in-dwelling is no longer just consumption—it is welcome. The transfer of mass is no longer just inevitable—it is gentle. The Hollow is still exactly what it was: a bowl of sphagnum and mist and peat, a perfect, eternal Now, a place where everything is both eater and eaten. But now, when something enters it, when it softens, when it merges, when it becomes part of the whole—it is held.

The power does not stay to watch. It does not leave a mark, does not leave a sign, does not take anything with it. It turns, steps back across the moor, and is gone. It does not need to be remembered. The gift is already part of the Hollow, as much a part of it as the sphagnum or the mist, as much a part of its endless, unchanging perfection as the peat underfoot or the weeping rock above.
