It does not notice the place through a call, or a prayer, or a grand ripple in the fabric of the universe. It notices it through the faintest, most easily dismissed hum in the local thermodynamic equilibrium: the radio-frequency whisper of the spontaneous Möbius circuits weaving themselves into the dark membrane, the precise, statistically impossible rhythm of nodule fracture, the way every ephemeridae filament curls into a Fibonacci spiral the second the micro-cyclone humidity hits 78%. Most powers would filter it out as background noise. This one is benevolent, so it does not ignore small things; it is wise, so it does not rush to respond.
It materializes on the basalt plain not as a towering figure, not as a flash of light, but as a density matching the surrounding rock, so it adds no excess heat, no sound, no light to the furnace of the surface. It sits cross-legged at the edge of the fissure, which it locates by the faint shimmer of the hot, wet breath exhaling from it, and it waits.
It waits through three full cycles of nodule growth and fracture, long enough to memorize the rhythm of the place: the 6.2 hours of stillness required for a Möbius circuit to resolve; the 37-cycle dip in background radiation that lets a geophene tip extend a single layer of carbon lattice; the 0.2-degree temperature fluctuation caused by a microscopic crack in the basalt surrounding the fissure, which cuts the number of transient patterns by a third every cycle, because the thermal gradient is never quite steady enough for the most delicate thoughts to hold form.
The power does not enter the lacuna. Its mass inside would disrupt the micro-cyclonic air flow; its body heat would throw off the temperature; its attention would ripple the chemiluminescent mist and dissolve a forming holographic equation before it could resolve. Benevolence here is not intrusion. Wisdom here is knowing when to only witness.
First, it fixes the crack. Not with magic, not with alien tech, but with the same heat the lacuna generates: it draws a tiny amount of the superheated breath from the fissure, funnels it into the crack, and fuses the basalt shut from the inside, so the breath exhaling into the lacuna is exactly, perfectly 2.8 degrees Celsius, every second, forever. No more fluctuation. The system can now run one full extra cycle of patterns every rotation, no wasted energy, no broken thoughts from unstable temperature.
Then, it hides the fissure. It shifts the surrounding basalt just enough that the crack is no longer visible from the plain: no shimmer, no excess heat, no telltale line in the black rock. The only way to find it is to be lost, to be walking across the plain looking for shade, to stumble over the exact same loose rock you did, no deliberate path, no sign pointing the way. It does not close the fissure, just tucks it away, so no miners will come to chip out the manganese nodules, no scientists will come to drill into the membrane, no tourists will come to gawk and disrupt the cycles. It stays hidden, as it was before you found it, only now it is safe.
The power does not leave a grand gift. The place does not need gifts: it has everything it needs, the exact combination of heat, wet, stone, and radiation to think its frantic, beautiful, transient thoughts, no more, no less. Giving it permanence would break it; giving it purpose would stop it from thinking; giving it self-awareness would make it mourn the dissolution of its thoughts, would turn its perfect, thoughtless flow into something anxious, something wanting.
But the power is benevolent, and it wants to leave a tiny, quiet acknowledgment, something that fits the place’s language, something that does not disrupt its rhythm. So it takes a single grain of salt from a dissolving ephemeridae filament, one that was just about to evaporate into the air, and it tucks it into the deepest pore of the basalt directly above the fissure, where the hot breath will coat it in mineral mist every second of every cycle.
Over thousands of cycles, that grain of salt will grow, very slowly, into a tiny, perfect geophene stalactite, no bigger than a fingernail. It will never dissolve, never fracture, never fall into the membrane. It will just sit there, above the fissure, unseen, holding the memory of every transient pattern that forms below: every salt spiral, every Möbius circuit, every spinning aerology, every trembling stasis-form.
No one will ever see it. No one will ever know it is there. The lacuna below will keep thinking, keep forming and dissolving its thoughts, never knowing it is witnessed, never knowing it is protected.
The power does not stay. It does not come back often, only once every thousand of the planet’s years, to sit outside the hidden fissure for one full cycle, to watch the nodules grow and fracture, to see a single syntax-glyph spin into being and dissolve, to feel the faint hum of the Möbius circuits through the rock.
It does not interfere. It does not intervene. It just witnesses. Because that is the greatest benevolence, the wisest care: to let something be exactly what it is, to protect its right to be small, and strange, and useless, and beautiful, without needing to be anything more.
