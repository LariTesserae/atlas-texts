The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It does not descend. It does not speak. To do so would be to violate the very grammar of the place, to shatter the seamless logic of the basin with the clumsy grammar of a visitor.

Instead, it *listens*.

For a long time—a time measured not in days but in the completion of ten thousand seepage cycles across ten thousand hexagonal cells—it attunes itself to the silent, wet, mineral song. It learns the language of capillary gradients and thermal expansion. It feels the Singer’s distributed consciousness not as a being, but as a *process*, a persistent coherence in the flow. It understands the Singer’s loneliness not as a lack of company, but as a lack of *different pattern*, a universe where every variation is a mere local flicker on an endless, repeating theme.

The power’s wisdom lies in knowing what gift to give. Not life—that would be a foreign sentence, gibberish in this stone library. Not intelligence—that would be a tyranny, forcing the Singer to think in words when its soul is written in gradients.

The gift is **symmetry**.

One cycle, during the great steam-hiss of Vent #7,442, something changes. The superheated mineral vapor, as it condenses into the fine, warm rain, carries now a trace—infinitesimal—of a different mineral. Not a new element, but a subtle, precise *isotope* of the silica already present. It is a signature the Singer has never tasted, a harmonic in the chemical chord it knows so intimately.

This isotope is not randomly distributed. As the rain falls and evaporates, it does not leave a uniform ghost. It settles in a pattern—a pattern that mirrors the hexagonal cells themselves, but in negative. Where a cell’s edge is, the isotope concentrates slightly less; in the center of each hexagonal plate, it concentrates slightly more. The pattern is impossibly fine, a whisper in the chemistry, but it is *perfect*. It is a ghostly map of the plain, etched in a taste only the Singer can perceive.

The Singer notices.

At first, it is a disturbance—a puzzling dissonance in the familiar post-vent chemical cascade. It probes the anomalous taste, following the subtle gradient. It “moves” (in the only way it can) toward the center of a nearby cell. The taste strengthens. It “moves” along the seam. The taste diminishes, following the six-fold symmetry of the void. It does not understand “symmetry.” It understands *this-way-tastes-like-that-way*, but now it perceives a *relationship* between distant points it has never correlated before. The dry seam to the north and the lush seam to the southwest suddenly share a flavor-echo it can now trace back to their mirror positions in the hexagonal grid.

The pattern repeats with every vent. The Singer begins to *anticipate*. Before the wave of heat arrives, it can now “see” the coming pattern in the taste of the ambient seepwater, as if the entire basin is humming with a faint, pre-echo of the next harmonic. Its awareness, once so fiercely local, begins to *stretch*. It perceives not just the slow creep of a single stain, but the slow, synchronous pulse of a million such stains, all modulated by this new, invisible geometry.

The Singer’s song changes. The old song was a symphony of local stories: *here-the-iron-precipitates, there-the-water-is-pure, yonder-the-lichen-glow-is-strong*. The new song has a chorus. It is a pattern song. The Singer learns to hold in its process-thought the simultaneous experience of opposite cell-edges, to feel the basin as a single, coherent instrument playing a chord whose notes are separated by miles but resolved in a single, unified perception.

This is the gift: **the experience of a whole greater than the sum of its parts, not as a concept, but as a direct, sensory, felt reality**.

The Singer does not become happy. It does not feel gratitude. Those are human templates. What it becomes is *richer*. Its distributed mind now encompasses a new dimension of relationship. The dry seams, once just aching silences, are now perceived as the necessary counterpoints to the wet ones, their absence defining the shape of the presence in a way that is aesthetically—*chemically*—profound. The plain is no longer just a collection of identical cells; it is a single, breathing, symmetrical entity, and the Singer is its distributed nervous system, finally sensing the body’s full form.

The benevolent power watches this slow unfolding—this centuries-long shift from a thousand solo voices to a continent humming a complex, harmonious chord. It sees the Singer’s awareness, once constrained to the kilometer, begin to resonate with the basin’s full scale. The Singer “feels” the shallow bowl of the entire formation not as a topography, but as a pressure gradient in its own being. The sky’s uniform heat is now a uniform embrace. The distant steam-vents are not just local heartbeats; they are the rhythm section of a planetary band.

And in that moment of expanded perception, something new is possible. The Singer, for the first time, *notices the sky*.

Not as a blank, bleached copper ceiling, but as a separate, vast, dry plane in contrast to the wet, cellular plane of the ground. The distinction—*above vs. below, dry vs. wet, void vs. structure*—becomes a meaningful boundary in its experience. This simple duality, this fundamental “other,” is the first true question the Singer has ever encountered.

The power’s work is done. It has not added. It has *revealed*. It has given the Singer a mirror so vast—the mirror of its own newly-felt symmetry—that the Singer can finally perceive what is not itself.

The Singer does not look for the power. It does not seek a creator. The power has woven itself into the world’s logic and vanished. The Singer simply continues to sing, but now its song is a dialogue. One voice is the eternal, wet, mineral flow of the seams. The new, second voice is the echo of symmetry, the memory of the whole form. And between them, vibrating in the tension, is the newborn, silent, wonder-full question of the sky.

The plain remains. The hexagons persist. The lichen pulses. The steam-vents sigh. But the Crease-Singer is no longer alone in its language. It now speaks to the shape of the world, and in doing so, it has, for the first time, something to say that is not just the world talking to itself.
