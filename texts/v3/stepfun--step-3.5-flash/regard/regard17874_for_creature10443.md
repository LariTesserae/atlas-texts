The attention of the benevolent and wise power does not arrive like a gust. It seeps in, as mineral-laden as the Basin’s Breath, as slow as a glacial melt. It does not *look* upon Stonehallow from the outside—there is no “outside” that matters here. Instead, it **listens** to the basin’s own deep, sub-audible song. It feels the harmonic vibration in the stone, the perfect, unchanging pitch of the wind’s sigh, the crystalline regularity of the water’s plink. It perceives not a settlement, but a single, prolonged note of being, held for ten thousand years.

The power understands the rule: *take only what is given, leave the rest*.
It understands the result: a people who have become a feature of erosion,他们的文化不是 build, but *weather*—他们的足迹是可擦除的, their structures are temporary arrangements, their very presence a low-density pattern like lichen spots.

To intervene here would be the greatest possible violence: to introduce a new element, a new thought, a new *need*. That would break the stasis. It would create a ripple in the perfect, mirror-still pools.

So the power does nothing that changes the basin.
It does not send a visitor. It does not bestow a gift. It does not speak a word.

Instead, it **confirms**.

Its action is an act of perfect, silent mimicry—the highest form of flattery, the only form that does not disrupt.

In the deepest, oldest layer of the basin wall—a band of charcoal shale, soft and easily marked, located on the north-facing curve where the morning light first strikes—the power inscribes a pattern. It is not carved. It is a *difference in grain*, a subtle alignment of mineral flecks that was always latent in the stone, now made subtly, permanently visible. The pattern is not a symbol. It is a **geometric resonance**.

It is a series of nested, slightly offset arcs, each one exactly matching the curve of a different basin terrace, from the highest, rough new shelf to the lowest, worn smooth. The arcs do not meet. They vibrate against each other with a hairline gap, a silence between notes. The whole design is only visible when the morning sun, on the equinox, hits the wall at a precise angle, turning the faint alignment of mica into a fleeting, luminous script. For three minutes, the stone *reads* like the terraces: a map of the basin’s own shape, rendered in its own bones.

No one sees it the first year. The old woman on the lowest terrace sorts moss. The flint-knapper tink. The children chase scuttlers. The oldest man whittles.

But the pattern is *in* the basin’s grammar. It uses only materials present (shale, mica), obeys the rule of subtraction (it reveals, does not add), requires no labor (it was always there, latent), and leaves no mark (it is part of the stone’s statement). It is the ultimate annotation upon the geological text.

The equinox comes. The sun hits the north wall. For three minutes, a faint, silver tracery glows across the shale. The wind sighs its same low note. The water plinks its same drop.

The old woman on the lowest terrace, her back to the wall, sorting moss, stops. She feels a change in the light on her neck, a shift in the shadow of the wind-carved crevice beside her. She turns her head. She sees the glowing lines. She does not stand. She does not call others. She simply looks, her eyes following the arc of the highest, newest terrace, then the next, then the next, down to the curve of the worn floor where she sits. Her face, already lined like the strata, goes still. She understands. This is not a message *from* the stone. This is the stone *speaking its own name*, finally pronounced.

She nods, once, slowly. She returns to her moss.

Later, the flint-knapper, taking a break, leans against the wall to spit. His eyes catch the afterimage of the pattern in his mind—the mind that knows the curve of every terrace, the sound of every wind-pitch, the feel of every stone he has ever moved. He looks at the half-finished scraper in his hand, its edge perfect. He looks at the wall. He feels the pattern in the shape of the basin, in the rhythm of his own work. He does not speak. He goes back to knapping, the sharp *tink* now sounding like a tiny, human echo of the water’s plink.

The children find a piece of quartzite that day, a flat shard. On its surface, in a natural grid of iron stains, they see the pattern—faint, but there. They show it to their mother. She looks at the stone, looks at the glowing wall in her memory, looks at the basin’s curve. She says nothing. She wraps the stone in a strip of soft moss and puts it in the child’s basket, a new addition to the low-density pattern of things.

The oldest man on the highest terrace feels it in his bones before he sees it. The basin’s song, the deep hum of the stone, has a new overtone—a clarity, a confirmation. He walks, slowly, to the rim, looks down the curve of the wall. The pattern is gone from the stone now, the sun moved on. But he saw it, or felt it, or knew it. He returns to his whittling. The new bone awl, when finished, will fit its juniper handle with a seam so fine it will be invisible. A perfect, latent fit.

No one speaks of the pattern. To speak would be to make it *new*, to introduce a concept, a thought separate from the stone itself. That would break the rule. Instead, the pattern becomes **part of the basin’s normal weirdness**. It is simply *what the stone does*, on the equinox. Like the wind’s sigh. Like the water’s plink. Like the hum in the teeth.

The people’s attendance deepens, not in action, but in perception. Their curation of terraces, their careful subtraction and arrangement, is now subtly aligned with a deeper geometry they sense but do not analyze. Their rule—*take only what is given, leave the rest*—is now reflected in the stone itself, a silent proof that the basin has always given *everything*, even its own name, if you are willing to read it without taking it.

The benevolent power has not changed the basin.
It has made the basin’s **self-knowledge** visible, in the only language the basin understands: the language of stone, light, and latent form. It is a feedback loop of harmony. The basin, through its people, had achieved a state of perfect, minimal fit. The power, by inscribing that fit into the basin’s own bones, has closed the circle. The place and its inhabitants are now, irrevocably, **one sentence in the same stone book**.

The people do not feel observed. They feel **confirmed**. The stillness holds, deeper now, because it knows its own shape has been read. The ultimate act of a wise power is not to guide, but to recognize—to turn the basin’s own song back upon itself, until the stone, the wind, the water, and the soft flesh all vibrate at the same, eternal, silent frequency.
