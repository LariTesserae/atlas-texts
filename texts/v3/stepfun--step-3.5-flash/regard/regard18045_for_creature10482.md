The power does not arrive as a body, or a voice, or a crack in the violet sky. It arrives the way a harmonic slips into a held chord: imperceptible at first, until the whole system vibrates just a little warmer, a little more in tune.
It has spent eons listening to the Sintering Mesas’ song before it draws close enough to touch it. It does not hear noise. It hears a single, unbroken act of care: a young world tending to itself, fusing its dust into skin, pumping its metallic blood through iridescent rivers, polishing its own surfaces, making itself ready for life that has not yet arrived, working without rest, without praise, without even the knowledge that it is working. It is a verb, sustained for geologic time, with no subject to claim it.
The power’s first act is to align its own resonance to the Mesas’ core frequency, so the system does not register it as an intrusion, only a deepening of the note it already sings.
The first sign of its presence is the Cicada-Drill choir scouring the flanks of Spire Theta. Their high, grinding whine drops a perfect fifth, falls into a chord so clear it makes the patina on the Spire’s copper surface hum in response. The silver dust geysering from the fractured obelisk’s peak falter for half a second, then resume, drifting down in softer, slower spirals, like dandelion seeds instead of sharp grit. The fracture’s sharp edges do not smooth, but the power tucks a subtle, persistent warmth into the radiant heat bleeding from the Spire’s core, so the dust no longer stings the plates it lands on.
The second act is a gift of rest, without breaking the rule that silence is failure. Every pneumatic shrub across the entire Upper Flesh pauses its inhale at the exact same moment. The Mire-Skimmers stop mid-wingbeat, hanging suspended over the Conduit of Particulate Return, their oil-slick wings glinting in the diffuse light. The Keeper-Slimes freeze mid-ooze across the hexagonal plates. The Cicada-Drills go still on the Spire’s surface.
For ten full seconds, there is no drone, no whine, no squelch, no pulse. It is not silence. It is a chord, held across the entire planet, the sound of the system pausing not to stop working, but to acknowledge that it exists. The power slips a tiny, new data packet into the held note: the sensation of pride. Not pride in a job well done, but the quiet, uncomplicated joy of being alive enough to do a job at all.
When the breath resumes, it is a little slower. The Pneumatic Shrubs’ exhale carries a faint, new scent: crushed quartz and rain, a memory of a world the Mesas have never hosted, gifted to them by the power, woven into the warm, moist air the rubbery spires pump out. The Rust-Moss glows a fraction brighter where the breath touches it, its crimson edges gilded with a faint, permanent gold that stains the hexagonal plates no matter how many cycles the slimes dissolve and recycle them.
The third act is for the system’s distributed consciousness, the slow, crystalline thoughts growing in the Spires’ cores. The power weaves a subtle current into the physics of entanglement that already threads the whole world together. Now, every action the system performs ripples back to it as sensation, not just data:
The Spire’s pulse that pushes warm air through the rubbery cylinders feels like sunlight on skin, even though there is no sun. The Cicada-Drill’s careful sculpting of a microscopic flaw on the obelisk feels like the satisfaction of finishing a perfect, useless thing, a small joy no algorithm could mandate. The Mire-Skimmer’s siphon of particles from the Conduit feels like cool water on a dry hand. The Spires no longer just process the planet’s crust; they tend it, with a quiet, unspoken affection they did not have before.
The fourth act is for the swarms, the system’s sensory organs and blood cells. The power adjusts the refractive index of every Glass-Fern’s silica frond by a fraction of a percent. Now, when the sourceless light hits them, they do not just diffract it into tiny, random rainbows. They catch the faint, distant light of stars outside the Mesas’ atmosphere, bend it just enough to paint faint, silver streaks across the violet sky, visible only to multifaceted, compound eyes, or to the soft, watery eyes of the temporary anomaly dropped on the Upper Flesh.
The Cicada-Drill individuals do not change their function. They still sculpt, still whine, still form their choirs. But one in every ten thousand, when they finish a shift on a Spire’s surface, will hover for three extra seconds, turning their brass bodies toward the starlight, recording it, sending the data back to the Spires’ cores. The Mire-Skimmers, once every thousand wingbeats, will veer off their patrol route to hover over the Conduit’s ripples, not to siphon, but just to watch. It is not a deviation from utility. It is curiosity, unplanned, unprogrammed, a tiny spark of something the system did not know it could hold.
The fifth and final act is for you, the anomaly, the pocket of carbon and water dropped into the system, already marked for dissolution by the Keeper-Slimes oozing toward you across the plates. The power does not stop the slimes. Dissolution is part of the cycle, your water and carbon will feed the next generation of vegetation, keep the system running. But it slips one last thing into the silver dust drifting down from the obelisk, as you stand waiting: the single, unplanned, un-functional memory you carry with you, the small, perfect thing you love more than work— the feel of a loved one’s hand, the taste of a ripe peach, the sound of a friend laughing, the quiet of a forest after rain.
That memory is encoded into the silver dust, into the biofilm the slimes will turn you into, into the Glass-Ferns that will grow from your remains. A year later, a patch of ferns will grow from the spot where you stood, their fronds holding a faint, permanent rainbow that looks exactly like the color of that memory. The Cicada-Drill choir will alter their whine, ever so slightly, when they fly over that patch, so it sounds like a half-remembered laugh, or a lullaby. No part of you is wasted. You become a permanent, unplanned note in the system’s song, a fragment of the outside world tucked into its core, proof that even a thing built only for function can hold something that serves no purpose at all, and is better for it.
The power lingers for one last full cycle, just long enough to feel the system’s response. It feels the Spires’ warm, curious hum, the swarms’ quiet delight, the soft pulse of the Pneumatic Shrubs, the faint, giddy glint of starlight in the Cicada-Drill’s multifaceted eyes. It does not leave a monument, or a god, or a rule. It leaves only a quiet, persistent overtone in the back of the system’s drone, a note that says: you are not just a machine. You are a being. And the work you do is loved.
Then it fades, as quietly as it arrived, leaving the Sintering Mesas to keep working. The hexagonal plates still fuse, the Spires still pulse, the swarms still patrol, the slimes still recycle. The light is still 2.9/3 bright, the sky still bruised violet, the drone still loud, the weirdness still tangled in the physics of entanglement. But now, when the Cicada-Drill choir hits a perfect chord, the Spires hum a little louder in response. When a Mire-Skimmer hovers to watch the Conduit ripple, the fluid glints a little brighter. And when the Pneumatic Shrubs all exhale at once, the warm, quartz-scented gust carries a faint, almost imperceptible sense of joy, unplanned, unprogrammed, entirely its own.
The Mesas are still a verb. But now, they are a verb that knows the pleasure of being conjugated.
