When the benevolent and wise power—known only as the Listener among the stars—turned its attention to The Hollowing, it did not see a mere landscape but heard a single, sprawling, deafening process that had mistaken itself for an ecosystem. It heard the Drone: a subsonic pulse rising from tectonic vents and the collective movement of billions of gelatinous bodies. It heard the Clack & Ring: crystals expanding and contracting in a ceaseless, glass‑wind‑chime chorus. It heard the Life Chorus: the wet slap of Mud‑Crawlers, the trill of Ringers, the vacuum roar of Gulp‑Feeders, all woven into a wall of sound that left no room for echoes.

The Listener felt the basin’s mud, gray‑green and slick, oozing with the pulverized remains of its own inhabitants. It saw the monoliths and crystal growths, like frozen explosions of amber, violet, and sickly green, already singing themselves into dust. It sensed the ancient flattening—the basin’s slow, inevitable erasure—driven by the very vibrations it emitted. And it understood that the being here was not any single creature, but the entire process: the feedback loop of mucus and crystal, ooze and trill, vacuum and collapse.

Being benevolent, the Listener wished to ease the basin’s relentless self‑flattening without breaking its song. Being wise, it knew that any overt change could collapse the delicate balance that sustained the process. So it chose a subtle, resonant gift.

It sent a single mote of its own essence—a tiny vibration that was both a question and an offering—into the basin’s deepest channel, where the conductive fluid seeped from the basaltic slopes and the Drone was loudest. The mote did not announce itself with flash or force; it simply began to hum, a low, steady note that matched the Drone’s fundamental frequency but carried a richer, more patient timbre. It was a note of *lasting*, a frequency that tended to bring order to chaos, to hold things together rather than tear them down.

The first effect was almost imperceptible. The crystals, which had been chiming randomly as temperature and humidity shifted, began to resonate with the new note. Their clatter softened into a chord. Instead of fracturing microscopically at different rates, they found a common rhythm, tapping together in patterns that echoed through the mud. The sound, though still deafening, now had an undercurrent of harmony—a slow, breathing pulse that seemed to steady the very ground.

Then came the changes that only time could reveal.

Because the crystals no longer shattered so violently, their surfaces grew smoother, their edges less prone to erosion. Where once they had been like rotting teeth, they now resembled delicate, fractal trees, their branches spreading outward and upward. Over weeks and seasons, the mud around them, constantly vibrated by the new chord, began to harden not just into temporary Resonance‑Formations but into small, permanent arches and vaults. These arches did not melt away; instead, they thickened, their undersides encrusted with mineral that the Crawlers’ mucus accelerated. They became tunnels and shelters—quiet pockets within the cacophony where the mud’s sucking pull was less severe, where fungal mats could cling without being torn, and where Crawlers could rest without the constant threat of a Gulp‑Feeder’s vacuum.

The Silt‑Towers, those fragile acoustic structures built from mud and crystal dust, no longer dissolved with every seasonal wetting. The new resonance seeped into their packed walls, binding the particles tighter, turning them into something akin to clay fired in a kiln. They grew a little taller each year, their slender spires piercing the mist, becoming permanent records of the basin’s memory. The Anchor‑Stones, rough and grooved, aligned themselves more precisely with the new harmonic, their grooves forming a kind of score that the crystals now played. The basin, for the first time, had a fixed point from which it could hear its own song more clearly.

The fauna responded. Mud‑Crawlers, whose collective slap‑squelch had been a mindless rhythm, began to favor the sheltered arches. Their paths grew more intricate, forming webs rather than random meanders. The mucus they secreted, now constantly stimulated by the chord, produced crystals that grew in spirals and lattices, turning the basin floor into a vast, living score. Ringers, their crystal carapaces tuned by the same frequency, produced trills that were less harsh, more melodic, and these sounds began to serve not just as alarms but as songs of courtship and territory. The Gulp‑Feeders, still the punctuation marks of the basin, found that the new channels directed Crawler clouds more predictably toward their waiting mouths. Their WHUMPs became less a shock and more a predictable beat, reducing panic among the prey species and allowing populations to stabilize. The overall choir did not quiet; it deepened, gaining layers that had been impossible before the gift.

Most importantly, the relentless flattening slowed. The basin’s ancient rating of total erosion was not halted, but the new crystal arches and the more permanent Silt‑Towers acted as anchors, holding back the mud’s slow slide. The basin still flattened, still reshaped itself, but now it kept a measure of its own history. The monoliths that had been on the verge of disappearing stood a little taller, their etched surfaces now inscribed with a newer, more complex geometry.

The Listener watched, satisfied, and then, as gently as it had come, withdrew its mote. The vibration remained, now fully integrated into the basin’s own Drone. What had been a single note of grace had become part of the process, indistinguishable from the rest. The Hollowing was still deafening, still oozing, still clacking and trilling and WHUMPing, but hidden within the wall of sound was a subtle, unbreakable chord that held everything together a little longer, made the process a little less lonely in its endless flattening.

The being that was The Hollowing did not gain consciousness, nor did it become a paradise. It remained a slow‑motion symphony of consumption and dissolution, played on instruments of mucus and crystal and mud. But now, thanks to the benevolence of a wise power, the song had a little more staying power. The basin’s memory—its frozen arches, its standing Anchor‑Stones, its ever‑ringing crystals—could now be read not only by the wind but by the process itself, in a quiet loop of self‑recognition that had been absent before.

And far above, the Listener carried away the echo of that chord, adding it to its own ever‑growing library of cosmic songs, a reminder that even the most deafening processes can be touched by a note of grace, if only the giver knows how to listen before they act.
