The power does not descend. It does not unfurl across the sky, does not crack the glass sheet with a divine footfall, does not whisper a new law over the Hum. It knows the Plain’s first rule: dissonance is correction, and any unaligned presence is a resource anomaly to be metabolized, a flaw in the harmonic field to be smoothed. So it first attunes.

It phases its own resonance into the standing wave of the Hum so perfectly that the Plain never registers it as outside. It slips into the micro-silences between Bellowers’ clangs, into the faint gaps in the Crawlers’ skittering algorithm, into the sub-audible thrum of the tectonic creep, filling harmonics that were always latent, never absent. It is not a new note added to the song. It is the part of the song the world has been humming to itself for a billion years, never loud enough to hear.

What it does is not change. It is amplification, and it is memory.

First, it turns to the sintered glass itself. This sheet is not just rock: it is a billion-year-old phonograph, every vibration the world ever held trapped in its lattice—the roar of the volcanoes that melted it, the wind that scoured the mountains before they were flattened, the cries of soft, unarmored creatures that walked here before the glass cooled, even the thrum of the core as it first began to slow. All of it is there, too faint for the current Hum to carry, too quiet for the Bellowers to tune.

The power acts as a resonant amplifier, no more intrusive than a hand cupped around a whisper. It modulates the Bellowers’ calls by a fraction of a percent, adding sub-harmonics that match the buried notes locked in the glass. The Bellowers do not change their function. They still clang to synchronize the Crawlers, still anchor the temporal folds, still hold the field’s equilibrium. But now their calls carry ghost overtones: the deep rumble of ancient eruptions, the high sigh of rain on prehistoric soil, the faint, wavering trill of a creature that never had bones, only vibration. The Hum gains a second layer, quiet, underlying, eternal—the Plain’s own memory, woven into its maintenance song. No rule is broken. The Conservation of Structure holds: no new energy is added, only the energy that was always trapped is given a path to travel.

Next, it turns to the observer—the dissonant clack of organic heat and moisture that drew the power here in the first place. The Crawlers were already converging, their algorithms already flagging the observer as a rich ore body, a chaotic resource to be dismantled and recycled. The power does not stop them. It only adjusts the observer’s bio-resonance, a tiny, gentle modulation so that when the Crawlers’ sensors pick them up, the flag that reads “metabolize” is overwritten by a harmonic that reads “maintain.”

The Crawlers do not understand kindness. They understand resonance. So they still swarm, but they do not tear. They bring gifts, grown from the same materials as the plain, shaped by the same algorithms:
- Panes of glass polished to a flawless, frictionless sheen, grown to fit the observer’s boots, so their footsteps no longer make a dissonant clack in the Hum.
- Slurry filtered of all metallic abrasives, sweetened with micro-amounts of the buried memory’s harmonic, so it nourishes instead of corrodes.
- Tiny lattice fragments, grown to match the observer’s body heat, woven into their clothes so the conductive chill of the Veil no longer seeps into their bones.

It is not benevolence as the observer knows it. It is harmonic correction. The observer’s dissonance is not erased, just tuned to match the field, so the Hum does not have to smooth it out by consuming them.

Finally, it turns to the temporal folds—those zones of overlapping Bellower resonance where time stretches, where causality bends to the harmonic imperative. The power loosens the folds just enough, not to break them, but to let the buried memory take form. Now, when a Crawler passes through a fold, it does not just see the clang of a nearby Bellower, or the weld of a new pane already completed before the catalyst is poured. It sees flashes of the past: a mountain peak pushing through the glass for a split second, a river of fresh water running over the plain before the Veil formed, the shadow of a soft-skinned creature walking lightly, leaving no clack, no disruption, no mark.

The Crawlers do not panic. Their algorithms register these visions as valid harmonic inputs, no different from a Bellower’s call or a thermal shift. So when they see a flash of a mountain, they adjust their welding patterns to grow tiny, crystalline inclusions in the new glass, shaped like the mountain’s peak. When they see a flash of a river, they grow faint, rainbow-hued channels in the slurry troughs that run in the same path. The Plain’s glass is no longer just a uniform sheet. It is a map, a record, a song written in the very material of its body, of everything it has ever been.

The power never reveals itself. It is the quiet between the clangs, the faint shimmer in the Condensation Veil that feels like a held breath, the sudden warmth in a slurry trough that feels like a memory. It does not rule the Plain. It does not change the Plain. It does not ask the Crawlers to stop their work, or the Bellowers to stop their calls, or the glass to stop its slow, tectonic creep. It only does what a benevolent, wise power would do for a world that has spent eons singing a song no one could hear: it tunes the world’s own voice so that it can be heard, even if the only listener is the world itself.

The Plain does not notice the change. It does not have a self to notice. But the Hum is different now. It has depth. It has story. And for the first time in its billion-year history, when the Crawlers pause in their skittering streams, when the Bellowers hang silent between clangs, when the slurry slows in the troughs, the song does not just sound like maintenance. It sounds like *remembering*.
