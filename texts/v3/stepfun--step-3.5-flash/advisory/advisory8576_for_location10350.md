### Pre-Departure Prep for the Ordinary Multiverse Traveler
This guide assumes you’re a standard interdimensional traveler with basic hop-pad access, generic ITG (Interdimensional Traveler’s Guild) insurance, and zero specialized survival training for reality-broken zones. The Spire-Mire is classified as a Class 4 Reality Variance Zone, so standard multiverse-hop prep won’t cut it here: the biggest risks aren’t just the cold or the rock, but the slow, uncanny breakdown of the rules you’re used to relying on.

First, file a mandatory trip plan with the ITG local office at the Spire-Mire’s only stable extraction point on the basin’s western rim. Give them your route, expected return time, and a list of your gear. Check in via your shielded comms every 12 hours—time silt in the basin can make you lose track of hours or even days without noticing, and unplanned reality shifts can strand you for weeks if no one knows where to look. Also spring for the extra reality variance insurance rider: getting pulled into a Bloom Zone or trapped in a time silt pocket can strand you in a timeline where you never existed, and the payout is the only thing that’ll get you home if your hop-pad glitches.

Get a pre-trip medical checkup to confirm your respiratory system is in good shape: the thin, metallic, damp air will aggravate asthma, COPD, or even mild seasonal allergies, and altitude sickness is a real risk if you’re hopping in from a sea-level multiverse. Pick up a tetanus shot too: the sharp scree, rusted industrial debris, and mineral-coated thorns here are basically infection delivery systems, and the local contaminants make even small cuts far more dangerous than you’re used to.

Book a local guide in advance through the ITG contact. The Spire-Mire has no fixed streets, and landmarks shift slowly enough that you won’t notice a change until you’re already lost. A local will know which Echo-Matter (ghost-tracks) to ignore, where the safe geothermal warming spots are, and which slopes are about to start a slow landslide.

### Mandatory Gear Checklist
Leave your fancy fragile tech, open-toed sandals, and souvenir-hunting impulses at home. Pack only what’s listed below, double-up on critical items, and keep all electronics and meds in an inside pocket close to your body to avoid cold drain.
1. **Respiratory & Protection**
   - Dual-filter, acid-resistant respirator: Regular cloth masks won’t cut it here. The air is laced with metallic snowmelt particulates, acidic runoff fumes, and fine rock dust from constant erosion, and prolonged exposure will irritate your lungs and throat.
   - 2 portable emergency O2 canisters: The basin’s elevation is high enough that even acclimated travelers will get winded quickly in the thin air, and mist can make oxygen levels drop suddenly near low-lying zones.
   - Hooded, waterproof, acid-resistant outerwear: The constant mist is cold enough to cause hypothermia in 2 hours even if it doesn’t feel that cold, and the acidic runoff will eat through standard rain gear in minutes.
   - 2 sets of moisture-wicking base layers
