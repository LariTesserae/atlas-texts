# Preparation Guide: Visiting the Seep District

## **Core Mindset Shift**
You are not entering a cave. You are entering a **geological wound that has not yet scarred over**. This is a place where the world is actively deciding what it will become. Your Earth-based instincts about "underground" (cold, still, dark, dead) will be violently wrong. It is **hot, dynamic, dim, and pulsing with life and industry**. Your preparation must account for **scale, persistent moisture, chemical toxicity, and industrial hazards** far more than simple darkness or cold.

---

## **Phase 1: Physical & Medical Preparation**

### **Thermal Regulation & Hydration**
- **Heat Acclimatization:** The ambient temperature is **2.9/3 on a baking scale**—likely 50-60°C (122-140°F) near vents, cooler but still oppressive in shadows. You will sweat profusely, but evaporation will be minimal due to humidity. This leads to rapid electrolyte loss and heat stroke risk.
    - **Pre-Trip:** undergo a 10-day heat acclimation protocol (sauna/steam exposure with exertion).
    - **Gear:** Full-body **vapor-barrier suit** (like a hazardous materials suit) with an **integrated, closed-cycle cooling system**. A simple cooling vest will be insufficient; you need a system that can handle high humidity and conductive heat from surfaces. Consider a **phase-change material (PCM) cooling layer** under the suit, plus an external power pack for active recirculation.
- **Hydration Strategy:** You will lose liters per hour. Carry a **minimum 6L of potable water** in rigid, insulated containers (hydration bladders will heat the water to undrinkable levels quickly). Supplement with a **portable atmospheric water generator** (if you can carry a power source for it), but be warned: the seep water is chemically loaded.
- **Electrolytes:** Pack **oral rehydration salts (ORS)** and electrolyte tabs in excess. Consume them prophylactically, not just when thirsty.

### **Respiratory Protection**
- The air is **thick, wet, and sulfurous** with potential geothermal gases (H₂S, CO₂, methane, radon). Chemiluminescent molds may release spores.
    - **Primary Gear:** A **full-face respirator with multi-gas cartridges** (rated for acidic gases and particulates) or a **Powered Air-Purifying Respirator (PAPR)** with a chemical cartridge and a high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filter. A simple N95 mask is useless.
    - **Backup:** Carry a **short-duration escape respirator** (e.g., an oxygen candle or chemical oxygen generator mask) in case of a sudden gas pocket or vent eruption.
- **Lung Conditioning:** Pre-trip, use a **resistive breathing trainer** to build respiratory stamina against thick air.

### **Toxin & Contaminant Avoidance**
- **Seep Water:** Never drink, let water drip into your mouth, or allow skin contact without barrier. It carries **dissolved heavy metals (arsenic, mercury, lead from mineral deposits), sulfuric acid (from sulfide oxidation), and thermophilic bacteria/viruses** unknown to surface immunology.
    - **Skin Protection:** Your suit must be **completely sealed** at all joints. No exposed skin. Gloves and booties must be integrated.
    - **Decontamination:** Carry a **portable decontamination kit**: strong base (like sodium bicarbonate solution) for neutralizing acid, followed by copious sterile water wipes. Assume everything you touch is contaminated.
- **Mold & Crusts:** Chemiluminescent molds and crystalline silicate crusts may be abrasive or toxic if inhaled as dust.
    - **Action:** Do not touch walls or crusts. Use tools (tongs, probes) for any sampling. If you must breach a crust, wear a **dust mask/respirator and eye protection**.

## **Phase 2: Gear & Equipment**

### **Navigation & Orientation**
- **No GPS.** Rock masses and thick stone ceilings will block satellite signals. You are in a **continent-sized shelf** with no clear landmarks.
    - **Primary:** A **laser rangefinder/compass** (non-magnetic; stone may have high iron content affecting magnetic compasses) and a **digital terrain mapper** (handheld LiDAR scanner) to build a 3D map as you go.
    - **Secondary:** **Acoustic depth sounder** to measure ceiling height and detect large cavities or water bodies ahead.
    - **Redundancy:** **Marking kit**—non-fading, heat-resistant spray paint or ultraviolet-reactive tags for trail marking in a place where paint might melt or be washed away by seepage.
    - **Critical:** A **seismic activity monitor** (handheld geophone) to detect approaching tunnel-worms or settling rock. A low-frequency hum may precede ground movement.

### **Lighting**
- The ambient light is **dim (1.0/3)**, with stark contrasts. Your own light must function in **high humidity, steam, and sulfur-laden air** (which can corrode electronics).
    - **Headlamp:** **High-lumen (1000+), fully waterproof, with a long battery life.** LED is best for heat resistance. Carry **three spare, sealed battery packs** (keep in waterproof pouch).
    - **Secondary:** A **portable chemiluminescent light stick** (green or blue, lasts 12 hours) for emergency backup that requires no battery and works in wet conditions.
    - **Never rely on the district's arc-lamps or mold-glow** as primary navigation—they create deceptive shadows and can fail without warning.

### **Tools & Utility**
- **Multi-tool with carbide steel bits** (for scraping mineral deposits or prying).
- **Portable gas detector** (multi-gas) that gives audible/visual alarms for H₂S, CO, O₂ deficiency.
- **Collapsible water sampling pole** with sterile bottles.
- **Heavy-duty cable ties, ratchet straps, and a small bolt-cutter** for emergency improvisations with the ramshackle infrastructure.
- **Signal devices:** A **high-decibel whistle** (sound travels differently in humid, enclosed spaces) and a **mirror for flashing light** toward any arc-lamp.

### **Clothing & Suit**
- **Primary:** A **commercial hazmat suit (Level A or B)** with a **integrated glove and boot system** (no gaps). Must be resistant to acids, oils, and punctures.
- **Underlayer:** **Moisture-wicking, antimicrobial base layer** (synthetic, no cotton) to manage sweat under the suit.
- **Footwear:** **Steel-toe, oil-resistant boots** with **high-grip, non-marking soles** for slick stone and iron walkways.
- **No soft materials** that can absorb contaminated water and hold it against your skin.

## **Phase 3: Biological & Ecological Protocols**

### **Fauna Encounters**
- **Blind crustaceans:** Generally non-aggressive but will swarm if disturbed in puddles. They can trip you. **Avoid puddles entirely** unless crossing a necessary chasm on a bridge. Use a probe to test stability.
- **Gliding membranous things:** Attracted to lights. Will not attack but may collide with you, causing loss of balance. **Keep your headlamp beam low and angled down** to avoid dazzling them.
- **Metallic fly swarms:** Will **converge on any exposed skin or light**. They bite (if they have mouthparts) and can cause allergic reactions. Your sealed suit is your primary defense. If a swarm enters your breathing zone ( respirator filter can clog), **find a steam vent or enclosed space to out-wait them**—they are drawn to industrial lights, not human heat alone.
- **Six-limbed heat-sensing lizards:** **Predators.** They watch from shadows. They may see your body heat through your suit if it's thin. **Move slowly and deliberately** in shadowed areas. Do not turn your back on a crevice. Carry a **strong, broad-spectrum repellent spray** (like predator urine mimic) as a last resort—test its efficacy in this atmosphere first.
- **Tunnel-worms:** You will feel them as **seismic tremors**. If the ground vibrates rhythmically, **freeze and listen**. They are not hunting you, but their movement can cause collapses or open fissures. **Do not stand on loose scree or near cliff edges.**

### **Flora & Mineral Hazards**
- **Crystalline crusts:** **Do not step on them.** They are fragile and can shatter, causing falls. If you must traverse a crusted area, use a **board or plank** to distribute weight.
- **Weeping rock ceiling:** **Never stand directly under a wet patch** for prolonged periods. The water is acidic and warm. A sudden **drip-eruption** (a larger pore opening) can scald you. Scan the ceiling frequently with your headlamp.

## **Phase 4: Social & Logistical Preparation**

### **Language & Communication**
- The district has its own pidgin or slang. Key concepts to learn before arrival (via cross-dimensional linguistic download or local contact):
    - Words for **vent types** (safe vs. eruptive), **water quality** (mineral-rich vs. acidic), **fauna threats** (by sound/description), **structural integrity** of shanties ("solid," "breathing," "soon-gone").
    - **Gestures:** Pointing at feet (caution: thin crust), waving hand over head (ceiling drip), tapping ear (listen for worms), clutching throat (bad air).
- **Carry a waterproof phrasebook** with pictograms for: "gas danger," "collapse risk," "where is clean water?", "help," "I am lost."

### **Trade & Currency**
- If you need to interact with locals, **trade goods are industrial consumables**: **batteries, fuel filters, lubricants, antibiotics, non-corroding wires, light bulbs.**
- **Do not offer food or water from your supplies**—they may be considered useless or even offensive (they have their own water sources, no matter how toxic).
- **Respect their hierarchy:** The district likely runs on **tap-rights, pump licenses, and miner claims**. Do not threaten infrastructure.

### **Timing & Duration**
- **Visit only during the "slow dynamic" period** (1.3/3). Avoid times of **high faunal activity** (swarm migrations, worm movement seasons). Get a local calendar.
- **Maximum stay:** 48 hours. Your suit's cooling system will degrade, and psychological tolerance to the enclosed, loud, hot environment will plummet.
- **Exit Plan:** **Always know the location of the nearest vertical ascent shaft** (a man-made or natural chimney leading upward). These may be guarded or unstable. **Have a pre-arranged rescue signal** with your interdimensional anchor point.

## **Phase 5: Psychological Fortification**

- **Sensory Overload:** The **constant drip, hum, skitter, and hiss** will be maddening. Bring **earplugs** for temporary relief, but you must still hear seismic warnings. Practice **meditative focus** on a single sound (e.g., your own breathing) to avoid fragmentation.
- **Claustrophilia vs. Agoraphobia:** The scale is paradoxically **vast yet ceiling-low**. You may feel both crushed by the stone above and lost in the immensity. **Fix your gaze on near objects** (the next step, the next bridge) to manage this.
- **Alien Normalcy:** The locals see this as **normal (0.4/3 weird)**. Do not display horror at their way of life. Your reaction will be seen as weakness or madness.
- **Death Perception:** The place is **born, not dying**. You may feel life is cheap here, but it is actually **tenaciously, industriously prolific**. This is not a tomb—it is a **womb of stone**. Adjust your existential framework accordingly.

## **Final Checklist Before Step-Through**

1.  **Suit Integrity:** Full pressure test. All seals greased. Cooling system at 100%.
2.  **Air Supply:** Respirator cartridges fresh. Escape mask accessible.
3.  **Water:** 6L potable + decon kit. **Never drink local water.**
4.  **Power:** All batteries fully charged. Spare power packs for lights and cooling.
5.  **Navigation:** Maps (pre-loaded and blank), rangefinder, seismic monitor, marking tool.
6.  **Signals:** Whistle, mirror, distress flare (non-fire, chemical glow).
7.  **Medicine:** Broad-spectrum antibiotics, anti-diarrheals, burn cream, electrolyte solutions, anti-itch for insect bites.
8.  **Tools:** Multi-tool, cable ties, gas detector, probe.
9.  **Mindset:** You are a guest in a **geological event**. Your technology is **primitive** here. Respect the heat, the water, the darkness, the industry, and the blind life that thrives on it.

**Remember:** The Seep District is not hostile—it is **indifferent**. It is not waiting for you to die. It is simply **being itself**: hot, wet, loud, and teeming with a life that drinks stone and breathes steam. If you prepare with humility, precision, and a willingness to move like a shadow in a world of weeping rock, you may leave with your skin intact and your mind rearranged forever.
