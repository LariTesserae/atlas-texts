Standard pre-trip prep for the Mantle-Still is less about survival against “dangers” and more about not accidentally breaking a billion-year-old perfect stasis that will collapse the entire planet’s ocean system if you sneeze wrong. This place doesn’t want to kill you—it just doesn’t know you exist, and your presence is the first variable it has encountered in eons. Almost all your prep will be focused on minimizing that variable, and not panicking when your brain insists you’re drowning for no reason.
### Pre-Trip Briefing & Planning (Non-Negotiable)
First, you’re not just packing a bag—you’re studying a frozen equation. Obtain a full, passively scanned map of the upper Mantle-Still strata before you go: mark every near-vacuum gap (the 0.1/3 empty voids between objects that will decompress you in half a second if you drift into them), every pressure gradient “elevation” band (you navigate by pressure, not up/down, since there is no gravity here), every drift path of the metallic grit ribbons and silica gear schools, and every location of active industrial infrastructure. Do not touch any of these objects during your survey pass—use only long-range passive sensors.
Second, memorize the system’s baseline thrum frequency: the subsonic pulse of the core pumps is constant, so any deviation in vibration (a cracking continental report, a groaning mineral shift, or the pressure wave of you moving) will be detectable to anyone paying attention, including the fluid itself. You will also undergo 40 hours of sensory deprivation training with constant low-level subsonic vibration to de-sensitize you to the thrum, so it doesn’t make you nauseous or disoriented mid-trip.
