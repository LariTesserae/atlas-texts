# Prep Guide for Visiting the Vellum Vale, from a Multiverse Hopper Who’s Skinned Their Shin on Its Ridges More Times Than They Can Count
This place doesn’t want to hurt you, but it will absolutely rearrange your schedule, your gear, and your understanding of “normal” if you don’t show up prepared. It’s not a hostile dimension, but it’s a *living, thinking, shifting* one—part geology, part fungal forest, part emergent smart-city that grew out of the landscape instead of being plopped on top of it. The rules are loose, the physics are suggestions, and the vale is always, always watching.
---
## Pre-Departure Prep (Don’t Skip This)
First off, tell someone back home exactly when you’re crossing in, and set a hard 4-hour return timer. Time dilation near the Silicate Spires is sneaky: you’ll think you’ve only been wandering for 45 minutes, and you’ll come back to find three days have passed. ### Gear Tailored to the Vale’s Weirdness
Leave all synthetic, non-compostable junk at home if you can. The vale’s symbiotic urban-ecosystem flags hard plastics, lithium batteries, and chemical preservatives as invasive, and the scuttler maintenance drones will disassemble whatever you leave behind to study it (including your favorite backup comms, if you’re dumb enough to drop it).
1. **Footwear**: Closed-toe, puncture-proof, waterproof boots with magnetic or quick-release laces (gravity shifts near spires will yank regular laces right off your feet, and floating shoelaces will get caught in Conduit Vines and trigger a scuttler swarm). Add a thin layer of non-conductive rubber between the insole and your sock—Conduit Vines carry high-voltage data/energy pulses, and you do *not* want to get zapped stepping on one by accident. Break the boots in before you go; the Mycelial Grids are springy and warm, but if you’re limping, they’ll adjust your path to avoid you, and you’ll end up rerouted straight into a patch of fresh obsidian shards.
2. **Clothing**: Skip the bulky environment suit. The mist is warm year-round, and the vale will interpret thick, non-breathable synthetic fabric as a threat. Go for a base layer with a built-in universal particulate filter (to catch silicate mist droplets and fungal spores—ozone from the spires irritates airways fast), a mid-layer of breathable natural wool, and an outer layer of muted silver-grey linen or woven fungal silk (blends in with the silver-blue grass, doesn’t startle Glimmer Stags, and breaks down naturally if you lose it). Skip bright colors, loud patterns, or anything that glitters—the Glimmer Stags’ antlers are light-capturing filaments, and they’ll mistake your neon jacket for a rival and charge.
3. **Eyewear**: Polarized, UV-blocking goggles with a secondary air filter for your eyes. The permanent haze diffuses light into a directionless glare that will leave you with a headache in 20 minutes flat, and the bioluminescent moss glows bright enough up close to give you temporary retinal burns if you stare at it too long.
4. **Power & Tech**: Ditch lithium batteries—their charge will drain twice as fast near spires, and ambient energy pulses from Conduit Vines can make them overheat and explode. Bring solid-state or bio-compatible power cells instead; you can recharge the bio ones by feeding them small bits of the silver-blue grass (the vale recognizes local organic matter as non-threatening, unlike your phone charger). Shield all your comms, chronometers, and multiverse tracers against temporal distortion and EM interference before you go—standard off-the-shelf models will glitch out the second you step within a kilometer of a spire. Bring a low-tech backup signal: a whistle carved from ceramic or bone (no metal, no plastic). It won’t interfere with the vale’s frequencies, and the vale’s intelligence recognizes three short-three long-three short blasts as a distress call.
5. **Medical Kit**: Skip the harsh chemical antiseptics—if you get a cut, they’ll irritate the local fungal ecosystem and draw Scuttlers. Bring fast-seal bio-gel for obsidian cuts (the glass is sterile, but fungal spores love open wounds), broad-spectrum non-synthetic antifungal meds, non-drowsy antihistamines for the sweet-rot smell of the fungal groves, and a portable lung rinse kit to clear silicate particulates out of your airways. No synthetic preservatives in any of your meds, either—the vale’s Mycelial Grid will detect them in your sweat and reroute you away from populated groves to “quarantine” you.

## Landing & First Steps
Always land in the middle of a silver-blue meadow, at least 500 meters from any obsidian ridge or Silicate Spire. First thing out of your portal: wipe your boots clean on the grass (not the obsidian—shards stick to rubber, and tracking foreign dimensional dust into the Mycelial Grid will make it spasm and try to shake you off). Do a quick atmospheric scan: if spore counts are over 15% above baseline, put your mask on full seal before you take your helmet off.
For the first 10 minutes, don’t touch anything. The vale is already aware of you—you’ll feel a faint warm pulse in the soles of your feet, that’s the Mycelial Grid assessing you. Don’t fight the grid’s gentle push-pull under your feet: it’s routing you along the safest, least disruptive path. If you try to veer off toward a grove or a ridge on purpose, the ground will gently firm up under your toes to guide you back. Go with it; the grid knows where the hidden obsidian shards and unstable gravity pockets are.

## On-the-Ground Rules & Etiquette
This is the part most new travelers mess up: the vale isn’t a park you visit, it’s a living, thinking system that’s as much city as it is forest. Disrespect it, and it will make your trip very unpleasant. Respect it, and it might even show you things no other traveler has seen.
1. **Don’t take anything without trading first**. The vale tracks every blade of grass, every reed, every shard of obsidian. If you pocket a patch of glowing moss or a crystalline reed without leaving an offering (a bit of dried fruit, a small vial of water from your home dimension, even a shiny lens cap) the grid will thicken under your feet, the mist will turn so thick you can’t see your hand in front of your face, and gravity will double in weight until you drop whatever you took. If you really want a sample, place your offering on the ground first, wait 10 minutes, and if the grass under the offering rearranges into a fractal pattern, that’s permission. No pattern? Leave it be.
2. **Leave all gear secured**. Gravity near spires is mutable—I’ve seen a traveler’s entire backpack float 30 feet up into a Spire’s smart-matter filaments because they didn’t clip it to their harness. Zip every pocket, clip loose gear to your belt, and never set anything down on a Conduit Vine or a spire base. If you accidentally drop a device on a Vine, leave a piece of dried fruit next to it immediately: the Scuttlers will take the fruit, fix the Vine, and leave your device alone. If you don’t leave an offering, they’ll disassemble the device to study it, and you’ll be lucky to get the memory card back.
3. **Don’t mess with the fauna**. Glimmer Stags are curious, but don’t approach them. Their antlers are arrays of light-focusing filaments, and if they get spooked, they’ll accidentally focus the diffuse vale light into a beam hot enough to burn through a jacket. Whisper Moths are harmless, but don’t swat them—their wingbeats drop local temperature by 15 degrees, and if you hurt one, a swarm will descend and lower the temp so low you’ll get hypothermia in 10 minutes. Scuttlers are maintenance drones, not pests: if a wave of them is coming, just step aside and let them pass. Stepping on one will trigger a swarm that will try to push you off the path and into the nearest obsidian patch.
4. **Keep it quiet**. The vale’s soundscape is a layered, non-biological rhythm: the slow creak of growing Spires, the soft chime of reeds, the subterranean thrum of the Mycelial Grid, the hiss of mist on warm stone.
