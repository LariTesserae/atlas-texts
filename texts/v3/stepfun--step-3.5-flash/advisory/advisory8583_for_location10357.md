# Pre-Departure Protocol: The Moss-Mill (Weeping Cliffs Region)

## **I. Mindset & Philosophical Calibration**
You are not an explorer here. You are a **guest in a digestion**. The landscape is not "wild" or "ruined" — it is **post-geological**, a place where the concepts of "natural" and "artificial" have dissolved into a single, slow, green process. Your usual instincts to navigate, conquer, or even *observe objectively* will fail. You must prepare to:
- **Surrender your sense of linear time.** The temporal turbulence means your watch may tick backward, a Seep-Mound may flow uphill in your periphery, or a Heart-Engine may appear both rusted and pristine simultaneously. Do not fight this. **Log it, but do not trust it.** Carry a **Temporal Anchor** — a simple hourglass with iridescent sand from a stable universe. Its flow will be erratic, but its *changes* will be your only consistent meter.
- **Reframe "sound."** The world is loud with geological tinnitus and biological grinding. This is not pollution — it is the **soundtrack of stability**. Silence would be a catastrophe. Your ears are not for *listening to* but for *reading*. Train before arrival: spend hours in a ship's engine room or near a glacier's basal melt, focusing on feeling vibrations in your molars and sternum. Learn to distinguish the **Myco-Phyte thrum** (low, 10-15 Hz, felt in your guts) from the **Rock-Sucker grind** (higher, 50-80 Hz, felt in your teeth) and the **Skitter-Mite chorus** (dry, 2-4 kHz, felt in your sinuses).
- **Abandon the explorer's gaze.** You will not see " vistas." You will see **gradients**. The sparse appearance from a distance is a lie. Your visual system must be retrained to **kneel**. Carry a **Macro-Lens Visor** (a simple tripod-mounted magnifier with LED ring) to perceive the infinite root-tapestry, the pulsing chlorophyll-green, the tiny Skitter-Mite swarms. What seems like empty stone at 50 meters is a city at 5 cm.

## **II. Physical Gear & Material Prep**

### **A. Clothing & Layering (The "Wet-Stead" Principle)**
- **Base Layer:** A **hydrophilic membrane** (not waterproof) that *wicks and holds* moisture against the skin without chilling. Think advanced synthetic wool, treated with a mild surfactant to prevent the clammy chill from leaching heat. Ordinary wool or cotton will become a hypothermia vector.
- **Insulation:** **Aerogel-infused fibrous padding** (thin, flexible). The cold is 0.5°C, but the damp is relentless. The geothermal hum prevents freezing, but conductive heat loss through dampness is severe. Multiple thin layers beat one thick one.
- **Outer Shell:** A **porous, brushed-nylon "breathable" shell** that *manages* mist penetration rather than blocks it. It should allow fine mist to pass through slowly, preventing a vapor barrier that would soak you from the inside. Seams must be **welded**, not stitched.
- **Extremities:** **Glove liners** with conductive fingertips (to operate touchscreens if needed) under **rubber-dipped knit gloves**. Boots must be **size up** to accommodate thick socks and allow for swelling feet. **High-cut, lace-to-calf** to prevent root-tangle ingress. Soles: **lugs with self-cleaning grooves** — the root-tapestry will clog smooth soles instantly.
- **Head & Neck:** A **hood integrated into the shell** with a **peak** to divert mist from your face. Underneath, a **neck gaiter** of the hydrophilic base layer. **Goggles** with **anti-fog hydrophilic coating** — standard anti-fog will fail in 100% humidity. **Side-vents** are non-negotiable for equalization.

### **B. Respiratory & Hydration**
- **You will not find liquid water sources.** The only water is atmospheric mist and Seep-Mound exhalations (alkaline, mineral-sweet).
- **Primary Hydration:** A **mask with a passive condensation trap**. A **hydrophilic foam insert** in a mask that covers nose and mouth. As you exhale, moisture condenses in the foam. Periodically, wring it out into a clean tube and suck. This recycles your breath's humidity. Do not rely on this for all fluids — it's a supplement.
- **Direct Drinking:** A **wide-mouthed, insulated titanium cup**. Cup the mist under a Seep-Mound's exhalation. The mist will coalesce into droplets on the cold cup surface. Drink slowly — the water is mineral-heavy and may cause GI upset if consumed rapidly. **Filter** only if you see visible particulate (from disturbed root-tendrils).
- **Air Quality:** The air is breathable but saturated. No mask for air filtration needed. However, the **Myco-Phyte's spores** are everywhere. If you have respiratory sensitivities, a **simple N95 mask** worn under your hood will filter spores without impeding breathing. It will become damp quickly — change it every 2 hours.

### **C. Mobility & Terrain Interaction**
- **Gait:** You will not walk *on* the root-tapestry; you will **part** it. The hair-thin tendrils are strong but flexible. A **wide, flat footbed** on your boots (like a snowshoe's principle) distributes weight and prevents slicing deep into the network, which would cause pain-reactive tendril retraction and instability.
- **Poles:** **Trekking poles with wide, slotted baskets** (like for deep snow). Use them to test root-tangle depth ahead and to help lift legs. The ground is a living sponge — it will resist and then give.
- **Kneeling/Lying:** Carry a **closed-cell foam sit-pad** (2 cm thick). The ground is cold and damp. Direct contact will chill you rapidly. The pad insulates and prevents you from crushing sensitive root-nodes, which release a faint, distress pheromone (smell: wet copper).

### **D. Light & Vision**
- **Primary Light:** **Red-lensed headlamps with ultra-low intensity (1-5 lumens)**. The Glimmercaps are on a slow circadian rhythm. Bright white light will shock and possibly disrupt their pulse cycles, causing local root-network distress. Red light is minimally invasive.
- **Secondary Light:** **Bioluminescent " mimics "** — small, soft-glowing blue-green orbs (mag-light style) that *approximate* the Glimmercap pulse (syncopated 30-second on, 90-second off). These are for navigation without overwhelming the local bioluminescence. Do not use steady lights.
- **No flash photography.** The sudden bright pulse could induce a network-wide shock response, temporarily halting nutrient flows.

### **E. Soundscaping & Communication**
- **Personal Sound:** **Custom-molded earplugs with a 15-20 dB reduction across all frequencies**, but with a **low-pass filter** (cutting above 200 Hz). This will:
    1. Dampen the painful Rock-Sucker molar-grind.
    2. Preserve the infrasonic Myco-Phyte thrum (felt more than heard, but the filter helps the inner ear's bone conduction).
    3. Make the Skitter-Mite dry *shhh* bearable.
- **Comms:** **Bone-conduction headphones** paired with a **text-only input device** (like a roll-up keyboard). Voice comms are useless — your voice will be swallowed by mist and you'll strain your throat shouting against the ambient hum. Text is silent. Have a **localized "chat log"** that timestamps entries. The time-turbulence means you cannot synchronize clocks — your messages will have temporal offsets.

## **III. Ecological & Temporal Navigation Tools**

### **A. The "Reading the Gut" Kit**
1.  **Root-Tap Probe:** A **thin, flexible, temperature-sensitive wire** (like a medical thermometer probe) with a soft silicone tip. Gently insert it 2-3 cm into the root-tapestry. Its color-change strip indicates:
    - **Stable (Green):** Current nutrient flow is normal.
    - **Turbulent (Blue/Purple):** Local time-slip or root-distress. Proceed with extreme caution; the ground may "flow" or become unstable.
    - **Digestive (Yellow/Orange):** You are over a Heart-Engine being actively assimilated. The root-network here is incorporating new metals. Brief exposure is safe; prolonged may cause metallic scent hallucinations.
2.  **Mist-Density Cloth:** A **black velvet patch** on a stick. In the mist, its wetness pattern reveals airflow. A smooth, even wetting = stable mist. **Streaks or drips = Mist-Wyer passage within last 10 minutes.** They are harmless but their wake disturbs the mist's density, creating temporary blinding curtains.
3.  **Seep-Mound Flow Monitor:** A **small, weighted disk** with a central hole. Place it on a Seep-Mound. The disk's rotation speed over 1 hour indicates the mound's flow rate. A sudden acceleration = local time-thickening; deceleration = time-thinning. Use to predict if a path will "flow" under you.

### **B. Industrial Archaeology Safeguards**
- **Heart-Engine Interaction:** **Never touch** any Heart-Engine component with bare skin. The metals are leeching. Wear **nitrile gloves** under your work gloves. Do not attempt to move, clean, or "restore" anything. You are a visitor, not a curator.
- **Material Sampling:** If you must take a sample (for science), use a **ceramic blade** to scrape a **microscopic amount** of root-tapestry or Glimmercap. Metal tools will be "tasted" by the network and may trigger localized defensive chemistry (a sour smell, temporary root-thickening).
- **Acoustic Awareness:** The Rock-Sucker grind is **sub-audible**. Carry a **portable seismic meter** (a simple app on a ruggedized phone with a geophone attachment). Its readout will show the 50-80 Hz band. A sudden spike = you are near a concentrated Rock-Sucker patch. They are harmless but their collective rasping can vibrate loose small stones from overhangs.

## **IV. Psychological & Sensory Endurance Training (Pre-Trip)**

### **A. Simulated Environments (48 Hours Minimum)**
- **Visual:** Watch loops of **gradient-heavy footage** (foggy moors, slow-motion ink in water, abstract green textures) on a screen with **reduced contrast and saturation**. Practice identifying macro-details in the blur.
- **Auditory:** Listen to a **continuous soundscape mix** of:
    - 12 Hz sine wave (felt in chest)
    - Fine gravel being slowly dragged over stone (50-80 Hz)
    - Dry chia seeds on parchment (4 kHz)
    - Distant, slow heartbeats (0.5-1 Hz)
    - Underlay this with **pink noise** at 40 dB. Do this while in a **cold, damp room** (15°C, >80% humidity).
- **Tactile:** Wear **damp socks** for 4 hours. Handle **sponges**, **moss**, and **cold, smooth stones**. Practice walking on a **trampoline covered with thick, wet rope** to simulate the root-tapestry's give.
- **Temporal:** Carry your Temporal Anchor. Every 30 minutes, note its sand flow. Erratic changes are normal. Practice not panicking when it runs *backward* for a minute.

### **B. Mental Anchors**
- **Your "I" is separate from the world's "is."** The Moss-Mill has no concept of individual. You are a temporary disturbance in its continuum. Your thoughts, your memories, your name — these are your anchors against dissolution into the green hum.
- **Adopt a ritual.** Every hour, touch a **smooth, dry stone from your home universe** in your pocket. Its *dryness* is alien here. Its *solidity* is a reminder of a world where erosion is not a philosophy.
- **Journal in fragments.** Write single words or phrases. Full sentences will feel False. The world thinks in pulses and gradients.

## **V. Emergency & Contingency**

### **A. What Could Go Wrong**
1.  **Root-Lock:** A root-tendril (unlikely but possible) might constrict an ankle if you move violently. **Do not pull.** Use your **Root-Tap Probe** to gently stimulate the node behind the constriction. It will release.
2.  **Temporal Dislocation:** You might experience a 5-minute "gap" where your perception skips. Your comms log will have a timestamp gap. **Do not search for the missing time.** It is *your* time, now elsewhere. Continue. Your Anchor will realign you eventually.
3.  **Industrial Poisoning:** Touching a Heart-Engine barehanded may cause a metallic taste, tingling, and mild nausea. **Rinse with mineral water from your cup, induce vomiting if severe, and evacuate.** The toxins are slow-leeching; a day's exposure is usually non-fatal.
4.  **Psychic Bleed:** The peace-aliveness paradox may induce a catatonic desire to *stop*, to let the roots take you. This is the world's gentle pull. **Your ritual stone, your text comms, your sound-dampened awareness of your own breath — these are your resistance.**

### **B. Exit Strategy**
You do not "fly away." You **condense out**. Find a **large, stable Seep-Mound** (monitor with Flow Monitor for 1-hour stability). Sit on your pad. Focus on the **dryness** of your home-universe stone. Breathe the damp air deliberately. Your personal "resolution" must overcome the world's "dissolution." After 10 minutes of focused meditation on a memory of *arid* wind, your form will begin to **phase**. The roots will lose their grip. You will not be thrown — you will simply *become less present*. The last sensation is the **Myco-Phyte thrum** fading from your bones.
