# Preparation Protocol: Visiting the Plateau of Silent Thunder

## **1. Physical & Sensory Conditioning (Weeks/Months Before Transit)**

**Auditory Desensitization & Bone-Conduction Training:**
*   **Acclimatization to Infrasound:** Begin with daily exposure to low-frequency sound generators (sub-20Hz) at gradually increasing volumes. The goal is not to "get used to" it, but to train your autonomic nervous system to recognize the Base Drone as a non-immediate threat, preventing constant panic-induced hypertension. Use padded, weighted vests during exposure to mimic the physical pressure sensation.
*   **Bone Conduction Awareness:** Practice "hearing" through your jawbone and skull. Clamp your teeth on a metal rod and tap it lightly—learn to distinguish vibrations transmitted through bone versus air. This will be vital for detecting the Creak & Sigh or the approach of a Basin-Stalker in a pocket of relative quiet.
*   **High-Noise Environment Drills:** Spend hours in environments with layered, chaotic sound (e.g., inside industrial machinery plants, during severe hailstorms on a metal roof). Practice "acoustic tunneling"—focusing on a single small sound source (like a dripping faucet) while ignoring the wall of noise. This is your primary awareness skill.

**Respiratory & Cardiovascular Conditioning:**
*   **High-Altitude Simulation:** Train at elevation (5,000+ meters if possible) or use altitude tents. Your blood’s oxygen-carrying capacity must be maximized; a minor nosebleed from thin air could become a major issue when combined with sound-induced vascular stress.
*   **Breath-Hold & Rhythm Training:** The wind’s Scrape is directional and gusty. You will be forced to hold your breath during sudden, sharp gusts to avoid inhaling abrasive grit. Practice controlled, calm breath-holds of 30-45 seconds while under stress (e.g., during intense exercise).

**Psychological Preparation:**
*   **Sensory Deprivation/Overload Drills:** Alternate between 24-hour sessions in total sensory deprivation tanks (to confront the internal dread of infrasound without external cues) and sessions with overlapping, chaotic noise and visual static (to simulate the plateau’s overwhelming input).
*   **"Geological Time" Mindset:** Meditate on concepts of millennia and inch-per-year movement. Your human-scale concerns must be intentionally sidelined. Carry a small, smooth stone from your home; regularly hold it and remind yourself that in 10,000 years, it will be as changed as the plateau’s surface. This reduces anxiety about the slow, inevitable.

## **2. Gear & Apparel (The Layered Silence)**

**The Core Principle: You are building a personal acoustic and thermal cocoon. All gear must serve vibration dampening, thermal insulation, and abrasion resistance first; mobility second.**

**Base Layer (Skin Contact):**
*   **Material:** Static-dissipative, seamless merino wool or advanced synthetic blend (like polartec Alpha). Must be hypoallergenic—any skin irritation will be magnified by constant grit exposure.
*   **Function:** Wicks minimal sweat (you won’t be sweating much; activity must be slow), provides slight thermal buffering, and most critically, prevents direct skin contact with outer shell vibrations.

**Insulation & Vibration-Damping Layer:**
*   **Material:** A padded, flexible vest and leggings made from a **viscoelastic polymer composite** (think advanced memory foam infused with sound-absorbing granules). This is your primary defense against the Base Drone and Creak & Sigh. It must be thick enough to absorb sub-audible vibrations but flexible enough to allow slow, deliberate movement. Custom-molded to your body.

**Outer Shell (The Aerodynamic Exoshell):**
*   **Material:** Treated, rigidized canvas or laminated silk, layered with a thin, flexible sheet of **anodized titanium** (scavenged from low-tech sources if possible—think high-end camping gear or medical implants). The shell must be:
    1.  **Aerodynamic:** No loose flaps. Seams are external and taped. Profile is smooth and rounded.
    2.  **Abrasion-Resistant:** Can withstand hours of grit impact without perforating.
    3.  **Acoustically Reflective:** The hard outer layer should reflect as much of the Scrape’s high-frequency energy as possible. A faint, dusty patina of local violet grit on the shell will actually improve this.
*   **Helmet:** Full-face, with a **layered lens system**. Outer lens is thick, polished quartz (scratches easily but is replaceable). Inner lens is a flexible polycarbonate. The helmet is padded with the viscoelastic layer and has a **sealed respirator port**.

**Respiratory System (Critical):**
*   **Mask:** A **positive-pressure, full-face respirator** with dual filters. Primary filter: multi-layer for silica grit and alkali dust. Secondary filter: activated charcoal + specialized catalyst for methane and other hydrocarbon vapors. The system must supply slightly pressurized, clean air to prevent any draw of outside air during gusts.
*   **Hydration/Intake:** A **insulated, steel camelback** containing only **distilled water**. All other liquids (brine, melted breath-tower ice) are strictly for external use (e.g., cleaning lenses). A straw with a one-way valve allows sipping without removing the mask.

**Footwear & Mobility:**
*   **Boots:** Double-walled. Inner boot is a rigid, knee-high polymer shell (like a ski boot) lined with the damping polymer. Outer boot is a flexible, heavily treaded rubber over-shoe with a **smooth, curved sole** to prevent catching on ventifact grooves. Crampons are forbidden—they shatter the delicate surfaces and create dangerous points for sound to focus.
*   **Movement:** You will **not walk**. You will **glissade**. Use a long, polished **basalt staff** (found locally) to test ground stability ahead. Your gait is a slow, controlled slide/skip from one stable lee-side hollow to the next, always with the wind at your back or side when possible. Never face a direct gust.

**Sensory Augmentation (Low-Tech):**
*   **"Silk-Ears":** A pair of fine, oiled silk membranes stretched over lightweight frames. Hold them against your temples to *subtly* dampen certain high-frequency Scrape components through sympathetic vibration—a crude analog to the Basin-Stalker’s ear. Not for hearing, but for acoustic *filtering*.
*   **Laser Rangefinder (if available):** A simple, rugged handheld laser to measure distances to ventifact fins and basin rims. Visual estimation is impossible due to the lack of haze and scale distortion.
*   **Seismic Whistle:** A precisely tuned metal tube (like a大型dog whistle for infrasound). Blowing it produces a pure tone that can reveal hidden chambers or the proximity of large, settling rock slabs by the way the sound is altered or echoed back.

## **3. Behavioral Protocols & Survival Strategy**

**The Law of Acoustic Niche:**
You must find and occupy your own. The **Basin-Stalkers** own the pockets of relative quiet. The **Stone-Skitterers** own the resonant wave-gaps. The **Drift-Whales** own the moist, turbulent basins. **You own the transitional, wind-sheltered lee-sides of major ridges at dawn/dusk.** That is your zone. Do not enter a basin with temporary brine unless you see no Drift-Whale silhouettes. Do not attempt to "hunt" or approach any fauna. Your presence is a low-frequency intrusion they will detect as a predator or anomaly.

**The Rhythm of the Day:**
*   **Dawn:** The air is most still. The most dangerous time—the night’s breath-towers are unstable and may collapse. Use this time for navigation, moving from your previous night’s shelter to a new, wind-sheltered ledge for the day.
*   **Day:** The Scrape is at its peak. You are **inactive**. Conserve energy, perform gear checks, filter any gathered brine (if desperate), observe from your sheltered perch. Study the wind’s direction on the dust patterns. The "silent" creatures are moving in acoustic pockets you cannot perceive—watch for the Stone-Skitterers’ sudden, teleportative leaps; they are your best indicator of a usable pocket of air.
*   **Dusk:** The wind often shifts and moderates. The breath-towers stabilize. This is your primary **travel window**. Move with the wind at your back, using glissading technique, staff probing. Your goal is to reach a pre-scouted, wind-sheltered depression before full dark.
*   **Night:** Sleep in a **lee-side hollow** dug slightly into the compacted grit. Build a low wall of loose stones to break the worst of the wind. Your viscoelastic layer and outer shell will keep you warm—the air is too dry for convective heat loss to be severe, but the ground radiates cold. **Do not sleep in a breath-tower.** They are structurally unsound and their collapse is silent until it’s too late.

**Hydration & Nutrition:**
*   **Water:** Distilled water only. 500ml per day maximum. Rinse mouth with small amounts. Your body’s water needs will be lower due to cold and inactivity, but dehydration happens quickly in thin, dry air.
*   **Food:** **High-fat, low-fiber, sterile paste.** Think: pemmican, nut butters, canned liver pâté. No crumbs. No packaging. All waste is packed out in an inner, sealed film bag—any organic matter will attract scavengers (or worse, the scent-sensitive Basin-Stalkers).
*   **No Local Consumption:** The lichens are acidic and mineral-heavy. Consuming them could cause heavy metal poisoning or renal failure. The brine is saturated with halite, magnesium, and methane derivatives—a lethal cocktail.

## **4. Mindset & Interaction Protocol**

**You Are a Ghost:** Your goal is **zero acoustic signature**. Move when the wind peaks mask your sounds. Your respirator’s exhalation valve must be the quietest possible. All gear is padded or muffled. You do not shout, even in an emergency; you use pre-arranged hand signals with any companion.

**Read the Soundscape:** You are not listening *to* the plateau. You are listening *through* it.
*   A sudden *dropping* of the Scrape’s intensity? You’ve entered a Basin-Stalker’s hunting cone. **Freeze, then retreat slowly.**
*   A deep, subsonic *pulse* that makes your fillings ache for 10 seconds? A Drift-Whale is communicating nearby. **Do not look up.** Its tendrils can sense movement. Crouch lower, remain still until the pressure returns to normal.
*   A new, pure organ-pipe tone? The wind has found a new, hidden fissure. This is a landmark. **Note its pitch and direction.**

**Respect the Equilibrium:** You are a temporary, fragile pattern in a system that has been in perfect, slow dynamic balance for eons. Do not mark the stone. Do not build. Do not take anything of size (a pebble is okay if you need a tool-stone, but replace it with a similar one from elsewhere). Your presence is a violent anomaly. Minimize it.

**Emergency Protocol:**
If caught in the open during a **Wind-Event** (a sudden, doubling of the Base Drone and Scrape, often preceded by a strange ozone smell):
1.  **Do not run.** Running increases respiration, grit inhalation, and chaotic movement.
2.  **Find the nearest aerodynamic hollow**—the lee side of a large, rounded ventifact.
3.  **Assume the Ball.** Curl tightly, helmet to knees, arms around head. Your dampened layer is your only hope against the pressure waves that can rupture ears or lungs.
4.  **Breathe shallowly.** The event will last 2-15 minutes. The silence afterward (a relative, ringing quiet) will be more terrifying than the storm.

## **Final Warning:**

The Plateau of Silent Thunder is **not a landscape to experience**. It is a **physical law to be temporarily endured**. You will not "enjoy" it in any human sense. You may, if you are perfectly prepared and profoundly lucky, achieve a state of **resonant humility**—a fleeting sense of being a permissible, temporary vibration in an ancient, screaming song.

If your preparations feel excessive, you are underprepared. If you feel confident, you are dead.

Go in silence. Leave in silence. The mountain does not remember you. But if you survive, **you will never forget it.**
