# Preparation for Visiting The Weeping Grasse

**Primary Directive:** You are not visiting a place. You are temporarily becoming a disturbance in a sealed, resonant equation. Your goal is not to explore, but to **inhabit without altering**. Success is measured by the basin’s lack of response to your presence.

---

## I. Psychological Conditioning (The Most Critical Prep)

**1. Unlearn "Exploration":**
- This is not a cave to map, a ruin to loot, or a society to understand. It is a **single, living room** of a world that has chosen—or been forced—into absolute stasis.
- Accept: **You will not discover anything.** The town has no history beyond its current arrangement. The humans have no names, no stories that extend beyond the basin’s cycle. Your curiosity is a form of violence here.
- Meditate on the concept of **"Frozen Equilibrium."** Practice sitting perfectly still in a quiet room for hours, listening to the hum of your own body until you can no longer perceive it as *yours*.

**2. Sensory Recalibration:**
- **Sound:** You will be immersed in a polyrhythmic, persistent drizzle of sound that *does not fade*. It will become the background of your thoughts. Practice with layered, non-musical white noise (e.g., multiple dripping faucets, rain on a tin roof) to desensitize your startle response.
- **Sight:** Light will be dim, sourceless, and colored (bile-yellow, bruised violet). There are no shadows, only gradients of gloom. Train your eyes in low-light conditions without reaching for a bright light source—learn to navigate by texture and sound.
- **Touch & Smell:** Everything is cool, damp, and slick. The air is thick with wet stone and ozone. Spend time in cool, humid basements or near waterfalls to acclimate to a perpetually moist atmosphere that does *not* smell of life (no loam, no vegetation).

**3. Suppress the Narrative Instinct:**
- Your mind will scream for causality: *"Who built this? Why? What's outside?"* These questions are **unanswerable and irrelevant**. The basin *is*. The humans *are*. Practice mental stoppage—when a narrative impulse arises, visualize the **Floor of Faces**—a perfect, fleeting arrangement with no author.
- You must approach with the mindset of a **piece of silt** settling in still water, not a **tourist**.

---

## II. Physical & Gear Preparation

**Clothing:**
- **Material:** Absolutely no synthetic fabrics (nylon, polyester). They shed microplastics, generate static, and rustle. Use:
  - **Undyed, unbleached wool or heavy cotton.** They are quiet, absorb moisture without feeling clammy, and dampen sound.
  - **Soft-soled, minimalist footwear** (like moccasins or felt-soled shoes). Thick soles will muffle your footsteps to near-inaudibility on the sand.
- **Color:** Muted, earth tones—charcoal, slate, dried blood. Bright colors disrupt the visual palette and may agitate mineral lattices (observed subtly in Echo-Moth behavior).
- **Fit:** Everything must be **silent**. No zippers, Velcro, or loose tags. Seams must be soft. Test your entire outfit in a silent room by moving, turning, breathing.

**Gear (The Absolute Minimum):**
- **A small, padded, non-metallic container** (wood, thick leather) for any samples you might foolishly attempt to take. *Taking is prohibited.* If you must document, use a **wax tablet and stylus** (no paper—its rustle is cacophonous; no electronics—the basin’s mineral hum may interfere, and screens destroy night vision).
- **A single, non-reflective, **steady** light source.** A **frosted glass lantern with a candle or slow-burning oil** (scentless). The light must be dim and diffuse, mimicking the ambient glow. **No flashlights, headlamps, or lasers.** Sudden or directional light is a violent stimulus to the lattices and Echo-Moths.
- **A polished **obsidian shard** as a mirror.** Not for vanity—to check your own face for signs of disturbance. Your expression will affect the ambient glow.
- **Water purification is irrelevant.** Do not drink the weeping water. It is not H₂O in a biological sense. It is a mineral suspension. Carry your own water in a **unglazed ceramic vessel** (it will sweat, blending with the ambient moisture).

**What NOT to Bring:**
- Firearms, loud tools, metal objects (they clink and may resonate).
- Perfumes, soaps with strong scents.
- Cameras with shutters, any device with an audible click or beep.
- Food with strong odors. Eat a neutral, bland meal before entering.

---

## III. Entry Protocol

**1. The fissures are narrow.** You must **crawl** on your belly, moving **millimeter by millimeter**. Do not rush. Your breath must be slow and silent through your nose. The worst sin is to scrape stone against stone.

**2. First 30 Minutes (The Acclimation Period):**
- **Do not stand up fully.** Remain in a crouch or sit against a wall. Let your eyes adjust. Let the sound wash over you without trying to parse it.
- **Observe the humans.** Watch one Keeper for a full cycle of their task—perhaps scraping a lattice or weaving basalt fiber. Do not make eye contact. You are a shadow. They will likely ignore you if you are still and silent.
- **Listen to the drip-heartbeat.** Find a single weeping point and follow its rhythm until it merges with the whole. Your pulse will try to sync—let it.

**3. Movement Protocol:**
- **Move only when the soundscape has a "lull."** These occur when a large droplet falls, creating a momentary cascade that drowns out all other noise. Step then, and freeze the instant the cascade subsides.
- **Your path must follow the contours of the structures, never cutting across open sand.** The sand is the Scribes’ domain. Stepping on their temporary trails is a disruption.
- **Never block a weep.** Let water trace its eternal path. Redirecting a single droplet is like stopping a heartbeat.

---

## IV. Interaction with the Basin's Features

**The Mineral Lattices:**
- Do not touch. Your warmth and bioelectricity will cause localized brightening—a shout in the visual language.
- If one pulses brighter near you, **exhale slowly and lower your gaze.** Calm is the basin’s preferred state.

**The Echo-Moths:**
- They may cluster around you if you emit any sound or heat. If this happens, **hold your breath and become utterly still** until they drift away. Their chime is their voice—do not add yours.

**The Humans (The Basin’s Keepers):**
- **No communication.** They are not "people" as you know them. They are functions. A shared task is your only possible interaction: if one is scraping a lattice, you could, after hours of observation, mimic the motion on a neighboring patch with your own tool. Not to help, but to *mirror*. This may be tolerated. Spoken language will cause a palpable chill in the air.
- If they look at you, they are seeing a **glitch in the texture**. Do not smile, nod, or gesture. Blink slowly and return to your stillness.

**The Floor of Faces:**
- If you see one, **do not stare.** Glance, then look away. To fixate on it is to demand meaning from play. The basin’s weirdest joke is that it arranges human features without human intent. To seek your own face there is the deepest hubris.

---

## V. Signs of Your Failure (Disturbance)

If you have failed, the basin will subtly **recoil**:
1. The **drip-rhythm** will skip or syncopate.
2. The **lattice glow** will dim in a 10-foot radius around you.
3. **Echo-Moths** will flee, their chime turning sharp and discordant.
4. A **Keeper** will stop their task and stare at you with a look not of anger, but of profound, weary disappointment—as if a stone has started to bleed.
5. The air will feel **heavier**, colder. The constant sigh through the fissures will seem to hold its breath.

**If you see these signs:**
- **Freeze completely.** Do not move for 20 minutes.
- Then, **slowly**, with exaggerated care, **reverse your path** back to your entry fissure.
- Do not look back. The basin will re-stabilize after your absence. You will have been a bad memory.

---

## VI. Aftermath & Decompression

**1. You will carry the sound.** For days, you will hear phantom dripping in every quiet moment. This is normal. The basin’s acoustic signature has imprinted on your nervous system.

**2. Your sense of time will be fractured.** Hours in the Grasse feel like minutes and millennia simultaneously. Do not schedule anything for 48 hours after exit.

**3. You will feel a desperate urge to **tell someone**.** Do not. Language cannot hold the Grasse. To speak of it is to shatter the delicate understanding you achieved. The experience must remain a silent, mineral fact within you.

**4. You may find yourself seeking **absolute stillness** in your own world.** Sit in a quiet room. Listen to the hum of your fridge, the drip of a tap. Try to find the **equilibrium** you witnessed. You will fail, but the attempt will remind you that the Grasse is not a dream—it is a different kind of real.

---

**Final Admonition:**

You are a **blink** in a million-year sigh.
Your worth is in your **invisibility**.
Do not fall in love with the eeriness. Do not romanticize the stasis.
The Weeping Grasse is not beautiful. It is **accurate**.
Go, be accurate in your stillness, and leave no footprint in the sponge of time.
