## Preparation Protocol: Visiting The Polished Sorrow  
*For the Ordinary Multiverse Traveler*

**Foreword:** You are not preparing for a hike, an expedition, or a tourist visit. You are preparing to **maintain your human sovereignty** in a place that passively dissolves the boundaries of self, time, and reality. The Polished Sorrow is not hostile—it is *indifferent*, and its indifference is a gravitational force that erodes identity. Your goal is not to "enjoy" the scenery, but to **leave with your mind intact**. Assume everything you know about "environment" and "danger" is inverted.

---

### **I. Psychological & Cognitive Conditioning (6–12 Months Prior)**
This is the most critical phase. Your mind must be fortified against ontological seepage.

1.  **Sensory Deprivation & Reorientation Drills:**
    *   **Objective:** Train your brain to distrust its own senses without panicking.
    *   **Method:** Spend hours in completely neutral, soundproofed, featureless environments (painted rooms, sensory deprivation tanks). Practice identifying when your perception *diverges* from logical expectation (e.g., "I see a door where there is a wall" → label it "Perceptual Error #1" and mentally note: *The environment is generating false data*).
    *   **Advanced:** Use VR to simulate "reality breakdown" scenarios—objects shifting during blinks, your shadow being in the wrong place, spatial recursion. The goal is to achieve a state of **detached observation**. When you see your finger become your other hand, you must think: *"Ah, the Sorrow is projecting a localized reality variant. This is not me. This is the place."*

2.  **Solitude & "Self-Anchoring" Meditation:**
    *   **Objective:** Build an unshakable internal reference point—a "core self" that cannot be overwritten by the ambient density.
    *   **Method:** Develop a complex, multisensory mental ritual. A specific memory (not of a place, but of a *feeling*—e.g., the smell of your grandmother's kitchen, the sound of a particular song, the weight of a childhood blanket). Practice invoking this "anchor" instantly, under stress. It must be emotionally potent **and sensorily distinct** from anything in The Polished Sorrow (no cool, dry, ozone smells; no mineral textures; no still light).

3.  **Temporal Disorientation Training:**
    *   **Objective:** Break your dependency on circadian rhythm and external time cues.
    *   **Method:** Live for weeks with only a **mechanical, non-digital timepiece** (e.g., a wind-up watch with a clearly audible tick). Never look at the sun for time. Learn to trust the watch's absolute, unchanging passage. In The Sorrow, you will need to know if 5 minutes or 5 hours have passed, because the place will tell you neither.

4.  **Exposure to "Null Environments":**
    *   **Objective:** Accustom yourself to absolute, pressure-filled silence and stillness.
    *   **Method:** Visit extreme deserts at night, high-altitude rock deserts, or deep underground caverns. Focus on the psychological impact of * profound silence* and *utter stillness*. Journal the creeping anxiety, the sense of being watched by the void, the distortions in your own thoughts. Normalize it.

---

### **II. Gear & Equipment (The "Anchor Kit")**
Your gear must serve as **external anchors**—tangible, unchanging, human-made objects that scream *"This is from elsewhere."*

1.  **Primary Chronometer & Journal:**
    *   A **mechanical wristwatch** with a strong, regular tick. Wind it precisely at the start of each "day" (set by your watch, not the sun).
    *   A **hardcover, paper journal** and a **mechanical pencil** (no ink, which might behave strangely). Write in it **constantly**, even if just to note: *"10:14 GMT. Still here. The dome at bearing 220 looks closer than the one at 180. That is wrong. I am anchored."* The act of writing, the feel of paper, the graphite mark—these are human actions in a place of fossilized stasis.

2.  **Sensory Regulators:**
    *   **Ear Protection:** Not for loud noise, but for **textural consistency**. Use high-fidelity earplugs that dampen all sound to a uniform, low hum. You need to hear your own breath and the watch tick. The sub-audible drone of the world must be muted as much as possible.
    *   **Vision Aids:** **Tinted, wrap-around glasses** with a *slight, uniform blue filter*. This combats the "normal light" which may have hypnotic, reality-eroding properties. The tint creates a consistent, human-made color bias. Do not use anti-glare coatings; you need the slight reflection on the lenses to remind you of a physical barrier.
    *   **Touch Points:** Carry **three small, smooth, man-made objects** of distinct, familiar textures: a **glass marble**, a **polished stainless steel bead**, a **piece of worn sea glass**. Every hour, hold each one for 10 seconds. Focus on the *known* sensation against your skin.

3.  **Navigation & Territorial Marking:**
    *   **Compass:** A liquid-filled compass. It will likely behave erratically (the "reality breakdown" will affect its needle), but its *physical consistency*—the fluid's movement, the housing's weight—is key.
    *   **Trail Markers:** **Bright orange, non-biodegradable tape** and a **chalk-based, large "X" marker**. Place markers at each campsite or major turn. The color orange should not exist in the natural palette of The Sorrow. When you look back, if the marker you placed 20 minutes ago is gone or changed, you know reality has locally rewritten itself. **Do not panic. Return to your last *confirmed* marker and start anew. Assume your path back is never the same as your path forward.**

4.  **Sanctuary & Sustenance:**
    *   **Shelter:** A **simple, single-person bivy sack** made of bright synthetic material (e.g., neon yellow). Its purpose is not for warmth (temperature is constant), but for **enclosure**. When inside, you are in a defined, human-volume space. The zipper pull, the crinkle of the fabric—these are lifelines.
    *   **Food/Water:** High-calorie, **tasteless nutrient bars** and **sterile water in opaque, marked bottles**. Eating and drinking should be a **ritual of bio-maintenance**, not pleasure. No flavors should be associated with the experience. Taste is another sense the Sorrow can mimic (the "baking bread" scent).
    *   **Light:** A **headlamp with a red-light mode**. Use it only when absolutely necessary. The "normal light" is constant; introducing a second, human-controlled light source helps demarcate your personal operational sphere. Red light is least likely to interact with the environment's optics.

### **III. The Visit: Operational Protocol**

1.  **Landing & Initial Assessment:**
    *   Do not move for the first **30 minutes**. Sit on your bivy sack. Perform your self-anchoring ritual. Check your watch. Write: *"Arrival. Coordinates [if known]. Sensation report: [describe ONLY physical body feelings: 'pressure in ears,' 'dry throat,' 'watch tick audible']. No interpretation."*
    *   Do not explore built forms for **24 hours**. Acclimatize to the silence, the stillness, the perceptual wrongness. Your first goal is to confirm your **temporal continuity**. Is your watch ticking normally? Does your journal page count increase?

2.  **Movement & Exploration:**
    *   **The "Anchor Step":** Every 20–30 minutes, **stop**. Do not turn around. Look at your marker. Consult your watch. Perform the "touch points" ritual (glass, steel, sea glass). Verbally state facts: *"I am at Marker Gamma. Time is 14:22. The large dome with the spherical cavity is at bearing 315. It has not moved."* Speaking aloud forces your brain to use language, a highly structured human system, to counter the place's fluidity.
    *   **Never trust memory.** The built forms will rearrange in your mind. **Always navigate from your last physical marker, not your mental map.** If a monolith you passed 10 minutes ago now looks different, **it is different**. Your job is not to reconcile it, but to acknowledge: *"The environment has altered that object. My previous data is obsolete."*
    *   **Rule of Threes:** Never go more than **three visible markers** from your primary shelter without establishing a new, solid marker and spending 10 minutes anchoring at it.

3.  **Interaction with the "Density" (The Fauna/Presence):**
    *   **Do not attempt to see, hear, or communicate with them.** You cannot. Their "presence" is a psychological pressure—a wooliness, a sense of occupancy in the space around your lungs.
    *   **When the pressure intensifies** (a feeling of your thoughts slowing, of the air becoming "thicker"), **stop.** Increase your breathing rate deliberately. Focus on the **tick of your watch** or the **texture of your glass marble**. Recite a **memorized, structured text**—a poem, a technical manual, a list of historical dates. You are fighting a **cognitive tide**, not a physical threat.
    *   The goal is to **not become part of the polish**. You are a disturbance. They are the medium. You must maintain your disturbance until you leave.

4.  **Encountering Built Forms:**
    *   **Do not enter.** Do not touch the cavities. These are **scars of a tool-use that is now geology**. They are not artifacts *for you*. They are the fossilized *moment* of use, frozen and absorbed. Touch is a profound risk; you may feel a **resonance** or a **false memory** of the tool's use. Abort contact immediately if you feel a "pull" or a sense of familiarity.
    *   **Document purely:** Sketch the *external geometry only*. Use your pencil. Do not speculate on purpose. Note: *"Form: rectangular prism, 2m x 1m x 0.5m. Surface: composite, with three blurry parallel grooves on east face. Cavity: sphere, 10cm diameter, centered."* That is all.

### **IV. Withdrawal & Debrief**

1.  **The Return Path is Not the Approach Path.** Assume the landscape has reconfigured while you were there. Follow your markers **forward only** until you are certain you are beyond the last dome cluster (use your compass and watch to verify you are moving in a consistent, directed line for a sustained period). Then, attempt a return to your point of entry **using a new, wide arc**.
2.  **Immediate Post-Exit Ritual:** Once in a "normal" multiverse transit zone (any place with variable light, sound, and organic texture), **do not remove your gear**. Find the most chaotic, sensory-rich environment possible—a bustling market, a storm—and **immerse yourself** for at least an hour. You need to *scrape off* the silence and stillness.
3.  **Debrief Protocol:** Write your entire journal **before sleeping**. Then, write a **second, "objective" report** as if for a scientist, stripping out all emotional or perceptual language. Compare. The gaps will show you where the Sorrow began to rewrite you.
4.  **Quarantine:** The psychological residue is real. You may experience episodes of "static stability" in your home world—moments where sound seems suspended, where your sense of self feels thin. This is normal. Seek a therapist familiar with **ontological displacement**. Do not dismiss it as stress.

---

**Final Warning:**  
The Polished Sorrow does not hate you. It does not love you. It is **finished**. Your human-ness, your change, your *process*, is the **only discordant note in its perfect, polished song**. It is not waiting to attack you. It is waiting for you to **stop being a disturbance**. To become still. To become polished.

Your preparation is not for survival. It is for **departure**. You must leave before you start to find the silence... beautiful.

**Go with your anchors. Leave before you forget you need them.**
