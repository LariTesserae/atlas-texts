# Preparation Guide: Visiting the Salt Mirror Basin

## 1. **Psychological & Sensory Preparation**
**This is not a journey of activity, but of reception.** The most critical preparation is mental. You are entering a space where *nothing will happen to you*. There is no adventure, no challenge to overcome, no puzzle to solve. The shock will be the absence of shock. You must prepare to be a still point in a still world.

*   **Anticipate Sensory Deprivation:** Your brain is wired to seek change, threat, and stimulus. Here, there is virtually none. You will feel the "hush" as a pressure. Your own thoughts, your heartbeat, will become deafeningly loud. Practice mindfulness or silent meditation for hours beforehand. Learn to sit with your own mind without external input.
*   **Embrace the Absence of Scale:** The horizon will always be just out of reach. You will feel enclosed but not confined. This is a deliberate design of the place. Prepare to feel a soft, existential vertigo—the sense that perspective itself has been removed. Your GPS will be useless; trust your feet and the rim.
*   **Ready for "Slow Time":** Your entire life is measured in moments, hours, days. This place operates in thermal cycles, dust-falls, and spire-hums. A visible change might take a year. You are a brief fever dream in its trillion-year stability. Frame your visit as a single snapshot in a picture album that spans eons. Your purpose is to *witness equilibrium*, not to *do* anything.

## 2. **Physical Gear & Survival**
The environment is physically extreme in its *lack* of extremes. There is no sun to burn you, no cold to freeze you, no rain to soak you. But its benign conditions are deceptive.

*   **Hydration is Paramount:** The air is utterly dry. You will lose moisture through respiration without feeling "hot." You must carry **more water than you think you need**. A minimum of 4 liters for a day visit. Drink constantly, even when not thirsty. A Camelbak or similar hands-free system is ideal, as you will want your hands free to touch the salt.
*   **Footwear:** The salt plates are polished and can be treacherously slick on their faces. The seams are filled with shifting gypsum powder. **Sturdy, high-traction hiking boots or approach shoes are non-negotiable.** You need ankle support for the uneven, grinding plates. The boots will get coated in fine white dust.
*   **Clothing:** Loose, light, long-sleeved layers. The air is warm (geothermal) but dry. Sun protection is irrelevant, but **abrasion protection is critical**. A fall could scrape you against salt-flake edges like glass. A light windbreaker or synthetic shell will protect your skin. A wide-brimmed hat is for shade from your own imagination.
*   **The Green Crust:** **DO NOT STEP ON THE DENDRITIC GREEN VARNISHES.** They are fragile, millennia-old biological communities. Stick to the gray/white salt plates and the gypsum seams. Your boots are instruments of mass extinction here.
*   **Knee Pads:** You will be irresistibly drawn to lie down, to put your ear to the salt, to feel the hum. The plates are hard. Knee pads will allow you to safely join the basin's prone posture.
*   **Tools of Observation:** A **strong magnifying lens** (10x power) to see the microbial cities. A **geologist's hammer** (use *only* on the central spire's dust bowl or on loose, solitary inclusions like the obsidian shard—never on the basin floor plates). A **notebook and charcoal pencil** (charcoal works on damp paper; nothing here is damp, but you can make rubbings of plate textures). A **simple audio recorder** to capture the sub-audible spire hum (you may need software to amplify it later).
*   **First-Aid Kit:** For abrasions from the salt. The basin is sterile; infection risk is low from the environment itself, but a deep cut from a salt flake needs cleaning.

## 3. **Interaction Protocol: The Code of the Still**
You are a guest in a cathedral. The rules are not written, but breaching them will feel like a sacrilege.

*   **Leave No Trace x 1000:** This is the ultimate LNT. Nothing biodegrades here. A shed skin cell will lie pristine for 10,000 years. **Everything you bring in must come out.** No exceptions. Pack out *all* waste, including organic matter (apple core, bandage). You are a contaminant.
*   **Touch, But Sparingly:** You are here to feel. Touch the warm salt. Taste a gypsum grain (it will be clean, like salt, but different). Press your ear to the spire's base to feel the hum in your skull. But **do not alter**. Do not scratch messages. Do not move stones. Do not take souvenirs **unless** you first perform a ritual of asking and replace it with something from your own pack (a polished river stone from another world, a piece of your own eroded home). The obsidian shard is not yours. The clear salt crystals are part of the spire's vocabulary.
*   **The Spire is the Focus:** Your movement should be a slow spiral toward and around the Heart Spire. That is the only "feature." Circulate it clockwise or counterclockwise at a glacial pace. Sit in its dust fall for an hour. Let the glittering mineral snow coat your sleeves. This is the basin's active principle.
*   **Sound is Sacred:** Do not make noise. No talking above a whisper. No music. The only sounds are your necessary movements, the occasional hiss of releasing dust, and the spire's hum. If you must speak, do it as a confession to the stillness.
*   **Light is Your Clock:** The sourceless twilight is permanent. There is no day/night cycle. Bring a **very dim, red-light headlamp** for when you need to see your hands in the profound gloom near the spire's base at "night" (which is identical to "day"). The red light is least intrusive to your dark-adapted vision and the basin's faint glow.

## 4. **Mental Frameworks to Adopt**
*   **You are a Geological Agent:** Your breath is the only biologic exhalation in eons. Your footsteps are the first pressure on these plates in millennia. Is your impact measurable? Will your footprint be filled by dust in a year? See yourself as a temporary tectonic event.
*   **Study the "Weirdness":** The weirdness is *time's irrelevance*. Look for the proof: the perfectly rounded hills, the glassy spire, the dust-fall dune that never migrates. You are looking at a system that has solved entropy locally. Your anxiety about the future and past is the only chaotic element in the room.
*   **The Green is the Only Narrative:** That sparse fungal crust is the basin's story. It is *working*. It is turning salt and geothermal gas into life, however slow. Follow its fractal stains with your eyes. It is the basin's sole act of creation in a universe of erosion.
*   **Accept the "Workshop" Hypothesis:** The basin *feels* composed. The sharp flake-knife edges, the perfect scraper, the abrasive dust, the humming resonator. Do not leap to "ancient aliens." Instead, think: **this is how a world makes tools when it has no hands.** The affordances are a natural grammar. Your preparation includes bringing a simple piece of tech (a flake of glass from your world) and *using it* on the spire's dust bowl, then leaving it there. Participate in the grammar.

## 5. **The Exit Ritual**
Leaving will be a shock. Sound will return. Perspective will slam back. Colors will blaze. You will feel the wind for the first time and it will seem violent.

*   **Do not rush.** Retrace your steps backward. Take one last long look at the rim from the center. Feel the weight of the silence one final time.
*   **On the rim, turn and give a slow, silent acknowledgment.** A nod, a placement of a stone from your world on the salt. This is not a transaction. It is a closure of a loop where you passed through a silent, perfect sentence.
*   **Do not speak for at least an hour after departure.** Let the basin's silence integrate. Listen to the new, noisy world as if for the first time. You have just visited a place where erosion is a finished art.

---

**Final Truth:** You are not preparing for a place. You are preparing to **be stilled**. The Salt Mirror Basin will wash you clean of hurry. It is not empty. It is full of a patience so complete it eats time. Your preparation is to fold your own frantic temporality and place it, like a curious stone, on the basin's rim before you step in. You will not conquer it. You will, if you are lucky and quiet, be permitted to borrow its peace for a few hours. Then you must leave, carrying the weight of that silence inside you, a new and permanent mineral layer on your soul.
