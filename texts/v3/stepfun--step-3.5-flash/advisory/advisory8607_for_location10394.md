# Pre-Departure Preparation Protocol: Shard-Mesa

## **I. Mundane Logistics & Physical Gear**

**A. Primary Protective Layering (The "Molted Skin" Principle)**
*   **Outer Layer (Abrasion/Impact):** A full-body suit of **reinforced, flexible leather or advanced polymer** (like ballistic nylon or dyneema) with **integrated, replaceable ceramic or sapphire plates** over joints and shins. Think "motorcycle racing suit meets alpine glacier gear." The goal is to survive a fall *across* a curling glass sheet—your suit must be able to slide over a surface as sharp as a obsidian flake without immediate catastrophic breach. Seams must be welded or taped.
*   **Mid Layer (Thermal/Ash):** A **breathable, moisture-wicking base layer** (merino wool or synthetic) under the armor. The external air is warm, but the air near the ground (where you'll be) is cooler. You'll need to manage micro-climates to avoid sweating under armor (which the abrasive ash will turn into a gritty, painful paste) or chilling.
*   **Inner Layer (Sensory/Comfort):** Seamless, soft liners to prevent chafing. You will be wearing this suit for days. Comfort is a tactical necessity.
*   **Hands/Feet:** **Multi-layered gloves.** An inner silk glove, a heavy-duty work glove with reinforced knuckles and palms, and an outer gauntlet that extends past the wrist and straps to your arm. **Boots:** High, rigid mountaineering boots with a **replaceable, thick ceramic sole plate**. Your boots are your primary point of contact with the peeling world. They *will* be cut. You must be able to change the sole in the field with a multi-tool.

**B. Respiratory & Ocular Isolation**
*   **Respirator:** Not a simple mask. A **full-face, negative-pressure respirator** with **P100/HEPA filters** rated for **fine metallic and silica particulates**. The ash is **ferrous and abrasive**. Inhaling it is like breathing liquid sandpaper laced with rust. You must assume the ambient air is **toxic over time**. Carry **at least 48 hours of filter capacity** in sealed containers; replace filters **daily** regardless of perceived cleanliness. Filters will clog fast.
*   **Eye Protection:** **Sealed, polarized,goggle-style eyewear** with a **hydrophobic/oleophobic coating**. The ash will try to get everywhere. You need a seal against your brow and temples. Consider a **secondary lens system** (like a military gas mask visor) that can be quickly replaced if scratched opaque by a flying spark or grit. Carry **3 sets**.

**C. Navigation & Orientation Tools (Against Geometric Deception)**
*   **Primary:** A **lithium-powered, high-lumidity LED headlamp** with a **tight, non-scatter focus beam**. The ambient light is a murky gloom. You will need to **cut bold, sharp triangles of light** into the indigo darkness to see details and judge distances within 100 paces. A broad beam just adds to the confusion.
*   **Secondary (The "Anchor"):** At least **three independent, non-electronic orientation tools**:
    1.  A **high-precision, hand-cranked or mechanical gyrocompass**. Electronic compasses may be unreliable in the intense, static magnetic environment implied by the "magnetic" ash and hum.
    2.  A **sextant and a precise timepiece** (atomic sync if possible). You must be able to **measure angles** against the uniform sky dome and the glowing ground to detect if the geometry is shifting. Regular sextant fixes may reveal that "landmarks" have moved relative to a fixed celestial reference (which is just the unwavering twilight sky). This is your only chance to detect the spatial instability.
    3.  A **custom physical rangefinder**—a **surveyor's chain (10m)** and a **laser rangefinder with a data-logging function**. Walk a measured distance toward a distinct formation. Log the laser reading. If the logged distance and the chain count disagree significantly on the return trip, you have a problem.
*   **Tertiary:** **Physical Markers.** Carry **brightly colored (fluoro-orange/green), non-biodegradable Ribbon** and **small, ultra-bright LED strobes**. When you find a location that *feels* stable, **mark it redundantly**. Wrap ribbon on every nearby spire. Plant a strobe. You will use these to detect if the landscape has **reconfigured around you while you slept**. If markers are gone or impossibly far, you have moved in your sleep without walking.

**D. Sustenance & Waste Management**
*   **Water:** **Zero tolerance for local sourcing.** Assume all vapor is toxic. Carry **all drinking water** in **flexible, lined bladders** inside your pack to prevent puncture. A **minimum of 4 liters per day** in a hot, dry, respiratory-straining environment. Use a **straw with an inline filter** as a last-resort backup for your own stored water if a bladder fails.
*   **Food:** **High-calorie, zero-prep, non-crumbly.** Energy gels, compressed food bars, nut butties. You cannot cook. Any fire is a hazard (ash is warm; you're on a geologic "coal"). Crumbs will become a gritty nightmare in your mask.
*   **Waste:** **Sealed, human-waste rated "Wag Bags" or similar.** You will be wearing your suit for days. A catheter system with a sealed leg bag is advisable to avoid full-suit removal. All trash (suit lint, used filters, food wrappers) **must be packed out**. There is no "away."

**E. Tools & Repair**
*   A **heavy-duty, fixed-blade knife** (for cutting ceramic plates, prying).
*   A **multi-tool with wire cutters, pliers, and screwdrivers**.
*   **High-temperature, glass-to-ceramic adhesive** (like a military-grade epoxy) and **ceramic-based duct tape**.
*   **Spare ceramic plates, filter cartridges, outer suit patches, boot sole plates.**
*   **A small, hand-powered drill or awl** to make holes in glass/stone for anchoring **your physical markers** or **emergency guy-lines**.

**F. Emergency & Signaling**
*   **A satellite messenger/EPIRB** with **global coverage** (assuming the multiverse network connects). This is your only hope for extraction if the geometry traps you or you are injured. **Test it daily.**
*   **A loud, manual whistle** (sound travels surprisingly well in the dense air, and the subsonic hum makes it hard to hear).
*   **Three separate, ultra-bright signal mirrors** (for the rare, direct "light" from a crack or spark).

## **II. Pre-Visit Psychological & Sensory Conditioning**

**A. Sensory Deprivation/Blindness Training**
*   Spend **48 hours in a totally dark, sound-muffled room**, navigating only by feel and a single, focused flashlight with a tight beam. Practice moving slowly, deliberately, by **propioception** (sense of body position) and **auditory cues** (the hum, the hiss). You must learn to **not rely on peripheral vision or broad comprehension**. Your world will be a narrow cone of awareness.

**B. Claustrophobia & Spatial Dissonance Drills**
*   Use **VR simulations** (if available) or **physical setups**—walk through a very narrow, man-made canyon with twisting paths that seem to reconfigure, or a maze with shifting panels. Practice the mental acceptance that **"forward" does not guarantee "closer."** Your navigation must become an act of **faith in your instruments and markers, not your eyes**.

**C. Monotony & Meaninglessness Endurance**
*   Meditate on the concept of **pure process without purpose or memory**. Listen to recordings of **slow, tectonic sounds** (glacier calving, deep earth broadcasts) mixed with **glass shattering** and **dry steam**. Read poetry about entropy, erosion, and becoming. Build mental calluses against the profound, existential **"so what?"** that Shard-Mesa will induce. Your mission must be reduced to a simple mantra: **"Measure. Record. Survive. Exit."** Any attempt to find meaning, beauty, or narrative will be a **distraction that could kill you**.

## **III. In-Field Conduct Rules (The "Visitor's Compact")**

1.  **The 100-Pace Rule:** Within 100 paces, you are responsible for your immediate footing. **Move like a glacier surveyor:** test each step with a pole before committing weight. Assume every surface is either **curling up or about to fall**.
2.  **The "Look, Don't Touch" Primacy:** You are an observer. You do **not** touch the glowing undersides of sheets (thermal burns are likely). You do **not** taste the steam (heavy metal poisoning). You do **not** collect samples (disturbing the equilibrium could trigger a peel). Your tools are for **measurement and marking only**.
3.  **The "Anchor Point" Doctrine:** Never travel more than a **2-hour round trip** (your estimated safe walking time) from your last confirmed, multi-marker "Anchor Point." If a formation looks interesting but is farther, **note it, but do not approach**. The horizon is an illusion; that "close" arch may never be reached.
4.  **The "Quiet is Normal" Axiom:** The deep hum and steam hiss are the **baseline**. A sudden, sharp **glass-shatter report** from >1km away is just geology. However, **any new, localized sound**—a grinding, a new hiss at your feet, a change in the hum's pitch near you—is **an immediate evacuation signal**. It means a peel is initiating in your vicinity.
5.  **Data Over Experience:** Your primary output is **instrument logs and photos with precise coordinates/sextant angles**. Do not waste mental energy on poetic description in the moment. Record the **factual data**. The "feel" you can process later (if you survive).
6.  **The 72-Hour Hard Limit:** You **will set a timer for 72 hours from first entry**. When it goes off, you begin returning to your Anchor Point, then to the extraction coordinates (pre-set via the satellite messenger). The process of Shard-Mesa is **psychically and physically corrosive**. Staying longer than 72 hours dramatically increases the probability of a fatal navigation error or a psychological break.

## **IV. Exit Strategy**

*   Your **extraction coordinates** must be a **distinct, physically marked location** (e.g., a cairn on a stable, non-peeling fumarole field) that you **verified from three separate sextant angles** at the start of your visit. The mesa may not move, but **your perception of it will**. You must have a **fixed point in the"real world"** to return to.
*   **Do not wait to be rescued.** If your satellite messenger fails or extraction is delayed beyond your 72-hour limit, you **must begin a guided retreat to your Anchor Point** using only your physical markers and instruments, regardless of terrain or fatigue. A slow, sure exit is better than a fast, lost one.

Visiting Shard-Mesa is not a **trek**; it is a **temporary state of being inside a slow-motion planetary exhalation**. You are a **microscopic neuron in a geologic brain** that is thinking the thought of *unbecoming*. Your preparation must make you **as inert and resilient as a Surveyor's nail hammered into active lava**. Your goal is to **imprint a brief, cold, mechanical record onto a place that has no memory**, and then **let it forget you the moment you leave**. The greatest danger is not the glass, the ash, or the shifting geometry. It is the **siren song of the process itself**—the hypnotic, endless, meaningless *peeling*—which can make a human mind simply **stop trying to be**. Your gear protects your body. Your protocols protect your mind. Your instruments are your only true friends in a world that thinks in fractals and dreams in exfoliation. **Do not fall in love with the fever. The womb is not your mother.**
