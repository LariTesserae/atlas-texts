### Pre-Trip Prep for First-Time Visitors to the Resonant Weald of Veridion
This is not a tropical getaway, a hiking trip, or a "wilderness adventure" in any sense you’reused to. The Weald is a living, thrumming beyond-modern planetary processor, not a passive landscape to explore. All prep below is tailored for an ordinary multiverse traveler (standard 30L travel pack, 2-3 weeks of multiverse-hopping experience, average fitness, no specialized xenobiology training) available from most major multiverse trade hubs for under 200 credits, with budget workarounds listed where possible.
---
#### 1. Non-Negotiable Pre-Trip Health & Physiological Prep
The Weald’s baseline environment will mess with your body before you even step off the arrival platform, so this is the one category you don’t skimp on:
- **Subsonic vibration mitigation:** Book an appointment at any multiverse travel clinic for custom low-frequency dampening earplugs (standard foam plugs only block high frequencies; these are molded to seal your ear canal against the Weald’s 18Hz baseline thrum that vibrates your molars and inner ear bones). Pair these with a thin, flexible silicone mouth guard to stop the thrum from cracking your teeth or giving you a migraine after 4 hours of exposure. Budget alternative: Pack 2 sets of wax earplugs + a cheap boil-and-bite football mouth guard; they’re 70% as effective for short stays.
- **Medical screening first:** The subsonic hum disrupts pacemakers, insulin pumps, and other implanted electronics. If you have any internal medical devices, get them shielded for high-vibration environments before you travel. If you have asthma, allergies, or respiratory sensitivities, pick up a P100-rated respirator (standard N95s won’t filter out the Weald’s sound-feeding lichen spores or fungal aerosols). Pack double your regular supply of any maintenance meds, stored in airtight, water-proof containers—damp air will ruin paper blister packs in 12 hours flat.
- **Temporal stability shot:** Get a standard multiverse temporal stabilizer injection 48 hours before departure. The Weald has unmarked time-dilation pockets in fungal rings and mycelial thickets; this shot stops you from aging 10 years in 2 minutes if you accidentally wander into one. Budget alternative: A cheap, 10-credit temporal anchor charm from the Nexus Hub bazaar works for stays under 72 hours.
- **Circadian adjustment pack:** The Weald has no day/night cycle, just permanent golden-green twilight. Pack a silk sleep mask and a 7-day supply of over-the-counter melatonin to avoid jet lag that will leave you disoriented and clumsy around glass-edged leaves and root bridges.
---
#### 2. Gear & Supply Checklist (No Exotic/Xenotech Required)
All items below are available at standard multiverse outdoor supply stores, no special permissions needed:
- **Clothing:** Leave all cotton, polyester, or crinkly fabric at home. The Weald’s leaves are edged with fine glass that will shred flimsy material in an hour, and crinkly clothing makes noise that can attract fauna or accidentally trigger the Weald’s "sound is matter" rule. Opt for:
  - 2 pairs of ripstop, non-adhesive hiking pants (avoid any with loud Velcro or zippers)
  - 3 layers of thick, non-crinkling merino wool base layers (warm, damp-resistant, and muted brown-green to blend into the twilight)
  - Waterproof, ankle-support hiking boots with deep lugs (the moss-covered petrified wood platforms and root bridges are slippery with damp, and you’ll be walking on uneven, spongy porous rock)
  - Budget alternative if you can’t afford a ~50 credit adaptive chameleonic jacket: Wear a plain, matte brown-green rain shell over your wool layers; it’s not camouflage, but it won’t stand out to the Weald’s inhabitants or fauna like bright colors will.
- **Protection:**
  - Shatterproof, anti-fog hiking goggles (fine glass leaf shards and drifting spore clouds will shred cheap sunglasses or scratch regular lenses)
  - A small, portable air filter for your respirator (the Weald’s air has a metallic tang from dissolved minerals in the rivers; this cuts down on throat irritation)
- **Navigation & Tech:**
  - A pre-downloaded map of the Weald’s designated visitor nodes and marked safe root bridge routes (get this for free from the Nexus Hub trade office; unmarked paths shift monthly as root bridges recalibrate, and unmarked zones have unmarked gravity/time pockets)
  - A basic, non-wireless compass tuned to the Weald’s magnetic field (all the local fauna navigate by this field, so it works even when GPS is fried by the Weald’s quantum bio-resonance)
  - **Critical rule:** Turn every electronic device you bring to full airplane mode, no exceptions. No Bluetooth, no Wi-Fi, no cellular signals, no haptic watch alerts, no beeping timers. The Weald is a planetary-scale biological processor; foreign radio signals act like malware, and will cause random sound-solidification spikes, bridge collapses, or agitated fauna. Even your phone’s silent vibrate mode emits low-frequency pulses that can interact with the baseline thrum.
- **Supplies:**
  - 3 days of vacuum-sealed, crinkle-free rations (avoid strongly scented food; the six-legged grazers and phase-shifting weasels are attracted to strong smells, and crinkly packaging creates noise that can trigger sound-solidification)
  - A 1L reusable water bottle + a high-grade portable water filter (the phosphorescent rivers have high levels of dissolved iridescent minerals that will give you kidney damage if you drink them unfiltered)
  - A small pack of biodegradable, fragrance-free hand sanitizer (the Weald’s microbes are not adapted to Earth bacteria, so you don’t want to introduce foreign germs to the ecosystem—and the inhabitants will ban you for life if they see you touching plants with dirty hands)

#### 3. Behavioral & Cultural Grounding (Before You Step Through the Portal)
The Weald’s inhabitants are not hostile, but they are protective of their symbiotic system, and they will eject you immediately if you break their unspoken rules. Spend 10 minutes watching the free orientation video at the Nexus Hub before you go:
- **Don’t conquer, listen:** This is not a place to "bag peaks" or take flash photos. The baseline thrum is the Weald’s breath; spend your first hour on the arrival platform just sitting and acclimating to the sound layers before you try to cross a root bridge.
- **No sudden movements or loud noises:** Even a dropped water bottle can create a focused sound pulse that accidentally solidifies into a sharp spike, or startles a herd of grazers into breaking a root bridge. Move slowly, step lightly, and if you need to call out to someone, hum in a low, soft tone—shouting creates a sound pulse strong enough to shatter a leaf or crack a crystal formation.
- **Don’t touch anything unless invited:** The black ice filaments shooting from the seedling you’ll see on the platform are growing root bridges; breaking one will destabilize the whole node network and get you banned. Don’t pick the metallic seed pods, don’t poke the pneumatic fungi, and don’t step on the glowing mycelial patches (they’re part of the Weald’s interstitial connection network, and damaging them can send you into a random gravity pocket).
- **Trade etiquette:** If you want to interact with the inhabitants, bring small, quiet, useful trade goods: sharp ceramic pruning shears for their mycelial management, sealed spice packets, or small, shielded solar phone chargers (they don’t use electricity, but they use the small batteries for their acoustic focusing mirrors). Don’t offer anything with loud alerts, harsh chemicals, or wireless signals; they’ll see you as a threat to the Weald’s system.
---
#### 4. On-Arrival Immediate First Hour Protocol
1. The second you step off the portal onto the mossy petrified wood platform, put in your earplugs and mouth guard before you take your first breath. The initial thrum is strong enough to make you disoriented and dizzy if you’re unprotected.
2. Don’t move for 10 full minutes. Watch the sound layers: the low baseline thrum, the higher chime of seed pods opening in the distance, the distant shriek of the bio-luminescent aerial predators. Let your inner ear adjust to the vibration so you don’t stumble and fall off the platform’s 300-foot drop into the phosphorescent canyons.
3. Locate the small, glowing amber marker by the platform edge— that’s the entrance to the marked safe root bridge path. Don’t wander off into the mist; the empty interstitial spaces between nodes are full of drifting leviathans and unmarked time pockets.
4. If you see the tiny silver seedling with the black ice filament growing from the crack in the platform, don’t touch it. Smile and nod if you see an inhabitant watching you; they’ll see you’re not there to damage the Weald, and might invite you to their monastic complex later.
---
#### 5. Day-to-Day Safety Rules
- Never stray from the marked root bridges and paths. The Canopy “continent” above you looks solid, but most of those fronds are thin, fragile layers of bioluminescent tissue that will break if you step on them, sending you falling miles into the mist.
- If you hear the synchronized high-pitched alarm call of a nearby insect hive, freeze. That means the phase-shifting weasel-like fauna is hunting nearby; they only attack if you make sudden movements. Stand still for 5 minutes until the alarm stops, then backtrack the way you came.
- If you feel the thrum get suddenly louder, or your teeth start vibrating harder than normal, you’ve walked into a sound eddy or pool. Put in your backup earplugs, find a sturdy rock or plant to lean against, and wait 10-15 minutes for the eddy to pass. Staying in a sound pool for more than 30 minutes can rupture your inner ear.
- Don’t take photos with flash. The Weald’s bioluminescence is tuned to the golden-green twilight spectrum; bright flash will blind the aerial predators and disrupt the sound-flow patterns the inhabitants manage.
---
#### 6. Emergency Protocols
- If a root bridge starts shifting under you (they recalibrate to better vibration lines once a month, so this is normal), drop to your knees, spread your weight evenly, and wait for it to settle. Don’t run—running creates more vibration, which speeds up the shift.
- If you get lost and can’t find the marked path, stay put, turn off all your devices, and hum the low baseline thrum note you acclimated to on arrival. The Weald’s mycelial network will pick up the vibration and guide an inhabitant to you within an hour. Wandering will only send you deeper into unmarked interstitial space, where you could end up in a time pocket or a leviathan’s migratory path.
- If you stumble into a fungal time-dilation ring (marked by pulsing, concentric rings of glowing fungi), back away immediately. Even 10 seconds of exposure can leave you 10 years older than you were when you left home, or stuck in a loop of 1 minute of time. If you accidentally step in, hold your breath and count to 120 as fast as you can—most time loops reset after 2 minutes of no external input.
- If a leviathan drifts within 100 feet of your node, stay completely still and quiet. They are slow, blind, and navigate by vibration; if you move, they’ll think you’re a threat and exhale a cloud of spore gas that will put you in a coma for 3 days. They will pass within 10 minutes if you don’t disturb them.
---
Last note: The Weald doesn’t want to hurt you. It’s just busy running a planetary processor and tending its gardens. Treat it like a quiet, very large, very loud housemate, not a tourist attraction, and you’ll be fine. If you listen close enough, you might even hear it hum your name back.
