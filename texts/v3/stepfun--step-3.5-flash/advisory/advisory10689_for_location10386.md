### **I. Physical & Gear Preparation: The Philosophy of Minimalism**

Your surface-world gear is an offensive here. It speaks of separation, of a hard boundary between you and the environment. Here, that boundary is a lie. Your gear must be an extension of the grove, not a barrier against it.

1.  **Clothing:**
    *   **Material:** Forget synthetics. Wear a single layer of **undyed, soft, loosely-woven natural fiber**—linen or raw silk. It must be porous enough to let the constant mist and bioluminescent spores settle on and into it without feeling clammy. It should not rustle. The sound of fabric摩擦 is a violent intrusion here.
    *   **Fit:** Loose. Anything tight creates a zone of friction, a "you" against the "world." Allow the damp, warm air to circulate against your skin. You will be perpetually damp; fight it, and you will go mad.
    *   **Color:** Muted earth tones—ochre, umber, slate gray. You are not a brightly colored insect; you are a piece of the shifting mosaic. Bright colors are a scream in the thick quiet.

2.  **Footwear: None.**
    *   This is non-negotiable. The floor is a complex texture: cool, damp fungi; warm, yielding moss; polished, cold basalt; the shocking, viscous warmth of the "damp" lagoons. Your soles must feel it all. The connection is part of the "reading" of the space. Callused feet are an asset. Move with deliberate, silent pressure.

3.  **Carried Items:**
    *   **Water:** None. The water here is *alive*. To drink from the "damp" is to ingest the ecosystem's blood. It may be harmless, or it may integrate you prematurely. You do not consume the matrix; you exist within it. Hydrate *before* ingress, and accept the film of moisture on your skin as sufficient.
    *   **Food:** Minimal, high-calorie, *completely inert* paste (think dense, flavorless carbohydrate gel) in a biodegradable capsule. Eat only when your surface-body screams, and do it quickly, swallowing the evidence. The scent of your surface-food is a pollutant. Hunger here is a different, slower signal.
    *   **Light:** **No electric lights.** The amber bioluminescence is your only friend. A single, soft, shielded candle of wax (beeswax, not paraffin) is the only permissible artificial light. Its flame is a living, flickering thing, a tiny kin to the polymer vines. But even this is a intrusion. Learn to see in the dimness. Your eyes will adjust, but not to the surface-world's definition of "seeing."
    *   **Tools:** A small, smooth, non-metallic probe of dense wood or bone. For testing the firmness of a fungal mat, or gently parting a vine-tendril. No knives, no metal. Metal has no place here; it does not weather, does not integrate, it *imposes*.
    *   **Notebook/Pen:** If you must record, use paper made of pressed local moss (collected *before* ingress, from the threshold) and a charcoal stick. Write with your non-dominant hand to break the "observer" pattern. But understand: your notes will feel alien when you read them in the surface light. The act of writing is more important than the content.

### **II. Sensory & Psychological Retraining (The Critical Prep)**

This is 90% of your preparation. You must re-learn your senses.

1.  **Hearing:** Surface-world hearing is for *locating*. Here, hearing is for *timing* and *resonance*.
    *   Train in a quiet room. Focus on the sound of your own heartbeat. Then, focus on the sound of a single, slow breath. Now, imagine that breath has a three-second delay between your lungs and your ears. Practice listening to the space *between* sounds. The "thick quiet" is a texture you must learn to feel in your molars.
    *   The "scuttle" of a thousand small things is not a noise; it is the grove's **nervous system**. Do not try to isolate individual sounds. Let them wash over you as a single, complex hum—the sound of becoming.

2.  **Touch & Temperature:**
    *   The air is hot, but your skin is cool and damp. This paradox is your new normal. Thermoregulation is an illusion. Practice sauna/steam room protocols, but *without* the relief of cool air. Sit in a warm, moist room until your surface-mind stops screaming about "overheating." Let the sweat bead and stay.
    *   The "wrongness" of textures is key. Find a cool, wet stone. Touch it. It feels wet. Now, find a dry piece of polished jade. Touch it. The moss *looks* wet, but if you press it, it may feel dry and springy. An obsidian surface may feel warm. Your brain must unlearn "wet= cool, dry= warm." The surface is a lie. The *sensation* is the only truth.

3.  **Sight & Time:**
    *   **Light:** Train your eyes in twilight. Use only candlelight at home for hours. Stare at shadows until they resolve into shapes, then dissolve again. Learn to see *movement in stillness*—the faint pulse of a vine, the barely-there expansion of a rock face.
    *   **Time:** The greatest shock. A falling water drop takes three seconds to sound. This is not a visual delay; it is a **temporal dilation**. Your actions will feel sluggish, then suddenly sharp. Practice slow-motion tasks. Stir a cup of tea for five minutes. Watch a pendulum swing and try to perceive each infinitesimal segment of its arc. Your surface-time is a frantic drumbeat here. You must learn to hear the grove's slow, seismic rhythm. The "collective holding of breath" is a **temporal anchor point**—a moment of universal stillness. Your entire preparation must be aimed at *feeling* that moment approach, and not breaking it.

4.  **Smell & Taste:**
    *   The air is a cocktail: petrichor (after-rain), honey (fermentation), iron (lifeblood), yeast (growth). These are not separate notes; they are one chord. Breathe deeply. Do not try to name them. Let the smell *coat* your receptors. The taste of the air settling into your lungs is the taste of the grove's metabolism. Do not fight the "sweetly decaying" note. It is the smell of recycling, of constant, gentle death feeding omnipresent life. It is not rot; it is **return**.

### **III. Mental & Philosophical Framing: Becoming a Mobile Cell**

You are not a visitor. You are a temporary, confused organ in a vast, slow body.

*   **Shed the "Observer" Ego:** You are not a scientist taking notes. You are a **sensation recorder**. Your presence is a disturbance. Your goal is to minimize that disturbance to the level of a passing insect.
*   **Understand the Rules:**
    *   **Nothing Dies:** Death is a process, not an event. A fallen creature is *immediately* a resource, a nutrient stream. There is no grief here, only integration. Your surface-mind will revolt at this. You must accept it. Your own body, should you perish here, will become part of the fungal carpet in moments.
    *   **Built = Seed:** The obsidian monolith is not a monument. It is a **seed of stillness**, a point of concentrated geology sinking back into the membrane. The woven arches are a **memory of structure** being slowly un-woven. They are not ruins; they are processes. Do not admire them as art. Witness them as *cycles*.
    *   **Weirdness = Local Physics:** The rock's breathing. The time-delayed sound. The dry-wet paradox. These are not magic. They are the **local expression of universal constants** under this membrane's unique pressure. Your task is to *feel* these expressions, not rationalize them.
*   **Movement Protocol:**
    *   Move when the hum is loud. Freeze when the collective stillness comes. It will come with no warning. Your instinct will be to snap a photo or note the anomaly. DO NOT. Become utterly, completely still. Let your breath match the held-breath of the ecosystem. In that moment, you are not a traveler. You are part of the grove. This is the only form of communication available.

### **IV. The Ingress Itself: A Ritual, Not a Walk**

1.  **The Threshold:** You will find a place where the world's skin is thin—a fissure, a sinkhole, a spot where the air tastes of wet stone. This is your point of ingress. Do not jump. Do not rush. **Settle.**
2.  **First Breath:** Do not gasp. The air does not *enter*; it *settles*. Take a slow, deliberate breath. Feel the warm, thick medium fill your lungs. Taste the iron and honey. This is your first act of integration.
3.  **First Step:** Barefoot. Place your sole on the nearest surface—likely the rubbery fungus. Feel its cool, yielding resistance. Do not look up immediately. Look at your feet. Read the terrain through your soles for the first ten minutes.
4.  **Finding Your Place:** You will feel a subtle pull—not a direction, but a *resonance*. A spot where the hum feels less like noise and more like a tone. A clearing between fungal thickets, near a warm, steaming "damp." That is your **anchor point**. Go there. Sit. Do not move for a full hour. Let your surface-mind panic and then tire. Let the grove's rhythm infiltrate your pulse.

### **V. The Departure: The Hardest Part**

You will want to leave the moment you feel the assimilation. The fear of becoming a permanent cell is overwhelming.

*   **Exit is a reversal of ingress, but harder.** You must *un-settle* yourself from the membrane. It will feel like tearing your soul from a warm womb.
*   **Do not run.** Run and you will trigger a panic in the ecosystem. The collective stillness may break in alarm. Move with the same slow, deliberate purpose you used to enter.
*   **At the Threshold:** Take one last, deep breath of the thick air. Seal it in your lungs as a souvenir. Then, step *out* into the surface world. The first lungful of thin, dry, *fast* air will feel like a shock—cold, sharp, and lonely.
*   **Aftermath:** For days, you will hear echoes. The three-second delay will haunt your perception. Normal sunlight will feel abrasive, *too fast*. You will crave the thick, sweet smell. This is **Grove-Sickness**. It is the price of the memory. You have not seen a place. You have *been* a process.
