# Preparation Protocol: Visiting the Sulphur Lowell  
*For the Ordinary Multiverse Traveler*  

**1. Mindset & Psychological Conditioning**  
- **Abandon Natural Expectations:** You are not visiting a landscape; you are entering a functioning, eternal factory. There is no “beauty” in the romantic sense, no “wilderness,” no cycles of day/night or seasons. Time is measured in production shifts and maintenance cycles. Prepare for sensory monotony punctuated by violent, random noise.  
- **Accept Functional totality:** Every feature—pools, “forests,” cliffs—exists for a purpose. Do not look for meaning beyond utility. If something seems pointless, it is either obsolete or part of a process you cannot perceive.  
- **Desensitize to scale and horror:** The sheer, mind-bending expanse of identical, soul-crushing industry will induce existential dread. Meditate on the concept of infinite repetition. Practice focusing on immediate, tactical details (e.g., “the pipe to my left is leaking blue sludge at 3 liters/minute”) to avoid psychic overload.  

**2. Respiratory & Chemical Protection**  
- **Primary:** A fully sealed, positive-pressure environmental suit with **triple-stage filtration**:  
  1. Particulate (polymer dust, grit, slag).  
  2. Chemical (ozone, sulphur compounds, heavy metal vapors, cyanide traces from almond-scented runoff).  
  3. Radioactive (if applicable—some zones may have residual activation from furnace isotopes).  
- **Backup:** A week’s supply of portable scrubber canisters. Never rely on local “air.”  
- **Note:** The “metallic tang” is itself a neurotoxin at prolonged exposure. Suit must have internal air quality monitors with audible/visual alarms.  

**3. Thermoregulation & Insulation**  
- **The cold is conductive, not convective.** Standard insulation fails. You need:  
  - **Layered reflective barriers** (like space-grade Mylar) next to skin to radiantly block heat suction.  
  - **Active heating elements** in gloves, boots, and core—powered by long-life batteries (fuel cells preferred; combustion adds CO₂ to suit atmosphere).  
  - **Outer shell:** Abrasion-resistant, waterproof, **non-metallic** (to avoid conducting cold from touching structures). Ceramic-reinforced polymer.  
- **Wetness management:** Condensation inside the suit is lethal. Suit must have active dehumidification. Evaporative cooling from the ambient “wet” will steal body heat rapidly.  

**4. Navigation & Orientation**  
- **GPS is useless** (gravity anomalies, metallic interference).  
- **Primary system:** Inertial navigation with **mechanical gyroscopes** (solid-state may glitch in EM fields).  
- **Secondary:** Dead-reckoning using pre-mapped **infrastructure signatures**—track magnetic fields of crushers, radio signatures of ventilation fans, thermal gradients of sludge channels.  
- **Landmarks are functional, not geographical:** “Navigate to the west bank of the blue sludge canal, then north until you hear the 7Hz sub-audible thrum of Press-4.”  
- **Carry physical mapping tools:** Seismic paper stylus, non-digital compass (may drift), altimeter (barometric, not GPS).  

**5. Mobility & Terrain Adaptation**  
- **Footwear:** Steel-free, deep-grip soles for slag gravel and viscous polymer patches. Hydrophobic seals to prevent “weeping” water ingress.  
- **Handholds:** Many surfaces are sharp, oily, or thermally unstable. Gloves must be cut-resistant and heat-shielded.  
- **Verticality:** Cliff faces are young and collapsing. Do not approach erosion cliffs. Use only designated walkways ( grated steel, often corroded).  
- **Flood risk:** “Wet lowlands” can rise rapidly from pipe bursts or channel shifts. Always know the highest ground in your sector—usually unused slag heaps, but these can be unstable.  

**6. Communication & Redundancy**  
- **Radio may be dead zones or jammed by machinery.**  
- **Primary:** hardwired tether to base camp if possible.  
- **Secondary:** Low-frequency acoustic communicators (sound travels strangely in mist).  
- **Emergency:** Pre-arranged **flash-and-sound signals**:  
  - 3 green flares = emergency extraction.  
  - 5 short horn blasts = structural collapse imminent.  
- **Log everything in waterproof paper journal.** Digital corruption is likely.  

**7. Interaction with “Ecology”**  
- **Fauna:** All local life is either toxic, parasitic, or mechanical.  
  - **Blind rodents:** Carry pathogens from chemical gutters. Do not touch.  
  - **Dragonfly drones:** They are surveillance or maintenance units. Do not interfere—they may report you.  
  - **Slime-mold consciousness:** Hypothetical. Assume it is a data-hacking biohazard. Avoid sumps.  
- **Flora:** “Cable-moss” can short-circuit suit electronics. Do not let tendrils contact ports. “Phototrophic lichens” may excrete corrosive pigments.  

**8. Dealing with “Weirdness”**  
- **Gravity fluctuations:** Carry **immediate fall response protocol**: If you feel weight shift, crouch, spread weight, and reach for nearest stable structure (pipe, railing). Have a **grab-line** with a carabiner always clipped to your suit.  
- **Time perception shifts:** Wear a waterproof **mechanical chronometer** and check it religiously. Do not trust “feeling” of time. Shift changes are marked by **synchronized horn blasts**—use these to reset your internal clock.  
- **Light flashes (arc furnaces):** These are momentary “days.” Do not look directly. Your visor must have **instant darkening filter** (like welding lens).  

**9. Supplies & Sustainability**  
- **Water:** Bring all hydration. Local water is industrial effluent—heavy with dissolved metals, likely boiling warm from subsurface processes.  
- **Food:** High-calorie, no-prep ration bars. No foraging.  
- **Waste:** Your suit must have **closed-cycle waste processing**. Any discharge into environment may be considered pollution and trigger automated defenses (e.g., high-pressure steam jets).  
- **Power:** Solar is useless under permanent twilight. Carry **hand-crank generators** for emergency battery top-up.  

**10. Exit Strategy & Emergency**  
- **Never travel alone.** Minimum team of 3, always in visual/audio contact.  
- **Check-in intervals:** Every 30 minutes by acoustic signal. No response = immediate retreat to last known safe point.  
- **Safe points:** Pre-identified **cooling tower bases** (structurally stable, often have backup power and emergency oxygen). These are your bunkers.  
- **If suit compromised:** Activate **emergency seal foam** from belt pack, then trigger **personal homing beacon** (broadcasts on a frequency known only to your extraction team). Do not move—wait for rescue in place.  
- **Extraction:** Only via **elevator shafts** to pre-industrial surface (if you have access) or via **airship pickup** in designated “plazas” (vast empty zones where cranes don’t swing). No extraction from active zones.  

**11. Mental Last Resort**  
- **You are an anthropologist of horror.** Your goal is to document, not understand.  
- **If you start seeing patterns in noise, or feel the “heartbeat” of the place,** terminate mission. That is the **bio-psychic pressure** of the Sulphur Lowell—it erodes sanity by simulating purpose.  
- **Remember:** This is not a living world. It is a **ghost of industry**, manufacturing its own eternity. You are a temporary anomaly in its logic. Your survival depends on being **quieter and more replaceable than a single bolt** in its machinery.  

**Final Checklist:**  
- [ ] Suit seal-check (positive pressure for 10 min)  
- [ ] Gyro calibration  
- [ ] 3-day oxygen supply (plus 1 day reserve)  
- [ ] Paper maps, stylus, waterproof bag  
- [ ] Emergency homing beacon (tested)  
- [ ] Team communication plan confirmed  
- [ ] Psychological “anchor” item (photo of natural landscape, etc.)  

**You do not conquer the Sulphur Lowell. You endure it. You leave before it incorporates you into its process.**
