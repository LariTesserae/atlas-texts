### **I. The Mental & Existential preparation (The Most Critical Phase)**

This is a place that will aggressively challenge your categories. Preparation is less about learning facts and more about *unlearning*.

1.  **Dissolve the "Urban" Paradigm:** You are not visiting a city. You are visiting a **sentient stomach, a slow-beating heart, a continent-sized mycelial network that has mistaken itself for a civilization.** There are no "residents," no "owners," no "workers." There is only the organism and its processes. Your human instinct to seek social cues, hierarchies, or "people" will lead you into profound loneliness and existential error. Do not expect to meet anyone. Expect to *be metabolized* by the environment's awareness.

2.  **Re-Calibrate Your Sense of Time:** The Front operates on **cyclical, metabolic time**, not linear, event-based time. Its "day" is a pulse of expansion/contraction. Its "year" is the slow creep of a vine. Your internal clock (heartbeat, breathing, impatience) is a frantic, disruptive drumbeat in this symphony of glacial chemistry. Practice meditative stillness. Your goal is to sync your perception to the **hiss-drip-groan** rhythm. An hour of watching a single condensation bead trace a path down a chitinous leaf is a valid and complete activity.

3.  **Reframe "Danger":** There are no predators. There is no malice. The danger is **passive integration**. You are a foreign enzyme. The risk is not being attacked, but being:
    *   **Chemically absorbed:** Your sweat, oils, and shed skin cells are nutrients. Your synthetic clothing is an indigestible irritant.
    *   **Spatially disoriented:** The lack of human-scale landmarks (doors, windows, signs) and the sameness of the damp, green-tinged light will scramble your navigation. You will feel tiny and scale-less.
    *   **Ontologically overwhelmed:** The constant, low-grade feeling of **being watched by the place itself**—a diffuse, chemical awareness—can induce panic or a dissociative state. This is the organism's baseline state of being. You must learn to bear it.

4.  **Study the "Negotiation":** The Front's weirdness is the tension between **genetic growth (chaos)** and **functional organization (order)**. A "street" is a growth pattern that happens to be traversable. A "plaza" is a basin that efficiently collects slurry. Your movement is a temporary perturbation in this negotiation. You are not a customer; you are a **footnote in the organism's log**.

### **II. Physical & Gear Preparation**

Your gear must be an extension of your non-interference policy. **Synthetics are anethema.** They do not breathe, they do not degrade, they are plastic islands in a organic sea.

*   **Clothing:**
    *   **Material:** Linen, organic (non-treated) cotton, raw silk, or finely woven bamboo. These will absorb moisture, will slowly biodegrade if you shed them, and do not create static.
    *   **Color:** Earth tones—moss green, clay brown, slate gray. Avoid bright colors, reflective surfaces, or anything that visually "pops." You are a smudge in the landscape.
    *   **Layering:** The air is cool and damp. A light, breathable wool or cashmere layer is ideal (it regulates temperature even when damp). No waterproof shells (they trap your sweat, creating a hostile microclimate you carry with you).
    *   **Footwear:** Soft-soled, minimal shoes. Thin-soled moccasins or barefoot shoes. You need to feel the damp, giving texture of the lichen paths. Heavy boots are a battering ram to the sensitive surface.

*   **Carrying:**
    *   A simple, unlined satchel or backpack made of natural fiber canvas or leather. No internal plastic compartments. Everything inside will be damp within minutes.
    *   **Containers:** Un glazed ceramic or seasoned wood. No Tupperware, no ziplocks. Glass is acceptable but fragile and *loud* if dropped.

*   **Essentials:**
    *   **Water:** *Do not drink from the Front's ponds or slurry.* Bring your own water in a ceramic or metal bottle. The Front's water is a bioactive, mineral-rich tea. It may contain compounds that alter human physiology or simply taste of deep, slow decay.
    *   **Food:** High-calorie, dense bars of nuts and honey, wrapped in beeswax paper. Eating will be a rare, deliberate ritual. Do not leave crumbs.
    *   **Medicine:** A minimal first-aid kit for cuts (the environment is biologically active but not septic; infection risk is low, but your open wound is a beacon). Antihistamines in case you react to spore clouds. **Do not bring antibiotics**—you do not want to be a source of disruptive, human-scale biocides.
    *   **Light:** A single, dim, **red-light headlamp**. The Front is in a state of perpetual twilight. A bright white light is a violent intrusion, like stadium lights in a forest. Red light is the least disruptive. Use it only when absolutely necessary.
    *   **Tools:** A small, **non-metallic scribe** (bone, wood) and paper made from natural fibers for notes. A simple, **brass or copper whistle** (not plastic) for emergency signaling—a short, clear tone is less disruptive than shouting. **No knives.** You have no need to cut anything. The act of cutting is a declaration of separation the Front will not understand.
    *   **Electronics:** **Forbidden.** The copper filaments pulse with a faint, organic current. A phone, camera, or radio is a screaming electrical parasite. It will feel like a drill in your teeth and may disrupt local functions. Your documentation must be analog.

### **III. On-Site Conduct & Survival Protocols**

1.  **Movement as Breathing:** Move when the Front exhales. Stillness during the slow contraction. Your steps should be soft, deliberate, and in rhythm with the walls' pulse. Do not rush. Do not explore "off the path." The "paths" are the organism's consent for your perturbation. Straying is an act of consumption—you are literally eating your way into its structure.

2.  **The Law of Non-Insertion:** **Do not touch.** Do not lean on walls. Do not pick up the golden sap pods. Do not scrape the verdigris from the valves. Your skin oils, temperature, and acids are contaminants. You are a **spectator**, not a participant. Observation is through the lens of air and light only.

3.  **Waste & Traces:** **You will leave no trace.** Literally. All human waste (including breath moisture, sweat, shed skin cells) is now part of the Front's chemistry. That is the point. But *pack out* anything that is not you or your purely organic gear: a bit of plastic from a label, a metal buckle, a synthetic thread. Your goal is to depart as cleanly as you arrived, metabolically speaking. The only thing you take is the memory and your analog notes.

4.  **Interaction with "Fauna":** Ignore the snails and the walking dewdrops. They are autonomic functions, not creatures. To study them is to study a liver cell. Your presence will stress them. Do not block their paths. The dewdrop thing dissolved because you presented a novel, complex chemical signature (human scent, heat, carbon dioxide) it could not classify. It retreated by dissolving into the ambient mist—a form of passive panic. If an organism reacts to you, **freeze and slowly retreat**.

5.  **Sleeping:** Find a smooth, concave "plaza" basin away from driplines and major structural seams. Lie on your unlined wool or linen wrap. Do not build a fire. The cold is a nutrient for the Front. Wrap yourself in its damp silence. Sleep will be a shallow, permeable state. You will dream in green-tinged pulses.

6.  **The Exit Protocol:** Your departure is the most important act of respect. **Do not say goodbye.** There is no one to say it to. Simply turn and walk back along the exact path you came, reversing your perturbation. Do not look back. Looking back is a human sentimental act; the organism does not remember you as an individual, only as a temporary chemical event. Re-entering the wet grassland should feel like surfacing from a dream you were never truly in.
