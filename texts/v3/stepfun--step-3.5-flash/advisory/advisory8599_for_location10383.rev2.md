### **I. Mindset & Psychological Conditioning (The Most Critical Layer)**
This is a place that will trigger existential and spatial dysphoria. Your brain, wired for agency, narrative, and fauna, will protest.

1.  **Reframe "Urban" and "Inhabited":** Do not expect streets, doors, or citizens. You are walking through a **geological hypothesis made manifest**. Every "plaza" is a coincidence of polish. Every "amphitheater" is an accident of slope. You must learn to see intentionality in pure process and find awe in absolute absence. Meditate on the concept of **"architecture without architect."**
2.  **Embrace the Non-Rhythmic:** Your body and mind crave pattern—bird song, wind gusts, your own footsteps. Here, the primary soundscape is *stochastic* (random drips, unpredictable seeps). You must practice finding peace in the lack of meter. Listen for the *texture* of silence, not its structure.
3.  **Prepare for the "Watched" Feeling:** The landscape's form is uncannily familiar. Your parietal lobe will scream "CITY!" while your logic knows it's stone. This creates a profound cognitive dissonance, a feeling of being observed by the geometry itself. This is normal. Do not fight it. Acknowledge it: *"The stone is not looking. It is simply being, and its being resembles my knowing."*
4.  **Set a Temporal Frame:** You are a brief, warm, noisy blip in a system that measures time in millennia. Your visit should be measured in hours, not days. The goal is witness, not integration. Decide your duration in advance. Lingering too long risks a dissolution of self, a merging with the "density of history" that can be unsettling.

### **II. Sensory & Physical Preparation**
Your ordinary human senses are not optimized for this. You must augment and protect them.

1.  **Footwear is Paramount:** The ground is a complex, treacherous sponge.
    *   **Boots:** Waterproof, with excellent grip on slick, polished stone. High ankle support for unstable crusts.
    *   **Socks:** Merino wool (wicks, insulates when damp). Carry spares. Your feet will be *constantly* cool and damp.
    *   **Gaiters:** To keep the fine, dark silt from pouring into your boots with every step.
2.  **Clothing System:**
    *   **Base Layer:** Synthetic or wool, wicking. Cotton will chill you.
    *   **Mid Layer:** Insulating fleece or down (the altitude and dampness create a penetrating chill).
    *   **Outer Layer:** A breathable, waterproof shell. The mist and weeping will condense on everything.
    *   **Crucially:** Multiple pairs of gloves. Your hands will brush against wet, cold stone. Nitrile gloves under wool ones prevent direct, chilling contact and protect from mineral stains.
3.  **Vision Management:**
    *   **Eyewear:** Clear, anti-fog lenses are a must. The persistent mist will fog ordinary glasses. Consider a visor or hat with a brim to keep moisture off lenses.
    *   **The Glass Mantis Hazard:** Carry a simple, tangible **tactile anchor**—a smooth, warm stone in your pocket, a specific texture on your glove. If you find yourself staring into a pool and losing the boundary between you and the reflection, *touch your anchor*. Focus on the dry, known warmth of your own skin against the stone. Do not gaze into the mirrors for more than a few seconds at a time.
4.  **Navigation & Documentation:**
    *   **Compass:** May be unreliable if the basin's stone has magnetic properties. Rely on it, but verify with landmarks.
    *   **GPS/Signal:** Assume none. The basin's shape and stone may interfere. Download topographic maps beforehand, but understand they will not capture the "urban" forms.
    *   **Field Journal & Pencil:** Not pen (moisture). Use a robust, weatherproof notebook. Sketch the "buildings." Note the direction of polish, the pattern of seeps. Your sketches will be your most accurate map, as the place subtly shifts.
    *   **Camera:** Waterproof housing is non-negotiable. Use a polarizing filter to cut through glare on wet stone. **Do not use flash.** You are a guest in a silent, dim world; a flash is a violent intrusion. Use long exposures and available light.
5.  **Provisioning:**
    *   **Water:** Do not drink from the seeps. The mineral content is unknown and likely extreme. Carry all your water. The dampness will make you thirstier than you expect.
    *   **Food:** High-calorie, easy-to-eat bars. You won't have an appetite, but you'll need energy.
    *   **Thermos:** For a hot drink. The act of holding warmth, tasting something *dry* and *strong* (like black tea or coffee), will be a profound sensory anchor against the pervasive wetness.
    *   **First-Aid:** Blister treatment is critical. Hypothermia gear (emergency blanket) is wise.

### **III. Protocol for Visiting (The "How-To")**
1.  **Enter Lightly:** Move slowly. Your footsteps are cannonades here. Test each step. Learn to walk on the crusts without breaking them. This is your primary physical skill.
2.  **Follow the Water:** The wetness is the lifeblood and the guide. The most interesting forms, the deepest "streets," the clearest "plazas" will be where the seepage is most consistent. Let the water lead you.
3.  **Listen for the Tectonic Sigh:** That deep vibration is your metronome. It happens on no schedule. When you feel it through your soles, pause. Be still. Feel the basin breathe. Let it reset your internal clock.
4.  **Vertical Awareness:** Constantly check your vertical field of view. The "density" you feel is in the cracks, the chutes, the layered grooves at your feet. The "emptiness" is in the vast, separated forms on the basin wall. Hold both perceptions simultaneously. This is the core visual paradox.
5.  **Do Not Collect:** Do not break off a piece of crystalline lace. Do not pocket a "polished stone." It is not a souvenir; it is a citizen. Taking it is an act of violence you will feel instantly as a wrongness, a sudden silence where a drip was. You are a visitor, not a miner.
6.  **The Exit Ritual:** Before you leave your camp or resting spot, perform a deliberate check. Ensure nothing has been left, nothing has been taken. Straighten any displaced crust. Speak aloud, softly, a simple phrase of departure—not to the place, but to *yourself*. "I was here. I leave as I came." This ritual helps your mind transition back to a world of cause, effect, and noise.
