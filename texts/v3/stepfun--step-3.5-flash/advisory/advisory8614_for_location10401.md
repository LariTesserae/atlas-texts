### Pre-Trip Preparation for the Verdant Under-Crypt
This is not a forest hike, a cave dive, or any other familiar outdoor excursion. You are visiting a single infant planetary organism that has merged rock, water, and life into one seamless system, and treating it like a standard tourist destination will get you hurt, lost, or stuck. First, adjust your mindset: you are a guest in a living body, not an explorer conquering a landscape. Everything below is tailored for an ordinary multiverse traveler—no military-grade gear, no biotech mods, just the standard kit and know-how most casual dimension-hoppers carry.
---
#### First: Medical & Physical Prep (1 Week Before Travel)
1. **Allergy & pathogen prophylaxis first.** The air is thick with coin-sized photosynthetic spores, motile plant seeds, fungal filaments, and microscopic biota unlike anything on Earth. Even if you have no usual plant allergies, this biosphere will trigger reactions. Get a custom broad-spectrum antihistamine, anti-fungal, and anti-parasitic prescription from your multiverse clinic, and ask them to whip up a quick spore-reactive serum from the tiny sample of ambient biomatter you can skim off the transit threshold before you fully submerge. Stash enzyme neutralizers for snap-vine digestive juice in an easy-access pocket—their strikes are the only fast thing in this world, and even a small injection will start eating through muscle if untreated in 10 minutes.
2. **Dental and back checkup.** The omnipresent subsonic hum is felt in your molars and the base of your spine, not heard. Constant low-frequency vibration will aggravate loose fillings, cavities, TMJ, or old back injuries, and can cause migraines or disorientation if you’re inside for more than a few hours. Get any dental work fixed before you go, and bring a cheap sports jaw guard and a thin back brace if you have prior issues.
3. **Avoid any cybernetic or neural implants.** The hum is the mycelial network’s data stream, and it will fry unshielded neural links, cause seizures, or corrupt your travel implant’s coordinates. Turn off all internal comms, jump chips, and AR overlays before you cross the threshold—use physical, sealed gear only.
---
#### Second: Gear & Supply Prep
Prioritize waterproofing, spore protection, and non-interference above all else. The air is 100% humid, the ground is a near-liquid living mattress, and even the smallest piece of plastic or synthetic waste will disrupt the infant biosphere.
1. **Protective layers:** Ditch cotton entirely—it stays wet and freezes instantly in the 0°C constant chill. Wear a tight-weave, waterproof, tear-resistant drysuit (no fuzzy outer fabrics, which trap spores and moss filaments) over moisture-wicking base layers. Add waterproof, puncture-resistant mid-cut boots with grippy tread (the “rock” surfaces are slick with constant condensation) and sealed gloves. Cover every inch of skin: a hood tucked into your suit collar, anti-fog sealed goggles, and a full-face respirator with HEPA-grade filters coated in mild anti-fungal agent (the coin-sized spores will clog standard N95s in minutes). Use a low-wattage red headlamp only—bright white light attracts spore kites and disrupts photosynthetic spores.
2. **Subsonic gear:** Stash small, portable subsonic dampeners (standard issue for travelers heading to weird-physics worlds) in your suit pockets to cut the worst of the chest/molar vibration. Keep standard foam earplugs as a backup for transit.
3. **Navigation & safety:** Bring a sealed, waterproof bio-signature tracker that maps the unique mycelial network signature of the crypt, so you can retrace your steps and find your entry transit point. Pack a small quantity of biodegradable, spore-reactive UV dye to mark your path (it glows under your goggles’ low-light setting and breaks down in 72 hours so you leave no trace) and a collapsible probe to test ground firmness before stepping—several meters of moss cover soft, warm composting layers that can trap you or release toxic gases if you sink through. Bring a portable motion sensor tuned to fast movement (the only fast thing in the crypt is snap-vine strikes, which move too quick for the human eye to track) and a fully charged, waterproof transit beacon with a 24-hour timer lock (set it for your planned exit time, so you don’t accidentally jump out early or get stuck if your stabilizer glitches from the shifting local physics).
4. **Supplies:** Pack 24 hours of high-calorie, low-waste food (no crumbs, which will attract fauna and disrupt nutrient cycles) and a portable water filter rated for fungal and microbial life (the ambient water is part of the global aquifer and may carry local pathogens). Bring a waterproof notebook or sealed digital log for notes, spare batteries kept in an inner pocket close to your body (the cold drains power fast), and a small, sealable hazmat bag for all waste.
---
#### Third: In-Crypt Protocol (Once You Submerge)
1. **Don’t rush, don’t touch, don’t interfere.** The world is growing in real time—vaults are still soft, root pillars are still crystallizing, and every surface is part of the planetary organism. Step lightly, test the ground with your probe before putting full weight on any surface, and never touch walls, ceilings, or plant structures: snap-vines grow out of porous surfaces, and even brushing a walking lichen or spore kite with your suit can damage their delicate structures. Avoid areas with dense spore kite swarms—they cluster around warm decay piles, which also attract more snap-vines.
2. **Keep noise to a minimum.** The hum is the planet’s breath; loud sounds or shouting will disrupt the mycelial network’s slow coordination of growth. Don’t use your voice unless absolutely necessary.
3. **Stick to a short first trip.** Set your transit timer for 6 hours maximum for your first visit. The slow, enclosed space and constant hum can cause disorientation or time dilation faster than you expect, and you need to learn how your body reacts to the subsonic vibration and spore exposure before staying longer. Check in with your home base every 2 hours via your sealed comms.
4. **If you’re attacked by a snap-vine:** Freeze first, then move laterally as fast as you can. Their strikes are hydraulic and only reach a meter or two from their anchor point on the wall/ceiling. If you do get hit, inject the enzyme neutralizer immediately, apply pressure to the wound, and activate your transit beacon to call for extraction—don’t try to walk back to the exit on your own, as the venom can cause paralysis in 30 minutes.
---
#### Post-Trip Decon (Non-Negotiable)
Before you leave the transit zone, strip off your suit and seal it in the hazmat bag to avoid carrying any local spores, fungi, or microorganisms back to your home world—an invasive crypt spore could wipe out entire ecosystems if released. Shower immediately with anti-fungal soap, swap all your clothes for sealed storage, and get a full medical checkup within 24 hours to test for any delayed spore or enzyme exposure. Log all your observations (the crypt is one of the rarest known biospheres, still in its infancy, and your notes will be invaluable to xenobiologists across the multiverse) before you jump to your next destination.
Do not treat this like a sightseeing trip. You are stepping inside a dreaming, growing world—leave no trace, do no harm, and it will not harm you.
