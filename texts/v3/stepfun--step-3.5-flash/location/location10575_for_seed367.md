You find it not by searching, but by being found. The entrance is a crack in the side of a mesa, no wider than your shoulders, that breathes a constant, humid warmth. Inside, the world inverts.

**The Place:** The Riven Bowl. A single, continent-sized cavern system collapsed into a pocket of eternity. You stand on a floor of **black, glassy obsidian**, worn smooth as a river stone by **erosion so complete** it has erased all but the most stubborn topography. The Bowl is a vast, hemispherical chamber, perhaps a kilometer across at its widest, its ceiling a dangling forest of **stone draperies and stalactites** so ancient they have fused into a single, porous ceiling lattice. The light is **dim**, a perpetual blue-grey twilight, sourceless but for the **lush vegetation**.

**Terrain & Materials:** The glass floor is scored with a million cart tracks—not of wheels, but of hooves, claws, and dragging wooden sleds. These are the only marks upon the stone, a palimpsest of habitation. **Verticality is everything.** The Bowl’s ecology climbs the walls, which are not sheer but layered like a soggy lasagna: bands of **wet, ochre clay**, shelves of **crystalline salt** (the only mineral precipitate in this waterless chemistry), and vast, **spongy mats of a leathery moss** that drinks the air’s humidity. The “urban” element is **grown, not built**. Settlements are **wattle-and-daub**, **woven from the fibrous stalks of giant ferns**, and **mortared with a paste of clay and beetle-shell lime**. Huts cling to rock shelves, linked by **rope bridges and spiral staircases carved into the soft clay layers**. There are no roads, only the churned paths.

**Atmosphere & Chemistry:** The air is **warm**, thick, and **dense** with moisture and smells. **No water falls or pools**, but a constant **geothermic seep** from the Bowl’s base mists the lower levels. The vegetation is **lush** because it is **chemosynthetic and atmospheric**. The dominant flora is the **“Breath-Fern”**—a towering, trunk-less mass of iridescent blue fronds that harvest dissolved minerals and trace gases directly from the mist, excreting oxygen in a slow, sighing release. Below them, **carpet-moss** glows with a faint, sourceless bioluminescence at night, and **salt-vines** crystallize the air’s minerals into edible, salty nodules.

**Sound:** The soundscape is **loud and layered**. A base hum—the **drone of a million tiny wings**: the **“Moths”** (pea-sized, metallic pollinators that flit through the mist). Over that, the **click and scrape of chitin**: the overwhelming **fauna**. The dominant sound is the **herd-thrum** of the **“Glass-Backs”**—herbivorous quadrupeds with backs of polished obsidian they use to thermoregulate, their hooves clicking on stone in herds of thousands. Predator sounds are discrete: the **scream of the “Sky-Rat”** (a gliding, six-limbed hunter), the **crunch of a “Mantis-Crab”** ambush from the salt shelves.

**Ecology & Overwhelming Fauna:** This is a **high-elevation, closed-system pressure cooker**. **Fauna density is 2.7/3—overwhelming.** Life is pressed into every cranny. **Insectoids dominate**: tunneling **“Drill-Worms”** that soften clay, **“Lace-Wings”** that filter-feed on the mist. Larger animals are specialized: the Glass-Backs migrate in slow, dusty clouds from the salt shelves to the breath-fern groves. Everything is a niche. **Scavenger “Gleaners”** (furred, many-legged) clean carcasses in minutes. The **“Kind”**—a small, tool-using primate with forward-facing eyes—are the only builders, and their population is dense, living in family clusters on mid-level shelves, farming moss and herding juvenile Glass-Backs.

**Rules & Weirdness (0.5/3 — Normal, but Intense):** The rules are physics, but compressed. **No sun, no moon.** Time is measured in breath-fern growth rings and herd migrations. The **only “weather”** is the **“Mist-Tide”**—a twice-daily thickening and thinning of the geothermic vapor, which triggers the Moths’ swarming and the Glass-Backs’ movement. **Death is recycled instantly**: a fallen creature is buried by gleaners, its nutrients absorbed by moss, which is then cropped by a herd. Nothing goes to waste. **Primitive tech (0.6/3)** is everywhere: flaked obsidian knives, drills of bent fern-wood, baskets woven from moss fibers. They harvest salt, mine soft clay, and trap moths for protein. There is no metal, no writing, only oral tradition and the physical record of the paths.

**The Emergent Feeling:** It is not a desert of dryness, but a **desert of form**. A single, stable, ancient geological bubble where life has raged in a **closed, dense, and deafening loop** for time out of mind. The **ancient erosion** has flattened everything into a smooth bowl, but life has re-textured it with a thousand frantic layers of grazing, building, hunting, and dying. The **scale is hidden**—you could walk the perimeter in a day, but the social and ecological maps are infinite. You are not in a cavern; you are **inside the fossilized heart of a world**, and it is beating with the thunder of a thousand thousand hooves on glass.
