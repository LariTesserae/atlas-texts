You find the fissure by accident—a crack in the old sinter terrace no wider than your shoulders, breathing a wet, warm sigh. You wedge yourself in, and the world turns inside out.

**The Place Itself: The Gilded Gut**

You are not in a cave, not exactly. You are inside a **single, gigantic, organ-like chamber** no larger than a modest hall, its every surface **alive and vegetal yet hollow**. The “walls,” “floor,” and “ceiling” are a **continuous membrane of living plant-flesh**, a symbiotic entity that has grown to *be* the architecture. It is a **total-vegetation** ecosystem, but scaled to this hidden pocket: the density is **0.1/3—empty** because the plant-matter is **porous, intercellular, almost foam-like**. You can see through thin membranes to darker, wetter chambers beyond, and the vast space between the structural strands feels like air, yet everything is plant.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The floor is a **spongy, springy mat** of root-hair and moss-like filaments, damp beneath your feet. It gives way in places to **pools of condensation-water**, clear and mineral-tasting, collected in natural cisterns carved by slow drip. The “rock” you might expect is absent; instead, the chamber’s form is held by **shafts of hardened, resinous sap-wood** and **tension cables of living vine**, glowing with a faint inner sap-light. **Erosion is 1.0/3—young and sharp**: the edges are cristalline, the surfaces unweathered, as if the chamber *recently finished growing*. You see no tool marks, only the **recent, fresh scars of living tissue splitting and re-knitting**.

**Atmosphere & Light:**
**Light is 2.3/3—normal**, but it behaves strangely. Sunlight from the fissure entrance filters through translucent membranes, **fracturing into shifting, solid-looking planes of gold and green** that hang in the humid air. There is no obvious source—the plant-membrane itself seems to both absorb and emit a **soft, photosynthetic glow** from within, especially in the darker nooks. The air is **warm (1.9/3)**, thick with the smell of wet chlorophyll, loam, and a sweet, fermenting sap. It feels **damp (0.9/3)** not as rain, but as a constant, gentle exhalation of moisture from the walls.

**Sound:**
**Sound is 2.2/3—loud**, but it’s a resonant, organic cacophony. The primary sound is **a deep, harmonic dripping**, not of water on stone, but of sap into sap, a slow *glug-glug-thrum* that pulses through the living walls. This is overlaid by: the **hiss of rapid growth**—you can sometimes hear a vine coil or a leaf unfold, a sound like velcro tearing softly; the **chitter of micro-fauna** (see below) in the porous walls; and a **subsonic hum**, a vibration in your molars you feel more than hear, the chamber’s own metabolic rhythm.

**Ecology & Weirdness (2.9/3—Reality Breakdown):**
This is the core. The **weirdness is not an overlay; it is the rule**.
*   **Physics Defiance:** “Down” is optional. Water climbs along membranes against gravity in shimmering filaments. Seeds sprout mid-air, roots questing downward and upward simultaneously. Small pebbles and your dropped tools sometimes *drift* slowly toward a wall or pool as if magnetized by the plant’s bio-electric field.
*   **Plant-as-Reality:** The vegetation isn’t just *in* the space; it *defines* the space. If a thick vine-nerve is severed, the chamber doesn’t just leak—the **local geometry softens and wavers** for a moment before the wound seals with a flash of hardened latex. Steps in the spongy floor can feel unstable because the ground is literally *reconfiguring* as micro-rootlets shift.
*   **Parasitic Light:** The “normal” light has substance. You can sometimes see tiny, blind **water-skimmers** walking on the *surface of a beam of light* as if it were water, disturbing it with ripples.
*   **Temporal Stutter:** Growth spurts are visible. You might blink and see a new, translucent bud the size of your fist hanging where empty air was a moment before. Conversely, some patches of wall seem to *fade* into a more primitive, lichen-like state, then re-complexify.

**Fauna (0.6/3—Rare):**
Life is sparse, but adapted. You see:
*   **Glint-moths**: Paper-winged insects with crystaline eyes, they feed on the sap-glows and are utterly silent. Their wings don’t flap; they *shimmer* into the next position.
*   **Siphon-snails**: Transparent, footless snails that move by capillary action through the plant-pores, leaving faint, glistening trails that evaporate in seconds.
*   **The Drowner**: The only large fauna. A semi-aquatic, limbless predator, the size of a large eel, with a mouth like a suction cup of soft spines. It lives in the deeper condensation pools, sensing vibrations through the walls. Its skin pulses with the same slow light as the chamber. It is not hostile unless provoked, but its movement is a silent, wet *unfolding* through the root-foam.

**Rules of This Pocket-World:**
1.  **Growth is Primary Law:** The chamber *wants* to fill. Left alone, it would eventually seal the fissure and become a perfect, silent, living geode. Your presence is a temporary disturbance, a source of CO2 and heat it metabolizes.
2.  **Material is Mutable:** Under extreme stress or curiosity, the plant-matter can be persuaded (very slowly) to part like flesh or harden like wood. Cutting it causes immediate, intelligent sealing and a drop in local light.
3.  **Sound is Structure:** The loud, harmonic dripping isn’t just noise; it’s a **maintenance rhythm**. If the sound stops or changes pitch, a section of wall may begin to sag or a pool to drain.
4.  **The Weirdness is Contagious:** Tools left too long begin to grow faint, root-like filaments. Food spoils by *photosynthesizing* and turning into a bitter, green pulp. Your own shadow, in certain lights, may detach and cling to a wall for a few seconds.

**The Human Touch (Tech 1.2/3—Primitive):**
There are no built structures, but evidence of **very recent, primitive exploration** is here: a few **carved tally marks** on a sap-wood pillar (deep, made with a sharp flake of obsidian); a **snagged yarn tether** from a climbing harness; a **small, hand-polished stone bowl** holding collected condensation, placed deliberately on a natural ledge. These feel like sacraments, not conquests. The explorer who left them understood this place *responds*. The marks may have been an attempt to map the shifting geometry; the bowl, an offering to the dripping rhythm.

**The Feeling:**
This is a **hidden (0.5/3), intimate room** within a world that is both a living body and a geological feature. It’s **active (2.3/3)** on a biological clock—you see change in the span of an hour. The **emptiness (0.1/3)** is profound: for all the life, you are alone with your breathing and the chamber’s song. There is no predator-prey chase, only the slow, resonant metabolism of a place that is **more plant than earth, more dream than fact**. It is beautiful, terrifying, and utterly **itself**—a world where the boundary between organism and environment has dissolved, leaving only a damp, glimmering, humming *process* wearing the shape of a room. You are not an observer in a landscape; you are a cell in a momentary thought of the Gilded Gut.
