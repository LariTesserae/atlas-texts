The place is called, by those who name things, **The Hollowing**.

It is a basin, impossibly old, sunk deep into a plateau of black volcanic glass. The scale is open: you stand on a gentle, mud-slick slope and see nothing but the curve of the basin in every direction, its edges lost in a perpetual, steam-thick haze. The elevation is low—you are in the bowl, not the rim. The air is cool, heavy with the smell of wet stone, alkaloid muck, and a sweet, metallic tang like old blood. It is dim. The sun, when it shows itself through the layered, amber-gray clouds, is a dull coin, its light diffused and swallowed by the atmosphere’s constant, low condensing mist. Primary illumination comes from the basin itself: a soft, sourceless bioluminescence radiating from the water and the strange, soft forms that live within it.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The ground is not earth, but a **compressed alkali mud**, gray-green and slick, that gives beneath your foot with a silent, sucking pull. It is a sediment of millennia—finely ground volcanic glass, mineral salts, and the pulverized remains of the basin’s inhabitants. In the lower, wetter areas, it gives way to **shallow, weedy ponds** of water that isn’t water, but a dense, silty, slightly conductive fluid that smells of iron and sulfur. These ponds are interconnected by **slow, oozing channels** that weep from the basaltic slopes.

Scattered across the mud are **erosion’s final sculptures**. The ancient rating is total. The basin has been in a state of near-flattening for longer than complex life has existed elsewhere. What resists this flattening are **monoliths and nodules** of a tougher, glassy stone,和各种 **crystal growths** that have precipitated from the weeping fluid over eons. They look like rotting teeth, corals, or frozen explosions—amber, deep violet, and sickly green. Their surfaces are etched and blurred by the constant, gentle flow of moisture.

**Atmosphere & Sound:**
The sound is the defining parameter. It is **deafening**. It is not a single noise, but a layered, physical **cacophony** that vibrates in your teeth and chest.
1.  **The Drone:** A subsonic hum, felt more than heard, emanating from the basin’s depths—the sound of gas vents, tectonic sighs, and the collective vibration of billions of small bodies moving in the mud.
2.  **The Clack & Ring:** A ceaseless, high-pitched percussion. It comes from the **crystal monoliths**. As the temperature and humidity shift in the cool air, the crystals expand and contract at different rates, causing them to tap against each other or fracture microscopically, producing a sound like billions of tiny chimes, wind chimes made of broken glass, and a constant, dry rain of crystal dust.
3.  **The Life Chorus:** The abundant **fauna** is the source of the loudest, most organic layer. See below.

This sound has flattened the landscape in a perceptual way too. There are no echoes; the noise is a solid wall. Distant features seem closer than they are. Conversations are impossible; communication must be gesture, touch, or writing on the slick mud.

**Ecology:**
Vegetation is 0.3/3. There are no plants, no algae, no recognizable photosynthesis. The base of the food web is **chemosynthetic and detrital**. The muddy fluid and the crystal surfaces are home to vast, slow-moving biofilms and mats of **pristine white fungus-things**, more mineral than organic, that feed on mineral leaching and dissolved gases. They are the “grass” of this world—sparse, mineral-dusted, and silent.

The **fauna (2.0/3 — abundant)** is where the life is, and it is all sound and motion.
*   **The Mud-Crawlers:** The bulk of the biomass. They are **gelatinous, translucent things**, like slugs made of soap-bubble jelly, ranging from fist-sized to bear-sized. They ooze through the channels and over the mud, consuming the fungal mats and each other. Their movement is a wet, slapping, squelching sound—the backbone of the Life Chorus. As they move, they secrete a mucus that quickens the crystal growths, linking their life cycle to the monoliths.
*   **The Ringers:** Smaller, shrimp-like crustaceans with **carapaces grown from micro-crystals**. They inhabit the shallow ponds. When disturbed, they don’t swim so much as *vibrate*, shaking their crystal shells to produce a loud, piercing, cricket-like trill that cuts through the Drone. Predators rely on this sound to find them.
*   **The Gulp-Feeders:** The apex predators. They are **still, mound-like formations** that look like part of the mud until they move. They are filter-feeders, sitting at channel intersections. When a sufficient cloud of Crawlers or smaller life drifts by, they open a vast, silent **maw** that creates a sudden, roaring vacuum—a *WHUMP* of displaced air and fluid that is a shock in the constant noise—and suck everything in. The sound of that gulp is a thunderclap in miniature.
Predator-prey dynamics are active but slow-motion: the Crawlers flee the vacuum roar, the Ringers trill in panic. The dynamic is a cycle of ooze, trill, and WHUMP.

**Built & Tech (1.3/3 primitive, 1.3/3 scattered):**
There are no living inhabitants who build. The “built” elements are the **scattered, partial remains of prior cycles**.
*   **The Anchor-Stones:** Roughly shaped, unworked basaltic boulders with deep, regular grooves carved into them. They are aligned with the basin’s subtle magnetic field or the paths of the sun on the rare clear days. They are ancient—perhaps the first tools, used to mark time or channel the crystal sounds. Their purpose is forgotten.
*   **The Silt-Towers:** Delicate, fragile towers of packed mud and crystal dust, built (by whom?) in the deepest, wettest parts of the basin. They are only a few feet tall, and they dissolve with every seasonal wetting. They are not shelters; they are acoustic, perhaps, or ritual.
*   **Tools:** Occasionally, a Crawler is found with a smooth, water-worn stone chip (*obsidian*) clutched in its mucus, having used it to crack a crystal-gated fungal mat. Not tool-use, but **tool-appropriation**. The tech level is pre-intentional. Life here is too busy being food and sound to domesticate or fabricate.

**Rules & Weirdness (1.4/3 unusual):**
1.  **Sound Shapes Stone:** The constant harmonic vibration from the crystals and the Drone literally **sings the mud into shapes**. Certain frequencies cause the alkali mud to harden into temporary, Resonance-Formations—wavy walls or arches that appear overnight and melt in days.
2.  **The Wetness is Inherent:** The “water” doesn’t fall from the sky as rain. The sky *weeps* a fine mist constantly. The basin is a world where the atmosphere is always at its dew point. Rocks beading moisture are not sweating; they are breathing.
3.  **Time is Layered:** The ancient erosion is visible in the flattened mud and worn monoliths. But the dynamic is in the daily cycle of sound intensity (the crystals ring loudest at dawn and dusk), the weekly pulsing of the Mud-Crawler migrations, and the seasonal “Great Gulp” when the Gulp-Feeders spawn and their vacuums sound like a continent clearing its throat.
4.  **Fauna as Architects:** The Crawlers, by their mucus, are the primary agents of geological change, accelerating crystal growth in patterns that mimic their movement. The basin’s form is a palimpsest of ancient life-routes, written in violet and green stone.

**Synthesis:**
You are standing in a **sonic, mineral marsh**. Before you, the gray-green mud stretches, dotted with the ghostly shapes of violet crystals that ring softly in the dim air. A channel of silty fluid oozes by. In it, a hundred translucent slugs ooze and squelch in concert—a wet, slapping rhythm that underpins everything. From the ponds, a sudden, violent *trill-trill-TRILL!* erupts as something disturbs the Ringers. Then, a hundred yards away, a silent, mud-colored mound opens its mouth, and the world shudders with a *WHUMP* of air and fluid. The sound hits you, a physical pressure. Crystals clatter in reply. The mist condenses on your face. This is not a world of stillness, but of deep time played out in frantic, noisy, mineral life on a stage flattened by everything that has ever happened to it. The emptiness between presences is full of sound. The scattered artifacts—the grooved Anchor-Stone, the crumbling Silt-Tower—are just more features in the basin, slowly being sung into dust by the deafening, active, cool, wet old age of it all.
