The place is called the Halcyon Steppes, though there is no grass here, and no hint of halcyon peace. It is a high plateau of black, glassy rock, fractured into immense, irregular slabs that lie like the scales of a drowned leviathan. The elevation is such that the air is thin and carries a permanent, cool dampness, but the temperature is warm—a deep, geothermal warmth that seeps from the rock itself, not from a sun that hangs in a perpetually overcast, pearl-grey sky.

**Water:** It does not fall as rain, nor collect in streams. The wetness is atmospheric, a condensation of the constant mist. Water beads on the dark, non-porous stone surfaces, growing fat and slow, then merging and weeping down in thick, viscous tears. These ephemeral rivulets vanish into hairline cracks within minutes. In the depressions between the great slabs, shallow, perfectly still pools of dark water form, reflecting the sky with a mercury-like sheen. The pools have no inflow or outflow; they simply *are*, their surfaces so calm they seem solid. You cannot hear a single drop fall—the rock absorbs sound. The wetness is a sensation on the skin and a dark sheen on the stone.

**Vegetation:** Lush, but sparse. Where a microscopic crack in the rock holds a persistent bead of water, life clamors. Thick, rubbery mosses in bottle-green and violet cushion the stone. Stunted, succulent plants with waxy leaves cluster in these micro-habitats, their blooms tiny, brilliant explosions of orange and electric blue. Elsewhere, the rock is bare, polished by a mist that never quite evaporates. The lushness is concentrated in islands, leaving vast tracts of the dark, wet plateau utterly barren.

**Terrain & Scale:** Young. The erosion score belies a landscape of terrifying newness. The rock is not weathered into smooth curves but fractured into sharp, angular planes and razor-edged crevasses. There are no rounded hills, only a chaotic, multi-kilometer expanse of tilted, broken slabs. The scale is open—you can see for what feels like dozens of kilometers across this grey-green and black checkerboard, to where the plateau’s edge is marked not by a slope, but by a sudden, sheer.drop into the eternal cloud layer below. The emptiness is profound, the spaces between the vegetated cracks measuring tens or hundreds of meters.

**Built & Tech:** Urban, but primitive. The settlements are not cities of soaring towers, but vast, low, organic assemblages that seem grown from the rock rather than built upon it. They are clusters of terraced platforms and dry-stone walls, constructed without mortar from the same black glass shards that litter the steppe. Dwellings are recessed into the rock slabs or built up with insulated clay and woven reed, their roofs hidden by moss. There is no metal—no nails, no hinges. Everything is fitted, tied, or stacked. Aqueducts are carved directly into the stone, their channels so perfectly graded they carry the weeping water for meters before it evaporates. The infrastructure is at a human scale, but it covers acres, a network of walls, pools, and shaded courtyards that maximizes every drop and every patch of moss-farm.

**Sound & Dynamic:** Silence, or near-silence. The lack of fauna is absolute. There are no birds, no insects, no-scuttling things. The only sounds are the rare, deep *glug* of a large water bead finally winning its race down a fissure, and the constant, sighing murmur of the mist—a sound felt in the sinuses more than heard by the ear. The landscape is in a frozen equilibrium. The pools do not shrink. The moss does not visibly grow. The condensation rate does not vary. Time here does not pass; it simply accumulates. The built structures show no signs of decay or renovation. They are part of the static tableau.

**Atmosphere & Uniqueness:** The light is normal, diffuse, and colourless. The air is still, heavy with humidity and the faint, clean scent of wet stone and vegetation. The feeling is one of profound, unnerving stillness. You are watched not by eyes, but by the sheer, unchanging fact of the place. The weirdness is low only in the sense that nothing violates the known laws of physics. The *unease* comes from the paradox: a warm, wet, lush world that is utterly silent, motionless, and devoid of any other life but its own human inhabitants, who move through their terraces and moss-beds with a quiet, ritualistic deliberateness, as if performing a maintenance task whose purpose has been forgotten, but whose correctness is written in the stone. They do not look up at the cloud-sea. They look only at the cracks, and the water, and the moss.
