# The Cistern of Chorformed

## Terrain & Scale
You stand upon a plateau of polished black glass, not volcanic but *cast*—a single, continent-spanning poured surface, scored with the parallel grooves of massive forms that were never quarried but *grown* or *extruded*. The elevation is high, the air cool and thin, but there is no mountain range. You are simply on a vast, flat-topped plinth of the world, and the land falls away sharply on all sides into depths filled with the same material. The scale is regional: this is a basin the size of a small country, a self-contained geography of manufacture. The horizon is a perfect, unwavering line where the black glass meets a sky of perpetual, shadowless noon (light: 2.7/3—bright).

## Materials & Atmosphere
Everything is made. There is no soil, no bedrock, only substrate: a composite of sintered metal, vitrified slag, and crystalline polymers. It ranges from the matte, pitted grey of ancient engine blocks to the iridescent, oil-slick sheen of new-growth conduits. The "sparse vegetation" (1.2/3) is not plants as known. It is a slow-cultivated lichen of metallic filaments and rust-colored spores that digest atmospheric trace ions, painting the surfaces in delicate, filigree stains of orange and green. The dampness (water: 0.9/3) is never rain. It is a constant, silent weeping of condensation from the cooler undersides of overhangs, collecting in basins that are themselves part of the geometry, or a periodic, rhythmic *pulse* of mist that vents from thousands of needle-thin fissures in the plateau floor, lasting an hour, then stopping for a day.

## The Built Environment (Density: 2.4/3, Built: 2.8/3, Tech: 2.2/3)
This is not a city. It is a single, contiguous, industrial organism. Density is everything: structures do not stand apart, they interlock, braid, and overgrow one another with no space for empty air. Towers are fused into continuous ramparts; conveyor arches become aqueducts become habitation tubes; the piping is the architecture. The material is not assembled but *deposited*—you see layers upon layers of built matter, like sedimentary rock, each stratum a different epoch of production. Some layers are smooth and automated, others are brutal, riveted, and stained with centuries of operational residue. The industrial tech is pervasive but often paradoxically silent. Massive gears turn inside transparent casings with no sound. Piston-rods the length of streets slide in their cylinders with a breathless, frictionless glide. Power is not transmitted by belts or wires but by *conduction* through the very material of the walls.

## Ecology & Fauna (Fauna: 2.0/3)
Life here is abundant but woven into the machinery. The "fauna" are not animals. They are:
*   **The Scrapers:** Six-legged, armoured things the size of cats, with mouths of diamond grit. They crawl the sheer surfaces, eating the slow-metals and excreting polished, functional nodules. They are numerous, silent, and utterly indifferent.
*   **The Vent-Moths:** Leathery-winged creatures that live in the warm exhaust plumes. Their wings are thin sheets of photovoltaic film. They cling to surfaces in vast, shimmering sheets, converting thermal gradients to energy for the system.
*   **The Lubricant Threads:** Centipede-like strings of iridescent gel that swim through the sealed coolant channels, sealing micro-fractures with their own bodies. They pulse softly with bioluminescence.
*   **The Foremen:** Larger, bipedal constructs of wood and brass—not robots, but symbionts. They wear the grime of the place as a second skin, making minute adjustments with tools of bone and stone. They communicate not by sound, but by shifting the pigment patterns on their faces, a slow, colour-coded language visible only at very close range.

## Sound, Light, & Dynamic (Sound: 0.6/3, Dynamic: 1.4/3, Light: 2.7/3)
The "mostly quiet" (0.6/3) is profound. The great engines do not roar; they hum at frequencies felt in the teeth. The dominant sounds are the whisper of the Scrapers on glass, the distant *thrum* of power conduction, and the soft, papery rustle of Vent-Moth wings. The slow dynamic (1.4/3) is felt in the geology. The black glass plateau imperceptibly *settles*—a millimeter every decade. Entire districts are slowly subducted into new, lower layers, a process so gradual you only notice by comparing markings on a wall from one year to the next. The light (2.7/3) is brutally even, bleaching all colour to its essential spectrum. Shadows are sharp, black, and absolute, but the density of the built forms creates a labyrinth of permanent twilight at ground level, pierced by occasional blinding shafts of light from high vents.

## Weirdness (2.8/3—Reality Breakdown)
The rules here are local and mutable.
1.  **Geometry:** Certain angles of junction—precisely 113.7 degrees—cause a slight warping of perception. Looking down a corridor of that exact angle makes the end appear both closer and farther than it is.
2.  **Material Memory:** The substrate remembers stress. If you strike a wall hard, a faint, ghostly afterimage of the impact *floats* in the air for a moment, a translucent crack.
3.  **Scale Fluidity:** Occasionally, a Foreman will be seen working on a component that seems both the size of a thumb and a house simultaneously, depending on how you focus your eyes.
4.  **The Quiet Symphony:** The low hum of the place is not random. If you lie perfectly still on the glass for hours, you can feel a complex, sub-audible rhythm—a true mechanical heartbeat—that coordinates the activities of the entire basin. It is the sound of the system’s own slow, industrial consciousness.

## Atmosphere & Rules of the World
The air is cool (1.4/3), dry except during the mist pulses, and tastes faintly of ozone and hot stone. There is no night. There is only the constant, bleached light. Navigation is by the slow colour-language of the Foremen and by reading the sedimentary layers of construction. The "rules" are simple: everything is a component. Nothing is wasted. Everything persists, buried or repurposed. To damage the habitat is to damage the system’s slow, thinking body, and the system’s immune response is not anger, but patient, inevitable reconfiguration. You are not an intruder in a landscape; you are a temporary, anomalous mineral inclusion in a vast, slow, and utterly actualized engine of becoming-made.

The place does not feel abandoned. It feels *complete*. The density is not crowding, but a perfect, interwoven saturation. The silence is not empty, but the sound of immense, slow force held perfectly in check. The sharp, young erosion is the clean break of a thing just now finished being built. And in the constant, shadowless light, the million Scrapers move, the Vent-Moths pulse, the Foremen shift their cryptic faces, and the black glass settles, millimeter by patient millimeter, into a future it has already perfectly machined for itself.
