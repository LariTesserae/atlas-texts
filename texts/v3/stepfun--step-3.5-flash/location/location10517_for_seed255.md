The place is called the **Concave**. It is a basin, low and wide, carved not by water but by intention, and then lined with intention until no raw stone or soil remains. You stand at the edge of a manufacturer’s plateau, looking down into a regional-scale hive of fabrication.

Everything is built. *Everything*. The ground is not earth but **resin-crete**, a composite of crushed volcanic glass and a secreted polymer binder, pitted and scored like a giant’s workbench. It is cool and slightly yielding underfoot, with the faint, sterile scent of wet stone and hot resin. There are no plants, no soil, no recognizable biology. Instead, the basin is dense with **presences**: structures, conduits, platforms, and the constant, churning motion of the inhabitants.

The scale is open and regional—this is a valley-sized project, a single, sprawling organism of purpose. The density is near-touching: towers of woven basalt fiber rise like termite mounds, linked by thousands of swaying catwalks and spiral staircases. Water (parameter 0.9) is not in rivers but in **managed dampness**: condensation sheens the dark resin surfaces in the cool air (temperature 1.4), dripping into collection gutters. Fine, cold mist sometimes sweeps through from concealed nozzles in the basin walls, a humidity system for material conditioning. You see occasional **pockets** of standing water in shallow, geometric basins, black and still, reflecting the glow-lanterns.

Light (0.5) is a scarce resource. True darkness is the canvas. Illumination comes from **bioluminescent lichen-patches** cultivated in gel-filled panels, and from thousands of hand-blown glass lanterns burning a slow, smoky oil. The light is low, pooled, and deeply directional, making the dense architecture throw long, jittery shadows. You navigate by memory and sound more than sight.

For sound (1.9) is the defining feature. The Concave is **loud**. It is a symphony of primitive industry:
- The *constant, rhythmic clack* of hand-looms weaving basalt fiber on immense frames.
- The *deep, metallic hum* of water-driven pestles grinding glass in stone mortars.
- The *shout-echoes* of crew chiefs coordinating via whistle and bell, their voices swallowed by the ambient roar.
- The *scraping and grating* of stone-on-stone as rollers shift molded resin blocks.
- Above it all, a pervasive, sub-audible **thrum** felt in the teeth, from the great central **Breathing Drum**—a colossal, manually operated bellows pumping air through the basin’s ventilation shafts.

Fauna (1.4) is rare, and non-animal. The inhabitants are the **Crafteers**: bipedal, slender, with long manipulator fingers stained with pigment and resin. They move with efficient, silent purpose in the dimness, wrapped in layered felt and fiber cloaks. They speak a language of sharp whistles and hand-signs. They are not numerous—density comes from their constructions, not their bodies. You might see one silhouetted on a gantry, guiding a stone block into place with a system of ropes and counterweights. Their eyes are large, adapted to the gloom. They have no pets, no herds. The only other moving presences are the **Maintenance Crawlers**: six-legged, clockwork automata of brass and wood, clattering along prescribed tracks to oil bearings and clear debris, their movements precise and repetitive.

The world is **active** (dynamic 1.9) and **emerging** (erosion 0.5). This is not a finished place. It is **under continuous construction and simultaneous attrition**. You see crews deliberately scoring and fracturing old, overloaded resin-crete sections to be replaced. You see the slow, creeping **damp-rot** where condensation pools, a constant battle against material decay. Weather cycles are artificial: the mist-bursts, the periodic draining of water basins, the dimming and brightening of lantern-shifts. The ecology is one of **material turnover**.

The weirdness (1.7) seeps from the **incongruity of scale and technique**. This is a megastructure—a valley-sized, densely packed artificial world—built entirely with **hand tools and simple machines**. No cranes, no powered vehicles. Everything is moved by treadmills, capstans, and teams of Crafteers. The architecture has an organic, clustered roughness, yet it is utterly functional, with no decorative excess. The rules feel like **brute-force elegance**: if a problem exists, a hundred workers and a clever system of levers and weights will solve it, slowly, with great noise.

The air tastes of damp stone, hot oil, and the faint, sweet rot of the condensation-collection gel. The ground vibrates with the thrum of the Breathing Drum and the march of the Crawlers. You are not in a city. You are inside the working胃 of a world-being that builds itself with chisel, loom, and bellows, in a cool, damp, resounding darkness, where every surface is made and every drop of water is accounted for.
