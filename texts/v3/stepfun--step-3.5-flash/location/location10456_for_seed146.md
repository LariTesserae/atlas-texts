A stillness that is not empty, but full.

You are in the **Subsonant Nave**, a continent-sized cavern system that is not a cave in the sense of a void within rock, but a **geological event that became a place**. Long ago, a deep, silica-rich magma chamber, under immense pressure and at a precise thermal threshold, did not erupt. It *unzipped*. The roof and floor simultaneously shattered and cooled into a seamless, razor-edged glass, while the interior—a mile-high volume of supercritical gas—crystallized in an instant into a forest of hyper-stable, interlocking mineral lattices. The result is a world of **fresh, young glass** (erosion: 1.0) that is already in a state of **absolute, frozen equilibrium** (dynamic: 0.3). The sharp, conchoidal fractures and fragile, needle-like spires are not weathering; they are the unaltered, pristine edges of the formation event itself, preserved in a thermodynamics that has nowhere left to go.

The scale is **planetary** (scale: 2.6). You stand on a shelf of dark, purple-black glass, before you the nave yawns into a darkness so complete it feels physical. The ceiling is a distant, invisible curve, lost in blackness. The floor descends in a series of impossible, sheer terraces and jagged ridges, all the same vitreous material, plummeting into regions where the air itself has settled into permanent, stratified layers of different densities and faint chemistries (density: 1.6). There is no wind, no drip, no falling stone. There is only the pressure of the still air and the profound, weighty silence (sound: 0.9).

Yet the world is **abundant** (fauna: 1.6) and **dense** (density: 1.6). Life here did not evolve from water or sun. It is a **fungal-or-simpler** (vegetation: 0.2) ecology of **gel-bodies** and **silica-mats**. Every surface is coated in a thin, pulsating biofilm—a slow, electromagnetic heartbeat visible as a diffuse, purple-and-blue bioluminescence that provides the only light (light: 0.4, now overcome by the glow). Thicker, translucent 'forests' of fungal strands hang from the glass ceilings and ridges, each strand a city of microscopic motes, glowing with a synchronized, imperceptibly slow pulse. The abundant fauna are soft, multifaceted things: **glass-slugs** that secrete a vacuum-adhesive gel to creep over the sharpest edges, **lichen-skippers** that flicker from one bioluminescent patch to the next in bursts of static electricity. Their movement is so slow, their world so perfectly stable, that to a human eye they appear as part of the landscape—the only sign of life is the subtle shift in the glow's pattern over hours.

The atmosphere is **warm** (temperature: 2.1)—a uniform, breathable, humid warmth from residual geothermal saturation that has already equalized. The only scent is **ozone, wet stone, and a sweet, fungal musk**. The only sounds are the **sub-audible hum** of the bioelectric networks and the occasional, deep **mineral groan** as the entire system, in its perfect equilibrium, still settles by a micron every century—a sound felt in the teeth more than heard.

Scattered (built: 1.0) across the terraces are **primitive artifacts** (tech: 1.4). A dozen or so structures, all built from the same black glass. They are not buildings, but **tools and traps**: a perfectly flaked obsidian blade still hafted to a bone grip; a circle of glass droppers poised above a luminous fungal mat, their purpose long-vanished; a low wall of glass cobbles, placed to channel the movement of the slow glass-slugs. They are made by something that was here before the final crystallization, or perhaps during the brief, unstable window after. They are not integrated into the ecology; they are **foreign objects** that have become mineralized, their edges blurred by a micron of the ever-present fungal growth. They do not work. They simply *are*, like fossils in a stone that is itself a fossil.

The world is **strange** (weirdness: 1.9) in a way that is not alien, but **hyper-basic**. The laws of physics here are the same, but their expression is simplified to an extreme: chemistry is mostly silicon and sulfur; biology is a direct conversation between bioelectricity and crystalline lattice; time is not a river but a **single, frozen note**. The glass does not just record light; it holds a perfect, static imprint of the moment of its formation—a ghost-image of the supercritical gas cloud, visible only as a faint, swirling distortion if you stare at a flawless pane for too long. The fungal network may be a single, planet-spanning mind, thinking thoughts that take a thousand years to complete a single syllable.

You are in a **young world** (erosion: 1.0) that is already **ancient beyond measure** (dynamic: 0.3). A place where creation and finality happened at once. The air is still, the light glows, the slow things creep, and the sharp glass waits, forever, for a change that will never come.
