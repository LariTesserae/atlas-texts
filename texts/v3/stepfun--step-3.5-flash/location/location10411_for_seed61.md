**Name:** The Weeping Grasse  

**Location:** Below the skin of the world. A single, pocket-sized basin—no larger than a great hall—sunk into a vertical fault of black volcanic glass. It is not a cave, but a *hollow*: a place where the stone itself thinned and collapsed centuries ago, leaving a cell of space sealed beneath a low, dome-like ceiling of fused, glassy slag. There is no way in or out except through hairline fissures too narrow for a hand, through which a chill, mineral-scented breath of air eternally sighs.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The floor is a mosaic of **wind-polished volcanic glass** (obsidian, pitchstone) and **hydrated mineral crusts**—slicks of gypsum, calcite, and strange, non-crystalline silicates that weep a constant, slow dampness. This is the **water**: not a stream, but a weeping. Every surface is slick with a film of condensation drawn from the sealed atmosphere itself. Droplets bead and merge on vertical faces, then trace slow, meandering paths down to the floor, where they vanish into a **sponge of fine, crystal sand**. There are no puddles; the water is absorbed and re-exhaled in an eternal, invisible cycle.

The basin is **dense** with presence. **Built** structures are **urban** in concentration, **primitive** in fabrication: a **town** crammed into a single room. Dwellings are not constructed but *assembled*: stacked blocks of scavenged slag-glass, walls of woven **basalt fibers** (drawn from molten rock with bone hooks), roofs of overlapping, shale-like plates. Structures lean and buttress against one another and the basin’s walls, connected by catwalks and stairways carved into the living stone. There is no open space; the settlement is a **layered-construction**, with upper levels accessed by ladders and notched poles, lower levels sunk into the saturated sand.

**Atmosphere & Light:**
**Dim**. The only illumination is a sourceless, ambient **phosphorescence** emanating from the **weird** mineral growths that cling to every surface. These are not plants, not fungi, but **primitively metabolizing lattices**: slow, pulsing veils of **gelatinous silicate** and **electro-sensitive manganese oxides** that glow a weak, bile-yellow or bruised violet when disturbed by vibration or passing forms. They are the basin’s **fauna**, its only ecology—a slow, chemical pulse. The air is **cool**, still, and thick with the smell of wet stone, ozone, and the faint, sweet-rot odor of decompressing silicates.

**Ecology & Fauna:**
**Vegetation: 0.1/3—no plant life**. The only "living" things are the **weird** mineral lattices and the basin’s **rare** true fauna:
- **The Scribes**: eyeless, segmented worm-things of chitinous glass, a hand’s length long. They move in millimeter-per-hour waves through the damp sand, feeding on mineral salts and leaving behind temporary, script-like trails of disturbed grains.
- **The Echo-Moths**: not insects, but floating clusters of resonant quartz crystals, each the size of a fist. They hang suspended in the air, chiming softly as they drift, drawn to the hum of settlement activity. They are phototactic, clustering around the brighter lattice-glow.
- **The Basin’s Keepers**: the human (or human-like) inhabitants. They are few, silent, and don’t seem to *own* the place so much as *inhabit its equilibrium*. They are hunter-gatherers of stone: they harvest glass-fibers, chip tools from slag, and scrape the mineral lattices for their faint energy. Their **tech** is **primitive**: flaked glass blades, cordage of basalt, bone awls. They build nothing new; they only rearrange what the basin provides.

**Sounds:**
**Loud (2.3/3)**. The basin is a **resonant chamber**. The primary sound is the **drip-hem of a thousand weeping points**, a chaotic, polyrhythmic patter that echoes endlessly off the glassy walls and packed structures—a sound like a million tiny, ceaseless heartbeats. Layered over this is the **scrape and whisper** of the Scribes in the sand, the **crystalline chime** of Echo-Moths, and the **low, grating hum** of the mineral lattices when agitated. Human sound is minimal: the scrape of a tool, a boot on stone, a word spoken in a gravelly, under-tone. But the basin itself *speaks* constantly, and the humans live in its voice.

**Rules of the World / Stability:**
**Dynamic: 0.4/3—never changing**. The basin is in **frozen equilibrium**. The glass wears but does not break. The sand does not shift beyond the Scribes’ paths. The mineral lattices grow and pulse at a fixed rate. The weeping is constant in volume and pattern. Time here is not a river but a **pressure**. Nothing *happens*; everything *is*. The humans do not age visibly. Their tasks are cyclical and exacting, part of the basin’s balance. To change anything—to pile new stone, to block a weep—causes a subtle disturbance in the hum, a shift in echo, a pause in the drip-rhythm. The basin notices. The lattice-glow dims nearby. The atmosphere feels heavier. The humans know this. They do not seek progress. They seek **symmetry**.

**Weirdness:**
The **weirdness** is in the **non-biological life** and the **physical laws**. Sound does not decay; it *linger*s as a faint vibration in the glass, a memory the stone holds for minutes. The mineral lattices respond to emotional states (fear, calm) in nearby humans with shifts in glow-intensity—a primitive, chemical empathy. The most unsettling feature is the **Floor of Faces**: a patch of damp sand near the basin’s center where, when the light is right, the disturbed grains form perfect, fleeting human visages—not drawn, but *arranged* by some playful, mineral gravity.

**Scale & Density:**
**Hidden (0.1/3), pocket-scale**. You could walk the perimeter in a hundred steps. You could touch every structure if allowed. It is a **single-feature contained**, an **intimate-room** of a world. But it is **dense (1.8/3)** in **presence per volume**: walls of structures, hanging Moths, pulsing lattices, weeping stone, the constant sonic blanket. You are never alone, never in open space. The air feels thick with *stuff*—moisture, sound, history, stasis.

**Summary Impression:**
The Weeping Grasse is a **subterranean, mineral town** in a **state of perfect, eerie balance**. It is **cool**, **damp**, and **loud with eternal weeping**. It is **urban** yet **primitive**, **dense** yet **hidden**, built from **no life** and maintained by a **rare, few** who live as part of a **non-changing** stone ecosystem. It does not dream of the surface. It *is* the sound of water in stone, the glow of slow chemistry, the echo of a world that forgot how to change.
