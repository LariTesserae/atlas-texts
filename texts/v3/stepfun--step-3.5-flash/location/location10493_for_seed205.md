You stand on a high, eroded mesa of dark, glassy stone, the kind worn smooth by a wind that carries not sand but a fine, iridescent dust. Below you, the world does not fall away into valleys; it *swells*. It is a single, contiguous organism—a continent of vegetation so total it has become the geology. This is not a forest. This is a *presence*.

**Terrain & Materials:** The ground is not earth. It is a composite of humus, compressed leaf-litter centuries deep, and the fibrous, often exposed roots of the great structures. These structures are the “trees,” but the word is a child’s term for mountains that breathe. Their trunks are not singular; they are clustered, twisting, merging columns of a wood-like material that gleams with a waxy, sepia-toned resin. Some are smooth and piston-like, others are crusted with bark that resembles petrified lace or armored scales. The surface is not solid. Step near the base of a “giant” and you see the substrate is a maze of breathing lenticels, slow pores that exhale a warm, wet vapor with a sound like distant sighing. The rocks you see are not mineral; they are nodules of hardened sap, glacial erratics of former growths, now inert.

**Atmosphere & Light:** The air is thick, warm (2.8/3 *hot*), and carries the perpetual taste of overripe fruit and ozone. Normal daylight (1.5/3) bleaches the upper canopies to a pale gold, but the world below exists in a perpetual, dappled gloom, pierced by shafts of light that ignite motes of golden pollen. You are at elevation (1.5/3 *high*), but the “treetops” are a kilometer above you; the canopy is a second, vaulted sky, shrouded in its own weather. The dampness (1.1/3 *damp*) is not from rain—rain is rare—but from condensation. Every surface weeps. Your skin beaded with moisture the moment you arrived.

**Sound:** It is not loud. It is *deafening* (2.6/3). It is a physical pressure, a wall of noise built of countless layers:
1.  The **substrate hum**: a deep, 20-Hz thrum you feel in your bones, the metabolic pulse of the continent-plant.
2.  The **sighing respiration**: the exhalation from a million lenticels, creating a constant, windy whisper.
3.  The **faunal choir**: the overwhelming (2.7/3) fauna is primarily avian, reptilian, and insectoid, but all are woven into the plant. There are skittering herds of six-legged, furry things that sound like dry leaves on stone. There are soaring predators with cryosote-like calls that shatter the hum in glassy streaks. There are the constant, high-frequency chirrs and clicks of insects that live in the bark, a noise so dense it becomes a single, shimmering tone. This is an ecosystem that communicates through sound, a continent with a voice box.

**Ecology & Weirdness:** This is a **pure-plant ecosystem** (2.8/3 *total vegetation*), but the weirdness (2.1/3) is in the *integration*. The fauna are not tenants; they are organelles. Some flying creatures have wings that are translucent membranes stretched between modified leaves. The “fruit” is often a living, breathing pod that splits open to release not seeds, but fully formed, squirming juvenile fauna that scuttle into the root-maze. Certain patches of the “forest floor” are actually slow-moving, predatory mats of moss that digest anything that rests too long. The established erosion (2.0/3) is on a biological timescale: you see where a giant column collapsed millennia ago, its ruins now a new, sprawling thicket of a different species. The dynamics (1.6/3 *active*) are glacial yet furious: a “branch” might visibly shift a meter over a week, straining and creaking, as it reorients toward a new moisture source.

**Scale & Density:** The scale (2.8/3 *vast*) is continental. There are no horizons, only transitions from one biome-plane to another. Weather systems are born and die within the canopy. A storm might rage in the upper canopy for a week, a localized downpour of nutrient-rich fluid, while you stand below in dry, dusty stillness. The density is paradoxically sparse (1.3/3). The “trees” are kilometers apart. The space between them is not empty; it is a scrubland of low, resilient, silica-based shrubs and hard, crusted lichens, all living in the deep shade and nutrient-siphoned soil of the giants. The emptiness is a vast, solemn plain punctuated by monumental life, making each encounter with a giant a rare, awe-inspiring event. The saturation is low—you can see for miles through the under-story.

**Built & Tech:** The built environment (0.8/3 *scattered*) is not architecture; it is *infestation*. There are perhaps half-a-dozen human (or human-like) sites in a day’s walk. A stone circle, its stones slick with resin. A single, dome-shaped shelter woven from living, flexible branches that have been shaped and hardened by fire—the fire long cold, the shelter now part of the plant, its entrance choked with bioluminescent fungi. A rusted, simple iron dagger, its blade pitted and blurred, half-swallowed by a creeping vine. The tech level is primitive (1.4/3): bone tools, knapped silica, simple cordage. They did not conquer this place. They hid in it, used its parts, and were slowly reabsorbed. Their greatest artifact might be a series of carefully carved, non-metallic totems placed at the bases of giants, now worn smooth by the breathing bark.

**Rules of the World:**
*   **Metal decays rapidly**, unless shielded in certain resins. It is a rare, fragile luxury.
*   **Sound is a primary nutrient**. The deafening chorus is not just communication; it is a physical force that stimulates growth and reproduction in certain species. Silence is a poison here, a void that causes plant-stress.
*   **Verticality is key**. Life exists in stratified layers, each with its own micro-climate, fauna, and plant-variants. To journey is to climb.
*   **Time is elastic**. A runner can traverse the sparse under-story for a day, only to find a giant’s shadow has moved only a few meters in that time.
*   **The plant is the law**. It grows, it adapts, it consumes. To fight it is to fight the atmosphere. The only sustainable practice is symbiosis—a tenuous, negotiated balance the primitive tech-holders seemed to have practiced.

You are not in a place. You are *inside* a thing. The heat (2.8/3) is its fever. The damp (1.1/3) is its sweat. The deafening sound (2.6/3) is its blood singing in its veins. The sparse human dots are like scabs on its skin, almost healed over. The erosion (2.0/3) is its old age, wise and settled. The weirdness (2.1/3) is its alien mind, thinking in centuries, breathing in weather, dreaming in spores. Look up. The second sky of the canopy is moving, not with wind, but with the slow, continent-wide decision to photosynthesize the last of the evening light. You are a mote. A brief, hot, moist, and terribly loud mote, in the lung of a world that is also a plant.
