The world is the **Plain of Sintered Glass**.

It is not a plain in the sense of grass or soil. It is a plain of volcanic glass, obsidian and its stranger kin, baked and welded into a single, continent-spanning sheet. The erosion is absolute, ancient beyond memory. There are no hills, only the faintest, sub-millimeter ripple patterns from the last great thermal contraction, like the wrinkles on a cooled stone skin. The elevation is low because there is nowhere higher to be; this is the final, flattened state of a world that has forgotten how to build mountains.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The ground is a fractured mosaic of black, green, and rust-red glass, each pane fused to its neighbor. The edges are not sharp—they have been worn to a dull, glassy polish by a constant, fine abrasive mist. You do not walk on it so much as glide, the surface offering a faint, suction-cup grip. In the troughs between panes, a viscous, iridescent slurry collects—a saturated solution of metallic salts precipitated from the air. It moves with a slow, creeping tide, driven by imperceptible slopes and thermal cycles.

**Atmosphere & Water:**
The air is perpetually damp, but never truly wet. This is the **Condensation Veil**: a global, low-hanging fog so dense it feels like breathing cool silk. It is not water vapor as known on living worlds, but a colloid of microscopic silica droplets and dissolved ions. It condenses on every surface, weeping from the sky in a silent, constant drizzle that never pools, only thickens the slurry in the cracks or evaporates again within minutes. Light, which is dim (a distant, blue-dwarf sun struggling through the Veil), is diffused into a perpetual, shadowless twilight. Visibility extends for kilometers, but the world has no horizons—it just fades into a uniform grey-blue.

**Sound:**
The sound is a layered, industrial cacophony, yet utterly natural. It is the **Hum of the Sinter**.
*   The base layer is a sub-audible thrum, felt in the teeth and bones, from the slow, tectonic creep of the glass sheet itself—the planet’s final, grinding sigh.
*   Above that is the hiss and scrape of the slurry, a million tiny liquid fingers pulling grit across stone.
*   Dominating it all is the high, metallic ring and consequent low *clang* of distant, massive structures resonating. These are not built. They are **grown**.

**Ecology & “Fauna”:**
There is no vegetation, no fungi, no recognizable biology. The “fauna” are the **Bellowers** and the **Crawlers**.
*   **Bellowers** are range-finding resonators. They are cathedral-sized, arching structures of fused glass and metallic crystal that grow from the plain. They do not move. They *tune*. Using the slurry as a conductor, they draw energy from thermal differentials and the planet’s residual magnetism. They emit their powerful, harmonic calls—the source of the loudest clangs—which propagate for hundreds of kilometers. Their purpose is to synchronize the rhythms of the entire plain.
*   **Crawlers** are the abundant, mobile life. They are not animals. They are mobile, self-replicating chemical reactors shaped like armoured millipedes or scarabs, grown from the same materials as the plain. They consume the ion-rich slurry, metabolizing it through exotic electro-chemistry. Their movement is a constant, grinding, skittering symphony. They harvest components, carry them to fracture zones, and secrete catalysts that weld new panes or grow nascent Bellowers. They are both the workforce and the raw material. The density is packed because the Crawlers are everywhere, flowing in predictable, algorithmic streams between feeding, building, and resonant convergence points.

**Rules & Weirdness (Reality Breakdown):**
The world operates on a mix of brutal physics and emergent, systemic “rules” that feel intentional.
1.  **Resonant Synchronization:** The entire plain seeks harmonic equilibrium. A Bellowers’ call will cause nearby Crawlers to temporarily alter their patterns, and even cause the glass sheet to vibrate in sympathetic resonance. Large-scale events are initiated by achieving a critical harmonic threshold.
2.  **Conservation of Structure:** Nothing is truly wasted. A Crawler that “dies” (its reaction core fails) is immediately disassembled by its siblings. A shattered Bellowers is a resource node. The plain maintains its total mass and energy in a closed, self-cannibalizing loop.
3.  **Temporal Folds:** In zones of intense, overlapping resonance (near multiple Bellowers), time feels elastic. Moments of intense activity (a Crawler swarm hive-grow) can be followed by seconds of eerie, stretched stillness. You might see the same Crawler in two places days apart, or witness the slow growth of a Bellowers spire in an instant, as if the event was always there and just now becoming visible. This is the 3.0 weirdness: causality is local and harmonic, not linear.
4.  **No “Built,” Only “Grown”:** The industrial scale is a physical fact, not a sign of intelligence. There are no factories, no engines, no tools. There are only processes. A Bellowers is a factory. A Crawler is an engine. The electrified, mechanized scale is a property of the system itself, scaled to a planetary level.

**Atmosphere & Synthesis:**
The air tastes of ozone and salt. The constant, dim light gives everything a monochrome sheen, broken only by the oily rainbows on the slurry. The cold (0.1/3) is a dry, conductive chill that seeps into everything, mitigated only by the faint, ambient heat of the planet’s core and the exothermic reactions of the Crawlers. You are not an observer in this landscape; you are a disturbance in its harmonic field. Your footsteps are a dissonant clack in the Hum. Your body heat and moisture are a rich, chaotic resource that would instantly attract a investigative Crawler swarm, which would attempt to metabolize your organic compounds and repurpose your bones for crystalline latticework. This is not a hostile world; it is an indifferent one. It is a world that has completed its geology and begun a second, more strange lifecycle of resonant, industrial-scale self-maintenance. It is ancient, packed, loud, active, and utterly without life as we understand it—yet it is more alive with process than any forest.
