The place is called the Singing Marsh, though there is no marsh in the conventional sense. It is a high basin, a bowl of worn stone and ancient soil cupped between shoulders of a long-eroded mountain range. The elevation gives the air a thin, clean quality, but the temperature is a persistent, humid warmth—like the breath of a sleeping giant. This is not a cold high place; it is a warm, damp lung.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The basin floor is a mosaic of textures. Dominating is a deep, peaty soil, dark and sponge-like, saturated just below the surface. It gives way to smooth, rounded domes of a pale, porous stone—the eroded bones of the mountains, worn smooth by a million cycles of condensation and seepage. Between these stones, water does not pool in open channels but *weeps*. It seeps from a thousand fissures in the stone domes, creating a constant, silent film of moisture. Where the water is most persistent, it has carved shallow, interconnected basins no deeper than a hand, their edges glazed with a slick of black algae. The vegetation grows not from soil alone, but *on* the stone, *in* the mist, its roots clutching at the porous rock.

**Atmosphere & Light:**
The air is thick with a fine, cool mist that condenses from the warm, wet breath of the basin itself. It is not raining, yet everything is perpetually beaded. Light is "normal"—a diffused, white daylight that has no sharp shadows. The mist scatters the sun into a soft, glowing ceiling, making the basin feel like a room with its own weather. At dawn and dusk, the mist takes on a faint, sourceless pearlescent sheen, the only hint of the "weirdness" that permeates the place.

**Vegetation (Lush, but Sparse):**
This is the key paradox. *Lushness* is everywhere, but *density* is low. A velvety carpet of mosses, liverworts, and tiny, ground-hugging succulents covers every available inch—stone, soil, fallen twigs. But these are low, patient things. True "plants" are widely spaced, ancient individuals. Great, gnarled trees with bark like cork and leaves like thick, waxy leather stand like solitary sentinels, their canopies just touching in a loose, open roof. Between them, wide, clearings of moss and stone stretch for fifty paces. The vegetation is lush in biomass and vibrancy of color—emerald mosses, ruby-throated pitcher plants, silver-grey lichens—but the spacing is generous, almost ceremonial. You can see clearly from one great tree to the next.

**Water & Hydrology:**
Water is the silent architect. The 1.7 "wet" parameter means water is *near the surface*, not in rivers. Step anywhere, and your foot sinks an inch into cool, saturated moss. The sound is not of flowing water, but of *dripping* and *seeping*—a constant, subdued *plink… plink… plink…* from a thousand sources, a rhythmic punctuation to the deeper hum. In the basins, the water is still and black, reflecting the misty sky like obsidian mirrors. It is water in its slowest, most patient form: condensation, seepage, held potential.

**Erosion & Form:**
The erosion is "established"—the place looks settled into its own perfect, slow state. There are no sharp edges. Every stone is a smooth, organic lump. Soil has accumulated in hollows, not been washed away. The landscape feels *finished*, as if it reached this balance millennia ago and now only rehearses the same gentle cycles. The few.gullies are shallow and moss-filled, not ravines.

**Soundscape (Loud, but Slow):**
Sound is 1.7 "loud," but the dynamic is 1.1 "slow." This creates a profound, resonant *presence*. The dripping is a constant, metronomic tattoo on stone. But the true loudness comes from the wind. When it moves through the basin (which it does, slowly, over hours, not gusts), it does not rush. It *sighs* through the unique morphology of the place. It hums in the hollows of the stone domes—deep, organ-like tones. It whistles through the fluted, petrified trunks of the old trees—a series of soft, melodic pipes. It vibrates the taut, dry leaves of the sentinel trees—a slow, creaking percussion. The result is a continuous, multi-layered composition: a slow, loud, and deeply atmospheric symphony of wind, water, and resonant wood/stone. It is never silent.

**Ecology & Fauna (Rare):**
Fauna is 0.6—rare. Life here is patient and small. The ecology is built on the slow drip. Glimmering, opalescent snails crawl the damp stone. Damselflies, with wings like shards of colored glass, hover motionless for hours above the black water basins. A few species of bird, silent and large-winged, glide between the treetops, landing only to pluck a slow-moving slug from a mossy bough. You might see the track of a small, six-legged mammal in the moss, but the creature itself is a ghost. The dominant life is vegetative and microbial; the animals are fleeting, rare guests.

**The Strange (Weirdness 1.5):**
The strangeness is subtle and geological, not supernatural. The stone domes, when struck, do not sound like rock. They emit a pure, sustained musical note, different for each dome, as if they are giant, buried bells. Some of the black water basins, on the rarest of still nights, emit a faint, cold blue bioluminescence—not from organisms, but from the water itself, a property of the minerals leaching from the stone. The sentinel trees, if you lie with your ear to the roots, seem to hum a different pitch than the wind—a deep, sub-audible vibration felt in the bones. The world is slightly more *responsive* and *resonant* than it should be.

**Human Element (Tech 1.0, Built 0.4):**
There are no structures. But there is evidence of "primitive tech" (early tool-use). Here and there, a stone dome has a single, perfect, man-sized hole bored through its side—a "wind flute" tuned to a specific note. Small, smooth stones are arranged in concentric circles on the moss, not as dwellings, but as acoustic dampeners or amplifiers for the wind songs. A few of the sentinel trees have careful, shallow notches carved into their bark at precise intervals—perhaps to alter the creaking pitch. The inhabitants (if they still come) are not builders, but tuners. They leave no permanent mark but the subtle adjustment of the basin’s innate music. They are few, or perhaps one, moving slowly through the groves with stone hammers and listening ears.

**The Rules of This World:**
1.  **Slowness is Law.** Growth is measured in centimeters per decade. Erosion wears a stone smooth over centuries. The mist cycle is a day-long exhale and inhalation. To hurry is to be violently, dissonantly out of place.
2.  **Resonance is Reality.** The landscape is a living instrument. Sound shapes the mood of the basin; a wrong, sharp noise (a snapped branch, a shout) creates a temporary "silent zone" where the wind dies and the dripping stops, a space of profound unease.
3.  **Water is Memory.** The black basins hold more than water; they hold reflections that are slightly out of sync with the present. Look into one, and you might see the mist of an hour ago, or the silhouette of a tree that has long since fallen. They are not portals, but slightly lagging mirrors.
4.  **Sparsity is Sacred.** The wide spaces between the great trees are not empty; they are *balanced*. To stand in a clearing is to be exactly equidistant from all things, a state of perfect, quiet listening.

**The Whole Emerges:**
You stand in a high, warm basin, breathing air thick with mist and the scent of wet stone and living moss. Before you, a great, cork-barked tree hums softly in the sighing wind. To your left, a dome of pale stone sweats a steady bead of water that falls, *plink*, into a black, silent pool that reflects a sky you cannot quite see. To your right, a second dome emits a low, cello-like drone as the wind passes through its natural flutes. The ground beneath your feet is springy, dark moss, dotted with tiny, jewel-like flowers. Everywhere, there is sound—but it is a loudness of endless, gentle layers, a symphony of patient processes. There are no animals, no people, no buildings. There is only the basin, breathing, dripping, humming, and waiting for the next slow note to be played. It is a place not of events, but of enduring textures and resonant time.
