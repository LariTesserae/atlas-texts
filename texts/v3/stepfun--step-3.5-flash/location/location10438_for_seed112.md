Welcome to the **Mycelium Minérale**.

You stand on a plateau that isn’t rock, but a skin. The scale is continental, a world of high, flat-topped mesas and deep, echoing chasms that stretch to a perpetually bright, cool horizon. The air is thin and sharp, tasting of ozone and something else—ionized minerals, like licking a battery wrapped in wet moss. The light is a brilliant, shadowless white, bleached by a distant, small star and filtered through a high, thin atmosphere that scatters photons into a perpetual late-morning glare. There are no clouds, only veils of fine, glittering dust that hang motionless for hours before a sudden, silent gust throws them into shimmering curtains.

**The Terrain & Materials:**
The ground is a composite. It is primarily a vast, porous crust of **fossilized salt and compressed mineral sediments**, a relic of an ancient, vanished sea. This crust is not inert. It is **the substrate**. Upon and within it grows the totality of the vegetation: the **Sylviform**. The Sylviform is not soil-dwelling; it *is* the soil, the geology, and the atmosphere. It presents as vast, undulating fields of **crystalline, silica-based filaments**—think millions of years of rust, but alive and shifting in slow, tectonic pulses. These filaments grow in fractal tufts and dense, velveteen plains, in colors of tarnished copper, oxidized iron, pearl-white salt, and deep violet manganese oxides. They are rigid yet pliable, humming with internal stress. Interspersed are structures that resemble petrified forests of **black glass coral**, but these are living conduits, pulsing with a faint, cyan bioluminescence that traces nutrient flows. Erosion here is not by water or wind in a conventional sense, but by **chemical exudation**—the plants secrete precise acids and Alkalis that dissolve and redeposit minerals, constantly re-sculpting their own landscape over millennia. The world is established because it has perfected this auto-geopoiesis. It is in perfect, violent balance.

**The Ecology & Rules:**
Life here runs on a non-aqueous, **chemosynthetic-electrical** metabolism. The Sylviform draws energy directly from the planet’s geothermal gradient and the constant electrical potential between the mineral crust and the ionosphere. Its “roots” are vast, shallow networks that tap into telluric currents. Its “leaves” are the exposed filament mats, which act as colossal, slow-charging solar panels and lightning rods. Its “waste” is the deposition of pure, refined crystals—silica, calcite, pyrite—which form the new geological layers.

**Fauna** are not separate from this system; they are **mobile organelles**. The abundant life consists of:
*   **Gilded Runners:** Six-legged, beetle-like creatures whose carapaces are living photovoltaic cells. They scuttle across the filament plains, absorbing light and heat, and periodically burrow into the crust to deposit concentrated energy nodules, which the Sylviform then consumes. Their sound is a constant, dry, papery rustle—a billion tiny feet on crystal.
*   **Atmospheric Spores:** Millions of floating, sac-like organisms that drift on thermal currents. They are part plant (photosynthesizing gas bladders), part animal (feeding on airborne mineral dust). Their life cycle ends in a deafening, synchronized pop as they release genetic spores, creating the **deafening soundscape**. It’s not a single noise, but a layered, relentless thunder: the deep ** HUM** of the planet’s magnetism vibrating the filaments, the **CRACKLE-POP** of mass spore dispersal that rolls across the plains in waves, the **GRINDING SHRIEK** of massive mineral plates slowly sliding past one another as the Sylviform reconfigures the land, and the endless, wind-like **RUSTLE-RATTLE** of the fauna.
*   **Chasm-Wyrms:** Far below, in the heated depths, are blind, sinuous things of molten rock and living slag. They swim through partially-melted mineral beds, their movements causing low, subsonic tremors that shake the high plateaus. They are the planet’s recycling system, consuming the oldest, most depleted crust.

**Atmosphere & Weirdness:**
The air is electrically active. St. Elmo’s fire constantly wicks from the tips of glass-coral spires. **Ball lightning**—packs of agitated spores—roll across the plains like silent, glowing tumbleweeds. The weirdness (1.3/3) is a low, pervasive unreality: the sense that the ground is breathing, that time is measured in crystal growth rings and tectonic yawns. Colors bleed at the edges of vision. Sound has texture; you can *feel* the hum in your molars.

**The Industrial Tech:**
There are no factories, no engines built by hands. The industrial scale (1.8/3) is **biological-geological industry**. The entire world is a single, planet-sized machine. The Sylviform *is* the engine, the factory, the power grid, and the construction crew. The Gilded Runners are its automated harvesters and miners. The Chasm-Wyrms are its smelters and waste disposal. The luminous pulses in the glass coral are its data-transfer cables. The deafening soundscape is the noise of its operation—a self-sustaining, turbulent, hyperactive process of constant creation and reclamation. The dynamic (2.9/3) is not chaos, but a furious, ordered, relentless **process**. The landscape is never still; it is always *becoming*.

**Sensory Experience:**
To stand here is to be assailed. The light is painfully bright, yet the temperature is a cool 12°C (54°F), because the atmosphere is thin and dry. The ground vibrates beneath your feet with a frequency just below hearing. The smell is of hot stone, wet metal, and burnt sugar. The density (0.8/3) is sparse: you can see for fifty kilometers across a plain of violet filaments to a distant range of glittering black glass peaks, with nothing but the moving, glittering specks of the Runners between. The emptiness between life is as vast and defining as the life itself. You are a mote of organic chemistry in a world of mineral industry, your every breath a temporary loan of atoms from the eternal, deafening, beautiful engine.
