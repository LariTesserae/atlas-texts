**Name:** The Stillings  
**Mnemonic:** A city built of roots, under a sea of time.

---

### **The Fundamental Paradox**
You stand at the bottom of a world that has no “up.”  
Elevation here is measured *downward* from a razored ceiling of blue-white ice, a kilometer above. The “extreme elevation” is the depth. You are at the base of the **Axis Abyss**, a vertical canyon system carved not by water, but by the relentless, slow growth and decay of plant-matter over epochs. The “water” is not H₂O as you know it. It is a dense, cool, mineral-saturated **pleromatic fluid**, a biological byproduct of the dominant flora, thick as syrup and buoyant as air. It fills every space, pressure increasing gently toward the ice-ceiling. You are submerged, but you do not drown. You float.

### **Terrain & Architecture: The Built Biosphere**
The “built: total” parameter is the core of the place. There is no natural substrate. Everything—every surface, every depression, every spire—is the constructed work of the **Primordial Groves**. These are not individual trees, but continent-sized, networked organisms whose roots excrete a resinous, stone-hard polymer. Over millions of years, they have *built* the landscape:

*   **The Spires:** Like the buttresses of a drowned cathedral, they rise (or descend, depending on perspective) in graceful, logarithmic spirals. They are composed of layer upon layer of fossilized vascular tissue, now a porous, opalescent stone, still faintly warm from metabolic processes. Their surfaces are etched with the immense, regular ridges of growth-rings wider than a house.
*   **The Vales:** Between spires are vast, open spaces in the fluid—the “sparse density.” Here, the fluid is clearest, and you can see the **Rootweave** below: a horizontal, continent-spanning lattice of living fiber, pulsing with a deep magenta bioluminescence. This is the circulatory system of the Groves. The spaces between the weave are canyons of darkness.
*   **The Platforms:** Cantilevered, leaf-like structures of fused, keratinous material project from spire walls. They are the “dwellings” of the abundant fauna, resting places in the fluid. Some are smooth and worn; others are still actively growing, secreting new layers at a rate of one millimeter per century.

The erosion (2.6 ancient) is absolute. Every edge is rounded, every surface polished by the constant, gentle current of the pleromatic fluid and the abrasive, slow touch of root-tendrils. Nothing is sharp. The world has settled into a final, glacial elegance.

### **Atmosphere & Light**
The darkness (0.3 light) is profound but not empty. The primary light sources are:
1.  **The Ice-Ceiling:** A fractured, glacier-like dome far above, diffusing a faint, cold, blue-white daylight that penetrates the fluid in weak rays.
2.  **The Biosphere:** The Rootweave’s magenta pulse. The surfaces of the Spires are dusted with slow-growing **Phosphor-moss**, emitting a soft, green-gold glow where the bioluminescence seeps through the stone. Individual fauna carry their own tiny points of light.
3.  **Chemical Flares:** Infrequently, a geothermal vent in the deep Rootweave will erupt a bubble of luminous gas that rises for hours, a silent, shimmering jellyfish of light in the syrup-fluid.

The air (or fluid) is cool (0.8), still, and tastes of wet stone and salt. It carries no wind; dynamic is nearly zero. The only “current” is the imperceptible drift of the fluid toward the low-pressure zones of the Rootweave’s intake vents.

### **Ecology: Weird Abundance**
Fauna (2.4 abundant) thrives in this stillness. They are all **gravitational specialists**, adapted to the dense fluid.

*   **The Drifters:** Jellyfish-like entities with bells of transparent membrane, trailing tendrils that filter nutrients from the fluid. They pulse gently, rising and falling centimeters per hour.
*   **The Clingers:** Six-limbed, armored grazers that move with agonizing slowness across the Spire platforms. Their mouths are grinding plates of chitin. They are the “herbivores,” scraping the Phosphor-moss and slow-growing lichens. Their movement is the main source of audible “sound” (2.0 loud)—a constant, deep, groaning scrape as thousands of them move in unison over geological time.
*   **The Silicates:** The apex beings. They resemble mobile, intricate clusters of black volcanic glass, flowing like mercury over the Rootweave. They do not eat; they *absorb* structure, dissolving sections of the organic polymer and re-constituting it into more of themselves. They are the “gardeners” of decay, ensuring the ancient erosion continues.

The vegetation (2.9 total) *is* the geology. The Groves themselves are the only “plants,” in a sense. They are alive, but on a scale that makes a redwood seem like a bacterium. Their “leaves” are the vast, platform-like growths. Their “fruit” are the gas vesicles that occasionally break free from the Rootweave and rise to the ice-ceiling, where they explode silently, seeding new micro-Groves in condensation drips.

### **Sound & The Rule of Equilibrium**
The soundscape (2.0 loud) is a **physical presence**, not an auditory one. The constant, sub-audible groan of the Clingers is translated by the dense fluid into a vibration you feel in your bones—a bass note so low it’s more a pressure than a noise. The Silicates moving over polymer produce a crystalline, glassy hum. Together, they create a harmonic drone that fills the Abyss, the sound of a world in perfect, slow motion.

This leads to the overriding rule: **The Stillness.** The “dynamic: never changing” is literal. All processes are in such slow equilibrium that change is only perceptible through records in the Spire rings or by comparing a Drifter’s position over a decade. There are no seasons, no day/night cycle (only the dim glacial light and the pulsing bioluminescence), no weather. The only “event” is the rare, millennia-spanning migration of a Grove segment, a continental shift that takes a thousand years to complete.

### **The Weirdness (0.8): The Cognitive dissonance**
The unusual feeling comes from the collision of scales and categories:
*   You are in a **city** of impossible architecture, but it is entirely **organic** and **alive**.
*   You are **submerged** in a liquid, but it is not water, and you breathe it easily.
*   You are at a **low elevation** in a valley, but the reference point is an inverted ceiling, making you feel you are at the bottom of a world-mountain.
*   The “built” environment is not made by a species with tools; it is the unintended byproduct of a single, planet-dominated biological process. There are no “devices,” no “technology,” but the result is a more complete, total construction than any civilization could achieve.
*   The “abundant fauna” move in a ballet of such slowness that they seem like fixed features of the landscape until you watch them for hours.

### **What It Feels Like To Be There**
To stand on a Spire platform is to feel a eternal, cool embrace. The light is dim and magical. The air-fluid is thick, making each movement a deliberate push. The bass-hum of the Clingers is in your teeth. You see the magenta pulse of the Rootweave far below, the green-glow of moss under your feet, the distant, slow dance of a Silicate rearranging a spire’s base. You are in a place that is simultaneously an ocean trench, a fungal forest, a ruined city, and a geological cross-section. It is a monument to a single, patient idea, grown over epochs until the distinction between nature and construction, life and geology, has utterly dissolved. It is not a snapshot of a moment, but a single frame from an endless, silent film, playing at a speed so slow the only change is the turning of the Spires’ rings, and the final, inevitable conversion of all polymer into silicate. It is beautiful. It is terrifying. It is perfectly, utterly still.
