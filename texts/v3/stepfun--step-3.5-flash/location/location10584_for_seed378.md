Not a world of growth, but of **process**.

You stand on a high plateau of dark, volcanic glass, ground smooth by a wind that carries not pollen but fine, hot grit. The elevation feels less like height and more like *removal*—a place set apart, above the ordinary. The scale is vast: the plateau stretches for what feels like a hundred valleys, each a shallow bowl of polished black rock, separated by sharp, eroded ridges. It is open not in the sense of emptiness, but in the sense of unobstructed perspective; you could see a storm gathering on the far horizon, a bruise of purple and gold in the bright, relentless light.

The **temperature** is a physical presence, 2.9 on the scale—a dry, radiant heat that shimmers the air. There is no sun, exactly, but a source of brilliant illumination *somewhere* above the perpetual haze, bleaching all colour to a palette of umber, charcoal, and metallic silver. The **water** is not in rivers or lakes. It weeps. From fractures in the glassy rock, slow, viscous ribbons of warm, mineral-rich fluid seep and condense, forming shallow, transient pools that evaporate before they can merge. The rocks themselves sweat a fine, salty mist that cools your skin for a moment before vanishing. The atmosphere is perpetually on the verge of rain that never falls, a damp, metallic tang on the tongue.

**Vegetation** is 0.2—there is none. No moss, no lichen, no blade of grass. The surface is a crust of consolidated ash and obsidian, pocked with geometric patterns that might be ancient cooling cracks or something else. Where the weeping water collects in depressions, it leaves behind crystalline salts in intricate, frost-like dendrites. This is not a dead world; it is a *pre-life* or *post-life* world. Life, here, follows other rules.

And it is **abundant**. **Fauna** at 2.1 is a constant, silent motion. They are not animals as understood. They are mobile architectures of the same dark, glassy material, segmented and jointed with a soundless *clack*. Some are slow, millimeters per hour, their forms reshaping as they absorb minerals from the rock. Others flicker—not running, but *displacing*—leaving after-images in the shimmering heat. They communicate through shifts in their internal structure, emitting brief pulses of infrared light that the bright daylight swallows. They have no mouths, no eyes. They are manifestations of density and pattern, seeking equilibrium with the hot, wet rock. A large, six-legged thing the size of a cow might stand motionless for a day, its surface flowing like slow mercury, before dissolving back into the ground. Their abundance is in numbers and variety, not in biomass. They are everywhere and nowhere, a scattered population that obeys a logic of mineral thermodynamics.

The **sound** is 0.1—a profound, weighted silence broken only by the absolute minutiae: the far-off sigh of a steam vent (the planet’s breath), the soft *pfft* of a mineral creature settling, the crunch of your own footsteps on the glass. It is a silence that makes other sounds feel invasive.

**Built** and **tech** are both 1.7—**urban** and **industrial**, yet sparse. This is the core paradox. The settlement, *Rest Point Sigma*, is not a city of streets and crowds. It is a string of massive, interconnected facilities anchored to the plateau: the **Condensate Array**, a forest of towering, blackened alloy stems that harvest the weeping moisture, dripping into humming, insulated conduits. The **Thermal Siphon**, a buried complex of radiating fins and throbbing pistons that taps the plateau’s geothermal heart, its exhaust a constant, silent plume of superheated steam that stutters into the bright air. The **Pattern Forge**, where the strange fauna are not hunted, but *guided*—magnetic fields and precisely timed sonic pulses shepherd them into slow-moving streams that pass through analysis chambers, their mineral compositions logged, their structural shifts harvested for data or rare catalysts.

There are no houses for people. There are **command spires**—sleek, windowless towers where operators (few in number, their faces perpetually squinting against the brightness) monitor flow rates and energy balances. The infrastructure is layered: pipes and power lines snake over and under the rock, some encased in ceramic, others exposed and glowing with contained lightning. It is a town of function, not of life. The density is in machinery, not in souls. The **scale** is regional; you can walk for an hour between facilities, crossing expanses of untouched, weeping rock where the glass-fauna flicker and flow.

**Erosion** is 1.5—settled, weathered. The sharp ridges are not new, but worn into gentle saddles by the slow, abrasive wind. The factories are stained and patched, their paint blistered by the heat. Everything is in a state of balance with this intense, slow-burning environment. The heat, the dryness, the mineral cycles—they are the true rulers here. Humanity’s industry is just another process, another pattern sitting within the larger, ancient geology.

The **weirdness** is 1.5, and it is not in strange sights, but in the dissonance of the experience. The sheer *activity* (**dynamic** 1.5) of a world with no visible growth: the ceaseless seep of water, the flicker-pace of the fauna, the rhythmic throb of the siphons, the shifting of heat-haze. It is a world of constant, slow, *inorganic* motion. The rules are thermodynamic, not biological. Life is a phase state of matter, like plasma or ice. The silence makes the constant motion feel dreamlike, unreal. The bright, bleaching light erases shadows, making everything look two-dimensional, like a diagram.

The **atmosphere** is thick with carried mineral dust and the ozone tang of the electrical systems. It tastes of salt, iron, and static. The light doesn’t so much illuminate as it *defines*, carving sharp edges on the black glass and the steel, leaving no mystery in shadow. This is a world of definition and process, where the only mystery is why everything is so *busy* in a place that feels, in its vastness and silence, so fundamentally alone.
