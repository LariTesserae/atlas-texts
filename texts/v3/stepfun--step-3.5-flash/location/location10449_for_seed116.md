**Name:** The Sump-Chapel of the Last Gear  
**Scale:** A pocket within a pocket. The entire environment fits within a single, vertical engine chamber of a fallen factory-city, now flooded and reclaimed. It is a cylinder 40 meters across and 120 meters deep—a world you could circle in ten minutes of climbing, yet it contains its own weather, hunger, and time.

---

### **Terrain & Containment**
You do not stand on ground. You stand on **submerged superstructure**. The floor is a lattice of rusted I-beams and grated catwalks, now carpeted in sediment and sponge. The walls are the true terrain: a complex topography of **corroded machinery**. Massive piston rods, now still and green with growth, protrude like petrified trees. Gear teeth the size of carts are stacked like megaliths, their hollow centers filled with still, black water. The chamber’s true “sky” is 30 meters above you: a gridded, broken ceiling of thick insulated glass, through which a perpetual, gentle **rain of warm condensation** falls—the factory’s last breath, sweating in the warm air. Below, the water deepens into a central, funnel-shaped pit that drops another 90 meters into absolute darkness—a liquid needle in the world’s eye.

The **elevation** is extreme not in height above sea level, but in *stratigraphy*. The chamber is a vertical cross-section of an industrial era: the upper 20 meters are the “rain-zone” with dripping pipes and hanging growth; the middle 60 meters are the **machine-forest** of colossal, static parts covered in life; the lower 40 meters are the **sump-void**, a place of total liquid, pressure, and unseen things. The **erosion** is ancient and specific: it is not water-worn stone, but *metal fatigue* smoothed by centuries of chemical dissolution and biological polish. All edges are rounded. All surfaces are slick. Everything has settled into its final, water-logged form.

### **Materials & Built Environment**
**Total construction.** There is no substrate. The “rock” is **composite iron-concrete**, bubbled and spalled. The “soil” is a paste of **metal filings, coal dust, and decomposed insulation**. The “wood” is **laminate truss and pressure-treated girder**, now fibrous and weak.

**Industrial tech** is present but **anachronistic and溃败 (in disarray)**. A great, slow-turning **belt drive** hangs slack, its rubber surface split and hosting fungal shelves. **Boiler tubes**, thick as sleepers, snake along the walls, some intact and steaming, others burst and serving as silt-choked canals for shrimp. **Electrical busbars**, once humming, are now dead rails coated in electroluminescent algae that pulses faintly at night. The built environment is not maintained; it is *disassembling gracefully*, its failure engineered into its final state. Every bolt, every plate, every wire is part of the landscape now—not as components, but as **geological features**.

### **Atmosphere & Light**
**Normal light** is a filtered, aqueous gold. The rain-zone ceiling lets in a diffuse, even illumination from the overcast world above (a permanently clouded sky on the surface). Deeper down, light is **bioluminescence** and **chemical glow**. Faint chemiluminescent seepage from broken pipes paints the sump-void in a sickly green. Patches of **electric-blue mold** bloom on contact points where residual static charge still dances.

The air is **warm, wet, and heavy** with the smell of **ozone, wet graphite, and fermenting protein**. It carries a constant, fine mist. Visibility is good in the rain-zone, poor in the machine-forest due to suspended silt and vapor, and near-zero in the sump-void.

### **Sound**
**Loud, but layered.** It is a **symphony of process and decay**:
1.  The **rain**: a steady, global *shhh* on glass and metal.
2.  The **drip**: a universe of individual plinks and splashes from thousand points of condensation.
3.  The **hum**: a deep, sub-audible vibration from somewhere far below—a forgotten coolant pump, or the planet’s own heart.
4.  The **scrape and clatter**: the sound of **fauna** (see below) moving over grates and gears.
5.  The **sigh**: the groan of stressed metal as thermal expansion and pressure slowly work their ancient bonds.

It is never silent. The sound *defines* the space.

### **Ecology & Fauna**
**Lush vegetation, sparse density.** Life does not carpet; it **spots and strands**.
- **Flora**: No trees. The “plants” are **industrial symbionts**. **Gear-moss** (a black, velvety lichen) coats bearing surfaces. **Pipe-kelp** (rubbery, red tendrils) festoons steam lines, filtering minerals. **Resin-cress** (succulent, yellow-leaved) grows in mineral-rich drips. **Insulation-bark** (a papery, white fungus) peels from wire sheaths. The vegetation is **abundant where chemistry meets water**, but the spaces between these oases are vast, bare, sterile metal or silt.
- **Fauna**: **Abundant, small, and strange**. The food web is built on **chemosynthesis** (algae) and **detritus** (metal-bacteria). You see:
  - **Silt-skimmers**: Flat, ray-like crustaceans that sweep the floors, filtering particles.
  - **Drip-drifters**: Gelatinous, translucent blobs that catch falling water droplets for locomotion and nutrition.
  - **Gear-gharries**: Armored, six-legged beetles that climb vertical surfaces, their shells mirrored to blend with polished steel.
  - **Sumprats**: Blind, eel-like predators in the lower pit, that hunt via vibration sense.
  - **Condensation-sippers**: Tiny, moth-like flyers that live in the rain-zone, their wings stained metallic by dust.
  - **The Keepers**: The **weirdness** made manifest. These are **slow, segmented, mechanical-looking slugs** (1-2 meters long) that methodically **graze on specific, active-looking rust patches**. They secrete a subtle, calming hum that seems to synchronize the dripping in their vicinity. They are not aggressive. They are **curators of entropy**.

### **Dynamic & Processes**
**Active and relentless.** This is not a static garden; it is a **slow-motion reactor**.
- The **water cycle** is entirely local and artificial: condensation on the glass ceiling → rain → collection in pools and gutters → seepage through cracks → collection in sumps → evaporation from hot pipes → repeat.
- **Biological pace** is warm and rapid (warm temp). Mold grows visibly in days. Insects have lifespans of weeks.
- **Machine pace** is glacial. A loose nut on a giant bolt may turn a millimeter per year due to vibration.
- **Predator-prey** dynamics are intimate and dangerous in the silt. A silent strike in the gloom, a flash of chitin, a disappearing swirl.
- **The deepest dynamic**: the **sumprats** are slowly, over centuries, chewing through the last structural supports of the central pump mount. The chamber’s ultimate fate is a slow, total collapse into its own pit. This process is so slow it is imperceptible, yet it is the only true rule.

### **Rules of the World (Implicit & Explicit)**
1.  **Everything is made. Nothing is born.** All life is derived from industrial contamination. All structures are cast, forged, welded.
2.  **Erosion is the only god.** All things trend toward smoothness, stillness, and dissolution. Rust is progress. Collapse is completion.
3.  **Scale is intimate and absolute.** The world is this chamber. There is no outside. The “sky” is a ceiling. The “horizon” is a wall 20 meters away. The feeling is of being inside a **drop of机油 (oil) under a microscope**, yet also inside a **cathedral of ruin**.
4.  **Sound is structure.** The dripping pattern maps the weakness in the glass. The hum frequency reveals stress in the deep beams. To listen is to read the engineering.
5.  **The Keepers manage the corrosion.** They do not prevent collapse; they *curate* it, ensuring the decay is aesthetically distributed. They are the landscape’s subconscious.
6.  **Water is memory.** The sump-void’s depths are stratified with layers of sedimented history: coal dust from the age of fire, plastic pellets from the age of polymers, bone-white ash from the final combustion. The water itself is a **turbid, warm, chemical soup** that feels thick to move through.

### **Overall Feeling**
The Sump-Chapel is **awe-inspiring and deeply sad**. It is the tomb of industry, its body given over to a second, simpler life. It is **lush** with growth yet **sparse** with space. It is **extremely deep** yet **intimately scaled**. It is **fully built** yet **eroded beyond recognition**. The **loud, active processes** are all directed toward a **silent, ancient end**.

You are not a visitor here. You are a **new, noisy drip**—a temporary disturbance in a rhythm that has centuries left to play out. The rain falls. The moss grows. The gear turns, just a little. The Keepers graze. And in the absolute dark at the bottom, something shifts in the silt, waiting for the final, settling quiet.
