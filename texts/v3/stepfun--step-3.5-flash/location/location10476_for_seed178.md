**Terrain & Geology:**
The basin is a vast, shallow bowl, perhaps twenty kilometers across, ringed by jagged, knife-edged mountains of a pale, blue-tinged rock that seems to absorb light. They are unnervingly young, these peaks. No talus slopes, no rounded weather. The cliffs are fresh fractures, as if the world was torn apart yesterday. Between the peaks, glaciers not of white ice but of a translucent, violet-hued material pulse with a slow, internal light, calving not with a crash but with a deep, sub-audible *thrum* that you feel in your teeth.

The floor is a complex mosaic. Great plains of perfectly level, steaming silt are broken by polygonal fields of cracked, dark mud that hiss with escaping gases.Spires of the same black glass as your shelf, some taller than trees, rise in crystalline forests, humming at frequencies that make your vision swim if you stare too long. And everywhere, the water. It doesn’t pool so much as it *seeps*: from the base of glass spires, from the joints in the violet ice, from the very pores of the soil. It’s never still. Roots are visible everywhere, thick and sinewy, gripping the glass and mud, sometimes glowing with a soft bioluminescence where they tap into geothermal veins. The density is overwhelming; you have to push through curtains of vine-like things that retract at your touch with a sighing *shush*. **Ecology & Rules of the World:**
This is a world of **Resonant Equilibrium**. The young, crystalline geology is inherently unstable and vibrates at specific frequencies. Life has evolved not to avoid this vibration, but to * harness it*. The plants’ growth, the animals’ communication, even the weather patterns (which are less "weather" and more "localized atmospheric resonance events") are all tuned to, and modulated by, the basin’s hum.

*   **Rule of Symbiosis:** The fauna, flora, and geology are locked in a tight feedback loop. A Resonator’s shriek might stabilize a dangerously vibrating spire, causing it to grow a sweeter-tasting, more nutritious shoot, which the Resonator then eats.
*   **Rule of Transience:** Form is temporary. Things grow fast, vibrate apart, and are reabsorbed. A beautiful, intricate ice-formation might crystalize on a rock face in an hour, hold its shape for a day, then dissolve back into mist with a sigh.
*   **Rule of Scale:** The same patterns repeat. The polygonal mud-cracks are mirrored in the fractal branching of the stalks, which are mirrored in the vast tectonic fractures of the basin rim.
