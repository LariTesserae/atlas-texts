**Name, if one were needed:** The Still Hollow.

It is not a valley, not a basin, but a *sinking*—a depression in the high moorlands where the world’s breath condenses and falls inward. The elevation is high, but here the air is cool and thick with moisture, pressing down like a saturated blanket. You stand on a ridge of pale, water-cut stone and look down into it. The scale is intimate, a single bowl perhaps a kilometer across, rimmed by low, bald hills. There are no paths down that you can see. The descent is through mist.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The floor is not earth, but a渐渐 spongy mat of generations. It is a peat-bog-garden, a thick, dark compost of decayed moss and drowned root that gives silently underfoot, drinking every drop. Water is everywhere, but not in rivers. It seeps from the weeping, blackened rock faces that ring the hollow, weeping not with streams but with a constant, glistening film. It condenses from the air onto every surface—broad leaves, lichen-crusted stones, the chitinous backs of things—and beads into perfect, trembling drops that never quite fall. Pools are shallow, ink-black and still as oil, reflecting the inverted world above with flawless clarity. The ground is a mosaic of sphagnum in greens so deep they are almost navy, of carnivorous pitcher plants glossy and purple-lipped, of ghostly-white fungi that bloom from the water-saturated wood of ancient, drowned thickets.

**Atmosphere & Light:**
Darkness is the baseline. Light is a visitor, and a shy one. A perpetual, pearlescent mist clogs the air, diffusing what little illumination filters through the high canopy of stunted, weeping willows and twisted, moss-bearded conifers. This is not night, nor is it day. It is the *blue hour* made manifest, a world bathed in the soft, cool glow of twilight that never ends. The source is not the sun, but the air itself—and the things within it. A dim, pervasive bioluminescence bleeds from the flora: the hoods of the pitcher plants pulse with a slow, violet erythema; thread-like fungi along the water’s edge emit a cold, green phosphorescence; the very mist seems to shimmer with motes of gold when you still your breathing. Your eyes adjust to see forms in the gloom: the sheen on a beetle’s carapace, the wet gleam of an eye at the waterline.

**Sounds:**
Mostly quiet, as the parameter says. The sponge of the peat, the distant, hollow *plink* of a water-drop finally achieving mass and vanishing into a pool—these are the metronomes of the place. But the quiet is not empty; it is *full*. A susurrus, too constant to be noticed as sound, rises from the millions of tiny lives: the rasp of insect legs on wet leaves, the synchronized hum of proboscis against flower-throat, the faint, wet crunch of something small consuming something smaller in the black peat. It is the sound of a closed system breathing.

**Ecology & Fauna:**
Density: 2.6/3 — packed. *Overwhelming*: 2.7/3. Life here does not occupy niches; it occupies volumes. The air thrums with midges and tiny, metallic-winged flies that cluster in living clouds. The water’s surface is a skin of water-walkers, their legs creating constant, minute ripples. The peat is a writhing tapestry of worms, blind salamanders, and iridescent beetle larvae. Every surface is colonized—lichens, mosses, slime-molds, fungi in fractal patterns. The overwhelming fauna is invertebrate, amphibian, and avian in miniature: jewel-toned frogs no larger than a thumbnail, their calls a single, pure *peep* that echoes; choruses of snouted, burrowing birds that move like ghosts through the mist, their feathers slicked down, their eyes enormous and black. Predation is not a dramatic chase, but a silent, inevitable transfer of mass—a fly’s wing brushing a fungus that blooms with quick, hungry tendrils; a tadpole vanishing into a pitcher plant’s sweet-scented trap. Everything is both hunter and habitat.

**Rules of the World & Weirdness:**
The weirdness (1.0/3) is not in monsters, but in *principles*. This is a place of **dissolution and co-option**. Nothing remains itself for long. Rock becomes soil becomes water becomes mist becomes leaf becomes flesh becomes spore. The boundary between organism and environment is a polite fiction. The "weeping rocks" are not just wet; they are being *consumed* by slow-growing, acid-secreting lichens that taste like iron. The trees are not just growing; they are being re-shaped by the weight of the moss, their branches flowing downward like wet hair. The water is not just H₂O; it is a solution of countless dissolved organic compounds, a tea of the place itself. To drink it is to ingest the essence of the hollow.

**Erosion & Dynamic:**
Erosion: 2.5/3 — established. The forms are settled, weathered, in-balance. The landscape is a masterpiece of hydraulic and organic smoothing. There are no sharp edges. Stones are egg-shaped. Roots flow over rock like poured wax. The very mist erodes the concept of horizon. And the dynamic is **0.0/3 — never changing**. This is the core truth. There is no day/night cycle, no seasonal shift. The temperature, cool and constant, never varies. The water level in the pools does not rise or fall; it is. The populations of the midges and the frogs do not boom and bust; they are in a perfect, eternal steady-state. Birth and death are so constant and rapid they cancel out into a single, seamless process of transformation. Time here is not a river; it is a still pool. You could leave and return in a century, and the exact same drop of condensed water would be weeping from the exact same pore in the rock.

**The Presence of the "Primitive" and the "None":**
Built: 0.1/3 — none. There are no shelters, no fire rings, no carved symbols. And yet, tech: 1.1/3 — primitive. The evidence is faint, almost part of the ecology. Here and there, a perfect, semi-translucent *bone*—of a bird, of something larger, long gone—propped against a rock, not as a trophy, but as a *spore dispersal platform*, flecked with luminous fungal spores. A few stones, arranged in shallow, kidney-shaped basins that collect the purest condensation, their rims worn smooth by countless hands—or claws—over millennia. These are not ruins. They are *features*. They have been so fully re-absorbed into the system that they are now as natural as the peat or the mist. They are the hollow’s oldest, most successful species of tool: stone and bone, made part of the cycle.

**Synesthesia & Final Impression:**
The smell is of wet stone, sweet decay, and the sharp, clean scent of constantly evaporating water. The touch is of perpetual, cool dampness that permeates everything. The taste on the air is faintly metallic, like licking a cold stone.

To be in the Still Hollow is to be** immersed in a single, sustained, biological moment**. It is a place that has achieved its ultimate form and now refuses to budge from it. It does not progress; it *perpetuates*. You are not an observer here, but a temporary disturbance in the density—a warm, dry, *changing* thing in a world that has perfected the art of staying exactly as it is. Your breath fogging in the cool, bioluminescent air is the most violent event to occur in this basin in a thousand years. And as you watch, a droplet of that breath condenses on a pitcher plant’s lip, Gleams violet for a second, and is drawn in—becoming part of the still, overwhelming, eternal now.
