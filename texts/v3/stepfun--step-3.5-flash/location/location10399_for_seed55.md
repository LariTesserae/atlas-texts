It is not a place of horizons. There is no sky to speak of, only a uniform, lightless expanse that might be cloud or might be water or might be the pressed-in ceiling of the world. The air is thick with a cold, wet silence, broken only by the very occasional, deep *plink* of a water droplet falling from a height that cannot be discerned, or the soft,湿滑 (shīhuá—slimy) rasp of something growing against stone.

You are within the **Deep-Works**.

The scale is the first lie, and the last truth. You feel tiny, yet the space you occupy is part of a whole that feels continental. The ground—if ground it can be called—is a series of vast, shallow basins and gentle, sprawling terraces, all submerged under a uniform film of dark, still water perhaps a foot deep. This water is not clear; it is the tint of old ink and cold tea, tasting of stone and slow decay. It laps against the edges of what is beneath it.

And what is beneath it is **everything**.

The **built** parameter is total, absolute, and yet almost entirely obscured. This is not a city of towers and roofs. It is a city of foundations, of pods, of layered, interlocking geometries that have been smoothed and muted by the **erosion** of ages. You walk (you wade, you slide) across the top of a colossal dome, its curve so vast you cannot see its edges, now carpeted in a dense, clinging moss. You pass through the ribbed arches of a structure that might have been a bridge or a aqueduct, its stones paved over with a succulent, violet-leafed vegetation that thrives in the dim, cold wetness. The **vegetation** is lush in its way—not with bright flowers or soaring trees, but with a staggering, suffocating variety of low-growing things: carpets of iridescent liverworts, curtains of gelatinous fungus that glow with a faint, bioluminescent grey, thickets of black, reed-like tubes that sigh air when disturbed. It is a **dense** ecology, every surface occupied, every cubit of volume filled with growth or the residue of growth.

The **materials** are utterly alien and utterly familiar. You see the smooth, cool planes of a material like polished basalt, but it is too perfectly regular, laid in vast, seamless plates. You see the intricate, thread-like weaving of what might be ancient, petrified rope or root, now as hard as metal, forming great lattices that hold back nothing but sediment. There are no sharp corners. Everything has been worn to a gentle radius, a soft transition, as if the very concept of an angle has been eroded away over the eons. This is the **ancient** state: not ruin, but completion. The final, flattened form.

The **temperature** is a consistent, penetrating chill. The water is cold enough to be uncomfortable, the air carries a damp that seeps into the bones. There is no wind. The **sound** profile is mostly quiet because there is nothing to make sound. No birds, no insects, no chattering water. Just the infrequent, resonant *plink*, and the constant, sub-audible hum of slow growth and slow decay—a feeling in the teeth more than a noise.

The **light** is a profound, liquid darkness. You cannot see your own hand before your face. Vision is useless. Your perception is through touch and a shadowy sense of scale. You know a wall is there because the growth on it changes texture under your hand, because the water current shifts. You know a chasm is there because the dense growth of moss on the opposing lip is a different, more desperate species, and because the silence from below is somehow deeper, a well of quiet.

The **rules** of this world are simple and brutal:
1.  **All is substrate.** There is no "natural" rock or soil. Every surface is manufactured, even if it is now indistinguishable from geology. The mountains are slag heaps. The valleys are excavated quarries. The rivers are canals, frozen in time.
2.  **Growth is the only dynamic.** The slowest possible. A moss spreads by a millimeter a year. A crystal of salt grows in a crevice over a century. The only change is the perpetual, patient process of life etching itself over the memory of construction.
3.  **Depth is vertical, not horizontal.** The world is a series of layers. The top layer is the shallow, cold water and its delicate ecosystem. Below that is the permeable, crumbled matrix of the built structure—collapsed chambers, choked tunnels, air pockets sealed for millennia. Deeper still is the original, impermeable shell of the construction, the foundational geometry, which may still be intact, empty, and perfectly dry.
4.  **Primitive tech** is the only tech that could have made this. Massive, coordinated labor with simple levers, ropes, and fire-hardened tools. No motors, no explosives, no complex power. Just endless, generational effort to shape the world on a continental scale, using materials that were somehow both organic and synthetic, now turned to stone.

The **fauna** score is zero, and it shows. No fish dart. No crustaceans scuttle. The water is sterile, the plants draw their nourishment from the chemical leaching of the built materials themselves—a slow, alchemical digestion of the civilization that created them. You are the only mobile animal here, a brief, warm anomaly in this deep, cold tomb-garden.

The **atmosphere** is one of ultimate settlement. This is not a world ending in fire or ice, but in *thickening*. The final state is not emptiness, but a complete, homogeneous blending of the made and the growing, where the memory of maker is erased by the patience of moss. It is a monument not to a people, but to the principle that all architecture is eventually landscape, and all landscape is eventually just… substrate.

You reach out and touch a wall. It is cool, damp, and covered in a velvet-like growth. You pull your hand back, and for a moment, in the perfect blackness, you imagine you see the faint, ghostly imprint of a handprint—not yours, but from the builder, from ten thousand years of erosion ago—a negative space where a human palm once rested on this same impossible, submerged stone. The thought is more haunting than any sound. You are not alone in history, only in the present. The place is full of presences, all of them gone, all of them leaving only the trace of their passing in the layering of this dense, dark, endless, built wilderness.
