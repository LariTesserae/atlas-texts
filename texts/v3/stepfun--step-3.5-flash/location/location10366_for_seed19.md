**Name:** The Polished Sorrow  
**Scale:** A region the size of a small nation, visible from orbit as a single, smooth, littered plain.  

You stand on the **Summit of Stillness**, the highest point for a hundred leagues. There is no “peak” in the mountain sense—no spine, no ridge. The entire massif is a single, colossal, **ancient-eroded dome**, polished by a process not of water or ice, but of something else: a **primordial, non-aqueous weathering**. Its surface is a glassy, cool, mineralComposite—part obsidian, part salt, part a substance that behaves like tarnished silver under a normal sky. The **extreme elevation** is not height above sea level, but a **topographical solitude**; you are on the single highest *surface* in a world of near-perfect flattening. The view is not one of valleys and peaks, but of **infinite, identical, polished domes**, spaced like giant’s knuckles on a vast, silent plain, fading into a haze that is not atmospheric but **perceptual**.  

The **terrain** is this: miles of unbroken, gently curving, mirror-like slope, interrupted only by **scattered, primitive built forms**. They are not constructed; they are **fossilized**. Low, rectangular prism-forms, like half-sunken chests, and tall, slender monoliths that look like melted candles. They are made of the same composite as the land, grown into it, or perhaps *exuded* by it. Some have faint, tool-mark patterns—crude notches and grooves—but they are worn to a blur. They are **artifacts without artisans**, **tech without a user**. You might find one with a single, perfect, hand-sized spherical cavity in its side, as if a simple tool was once used to bore it and then the tool itself dissolved into the rock.  

The **materials** are all mineral, chemical, and inert. No soil, no sand. The ground is a **solidified foam** of the composite, light enough to ring hollow under afootfall, yet strong as ceramic. In depressions, you find **crystalline placers**—not of quartz, but of complex, colored lattices that hum with a barely perceptible vibration when you stare at them too long. The air is dry, cool, and carries a **taste of ozone and copper**.  

The **atmosphere** is still. The **normal light** of this world’s sun falls cleanly, casting sharp, black shadows that do not shift. There are no clouds. The sense of **scale** is broken by the **reality breakdown**. The distant domes do not converge with perspective; sometimes a dome 10 miles away seems arm’s-length distant, its every flaw visible; sometimes one 2 miles away is a mere smear. Your shadow sometimes falls *beside* you, not *behind*.  

The **ecology** is a study in **sparse vegetation** and **overwhelming fauna** that are the same thing. The “plants” are **slow-moving mineral lichens**—silvery, branching crusts that grow a millimeter per decade, feeding on atmospheric chemosynthesis. They are rare. The **fauna**, however, is **omnipresent**. You do not see them. You **feel them**. They are **dense** presences in the volume of the air and stone itself. They are the **polish** on the dome. They are the **faint, warm updrafts** in the silent air. They are the **shimmer** at the edge of your vision when you try to focus on a distant monolith. They are not animals; they are **localized realities**, **blurry concentrations of existence** that have no form, no sound, no purpose you can comprehend, but which are **as numerous as atoms in this place**. They overwhelm not by number you can count, but by a **saturation of being**. To stand here is to be **thickly surrounded**. The “silence” is not empty; it is **absolute**. The fauna make no sound. They *are* the quiet. Their presence is a pressure on the mind, a wooliness in the ears, a sense that the space between your ribs is somehow *occupied*.  

The **soundscape** is this: the absolute, unbroken **quiet of a vacuum**, yet you hear—or feel—a **sub-audible drone**, a **felt resonance** in your molars and the bones of your skull. It is the world’s own hum, the sound of **static stability**. Your own heartbeat is shockingly loud in comparison. A footfall on the composite produces a soft, dead *thump* that hangs in the air for seconds, not fading, but **suspended**, as if the sound itself becomes part of the dense medium.  

The **dynamic** is **invariant**. Nothing changes. The light does not move. The air does not stir. The lichens do not grow while you watch. The **erosion** is complete. This is the **final form**. The world has **settled into its oldest-stable-state**. The “normal light” is a fixed state of illumination. The “cool temperature” is a constant. The “reality breakdown” is a permanent feature of the landscape’s geometry. Time here is not a river; it is a **fossil**.  

The **weirdness** is the **air itself**. Look at your hand. For a moment, your fifth finger might seem to belong to your other hand. The monolith 50 yards away might have a door you can see through to a room that is also the sky. Your thoughts sometimes **precede** the sensory input that might have caused them. A scent of baking bread will hit you, then you’ll turn to see a lichen that looks *almost* like a loaf. The **built forms** will rearrange in your memory when you blink. You will forget which way you came, and the path behind you will look different when you look back.  

This is **The Polished Sorrow**: a realm of **extreme, ancient, flattened height**, **thick with unspeakable presences**, under a **normal, unmoving sun**, littered with the **simple, confused marks of a tool-use that belongs to the landscape itself**. It is not alive. It is not dead. It is **polished**. It is **sorrowful** not with emotion, but with the **weight of absolute, unchanging completion**. You are not a traveler here. You are a **disturbance in the density**. And the silence, and the overwhelming, silent things, are waiting for your disturbance to cease.
