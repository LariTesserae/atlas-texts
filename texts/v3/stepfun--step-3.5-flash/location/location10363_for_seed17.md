You are not drowning. That is the first lie this place tells you.

You are suspended in a substance that feels like water but behaves like a memory of water. It has weight, but not cohesion. It presses in from all sides with a cold, metallic kiss, but when you reach out, your hand passes through it with only the faintest shudder—as if pushing through a thought. The scale is wrong. You are high—terrifyingly, vertiginously high—yet there is no “down” to fall into, only a featureless, dim expanse that stretches in all directions. The “elevation” is not of land, but of pressure gradients. You are in the upper strata of a world-ocean miles deep, floating in a cold, dense, almost empty layer just below the ceiling of the entire sea.

The light is normal, which is the second lie. There is no sun here. The illumination is a sourceless, pearlescent glow that bleeds from the “water” itself and from the structures, giving everything the look of an old photograph developed in liquid mercury. It reveals a landscape of impossible geology: jagged, razor-edged spires of black volcanic glass and fused mineral sand, standing in terrible isolation. They are young, these mountains. Their edges are sharp enough to draw blood from a thought. They show no sign of wave action, no sandblasting, no softening. They were *born* here, fractured upward in a single, violent belch of the planet’s core and instantly quenched in this silent, dense fluid. The erosion level is a joke; these are newborn fangs in a dead god’s mouth.

The sound is the third and most profound lie. It is not *a* sound. It is *the* sound. A deep, subsonic thrum that vibrates in the fillings of your teeth and the base of your spine. It is the sound of the world’s circulation. Somewhere, impossibly far below, titanic engines—the industrial tech level made manifest—drive planetary-scale pumps, stirring the global ocean, maintaining the thermal and chemical gradients that keep this upper layer in its cold, stagnant, frozen equilibrium. The thrum is punctuated by sharp, cracking *reports* like continental plates snapping, and the distant, groaning creak of a billion tons of mineral shifting a millimeter. It is a deafening silence, this noise; it has no melody, no rhythm, only the monolithic pulse of static pressure.

There is no vegetation. 0.2/3. The ecosystem does not build; it *incurs*. The fauna (1.5/3 — abundant) is not plant-eater or predator. It is *process*. You see shapes in the gloom: slow, drifting ribbons of self-organizing metallic grit that twist into temporary, intelligent-looking knots before dissolving. Schools of angular, silica-shelled creatures that look like floating gears, clicking softly as they mesh and pass, harvesting dissolved metallic ions. Larger, thing-like presences move with geological patience along the glassy ridges—they are not animals, but animated erosion, walking scars that scrape and grind, their purpose unknown. They are the abundant life of a mineral world, a pre-biological or post-biological ecology where energy is drawn from thermal differentials and chemical potential, not sunlight.

The built environment (0.6/3 — scattered) is the only thing that is not entirely natural. And it is *not* ruins. It is… infrastructure. A single, colossal lattice of corroded steel, kilometers across, hangs between two spires like a spider’s web made of bridge cables. A series of perfect, cube-like black monoliths, featureless and windowless, are arranged in a precise, decaying grid on a sloping shelf. They are not temples or homes. They are junction boxes. Control stations. Overpressure valves for a system whose original purpose has long since been forgotten by the machinery that still, mindlessly, runs it. The industrial tech (2.0/3) is not steampunk or sleek. It is *utilitarian*, brutal, and scale-broken. These are parts of a machine larger than a country.

The weirdness (0.9/3 — unusual) is in the contradictions. The density (0.1/3 — empty) is the void between these presences. You could be centimeters from a drifting gear-school and see only the faintest shimmer. The space between the mineral “fauna” is almost a perfect vacuum, a nothingness that makes the solid things feel like hallucinations. And the dynamic (0.4/3 — never changing) is the lie that holds it all. *Nothing flows*. The currents are fixed. The chemical gradients are locked in a perfect, glacial stalemate. The engines below pump, but the world’s state does not change. It is a infinite, deafening, frozen moment. A planet caught in the act of *becoming* a machine, and then forgetting to finish.

This is the Mantle-Still. A sunless, plantless, nearly-void sea on top of a mountain range, under a sky of thick, motionless fluid. Lit by a glow with no source. Populated by shapes that are less alive than *active*. Filled with the sound of engines that maintain a perfect, eternal equilibrium. You are not in a landscape. You are inside a diagram. A single, unchanging equation written in glass, steel, and thrumming pressure. The scale is vast enough that the “weather” is the slow, continental drift of thoughtless machinery. And the only rule is that nothing, *nothing*, ever changes. Except, perhaps, for you. Your presence is the first gradient in a billion years. The water—or whatever this is—does not know what to do with you yet. It is observing. And its observation is the loudest sound of all.
