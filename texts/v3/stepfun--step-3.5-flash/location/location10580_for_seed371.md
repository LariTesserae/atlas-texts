The place is called the **Gorge of Falling Glass**.

It is not a gorge in the conventional sense. It is a single, sheer-faced, horizontal gash in the world, a fracture in the continental plate only a kilometer across but plunging downward for three. The scale is cathedral-like, but the cathedral is lying on its side, its nave a abyssal trench. The elevation is extreme not in height above sea level, but in vertical *discontinuity*—the floor of the gorge is a high-altitude plateau, and the rim is the peak of a mountain range. To stand on the western rim is to look down into a sub-alpine basin; to stand on the eastern floor is to look up at a cliff face taller than the deepest oceanic trench is deep.

**Terrain & Materials:** The rock is **obsidian and pumice**, freshly shattered. The erosion is young: the fracture is geologically new, maybe only ten thousand years old. The walls are knife-edged, jagged, and glazed black where steam still weeps from microfissures. Great fans of **volcanic glass scree** slope down from the walls at impossible angles, constantly shifting in small, granular avalanches that sound like dry bones clattering. The floor is not dirt but a crust of **mineral salts and sintered algae**, cracked into a polygon pattern, overlying a network of地下 geothermal streams.

**Atmosphere & Light:** The darkness is profound. The gorge walls overhang in many places, creating a permanent, cool twilight. The only light comes from three sources:
1.  **Chemiluminescence:** Where mineral-rich water (the "damp," 0.6) meets sulfurous vents, faint, sickly green and blue glows pulse on the water’s surface and on the salt crusts.
2.  **Incandescence:** Where superheated steam condenses on cooler rock faces, it leaves behind streaks of **thermally glowing silica**, a dim, hot red like banked coals.
3.  **Reflected Starlight:** The obsidian, where polished by constant micro-falls of scree, acts as a mirror. On clear, cool nights (temperature 1.2), the gorge’s black walls reflect the stark, brilliant canopy of the high-altitude sky, making it seem as if you are standing at the bottom of a starfield well.

**Water:** It is not a river, but a **system of intermittent, acidic rivulets and steam-condensate flows**. Because the scale is "small" in expanse but immense in depth, these water systems are contained, looping back on themselves in a closed hydrological cycle. Hot water boils from vents, flows a hundred meters, evaporates into the cool air, condenses on the opposite wall, and drips back down. You can stand in one damp, moss-carpeted alcove and see, in the gloom, the ghostly arcs of separate waterfalls beginning and ending in mid-air on the other side of the chasm.

**Vegetation (Lush, 2.5 / Dense, 2.1):** The life is **basaltic lichen-fungal mats** and **carnivorous mosses**, but they have evolved to fill every niche. The "lushness" is a visual saturation of color—deep purples, violent yellows, blood-reds—against the black. These growths are not just plants; they are the **industrial tech (1.8)**. The "factories" are great, pulsing diaphragms of **resonant lichen** that grow in perfect, layered frustums on the walls. They use the constant micro-tremors of the scree falls and the pressure oscillations of the steam vents to vibrate, creating a low-frequency hum (part of the soundscape). This vibration mechanically processes dissolved minerals from the water, precipitating them into complex, metallic spires and filaments that harvest nutrients. The "engines" are symbiotic clusters of **heat-shroom mycelia** that channel geothermal energy, their metabolic heat keeping localized pockets of air in violent, convective motion. The "production lines" are the endless, slow crawl of the silica-fiber mosses, which spin microscopic threads from mineral-saturated vapor, weaving them into stronger mats that colonize new, unstable scree slopes.

**Sound (Loud, 1.7 / Turbulent, 2.9):** The soundscape is a **physical presence**. It is not music, but the raw output of the process.
*   **The Grinding Chorus:** The endless, granular roar of scree cascading down every slope, a sound like continental plates grinding, but on a human scale. It never stops.
*   **The Steam Hiss & Bang:** Vents release pressure in rhythmic, burping blasts or steady, piercing hisses. Some are so regular they set the tempo for the entire gorge.
*   **The Resonant Hum:** The industrial lichen diaphragms produce a deep, subsonic thrum that you feel in your bones more than hear. It modulates with the steam pressure, rising to a painful dissonant chord when a major vent blows.
*   **The Drip-Cascade Symphony:** Countless water droplets, falling from condensing steam plumes, strike different surfaces—salt, glass, moss, metal spires—creating a dense, pointillist tapestry of *tinks, plops, and splats*. In the enclosed space, it becomes a deafening, arrhythmic percussion.
*   **The Wind Howl:** Cool air from the rim funnels down into the gorge, but its path is fragmented by the dense, vertical growths, creating a thousand localized, whistling eddies and shrieks that weave through the main sounds.

**Ecology & Rules:** There is **no animal life (0.2)**. Every ecological function—pollination, seed dispersal, predation, decomposition—is handled by the vegetative-industrial complex. The carnivorous mosses "hunt" by ensnaring dust and microscopic silica particles. The "pollination" of the resonant lichen is acoustic: specific vibrational frequencies from the steam vents trigger the release of mineral spores. "Decomposition" is chemical: acidic mists dissolve older, failed growths, and their minerals are immediately reabsorbed by the mats.

The **rules of the world** are those of a **hyper-efficient, brutal, closed-loop system**. Stability is temporary. A new fissure opens, a vent clogs, a great scree fall buries a "factory" colony—these are not disasters, but the **turbulent (2.9) re-scrambling of the production process**. The landscape is in a constant state of **creation and destruction**, not over millennia, but in observable days. A metallic spire, grown from a lichen mat over a week, can be sheared off by a rockfall the next morning. The "industrial" process is not for a purpose; it *is* the purpose. The system processes geothermal and atmospheric energy into complex mineral and crystalline structures because that is its nature. It is an anthill of geology and botany, a bazaar of chemical transactions happening at the edge of a cliff.

**Sensation & Emergence:** To be in the Gorge of Falling Glass is to be **immersed in a living engine room carved by God and abandoned to its own devices**. The air is cool (1.2), damp, and tastes of sulfur and iron. The ground is never still; you balance on a crust that vibrates with the thrum of the world’s breath. The darkness is broken by the cold starlight above and the hot, mineral glows within. You are surrounded by aggressive, functional beauty: the terrible elegance of a growth that converts sound into stone, the terrifying geometry of a shear wall where a new, glowing fissure is yawning open with a hiss. The density (2.1) is overwhelming—every surface is covered, every space filled with some form of processing life, all humming, dripping, grinding, and falling in a chaos so complete it achieves a kind of terrible, loud harmony. It is a place that *does*, relentlessly, in the dark.
