## The Salt Lick

**Terrain & Scale:**  
You are in the Salt Lick, a subsided industrial district the size of a small hamlet, perhaps three quarters of a square mile. The ground does not slope so much as *remember* sloping; it is a series of broad, shallow basins and low, weathered berms, all of it underlain by a labyrinth of flooded vaults and choked tunnels. The elevation is below the original surface—you walk upon the roofs of drowned buildings, or across salt-crusted decks that were once streets. The scale is intimate and confining: a tall brick chimney looms like a cathedral spire, but you can throw a stone from one massive, moss-eaten boiler to the next. Boundaries are clear: a collar of higher, drier ground (the ruined city proper) rises just beyond sight in the perpetual mist, and beyond that, the true open marsh—a vast, wet silence.

**Materials & Atmosphere:**  
Everything is permeated by a cool, mineral dampness. The primary materials are: 
- **Brick and Iron:** Weathered to a uniform, soft black-grey, sugar-grained by salt. Iron has not rusted so much as *bloomed*—great, flaky, rust-orange scabs that peel away to reveal smoother, darker metal beneath, as if the metal is exhaling.
- **Salt:** Not just on surfaces, but *in* them. A white, glittery crust coats everything in hollows and leeward sides, thick as wool on horizontal surfaces. In places, it has precipitated into fragile, translucent sheets—salite—that hang like forgotten parchment from steel beams.
- **Sludge & Slime:** A fine, black, oily silt coats lower surfaces. Where water is still, a skin of iridescent bacteria floats, smelling of iron and eggs. Where water moves in thin sheets over salt, it leaves a damp, dark stain that never quite dries.
- **Glass:** Shattered, of course, but the interesting glass is the *condensation glass*—a natural, vitreous glaze formed where warm, moist air from deep vents meets cool ambient air. It coats pipes and walls in smooth, milky rivulets, sometimes dripping with a slow, syrupy persistence.

The air is cool (1.1/3), thick with a brine-and-ozone tang, and perpetually dim (0.8/3) under a flat, white sky that seems to be the ceiling of the world, not a sky at all. Light is diffuse, directionless, coming from the overcast above and a weak, sourceless glow from the water itself—a bioluminescence from certain algae and bacteria, strongest in the deepest, most trapped pools. Shadows are soft, indistinct.

**Water:**  
Water is the master here (2.3/3). It is not in rivers, but in *presence*. It seeps from the soil in slow, steady tears. It *condenses* on every cold surface, dripping in a nearly silent rhythm. It fills every depression, not as a deep pool but as a sheen, a film, a reflects-the-sky mirror an inch deep. Canals—once drainage ditches—are now slow-moving brines, brackish and lifeless-looking until you see the blind, pink fish flickering in the gloom. The water table is at your ankles in some places, a foot below in others. It is a landscape sweating itself into existence.

**Vegetation & Fauna:**  
Vegetation is sparse (1.3/3), but determined. It grows only where salt and water allow: 
- **Salt-moss:** A grey, crunchy lichen that thrives on the salt crusts, drinking the humidity.
- **Weeping Rock-grass:** Tufts of stiff, dark grass that emerge from cracks in the salt, their blades perpetually beaded with water.
- **Rust-Vine:** A parasitic, iron-coloured fungus that feeds on decaying metal, leaving behind beautiful, lace-like patterns of corrosion.

Fauna, however, is **abundant** (2.3/3) and **strange** (2.2/3). Life has adapted to the wet, dim, mineral world:
- **Gear-Birds:** Raven-sized, with stiff, jointed wings that clatter softly in flight. Their beaks are metallic-blue and shaped like calipers, used to pry salt-scales from surfaces and crack open the hard-shelled brine-shrimp that swarm in the pools. They nest in the empty bellies of boilers.
- **Blindfish:** Translucent, eel-like creatures in the canals, navigating by sensing minute electrical fields and vibrations. They pulse with the same sickly green bioluminescence as the water.
- **Salt-Goats:** Stocky, shaggy mammals with salt-crusted hooves that can walk on the crusts. They chew the salt-moss and the rust-vine, their droppings little pellets of metallic ore.
- **Condensation Spiders:** Tiny, that build webs not between branches but across the mouths of dripping pipes, catching the constant fall of water droplets. Their silk is slightly conductive, and whole patches of machinery are crisscrossed with these faintly humming, dew-laden nets.

**Built & Tech:**  
This is an **urban** (2.2/3), **industrial** (2.3/3) ruin that has not collapsed but *petrified*. The buildings are not crumbling; they are *re-mineralizing*. Brick walls are being replaced by aatural, brick-like concretions of salt and silt. Steel beams are thickening with rust that behaves like coral. A great, brick stockhouse stands, but its interior is a forest of stalactites and stalagmites of salt and stained concrete, formed by centuries of dripping water. 

The technology is a strange hybrid. Some machines are dead, cold heaps. Others have become *part of the ecology*. A massive, piston-driven pump, its housing long gone, still *breathes*—its piston slowly, rhythmically rising and falling (a 0.7/3 dynamic, a cycle of hours) not from steam, but from a localized geothermal gradient and the slow crystallization of salt in its cylinder. Wires, stripped of insulation, are wrapped in living moss that conducts a weak current. The "power grid" is a web of biological and mineral conductors, humming at a frequency you feel in your teeth more than hear. There is no industry *as we know it*—production has ceased. But the *processes* linger: slow, mineral, biological forms of "manufacture" continue. Salt is mined by the goats. Rust is harvested by the birds. Brine is filtered by the porous stone of the old settling tanks.

**Erosion & Dynamic:**  
The state is **established erosion** (2.3/3). This is not a place in decline, but a place in its *own* state. The forms are settled, weathered into balance. The slow processes (0.7/3 dynamic) are everything: 
- The **salt creep:** Salt crystals grow, millimeter by millimeter, into the cracks of the oldest bricks, pushing them apart with the force of crystals.
- The **tide of attrition:** A twice-yearly flood from the outer marsh brings new silt and new seeds, then recedes, leaving a thin, fertile layer that spurs a brief, intense bloom of salt-moss.
- The **breath of the deep:** Once a century, a geothermal pulse causes the central vent to sigh a plume of warm, mineral-steam that alters the local ecology for a generation.

**Sound & Density:**  
It is **mostly quiet** (0.6/3). The dominant sounds are the slow, liquid **drip… drip… drip** from a thousand points, the whisper of wind through broken windows, the distant, soft *clatter* of a Gear-Bird landing on a salt-encrusted girder, the low, sub-audible hum of the great pump. Occasionally, a sharp *crack* as a salt formation splits under its own weight. The density of *things* is sparse (0.7/3)—buildings are far apart, linked by decaying catwalks and flooded roads. You can stand in the open and see mist and water and ruins in every direction, with vast, quiet spaces between them. The density of *life* is the opposite: it is thick in the water, on the salt crusts, in the ruins. You are more likely to be surrounded by chirring insects or the watchful eyes of Gear-Birds than by other structures.

**Weirdness & Rules:**  
The **weirdness** (2.2/3) is not in overt magic, but in the *accepted strangeness* of the physics. Here, entropy has a competitor: **mineral persistence**. Things don't just decay; they转化 (transform). Metal becomes rock. Water becomes architecture. Light becomes a taste (the air has a faint, cool, greenish flavour). Sound becomes texture—the pump’s hum makes the salt on your skin tingle. The rules feel geological and biological, not mechanical. The built environment is not a container for life; it is a substrate *for* life, just like a cliff face or a tidal pool. The city was not abandoned; it was *outcompeted* by slower, more patient processes.

**Synthesis: The Heartbeat of Stone**  
You stand on a salt-crusted catwalk that was once a railway bridge. Below, a canal of black, still water reflects the white, wet sky. On the far side, the skeleton of a great factory looms: its brick has been replaced by conical mounds of crystalline salt, its smokestack is a hollow tower of fused, glassy slag, inside which hundreds of Gear-Birds roost, their metal feathers clinking softly with every shift. The air is cool and smells of wet stone and brine. From the depths of the complex, a rhythm: the slow *thump… shudder… thump* of the ancient pump, a heartbeat for a creature of iron and salt. A Salt-Goat ambles across the crust, its hooves leaving prints that fill with Condensation Spiders’ webs within the hour. In the water, the blindfish pulse in unison with the pump’s rhythm. 

This is a place where industry didn’t die—it *fossilized*. Where wetness doesn’t erode—it *cements*. Where life doesn’t just survive in the ruins—it *is* the ruin, and the ruin is life. The scale is small, a single, drowned room in the world’s cellar. The light is dim. The change is slow. And in the profound quiet, you feel the patient, mineral pulse of a world settling into a new, strange, and enduring form.
