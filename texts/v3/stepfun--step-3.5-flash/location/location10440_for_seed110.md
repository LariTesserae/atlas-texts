You are not looking at a landscape. You are looking *into* one.

Scale is the first lie you must unlearn. The parameters whisper *hidden* and *dense* and *high*. You are not on a mountain slope; you are inside one. Specifically, you are in a **lithophysal cavity**—a gas-bubble relic from a long-cooled lava flow, miles beneath the surface of a high, arid plateau. The “elevation” refers to your depth’s pressure-equivalent, not your sky-proximity. The entrance, a fracture sealed by a permanent frost of sublimated gases, is a rumor even to those who know it exists.

**Terrain & Materials:**
The floor is not rock, but a **conglomerate of suspended sediment and gel**. Think of a slow-motion whirlpool of fine grey ash, salt crystals, and colloidal silica, all held in a viscous, slightly elastic medium that gives like firm gelatin underfoot. It is perpetually, minimally *wet* (water 2.3) not from liquid, but from a **supersaturated vapor** that condenses into a nearly imperceptible film on every surface. This moisture feeds the“vegetation,” which is not plants. **Sparse** (vegetation 1.0) is the word, but the form is **mineral-biofilms**: thin, pulsating sheets of oxidised iron and manganese bacteria, coloured rust-red, deep violet, and electric blue. They grow in slow, veined patterns across the walls and over the sedimentary “rags” on the floor, breathing the cave’s trace gases. Where they are thickest, they emit a faint, sourceless bioluminescence—a weak, ultraviolet-tinged glow that is the only light (light 0.0) here, making the emptiness *visible* in a way true darkness never is.

**Atmosphere & Density:**
The air is **warm** (temperature 2.4), still, and **dense** (density 2.4) with suspended particles: microscopic silicate motes, bacterial spores, and the evaporative salts of the condensate. To breathe is to taste metal and wet stone. This “density” is also a **density of presences**. The walls are not bare. They are **layered** with:
1.  **Geological laminae** of obsidian, pumice, and welded tuff, frozen in the act of deforming.
2.  **Chemical efflorescences**—cottony blooms of sulphate salts, glittering veils of alum.
3.  **The biofilms**, alive and slow.
4.  **The built** (built 1.0): **scattered** and faint. A shard of knapped chert, its edge still sharp, half-swallowed by a violet film. A spiral of what looks like woven grass, now a mineralised fossil-thread, clinging to a niche. A shallow, smooth-walled **cistern** dug into the sedimentary floor, its bottom lined with desiccated algae. No tools lie with them. The people who made these marks are **rare** (fauna 0.7)—not gone, but absent for a time measured in centuries, not years. Their technology (**primitive**, tech 0.6) was not of metal, but of controlled dehydration, of knowing which biofilms were edible, of shaping the sediment with paddles of bone.

**Erosion & Dynamic:**
**Erosion** (0.1) is **emerging** and **weird** (weirdness 1.3). The cave is in a state of **proto-formation**. The sediments on the floor are not settled; they are in a state of **creeping equilibrium**, shifting millimetres per decade as the condensate weight changes. The biofilms do not merely grow; they **dissolve and redeposit the rock**, etching faint, weeping patterns into the volcanic glass—a geological version of tears. This is the only sound (**mostly quiet**, sound 0.8): not drips, but a **sub-audible susurrus**, the vibration of crystal lattices adjusting, the soft grit of a million bacterial mouths pulling minerals. It is a sound you feel in your molars.

**The Rules & The Ecology:**
-   **Light is Memory.** The bioluminescence reacts not to presence, but to chemical history. A patch of film will flare briefly if you disturb a specific pocket of methane from the floor sediment, as if the cave is *remembering* the gas’s release.
-   **Wetness is Non-Negotiable.** To touch the walls for more than a minute without gloves is to retrieve a hand dusted in a fine, damp powder that will not wipe off. It is the cave’s epithelium, and it is mildly alkaline.
-   **Fauna is Process.** The only “animals” are **chemosynthetic mites**, nearly microscopic, that swim through the gel-like floor sediment and the moisture films. They are the primary consumers. Nothing hunts them. Nothing larger exists. They are the top of the food chain.
-   **Built Things Fade Symbiotically.** A human artifact does not simply decay. It is **incorporated**. The chert shard will, in another thousand years, be a core within a new, complex crystal. The woven grass will be a fossilised conduit for salt. The human trace is not being erased; it is being drafted into the cave’s nascent geology.
-   **Scale is a Trap.** The cavity is roughly the size of a modest cathedral (hidden-scale 0.4). But its features operate at micro and macro simultaneously. The whole space feels like a single, complex crystal, and every film, every salt beard, every grain in the sediment is a cell in that crystal’s growth. To look at one patch of wall is to see a universe; to stand back is to see a vein in that universe’s stone.

**The Atmosphere’s Final Word:**
You feel a profound, liquid **stillness** (dynamic 0.2). Not empty, not dead, but **locked in a frozen equilibrium**. This is not a place that *changes*. It is a place that *becomes*, at a pace so slow it is indistinguishable from stasis. The wetness, the warmth, the biofilms’ pulse—these are not signs of activity, but of a **single, glacial state** persisting. The “weirdness” is the intuition that the cave is not just a void under a mountain, but a **womb**. It is the planet’s quiet rehearsal for a new kind of surface, a rehearsal that has been underway for ten thousand years and will continue for ten thousand more, utterly indifferent to the faint, fading scratches left by the last species that thought itself a builder. The only rule is: *Everything here is already becoming part of the rock.*
