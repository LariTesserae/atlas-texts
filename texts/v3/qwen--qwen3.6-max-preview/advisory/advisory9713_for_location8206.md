# Preparation Protocol for Visiting a Calibrated Hydro-Botanical Steady-State Enclosure

## I. Understanding What You Are Entering
This is not a wilderness. It is not a greenhouse, a conservatory, or a ruined biome. It is a **closed-loop, initialized life-support engine** masquerading as foliage. Every droplet, leaf, and alloy flange exists in mathematical equilibrium. The space does not respond to presence; it compensates for it. You are not an explorer here. You are a temporary thermal, moisture, and particulate anomaly moving through a system tuned to millimeter-scale gradients and century-long cycles. Preparation is not about survival. It is about **friction minimization**: biological, physical, acoustic, and psychological.

Traditional outdoor or expedition gear will fail you. Cotton will saturate and mildew. Treaded boots will trap and redistribute microscopic films. Perfumes, lotions, and shed skin will introduce organic variables into a loop designed to operate without them. Your goal is to become as inert as possible while maintaining full physiological and mental autonomy.

---

## II. Biological & Decontamination Protocol
The water film is the circulatory system of this place. It wicks nutrients, dissolves shed biomass, and routes hormonal triggers. Introducing external microbes, lipids, or particulates can cascade through fluidic logic gates and nutrient reservoirs, potentially triggering compensatory overgrowth, biofilm resistance, or silent sector isolation.

**Pre-Entry Decontamination (24–2 hours prior):**
- Shower with unscented, non-oily, pH-neutral soap. Do not apply moisturizers, sunscreens, hair products, deodorants, or makeup. These shed lipids and polymers into the humidity.
- Trim fingernails and toenails short. File edges smooth. Long nails trap skin cells and oils.
- Wash hair thoroughly; bind it tightly or cover it with a non-shedding cap. Loose hair introduces keratin into a system that does not process it.
- Lint-roll all clothing inside and out. Use a garments bag sealed until entry.
- If possible, wear your entry clothes for 1–2 hours in a clean, low-dust environment before departure to allow initial fiber shedding to occur elsewhere.

**Biological Containment Mindset:**
You are a contamination vector. Assume the space has no immune response to foreign organics. Do not spit, blow your nose, shake out clothing, or wipe sweat onto railings or grating. If you must cough or sneeze, do so into a sealed personal filter cloth and pack it out. The system is designed to recycle its own biomass, not yours.

## III. Physiological Acclimation & Health Maintenance
The environment is warm, saturated, and ozonated. It feels breathable, but prolonged exposure without preparation leads to stealth dehydration, mucosal irritation, and dermal maceration.

**Respiratory & Hydration Prep:**
- The ozonated tang indicates active atmospheric scrubbing and possible low-level corona discharge from fluidic regulators. Ozone at low concentrations is safe but drying. Begin saline nasal rinses 3 days prior. Carry a small personal humidifier mask or saline spray if staying >2 hours.
- Humidity masks sweat evaporation. You will lose water and electrolytes without feeling hot or damp. Pre-hydrate with balanced electrolytes. Monitor urine color. Sip water on a schedule, not by thirst.
- Avoid caffeine or diuretics 12 hours before entry. They compound stealth dehydration.

**Dermal Protection:**
- Constant dampness softens skin, especially in folds, between toes, and under straps. Apply a thin barrier of dimethicone or zinc oxide cream to high-friction zones before dressing.
- If prone to fungal sensitivity, carry a single-dose topical antifungal. The space has no decay, but your own microbiome will react to sustained humidity.

**Circadian Management:**
- The diffused, shadowless light does not cycle. Melatonin suppression is likely. If staying >3 hours, wear an eye mask during rest periods. Maintain a rigid personal time schedule. Do not trust your internal clock.

## IV. Clothing, Footwear & Personal Barrier Systems
Everything you wear must shed water, resist microbial colonization, and leave no trace.

**Clothing:**
- Base layer: Seamless, hydrophobic synthetic (polyester or polypropylene blend). No cotton, no wool, no bamboo. Flat-lock seams only.
- Mid/outer: Light, quick-dry shirt and trousers with minimal pockets. Zippers over buttons (buttons trap moisture and shed threads). Avoid velcro; it sheds microfibers and catches on root-mats.
- Color: Muted tones. Not for camouflage, but to reduce visual contrast against the greenery. High-contrast clothing can amplify the psychological weight of being an "anomaly."
- Fit: Close but not restrictive. Loose fabric creates air currents that disrupt moisture tension and thermal stratification.

**Footwear:**
- The gridded platform is wet alloy and ceramic. Traction is paramount, but deep treads are liabilities. Use marine deck shoes, approach shoes, or clean-room-rated wet-environment footwear with non-marking, micro-siped rubber soles.
- Ensure soles are scrubbed free of grit, soil, or organic residue before entry. A single grain of sand can scratch the water-tension film or lodge in capillary channels.
- Wear thin, seamless synthetic socks. Change them if visiting >4 hours to prevent maceration.

**Accessories:**
- Gloves: Optional. If used, thin nitrile or silicone-dipped knit with non-shedding palms. Do not touch foliage or water regardless.
- Head/eyes: Moisture-wicking headband. Polarized lenses are unnecessary; the canopy diffuses glare. Clear or light-tint lenses protect against occasional droplet fall from manifolds.
- Jewelry: Remove all. Rings trap moisture. Chains swing and create micro-currents. Watches should be sealed and non-reflective.

## V. Gear, Sustenance & Technical Logistics
Assume zero resupply. The water is not potable. The plants are not edible. The machinery is not user-serviceable.

**Sustenance:**
- Pack all food and water in sealed, non-shedding containers. Calorie-dense, low-moisture foods are ideal (nut bars, dried fruit, electrolyte chews). Avoid crumbly or oily foods.
- Water: 1.5–2 liters per 4 hours. Use a closed hydration bladder with bite valve, or sealed bottles. Do not pour, spill, or vent liquids onto the platform.
- Waste: Double-bag all trash, including food wrappers, used wipes, and personal hygiene items. Pack out everything. The loop does not process synthetic waste.

**Electronics & Instruments:**
- IP68 minimum. Conformal coating recommended. Humidity will find unsealed ports.
- Store in dry bags with fresh silica gel. Condensation forms rapidly when moving between microclimates or during door cycling.
- Cameras: Lens fogging is guaranteed. Use anti-fog inserts or allow gradual acclimation before opening housing. Flash is unnecessary and disruptive.
- Comms: Assume total signal attenuation. Alloy walls, water mass, and fluidic shielding block RF. Pre-arrange check-in windows. If hardline ports exist, do not interface without authorization.

**Optional but Recommended:**
- Low-lumen amber/red headlamp (for preserving dark adaptation if resting)
- Hygrometer/thermometer (for personal awareness, not system interaction)
- Small personal journal (paper swells; use synthetic notebook or digital)
- Earplugs (not for noise, but to create a psychological boundary against the saturated silence)

## VI. Movement, Etiquette & Systems Awareness
You are moving through a calibrated equation. Behavior dictates impact.

**Platform Discipline:**
- Stay on the gridded walkway. Do not step onto support flanges, pipe insulation, or planter ledges. The water tension floor is stable but not load-rated for off-grid weight.
- Walk slowly. Heel-to-toe rolling steps minimize vibration and air displacement. Sudden movements create thermal eddies that disrupt moisture beading and leaf orientation.
- Do not run, jump, or drop gear. Impact vibrations travel through alloy struts and can trigger pressure-equalization valves.

**Interaction禁忌 (Prohibitions):**
- Do not touch the water film. It is not pooled; it is tension-held and nutrient-charged. Breaking the surface tension locally can alter capillary routing for hours.
- Do not prune, pick, or lean on foliage. Leaves are hormonally regulated. Physical stress triggers compensatory growth or nutrient reallocation.
- Do not open panels, adjust dials, or peer behind acrylic housings. Fluidic logic gates self-regulate. Manual interference can cascade into silent overpressure or thermal drift.
- Do not breathe directly onto control surfaces or leaf clusters. CO2 and moisture plumes alter local gradients.

**Acoustic & Thermal Awareness:**
- Speak softly or not at all. Sound is absorbed, but low frequencies travel through water and alloy. Your voice will feel unnaturally close, then vanish. This can trigger vocal strain or disorientation.
- Your body heat will create a micro-thermal column. The system will compensate via floor heat-exchange coils. This is normal. Do not attempt to "cool down" by fanning or removing layers abruptly.

## VII. Psychological Navigation & Temporal Grounding
The greatest challenge is not physical. It is perceptual.

**The Steady-State Trance:**
The absence of decay, animals, wind, and shadow creates a phenomenon multiverse travelers call "green static." The brain expects entropy, response, or cycle. Here, there is only calibration. Visitors often report:
- Time dilation (minutes feel like hours, or vice versa)
- Hypnagogic drifting despite full wakefulness
- A sense of being watched, followed by the realization that nothing is watching; the space simply registers
- Mild dissociation or "biome fatigue" from visual density without focal points

**Grounding Techniques:**
- Anchor to rhythm: Count steps, monitor breathing, tap a finger against your thigh in a steady pattern.
- Use temporal markers: Check a watch every 30 minutes. Note it aloud or in writing. Do not trust internal time.
- Accept the uncanny: This place is lush without hunger, industrial without noise, young without vulnerability. It is not broken. It is initialized. Let go of the expectation that it should "feel alive" in a terrestrial sense.
- If anxiety or claustrophobia rises, focus on the glass canopy. Watch the cloud-shadows. They are your only tether to external time and weather.

**The Ethics of Presence:**
You are a guest in a system that does not know you exist. It will not welcome you, warn you, or reject you. It will absorb your heat, route around your moisture, and return to equilibrium after you leave. Your role is witness, not participant. Move with reverence for precision. Leave nothing but displaced air that will settle within minutes.

## VIII. Contingencies, Exit & The High-Altitude Threshold
**Slip/Fall Protocol:**
- If you slip, do not grab foliage or conduits. Tuck, roll onto the grating, and brace on open platform. The alloy can handle impact; the plants and water film cannot.
- Check for gear displacement. Repack calmly. Do not rush recovery.

**Medical Emergencies:**
- Dehydration, dizziness, or respiratory irritation are most likely. Sit on the platform, elevate legs, sip water, use saline spray. The warmth and humidity will mask shock symptoms. Monitor pulse and breathing carefully.
- If severe, trigger exit protocol immediately. Do not wait for "recovery." The environment will not assist you.

**Door & Pressure Awareness:**
- The iris door is pressure-sealed. Outside air is thin and biting. If the door cycles unexpectedly, brace for a pressure differential. Equalize ears early.
- Know the exit mechanism before entry. Is it timed? Sensor-triggered? Manual override? Assume it will not wait. Position yourself within clear line of sight 10 minutes before departure window.
- Carry a compact emergency oxygen mask or altitude-rated respirator if the external threshold exceeds 3,500m. The transition from saturated warmth to thin, cold air can trigger bronchospasm or vertigo.

**System Response to Anomaly:**
- If you accidentally disrupt a seal, spill liquid, or trigger a valve, do not panic. The system will compensate silently: rerouting flow, adjusting pressure, or isolating a micro-sector. You may hear a soft click, see a luminescent conduit dim, or feel a thermal shift. This is normal. Step back. Breathe. Prepare to exit. Do not attempt to "fix" anything.

## IX. Departure & Post-Visit Recovery
- Exit slowly. Allow pressure equalization. Do not rush into thin air.
- Seal all gear immediately upon crossing the threshold. Humidity will condense rapidly in cooler, drier air, trapping moisture against electronics and fabric.
- Shower within 2 hours. Use mild soap to remove nutrient film and ozone residues from skin and hair.
- Rehydrate aggressively. Electrolytes + water. Monitor for delayed respiratory irritation or dermal sensitivity.
- Journal or record impressions within 24 hours. The steady-state trance causes rapid memory smoothing. Details will fade into a single impression of "green quiet" unless anchored quickly.

## Final Note: The Traveler's Posture
You do not conquer this place. You do not camp in it, harvest from it, or map its secrets. It has no secrets. It is entirely legible, entirely made, entirely waiting. Your preparation is an exercise in humility: stripping away the habits of wilderness, shedding the expectation of response, and learning to move through perfection without leaving a scar.

Pack light. Breathe slow. Step carefully. Watch the water hold itself together. Listen to the silence that is not empty, but full of held breath. And when the iris door sighs open behind you, step through without looking back. The system will close. The tension will restore. The green will continue its millimeter march. You were never part of the equation. And that is exactly how it should be.
