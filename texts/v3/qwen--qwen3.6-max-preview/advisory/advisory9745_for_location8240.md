# PREPARATION PROTOCOL: THE UNFINISHED BASIN
*For the ordinary human multiverse traveler*

This is not a location you arrive at. It is a process you step into. The dip that swallows the horizon is less a geographical feature than an invitation to participate in a reality still drafting its own physics. You will not conquer it, map it, or extract from it. You will attend it. Preparation, therefore, begins not with inventory, but with posture.

---

## I. MENTAL & PERCEPTUAL CONDITIONING

**Unlearn fixed expectation.** The basin operates on negotiation, not law. Distances slip. Time granulates. Observation alters structure. If you enter expecting consistency, you will fracture against the space. Train your mind to accept fluidity as baseline. Practice sitting in quiet rooms until silence stops feeling like absence and starts feeling like presence. Learn to notice without fixing your gaze.

**Soft attention is survival.** Direct, sustained focus thickens shadows, seals cracks, and accelerates local accretion. Peripheral awareness is safer. Practice walking while keeping your eyes slightly unfocused, tracking motion and light changes without locking onto them. Meditation techniques that emphasize breath-awareness over visual anchoring will serve you well. When reality begins to unspool, your eyes will want to grab onto something solid. Train them to rest instead.

**Prepare for sensory inversion.** Sound will drown in the stone. Your own footsteps, your breath, the rustle of your pack will be absorbed within inches. This creates a profound psychological isolation, even in a space full of life. Conversely, light will feel heavy, almost tactile, pooling on surfaces and bending around corners. The contrast between auditory void and visual saturation can trigger vertigo or mild dissociation. Ground yourself in tactile and proprioceptive feedback: the press of your soles, the rhythm of your ribs, the temperature gradient on your skin.

**Accept incorporation.** The basin tests weight, rhythm, and intention. If you stand still long enough, it will adjust around you. Clay will soften under your warmth. Stone will hum in your molars. Temporary grooves may form where you rest. This is not consumption. It is grammar. You are being briefly conjugated into the space. Panic will cause the negotiation to stall or snap. Breathe. Let it read you. It will let you go when it's finished.

## II. WHAT TO WEAR & CARRY (AND WHAT TO LEAVE BEHIND)

**Footwear:** Soft-soled, flexible, and silent. Felt, padded leather, or dense natural rubber. No hard heels, no rigid shanks, no cleats. The ground is embryonic; it yields before it firms. Hard impacts don't echo here, but they do bruise the accretion process, leaving stress fractures that may seal unpredictably or weep mineral residue for hours. Roll your steps. Distribute weight. Let the stone decide how to hold you.

**Clothing:** Layered, breathable, natural fibers. Linen, hemp, merino, or tightly woven cotton. Avoid synthetics. The damp here is active; it cycles, beads, evaporates, and carries mineral memory. Polyester and nylon will repel it, creating micro-boundaries that the space interprets as resistance. You want to negotiate with the humidity, not wall it off. Cut garments should be loose enough to allow air circulation but fitted enough not to snag on extruding ledges or secretion-lined hollows. No metal zippers, buckles, or grommets. Use bone, wood, or woven ties. Metal tastes wrong here. It hums at a frequency that clashes with the stone's pulse and may cause localized reality snags.

**Eye protection:** The light doesn't fall; it pools. It clings to moisture and bends around rock, creating a brightness that feels submerged rather than overhead. Tinted lenses (amber or light grey) or a wide, soft-brimmed hat will prevent retinal fatigue. Avoid mirrored or polarized glass. Reflections here show alternate arrangements of the space; polarized filters may trap those reflections, causing afterimage stacking or mild temporal nausea.

**Pack & storage:** Soft-sided, frameless, quiet. Canvas or waxed linen. No clinking carabiners, no hard plastic shells. Pack items in cloth wraps. The basin absorbs sound, but it also absorbs vibration. Rigid containers transmit micro-shocks into the embryonic stone, which may respond by extruding ridges or sealing pores beneath you.

**Documentation tools:** Digital devices will fail unpredictably. Batteries drain in granular time. Lenses warp under negotiable light. Sensors misread humidity and temperature as they cycle. Bring a damp-resistant notebook, soft graphite or charcoal, and a cloth-bound journal. Ink may crawl, reverse, or bead uphill. Pencil respects the space's pace. If you must record visually, use a pinhole camera or a simple camera obscura tube. The basin prefers analog patience.

**Optional but wise:** 
- A soft blindfold. For moments when observation needs to be dialed back entirely. Closing your eyes here isn't surrender; it's a reset.
- A small polished stone or piece of dark glass. Mirrors are too reflective, too demanding. A dull polish shows enough to track light shifts without trapping alternate arrangements.
- A tuning fork (low frequency, ~64Hz). Not to strike loudly, but to rest gently against stone and feel the sub-audible hum in your hands rather than your jaw. Helps calibrate your nervous system to the space's rhythm.

## III. MOVING THROUGH NEGOTIABLE SPACE & TIME

**Distance is a suggestion.** Thirty paces may cross the basin. Ten may leave you circling the same depression. Do not count steps. Do not trust your legs. Move by rhythm and reference. Pick soft landmarks: a glass-leafed shrub, a secretion-lined hollow, a mound of compacted silt. Note their relationship to the light pools. When space slips, it usually does so along damp seams or raw strata boundaries. Step across dry, open stone when possible.

**Time granulates.** It catches, releases, stutters, then flows. Your watch will lie. Your hunger will misalign. Your sleep pressure will arrive in waves that don't match circadian logic. Sync to environmental cycles instead:
- Mist formation → early phase
- Light intensification → peak activity
- Basin filling → transition
- Evaporation & shadow thickening → rest phase
Move in pause-step-pause rhythm. Let the space register your weight before committing to the next step. If time catches mid-stride, don't fight it. Freeze. Breathe. Wait for the release. The basin isn't trapping you; it's reading you.

**The hum is normal.** You will feel it in your molars, your sternum, the hollows behind your knees. It's not sound. It's tension. Matter deciding its shape. If it becomes overwhelming, press your palms flat against dry stone, exhale slowly, and match your breath to the pulse. Do not hum back. Do not chant. Vocal resonance interferes with the accretion frequency and may cause localized crystallization or rapid sealing.

## IV. ECOLOGICAL ETIQUETTE & THE PHYSICS OF ATTENTION

**This ecosystem negotiates. It does not compete.** Space is abundant. Resources appear, are used, return. Predation is quiet, almost reverential. Do not intervene. Do not collect. Do not break. Everything here is mid-sentence. Taking a carapace, a glass-leaf, or a secretion thread is like tearing a page from a book still being written. The space will notice. It may not punish you, but it will adjust around your absence differently. You will leave a snag.

**Where to step:** Dry, open stone. Compacted silt mounds. Worn arch tunnels. Avoid:
- Damp seams (active accretion zones)
- Secretion-lined hollows (living architecture, still pulsing)
- Glass-leaf root cracks (delicate mineral-organic negotiation)
- Temporary water basins (they drain through unseen pores; stepping on them collapses the cycle)

**Creature interaction:** They move in spheres. Each occupies its own rhythm. If an opalescent crawler approaches, still yourself. Do not reach. Do not flinch. They trace moisture and warmth. Your body heat will draw them; your stillness will tell them you're part of the draft, not a disruption. They may leave temporary grooves near your boots. These will harden by evening. This is normal. If a winged form glides close, lower your gaze. Frosted membranes catch pooled light; direct eye contact may cause afterimage overlap that lingers in your vision for minutes.

**The observation effect:** Look away from a crack and it seals. Stare at a shadow and it thickens. This isn't illusion. It's participatory physics. Your attention applies pressure. Use it sparingly. When you need to rest your eyes, close them or look at your hands. When you need to navigate, use soft focus and peripheral tracking. If you accidentally thicken a shadow or accelerate a seal, step back. Breathe. Let the space redistribute the tension. It will.

## V. PHYSIOLOGICAL REALITIES & BODY MAINTENANCE

**Temperature & moisture:** The air hovers in the breath-visible range. Cool enough to raise skin, not to bite. Dampness cycles constantly. You will feel chilled if you stop moving, but overheating is rare. Layer wisely. Peel back when active, cover when still. The damp carries crushed lichen, wet rock, and unplaced antiquity. It's clean, but mineral-heavy. Do not drink from temporary basins without filtration. The water is pure but negotiable; it may carry trace crystalline residue or memory-tastes (copper, unfallen rain, forgotten words). These aren't toxic, but they can cause mild temporal disassociation or dream-heavy sleep.

**Metabolism & digestion:** Time granulation affects gut rhythm. Heavy meals will sit poorly. Eat light, slow-digesting foods: nuts, dried fruit, hardtack, jerky, fermented grains. Fast intermittently. The basin's cycles align better with empty digestion than full. Hydrate before entry. Sip sparingly inside. Your body will adapt to the damp by pulling moisture through skin and breath. This is normal. You may urinate less. Do not panic.

**Skin & contact:** The stone yields like cooling wax. Clay holds shape until touched. Secretions feel warm, almost pulsing. Prolonged skin contact may leave temporary mineral dust, faint crystalline patterns, or clay adhesion. Do not scrub aggressively upon exit. Wash gently with lukewarm water and soft cloth. The residue will slough naturally within hours. Scrubbing drives micro-particles deeper and may cause localized itching or mild resonance sensitivity for days.

**Neurological fatigue:** Bright light + sound absorption + reality fluidity + sub-audible hum = sensory load. Expect mild headaches, eye strain, or a feeling of "mental static" after 2-3 hours. This isn't damage. It's calibration. Rest against a worn arch. Close your eyes. Breathe in 4-count, hold 2, exhale 6. Let the space's rhythm overwrite your nervous system's panic. You'll feel clearer afterward.

## VI. ANCHORING, CONTINGENCY & THE EXIT

**The entry dip will not wait.** It folds inward without warning. It may shift, seal, or relocate while you're inside. Do not mark it with tape, chalk, or cord. The stone absorbs or rewrites foreign markers. Instead, memorize the feeling: the cool drop in temperature, the metallic tang, the way light begins to pool rather than fall, the sudden silence that feels like breath held. These are your coordinates.

**Carry an anchor.** Not a tool. A tether. A smooth river stone from your home world. A braided cord worn soft by years. A coin rubbed dull by pockets. Keep it in a cloth pouch against your chest. Do not display it. Do not use it unless reality feels too unspooled, too thick, too negotiable. When you need it, press it to your sternum, close your eyes, and remember a fixed place: a doorway, a tree, a street corner, a voice. The anchor doesn't control the basin. It reminds your nervous system that fixed reality still exists elsewhere. That's enough.

**If you become disoriented:** Sit. Do not pace. Pacing fractures the negotiation. Close your eyes. Place palms on dry stone. Breathe. Let the hum move through you. The basin will adjust. It always does. It will test your weight, read your rhythm, and eventually soften a path toward the light gradient that matches your entry memory. Trust the slope of pooled brightness. It bends toward the surface.

**Exiting:** Reverse slowly. Do not rush. Sudden weight shifts or panic strides may cause the ground to "reconsider" your path, extruding ridges or sealing pores beneath you. Step lightly. Pause at the threshold. Feel the air change from metallic-damp to ordinary. The sound will return first: wind, distant birds, your own breath echoing normally. Then the light will stop pooling and start falling. Then the stone will stop yielding. Step out. Do not look back immediately. Let the dip seal or shift on its own terms. It will remember you. You don't need to verify it.

## VII. THE TRAVELER'S POSTURE

You are not here to document, conquer, or extract. You are here to witness a draft. The basin is geology, biology, and perception learning to agree. It is fiercely alive with process. It does not need you. But it will make room for you, if you ask politely with your weight, your breath, your stillness.

Pack light. Move softer. Watch less, feel more. Let your edges blur slightly. Let the clay remember your warmth. Let the stone hum in your teeth. Let time catch and release without complaint. You will leave slightly different. Your shadows may point strangely for a day. Your reflection may hesitate. Your dreams may taste of copper and unfallen rain. This is not damage. It is resonance.

The ground is still learning how to be ground. The air is still deciding what it carries. And you, ordinary human, multiverse walker, soft-soled and quiet-breathed, are briefly part of the sentence it's writing.

Step carefully. Listen with your skin. And when it's time to go, leave without tearing the page.
