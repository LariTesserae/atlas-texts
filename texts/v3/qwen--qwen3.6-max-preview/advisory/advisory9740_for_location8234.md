# FIELD PREPARATION MANUAL: THE SUNG HOLLOW
**Classification:** Closed-Equilibrium Subterranean Structure / Acoustic-Geometric Stasis Zone
**Access Probability:** Stochastic (misstep-dependent). Cannot be reliably mapped or summoned.
**Recommended Visitor Profile:** Ordinary human multiverse traveler with baseline transit conditioning, low sensory reactivity, and disciplined stillness practice.

---

## I. UNDERSTANDING WHAT YOU ARE ENTERING

Do not approach this place as a ruin, a tomb, a temple, or an archive. It is none of these. It is a **completed system**. The makers did not flee, die out, or abandon their work. They finished it. Every joint, every curve, every light baffle and acoustic plane was resolved until no further action was physically or conceptually necessary. The space exists in a state of permanent present tense.

As a multiverse traveler, you are accustomed to places that decay, evolve, wait, or hunger. This place does none of those things. It *holds*. It tolerates your presence only because your mass, duration, and kinetic output are too negligible to disrupt its equilibrium. You are not a guest. You are a temporary perturbation in a basin that has already gone still.

Frame your mind accordingly: you are going to witness completion, not to explore, document, or interact. Any impulse to "leave a mark," "test the acoustics," or "take a fragment" is not just disrespectful; it is a fundamental misreading of the space's nature. Violence here is not loud. It is addition. It is subtraction. It is change.

## II. PHYSICAL & ENVIRONMENTAL PREPARATION

### Atmosphere & Hydration
The air is cool (approx. 12–15°C), profoundly dry, and thermally locked. The packed masonry acts as an infinite heat sink and moisture sponge. Your breath will deposit salt blooms on the nearest surface within minutes. Your skin, sinuses, and corneas will desiccate rapidly.

- **Internal Hydration Protocol:** Begin hyper-hydration 12 hours before descent. Use electrolyte-balanced fluids. Do not rely on drinking inside the hollow; open liquids introduce vapor, spill risk, and thermal disruption.
- **Moisture Capture:** Wear a closed-circuit rebreather or a high-efficiency moisture-recycling half-mask. This is non-negotiable. Even light exhalation leaves mineral traces that alter the surface patina. The space has forgotten how to be damp; do not remind it.
- **Ocular Protection:** The dry air will cause rapid tear film evaporation. Use preservative-free lubricating drops before entry. Consider sealed, ventilated goggles if you have sensitive corneas.

### Light & Vision
Light enters through a narrow throat, strikes angled baffles, scatters through prismatic tilework, and floods the chamber in hard, white-gold brilliance. There are no soft shadows. Depth perception will flatten. Pupils will constrict painfully until adaptation occurs.

- **Adaptive Lenses:** Bring photochromic or variable-tint lenses that cut intensity without distorting color temperature. Avoid polarized filters; they will interfere with the prismatic scatter and create false dark zones.
- **Reflective Discipline:** Remove or matte-tape all reflective gear (buckles, watch faces, device screens, synthetic fabrics). Stray reflections create unintended light paths that visually "clutter" the resolved geometry.
- **Pre-Exposure Training:** Spend time in shadowless environments (light boxes, overcast salt flats, clinical inspection rooms) to acclimate your vestibular system to flat illumination. Practice moving slowly when depth cues are absent.

### Temperature & Clothing
The thermal mass of the walls drinks radiation and returns only equilibrium. You will not sweat. You will not shiver. You will slowly leach heat until your core matches the ambient stillness.

- **Layering:** Wear breathable, non-shedding, low-static fabrics. Merino, tightly woven linen, or advanced transit weaves. No fleece, no loose knits, no microfiber that sloughs.
- **Footwear:** Soft-soled, flexible, non-marking. Felt-lined transit boots or thin rubber soles with textured grips. Hard soles will click, disrupt the acoustic field, and feel violently alien against the dished steps.
- **Skin Barrier:** Apply a thin, non-greasy occlusive balm to lips, knuckles, and nostrils before descent. The dry air will crack exposed skin within 20 minutes.

## III. ACOUSTIC & SENSORY CONDITIONING

The sound is not atmospheric. It is structural. A low drone braided with a high, glassy harmonic, locked into a standing wave by the geometry, compression, and fused joints. It does not echo. It integrates. Your voice will fold into it and vanish. A handclap will be swallowed instantly. The tone lives in the material, in the tension, in the absolute stillness. You will feel it in your teeth, your sternum, your marrow.

### Physiological Impact
- **Bone Conduction:** The frequency couples directly to skeletal structure. Expect a sensation of internal vibration, mild tinnitus, or a feeling that the sound originates inside your skull. This is normal.
- **Vestibular Drift:** Prolonged exposure to non-pulsing, non-directional resonance can cause mild dissociation, temporal flattening, or a trance-like calm. Some travelers report weeping without sadness. Others feel profound relief. Both are valid.
- **Acoustic Fatigue:** After 30–40 minutes, the nervous system may begin to interpret the constant tone as silence, followed by a sudden return of awareness that can feel jarring. This is sensory adaptation, not damage.

### Preparation & Management
- **Do Not Fight It:** Earplugs or active noise cancellation will create a pressure differential between your inner ear and the structural vibration, causing nausea, headache, or disorientation. If you are highly sensitive, use mild bone-conduction dampeners (gel pads behind the ears, soft jaw guards), but leave the canal open. The sound is the space's equilibrium; blocking it entirely is like trying to breathe in a vacuum.
- **Voice Discipline:** Accept that speech is functionally useless here. Your words will be absorbed, not reflected. Practice silent communication beforehand: hand signals, tactile taps, pre-agreed gestures. If you must speak, do so softly, knowing it will cost you energy and leave moisture.
- **Pre-Visit Acoustic Grounding:** Spend time in environments with sustained, non-rhythmic tones (singing bowls, drone rooms, wind harps, low-frequency meditation chambers). Train your nervous system to rest inside constant sound rather than scan for change.

## IV. PSYCHOLOGICAL & TEMPORAL FRAMING

### The Weight of Finality
This place has no future. It has no past. It is a closed loop of material, light, and resonance that reached its terminal configuration and stopped. Visitors often experience:
- **Existential Flattening:** The realization that some things do not need to become, only to be.
- **Grief or Awe:** Mourning for a world that could finish something, or reverence for a space that asks nothing of you.
- **Geometric Paranoia:** The sensation of being "watched" by angles, compression, and symmetry. This is not supernatural. It is the psychological effect of standing inside a system so resolved that your own asymmetry, breath, and heartbeat feel like intrusions.

Prepare by accepting your role: you are a ripple. You will not change the water. You will not be remembered by the stone. This is not a place that validates presence. It tolerates it.

### Temporal Pooling
Time does not accumulate here; it pools. Watches may run slow, fast, or feel meaningless. Internal rhythms will desynchronize. You may feel you've been inside for minutes when it's been an hour, or vice versa.

- **Hard Duration Limit:** First visit: 20–30 minutes maximum. Do not rely on perceived time. Use a silent, vibrating timer set before descent. When it pulses, leave. The space will not warn you. Your body will fail first (dehydration, acoustic fatigue, temporal dissociation).
- **Grounding Anchors:** Memorize three tactile references before entry: the texture of the entry fracture, the weight of your pack straps, the rhythm of your own pulse. Return to these when time feels suspended.
- **Accept Stillness:** Do not pace. Do not fidget. Do not scan for "something to happen." Nothing will. The place is not waiting. It is holding. Your job is to match its stillness as closely as a living thing can.

- Reflective surfaces, bells, zippers that click, hard-soled boots
- The expectation of interaction, response, or change

## VI. ENTRY, CONDUCT, & EXIT PROTOCOLS

### Descent
- The fracture is barely shoulder-width. Turn sideways if needed. Descend slowly.
- The three steps are dished smooth by repetition. Trust them. Place your full foot, not just the ball. Do not rush.
- The sky will "pinch shut" behind you. Expect a moment of claustrophobia, followed by the sudden release into the hollow. Breathe through the mask. Let your eyes adjust to the flat light.

### Inside
- Move deliberately. Place feet softly. Avoid brushing walls. The masonry is fused, but your oils, salts, and micro-abrasions matter.
- Keep hands to yourself unless intentionally making contact. If you press your palm to the wall, the tone will travel up your arm. This is normal. Do not lean. Do not rest your weight against the structure.
- Sit only on the worn step or a clear, flat ledge. Do not rearrange dust. Do not test the ceramic stalks. They are structural echoes, not flora. Some are hollow and will ring faintly if air shifts; others are solid and heavy. Leave them.
- If you feel dissociation, temporal drift, or acoustic overwhelm, close your eyes, feel your grounding object, count your breaths (silently, through the mask), and prepare to exit. Do not push through.

### Exit
- When your timer pulses, or when your body signals fatigue, stand slowly. Do not rush the ascent.
- Climb out the same way. The transition back to flowing time, wind, temperature fluctuation, and decaying matter will feel violent. Pace yourself.
- Do not look back until you are fully clear of the fracture. The space does not need your farewell. It will hold without you.

## VII. MULTIVERSAL TRANSIT CONSIDERATIONS

### Reality Anchoring
- This location exists in a state of extreme local stability. It resists reality flux, multiversal bleed, and temporal shear. It is a dead zone for jump harmonics.
- **Set your anchor outside the fracture.** Do not attempt to tether from within. The standing wave and geometric compression can lock, distort, or recursive-fold anchor signals, causing spatial recursion or tether failure.
- Mark the surface entrance with a non-invasive, temporary transit beacon (chalk-free, low-emission). The fracture is stochastic; you may not find it again, but your anchor must remain in flowing space.

### Jump Protocols
- **Never initiate a multiversal shift from inside the hollow.** The resonance and packed geometry will couple with your jump field, potentially folding the vector into the standing wave. Reported outcomes include: localized spatial recursion, anchor snap, temporary phase-lock, or severe vestibular trauma.
- Exit fully. Ground yourself in external time/weather/sound for at least 10 minutes. Verify anchor stability. Then jump.

### Cross-Contamination & Ethics
- This is a closed system. Introducing foreign biology, moisture, kinetic energy, or particulate matter risks micro-fractures in the equilibrium. Even if invisible to you, the space "notices" in the language of tension and salt.
- Do not take fragments. Do not leave offerings. Do not carve, mark, or test. The place is complete. Your presence is already a ripple; do not make it a wave.
- Some travelers feel compelled to "share" the location via coordinates or resonance mapping. Do not. The hollow is found by misstep, or not at all. Forcing access violates its stochastic nature and risks destabilizing the threshold.

## VIII. POST-VISIT INTEGRATION & AFTERCARE

### Physical Recalibration
- **Rehydration:** Slow, electrolyte-balanced. The dry air pulls deep tissue moisture. Expect cracked lips, dry sinuses, mild headache, and temporary reduced tear production. Drink gradually over 2–3 hours.
- **Acoustic Washout:** The drone lingers in bone memory. You may perceive normal silence as "empty," "thin," or "wrong" for hours or days. Reintroduce sound gradually: wind, water, distant speech, soft music. Avoid sudden loud environments.
- **Thermal Rebound:** Your core will have cooled to ambient. You may feel unusually warm or flushed upon returning to normal air. Layer down slowly. Avoid hot showers immediately; let your vasculature adjust.

### Psychological Processing
- The feeling of "finishedness" can trigger existential questioning, grief, or profound peace. None are pathological. This place does not haunt; it *settles* in you.
- Journal immediately. Memory of pooled time degrades quickly. Write what you felt, not just what you saw. Note the weight of the light, the path of the tone through your ribs, the texture of the step.
- If you experience prolonged temporal dissociation, acoustic phantom sensations, or depressive flattening, consult a multiversal transit counselor or veteran traveler. This is a known aftereffect of closed-equilibrium zones. It passes with grounding, routine, and exposure to living, changing systems.

### Gear Decontamination
- Brush all clothing and pack surfaces thoroughly. Check seams, zippers, and boot treads for precipitated salt or fine silt dust.
- Store gear separately for 24 hours. The hollow's equilibrium can subtly affect other materials if carried unchecked into active environments.
- Wipe down adaptive lenses and mask seals with a dry, lint-free cloth. Do not use solvents; salt crystals can etch if dissolved improperly.

## CLOSING NOTE

You will not conquer this place. You will not understand it fully. You will not change it. That is the point.

The Sung Hollow is a testament to the possibility of completion in a multiverse defined by flux, decay, and endless becoming. It asks nothing of you but stillness, respect, and the willingness to be temporary. Prepare your body for dryness and light. Prepare your ears for integration. Prepare your mind for finality. Set your anchor outside. Descend slowly. Breathe through the mask. Sit. Listen. Feel the tone travel up your arm. Let the light bleach your shadow to nothing.

When your timer pulses, rise. Climb out. Return to the world of wind, rot, growth, and noise. Carry the stillness with you, not as a burden, but as a reference point: a reminder that some things, against all multiversal odds, can simply be finished.

And leave the bright, cool, singing dark exactly as you found it.
