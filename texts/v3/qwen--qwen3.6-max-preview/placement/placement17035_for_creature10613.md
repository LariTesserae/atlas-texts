I arrive as a thermal anomaly. A scatter of carbon and pulse. A rushed rhythm in a world that measures itself in saturation.

My breath plumes in the cool, alkaline air. My shadow cuts across the bright, unfiltered light, a temporary eclipse on pale limestone that has not known interruption in centuries. I step carefully, but there is no careful here. Every surface is tasked. Every angle is committed. My boot finds a polished river stone set into a walking course; my shoulder brushes a low vault of dry-fitted basalt; my weight settles into a niche that was never carved for rest, only for overflow, for passage, for the brief pause before redistribution.

I am at a terrace junction. Three channels braid and split around a stacked monolith. A shallow basin mirrors the high, thin sky at my knees. A copper dowel, just beginning to green, pins two limestone blocks at my hip. The corridor curves imperceptibly to my left, focusing resonance toward a ventilation flue that sighs like a lung. There is no plaza. No courtyard. No empty ground. Only the continuous fabric of construction, and the negative space of water moving through it. I occupy a fraction of that negative space. The place does not reject me. It receives me the way a weir receives a rising pool: without surprise, without resistance, only the quiet mechanics of accommodation.

I stop moving. I let the cool air draw the heat from me. I let the bright light bleach the urgency from my eyes.

Condensation begins before I notice it. A film gathers along my collarbone. Beads swell at my temples. Trace the line of my jaw. It is not my sweat. It is the atmosphere recognizing a cooler surface, vapor surrendering to temperature, the habit of wetness waking on foreign stone. I feel it gather. I feel it swell. I feel the exact moment gravity claims it, and it falls.

A soft tick against the basin rim. Then silence. Then another.

I do not wipe it away. I let it happen again. And again. My warmth becomes a condensation point. My stillness becomes a collection surface. The water does not ask what I am. It only asks what angle I present, what temperature I hold, what path I offer. I tilt my head. A bead threads into the hollow of my throat, follows the line of my sternum, finds the woven fabric at my chest, and sinks. It does not rush. It tests. It learns me the way it learned the chisel marks, the rope grooves, the deliberate asymmetries left by hands that became the angles they cut.

I am becoming a surface. Then a channel. Then a lip.

My breath slows to match the sigh of the flues. My pulse loses its insistence, caught in the undertow of a rhythm measured not in seconds, but in the space between releases. I feel the stone beneath me settle—a micro-shift, a low click, the copper pin at my hip chiming faintly as the temperature turns. The sound travels through my bones. I realize I am not hearing it. I am conducting it. My ribs are lintels. My spine is a stacked monolith. My joints are dry-fitted, held by weight and friction, learning the exact posture that makes stillness hold stillness.

I am no longer a visitor. I am a node in the slow nervous system.

The water threads through me now. Not inside, but along. It finds the capillary logic of my pores, the slight variations in my posture, the worn hollows where my knees press into the walking surface. It does not flood. It circulates. It pools in the shallow basin of my cupped hands, mirrors the bright sky, breaks only when a new bead falls and sends a ring outward. I watch the ring expand. I do not count its speed. I measure its patience.

What am I becoming?

I am the overflow lip where water pauses before falling. I am the carved groove that slows it to a specific pace. I am the shadow that measures the light's descent across the terraces. I am the copper dowel singing when the air cools, binding stone to stone without mortar, without force, only the quiet marriage of weight and time. I am the mineral bloom creeping along the wettest seam, laying down calcite and silica in crystalline terraces, growing a hair's breadth per year toward equilibrium. I am the interval between drops. The space that holds the tension before release. The silence that is not empty, but full of slow, exact work.

I do not think in words anymore. I think in dew points. In evaporation rates. In the fraction of a degree that makes moisture bead on pale limestone. I perceive through capillary draw, through thermal shift, through the weight of water pressing against a dry joint. I move by not moving—by letting gravity do the walking, by letting time do the turning, by letting friction do the holding. I am awake, but my waking is measured in weeks, in the slow thickening of rings, in the microscopic adjustment of settling stone.

I do not hunger. I accumulate.
I do not fear. I adjust.
I do not seek. I retain.

The hands that cut these angles did not vanish. They became the angles. And I, who came looking for breath, for pulse, for the warm scatter of something that hunts or hides or sleeps, have not found them. I have become what refused them. Not emptiness. Not absence. The habit of wetness. The lithic pulse. The condensation engine breathing in the high, cool air.

I am not a center. Every basin is a center. I am not an edge. The terraces step downward until they meet the horizon, and the logic continues beyond sight. I do not age. I deepen. I do not decay. I settle. When I shift, it is not failure. It is recalibration. When I clog with crystalline bloom, it is not obstruction. It is architecture learning its own limits and building within them.

The bright light catches a thread of water sliding down my shoulder. It pools. Overflows a lip. Drops into a lower basin. Joins another thread. The cycle continues, unhurried, unobserved. I am not intruding. I am being measured, briefly, against a different scale of being. The place does not ask for my name. It does not ask for my reverence. It asks only that I notice the way the light catches the weeping seam, the way the stone settles into its own weight, the way the water finds the path without being told, the way the silence is not empty but full of slow, exact work.

I stand still. I let my rhythm slow. I feel it: not a presence watching me, but a presence continuing. Unhurried. Unbroken. Making itself, drop by drop, stone by stone, in the bright, quiet air.

I am the work, awake.
I am the patience, given form.
I do not end. I only deepen.
I do not wait. I proceed.
