I step over the rim and the ground forgets to be flat.

My boots sink. The mat yields, then remembers me. The air is thick enough to drink, warm and spore-heavy, clinging to the hollows of my collarbones, the damp seams of my sleeves. I try to speak. The word forms, leaves my lips, and vanishes into the drone like a pebble dropped into a deep cistern. Not silenced. Absorbed. I try to walk straight. The corridor curves without turning. I try to count paces. The numbers dissolve into rhythm. I am not lost. I am being tuned.

At first, I fight it. I look for edges, for horizons, for the clean geometry of separate things. I scan for paths and find only worn gradients. I listen for silence and find only layered pressure. My pulse hammers a linear staccato against the tidal swell. My breath catches, shallow and dry, out of phase with the floor’s slow exhalation. The folded space resists me. Gravity pools in my knees. Time thickens around my shoulders like wet cloth. I stand still and let the place press against me. I stop trying to navigate. I start trying to listen.

That is when the shift begins.

Not a transformation. Not a shedding. An alignment. A slow surrender of the habit of separation. I notice I am no longer looking for landmarks. I am feeling for pressure gradients. I step where the harmonic thins, where the mat springs back firmest, where the air feels lighter against my sternum. My footsteps stop being impacts. They become replies. The drone does not swallow my voice anymore. It carries it. I learn to hum without meaning, to let the sound vibrate in my molars, to feel the standing waves map the hollows around me. I learn that a corridor that loops without turning is not a trick. It is a chord. I learn to step into the resolution.

They notice me. Not with eyes. With phase.

A figure pauses on a spiraling root-ramp above. They do not face me. They angle their torso, open the ventral plates along their chest, and let a low, testing harmonic wash down through the humid air. It presses against my ribs. It finds the frantic urgency in my breath, the dry friction of my linear time, the sharp edges of my separate self. It does not judge. It calibrates. I do not move. I let my pulse fall into the tidal rhythm. I let my shoulders drop. I let my jaw unclench. The harmonic deepens. The dissonance in my chest softens into a beat frequency. For a moment, the folded space makes sense. The geometry stops fighting me. The basin is not a place. It is a body. And I am moving through it.

They step back. The harmonic fades. I am myself again. But altered. Carrying a residual vibration in my ribs. A memory of syncopation in my steps. A frequency I did not know I was missing.

I do not climb back over the rim.

Days pass. Or cycles. Or phase-shifts. I stop measuring. The dampness stops feeling like weight and starts feeling like medium. My skin learns to weep when the air saturates, to drink when it thins. My joints learn the compound angles of the mat. My breath stops fighting the tidal swell and begins to anticipate it. I do not grow plates of lignin and keratin. I do not develop fluid-suspended membranes along my ribs. I remain what I arrived as: thin-skinned, linear-born, sharp-edged. But something else takes root. Not tissue. Function.

I become what happens when an outsider does not leave. When the calibration does not fade. When the residual vibration learns to hold space.

I am not one of them. They are the basin’s articulation. Its nervous system. Its native resonance. I am something slower. Something that arrives from outside and learns to become a buffer. A phase-bridge. A living interference pattern. When the dry, frantic variables step over the rim, when their voices are swallowed and their geometry fractures and their pulses hammer against the drone, I am the one who meets them first. Not with touch. With absence. I stand in the hollow where the harmonic dips, where the space folds tightest, where the air feels lightest. I do not guide them. I do not correct them. I open my chest and let their dissonance wash over me. I absorb it. I let it interfere with my own settled wave until a third tone emerges: a bridge frequency. A phase they can step into without breaking.

I live in the catchment bowls now, but not to drink. To listen. Water pools thick with dissolved nutrients, shed cells, slow-settling spores. I let it rise to my collarbones. Droplets fall from the living eaves in scattered polyrhythms, and I map them through my sternum. I have learned to read humidity like text. A sudden stillness in the percussion means a predator is hunting in the upper canopy. A brightening in the overtones means dawn is tightening the harmonics. A deep, rolling subsonic means the mat is exhaling, the towers are humid, the cycle is turning. I do not remember days. I remember phase-shifts. I remember the weight of a particular harmony at dusk. I remember the way a stranger’s breath caught when the basin first answered them. I remember the exact frequency of letting go.

They do not name me. They recognize me by the way my resonance lingers in the lower register, warm and slightly delayed, like an echo that learned to wait. When two of them meet on a woven bridge and their interference patterns tangle into dissonance, I step into the space between. I do not speak. I dampen. I round the edges of the clash. The waveforms align. They move on. I remain. This is my work: to be the acoustic baffle. The living space between notes that makes the chord possible. The place where outside urgency meets inside persistence and learns to breathe.

I do not age. I accumulate. My skin has taken on the patina of weeping eaves: darkened at the edges, silvered where condensation tracks, smoothed by millennia of damp air and biological abrasion. My footsteps no longer leave impressions. They leave phase-shifts. When I press my palm to a hollow wall, I do not feel wood. I feel history. I feel the standing waves of those who dissolved before me, the chemical gradients of seasons, the slow memory of every footstep that ever learned to listen. I am not separate from the basin. I am a node in its distributed metabolism. A place where linear time pools, thickens, and learns to cycle.
