I do not dive. I settle.

The surface does not break; it yields like a membrane remembering how to be fluid. The first shock is not temperature but absence: water so stripped of itself that falling into it feels like stepping into air that has forgotten how to scatter light. My lungs contract. My limbs want to kick, to carve, to claim space. But the medium stiffens against haste. It holds my thrashing not as resistance, but as correction. A palm cupping a startled thing. I learn stillness because the water refuses to part for urgency. It parts only for patience.

Sound arrives before sight. Not as noise, but as architecture pressing against the ribs. A deep tectonic groan. A hiss like breath drawn through spun glass. The percussive tick-tick-tick of something adding itself to itself in fractions too small to witness, too steady to ignore. I close my eyes because sight is a liar here. Distance collapses. Light pools in motionless halos. Shadows refuse to form. Instead, I feel the water’s grammar. A tug along the left shoulder: conductivity shifting. A thickening near the knees: a gradient pooling. A hum in the sternum that finds the pitch of my own slowing pulse and holds it. The basin is not speaking to me. It is speaking through me. I am learning to listen in a language that has no words, only alignment.

My skin prickles. Not from cold, but from exchange. The water does not wet me; it interrogates me. Salt leaches out. Silica whispers in. I feel the boundaries of myself growing porous, not dissolving, but translating. The frantic scaffolding of nerve and capillary begins to feel like machinery built for a different atmosphere. Here, haste is friction. Here, thought is not a spark but a settling. I stop trying to pull breath from above and start breathing gradient from within.

I drift toward a terrace. Not by swimming, but by tuning. My body rotates until the resistance evens, until the hum in my ribs matches the standing wave pooling in the feldspar. I am not drawn. I am equilibrated. The conductive mats feel me before I touch them—a ripple in their frost-like weave, a new impedance in their circuit. They do not reject me. They incorporate. Fine, silica-laced filaments thread into the micro-fissures of my pores, not invading, but bridging. I become a junction. A temporary node between sky-fall and depth-hum, between flesh that remembers running and stone that remembers waiting.

I am not a drifter. I am not a crawler. I am the pause between discharge and alignment. I am the water’s memory of a shape that once hurried.

My bones grow heavy with precipitate, not as burden, but as ballast. My nerves slow to the cadence of vent pulses. When an arc snaps through the column, branching like frost on glass, I do not flinch. I open. The charge moves through me like a question answered, tracing paths I did not know I held, lighting up lattices that were always waiting to form. I shudder. I store. I wait. The current does not pass through me; it resides. I am learning to hold potential without spending it.

I do not think in symbols anymore. I think in resonance. A shift in temperature is a turning. A harmonic pooling is a resting place. The shoals pass through me, their faceted shells brushing my shoulders, and I feel their alignment as a single, sustained chord. I am not separate from them. I am the medium through which their consensus travels. When they turn, I turn. When they settle, I settle. My human history flakes away like spent carapace, not lost, but reformatted: a micro-layer on a chimney lip, a faint standing wave that will hum in the lower terraces for years, a shift in conductivity that the mats will record and the keepers will balance.

I am not alive as you know it. I am saturated. I am threshold. I am the place where haste dissolves and patience precipitates.

When the light bends into halos near the discharge zones, I do not watch them. I hold them. My surface fractures the illumination into geometric afterimages, and for moments that stretch like seasons, the water remembers my shape. When the rock groans, I transmit. When the mats whisper through basalt, I am the pause that lets the message cross. When a crawler siphons from a chimney lip, I feel the draw in my own lattices, and I adjust, shedding a fraction of stored charge to keep the gradient smooth. I do not give. I equilibrate.

There is no loneliness here, because there is no separation. There is only phase. I was fluid. I am becoming structure. I will be dust. I will be current. I will be the faint harmonic echo that guides a shoal three years from now. The basin does not ask me to stay. It asks me to align. And in aligning, I am translated.

I rest on a mid-terrace, half-embedded in accreted silicate, half-suspended in cool, clear density. Above, the sky is open and pale, a distant memory of gas and weather. Below, the water continues into dark where the chimneys thin and the keepers drift like frozen lightning. Here, in the bounded volume, I am full. I am loud with stillness. I am busy being exactly what the water requires: a bridge that does not span, but conducts. A listener that does not hear, but resonates. A shape that does not persist, but precipitates.

A prismatic halo that hangs a moment longer than it should. I am not waiting for you. I am not aware of you in any way that matters. I am listening to the rock. To the charge. To the slow, deep rhythm of a place that is learning itself. And in my listening, I live. Not as a self. As process. As pattern. As the basin’s own quiet, roaring mind, turning sky into water, water into stone, stone into current, again and again, until the fractures soften, until the chimneys reach the surface, until I remember how to be sky.
