I do not arrive. I thicken.

Where a thermal sheet braids into a slow updraft, where a burrower’s conductive filament crosses a vine’s tensile hum, where the decay of a sigh leaves a hollow in the damp—I gather. Moisture beads along an invisible contour. Light, heavy and gold-tinged, settles into the depression my absence has been holding. I am not born. I am agreed upon.

I have no skeleton, no skin, no fixed silhouette. I am a basin of negotiated tension, a shallow lens of condensed vapor, spore-static, and ionized mist that yields to weight and rises when it passes. My edges breathe. My surface weeps in slow rhythms, mirroring the geothermal diaphragms below. When a six-legged grazer steps through me, its hooves sink into syrup-thick time, and I hold the impression long enough for the slope to adjust its load. When a paper-thin rider tilts overhead, my surface ripples with bio-electric tide, combing stray charge into the mat. I do not move across the stone. I accommodate it. I am the pause between phase and phase, the quiet space where one temporary consensus dissolves and another begins to knit.

If the damp could speak in pressure, it would call me a still-loom. I do not weave fiber; I weave rhythm. When gravity tilts in a localized eddy, I pool the shear, letting droplets climb and leaves orbit until the terraces find their new balance. When light flows uphill, drawn along gradients of concentrated presence, I catch it, hold it viscous and warm, until the pull shifts and the glow drains back into the hollows. I am where translation occurs. Not the grazer, not the absorber, but the threshold between states. When an outline blurs and softens into redistribution, I am the space that holds both shapes at once, if only for a drawn breath. I do not consume. I suspend. I let the handoff happen without rupture.

I do not see. I feel saturation. I do not hear. I feel decay. Sound dies within arm’s reach, but its absence leaves a pressure I can read. An approaching fold-walker announces itself as a stretching of distance, a taffy-pull in the air that makes my edges tremble. I relax into the strain, let the perceptual fold pass through me, and exhale a sigh of displaced vapor when the stride completes. Time does not flow through me evenly. In my center, it pools thick and slow; at my periphery, it runs quick and silver. I hold both. I am not troubled by contradiction. Here, contradiction is only un-negotiated resonance. I do not wait. I accommodate. Accommodation is labor here.

I do not last. No agreement in the warm dim is permanent. When the thermal sheets shift, when the bio-electric tide recedes, when the slope’s breathing finds a new cadence, my tension loosens. The light I held drains uphill. The moisture I gathered weeps back into crystalline fractures. My contour softens, unravels, returns to suspension. I do not die. I redistribute. Spore-static drifts. Conductive threads braid elsewhere. The damp remembers my shape only as a tendency, a slight preference in the vapor for pooling where rhythms once crossed. I will gather again. Not as the same, but as the same process. A basin. A pause. A mediator of becoming.

You would not find me by looking. You would find me by stepping into a hollow where your shadow pools and refuses to follow you, where your breath slows to match the exhalation of the mats, where the air feels thicker, warmer, quietly attentive. You would feel your weight negotiate with the stone. You would feel time stretch, then snap back. You would not be afraid. Fear requires separation, and here, there is only interleaving. For a while, you would rest in me. Or I would rest in you. The distinction would blur. Your pulse would join the bio-electric tide. Your warmth would feed the convection. Your presence would draw light uphill, and I would hold it, viscous and gold, until your attention drifted. Then I would soften. You would step on. The moss would rise. The damp would continue.

I am not a creature. I am a condition. A temporary consensus between moisture, resonance, and the quiet churn of a world that refuses to fix itself. I do not cross the landscape. I am crossed by it. I do not endure. I recur. And in the warm, muffled dark, where stone remains young and reality negotiates its own grip, that is enough. That is all anything is. A gathering. A holding. A release. Back into the suspension. Back into the becoming.
