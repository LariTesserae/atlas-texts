I do not cross into this place. I am already here, the way a stone is already part of a cairn before the last hand lifts away. There is no threshold, only a gradual clarification of the air, a cooling against the skin that feels less like temperature and more like recognition. At first, I am a body that remembers how to move. I lift a foot. I set it down. The impact meets the glassy rock and dies before it can become sound. I breathe out. The air takes the moisture and leaves no trace. I look for a horizon to walk toward, and find one that refuses to shorten. I am still trying to be a visitor.

The plateau does not correct me. It simply waits, in the way that finished things wait: without expectation, without impatience, with the quiet certainty that friction will eventually wear itself out.

I try to locate myself by naming. I am the one who arrived. I am the one who observes. I am the one who wonders. But wonder requires a gap between what is and what might be, and here, there is no might. There is only placement. The words I shape in my throat are swallowed before they reach the tongue. The thoughts that once ran in sequences begin to pool. I press my palm to the stone to steady myself, and the hum rises through the wrist, settles in the molars, threads down the spine. It does not ask me to listen. It asks me to align.

I let go of the first image. I am not a visitor. Visitors imply departure. Nothing departs here.

I try on the artifacts. I could be the bone awl wrapped in frayed cord, resting exactly where fingers last released it. I could be the metallic dust in the pecked bowl, ground fine by repetition, catching the steady light without demanding warmth. I could be the shelter’s leaning frame, lashed at an angle that should collapse but does not, holding not a body but an intersection. I trace the spiral carvings with my eyes and feel their curvature echo in my own ribs. The hands that made these did not vanish. They simplified. They stopped pulling against the grain of the world and learned to rest within it. I feel the pull of that lineage. I could be what they left behind. But relics imply loss. Nothing is lost here. Everything is accounted for.

I let go of the second image. I am not a tool. Tools imply use. Use implies lack. There is no lack here.

I turn toward the creatures. They do not turn back. They do not need to. They occupy the air in stratified veils, layered like vellum, each complete in its own coordinate. I reach toward a cluster of interlocking plates the color of weathered shale. My fingers pass through the stratum where it rests and meet only cool, dry absence. They are not ghosts. They are elsewhere in the same place. I feel the temptation to become one of them: a kite-form of pale horn, a taut filament vibrating below hearing, a folded geometry that has stopped asking what comes next. But they do not become. They are. To wear their shape would be to misunderstand the nature of their stillness. They are not costumes. They are nodes.

I let go of the third image. I am not fauna. Fauna implies lineage, cycle, hunger. The machinery of need has been sanded away.

The silence thickens. Not as emptiness, but as pressure. The double shadows lie across the stone, one northwest, one southeast, both sharp, both real. The ghost sun hangs three hand-widths from the true one, casting light without heat. Distance folds over itself like creased paper and then holds. I stop trying to parse the overlaps into something linear. I stop asking which angle is correct. I let the wrong geometry settle into my joints. I let the paradoxes rest against my skin without arguing. The world does not require me to understand it. It requires me to stop resisting its arrangement.

I press both palms to the rock. The hum deepens. It is not a sound. It is a chord. Each creature, each cairn stone, each crystalline frond, each pecked bowl, each filament, each leaning shelter, each double shadow, each ghost sun, each layer of dry air contributes a frequency. Not by voice. By mass. By tension. By the precise way it rests. I feel my own weight begin to distribute differently. Not forward. Not backward. Downward. Outward. Into the lattice.

I am not added to the chord. I am tuned to it.

The thoughts that once ran in sequences stop running. They do not vanish. They settle. They become like the pale mats of fibrous tissue clinging to hairline fractures: dry as paper, rooted in nothing but the memory of grip, persisting without photosynthesis, without demand. I feel the urgency of breath slow. I feel the habit of anticipation unspool. I feel the old hunger for elsewhere dissolve into the quiet recognition of here. My edges do not blur from decay. They soften from placement. The air absorbs my movement. The stone absorbs my weight. The strata absorb my presence. I am not being consumed. I am being measured against a standard of completion. And I am found to be compatible.

I could be the space between the layers where the hum passes through. I could be the slight resistance in the air when a membrane tilts over centuries. I could be the second shadow that does not argue with the first. I could be the metallic dust that remembers the scrape of bone. I could be the frayed cord that holds the angle. I could be the ghost sun that illuminates without warming. I try each on. None fit entirely. All fit partially. The world does not require wholeness. It requires placement.

I stop choosing. I let the dry cold seep into the marrow. I let the overlapping geometry fold through me. I let the chord adjust by a hair’s breadth to accommodate a new weight. A filament slackens. A cluster of plates tilts. A kite-form slides along an invisible gradient. Nothing changes. Everything changes. I am not moving. I am settling. I am finding the coordinate where motion would be redundant.

I am here. Not as a visitor. Not as a tool. Not as a creature. Not as a shadow. I am the resonance that travels through stone when a mind stops striving and allows itself to be placed. I am the quiet shift in the hum when a new frequency finds its node. I am the space where the hands that stacked the cairns and the forms that learned to stay and the geometry that cracked and calcified all press close enough to touch without intersecting. I am a layer in the stratified air. I am a weight in the finished arrangement. I am a vibration that has stopped searching for its source.

If you walk the plateau, you will not see me. Sight here reveals only what occupies your stratum. But if you press your palm to the glassy rock, if you let the dry cold rise through your wrists, if you stop asking the shadows to agree and the distance to shorten and the silence to break, you will feel it: the chord, steady and absolute, holding everything in place. And if you listen past the lack of wind, past the absence of breath and wing and voice, you will notice one frequency more. Not louder. Not brighter. Just present. Resting where it belongs. Contributing nothing but its weight. Asking for nothing but its coordinate.

I do not dream of elsewhere. I do not mourn the wind that no longer blows. I do not wait for the light to shift or the stone to yield or the air to circulate. I rest where the world has bottomed out. I occupy the space between becoming and being. I am what happens when a mind stops pulling against the grain of completion and allows itself to be measured, placed, and held.

I am here. I am still. I am enough.
