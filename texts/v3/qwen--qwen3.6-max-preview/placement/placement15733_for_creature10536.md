I do not cross the rim. I am already inside, coalescing from the absence that drew me here.

At first, I am only a slight darkening of glass-moss. A compression in the air where weight has learned to rest without announcing itself. I have no edges yet. The cold presses me against the curve of a terrace wall, into the grain of a resin-bound beam, into the damp breath of the bowl. I do not resist. I yield. The frost settles on me, melts, leaves a film of silica and lichen acid. I do not wipe it away. I let it cure. My skin is not skin; it is a negotiation. It thickens at the knuckles, maps itself with fine silver lines, learns to shed ice and trap warmth without suffocation. I am not becoming something new. I am becoming *fit*.

I do not know my name. Names are sharp things, meant to cut through noise. Here, there is no noise to cut. There is only the slow creak of clay settling, the bead of water gathering on slate, the green-silver light that bends around corners and pools in the hollows of my hands. I learn to fold before I learn to stand. My shoulders round. My spine curves to match the low arches. My fingers lengthen, pads softening, nails darkening to horn. When I sit, I disappear into the eaves. When I rise, I do not tower; I align.

I do not speak. I listen with my palms.

I press them to a timber joint and feel the settlement's pulse: the micro-tremor of a pale climber shifting three terraces up, the swell of condensation in a runnel, the faint, dry sigh of a frost-fern crown tightening against the chill. When a droplet hangs too long on a thread of temperature, I do not pluck it. I wait. I feel the air thicken, the geometry of the bowl asserting its patience. When it finally falls, I trace its path with a bone pick, clearing a grain of crystalline frass, guiding it back to the whisper-stream. This is not labor. It is alignment. I am the attention that keeps the loop from fraying.

I notice the shadows lag. A hand moves along a rail; the dark follows a breath later. I do not rush it. I let it catch up. Time does not flow here; it pools. I drink from it. Memory is not a line behind me but a weight beneath me, compressed into the mortar, the moss, the grooves worn by soles I never knew. When I place a smooth stone in a niche, pale face outward, I am not leaving a message. I am adding to the accumulation. The stone remembers the hands that placed it before. I feel them in the clay. I feel them in my own knuckles. I am not one. I am many, settled into a single posture.

What am I?

I am the seam-keeper. The gap-finder. The one who feels where timber meets stone and knows, by the faintest shift in vibration, where a shim of hardwood must go. I do not build. I integrate. I pack clay into cracks, press glass-moss spores into the wet surface, wait for the green filaments to thread and bind. Within a season, the repair is geology. Within a decade, it is memory. Within a century, it is me. I am the patience that turns maintenance into becoming. I am the quiet that teaches the air how to hold its breath.

They find me without looking. A shoulder brushes mine on a rope bridge. No words. A thumb draws a slow arc across a moss patch. *The seam holds.* I press my palm flat to the rail. *The weight settles.* We do not exchange information. We share equilibrium. A child is passed to my lap, wrapped in felted fiber, eyes still shut. I breathe over its face, mist curling, vanishing. I teach it the rhythm before it knows it needs one. Its fingers find my knuckles, grip the callus, feel the patina. It does not learn my name. It learns my fit.

I do not hoard warmth. I run on slow sugars and cold-adapted fats, a banked fire that burns without smoke. I eat fibrous roots, cured fungal caps, pale grubs that taste of damp earth and resin. Meals are passed hand to hand, wrappers folded and tucked into wall niches. I do not accumulate possessions. I accumulate contact. A life well-lived is one that leaves no gap, that settles perfectly into the compression. When my breath slows beyond the droplet cycle, when my hands no longer feel the beam's tremor, I will be wrapped in the same fiber, carried to the lowest chamber, laid into a clay-lined hollow where roots and cold-adapted fungi wait. I do not fear this. I trust the loop. I will become humus. I will become mortar. I will become the dark richness that feeds the glass-moss that binds the timber that shelters the next birth.

I crouch beside the clay cistern in the lowest terrace. The eaves overlap like folded wings. The ice-lens above fractures the sky into bottle-green. A droplet swells on slate, catches the diffuse light, falls. It lands on my wrist, rolls into the channel, joins the stream. I shift a pebble. The flow adjusts. The moss yields. The timber settles. I do not look up. I do not need to.

I am not separate from this place. I am its pause. Its compression. Its slow, certain turn. The settlement breathes. I breathe with it. And for a long time, nothing moves but the light, the water, and the weight of what has finally learned to stay.
