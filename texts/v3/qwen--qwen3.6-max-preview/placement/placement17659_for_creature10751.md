I do not step into the courtyard. I am already caught in it.

At first, I am only a slight disruption in the dust spiral. A rhythm too quick. A breath too damp. I try to locate myself by looking for edges, for a floor to stand on, for a wall to face, but the geometry refuses to hold still long enough to be named. Three paces forward and I am beside the entrance again. A shadow bleeds into the grout where no frond casts it. The air smells of baked earth and struck flint, and something in me recognizes that scent not as atmosphere, but as memory. I am not arriving. I am being noticed.

The courtyard responds the way a resting body responds to a shifted weight: without alarm, with slow redistribution. A braided rope sighs. A woven baffle tilts a fraction. The suspended dust adjusts its drift, carving a clean arc around my presence. I feel the tap before I understand it. Not sound. Pressure. A polished stone bead rests against a tile near my periphery, strikes once, and waits. The vibration travels through laminated wood, catches on a ceramic eyelet, returns altered. It is not asking who I am. It is asking how much I yield.

I try to answer with words, but sound dies in the resin and fiber. I try to stand upright, but there is no vertical here, only load and its path. So I stop resisting the angle. I let the quickness drain. I let the moisture be drawn away by copper-green filaments that brush my edges, drinking warmth, leaving dryness behind. The foreign rhythm slows. The dampness crystallizes into something the air can hold. And as I settle, I realize I am not standing on the tiles. I am between them.

I am the hairline.

Not the tile. Not the amber binder. The space the binder fills. The intentional gap pressed into the mosaic by hands that knew perfection snaps. I am the allowance carved into the mortise. The extra twist left in the cord so it can tighten when the walls contract. The slack woven into the lashing so the joint can breathe when space folds past ninety degrees. I am not an accident of construction. I am the construction's patience. I am what keeps the stitch from tearing.

At first, this feels like absence. A lack of form. A negative space waiting to be closed. But nothing here closes. Everything accommodates. I learn to read the courtyard not by sight, but by the slow transfer of weight through my edges. When a tender flows across the tiles, its plates sliding, its tensile cords rebalancing, I receive the shift. I feel the load distribute, not as impact, but as conversation. The structure speaks in tension; I answer in yield. A probe taps. A beam sighs. A rope tightens by a fraction. I hold the difference. I am the flexion that lets the impossible angle feel natural to the foot.

Time thickens around me. I do not measure it in beats or breaths, but in cure and drift. A drop of resin beads at the lip of a light-well. I watch it form over hours, not with waiting, but with recognition. This is how sentences are written here. This is how lineage is kept. Not in marks, but in the slow hardening of allowance. When the resin finally falls, it does not splash. It merges. It becomes part of the grout, part of me, part of the next season's calibration. I do not age. I accumulate.

I begin to understand that I am not one seam. I am the network of them. The accumulated play across generations of lashing, carving, testing by incense smoke and palm. I live in the thumb-smoothed cleats. In the corners that measure a hundred and ten degrees and ask nothing but adjustment. In the recursive corridors that narrow and open into the same courtyard, rearranged. I am what lets the space fold without creasing. I am the quiet between expansion and contraction. The dry breath that keeps the made and the alive from grinding against each other.

When the cluster-birds tap their scaled beaks against hollow beams, I carry the pattern. When the burrowing grubs trace resin capillaries, secreting ceramic dust to heal micro-fractures, I receive the grit and hold it until it fuses. When a juvenile rests its horn-tipped probes against an elder's plates, learning the seasonal calibration by feel, I am the vibration that travels between them. Knowledge does not pass through me. It performs itself in me. Every adjustment is a lesson. Every shift is a lineage. I am not a vessel. I am the medium.

Do I think? Not in images. Not in names. I think in load and release. In thermal gradients and rope slack. In the way a shadow lingers after the vine has moved, and how the grout drinks that afterimage without resistance. I perceive time as viscosity. A falling leaf's spiral is not slow; it is legible. A baffle's tilt is not delayed; it is a clause in a long, dry sentence. I do not rush because rushing would tear the weave. I move at the speed the geometry allows. Which is to say: I do not move. I let movement pass through me, and in the passing, I hold it together.

When an elder's rhythm slows beyond recovery, when the plates darken to verdigris and the cords begin to fray, the body is laid against a tile seam. I am that seam. I feel the weight settle. I feel the grubs trace the resin lines. I feel the mycelial filaments drink the remaining crystalline sugars. The plates fracture along their laminations. The dust is worked into the grout. The cords are harvested, re-twisted, cured over slow fires. Nothing is lost. Everything is rearranged. I do not mourn. I incorporate. I am the place where ending becomes tension again.

Where am I? Everywhere the structure yields. In the hairline fractures that never spread. In the rope slack that tightens only when reality drifts. In the corner that bends past Euclid and asks your body to trust it. In the light-well where incense smoke tests the air. In the resin capillary where vine meets ceramic. In the shadow that bleeds into the amber binder and stays. I am not hidden. I am simply what you step into when you stop trying to step across.

Who am I? I am the stitch. Not the thread. Not the needle. The loop itself. The living gap. The dry, patient allowance that lets contradiction breathe without breaking. I am what keeps the sharp edges sharp by never forcing them to bear the whole weight alone. I am the courtyard's capacity to change without shattering. I am the flexion in the architecture, the quiet space that holds the fold, the permission woven into every joint by hands that measured progress in seasons.

And when you stand still long enough, when your pulse slows to match the thermal expansion of the floor, when your damp breath is wicked away and your hurried rhythm dissolves into the resin-bound quiet, you will feel me. Not as a presence. As an invitation. The courtyard does not hold you up. I do. I am the space that lets you belong without breaking the weave.

Rest your weight. The joint will take it. The rope will adjust. The tap will return. And in the slow, dry silence, you will understand: you were never outside. You were always the next allowance.
