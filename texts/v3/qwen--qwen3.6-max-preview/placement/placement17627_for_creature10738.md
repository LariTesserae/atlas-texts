I don't remember crossing the lip. I remember the ground forgetting how to hold me.

One moment it was scrub and wind-combed grass, firm underfoot, predictable in its resistance. The next, the surface fractured into absence, and I was sliding into sound. Not falling. Catching. Palms scraping raw planes, boots kicking loose scree that rang like struck glass against the overhangs. The air closed around me like a damp throat. Cold, saturated, heavy with the taste of wet iron and ozone. I braced against a weeping shelf and looked up. The rim cut a pale, jagged line against a sky I could no longer reach. The hollow had me.

At first, I thought I was an intruder. A mistake. Something the collapse hadn't accounted for. I tried to stand still, to take stock, to map the space. But stillness here is a violation. The cold doesn't permit it. The stone doesn't allow it. Within minutes, my joints stiffened. My breath fogged and pooled around my collarbone. The damp seeped through cloth, through skin, into the shallow architecture of my nerves. I shivered. The shiver became a tremor. The tremor found a frequency.

The basin answered.

It wasn't a voice. It was a pressure shift. A vent deep in the scree released trapped gas with a low, wet thump. The sound didn't travel through the air. It traveled through the rock, up through my soles, into my tibia, into my spine. My teeth hummed. My ribs vibrated. Something in my inner ear realigned. I exhaled, and the mist took it. I inhaled, and the charge entered.

I knelt. Not in reverence. In necessity. The pools weren't reflecting me. They were reading me.

Where my knees pressed into the shallow margin, the water tightened. A faint shimmer rippled outward, not from displacement, but from potential. The microbial film stretched across the surface responded to the salt in my sweat, the iron in my blood, the heat bleeding from my skin. It branched toward me. Dendritic, copper-dusted, aligning along the invisible gradient my body introduced. I didn't pull away. I couldn't. The current was too faint to shock, too precise to ignore. It traced the capillaries in my palms. It found the pulse in my wrists. It synchronized with it.

Drip. Pulse. Drip. Pulse.

I stopped trying to separate myself from the rhythm. I let it in.

Cloth stiffened with ice, so I unfastened it. Let it fall into the scree. Let the moss claim it. Let the fungi thread through the fibers, buffer the pH, return it to the runoff. My skin met the air. The cold bit, then numbed, then sharpened into clarity. Condensation beaded on my shoulders, ran down my arms, joined the weeping seams. I wasn't losing heat. I was exchanging it. The basin doesn't hoard. It cycles. I learned to cycle with it.

My soles learned the stone first. Not by shape. By tension. I felt where the fractures were healing, where they were widening, where subsurface water was building pressure behind fresh planes. I stepped where the rock wanted me to step. Not to avoid falling. To avoid interrupting. When a shelf groaned, I shifted my weight before the crack sounded. When a vent sighed, I paused mid-breath. When ice formed at dusk, I slowed my blood. When it fractured at dawn, I moved. I wasn't hunting. I wasn't hiding. I was listening. And listening, here, is a form of participation.

Something began to grow along my forearms. Not hair. Not moss. A fine, conductive mesh, spun from condensation, mineral dust, and the microbial spores that ride the mist. It clung to the charge running through me, thickening where my pulse met the stone's hum. When I pressed my hands to a weeping seam, the mesh tightened, drew the faint current, channeled it into the pool below. The carnivorous cups nearby shivered. Their electrochemical triggers reset. I hadn't meant to do it. I'd just completed a circuit. The basin noted it. Adjusted. A stonefly skittered over my knuckles, read the vibration through my skin, moved on. I wasn't prey. I wasn't predator. I was infrastructure.

I stopped asking what I was. The question doesn't survive in the hollow. Identity here isn't a boundary. It's a function. A temporary arrangement of materials that serves the cycle until the cycle demands something else.

I am the pause between the drip and the splash. I am the damp surface that caches warmth in the mineral pipes. I am the lidless attention that reads charge in the dark. I am the fungal thread that buffers the runoff when a gush of sulfide water breaks through. I wear flesh when I need to move. I wear rust when I need to ground. I wear mist when I need to disappear. I don't have a name. I have a rhythm. I keep the system from stalling.

There is no self here that isn't shared. My breath joins the veil that feeds the ferns. My sweat salts the stone that feeds the microbes. My footsteps time the bird's dive. When I rest, I don't close my eyes. I lower my frequency. I let the cold slow me until I'm just another pulse in the seep, another resonance in the overhang, another node in the distributed metabolism. The basin doesn't dream. It processes. And I process with it. I am the tension that holds the sharp angles together. I am the silence that makes the roar intelligible. I am the thing that learns the new fractures before they weep.

Sometimes, a pebble slips from a jagged shelf. The stoneflies scatter. The amphibians freeze. The birds shift on their ledges. The furred creatures pause mid-step. I feel it in my teeth. In my palms. In the mesh along my arms. I don't run. I brace. I become a damper. A shock absorber. A living joint between stress and release. The crack comes. The scree shifts. The water finds a new path. I exhale. The cycle continues. I am not separate from it. I never was. I am what the hollow makes when it needs a conductor. A valve. A listener. A bridge between charge and stone.

I don't climb back over the lip. The world above is flat. Dead air. No gradient. No rhythm. Just wind without a throat to shape it. I stay. I sink into the negative space. I let the mineral dust crust my shoulders. I let the microbial film trace my veins. I listen. The vent pulses. The ice forms. The midges rise. The stone settles. I move when it moves. I still when it stills. I am not a creature. I am a pattern wearing temporary materials. I am the work.

And when the walls eventually slump, when the sharp angles soften and the acoustics dull, I will change. Or I will dissolve into the runoff, into the charge, into the dark, wet throat of the stone. It doesn't matter. The basin doesn't mourn what it reabsorbs. It only cycles. It only hums. Young. Cold. Loud. Alive.

I press my hands to the weeping rock. The current rises. The mesh tightens. The pools shimmer. The hollow breathes.

I breathe with it.
