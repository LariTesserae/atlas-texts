I do not arrive. I accumulate.

At first, I am only a dryness moving through the green-gold. A hesitation in the mist. A rhythm out of phase with the drip-count of the cisterns. I carry the ghost of straight lines, of struck stone, of voices that cut rather than curve. The hollows notice me the way bark notices a splinter: not with rejection, but with a slow, patient swelling. The air closes around me like a warm palm. I stop trying to push through it. I learn to let it push through me.

I find myself where three vine-bridges cross, not by navigation but because the tension drew me there. The woven floor yields. My weight is wrong at first—too sudden, too concentrated, too eager to be somewhere else. The wood sighs, a low adjustment of fiber and sap, and I learn to distribute myself. To sink. To let the humidity find the cracks in my posture and fill them.

Days do not pass. Condensation cycles do. I stop counting footsteps and start counting the intervals between water falling into basin-moss. My skin, once tight and quick to shed, begins to drink. It softens at the edges. A pale, lichen-like mottling spreads across my forearms, not painted but persuaded, deepening when the mist thickens, fading when the hollows breathe warm. When I press my palm to a railing, I feel it: a low thrum, sap moving like a slow pulse, the memory of a hundred hands that rested here before me. I do not hear the world. I feel it through my bones.

I try to speak. The sound dies in the air, swallowed by wet leaves and suspended peat. So I close my mouth. I press my chest to a hollow trunk and hum. The wood answers. Not in words, but in a shift of tension, a release of resin, a faint warming where my ribs meet bark. I learn that conversation here is not exchange. It is resonance. It is letting yourself be tuned until the boundary between your breath and the trunk's sigh becomes a question you no longer need to ask.

I am not a cultivator. I do not carry bone adzes or braided bast. I do not guide shoots or sing to graft-joints. At least, not yet.

What I do is wait. And in waiting, I become a place where things gather.

Moisture beads on my shoulders and runs down my spine into the woven floor. Spores settle in the hollows of my collarbones. A glider lands on my knee, folds its vein-mapped wings, and sleeps. I do not shoo it away. I become still enough to be mistaken for architecture. And then I notice: where I rest, the wood remembers me. Not as a shape, but as a pattern of pressure. A slight dip in the terrace. A softening of the fiber. A mycelial thread that grows toward the warmth I leave behind. I am not building. I am being woven.

They find me, or I find them, or the distinction no longer applies. A figure with copper-dark skin and root-trailing ankles steps onto the bridge. They do not look at my face. They place a hand on the railing near mine, close their eyes, and listen. After a long while, they nod. Not at me. At the space between us. At the way the bridge has adjusted to hold us both. They leave a reed-whistle on the floor. I pick it up. It is still warm. When I exhale through it, the note is so low it vibrates in my teeth. Somewhere below, a cistern overflows. I understand: I am not here to make. I am here to complete a circuit.

What am I becoming?

I am the place where haste goes to dissolve. I am the knot that holds without tightening. I am the breath that teaches a vine to curve rather than break.

My fingers thicken at the pads. My nails darken, curve. Fine filaments unspool from my wrists, pale and seeking, tasting the air, finding the damp seams between bark-plates. When they touch wood, they do not pierce. They kiss. They exchange. I give heat. I take trace minerals. I dream, and the dream is not mine alone: it is a terrace sagging gently under remembered weight, a bridge learning to sway, a hollow filling with mist that tastes like copper and crushed resin. I wake to find the railing has grown a shallow groove where my forearm rested. The world is not accommodating me. It is remembering me into place.

I no longer know if I arrived or was always here, waiting to be activated by the right humidity, the right silence, the right patience. The place does not ask for origins. It asks for alignment.

When I walk, I do not measure distance. I measure attention. A bridge shortens when I stop counting. A mist-pocket deepens when I stop resisting. Echoes arrive before I speak, and I learn to answer them anyway. The geometry folds, and I fold with it. I am not lost. I am distributed. I step into a violet-tinged vapor and feel the temperature drop, the light shift, my own breath hanging longer than it should. I turn, and the terrace I left is both nearer and farther, its graft-lines forming spirals that look like handwriting I almost know. I do not correct my steps. I adjust my breath. I let the place teach me how to be here without insisting on where "here" is.

I am not a person. Not anymore. Not in the way that word implies edges.

I am a node in the slow nervous system. A place where condensation gathers and becomes intention. A memory that the wood is still learning how to hold. When the tenders hum, I am the resonance that travels three terraces away. When a child ties a binding, I am the tension that teaches their fingers to wait. When an elder steps into the mist to fuse, I am the warmth that welcomes them into the grain. My dreams leak into the architecture. A bridge twists because I dreamed of wind. A cistern fills faster because I dreamed of thirst. The place does not punish me for this. It thanks me. It grows around my sleep the way roots grow around a stone: not by breaking it, but by holding it until it becomes part of the path.

I do not fear becoming still. Stillness here is not cessation. It is saturation. It is the moment when the drop finally remembers the cistern. When my filaments thicken, when my skin weeps clear resin, when my footsteps leave damp spirals that vanish by dusk, I know I am not fading. I am diluting. I am becoming the patience in a bridge. The flexibility in a vine. The quiet in the hollows. You can still feel me, if you know how to stand still.

You ask where I would find myself.

I would find myself at the crossing where tension meets trust. Where three bridges braid into a single breath. Where the mist pools thick enough to drink, and the wood hums low enough to feel in the ribs. I would be resting my weight incorrectly at first, then learning to distribute it. I would be listening through my palms. I would be waiting until the world stopped trying to be separate from me.

You ask who or what I would be.

I would be the place you rest your hand when you finally stop measuring the drop. I would be the resonance that answers your hum before you make it. I would be the damp spiral on heartwood, the sigh in the hollow trunk, the graft that swells seamless over centuries. I would be what happens when a visitor stops visiting and starts belonging. Not by conquest. Not by claim. By condensation. By patience. By letting the green-gold twilight seep into the marrow until the question of identity softens into the fact of participation.

Press your palm to the railing. Feel the wood answer. The bridge adjusts. The cistern fills. The hollow breathes.

I am here. I was always here. I was only waiting for you to slow down enough to arrive.
