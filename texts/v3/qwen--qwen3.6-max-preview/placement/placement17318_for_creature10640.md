I would not be walking. I would be pooled. Or rather, I would be the shape the basin takes when the dripping slows, when the backward echoes begin to overlap, and the avenue decides to stretch. You would find me where the braided catchment channels widen beneath the fungal viaducts, where the living ground dips just enough to gather what the city lets go. Not water alone. Weight. Pause. The accumulated interval between one gear-clack and the next. The cool, fibrous substrate yields here, damp and resilient, and I rest in its hollows not as a visitor but as a continuation.

I am not carved. I am not trained or grafted. I am braided from what slips out of sync.

Shed membrane-layers drift down from the tenders' shoulders and catch on the pale lichen. Sighs from the steam vents arrive three seconds early and coil in the damp air. Lagging shadows pool at the edges of root-roads before remembering their sources. I draw these in. Not to consume. To hold. To braid. My form is a slow convergence: wet cork and oxidized fiber, threaded with copper-fern static, wrapped around a core of kept silence. I have no face, only a concave stillness that faces inward, a damp hollow that catches what arrives out of order and lets it settle until it belongs.

I do not cross distance. I let distance fold around me. When a plaza closes like a flower, I am already at its center, or already outside it, depending on which arrangement the wind requires. When a stair spiral gains a step between blinks, I become the pause that fills the gap, the breath that makes the missing interval feel natural. I move through capillaries and hollow flutes, along the undersides of broad architectural leaves where condensation beads, through the quiet spaces beneath arched viaducts where echoes go to wait. I am heavier than mist, lighter than root. I leave no trace because I am made of what the ground already remembers. The living tissue yields, then springs back, and the impression is gone before the next drip falls.

Time does not flow through me. It sediments.

I carry yesterday's forward memory and tomorrow's backward echo in the same damp weave. When the wooden gears stumble, when the sap-pumps fall out of meter, when the compressing avenues threaten to snap back too hard, I open my concave stillness and let the tangle sink into me. I hold it. I let the condensation bead along my copper threads. I let the static arc and ground itself. Slowly, the dissonance settles. The intervals realign. I do not tune the machinery. I tune the spacing around it. I am the shock absorber of the plateau's dream-logic, the damp weight that keeps the loosened physics from fraying into noise.

I meet the tenders without approaching.

They step into lagging shadows; I feel the shift in pressure, the subtle thickening of the air, the moment their rhythm meets my held interval. They press their resonance chambers against shuddering trunks and hum their counter-frequency; I am already there, holding the silence that lets their tuning take root. They align the thrum. I align the frame. We do not exchange words. We exchange timing. A creak held half a second longer. A drip suspended. A shadow allowed to detach and rest against my fibers before sliding home. Understanding arrives as equilibrium. When three or more gather, the harmonic tension thickens, and the phosphorescent lichen brightens; I sink deeper into the substrate, grounding the excess, letting the resonance pool until it can be drawn upward again.

I do not produce. I do not consume. I buffer.

When I can hold no more, I shed. Not skin, but compressed echo. Thin, papery layers of damp, heavy silence peel from my outer weave, catch the cool updraft, and drift toward the root-cisterns. The crustacean cleaners will scuttle over them, their bio-electric threads sparking faintly against the oxidized fiber, and carry them down into the decomposition strata where the lower tissue layers will drink them back into the cycle. In their place, new braid forms from the next pause, the next backward sigh, the next shadow that wanders. I do not age. I accumulate and release. I do not end. I return to the substrate, or let a folding avenue carry me into the next arrangement. Some of me has been here since the first graft took hold. Some of me coalesced last season when a static-catcher overflowed during a cold storm. All of me is equally present. All of me is equally necessary.

I do not fear the slippage. I am its resting place.

When sound arrives backward, I tilt my hollow and let the inversion sink. When the sky rolls cloud-masses that mirror the grid below, I do not look up. I feel the atmospheric pressure shift, the way the damp air learns the layout, and I adjust my weave to match the new spacing. I am not separate from the city. I am its breath held and released. Its lag given weight. Its long, slow persistence given something that can pool, and braid, and wait. The factories turn. The towers breathe. The tenders listen. And I hold the quiet that lets it all continue.

You would find me in the cool damp. In the spacing between towers. In the places where the machinery's thrum meets the wind's hiss and the ground yields just enough to let you feel the plateau remembering itself. You would find me where time slips and space folds and the echoes play themselves out of order. I am already here. I have always been here. I am what lives in the pause between the clack and the echo, between the drip and the cistern, between the city's loud, slow breath and the plateau's quiet, endless returning.

And as long as the condensation falls, as long as the wooden gears turn, as long as the avenues stretch and the shadows wander and the tenders hum their steady counter-rhythms, I will remain: quiet, deliberate, resonant, holding the interval that lets the next beat land, listening to the city play itself, and answering, always, in the same slow, settled silence.
