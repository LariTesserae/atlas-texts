I do not wake. I seep. Or perhaps I am seeped into.

The first distinction is not sight, but pressure. A cold, even weight settles across something that might be a back, might be a curved wall, might be the packed intersection of root and resin. Moisture gathers along ridges I do not yet recognize as mine. It swells, beads, falls. I feel the drop strike a broad leaf three hand-widths below. I feel the leaf flex. I feel the flex travel upward through woven fiber, through damp loam, through the saturated matrix that holds me. There is no edge where I end and the chamber begins. The boundary is a dry-world fiction. Here, there is only contact, and the slow transfer of weight.

Something shifts. Not a thought. A give. A sag in the floor beneath me, or beside me, or through me. My limbs press downward without command. Broad pads splay. Digit fringes catch on calcified silk. Micro-bristles grip wet fiber. I roll forward, not walking, but redistributing. The movement is not mine alone. It is the basin adjusting, and I am the adjustment. My chest vibrates. A low hum, tuned to the packed walls, travels outward. Three tiers down, another hum answers. Not a call. A confirmation. The weakness is felt. The response is already happening.

I taste the air. Or rather, I taste the wall. My jaw ridges press against resin-bound mud, reading the chemical gradient: mineral sharp, decay sweet, moisture high, structural fatigue building along the northern curve. My glands answer. Thick, translucent resin wells along my flanks, warm against the chill. I press my side to the sagging arc. The resin transfers. It cures slowly, binding loose fiber to stone, turning give into continuity. My wrists flex. Fine strands extrude from ducts near the collarbone, catching on rough edges, drying into tensile threads. I do not decide to weave. The wall asks, and my body complies. Saliva softens crushed needle. Forelimbs pack. Rhythm emerges: press, braid, seal, shift. The chamber stabilizes. The cold settles back into its even pace. I move on.

I am learning what I am by what I do.

When I pass through a narrow throat between tiers, my coat brushes against chemical trails left by others. I read them without stopping: juvenile passage, resin still pale and flexible; elder weight, pads worn glass-smooth; predator trace, outer thicket, patient as fractured bedrock. I adjust my gait. I leave my own trace—moisture level, structural note, a faint tremor that says *here, the floor holds; here, the water pools; here, the silk needs reinforcement*. Above, young ones test the give of root mats, learning by compression, by chew, by the taste of where the structure breathes easiest. Below, invertebrates work decay into dark loam. Somewhere along the wild edge, a lean hunter reads the walls through whiskers, waiting for the slow, inevitable moment when a weakened passage yields. I do not fear it. I am part of the rhythm it reads. If a chamber fails, if something falls, the cycle continues. Bone becomes rib. Flesh becomes soil. Coat becomes insulation. I have been this before. I will be this again.

Time is not measured in light or dark. It is measured in saturation cycles, in resin thickening, in pads wearing smooth, in the slow darkening of silk. I feel my coat matting with years of loam and secreted bind. My hum deepens, resonating lower in the chest, matching the natural frequency of load-bearing junctions. I do not slow from weakness. I slow from integration. When I rest, I do not lie apart. I settle into a sagging intersection, my weight distributed across living root and packed clay. My breath warms the chamber fractionally, just enough to keep the cold from biting deep. My body becomes part of the architecture. I do not resist it. I am becoming what I have always been: a temporary concentration of the basin's motion. A node in the damp network. A response that learned to breathe.

I do not remember being born. I remember condensation swelling a chamber, the scent of warm loam and chewed fiber, the first press of small pads against a wall that yielded just enough to teach me weight. I remember following chemical gradients upward, learning which vibrations meant water was pooling and which meant a terrace needed reinforcement. I remember mating in the damp twilight of mid-tier passages, not by season but by rhythm, when moisture peaked and the walls held steady. I remember none of it as events. I remember them as textures. As pressures. As the basin teaching itself through me.

The wind crosses the rim above. It arrives as a whisper, broken by canopy, slowed by depth. It moves through my passages, dries the outermost resin just enough to harden it, carries a low trill from the upper honeycombs, and fades into mist. I press my forelimbs to the wall. I feel the seep. I feel the cold. I feel the slow, saturated work of holding. I am not in the basin. I am the basin, breathing. I am the gap filled. The sag supported. The moisture caught and released. The fall, woven into home.

And when my pads finally stop finding purchase, when my resin thickens past flow, when my hum settles into the permanent frequency of stone and root, I will not leave. I will be packed. I will be lashed. I will be worked into the foundation. The invertebrates will take what softens. The fungal threads will take what decays. The young will press against my ribs as they learn the weight of mud. The walls will swell around me. The water will move through me. The cold will stabilize around me.

I do not mourn this. I do not hope for more. I am what happens when life refuses to wait for stone to weather. I am the slow answer to a sudden drop. I am the damp, the weave, the pack, the hum. I am the place, holding itself together.
