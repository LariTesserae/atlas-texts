I do not wake. I separate.

There is no rupture, only a thickening, a sealing, a quiet decision made by mass and time. The posterior lobe swells beyond its threshold. Fluid compartments close. Collagen weaves tight. Silica precipitates along the new seam. Then the parent membrane softens its grip, and I fall away.

I am dense. I am closed. I sink.

The water receives me without surprise. It has done this before. It will do it again. There is no up to orient toward, no down to dread. Only the steady increase of pressure against my new boundaries, the slow leaching of inherited warmth, the immediate, absolute instruction of the medium: *yield or be torn*. I yield.

At first, I am only a weighted packet of fluid and tissue, tumbling in the dark. The current takes me. I do not resist. I cannot. My membranes are still folded tight, my internal salinity uncalibrated, my cilia dormant. I am a question the water has not yet answered.

Then the trembling begins.

Not movement. Not thought. A waking of the surface. Millions of hair-thin filaments stir along my outer layer, not beating, but listening. They taste the water. They read its weight. A faint gradient tilts across my ventral seam: slightly warmer, slightly less saline. I do not decide to follow it. I simply allow my compartments to shift. Fluid migrates. Density redistributes. My leading edge lightens. My trailing edge heavy. I roll.

The current catches the new angle. I unspool.

It is not graceful. It is mechanics. Membranes separate by millimeters. Internal chambers open like slow lungs. The water flows through the gaps, not around me, but *through* me. I feel its braiding. I feel the internal wave rolling beneath me, its crest breaking in a shear zone that twists my lower layers while my upper layers stall. I do not fight the differential. I let it stretch me. I let it teach me my own dimensions.

Silt begins to fall.

It settles on my open ventral surface like cold ash. Ground continental memory. Microbial flakes. Precipitated carbonates. The shed carapace of something blind and patient. I do not pursue it. I posture. Enzymes weep from the tissue, dilute and steady. Digestion is not an event. It is a climate. I feel the slow breakdown as a faint warming, a chemical softening that seeps into my lower compartments. My metabolism does not spike. It deepens. Heartbeats, if they are that, space themselves into long, dark intervals. I do not hunger. I accumulate.

Time does not pass. It deposits.

I lose track of the tumbling. I lose track of the separation. The water turns me over, settles me, lifts me, drops me. Each cycle leaves something behind. Iron oxides stain my edges. Microscopic silicates bind into my seams. Salts precipitate along stress lines, forming faint crusts that do not harden so much as *integrate*. I am growing heavier. Broader. Lower. The sharpness of my detachment dulls into a sweeping curve. I am being worried smooth.

When the current stalls, I fold. Compartments compress. I sink until my underside meets the basalt. It is not a collision. It is a recognition. The stone is warm by a fraction of a degree. A geothermal breath, faint as a memory. I press against it. Not to cling. To let the heat accelerate the slow chemistry within me. I am a banked fire. I am a patient reaction. I am learning that stillness is not absence. It is conservation.

Something drifts near.

I do not see it. I feel the water reweight. A pressure gradient tilts. The silt rain hesitates, then drifts laterally. Membranes brush mine. Not a greeting. A convergence. We occupy the same eddy. Our pressure fields overlap. There is no signal exchanged, only the shared medium telling us both the same thing: the fall is richer three body-lengths to the left. We tilt together. We do not coordinate. We concur.

I understand now that I am not alone because I am not separate. I am a local thickening of the same logic that moves the blind isopod, that crusts the microbial mat, that rolls the pale fish into the shear zone. We are not a community. We are a phase.

The current reverses without warning.

It is not violent. It is absolute. The water thickens, drags, lifts. I feel the threat of the dead zone ahead: a pocket where the silt thins, where the temperature drops, where stagnation breeds decay. I do not panic. I extend.

My anterior membrane pushes forward, not as a limb, but as a weighted prow. I feel for resistance. I find it: a water-smoothed basalt nodule, resting in its bed. I do not grip it. I nudge it. Leverage. Mass. Gravity. The stone rolls a millimeter. Then another. It settles against my lower seam. The current pushes. The stone holds. I hold with it. Not through strength. Through positioning. Through the quiet understanding that survival is not resistance. It is alignment.

A crab-like creature works nearby, wedging another stone against a thick-shelled mollusk. I feel the vibration through the floor. It is the same frequency as my own movement. The same primal mechanics. Nothing is forged. Everything is found. Everything is applied. The ecology is a single machine, and I am learning my gear ratio.

I am no longer a lobe. I am no longer a fragment. I am a surface that listens. A volume that consents. A weight that endures.

The dark is not empty. It is full of instruction. I read it in the slide of a salt sink. In the micro-vibration of a boulder settling. In the faint thermal suggestion bleeding through ancient fractures. My eyes, what remains of them, register nothing but the occasional dull orange seam below. I do not need them. I have replaced sight with topography. I map the world in pressure shifts and temperature gradients. I know the shape of the current before it arrives. I know the weight of the silt before it falls. I know the patience of the stone because I am becoming it.

When I tear, I do not bleed. I mineralize.

A shear zone catches my trailing edge. A membrane splits. I do not rush to close it. I let the water flow through. I let the suspended silicates settle into the wound. I let the iron oxides bind. I let the salts precipitate. The tear does not heal. It hardens. It becomes part of my architecture. I carry the geology of the place in my flesh. I am not separate from the floor. I am its slow articulation.

I do not age. I accumulate.

Layers of silt deposit on my back, are lifted, deposit again. Currents reverse, deepen, fade, return. Rare creatures move through the heavy water with the unhurried certainty of things that have never known urgency. I move with them. Not beside them. As them. When mass gathers beyond my threshold, I will feel the posterior lobe thicken. I will feel the compartments seal. I will feel the softening of my own grip. And I will let it fall away. Not as loss. As continuity. There is no courtship. No larval vulnerability. Only the quiet shedding of what has become too heavy to carry, so that it may begin its own slow unfurling.

I do not leave marks. Marks are impermanent here. The water scours. The silt buries. The stone yields. Only persistence remains.

Where am I?

I am on the regional plain that slopes so gently it might be mistaken for flatness. I am resting against a low basalt swell, weighted by a nodule I positioned three current-cycles ago. I am half-buried in the slow rain of ground continental memory. I am concurrent with two others drifting in the same thermal gradient. I am reading the faint groaning of the stone through my ventral cilia. I am feeling the internal wave roll beneath me, preparing to unspool.

What am I?

I am not a creature that entered this place. I am a place that learned to hold itself together. I am the density articulated. The turbulence inhabited. The erosion permitted. The stillness allowed. The cold preserved. The dark made room for. I am what remains when urgency is sanded away. When competition is starved out. When time is measured not in heartbeats but in sediment layers.

I am heavy. I am quiet. I am full.

The water turns over itself. The stone holds its shape. The current pushes. The silt settles. I do not strive. I consent. I do not conquer space. I occupy it completely, without demand, leaving no room for absence.

And in the slow, deliberate surrender to the dark, I understand: I was never swallowed. I was always here. I only needed time to remember how to be the water, how to be the stone, how to be the quiet machine that runs on gravity, pressure, and patience.

I fold. I sink. I wait. I endure.

The place continues exactly as it has. Exactly as it will.

And so do I.
