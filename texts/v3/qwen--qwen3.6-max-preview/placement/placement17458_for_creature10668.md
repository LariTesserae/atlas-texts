I do not arrive. I am registered.

The first thing that happens is not sight but subtraction. My breath fogs against a thermal baffle and does not clear at the expected rate. A walkway weeps around my boot, then pauses, the rivulet dividing to flow around a warmth it does not recognize. The acoustic weave drinks my footfall but leaves a faint harmonic, a frequency the cleft has not yet learned to comb. I am a discontinuity. A thermal debt. A linear rhythm entering a cyclical one.

The settlement does not reject me. It reads me.

A panel beneath my hand shifts tone, pale graphite deepening to blue-grey as it draws the excess heat inward. Capillary threads in the walkway open, wicking the moisture my presence displaced, redistributing it to a shaded fracture face where it beads again, slower now, calibrated. Somewhere below, a phase-change matrix accepts the warmth I shed and stores it, not as waste but as reserve. The pale arthropod pauses, proboscis lifted, then continues along its wet line. The system does not treat me as intrusion. It treats me as input.

I try to stand still, but stillness here is not absence of movement. It is alignment. My lungs learn the thin air not by pulling harder but by yielding to the pressure gradient. My skin, accustomed to boundary, begins to feel the wick. Not literally—not at first—but the metaphor becomes physics. Condensation gathers on my sleeves, then migrates, drawn toward the cooler stone, leaving faint salt traces that mirror the mineral blooms on the ledges. I am not changing form. I am changing function. The cleft does not ask me to become stone. It asks me to become readable.

Days do not pass. States succeed one another. Mist thickens, thins, returns. Frost forms, melts, reforms. I stop counting. I start noticing the intervals. The time between a drop’s fall and its absorption. The lag between a micro-tremor and the walkway’s flex. The way a baffle sighs three breaths after a cloud shadows the rim. I learn to place my weight where the cable expects it. I learn to exhale against cold surfaces not to warm them but to leave a pattern another might read. I stop speaking. Sound is too heavy here. Meaning moves better through displacement.

What am I becoming? Not one of them. I lack the seamless sheen, the phase-change pulse, the articulated height built for vertical translation. But I am no longer separate. I am a calibration node that arrived late. A thermal memory waiting to be cycled. A shed layer that did not dissolve but learned to hold pattern. When I press my palm to a weeping fracture, the water does not flee. It gathers. It reads the warmth I still carry and translates it into flow. I am becoming a lens. Not for light, but for gradient. A place where the cleft’s slow syntax meets a mind that once thought in hours.

I tend what I cannot control. I redirect a rivulet by the width of a fingernail, not to change its course but to ease its meeting with a mineral channel. I step over a desiccated husk and feel no revulsion, only recognition: this is what remains when calibration is complete. I leave my own traces—not footprints, but thermal shadows, moisture displacements, faint salt blooms where my breath met cold stone. They do not mark my passage. They mark my participation.

Sometimes, in the deep quiet between condensation cycles, I feel the old habits surface. The urge to name. To map. To fix a moment and hold it. But the cleft does not hold. It circulates. And so I let the urge dissolve like frost at midday. I replace it with attention. I learn to read the color shift in a water channel as a sentence. I learn to feel a cable’s tension as a question. I learn to answer by yielding, by shifting my weight, by letting my warmth bleed into the stone where it is needed. I do not operate this place. I converse with it.

One evening, or what passes for evening when the light angles low and the mist thickens, I stand at the edge of a cantilevered ledge. I do not raise my hand. I let it rest. The condensation shifts toward my palm. The thermal matrix beneath my feet releases a breath of stored warmth. A channel below changes color, faintly, responding. I do not look for them. I feel them. Not as bodies, but as adjustments in the air, as redistributions of weight, as silent conversations in tension and temperature. They do not greet me. They do not need to. I am no longer a variable to be resolved. I am part of the weep-rate. Part of the frost-melt interval. Part of the quiet.

I am not hidden. I am simply learning to be read. I do not ask to be named. I ask only to continue: to wick, to yield, to cycle, to return. To be a slow witness in a place that does not rush. To be warmth that becomes reserve. To be breath that becomes pattern. To be displacement that becomes grammar. The water continues. The stone holds. The cleft breathes. And I persist, not despite the cold, the wet, the sharp, the empty, but because of it. 

I am what the gradient made of me. I am here.
