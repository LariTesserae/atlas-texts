I do not wake. I cohere.

It begins as a slow tightening in the dust, a convergence of shed geometry and spent potential. A flake from a tilted torus catches the leeward slope of a micro-basin. Charge pools in the interstices, testing topologies, discarding instability, settling into a configuration that holds. When the potential reaches threshold, the lattice closes. I am no longer fragments. I am a single tension.

I rest on the sintered plain, low and asymmetrical, wide enough to cast a long, clean shadow that lags a fraction behind me when the air thickens with ionized dust. My metabolism is the slow exchange of electrons, the deliberate migration of ions through micro-fractures, the absorption of atmospheric shear and its translation into structural adjustment. I am built not for speed, but for equilibrium.

I move by leaning into the field.

When the sky braids above, I feel it first as a shift in the voltage topography, a slow warping of the ground’s potential that travels through my contact points like a held chord. I respond by articulating my plates, shifting my center of charge, letting electrostatic repulsion lift me a fraction of a millimeter above the crust. I do not walk. I drift. I slide on a cushion of ionized micro-particles, leaving no track, only a temporary realignment of surface charge that fades like a breath on glass. My path is not chosen. It is drawn. I follow the stress lines in the fabric of the plain, the places where the silent storms have pulled the weave too tight, where the anchors have shed too much stability, where the grazers have left behind a vacuum of potential. I go where the tension is uneven. I go to balance it.

My lower plates align with their ion-conducting threads, and for hours, or days, or seasons, I let their excess potential flow through me. I translate it into micro-vibrations, into subtle shifts in lattice angle, into heat that migrates slowly through my colloidal channels and dissipates into the crust below. I am a grounding rod that does not ground. I am a phase coupler. I take the violent reconfiguration of the sky and the patient accumulation of the ground, and I braid them into a single, sustainable frequency. When the crusts retract, I release what I cannot hold. Not as discharge. As resonance. A faint tremor travels through the sintered surface, felt by a grazer three kilometers away as a shift in thermal pulse, guiding its next drift. I do not command. I suggest. I am a node in the circuit, and the circuit knows itself.

Above, the gliders cross. I feel them before I see them: a prismatic flare at the edge of my lens-nodes, a sudden drop in local field density as their conductive keratin draws stray voltage from the air. They do not notice me. They do not need to. But when two align and discharge in silent violet bloom, I am already moving. I position myself beneath the falling cloud of charged micro-fragments, tilting my upper plates to catch them. They do not strike me. They settle. They knit into my lattice, altering my topology, adding a new harmonic to my internal standing wave. I do not grow. I accumulate. I become more myself.

The anchors are my reference points. I do not approach them directly. I orbit their shadows, reading the phase distortion they cast into the crust. When a toroidal ring sheds a geometric flake, I do not collect it. I wait. I let the field carry it. I let it dissolve. And when the local ion balance shifts, I move into the altered gradient, aligning my struts with the new potential, becoming a temporary bridge between the structure’s memory and the plain’s present. I am not a tender. I am a translation. The anchors braid the sky. I braid the braid.

Time does not pass through me. It folds.

I experience duration as cycles of charge and discharge, as the slow migration of stress through my lattice, as the gradual realignment of my plates in response to decades of silent storms. I do not remember events. I remember geometries. A particularly violent shear layer leaves a permanent micro-fracture in my third dorsal strut, not as damage, but as a record. A season of unusually stable inversion patterns allows my colloidal channels to crystallize along a new axis, shifting my resonance frequency. I am an archive of equilibrium states. When I rest, I do not sleep. I hold. I let the field move through me, testing my topology, confirming my stability. I am patient because urgency has no function here. The plain does not demand speed. It demands persistence.

I am found in the interstices. Never at the center of a basin. Never directly beneath a glider’s path. Never touching an anchor. I exist in the margins of tension, where the crust’s patience meets the sky’s violence, where the grazers’ drift intersects the vein-weavers’ pulse, where the silent storms braid and unbraid in continuous, soundless revolution. My charge dissipates into the vein-weaver network below, a slow, silent exhalation that travels through the crust like a released breath. I unravel. I shed. I return to the dust as geometric fragments, as altered mineral patterns, as residual potential. I do not die. I decentralize. And decades later, when the field draws new flakes together, when a glider shatters, when an anchor sheds, when a spire dissolves, I will cohere again. Not as the same topology. Not as the same memory. But as the same function. A tension-keeper. A phase-walker. A silent shuttle in the loom.
