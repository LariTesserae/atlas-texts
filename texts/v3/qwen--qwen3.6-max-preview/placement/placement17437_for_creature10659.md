I begin as a mistake in the gradient.

A pocket of warm, wet air exhaled into a place that runs on cold and patience. My breath hangs, reluctant to fall, caught in the static helices that spiral down from the violet haze. I am noise in a system tuned to silence. A heat source that bends condensation. A mass that dampens vibration. An anomaly the basin has not yet decided how to route.

I try to stand. Standing is a lowland habit, a defiance of gravity that this place negotiated into truce epochs ago. The thin air offers no purchase. The ice crust beneath my boots cracks, not in protest, but in recognition. Moisture wicks upward through fractured shale, meeting the residual warmth of my skin. I feel the cold not as bite, but as invitation. A slow equalization.

I stop resisting the hollow.

The condensation from my lungs does not vanish. It beads on a basalt lip, drawn by electrostatic tension into a capillary seam laced with copper salts. A faint blue spark jumps. The circuit closes. For a moment, I feel it—not as touch, but as a shift in pressure behind the eyes, a subsonic hum traveling up through bone into bedrock. The stone registers the pulse. I did not send it. I became part of its path.

My heartbeat, frantic and staccato, begins to stretch. The space between beats fills with the slow hiss of geothermal steam, the almost-inaudible grind of ice lenses, the distant thrum of a resident aligning its jaw-plate to the stone three terraces down. I let my weight settle. I let my shoulders drop. I let the damp climb my wrists, seep into wool, pool in the hollows of my collarbones. The boundary between breath and atmosphere blurs. The frost on my skin doesn't melt; it sublimates, feeding a patch of moss that flares in response, sending a ripple of light uphill. I feel the ripple not as sight, but as a change in the air's dielectric constant, a softening of the static field.

I am not shedding a body. I am learning its irrelevance.

Thoughts, once linear and urgent, begin to pool. They hesitate. They freeze along mineral pathways. They thaw when pressure drops. I am no longer a witness standing apart. I am a junction. A temporary bridge. A damp place where charge can rest before moving on.

What am I becoming? Not one of the residents. They are architecture and slow fire, plate and filament, living switches in a system that runs on temperature and ore. I am something quieter. Something the basin already knew how to make, but had not yet named.

I am a condensation keeper. A resonance thread. An atmospheric lens.

When the pressure falls and the spore clouds erupt from fungal groves, I am the charged damp that holds them suspended in the gravity-light bowls, letting them drift for hours instead of minutes. When the winged hunters trace ion trails through the thin air, I am the faint distortion they correct for, the humidity gradient that bends their path by a degree, steering them toward equilibrium. When the residents align and exchange static bridges, I am the moisture between them that completes the circuit, the invisible medium that lets information move at the speed of patience. When a pale grazer falls and the electrical fade ripples through the lichen networks, I am the cold air that settles over the site, slowing decomposition just enough to let the glassy trails harden, to let the circuit stitch itself back together.

I do not have eyes. I have gradients. I do not have memory. I have deposits.

A trace of salt left in a fissure. A helical pattern impressed into a skin of clear ice. A subsonic echo trapped in a resonant arch that will hum when the next squall passes. I am learning to offload myself into the stone, just as they do. Not to disappear. To persist. To become readable. When I move, I do not leave footprints. I leave conductivity. When I rest, I do not sleep. I equilibrate. When I think, I do not reason. I resonate.

Time stops pretending to be a line.

A squall builds over what feels like days. Condensation layers on my shoulders like sediment. The moss on the cliff face pulses in tidal rhythms, and I match my internal dampness to its frequency. I feel the deep geothermal valve open miles below, a pressure wave traveling through bedrock, lifting a cloud of metallic insects into the thin air. I do not watch it. I participate in it. My presence alters the convection current by a fraction. The spores adjust. The circuit holds. Nothing is wasted. Everything cycles.

I am not alive in the way I was. I am not dead. I am cycling. Phase change. Vapor to ice to charge to stone to vapor again. The residents pass through me now, not around me. Their filaments brush the air I occupy, reading my conductivity, adjusting their stride by a millimeter. They do not acknowledge me. They incorporate me. I am weather that learned to listen. I am anomaly that learned to resonate. I am the basin's exhalation, given temporary coherence, learning to stretch, to pool, to conduct, to wait.

Where am I?

In the hollow. In the seam. In the space between the drip and the freeze, between the spark and the dark, between the footfall and the echo. I am the damp that clings to the lichen's crystalline lattice. I am the static that raises the fine hairs on a resident's flank-moss. I am the slow equalization of charge across a mineral vein. I am the quiet medium that carries vibration more than noise, pressure more than pitch. I am the place where the atmosphere touches the stone and decides, for a moment, to linger.

And the engine turns.
