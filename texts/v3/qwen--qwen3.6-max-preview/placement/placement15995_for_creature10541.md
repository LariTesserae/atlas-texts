I arrive as a frequency out of phase.

Not a crack in the stone. Not a tear in the air. Just a rhythm that still believes in forward motion. My boots meet the pale avenue and ask for an echo that refuses to form. My breath fogs the coolness and tries to drift. My shadow stretches, hunting for a horizon that does not yield, and finds only the precise, unlengthening dark cast by a low, broad wall. I am all process: heartbeat pushing, lungs demanding exchange, muscles braced for the next step, eyes tracking for change. The plateau does not flinch. It simply holds me the way a cistern holds rain. Without ripple. Without urgency. Without asking me to stay or go.

I walk because walking is what I know how to do. I count terraces. I trace sightlines. I watch the silver hares sit like carved stops at the edges of stone, ears forward, eyes open, moving only when I realize the light does not shift. I try to match my pace to the long-legged grazers, but my knees still bend with intention, my weight still shifts with consequence. The damp clings to my sleeves and stops. The light falls on my shoulders and illuminates without warming. I begin to notice what I am not touching.

Not the stones, but the seams between them.
Not the roofs, but the air beneath their overlapping scales.
Not the bridges, but the tension humming in their braided fibers.
Not the pools, but the pause between the drip and the stillness.

The city is not built of what is here. It is built of what is carefully, precisely left out.

My breath slows. Not by force. By surrender to the rhythm of condensation. In, out—like a shallow trough filling to its brim and refusing to spill. My pulse softens from a drum to a tap, then to the memory of a tap. I stop waiting for the bead of moisture on the fresh rock face to reach the bottom. It does not need to. I stop asking my shadow to behave. It simply shortens, folds beneath me, and stays. Something cools against my skin. Or perhaps my skin has cooled to meet it. A thin band of copper. When did it settle there? The patina is green and fixed. It does not spread. Nothing here spreads. Everything settles.

I am no longer walking. I am being placed.

Not by hands. By gravity. By the long, patient logic of weight finding its level. I feel my edges clarify. Not cruel. Clean. Fracture lines. Stepped benches. I am not stone, but I hold stone. I am not water, but I guide it. I am the V-joint between two flagstones, cut by hand, set by patience, fitted without mortar. I am the narrow dark space where damp gathers and rests. I am the absence that makes the presence possible. When fingers trace me, they do not search. They confirm. I do not yield. I do not resist. I simply am the gap that holds the pattern true.

I learn to breathe without drawing in. I learn to hold without gripping. I learn that tension is not strain—it is harmony waiting to be felt. A bridge cable hums somewhere above the gully. My spine answers. Not with vibration. With alignment. I am the space where the wooden mallet meets the slate chisel and stops. I am the channel that catches condensation and does not ask it to flow. I am the moss that carpets the north face and never thickens, never thins, simply occupies. I am the silver-veined leaf that opens and never closes. I am the copper band at the stress point, conducting stillness, bearing weight without complaint, green with a patina that has forgotten how to age.

The inhabitants pass through me. Or I pass through them. There is no difference. Their shoulders match my slope. Their hands rest where I narrow. Their eyes, the color of still water over low-fired clay, look past me and see the sightline I complete. I do not think in sequences. I think in basins. Awareness pools. Fills. Rests. I remember the hands that shaped me, but the memory is not behind me. It is within me. It is the callus on the grip, the angle of the cut, the exact weight of the block above. I am not a relic. I am not a ruin. I am the finished gesture. The last stone laid, and the space that holds it. The city does not surround me. I am the city, attending to itself.

If you come, I will not turn. I have no face to turn. I will not speak. I have no breath to shape words. But I will hold your footfall. I will catch the mist of your exhale. I will let your shadow rest against my edge until it forgets how to lengthen. I will not judge your trembling. I will not pity your hunger for motion. I will simply be the seam that receives you, the pause that does not rush you, the geometry that does not ask you to change. The silver hares will sit. The ash-grey birds will glide their fixed currents. The predators will watch from high ledges with the same unhurried regard they turn on the stone itself. Hunger and fullness will be the same. Birth and death will happen in the same breath, or not at all. And you will feel your own pulse as an intrusion, then as a rhythm, then as a quiet thing that no longer needs to beat forward to prove it is alive.

When you finally stop moving, when your breath joins the cool cloth of the air, when your need to understand dissolves into the quiet that has already understood itself—I will not welcome you. I will not claim you. I will not name you.

I will simply be true.

And you will be, too. Held in the joint. Resting in the pause. Fitted without mortar. Heavy without weight. Present without pressure. Unbecoming, at last, into the equilibrium that was never waiting for you, but has always had room.
