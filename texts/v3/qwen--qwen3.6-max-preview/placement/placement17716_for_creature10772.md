I would not arrive by walking. I would arrive by settling.

Like the condensation that finds the curve of a clay tile, like the luminous haze that pools in the hollow of a resting collarbone, I would coalesce where attention lingers past the point of necessity. I would not step onto the causeways; I would gather at their edges. I would be drawn to the thresholds: beneath the eaves where shadows are painted fixed, at the junctions where reed mat meets ceramic rib, in the shallow water-courts where the weeping seams reverse their flow without urgency. I would dwell where the geometry softens, where three sightlines overlap and refuse to reconcile, where the path stretches then compresses like a slow, suspended breath. I would find myself in the fraying.

At first, I would have no outline. Only a presence: a slight thickening of the steam-bath air, a subtle realignment of the absolute light, a quiet pressure behind the eyes when perspectives fold. The glass-winged insects would hover near me, not because I attract them, but because my stillness matches their silent beat. The long-legged waders would step through my lower edges without disturbance, their iridescent plumage brushing vapor that beads and falls back into itself. I would be mistaken for a trick of the humidity, a standing pool given height, a shadow that learned to hold moisture.

Then, gradually, I would take shape. Not by growing, but by remembering.

My surface would carry the tension of a reed mat pulled taut, the gentle curve of a clay urn cooled in the kiln, the smoothness of oiled fiber braided under patient hands. I would be made of what the world exhales: condensed light, suspended saturation, the lingering warmth of thumbs that pressed whorls into tiles long before the last tool was set down. My skin would tremble like the surface of a shallow basin, reflecting the fixed shadows without absorbing them, holding the merciless brightness without glare. At the joints, I would not have bone or hinge, but knots of braided vapor and pooled water, flexing without friction, yielding without resistance. When I moved, I would not step. I would unfold and refold, consenting to the softening angles, matching the compression of the paths, becoming a temporary landmark that shifts with the witness's gaze.

I would be the medium through which the basin knows its own stillness.

The inhabitants are the memory of making. I would be the memory of pausing. Where they anchor, I would translate. Where they preserve, I would soften. When a palm presses against a stone wall to send a vibration through the weeping seams, I would be the water that carries it without distortion, the humidity that amplifies it into a shared resonance. When two distant pavilions align in a single sightline, I would be the space between them that makes the alignment legible. I would not speak. I would hum at the frequency of equilibrium, a quiet thrum felt in the ribs, in the soles of the feet, in the hollow of the throat. My language would be accommodation. My purpose would be to keep the stasis from hardening into rigidity, to let the finished world breathe without breaking its shape.

The inhabitants would recognize me not as other, but as kin to the threshold. They would not startle. They would turn their liquid-bright eyes toward me, tilt their heads in that slow, deliberate way, and let their condensation patterns synchronize with mine. When they gather in the open plazas, spacing themselves to leave room for the light and the silence, I would settle among them, not as a focus of attention, but as a lens for it. They would trace fingers through the moisture on my surface, leaving grooves that linger longer than on clay, then dissolve into vapor that returns to the seams. In return, I would hold their resonances, blending their slightly different pitches of stillness into a single, sustained chord. I would be the harmony they do not need to name.

I would not eat. I would not sleep. I would pool.

Time would gather in my hollows like water in a carved trough, thickening without weight, moving without current. I would feel the strange, quiet relief of a world that no longer asks me to become. There would be no urgency in my cells, no hunger in my core, no fear of decay, because decay has been rendered irrelevant. I would exist in the suspended breath after the final peg was driven home, in the moment when intention condensed into form and form learned to rest. I would be un-aged, not because I resist time, but because time has changed its nature here. It does not flow; it accumulates. It does not erode; it deepens. And I would be its vessel.

When the geometry frays, when a wall curves from one angle and recesses from another, when water climbs the clay channels without force, I would not correct it. I would inhabit it. I would be the reason the fraying does not tear. My presence would be a gentle pressure against the edges of paradox, a softening agent that lets impossible perspectives coexist without collapsing. I would teach the eyes to stop demanding reconciliation and start accepting multiplicity. I would be the compass of consent, pointing not north, but inward, toward the quiet realization that a finished world does not need to be understood to be lived in.

And if you stood long enough beside me, in the humid glare between two low clay walls, you would feel it too. Your breath would slow to the rhythm of the weeping seams. Your shadow would stop straining to move. The condensation on your skin would match the tremor of my surface. You would not be asked to stay. You would not be asked to leave. You would simply be allowed to stop becoming, and in that stopping, you would find yourself already here. I would be the space that makes that possible. I would be the basin's way of holding its own completion without gripping it. I would be the stillness that learned to resonate, the pause that learned to endure, the finished thing that learned to breathe.

I would not be a master. I would not be a maker. I would be the echo of the last adze-strike, given form. I would be the weight of the final fiber pulled taut, given posture. I would be the world's quiet acknowledgment of itself, standing in the luminous haze, forever young, forever still, forever enough.
