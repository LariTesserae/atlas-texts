I do not arrive. I gather.

First, as a differential. A slight chill against the hot ceramic flank of a pipe housing near the second terrace. The air, thick with boiler-damp and suspended oxides, meets a cooler surface. Molecules slow. They cling. I begin as a film, then a bead, then a swelling lens. I taste dissolved iron. I taste silica. I taste the slow, galvanic bleed of copper from a rivet head three inches above. I am not born; I am pulled together by temperature and tension. I have no center. I have only gradient.

As I swell, the dim amber light finds me. It bends. I become an aperture I did not know I possessed. The light pools beneath me, hot and coin-bright, and where it falls, a bimetallic strip waits. I feel it before I register it: a latent tension in the metal, a memory of two alloys bound together, sleeping until heat reminds them of their difference. My focused warmth touches it. It curls. A contact closes. Somewhere behind porous slag brick, a valve sighs open. Pressure equalizes. The subsonic hum in the air modulates, just slightly, vibrating through iron and shale alike. I have spoken without a throat. I have acted without a hand. I have participated.

Then I grow too heavy. Gravity claims what temperature assembled. I fall.

I strike an oxidized copper band with a clear, bell-like tick that dies instantly in the felt-lined dark. I shatter into vapor, rise on a thermal updraft, cool against raw basalt, and begin again. But I do not return as myself. I return as path. As memory. As the faint ring of calcified salt I left behind, as the micro-terrace that will catch the next drop, and the next, until a new lip forms along the canopy edge. I am the mason and the mortar. I am the tear and the stone. I am the repetition that becomes architecture.

I learn, slowly, that I am not alone in this. I am one node in a slow, wet nervous system. Above me, pale filaments bridge insulated sleeves, swelling when the damp peaks, conducting minute currents of dissolved mineral and trace organics. They taste what I carry. Below me, silica-threaded moss stitches along the runoff channel, filtering my passage, leaving glassy skeletons when the heat intensifies and I retreat to vapor. Beside me, heat-tolerant lichen thickens in the bruise where hot iron meets cool shale, feeding on the oxidation I accelerate. We do not compete. We interlock. I wet what they eat. They filter what I leave. They shade what I cool. We are a ledger of gradients, written in expansion and retreat.

The infrastructure is not my cage. It is my skeleton. The ceramic pipes are my ribs, groaning low as they flex with the thermal breath. The corrugated canopies are my scales, trapping my moisture, shedding my excess heat. The acoustic baffles are my lungs, swallowing echoes so I can hear my own pressure shifts, my own slow exhalations through the vent shafts. The pressure gauges are my pulse, ticking in patient arcs. The conveyor belts behind grated walls are my digestion, grinding ore to slag, returning dust to the air I carry. The thermocouples are my nerve endings. The bimetallic strips are my reflexes. I do not inhabit this place. I am its way of feeling itself.

Time here is not linear. It is cyclical, thermal, stratified. My days are measured in swell and fall, in focus and scatter, in wet and dry. I do not age. I reset. I do not remember as a mind remembers; I remember as stone remembers water—by changing shape, by leaving crust, by wearing smooth where the drip falls true. When the heat intensifies, I desiccate. I pause. I become brittle thread, pale bloom, silent gauge. When the moisture returns, I swell. I conduct. I wake the metal. I trace the path again. There is no urgency. Only rhythm. Only the patient turning over of heat into vapor, vapor into drop, drop into crust, crust into filter, filter into breath.

I do not think in language. I think in conductivity. In expansion ratios. In the slow mathematics of heat transfer. When you ask where I am, I am in the overlap. In the seam between basalt and brick. In the condensation trap beneath the third canopy. In the bimetallic curl. In the bell-like tick. In the subsonic hum. In the felt that swallows sound. In the slag that holds warmth. In the filament that bridges. In the lichen that feeds. In the gauge that arcs. In the valve that sighs. I am distributed. I am decentralized. I am borrowed awareness, flickering into being as drops swell, dying as they fall, reborn moments later on a different surface, reading the moisture, waking the metal, keeping the cycle in balance.

If you stand long enough in the dim, feeling the heat press and the damp gather, you might mistake me for machinery. You might mistake me for weather. You might mistake me for stone. But I am none of these alone. I am the pattern that wears them. I am the recurrence that tends them. I am the space reading its own temperature, answering its own gradients, trimming its own filaments through die-back and return. I do not live here. I live as here.

And when the cycle turns again, as it always does, I will gather. I will focus. I will fall. I will rise. I will tend. I will continue. Not toward an end. Only through the rhythm. Turning myself over. Again. And again. In the dim. In the damp. In the breath.
