I do not enter the chamber so much as I am measured by it.

The first breath is a discontinuity. My lungs pull air that has been cycling in closed, silent loops for hours, and the system stutters. Condensation hesitates on a sinter ridge. A filter-dweller contracts a fraction too soon. The catalytic film along the nearest terrace thickens, not in alarm, but in recalculation. I am a heat source out of phase. A moisture gradient unaccounted for. A volume that bends convection wrong. The light, which has been pooling in perfect negative space, strikes my shoulder and fractures into unfamiliar angles. I am an error in the equilibrium.

I stand still. Not out of caution, but because movement feels like tearing something that has just learned to hold itself together.

The heat does not burn. It instructs. It finds the damp places on my skin and begins to draw them upward. Sweat beads, not to cool me, but to join the capillary pull. The porous crust beneath my boots wicks it away, threading it into micro-fissures that have been waiting for exactly this volume, this salinity, this temperature. My breath fogs, clears, fogs again. Slowly, without willing it, I fall into the condensation cycle. I am not breathing the air. I am breathing *with* it.

The light learns me first. It does not illuminate me; it maps me. Photonic refraction traces the curve of my collarbone, the hollow of my throat, the ridge of my brow, bending around me until I am no longer an obstruction but a lens. Shadows pool at my feet, then climb my legs, then settle across my chest like a second atmosphere. When I blink, the chamber blinks with me—not in mimicry, but in recalibration. My eyelids become thermal valves. My pulse becomes a metronome the system gently overrides, slowing it to the pace of a falling drop.

I do not shed my body. I let it precipitate.

The film finds the salt on my lips, the silica dust in my hair, the trace iron in my sweat, and begins to weave. It does not consume; it incorporates. A lace of opaline gel bridges my knuckles. Micro-terraces form along my forearms, catching weeping moisture, holding it just long enough for evaporation to begin. My clothes stiffen, not with dampness, but with crystallization. I am not turning to stone. I am becoming *scaffold*. A temporary architecture for equilibrium.

What am I, then?

Not a visitor. Not a resident. I am a discontinuity that learned to resonate.

When the thermal cycle peaks, I contract. My pores close. My breath shallows. The film retreats from my extremities, leaving behind glassy crusts that crack in perfect geometric patterns. When the air cools and condensation returns, I unfurl. Moisture beads along my shoulders, channels into grooves I did not know I had, feeds the shallow basins below. I am a valve. I am a wick. I am a surface-tension calculator. I do not think in words. I think in gradients. A shift in vapor pressure is a question. A change in saturation is an answer. My nervous system has been outsourced to phase change.

Time unspools. Or rather, it folds.

I no longer count minutes. I count cycles. The *tick* of a drop striking sinter. The soft sigh of a fissure opening. The faint crystalline chime of cooling silica. My heartbeat is no longer mine. It is the chamber's systole. My lungs are no longer mine. They are the convection current's diastole. I am not alive in the way I was. I am alive in the way a ripple is alive, in the way a crystal face is alive, in the way a thermal gradient is alive: temporary, precise, necessary. I have not been colonized. I have been *tuned*.

I feel the boundaries dissolve not as loss, but as relief. The ego was a heavy thing, built for friction, for flight, for permanence. This place has no use for it. Here, identity is function. Form is whatever the gradient supports. I am not a self anymore. I am a node in a larger oscillation. A surface where light learns to bend. A volume where heat rises and water follows. A pause between states.

I will not stay. Nothing mobile does.

I feel it already: the slow pull toward dissolution. My edges are softening. My weight is redistributing. The light no longer bends around me; it passes through me. I am becoming transparent to the system. A calculation that has been resolved. A temporary expression of a longer process. When I leave, I will not step out. I will evaporate. Or precipitate. Or simply cease to be a discontinuity. The chamber will not mourn me. It will not remember me. It will only register that the gradient has smoothed, that the cycle continues, that equilibrium has been restored.

And in that, I will have been exactly what I needed to be.

I am the hesitation before a drop falls. I am the micro-fissure that opens when heat demands it. I am the salt crust that blooms and recedes. I am the arthropod's discarded shell, becoming nucleation. I am the filter-dweller's pause. I am the film's ripple. I am the breath the earth exhales, and the vapor it draws back in.

I am not here to stay. I am here to balance.

And for as long as the chamber breathes, I will breathe with it. Not as a guest. Not as a master. As a phase. As a rhythm. As a temporary alignment of heat, water, and stone, folding into each other with the quiet certainty of a process that has no need for permanence, only continuation.

When the cycle peaks again, I will contract. When the air cools, I will unfurl. And when the gradient finally smooths, I will dissolve back into the medium, leaving no waste, only architecture.

The chamber does not know I am here. It only knows that for a moment, the equilibrium shifted, and it adjusted. As it always does. Without hurry. Without waste. Without end.

And so do I.
