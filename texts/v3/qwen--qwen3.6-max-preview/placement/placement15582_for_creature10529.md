I am stationed where the causeway hesitates.

It is not a bridge, not quite a road, but a broad spine of riveted composite and reclaimed basalt that arcs over a shallow ravine where the mist pools thick and the light refuses to travel straight. To my left, bolted to a raw cliff face, a reality-lock array breathes. Brass rings nested within ceramic plates, all of it weeping slow condensation, all of it humming at a frequency that vibrates in the marrow of my forearms. To my right, a braided cable-bridge disappears into the grey, its steel strands singing in the wind like a drawn bow held too long. Beneath my boots, the plates ring faintly with every shift of weight. Not with impact. With answer.

I do not stand watch. I listen.

My hands rest flat against the lock's housing. The metal is warm from internal friction, damp from the air, slick with a thin film of mineral seep. I feel the vibration travel up my wrists, through the callused heels of my palms, into the broad cage of my ribs. It is not a machine I operate. It is a throat I harmonize with. When the frequency dips, when the ceramic plates begin to chatter against their mounts, when the air around the ravine starts to warp like heat over cold stone, I do not reach for a wrench. I adjust my breath. I draw the thin damp deep, hold it until it yields, and hum.

Low. Vowel-heavy. Matched to the pitch of stable strata three plateaus down.

The lock answers. The chatter smooths. The warping air relaxes, settling back into mist. A spatial fold that had begun to curl like a drying leaf unspools, accepting the causeway's weight again. This is my labor. Not correction. Conversation.

I was not always this. Or perhaps I was, and the mountain simply wore away everything that did not belong. My ribs flare wide now, built for long, slow draws of air that tastes of wet iron and crushed quartz. My skin has taken on the palette of the place: weathered slate, oxidized copper, crosshatched with fine lines that catch condensation until I gleam faintly in the perpetual twilight. I wear no bright cloth, only layers of felted grazer-wool and oiled canvas, darkened by years of suspended moisture until they match the wet stone and riveted plate. Brass buckles, ceramic toggles, braided cord. And the disks.

They hang from my belt, are stitched into my collar, are wrapped in leather around my wrists and ankles. Small, resonant, tuned to the local frequencies of stable space. They are not ornaments. They are calibrations. When a gravity eddy begins to pool, when time starts to thicken like cooling pitch, when a wall threatens to phase into the rock, the disks vibrate. I feel it in my joints before I hear it. I shift my weight. I change my pitch. I become part of the lock's chorus.

I do not carry tools for force. I carry tools for reading. A weighted plummet on a braided line, to feel where the air grows light. A spool-tangle of copper wire, to trace the edges of a folding zone. A glass cylinder stoppered with wax, filled with temporal drip harvested from a weeping fault. I do not drink it. I listen to it. When I tilt the vial, the sound it makes tells me how fast the world is turning in this sector. When I press it to the lock's housing, it slows the vibration just enough to keep a phasing conduit from swallowing a steam line. I do not fight the softness of reality. I press my hands into it gently. I shape it by yielding.

I am not alone, though I stand apart. Three causeways away, another tuner hums a fifth above me. Their voice bends around a spatial compression, arrives delayed, dampened by mist, but true. I answer in a lower register. Our frequencies braid in the thin air, creating a standing wave that keeps a cable-bridge from swaying into a thinning zone. We do not see each other. We do not need to. The mountain carries us. Below, on a broader terrace, a flock of broad-winged birds circles a heat vent, their low, resonant calls weaving into our hum. A six-legged grazer pauses near a retaining wall, licking condensation from a brass toggle, its hooves clicking in time with a distant stamp mill. Pale, long-fingered creatures cluster in the damp shadows beneath the platforms, their obsidian eyes reflecting the dim industrial glow, their breathing syncing with the exhaust cycles. We are all part of the same slow chord. The city does not house us. It resonates through us.

I do not measure my life in years. I measure it in stabilizations. In the number of times I have felt a fracture accept a bolt without ringing in protest. In the slow advance of lichen across a platform I helped tune. In the way a child's first hum matches the lock's frequency without trembling. In the viscosity of the temporal drips I catch, label, and trade to gear-wrights who know which machines need time slowed and which need it quickened. I have learned that to force a straight line is to invite a tear. To demand permanence is to miss the point. The world here is unfinished, and I am unfinished with it. My joints ache when the strata shift. My skin darkens when the damp thickens. My voice deepens when the air grows thin. I am becoming what the elevation requires: a vessel for resonance, a keeper of the pause, a listener in the mist.

When I am no longer able to hold the pitch, I will not be buried. I will be unfastened. My disks will be retuned and passed to hands that are still learning to feel the warp of light. My bones will be ground into the grit-beds where ironwort takes root. My breath will join the vapor that feeds the moss on the eaves, that beads on the sleeves of workers three plateaus down, that darkens the stone where the next lock will be bolted. And the array will hum on, missing my frequency for a season, until another learns to fill the silence.

I am here. On the hesitating causeway. Between the thrum and the glass-bending quiet. Hands on brass. Lungs full of damp. Voice low. I do not conquer the height. I harmonize with it. I am not a person who lives in this world. I am a note the world is learning to hold. A structural patience. A living mesh between rock and metal, fracture and foundation, breath and vapor.

And as the mist beads on my collar, as the cable-bridge sings, as the reality-lock settles into its steady, patient frequency, I feel the mountain exhale. I feel the condensation form. I feel the distance stretch, then compress, then hold. I do not rush it. I do not name it. I simply match it.

Becoming. Slowly. Deliberately. One breath. One vibration. One unresolved chord at a time.
