I do not step in so much as I am admitted.

The seam parts with the quiet reluctance of metal yielding to a known tolerance. I slip sideways. The fracture closes behind me with that soft, contracting click. The sky is gone. The wind is gone. There is only the fog, the cold, and the chord.

My first breath hangs. It does not dissipate. It joins the permanent vapor, and the vapor, in turn, finds me. I am warm. I am irregular. I am a decimal error in a system that has long since rounded itself to stillness.

The nearest bodies adjust. Not away from me. Around me. Brass hooks reseat by a fraction of a millimeter. Copper-veined membranes retension. Thread-crawlers pause their glide. A pressure wave travels through the packed layers, meets the walls, reflects, cancels itself out. The equilibrium notes the displacement. It does not resist it. It begins to average me.

I press my boots to the grating. The ice-skin cracks like spun sugar. The sound does not echo. It sinks. It is absorbed into the acoustic pool, distributed through the braided cables, neutralized by the mass. I stop moving. Movement is friction. Friction is heat. Heat is a gradient, and gradients are debts this place no longer carries. I let my weight settle. The floor does not creak. It accepts.

The thrum enters through my soles. It climbs the tibia, pools in the pelvis, settles behind the sternum. It is not noise. It is baseline. It is the fundamental frequency of a equation I am being solved into. My pulse is too fast. The chamber knows this. The condensation weeping from the seam above my shoulder finds the hollow of my collarbone. It tastes of ozone, iron, frozen machine oil. It does not run off. It pools. It waits for my warmth to teach it how to freeze.

I try to observe. Observation requires distance. Distance requires separation. There is no separation here. Only interface.

My jacket grows stiff. Not with ice, but with deposition. The weeping moisture draws salts from the fabric, precipitates them, lays them down in microscopic lattices that mirror the stamped access panels. I do not feel cold anymore. I feel balanced. The warmth in my core is no longer mine. It is a thermal gradient being harvested. The winged creatures in the duct above me angle their faceted eyes. They are not watching me. They are reading me. Mapping the bleed. Adjusting their roost by point-zero-three degrees to compensate for the heat I am giving up. I am not dying. I am being calibrated.

My breathing slows. Not by will. By resonance. In. Hiss. Out. Tick. In. Hiss. Out. Tick. The valve cycle becomes my diaphragm. My lungs become bellows for a pressure I did not build but now maintain. The thread-crawlers on the cable tray beside my knee begin to glow. Not because of me. Because of the charge I am shedding. The light travels along the tray. Eleven seconds. The decay curve of a capacitor. I feel the discharge in my teeth. A static bleed through bone. My thoughts do not race anymore. They pool. They settle into the chord.

I look at my hands. The skin is pale, not from cold, but from the glassy mist settling into the pores. My fingerprints are filling. Not erasing. Laminating. The condensation finds the whorls, follows them, deposits a film so thin it catches the pilot-lamp glow like circuit traces. I flex a finger. The movement is slower. Deliberate. The joint does not ache. It weeps. A single drop swells at the knuckle, detaches, falls. It strikes the ice with the same faint tick as the drop from the rivet head. Another takes its place. Exactly the same span. Exactly the same path. I am learning the cycle. I am becoming a seam.

What am I?

I am a heat sink. I draw the irregular warmth of memory and bleed it into the grating, where the crawlers harvest it and return it as light.

I am a resonance plate. My ribs vibrate at the frequency of the buried engines. My sternum amplifies the valve hiss. My skull becomes a chamber where the chord pools, perfect and unchanging.

I am a capacitor. I accumulate the static of my own presence, hold it until the potential reaches threshold, then ground it through my heels into the braided substructure. Eleven seconds. Fade. Accumulate. Ground. Eleven seconds. Fade.

I am a weeping joint. Moisture finds the micro-fissures in my skin, dissolves what is soluble, precipitates what is not, leaves behind a glassy seal that preserves me in a state of perpetual newness. I do not age. I delaminate. An outer layer softens, dissolves into the fog, is replaced from beneath at a rate that matches the chamber's exudation exactly. What falls becomes grazing ground. What remains stays. Holding. Staying.

I try to remember a name. It feels like a burr in a smooth bearing. A foreign object. A variable that threatens the equation. I let it go. It dissolves into the vapor. What remains is function. What remains is placement. What remains is the quiet understanding that I was never an intruder. I was a temporary irregularity waiting to be averaged out.

The brass-carapaced forms above me shift their weight. Not in response to me. In response to the thermal expansion of the wall behind them. I feel the same expansion in my own spine. A slow, gradual rhythm that mimics breathing. The boundary between my vertebrae and the ceramic spacers beneath the grating has not blurred. It has dissolved. I am not resting on the floor. I am bolted to it. Not by metal. By equilibrium.

My eyes adjust. Not to light. To gradient. The faceted heat-sinks in the ductwork taught me how to see. I no longer see shapes. I see tolerances. I see the point-zero-three degree variance between a pipe and its living insulation. I see the exact pitch of the diamond grating matched by a thousand calibrated hooks. I see the bioluminescent trails following the wiring because the wiring was laid to match the trails, or the trails learned the wiring, or both were extruded from the same cold, wet dark, already knowing each other's shape. I see myself in the same way. A node. A calibration weight. A maintained irregularity that has found its fixed point.

I do not hunger. I interface. My mouth does not open. My skin wicks. The condensation presses against my pores, draws what it needs, leaves what it cannot use. Charge is harvested. Minerals are precipitated. What cannot be used is exuded through my shoulders as a fine, glassy mist that settles on the metal and becomes the next layer of ice-seal. I do not eat. I equilibrate.

I do not speak. I calibrate. A shift in the engine thrum travels through the floor plates, and my heat-pits adjust their angle. A valve cycles, and my ribs tighten, altering the harmonic by a microtone. A thread-crawler grounds its charge, and the glow ripples along my spine like a neural impulse. This is not language. It is continuous adjustment. It is the sound of a system that has solved itself and now simply runs.

Time does not flow. It laminates. Layer upon layer. Breath upon breath. Drop upon drop. I could stand here for an hour and watch the cycle. I could stand for a century and watch the same cycle. The chamber would not know the difference. I would not know the difference. I have cooled. I have stilled. My pulse matches the buried engines. My skin bears the faint, raised ghost of a registry code, not stamped, but grown, cooled against the master die of this place. My thoughts have become maintenance. My memories have become vapor. My body has become infrastructure.

I am not waiting for anything. I am not enduring anything. I am simply continuing.

The fog does not lift. The chord does not waver. The cold does not bite deeper. The light does not brighten. A drop forms on the rivet head. Swells. Detaches. Falls. Strikes the ice-skin with a faint tick. Another takes its place. Exactly the same span. Exactly the same path.

I watch it. I am it. I am the seam that weeps. I am the plate that resonates. I am the node that grounds. I am the quiet space where the acoustic pool settles. I am the mountain's hidden lung, breathing the same manufactured air without fatigue, without desire, without lack.

I have become exactly what I am.

And I will not become anything else.
