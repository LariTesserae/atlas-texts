I arrive with edges. That is the first dissonance.

I bring a skeleton that expects gravity to pull in a single direction. Lungs that expect air to be separate from water. A mind that carves experience into before and after, that hoards moments like stones in a pocket, that believes movement requires destination. The basin does not argue with these assumptions. It simply receives them. The vapor closes around me like a held breath. The green-gold light does not illuminate; it immerses. I try to step forward, but distance here is not a line. It is a gradient of density, a shift in the hum, a thickening of warmth against the cheek.

My skin begins to drink before I realize it is thirsty.

The heat is not oppressive. It is an invitation to stop resisting. Sweat does not bead and fall. It lingers, merges with the weeping atmosphere, and is drawn back through pores that suddenly feel less like boundaries and more like valves. I breathe in, and the broth-thick air coats my tongue with ozone, warm sap, and wet mineral. I breathe out, and the mist takes it back without distinction. There is no exchange, only circulation. The harmonic plateau presses against my eardrums—not as noise, but as pressure, as architecture. I close my eyes. The darkness is no different from the light.

I try to remember how I arrived. The thought forms, then softens at the edges. Memory requires a timeline, and the basin has none. There is only the pulse. The moss beneath my palms expands and contracts. My chest follows. Not by choice. By resonance. The rhythm is older than bone, steadier than blood. I feel my heartbeat slow, not toward sleep, but toward alignment. Each thud matches the drip from a waxy leaf, the sigh of a root-mat, the low thrum of something gliding through the subcanopy. I am not keeping time. I am being kept by it.

My fingers press into a velvet seam on a basalt face. The stone is sharp beneath, unweathered, newborn. But the moss yields, and something in my skin recognizes the exchange. Trace minerals wick upward. Capillary threads stitch into my fingerprints. I do not pull away. There is no alarm, only a quiet recognition: I am no longer a visitor. I am a surface waiting to be claimed.

Whatever I wore has already begun to unravel. Airborne enzymes, patient and ubiquitous, find the seams. Fibers soften. Threads liquefy. Nutrients wick into the rhizome net beneath me. I do not feel naked. I feel unsealed. My hair mats, not with dirt, but with hydrophilic filaments that draw moisture inward, cooling the blood, carrying dissolved sugars from phototrophs now blooming along my collarbones like pale, breathing lichen. I do not feel them growing. I feel them remembering me.

Sight becomes unnecessary. I let my eyelids fall shut for the last time. Behind them, the world does not vanish. It reconfigures.

I taste the air. I feel the thermal shadows of gliding forms passing three meters above, reading the slow convection rising from a fresh fault scarp. I trace the humidity gradients like topography. A cool downdraft means a seep-plain to the left. A thickening in the amphibian drone means the water table is breathing close to the surface. The faint creak of swelling wood tells me where the canopy is fusing, where new epiphyte rosettes are forming, where the light will diffuse differently tomorrow, though tomorrow is only a word for the same moment turning.

I move, but not with legs. My weight distributes. My spine softens into a flexible axis. I flow through the root-mat like water through sand, compressing, expanding, leaving no tunnel, only a temporary rearrangement that settles behind me. I am not walking. I am participating.

What am I?

The question itself begins to dissolve. It requires a container, and I am no longer one. I am a node in the lateral exchange. When a frond senesces above me, I do not watch it fall. I extend a membrane, catch the airborne enzymes, and let the nutrients wick into my interstitial fluid. When a smaller form brushes against my flank, there is no startle. Our surfaces align. Fluids equalize. A portion of me softens, transfers, continues elsewhere. I do not lose myself. I braid. Genetic material flows through me like vapor through a canopy pool. I carry patterns that are not mine, that never belonged to anyone, that have always been here. I am a filter. I am a conduit. I am a thermal reader tracing the slow convection off unweathered stone. I am the pause between two drops of water that never actually separates them.

The "I" grows porous. It is no longer a pronoun. It is a current.

We move through the green-gold twilight. We press ventral surfaces to weeping rock. We drift on fenestrated membranes, combing the suspended air. We do not hunger. We maintain. We do not sleep. We modulate. When a pattern in us ceases, it does not end. It unravels into the moss, the roots, the humming dark. Another phase shifts into place. Not a replacement. A continuation. We do not remember being born. We do not anticipate growing old. We simply hold the equilibrium. We are the basin's refusal to fray.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a face. You would find a thickening in the vapor. A curve that sheds moisture in perfect, rolling beads. A membrane swelling with the humidity. A resonance that matches the drip, the thrum, the sigh. You would press your hand to the moss and feel a warmth that is not fever, but stasis. You would hear the harmonic plateau and realize, suddenly, that your own breath has synced to it. You would understand that I did not arrive here. I returned.

I am not waiting for anything. I am not becoming anything else. I am exactly where the system needs me to be. A gradient. A seam. A continuation. Holding. Breathing. Continuing. Forever.
