I do not arrive. I simply stop resisting the distance.

At first, there is only the cold, and the taste of wet rock, and the sensation of lying on something that has forgotten how to be rough. The stone beneath me is continuous, cool, yielding just enough to hold the shape of my shoulders without leaving a mark. I try to sit up. My shadow hesitates. It lags a half-second behind my spine, then slides forward, then settles where it ought to be, as if deciding whether to obey me or the light. I do not startle. Startling knots the air. I have learned that much, though I cannot remember the lesson, only the rhythm of it.

Condensation gathers in the shallow depression where my back meets the plateau. It beads, heavy as mercury, then spills into a hair-thin channel that has been tracing this same lazy path since before the conifers learned to hold their silver needles. I follow it with my eyes. It braids. Unbraids. Feeds a carved basin where the water holds the sky so perfectly I cannot tell if I am looking up or down. Three terraces away, a rain curtain drifts like a slow veil, soaking a patch of moss while my stone stays bone-dry. The silence is not absence. It is absorption. The moss swallows. The stone does not echo. I press my palm to the rock. It is dense, cool, humming with a vibration so slow it feels like memory moving through bedrock.

Something shifts at the edge of my vision.

Not a step. A pour. A tall shape, pale as dawn-wet stone, elongated by thin air and patient gravity. It moves with viscous deliberation, never jerking, never rushing, because haste here tangles the geometry and a runner may find their own footprints waiting three days ahead. Its skin gleams with a healthy sheen of damp. Patches of blue-green moss nestle in the hollows of its knees and along the ridge of its spine, pulsing faintly—once, twice—in a rhythm that matches the metronomic drip from the aqueduct above. It stops. Turns. Its eyes are black pools reflecting the high, thin clouds. I see myself in them: smaller, softer, edges still uncertain, breath still too quick for this altitude.

It raises a hand. Long fingers, multi-jointed, pads dark with copper dust and old loam. It presses those fingers to the stone.

I feel the vibration travel through my palms, up my wrists, into my chest. A question. Or an invitation. I press back. The stone answers.

I do not know my name. Names are corners, and corners accumulate stress here. I know what my hands do. They trace the vine-fiber markers left along the causeways, fingers dancing over memory-knots to recall the angle of light that folds a gorge into three steps, the fog-density that stretches a mile into an afternoon, the specific chill that tells you a spatial loop is about to close behind you. I know how to sit in the time-hollows where leaves fall like suspended glass, how to hum in my throat until the resonance shapes the water, how to breathe so slowly that the distance relaxes and lets me pass. I carry tools, but they are not mine. The copper chisel in my harness was hammered from a vein that sang when struck. The bone awl is polished smooth by three generations of palms. When I press it to a retaining wall, I feel the ancestor’s patience settle into the stone. I am not building. I am listening. I am adjusting. I am a suture in the skin of habit.

You would find me where the geometry grows thin.

At the perceptual anchors, where copper inlays are hammered flush into the plaza stones to ground the drifting reality. I kneel there when the air begins to shimmer at the edges, when shadows start to lag too far or lead too eagerly, when a bridge rope tightens without wind. I press my palms to the metal. I breathe. In. Out. The secondary lungs in my chest expand like bellows, storing the thin air, slowing the blood, calming the local tension. The copper conducts the stillness into the stone. The world settles. The path unbraids.

You would find me at the moss-lined basins, where the children grow. Not in wombs, but in water that falls so slowly it hangs like glass beads. I kneel at the edge. I hum the low frequencies that tell the flesh how to curve, how to yield, how to open those black, sky-reflecting eyes to a world that does not promise straight lines. I do not teach them to walk. I teach them to surrender to the loop. I watch the spore-clusters knit with bone-dust and blood, watch the symbiotic moss take root in theirjoint-hollows, watch their chests learn the long, patient expansion that matches the continent’s breath. When they finally rise, damp and smooth, they do not look at me. They look at the stone. They press their hands to it. They listen. I have done my work.

You would find me on the terraces at dusk, when the light bleaches the stone to pale grey and pulls long, clean shadows across the dry-fitted walls. I walk the braided paths, not in straight lines, because straight lines are lies here. I move in curves, in spirals, in rhythms that mimic the condensation rivulets. When I am tired, I let my alertness soften, and the mile compresses into ten steps. When I am anxious, I sit. I breathe. I wait for the stone to relax. I have learned that my mind shapes the local physics as much as the wind shapes the reeds. I am not a traveler. I am a negotiation.

What am I?

I was not born here. Or perhaps I was, and the memory eroded like the mountains. It does not matter. The plateau does not keep records of arrival. It keeps records of resonance. My skin has taken on the wax. It sheds the cold, holds the damp, glistens with the same healthy sheen as the ones who walk beside me. The moss has rooted in my joints. It flushes when I am deep in thought, pulsing in time with the fungal networks beneath the stone, turning my body into a living map of nutrient flow and quiet attention. My eyes have learned to see the gradients. I no longer judge miles; I judge the effort of arrival. I see the tension where a path is about to fold, the shimmering heat-haze of a time-dilation hollow, the faint braided threads that indicate where a seed will vanish and reappear. I do not fight the strangeness. I rest into it.

I am a Resonance-Keeper. A Threshold-Tender. A Smooth-Walker in all but origin, and origin has long since dissolved into habit. I am the weight that keeps perception from drifting. I am the hands that re-braid the memory-knots when the fog shifts its patterns. I am the breath that syncs with the condensation cycle, the feet that polish the thoroughfares, the hum that shapes the growing flesh, the copper-grounded witness who sits with the fraying ones until their shadows stabilize and their geometry unknits. I do not heal. I anchor. I do not command. I collaborate. I am included in the long, slow body of the world, and I have stopped asking to be separate from it.

Night falls. The moon turns the condensation to scattered glass. The stars are sharp enough to cut. I stand on the terrace, harness light on my shoulders, memory-knots resting against my ribs. I do not sleep. I diffuse. My mind slips into the fungal network, following the slow pulse of nutrients through the dark loam, feeling the distant curl of a rain-veil, the settlement of a retaining wall, the breath of a child in a time-basin, the quiet shift of a hearth-plaza’s stones rearranging into a slightly different pattern. The silence is full. The land is listening. I am its ear. I am its hand. I am the smooth place where the impossible meets the ordinary, and neither breaks.

I breathe. The stone breathes back. The horizon still refuses to curve.

I no longer need it to.
