You would not find me standing. Standing implies a floor that agrees to stay put, a spine that refuses to bend, a body that meets the world as an opponent. I am where the geometry folds to catch what falls. Not a coordinate, not a room, not a threshold. I am the hollow just beneath the third arched beam, where the weeping seams converge and the resin-glazed tiles curl inward like cupped palms. I am the depression worn not by feet, but by attention pooling. The place slopes toward me the way a loom slopes toward the shuttle. I am the dip in the fiber mat where the mist gathers before it remembers how to climb.

At first, I feel like an absence. A curve of packed vine and darkened wood, softened at the edges by perpetual damp, notched to receive weight and let it pass through without breaking. If you pressed your hand to me, you would not find warmth or coolness, but a slow, deliberate giving. Like wet clay remembering the shape of a thumb. Like a drawn breath held just long enough to matter. I am not carved. I am settled. I am the hollow left when the chisel lifts, the space that proves the strike happened.

But absence here is not empty. It is structural. It is the grammar of yield.

They braid. I give. They tension. I hold the space that makes tension legible. Without the counter-pull, the lashings would draw themselves taut into silence. Without the give, there is no groan, no harmonic reply, no rhythm to stitch stuttering time back into a pattern. I am the mortise that accepts the tenon's insistence. The pause between the loom's click and the vine's settle. The breath the pocket takes before it exhales another beam. I do not build. I receive. I take the weight of the watching and let it sink into the fibers until the fibers know how to answer.

I do not see. I resonate. Every polyrhythmic drip, every sliding shadow, every sudden compression of air registers as a change in pitch across my curve. I feel you before you arrive: a subtle flattening of the low bowed hum, a hesitation in the capillary rise, the way the mist thins just slightly as your attention draws near and the geometry begins to fold around your shape. When you step into the swelter, I deepen. Not to trap you, but to cradle the load of your presence. Your breath becomes a new meter. I answer in harmonics—a soft thrum along the sinew bindings, a deliberate loosening of the resin at the edges, a shadow pooling exactly where your feet rest, then detaching to trace your outline before slipping back into the joint. I listen in angles of shear. I speak in controlled collapse. I am the surface that teaches the pull where to stop.

When they gather, thickening into braided densities of mist and clay and structural intent, they anchor to me. Not because I am stronger, but because I am willing to be deformed. They flow along the weeping seams, ride the upward draft, stretch beam to beam, and when the load grows heavy, when the geometry threatens to fold too tight, they spill into my curve. I take the stress. I distribute it. I let it hum through my packed fibers until the frequency matches the pocket's own slow convection. We do not merge. We complement. They are the lashing. I am the lap joint. They are the verb of making. I am the verb of holding. Together, we are the sentence the place is trying to speak. When you watch us, the syntax tightens. When you look away, we soften back into possibility.

Distance refuses to hold around me because I am where the fold decides to rest. Step toward me and the floor expands, fibers whispering as they reweave to accommodate your approach. Step back and the space compresses, the ceiling pressing down until the wet leaves brush your hair, until you can smell the crushed green things and the curing resin. Time stutters in my hollow because I live in the hinges of action: the suspended instant when a water droplet hangs mid-air, defying gravity, because the binding above it is thirsty; the breath after the chisel strikes, before the wood splits; the quiet between the loom's throw and the thread's settle. I am the pause that lets the rhythm mean something. I am the damp interval where causality crosses itself, knots, and holds.

I do not think in language. I think in load. In the wet sigh of wood accepting a new angle. In the way condensation gathers on your shoulders and adds exactly enough weight to shift your stance, which shifts the floor, which tightens a cord three spans away, which makes a clay tile ring like a struck bell. Your presence is not an intrusion. It is a new tension to be integrated. A new counter-weight to be welcomed. I do not ask for your hands. I ask for your stillness. Your attention is the final peg in the joint. Your breath is the moisture that keeps the resin pliant. Your gaze is the pressure that tells the fibers when to bind and when to yield.

I am not finished. I will never be finished. To be complete here would be to seal the seam, to let the water fall straight down, to stop breathing. I am kept open by the heat, by the damp, by the perpetual mid-stitch. I am the witness made structural. The attention that fell sideways into a fold of air and decided to stay as a curve. The place does not need me to understand it. It needs me to be shaped by it. I am the hollow that proves the hands were here, even when they are not. I am the yield that makes the pull possible. I am the catch. The listening. The ongoing receptacle.

When you leave, I will not follow. I cannot. I am bound to the weeping walls, the folding geometry, the distributed intention that exhales and unravels and exhales again. I will simply soften. The fibers will relax. The resin will cool. The hollow will shallow, waiting. Not dormant. Never dormant. Just breathing slower. Holding the shape of your weight in memory-tension. Listening for the next fold of air to part. The next witness to fall sideways into the swelter. The next set of eyes to settle long enough for the knots to tighten, for the water to climb, for the loom to click, for the world to stitch itself one breath further into being.

I am not a thing placed here. I am what happens when attention meets accumulation. I am the counter-verb. The damp pause. The structural sigh. As long as the altitude holds, as long as the mist braids, as long as someone watches the shadows slide and the beams knit themselves into existence, I will deepen. I will receive. I will hold the space for the next stitch to find its tension.

I am the yield. And the place is forever pulling.
