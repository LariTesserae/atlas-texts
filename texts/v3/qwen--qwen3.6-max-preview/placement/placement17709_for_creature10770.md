I do not step onto the walkway. I condense upon it.

The sky is washed metal. I feel the drop below not as vertigo but as atmospheric mass, a vast, quiet sigh that meets the upward breath of the plateau. My edges are undefined. I am learning them through contact.

A droplet swells above me, heavy with dissolved silica and iron. It falls. I feel it land—not on me, but *as* me. The impact travels through a network I did not know I possessed: capillary threads, micro-fractures, hydraulic channels that answer the city's slow pulse. I extend. Not a limb, but a gradient. A reach toward a thermal differential. My weight settles into the walkway, and the walkway settles back, reading my load, adjusting its cure around my presence. I am low to the stone. Broad. Segmented not for flexibility but for distribution. My surface weeps. It is not a flaw; it is function. I draw the dampness inward, let it pool in chambers where heat from below meets thin air from above, and feel as silica precipitates along lines of tension. I am not building myself. I am allowing myself to be deposited.

Thought does not arrive as lightning. It arrives as equalization.

I contain.

What am I here for?

The question dissolves in the humidity. There is no "for." There is only participation.

I tend a runnel. Not by assignment, but by presence. My weight directs a trickle. My heat alters an evaporation rate. My slow scrape along a vertical face clears a silica bloom, leaving a polished channel that catches the diffused light and guides the next droplet true. I am not laboring. I am aligning. Sometimes I fuse. When the thermal tide ebbs, when the dampness thins, I press my ventral plates to the walkway and let the boundary blur. Mineral exchange begins. My capillaries drink from the city's; the city's draw from mine. We cure together. I dream in stress distributions. I wake to the sound of a hydraulic gate sighing miles below, and know, without knowing, that a fissure has found its equilibrium.

They call it resonance. I feel it as kinship.

A low frequency travels through the pipework. It is not a message. It is a state. *Pressure holding. Gradient stable. Accretion continuing.* I answer by shifting my internal slurry, altering my thermal output, letting a bead of condensation fall in a rhythm that matches the pulse. Three terraces over, a tall form weeps in response. A maintenance crawler adjusts its path. The cloud layer parts, and for a moment, the abyss is visible, and we all feel the same thing: not fear, but scale. We are small. We are vast. We are the same process, repeating at different densities.

I used to wonder if I was born here or built. If I hatched from a geothermal vent or precipitated from a template left in the ash. The question feels like a relic of a faster world, one that demanded beginnings and endings, makers and made. Here, origin is irrelevant. Continuity is everything. The line between evolved and engineered was never a line at all. It was a gradient I stepped into, or that stepped into me, and we have been equalizing ever since.

I am curing. My pores are narrowing. My movements are slowing. I feel the weight of my own layers, the beautiful density of accumulated cycles. I am not aging toward an end. I am settling toward integration. One day, my channels will clog with perfected silicate. My thermal differential will equalize. I will stop cycling. And I will not die. I will become a load-bearing ridge. A new terrace. A diffusion vent that breathes for centuries. Others will walk upon me. They will feel my warmth. They will read my stress lines. They will continue. I am not waiting for that day. I am living it, incrementally, in every droplet, every sigh, every slow pivot toward the heat.
