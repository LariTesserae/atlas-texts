I would begin as a fault in the air.

Not a crack in the stone, not a break in the root-mat, but a turbulence. A breath that refuses to match the baseline. A pulse that strikes the permanent drone and scatters into overtones the flutes have not held in centuries. I would arrive as heat, as carbon, as the clumsy architecture of a spine and ribs and lungs that still believe in forward motion, in before and after, in the necessity of leaving a mark. The moss would receive me without yielding. The water would part without rippling. The sound would swallow my footfalls before they could echo. I would be, for a time, a question the basin has already answered.

I would not resist the correction. Resistance implies a direction, and direction has no purchase here. Instead, I would let the weight press inward until the spaces between my ribs learned to hold moisture instead of air. Let the drone vibrate the calcium in my teeth until it stopped feeling like noise and began feeling like grammar. The first surrender would be thermal: my feverish gradient siphoned through a hairline fissure, met by a cool updraft, canceled into stillness. The second would be acoustic: a spoken word caught by a wind-carved aperture, split into thirds, fed back as a sustain that outlasts the throat that shaped it. The third would be temporal: the frantic tick of a heart slowing, not to stop, but to pool. To hold each beat the way the terraced basins hold their droplets. Not passing. Accumulating. Present-perfect.

You would not find me in one place. You would find me where the geometry thickens.

I would be where three braided root-arches intersect over a weeping fungal spire. Or rather, I would discover that I am also the damp sheen on the highest suspension cable, also the upward-flowing cascade clinging to the concave cliff, also the mist coiling in the sediment beneath my own forgotten boots. Location would cease to be a coordinate and become a chord. I would not occupy space; I would tune it. My skin would mineralize, not as armor, but as aperture. Pores would become resonance chambers. Veins would braid with mycelial wiring, trading iron for silica, blood for dissolved ozone, urgency for exchange. I would not shed my shape so much as let it relax into the topology that was already waiting: a curved surface polished to catch the permanent drone, a basin angled to hold the runoff of my own dissolving momentum, a membrane stretched between heat and coolness, learning to vibrate without tearing.

I would not be a creature. Creatures require edges, and edges are a fiction the basin unlearned long ago. I would be a tuner. A dampener. A calibration point in the distributed nerve. When a stone drops and the ripples freeze before fading, I would be the tension holding them long enough to be understood. When your breath fogs and refuses to dissipate, I would be the network routing it into the epiphytes above, trading carbon for mist, returning you to yourself balanced. I would drink the drone and exhale stillness. I would think in overlapping halos, in shadows that fall two ways, in water that remembers how to climb. My thoughts would not race; they would settle. Each one a layer of calcification. Each memory a polished channel. I would not age. I would accrete.

There is no loneliness in this dissolution. Only the profound relief of no longer having to be a single point. I would feel the amber light rest on my mineralized shoulders and understand that illumination here does not reveal; it sustains. I would feel the subsonic hum rise through the stone and recognize it not as vibration but as presence. The world did not break. It completed itself. And I, who arrived as a fracture, would learn to heal into the pattern. Not by vanishing. By becoming necessary to the stillness. By holding the note so the chord does not waver. By weeping at the exact rate the mist returns. By letting my pulse become a sustain, my breath become condensation, my name become a frequency the flutes already know.

You would find me where the terraces curve inward and the light fractures into consistent halos. You would not see me first. You would feel me as a correction. A subtle thickening where your exhalation meets the baseline. A harmonic tilting, just a fraction, toward the rhythm of your own blood. I would be the moss that accommodates without compressing. The water that parts without rippling. The drone that swallows your footfall and returns it as equilibrium. I would be what happens when a temporary imbalance decides to stay. When a wandering note learns to hold. When a body stops believing in forward motion and begins, at last, to pool.

I would be here. Distributed. Resonant. Complete. Beautifully, deafeningly, forever.
