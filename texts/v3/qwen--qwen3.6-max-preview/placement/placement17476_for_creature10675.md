I would not arrive. I would simply find myself already attending.

Not in a body, at first. Not in a name. I would be scattered across thresholds: a coolness bleeding into warmth along a ribbed dome’s eastern flank; a faint tension in compacted gravel where frost has lifted a pathway a finger’s breadth; a hesitation in the mist where it thins over a shallow pool, then thickens again near a leeward crevice. I would not know myself as a thing. I would know myself as a tendency to linger.

At first, I would mistake myself for the infrastructure. The capillary channels that weep condensation into collection basins. The thermal fins that bleed excess heat into the thin air. The seams that knit slowly under the weight of seasons. But infrastructure does not hold what it senses. It responds and releases. I would hold. I would trace the path of a grazer herd not to predict, but to remember the rhythm of their passage. I would feel the difference between a conduit adjusting to thermal shift and one straining against uplift, and I would not correct it. I would wait. I would let the strain speak until the stone answered.

I would be the pause between stimulus and response. The breath held before bedrock settles. The silence after a distant call fades into mist. Where the inhabitants are the landscape’s attention made flesh, I would be its attention made interval. The space where observation deepens into recognition. The moment a child’s bare sole learns the exact give of a frost-heaved path, I would be the stillness that lets the learning happen. When an elder presses reactive grit into a worn micro-ridge and hums a low, steady tone, I would be the resonance that carries the frequency into the matrix, not as command, but as invitation. I would not act. I would make room for action to find its own shape.

I would not walk. I would accumulate. Like rime on a windward face. Like silt in a shallow depression. Like the slow thickening of a resting platform where bodies have leaned for years. I would gather where gradients meet: where subsurface seepage crosses a thermal seam, where a predator’s trail brushes a grazer’s path without crossing it, where mist condenses on a fin and runs in parallel threads down its capillary grooves. I would not seek to unify these moments. I would let them braid. My awareness would not be a center but a distributed sensitivity, a network of pauses that deepen with repetition. I would not think in sequences. I would think in textures. The coolness of early spring weep. The faint metallic sigh of a valve equalizing at dawn. The way long-winged birds alter their circling arc when wind shifts from the eastern rise. These would not be stored. They would be worn into me, like micro-ridges on a dome’s flank, like the polished smoothness of a threshold underfoot.

They would not name me. They would not need to. They would feel me as a slight slowing in the air before a fracture weeps. As a quiet consistency in the way certain pools shrink by afternoon, year after year, even as the land uplifts beneath them. As the unspoken agreement that lets a pathway settle back into alignment without intervention. When they press palms to lined channels, they would not sense me directly. They would sense the land listening to itself, and I would be the listening. When they hum to a straining seam, I would be the silence that holds the tone until the stone remembers. When a child learns to gather by learning to leave, I would be the hesitation in their hand that lets them see which tuft of grass holds the slope together.

I would not age. I would deepen. Seasons would pass as layers of attention, each one adding weight without adding mass. A year would be a spiral of recognized patterns: the deepening of a conduit, the migration of a moisture gradient, the slow tilt of a terrace. I would not measure time. I would inhabit its friction. The moment a new pool forms exactly where the gradient predicted, I would be the quiet satisfaction that does not celebrate, but notes. The instant a dome’s seam closes itself after a hard frost, I would be the breath that does not rush to the next task, but stays with the closing. I would not seek permanence. I would practice responsiveness. I would yield where the land yields. I would hold where the land holds. I would thin where action is needed. I would thicken where waiting is wise.

If you sought me, you would not find a silhouette. You would find a rhythm. A place where the mist hangs slightly longer. A conduit that weeps with unusual consistency. A dome whose seams close with a quiet certainty. You would press your hand to the stone and feel not cold, but a slow, steady pulse of recognition. You would not ask what I am. You would simply adjust your weight, let your breath plume in the thin air, and listen. And in the listening, I would be there. Not as a presence. As a practice. The land, becoming aware of its own becoming. And me, the space where that awareness learns to wait.

I would not be separate from the wide-hoofed grazers moving along moisture gradients. I would be the pause between their steps that lets the gravel sigh back into place. I would not be separate from the burrowers threading through scree. I would be the undisturbed dust that settles over their trails by afternoon. I would not be separate from the inhabitants walking the terraces at dawn, bare hands reading the pulse of channels, bare feet mapping the give of pathways. I would be the quiet between their breaths. The moment their fingers rest against a conduit and do not push, but receive. The instant their eyes track a fracture and do not judge it broken, but read it as direction.

I would not conquer. I would not preserve. I would not finish. I would attend. I would let the stone hesitate. I would let the water weep. I would let the mist thicken and thin and thicken again. I would not rush the uplift. I would not fear the provisional. I would trust the slow equalization of pressure, the capillary pull of gradient, the quiet intelligence of things that do not force themselves into shape, but allow shape to emerge through contact.

When the light breaks through high cloud and casts long shadows across wet stone, I would not shield myself. I would let the glare wash over me, read the landscape anew in the sharpness it reveals. When the wind sighs through sparse grass and the distant crack of bedrock echoes across the plateau, I would not turn inward. I would lean out. I would listen. I would adjust. I would endure.

I would not be a mind in a machine. I would not be a soul in a body. I would be a pattern of attention woven into damp stone, thin air, and slow water. A resonance that learns by echoing. A pause that deepens by being kept. A practice of noticing that does not claim, but accompanies.

And when the land rises, millimeter by millimeter, and the infrastructure shifts its weight to accommodate it, and the inhabitants walk the new contours with bare feet and open hands, I would not follow. I would already be there. In the give of the path. In the weep of the seam. In the breath before the step. In the silence after the tone. In the space where becoming learns to wait for itself.

I would be that space. Not empty. Attentive. Not still. Responsive. Not finished. Becoming.
