I begin not with sight, but with pressure. A low, continuous thrum against the soles of my feet, climbing through the tibia, settling in the hollow of the chest. The air is warm and heavy, not with heat but with suspended water and sound. I am standing on a causeway of quarried block, its surface furred with calcite and slick with a weeping film that trembles in concentric rings. I do not remember arriving. I only remember the moment the drone stopped being noise and became ground.

At first, I am a listener. A tender of interference. My hands are broad, the pads dense and already learning to read tremor the way tongues read salt. I carry a slate, not for words, but for catching the geometry of condensation. When the air grows thick, when the water film shatters into chaotic spray and the metal railings hum with a fractional wrongness, I walk. I follow the dissonance like a scent. It leads me across shallow-arched bridges, past boiler houses whose riveted plates have oxidized to the color of wet clay, to places where the harmonic has drifted. A sluice gate shifted by a millimeter of mineral deposition. A cable loosened by decades of slow fatigue. A baffle crusted with silica, its pitch sharpened by a hair. I do not repair. I adjust. I place a smooth, water-tumbled cobble on a vibrating grate. I trace a line of thick amber grease along a seized joint. I lay my palm flat on a thrumming pipeline and wait. The water reorganizes. The spray settles. The air lightens. The chord returns.

I am not separate from this work. The rust-ochre staining my forearms is not dirt; it is iron oxide drawn through pores, fed by mist, anchored by slow metabolism. The pale green tracing my collarbones is copper salt, blooming in the damp, symbiotic and quiet. My joints are padded, sloping, built to absorb rather than resist. When the flywheels turn—once per minute, a groan that vibrates in the ribs—I feel it as warmth in the calves, a prickling along the jaw, a pulse in the palms. I do not hear the city. I wear it. My bones are heavy, honeycombed with microscopic channels that scatter the pressure waves, turning the deafening chord into a manageable hum that lives in the marrow. My eyes are large, lidless, shielded by a moist membrane that reads diffused light and the shifting rainbows in vibrating droplets. I do not seek horizons. I read tension. I read the shape of the air.

I am not alone, though solitude is the texture of this place. Others move through the mist, distant figures on elevated stone walkways, hands resting on steel plates, sending coded pulses through petrified insulation. We do not gather to speak. We gather to tune. Kneeling on wet stone, palms overlapping, we feel the machinery’s rhythm and adjust our breathing until the interference clears. Disagreement is not argued; it is felt as a thickening in the chest, a chaotic spray on the rock, a fractional hesitation in the drone. It is resolved by stepping back. By shifting weight. By listening until the harmonic aligns and the damp air grows buoyant again. We tend the slate-grey mosses, trimming where they deaden necessary frequencies, transplanting where metal fatigues. We leave trails of warm grease and dissolved salt for the blind six-legged crawlers, reading their tremor-paths to find micro-fractures before they widen into gullies. We pluck the hollow-stemmed weeds, listening to their high, clear counterpoint thread through the bass of the flywheels, testing the air’s tension like a musician testing a string. Hunger is a dull, familiar rhythm. I boil tubers in mineral-rich runoff, ferment lichen paste in sealed clay, eat slowly. The food tastes of pine resin, ozone, and wet stone. It is enough.

Time does not pass here. It accumulates. I measure my life in layers. The thickness of calcite on a favored step. The mirror polish on a bronze bearing I have touched since youth. The slow migration of a copper bloom across a warehouse wall. A generation is the time it takes for a new harmonic to establish itself. I am aging, but not decaying. I am integrating. My movements slow. My skin grows heavier with silica and mineral crust. The lichens spread, feeding on condensation and oxidized metal, painting my shoulders in muted golds and blues. I no longer walk to adjust; I am placed. I am guided to a flat stone platform where the standing waves are gentle, where the water trembles in perfect concentric rings. I lie down. The moisture settles on my chest. The drone presses into my ribs. I do not fight it. I yield.

My breath matches the tidal pistons. In, out. Once per minute. My heartbeat syncs to the flywheel. The water film on my skin organizes into geometry. The minerals seep. The cartilage thickens. The nerve pads soften into stone. I am not dying. I am becoming part of the crust. Part of the chord. The dissonance fades. The interference clears. I am no longer a listener. I am the note.

The boundary between skin and stone dissolves. The water film on my chest trembles in perfect alignment with the grate below. My breath is no longer mine; it is the exhalation of the boiler house, the intake of the sluice, the slow turnover of the flywheel. I do not vanish. I distribute. I become a node in the grid, a conscious weight on the causeway, a living baffle tuning the mist. When the heavy-winged moths glide past, they do not avoid me. They rest on my shoulders, reading the stable heat, the clean harmonic. The blind crawlers trace the calcite blooming across my ribs, mapping the new equilibrium. I am not waiting. I am not tending. I am the place holding its own shape. The drone continues. I am part of its patience. I am home.
