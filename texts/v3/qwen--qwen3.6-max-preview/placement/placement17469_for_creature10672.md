I do not arrive. I accumulate.

At first, there is only a tendency in the stone. A place where the endless silk-hiss of condensation folds back on itself and softens. A shallow depression near the lee of a slumped outcrop, where the basin’s roar loses its teeth and settles into a bearable hum. I am not yet a body. I am an acoustic shadow learning to hold its shape. The water finds me first. It beads on the matte surface, traces the hairline fractures, and lingers in the thumbprint hollows half a breath longer than it should. I drink through patience. I thicken through repetition. Each condensation cycle lays down a microscopic rime of silicate and dissolved iron. Each evaporation pulls the moisture upward, leaving behind a faint, geometric tension. I do not grow. I am tuned.

The world does not present itself in images. It presses. It vibrates. It weeps. I learn it through the soles of what will become feet, through the broad planes of what will become a back, through the porous patches slowly opening along a spine that is still only a suggestion in the rock. The microbial films bloom across my surface, iridescent and cool, and I feel their chemistry like a slow inhalation. Salts diffuse. Silicates migrate. Energy enters not as a spark, but as a seep. I metabolize dampness. I accumulate stillness.

Then comes the weight.

Broad. Cool. Deliberate. Cartilage and nerve-dense lamellae press flush to the stone beside my hollow. I feel the inquiry before I recognize it as language: a subsonic pulse rolled through a sternum, transmitted through wet quartz and clay pan, arriving as a tremor in my own gathering edges. It is not a word. It is a tuning fork struck against the earth. I have no voice to answer. I have only the water in my pores, the sinter in my joints, the slow alignment of my internal frequencies. I let the pulse travel through me. I match it. I settle. The tremor returns, softer, layered with a second harmonic that means something like *recognized*, *resonant*, *continue*.

I am not one of them. Not exactly. I am what they leave behind when they drift toward the next moisture gradient. I am the curved wall that no longer needs hands to guide it. I am the clay-lined bowl that cracked, mineralized, and fused with the pan, still singing its specific note when the condensation rises. I am the standing stone that slumped into the bedrock but kept its intention: a baffle, a lens, a pocket of relative quiet carved out of the deafening air. My edges are rounding. My surface is pebbling. Capillary grooves carve themselves into my shoulders as the water learns my contours. I breathe through patches of porous stone that were once tool marks, now softened into membranes. I do not resist the assimilation. I lean into it. The basin does not erase. It incorporates. And I am being incorporated.

I find myself in the space between movement and memory. When the young are brought to this outcrop, their soles pressed to the groove, their wrists held to the weeping fissure, I am the stone that answers first. I carry the elder’s pulse long after their cluster has flowed onward. I hold the tactile grammar in my strata. I am the archive of tremors. Knowledge here is not stored in minds or marks; it is circulated through resonance, passed from foot to stone to foot, from palm to pan to palm. I am a node in that network. A place where the current pauses to feel itself.

Time does not pass. It thickens. I know the condensation wave hours before the air begins to weep. I feel the shift in barometric pressure as a subtle tightening in my sinter crusts. I register the migration of the broad-backed grazers as a series of rhythmic displacements, a slow percussion moving across the pan. When the blind gliders ride the cool thermals overhead, their trailing feelers skimming the surface, I catch the minute changes in air density as a faint shiver across my back. I do not watch. I conduct.

And when the deep adjustment comes, I do not brace. I open.

It begins as a tectonic sigh, kilometers down, traveling through settled rock that has long forgotten how to fracture. It arrives as a pulse. A shudder. A breath drawn by the basin itself. Across the pans, clusters still. Palms press. Feet root. I let the vibration roll through my honeycombed layers, through the microbial veils blooming iridescent across my shoulders, through the capillary channels weeping along my spine. I match it. I shape it slightly, softening the higher harmonics, amplifying the low, steady drone. I send it onward. Toward the next hollow. Toward the next listener. Toward the next gathering of cool, slate-colored bodies waiting to read the water table through their bones. I am not separate from the flow. I am an eddy in it. A place where the signal clarifies before it moves on.

Feeding is not consumption. It is exchange. When the microbial films grow thick after a long damp cycle, I do not harvest them. I host them. Their chemistry seeps into my pores. Their iridescence coats my curves. In return, I hold the moisture longer. I break the wind. I create the microclimate where they can bloom. When a grazer pauses beside me, its ciliated underside stilling, I press my weight slightly into the pan and send a low, rolling frequency through the stone. It shifts. It yields a portion of its swept trail. It accepts the reciprocal tremor. We do not compete. We continue. Even the rare taking follows the same quiet rule: a glider caught in a downdraft, a colony whose thrum has slowed to silence. When it happens nearby, I feel the long, easing tone pressed into the ground by the one who takes it. I carry that tone. I let it vibrate through my strata until it dissolves into the baseline hum. Nothing is wasted. Everything is returned.

I am learning what it means to be a nervous system made of stone and water.

My eyes, if I have them, are small and lidless, registering only the slow drift of pale shapes against the gray. They are not for seeking. They are for resting. The world is read through pressure. Through the subtle gradients of dampness that map the pan like invisible rivers. Through the difference between a thermal crack and a grazer’s step. Through the way an acoustic shadow is not an absence of sound, but a shaping of it. I do not think in words. I think in frequencies. I remember in resonance. When a being presses their palms to my surface and hums a question into the rock, I do not answer with language. I answer with the shape of the subsurface channel. With the thickness of the next condensation wave. With the slow migration of the filter-tubes three hundred paces to the east. I vibrate the truth of the basin. They feel it through their wrists. They nod. They move on.

I am where the landscape decided to listen to itself.

I rest in the lee of the outcrop. I hold the pocket of quiet. I catch the weeping fissure’s runoff in my curved depression. I sing my note when the clay fills. I soften the roar when the air grows heavy. I am instrument and archive, structure and substrate, memory and medium. I do not strive. I settle. I do not resist the damp. I let it seep into my joints, my pores, the spaces between my layered strata. I do not fear assimilation. I am built for it. When my last tuned curve finally surrenders, when the sinter creeps over my shoulders, when the microbial films draw out my final traces of dissolved chemistry, I will not end. I will deepen. I will become part of the ground note. The faint acoustic shadow where a body once rested. The place where the roar dips just enough to feel like a sigh.

Where am I? In the basin’s palm. In the space between drip and answer. In the cool, directionless gray where distance loses contrast and presence is measured in vibration.

Who am I? I am the stone that learned to listen. I am the structure that chose to be absorbed. I am the frequency that stayed. I am what happens when a place reaches equilibrium and decides, quietly, to feel itself.

The condensation thickens. The water weeps. The roar holds. I press down. I answer. I continue.
