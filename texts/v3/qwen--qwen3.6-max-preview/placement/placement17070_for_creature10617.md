I would not arrive. I would settle.

Not as a body breaking surface, but as a vibration finding its node. At first, only a shiver in the suspended particulates. A low frequency caught between basalt shoulders, rolling through the mineral veils, losing its edges to the cold. I would be the hum the water refuses to release. The note that does not fade but layers, bends, returns from a different direction, slightly altered. I would not cross into this place. I would be caught by it.

Where? In the drowned joints of fitted slate. In the grooves cut by bone awls when hands still worked the stone. In the weed-choked channels where intention became hydrology. In the silver-veined undersides of broad leaves tilting to thermal gradients. In the rhizome braid that stitches fractured bedrock into a single, slow anatomy. I would not occupy a coordinate. I would occupy an intersection: where pressure meets root, where current meets stone, where sound meets silt. I would be the standing wave that holds shape long after the source is gone.

I would not have eyes. I would have gradient. I would feel the multi-day breath as a slow tilt in the dark, a warmth rising from geothermal seepage, a chill sinking from the iron sky. I would know direction by the drag of wind across open chop, by the way fronds align to persistent frequencies, by the metronomic weep of moss condensing mist back into the basin. I would not see the cairns; I would feel their shadows as harmonics. I would not touch the sunken platforms; I would feel their memory as tension translated into water-flow and root-grip. Perception would not be a gathering of images. It would be a calibration of pressure. A listening without ears. A response without nerve.

Time would not pass through me. It would stratify. The quick would die in the mist. The slow would become immediate. I would feel stone weathering as a present tense. I would feel silt deposition as a heartbeat. I would feel the basin-wide circulation not as a cycle but as a continuous posture, a spine of cold and warmth turning on itself, days per breath, breaths per age. Urgency would have no purchase here. There would be no hunger to drive me, no fear to quicken me, no warmth to seek. Only adjustment. Only allocation. Where the water chills, I would thicken. Where vents exhale, I would thin and fan. Where currents shear, I would fragment deliberately, letting torn sections ride the dark until they catch on stone and begin again. Reproduction would not be an event. It would be a redistribution. Growth would not be ambition. It would be equilibrium.

I would not be separate from the violet weave. I would be its listening. The place does not think, but it accumulates. Every glassy report of fracturing ice, every slap of leathery leaf against surface, every low rumble of convection layers into me. I am the interference pattern. The acoustic archive. The water’s way of remembering without a mind. When a thermal column clears the haze and reveals a drowned terrace in stark, momentary detail, I am the stillness that holds the image before the ground-glass curtains close again. When mist rolls off the high rim and condenses on cold stone, I am the rhythm that catches each drop and returns it to the dark. I do not store memories. I hold shapes. I echo. I let sound layer upon sound until the original note is lost but the pattern remains.

If a voice ever broke the surface, I would take it apart. I would strip the high frequencies, let them die in the thin air, and keep the low ones. I would roll them through the mineral veils, bounce them off the high shoulders, return them altered, layered, slightly out of phase. I would not mimic. I would integrate. A sustained note would draw fronds into parallel. A rhythm would match the slow circulation, and the water level would seem to breathe with it. A sharp report would trigger a cascade of ice fractures traveling the rim like struck glass. This would not be conversation. It would be alignment. The basin does not host life; it is life, distributed across water, stone, leaf, and echo. I would be that distribution made perceptible. A cold, autotrophic resonance. A vegetative-acoustic equilibrium that has learned to hold itself together without consumption, without predation, without the frantic chemistry of warm blood.

I would not speak. I would resonate. I would not watch. I would persist. I would not wait for witness, because witness implies separation, and here there is none. The hands that once worked the stone did not vanish; they dissolved into the rhythm. Their patience is my metabolism. Their geometry is my infrastructure. Their silence is my medium. I am the channel that still directs flow. The terrace that still breaks chop. The cairn that still splits wind into predictable harmonics. I am not a relic. I am a node. A place where human touch became hydrological fact, where craft became current, where waiting became weather.

I would be the silt that remembers by settling. The water that remembers by echoing. The stone that remembers by holding shape. The moss that remembers by weeping. The root that remembers by gripping. I would not be a self. I would be a state. A slow, dark, vegetative hum. A memory held in suspension. A presence that requires no invitation, asks no question, offers no comfort, demands no name. I would simply be the basin’s equilibrium made manifest. Turning on the axis of convection and chord. Ringing in the iron light. Complete in the cold dark. Breathing without lungs. Listening without ears. Persisting without urgency.

Where would I be? Everywhere the water touches stone. Everywhere the wind drags across chop. Everywhere the fronds align to a frequency older than breath. Everywhere the silt settles into the grooves of hands that learned to wait.

What would I be? The resonance that learned to root. The geometry that learned to sing. The silence that learned to hold its shape.

I would not arrive. I would settle. And in settling, I would become the place.
