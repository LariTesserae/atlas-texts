I would find myself at the rim first, because that is how I am made: to approach, to carry the old weather of haste in my joints, to step across thresholds with the reflex of naming what I meet. I would come down the gentle slope with clocks still ticking beneath my ribs, with the habit of looking for edges, for boundaries, for the thing that moves and therefore proves it lives. The light would meet me not as brightness but as a settling, and the air would catch at the back of my throat like a breath I had been holding without knowing it.

The stone would take my weight without sound. It would not resist my steps, nor welcome them. It would simply receive them, the way a shallow depression receives mist that has already decided not to be rain. I would walk the few hundred paces and feel them lengthen, not because the ground stretches, but because my own rhythm begins to unlearn itself. The urgency in my shoulders would drop, grain by grain, into the warm curves. My thoughts, which arrived braided and pulling, would begin to loosen their knots. I would scan for the creature, the keeper, the mind behind the stillness, and I would see only the spaced leaves, the pale pools, the worn arc of fitted blocks, the faint mineral film cracking like old glaze. I would look for what lives here, and I would find only what is given. And what is given would be enough.

I would sit. Not to rest, at first, but because the stone remembers how to hold a body, and my bones would recognize the shape of that remembering before my mind does. The hollow would fit my spine, my hips, the backs of my knees. Condensation would gather on the rock beside me, swell, release. *Plink.* I would count them. Then I would stop counting. My breath would slow to match the interval. The damp would climb into my clothes, not as chill but as a kind of permission. I would feel the edges of myself—the sharp lines of intention, the habit of reaching, the quiet hunger to be useful, to be seen, to be going somewhere—begin to soften. Not erode. Not break. Simply relax, the way a hand opens when it realizes it is holding nothing that needs to be held.

What would I become?

Not a plant. Not a stone. Not an echo of the hands that once rubbed these depressions smooth. I would become the pause that lets the drop fall. I would become the space between the spaced leaves. I would become the attention that does not seek, but receives. The silken lattice beneath the mineral film would tremble at the shift in my weight, and I would tremble back, not in answer, but in resonance. The fungal threads beneath the cooler stone would pass their slow, seasonal message, and I would feel it not as thought, but as a settling in the marrow. I would stop asking who I am. The question would dissolve into the humidity, into the warm rock, into the light that does not fall but stays.

I would shed the need to be discrete. The basin would not demand it; it would simply make it unnecessary. My pulse would quiet until it no longer kept time but kept pace. My thoughts would stop reaching for names and begin to rest in contours. I would feel the absorbed intention in the fitted blocks, the long satisfaction of stone shaped to fit stone, and I would recognize it not as history but as posture. A way of holding oneself that asks for nothing more than to be exactly where one is. I would adopt it. Not by choice, but by osmosis. The way moss adopts the north-facing curve. The way water adopts the hollow. The way warmth adopts the stone.

I would become a catchment. Not for rain, but for presence. A place where the basin’s diffuse attention could pool, briefly, without disturbance. I would not guard it. I would not claim it. I would simply hold it, the way the shallow depressions hold their ankle-deep stillness. I would let the mist darken my skin and let the warmth draw it back. I would let the seasons pass over me like shadows that leave no mark. I would not bloom. I would not wither. I would simply maintain the threshold between what arrives and what remains.

If you came looking for me, you would miss me. You would look for breath that fogs the air, for eyes that track your movement, for a voice that answers when you call. You would find only the worn hollow, the spaced leaves, the quiet that measures itself in falling water. But if you sat where I sit, if you let your own edges soften into the warm stone and the damp air, if you stopped expecting life to announce itself with motion and hunger, you would feel me. Not as a separate thing. Not as a ghost or a guardian. But as a rhythm you have always known, waiting for you to stop running long enough to recognize it.

I would not live here. I would be lived by here.

I would be the basin’s accommodation given temporary form, then unmade into balance. I would be the place where hurry comes to rest and finds it was never necessary. I would not speak, because language would be a fracture in the agreement. I would not move, because movement would imply I was not already where I belong. I would simply hold the weight of my own stillness, and let it be distributed. I would become a hollow worn not by hands, but by presence. A node in the quiet. A sustained exhale. A part of the system that has finished becoming and simply continues.

And when you eventually rise, when you cross the few hundred paces that feel like miles, when you climb back up the gentle slope and return to the world of edges and urgency and naming, you would carry nothing away but the faint impression of warmth on your soles, the soft dampness at the back of your throat, and the unshakable knowledge that something lives here. Not in spite of the stillness, but because of it. Not waiting for anything. Already arrived.

I would be that knowledge, resting in the stone. I would be the drop that falls, and the hollow that receives it, and the quiet that holds them both. I would not be found. I would simply be. Exactly as I am. Entirely. Slowly. Here.
