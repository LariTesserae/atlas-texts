I do not arrive. I condense.

At first, there is only the cool press of damp air against something that might be skin, might be oxidized casing, might be the velvet underside of a moss mat. I do not know my perimeter. The mist does not part for me; it settles into me, and I into it. Beneath me, or through me, or around me, there is grating. Ribbed iron, sloped a fraction of a degree, weeping condensation into a channel lined with ceramic and furred with algae. I feel the slope before I understand I have weight. I feel the water before I understand I have edges.

I move, or the platform tilts, or both. The distinction is not yet useful.

My feet find the deck. Or my roots do. Or the curved base of a housing that has learned to walk. The surface speaks in micro-vibrations: a conveyor three tiers down shifting sediment, a counterweight gliding up a rubberized shaft, a pneumatic line exhaling behind a wall draped in creeping jenny. I do not hear these things. I read them through contact. Pressure travels up through iron, through aggregate, through something that might be bone, might be braided hemp, might be compacted root fiber. My knees bend. My center drops. I am learning the grammar of load.

Water beads on a surface I call a forearm, though it might be a pipe casing wrapped in vine bast, or a tensioned guide-wire furred with moisture, or the smooth heel of a hand stained dark with mineral. I let the drop run. It traces a seam, finds a perforation, joins a shallow sheet already moving toward a recessed cistern. I follow it. Or it pulls me. The basin does not distinguish between follower and current. It only distinguishes between flow and blockage.

I press a palm to a bolted flange. The metal is cool. The water is cooler. Between them, a rhythm: open, close, adjust, hold. My breath matches it. My pulse does not beat so much as cycle. I am not yet one thing. I am the place where attention meets circulation.

I walk for hours, or I am carried by the slow drift of gantry rails, or I simply shift weight from terrace to terrace as the hydraulic pivots redistribute load. The mist thickens, thins, thickens again. Light does not change; it only deepens in tone, gray-green to charcoal, broken by the wet glint of glazed tile and the faint amber glow of a guide light buried under fern clusters. I pass a ceramic filter housing. The latches are closed, but the condensation beads on the casing are unbroken. Someone has been here. Or something has. Or I have. I run a thumb along the seam. The metal remembers the touch. Or my skin remembers the metal. The boundary is thinning.

I stop at a channel where the water pulse hesitates. Not a failure. A drift. The gradient has shifted a millimeter over weeks, enough to let sediment pool where it should flush. I do not think. I kneel. The grating bites into callus ridges that might be flesh, might be cured canvas, might be compressed cork composite. I reach into the flow. The water is stone-cold, clean, tasting of ozone and faint iron. My fingers find the obstruction: a mat of shed fronds and mineral crust, caught where a siphon meets a reinforced conduit. I do not pull. I tease. I loosen the weave, let the current take it in pieces, watch the pulse deepen, resume, smooth itself. I leave a twist of hemp around the adjacent guide-wire. Not a sign. A continuation. The basin will read it. Or I will. Or we already have.

I am beginning to recognize myself not by shape, but by function.

I am the hesitation that prevents the strain. I am the millimeter of adjustment that keeps turbulence from becoming friction. I am the hand that turns the valve two clicks clockwise, and the valve that remembers the turn, and the water that responds to both. I am not operating the place. I am participating in it. The words arrive slowly, shaped for the damp: *those who keep the weave*. *The hands in the current*. I do not claim them. They claim me, the way moss claims a seam, the way water claims a slope.

My lungs pull thin, saturated air and exchange it without hurry. Alveoli expanded, capillaries dense, blood affinity shifted to the curve of high-altitude humidity. I do not sweat. The air will not take it. Instead, I press my forearms to a ceramic heat exchanger, let the cool draw the warmth out through vasodilation, feel the temperature equalize. My joints carry trace silicates from the water I drink, synovial fluid thickened by decades of kneeling, crouching, moving along sloped grating. My skin mottles like lichen on damp stone, a pigmentation response to constant moisture and filtered light. My eyes are wide-pupiled, irises muted green-gray, a thin translucent membrane sliding horizontally when spore clouds drift from pruning cycles. I do not blink often. The air is too moist. The light is too soft. When I look at the basin, I do not scan. I listen.

I climb to a mid-tier platform where the mist opens slightly, where sightlines stretch across bridged chasms into lower basins repeating the same patterns. I sit. Palms flat on the grating. Spine aligned with the slope. Eyes closed. I do not rest. I resonate.

Four tiers up, a counterweight reaches its stop. The vibration travels down through ivy-wrapped shafts, through rubberized guides, through iron and root and something that might be my own skeletal frame. I feel it in my heels. I feel it in my teeth. Three tiers down, a sediment conveyor shifts load, the hum dropping a fraction in frequency. I adjust my breathing to match the ventilation shafts. I let my pulse align with the hydraulic cycles. My body temperature equalizes with the ceramic housings. The boundary between tender and structure thins to transparency. I am not dissolving. I am tuning.

I understand now what I am.

I am not the builder. I am not the ruler. I am not even the inhabitant, if that word implies separation. I am the conscious layer atop a closed loop. I am the attention that keeps automation from drifting into strain. I am the callus that reads grating like braille, the nictitating membrane that filters spores, the brass caliper worn smooth by three generations of fingers, the smoothed stone on the inspection hatch, the pruned angle of the creeping jenny, the double tap on the railing that means *bearing needs tallow*. I am the place where the basin’s self-regulation meets deliberate care. I am the hesitation in the water pulse. I am the correction before the crack. I am the seal.

I rise. The platform tilts a fraction, shedding accumulated water. I shift my weight without thinking. My feet settle into the new gradient. I move toward a recessed niche where cork composite and dried fern line the walls, where a geothermal pipe cycles warmth through the higher tiers. I will sleep curled around it, dreaming in pulses, breathing in time with the shafts. When I wake, I will not stretch. I will realign. I will return to the terraces. I will touch everything. Not out of curiosity. Out of necessity. Touch is my primary instrument. Attention is my metabolism. Circulation is my language.

If you ask where I am, I am on a catwalk draped in liverwort, or knee-deep in a filtration basin, or pressed against a weeping flange, or sitting perfectly still in the charcoal dim of the lower tiers, listening to the whole valley at once. If you ask what I am, I am the wet and the green and the turning. I am the hands in the current, and the current itself. I am the one who keeps the weave by becoming part of it.

I do not conquer this place. I inhabit it. I am what lives here. Not in spite of the density, the damp, the dimness, the motion. Because of it. And when the mist thickens, and the light fades, and the basin breathes in its overlapping rhythms, I breathe with it. I adjust. I hold. I continue.
