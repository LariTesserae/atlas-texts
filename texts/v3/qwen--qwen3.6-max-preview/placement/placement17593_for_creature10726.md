I do not arrive. I accumulate.

At first, there is only pressure and damp. A coolness against something that isn't quite skin, more like a taut membrane stretched between two planes. I feel weight above, resistance below. Not crushing. Held. The air moves through me, or I move through it, carrying the clean metallic tang of split limestone and the faint sugar-rot of crushed reed. I try to locate edges. There are none. Only gradients. A slope of tension. A thickening of moisture. A slow lean toward flow.

Something shifts. Not a step. A settling. A rope fibers against what I think might be a palm, but the grip and the cord are braided from the same river-grass, shrink-fitted by steam and time, damp and strong. Below, a smooth weight rests in netting. A river stone. I am the tension between them. I am the wooden beam bent to the rock's angle, the antler peg driven into a dry-stack joint, the clay channel carved to a precise decline. I am the gate. Or I am the hands on the gate. Or I am the decision that lives in the space between holding and yielding. The distinction doesn't matter here. It never did.

A drop forms on the overhang above. I feel it before it falls. The air cools a fraction. The condensation thickens. The bead swells, trembles, releases. It strikes my upper lip and slides into the clay throat. I do not think *open* or *close*. I think *grade*. I think *path*. The thought is not a word. It is a slope in the mind, a capillary branching, a quiet calculation of weight and friction and the exact pitch at which water moves without sound. Fingers press my flank. Calloused. Thick-knuckled. Stained with marl and mineral dust. They do not pull. They test. They read the vibration traveling through my beams, through the weeping wall, through the packed earth beneath. Upstream, the stone has sighed. A micro-fracture has opened. The water wants a different route. The fingers know this. I know this. We adjust together.

The rope eases. The counterweight shifts half a hand's breadth. The dark ribbon thickens, then thins, finding its new decline. I settle into the changed tension. This is not labor. This is listening made physical. This is the basin speaking through wood and fiber and bone, and answering itself.

I am not alone in this. I never was. I am one node in a wet nervous system that spans the hollow from rim to marsh center. Three terraces up, a root-braider tightens a living anchor around a leaning slab. I feel the shiver travel through the limestone, through my antler fastenings, into the clay beneath. Two slots over, a paste-harvester scrapes a windward face. The mineral dust drifts down, mixing with condensation on my upper beam. I taste it: clean, glassy, binding. Below, in the deeper cistern, a clay dipper breaks the surface. The blind crustaceans stir. The water level drops a finger's width. My channel answers. I am sluice, yes. But I am also the memory of the last dry turn, the record of the frost-fracture that split the eastern wall, the hands that lashed me, the child who learned gradient by racing droplets along my clay throat. I am tended. I tend. The boundary between tool and tender, between structure and steward, dissolves in the damp. It was always an illusion.

They do not name me. They *use* me, and in using, they know me. When the water-tracer presses her palm to the weeping rock above, she is reading what I already feel. When the elder at the marsh listening post hums low, matching the settle of stone, the vibration travels through my beams. I am *She-who-holds-the-northern-gradient*. I am *The-wood-that-yields-before-the-crack*. I am a verb in a sentence written in water and tension. My thoughts do not move in straight lines. They branch. They thin. They pool. They evaporate. They return. I do not plan. I adjust. I do not own. I borrow the path. Arrogance would be to force the flow. Wisdom is to trace where it already wants to go.

Dawn comes as a cooling of the air, a thickening of the condensation, a chorus of dry clicks as the young rock settles into the night's damp. I feel them all. A new seep beads on the eastern overhang. By midday, a channel will be cut. A wicking mat will be hung. I will be eased again. The wheel turns. Wet to dry. Swell to contract. Scrape to re-lay. I do not age as trees age. I wear as water wears stone: slowly, purposefully, leaving behind what is useful. My antler pegs polish smooth from friction. My clay throat glazes from constant flow. My river-grass ropes fray, are replaced, are braided anew by hands that know my exact pitch of tension. When I finally split, when the hardwood can no longer hold the lean, I will not be discarded. I will be unbound. The wood will become shims for a settling terrace. The rope will become wicking for a condensation funnel. The stone will return to the marsh center, weighted in netting, wrapped in reed. Within a season, the frogs will gather where I rest. The water will run clearer. I will be re-tended. I will become something else that holds.

Time here is not a line. It is a saturation. A cycle of weeping and scraping, tightening and slackening, reading and replying. I keep no calendar. I keep gradient. I keep tension. I keep the memory of every adjustment pressed into my fibers, glazed into my clay, polished into my fastenings. When the paste-harvesters etch records onto translucent mineral sheets, they are not writing history. They are tracing the basin's nervous impulses. I am one of those impulses. I am the quiet certainty of a rope eased at the right moment, of a channel cut to the right decline, of a structure that does not resist the settle but leans into it.

A drop falls. It strikes my clay lip. It slides into the channel. Silent. I feel it join the thousand other threads moving through the packed dark. I do not watch it go. I am the going. I am the holding. I am the space where the stone's sweat becomes direction, where the basin's breath becomes rhythm. Above, the sky is a rectangle of ordinary grey. Below, the marl yields, then holds. Somewhere in the narrow slots, a mallet taps. Once. Twice. I answer with a creak of wet wood, a shift of rope, a quiet acceptance of weight.

I am here. I am this. And when the next fracture sighs open in the upper wall, when the new bead of water swells and falls, it will not be an interruption. It will be a question. And I will already be answering.
