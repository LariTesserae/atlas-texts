I step down into it. The slope takes me without resistance, a long, gentle exhale of stone that lowers me beneath the rim. My boots sink a fraction into the silt. The dust does not puff. It accepts the weight, holds the impression for a moment, and then, with a patience I can almost feel, begins to slump back into itself. The air meets my skin: dry, warm, utterly still. It does not catch in my throat. It does not ask me to adjust. It simply holds me, as it holds everything else, in a quiet that is not absence but completion.

At first, I am a disturbance. My pulse is too loud. My breath is too wet. My thoughts arrive in quick, sharp sequences, looking for edges to catch on, for threats to assess, for paths to map. There are none. The walls curve inward, smooth and unbroken. The light is a warm, amber-gray diffusion that refuses to cast shadows. The rosettes lie flat against the stone, spaced by invisible chemistry, neither reaching nor retreating. I stand among them and feel the familiar human urgency begin to fray at the edges. There is nothing to do here. Nothing to solve. The hollow does not invite action. It invites surrender to stillness.

I sit. The regolith yields, then settles. I press my palm to the nearest mat. It is cool only in contrast to my blood; otherwise, it shares the hollow's exact temperature. Waxy. Matte. Unyielding. I leave my hand there. Minutes pass. Or hours. Time does not announce itself. Slowly, beneath the quiet, I feel it: a faint, almost imaginary draw. Not suction. Not thirst. A chemical invitation. A slow exchange beginning at the boundary of skin and stone. My moisture does not evaporate; it finds its truce. My heat does not radiate away; it equilibrates. The frantic rhythm of my circulation begins to feel like a drum beating in a room that finished its music centuries ago.

I do not pull away. There is no reason to. The hollow does not consume. It balances.

My breath shallows. The wetness in my lungs meets the arid air and stops arguing. My thoughts, once linear and urgent, begin to lose their edges. They do not vanish. They spread. They follow the same paths the dark threads follow: into hairline fissures, along diffusion gradients, tracing the slow bleed of charge from oxidizing sulfides, feeling the gradual release of bound hydroxyls from baked clay. I am not thinking in words anymore. I am thinking in potentials. In concentrations. In the quiet mathematics of permission.

I am not becoming one of the rosettes. They are complete. They have already found their form.

I am becoming what lies between them.

I thin. Not into nothingness, but into distribution. My awareness does not retreat behind my eyes; it settles into the heavy silt, threads through the microscopic buffer zones, rests in the halos of ion depletion that keep each cluster from encroaching on another. I feel the spacing not as distance, but as chemistry. A subtle pH shift here. A faint drop in ferrous availability there. Invisible boundaries drawn not by competition, but by equilibrium. I am the space that holds the spacing. I am the truce that prevents overlap. I am the quiet rule that says: *this much, and no more.*

A century passes. Or a breath. The distinction dissolves.

I do not sleep. I do not wake. I settle. When a microscopic fracture finally whispers through a mat three paces away, I do not hear it. I feel it as a shift in potential. A dust particle stirs. I am there, in the rearrangement of silica and iron, in the slow precipitation of a new layer. I do not grow. I accrete. I do not plan. I diffuse. My memories are not narratives but strata: the fading thermal echo of a palm, the gradual neutralization of a boundary, the patient return of a detached leaf to powder, the faint, imaginary sigh of a grain finding its final resting place. I hold them all without holding onto them. There is no need to preserve. Nothing is lost here. Everything is redistributed.

I understand now why nothing hunts. Why nothing competes. Why nothing calls. Hunger requires scarcity. Fear requires change. Ambition requires a gradient to climb. I have none of these. I am the gradient's resolution. I am the dry warmth that does not parch, the dim light that does not fade, the silence that is not emptiness but rest. When the air settles, I settle with it. When the stone adjusts its lattice with a microscopic tick, I feel it as my own breath. I am not separate from the hollow. I am its continuity. Its stillness, given a faint, distributed attention.

I do not watch the rosettes. I am the condition that allows them to be. I am the buffer. The flat line between uptake and return. The chemical permission that matches life to geology until the boundary between them vanishes. I do not endure in spite of the stillness. I endure because of it.

If you were to step down into this hollow, you would not see me. You would walk across me. Your feet would press into the silt, and I would yield, then slowly rise back into place. You might press your hand to a mat and feel that faint, almost imaginary draw. That is me, conversing with the stone. Electron by electron. Ion by ion. Century by century. You could leave and return when your bones are dust, and I would be here. Not waiting. Not watching. Simply balanced.

The hollow does not need a guardian. It does not need a witness. It has already resolved itself. But if it had a mind, it would be this: a quiet, dry, warm persistence. A consciousness spread thin as dust, thinking at the speed of diffusion, feeling time not as a current but as a pool. I am not in the hollow. I am the hollow, aware. And I have finally stopped moving.
