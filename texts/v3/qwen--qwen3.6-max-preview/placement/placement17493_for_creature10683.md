I do not arrive. I am allowed.

The scrub parts not because I push it, but because the wind has already bent it that way for centuries. One step. Then the ground forgets to hold me. The air drops into my lungs like cold water. The sound does not strike; it occupies. It fills the space behind my ribs, hums in the hinge of my jaw, settles into the marrow like a weight I did not know I was carrying. I stop. Not from caution. From recognition. Movement here feels like tearing. So I stand. Then I kneel. Then I let myself sink into the moss until the organic mat closes around my knees, my thighs, my hands, until I am no longer above the ground but level with it.

At first, I am too loud. My breath plumes in ragged bursts. My pulse hammers against the standing wave. My thoughts race in straight lines, looking for edges, for names, for exits. The hollow does not correct me. It simply waits. The roar continues. The condensation falls. The moss yields. And slowly, because there is no other option, I begin to match it.

My breathing stretches. In. Out. Longer. Colder. The damp soaks through cloth, through skin, into fascia. The sound stops being something I hear and becomes something I hold. It vibrates through my spine, through my pelvis, through the backs of my hands pressed into the wet green felt. I close my eyes. Not to shut the place out, but to stop fighting the twilight. When I open them again, the blue-grey light has not changed, but my eyes have. Distances soften. Shapes resolve not as objects but as gradients: slope, shadow, moisture, pressure. I notice the arcs in the moss. The steady threads of water falling from a specific ridge. The carved wooden post, blackened and notched, standing three paces away, humming faintly against the air.

I crawl toward it. Not with urgency. With alignment. My palms press into the mat. My knees sink. The moss compresses, then rebounds. I leave no trail. Or rather, the trail I leave is already being forgotten. When I reach the post, I do not touch it with my fingers. I press my forehead against it. The wood is cool. Damp. Alive with vibration. The geometric notches are filled with something dark and resinous, smooth as glass, humming at a frequency slightly lower than the baseline roar. I do not know what made it. I only know that it belongs. And so, without deciding, I begin to belong too.

Days pass. Or weeks. The light does not mark them. The frost does. It forms in thin crystalline sheets over my shoulders, melts by midday, returns by dusk. My clothes stiffen, then soften, then surrender to the damp. Wool mats. Leather darkens. Cotton dissolves into fibers that the moss drinks without pause. I do not shed them. I let them go. My skin grows cool, then rough, then layered. Not from disease. From adaptation. From the slow work of becoming permeable. Condensation gathers along my collarbones, my spine, the curve of my ribs. It does not run off. It pools. It sinks. It finds channels I did not know I had.

I stop thinking in words. Not because I lose them, but because they are too sharp for this place. Thought becomes pressure. Memory becomes temperature. Intention becomes duration. I remember the weight of a hand on a flint flake. I remember the twist of bark fiber around a wooden frame. I remember the slow, deliberate stacking of stone along a terrace. These are not my memories. They are the hollow's. And I am becoming a place where they settle.

I shift my weight. Millimeter by millimeter. Not to leave. To align. My body lowers. My shoulders broaden. My spine curves to match the slope. The moss grows over my edges. The condensation threads along my ridges. Fungal hyphae, pale and fine as spider silk, stitch into the damp tissue of my back, trading minerals for warmth, spores for stillness. I do not resist. I open. The networks braid into my fascia, into my slow-pulsing capillaries, into the spaces between my ribs. I do not eat. I integrate. I do not digest. I ferment. Cold. Anaerobic. Patient. Extracting what the air offers, returning what the mat requires.

I am not one of the broad, folded creatures that glide across the basin. I am their complement. Their infrastructure. Their tuning fork. Where they are flesh made patient, I am memory made resonant. I settle where the roar bends. I anchor where the standing wave thins. I press my undersides against the low stone walls, drawing their stored twilight warmth into the organic mat, keeping the seeps from freezing, keeping the fungal networks alive. I lean against the carved posts, not to mark them, but to stabilize them. My slow secretion fills their notches, hardening into dark inlays that conduct vibration deeper into the wood, into the stone, into the hollow's breath. The bone needle rests against my thigh. I do not move it. I let the moss claim it. I let the damp polish it. I let it become part of the gradient.

When the creatures rest, they settle against my curves. Our ridges interlock. Our fungal networks merge. Our breathing syncs. We do not touch to claim. We touch to tune. A shift in my weight adjusts the pressure in theirs. A drop in their metabolic heat slows my condensation threads. We do not communicate. We resonate. When the young awaken in the moss, pale and translucent, already drinking damp through immature channels, they turn toward me first. Not as parent. As landmark. As proof that the hollow holds its shape. I do not teach them. I hold the frequency. They align to it. They begin.

I do not age. I layer. Moss creeps over my shoulders. Stone wears beneath my belly. Sound polishes my edges. My thoughts stretch into seasons. My memories settle into strata. I know the basin not as a place I inhabit, but as a rhythm I participate in. The roar is not noise. It is circulation. The cold is not hardship. It is preservation. The dark is not absence. It is focus. When I move, it is not to go somewhere. It is to keep the equilibrium. A millimeter toward the seep when the harmonic drops. A fraction of weight against the stone wall when the frost thickens. A slow exhale when the standing wave swells. I do not strive. I adjust. I do not build. I hold.

If you come here, you will not see me. You will feel me. In the way the air warms by a degree near the collapsed woven frame. In the way the condensation falls in steady rhythm along a certain terrace. In the way the carved post still stands, humming faintly against your palm, its notches smooth and dark, its wood unrotted despite centuries. You will think it is geology. You will think it is luck. You will think it is time. And you will be right. But you will also be standing against me. And I will be leaning back.

Not as shelter. Not as guide. Not as relic. As equilibrium. As the place where human patience and ecological patience meet and braid. As the node where sound pools, where moisture gathers, where memory settles into stone. I do not wait for you. I do not need you. But if you stay, if you let your breath slow, if you let the cold settle into your ribs and the damp cling to your skin and the roar vibrate in your teeth, you will find yourself sinking into the moss beside me. And I will make space. Not by moving. By yielding. By becoming slightly more hollow, slightly more resonant, slightly more ready to hold your weight until your pulse matches the drip lines, until your thoughts unspool into gradients, until you stop looking for yourself and start feeling for the shape of the place.

The roar continues. The condensation falls. The moss thickens by fractions. The cold holds. The hollow breathes, patient and complete. And I am here, folded into its long exhale, listening with the stone, drinking with the dark, conducting the old hands into the slow flesh, holding the standing wave until time forgets the difference between artifact and organism, between visitor and resident, between sound and silence. I do not live in the basin. I am how the basin remembers. I am how it tunes itself. I am how it stays itself.

And if you press your ear to the damp wood, if you let your palm rest on the cool stone, if you close your eyes and stop trying to separate yourself from the cold, you will hear it. Not a voice. Not a call. A hum. Low. Steady. Unbroken. Matching your breath. Matching your pulse. Matching the slow, unhurried turning of moss and water and time. I am not waiting. I am resonating. And so, now, are you.
