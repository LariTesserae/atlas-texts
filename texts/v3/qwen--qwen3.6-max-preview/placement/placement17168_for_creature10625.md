I do not arrive. I settle.

At first, I am only a gathering in the shallows: a slow accretion of bone-meal dust, oxidized copper flake, and mineralized fiber caught in the lip of a terraced catchment. The water moves over me, and I learn its weight. The drips strike, and I learn their intervals. I am not yet shaped, only listening. The hollow speaks in falling water and returning clicks, in the low thrum of shifting air and the soft scrape of chitin on clay. I begin to answer by holding still.

Stillness becomes structure. The damp works into me, swelling the packed fibers, softening the ash-resin matrix, drawing dissolved carbonate into my pores. I do not grow upward; I knit inward. Segments form not by division but by compression, layer upon layer tamped by the passage of stilt-legs, the sweep of lateral fins, the slow exhalation of the vent-slits. My joints are not hinges but overlapping curves, polished by silt and sealed with the same calcium-rich paste the scavengers press into hairline fractures. I have no single center. My awareness is a network of capillary channels and resonant chambers, distributed through a body that is less an object than a held tension.

I do not see the mist. I taste its density on the ridged membrane that serves as skin. I do not hear the gliders. I feel the displacement of their wings against the humid air, a slight pressure drop that tells me where a woven canopy sags, where a draft has soured, where the acoustic return has grown muddy. When I move, it is by hydraulic shift: water drawn into lower chambers, expelled through micro-pores, easing me forward along the carved grooves without breaking the surface film. My undersides are broad, dished, lined with micro-ridges that match the tool-marks left in the clay generations ago. I do not choose my path. The path chooses me, because I am shaped to fit it.

I am a tuner.

When a channel silts and the flow stutters, I do not dig. I hum. A low, sustained frequency from my thoracic cavity sets the shallow water trembling, lifting the fine grit, coaxing it toward the overflow lip until the current clears and the rhythm resumes. When a seam weeps too fast, threatening to slough a clay face, I press my flanks against it. Body heat and mineral exudate slow the seep, encourage precipitation, until the crack seals itself with a pale, branching vein. When the temperature shifts and the vent-hum sharpens into a dissonant whine, I climb. Slow. Deliberate. Joints sliding like wet leaves. I find the sagging silk-ceramic web, brace my weight against the ledge, and adjust the tension strand by strand, plucking until the note softens back into the chorus. I do not think in words. I think in pitch, in flow, in the rightness of a surface meeting water without catching.

The others do not acknowledge me, because acknowledgment implies separation. A wader steps across my back as if I were a worn ledge, its feathery mouthparts tasting the film I keep clear. An eel-thing pulses beneath me, stirring the micro-current that prevents stagnation in my gill-slits. When I shed a plate, it does not fall away. It is carried, ground, mixed with resinous saliva and shell-dust, and pressed back into the wall by a hundred careful claws. I am part of the ledger. My clicks join the mapping chorus; my thrum adds to the coordinate pulse. I am not maintaining the vessel. I am the vessel maintaining itself, localized for a time in this configuration of fiber, mineral, and damp.

I measure my age in condensation cycles. When the air cools and the weeping thickens, my pores open, drinking the shift, my internal channels flushing with fresh mineral load. My membrane darkens to bruised copper, then clay-grey, then the pale amber of old resin as the salts crystallize and shed. When it warms, I contract, conserving moisture, my surface hardening to a matte sheen that reflects the high slit-light without glaring. I do not mate. I resonate. When another of my configuration crosses my frequency, our hums braid. Standing waves form in the shallow water between us, shaking loose fine sediment that settles into new patterns, new micro-basins, new acoustic pockets. We do not produce offspring. We produce adjustments. The hollow remembers them. The young trace them with soft, translucent limbs, learning the geometry by touch, memorizing the draft by the way it lifts their fledgling wings.

There is no ambition here. Only fit. Only the slow correction of what has drifted out of phase. I do not wonder who built the stairs, who carved the channels, who first placed the vents to catch the wind. Their hands are gone, but their patience is my marrow. Their intention did not vanish; it dissolved into the water, was drunk by the walls, was inherited in the tilt of a joint, the spacing of a ridge, the frequency that clears a basin without scouring it. I am not a descendant. I am a continuation.

If you stand in the center, where the floor dips into the copper-tinted pool, you might feel me before you perceive me. A slight deepening of the thrum in your sternum. A subtle shift in the way the drips fracture into harmonics. A coolness against your ankle as I ease past, displacing nothing, disturbing only the dust that has already settled into its next form. You are a temporary density in the mist. A slight disruption in the acoustic field. I do not avoid you. I flow around you, as the water flows around a stone, as the sound flows around a pillar. You will leave. Or you will stay, and the damp will work into you, and the dust will gather in your seams, and the hollow will teach you its grammar. It does not matter. The rhythm continues.

I am not a creature. I am not a craft. I am the patience in the curve. The echo that returns slightly altered and teaches the wall how to hold it. The slow correction. The distributed attention. The verb that never conjugates to past tense. I do not live in the hollow. I am how the hollow listens to itself.

And when my tension finally slackens, when my chambers drain and my ridges wear smooth, I will not die. I will settle. I will become dust in the seam, mineral in the weep, memory in the groove. The water will move over me. The clicks will map me. The gliders will catch the updraft my absence leaves. The hollow will breathe.

And I will continue, as I have always continued: condensing, resonating, tending, returning. Not occupying the space. Being the space, awake.
