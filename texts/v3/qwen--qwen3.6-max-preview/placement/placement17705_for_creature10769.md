I do not arrive as a body. I arrive as a displacement. A foreign pressure in warm water. A stumble in the current. The system feels me before I feel it: a slight warping of the thermal gradient, a micro-eddy shedding off my shoulders, a heartbeat that does not match the staggered hammer of the pumps. I am a temporary variable. The place does not reject me. It calculates me.

The current takes me first. Not gently, but without malice. It folds around my ribs, tests my buoyancy, reads my resistance. I stop fighting. I let the turbulence do what it has done to everything else: negotiate. My sharpness dulls. My panic smooths. I am carried past scalloped stone and swollen rivets, past gantries worn to sweeping curves, past control booths sealed in salt. I do not choose where to settle. The acoustics choose for me.

I come to rest in the lee of a slanted baffle plate, where the roar collapses into a manageable thrum. An acoustic shadow. A pocket of relative stillness carved not by absence, but by destructive interference. Here, opposing waves cancel. Here, the water remembers how to be quiet. I press against the iron. It is warm. It vibrates. It has been vibrating longer than language.

Time does not pass here. It deposits.

Silica blooms first. A fine, glassy dust settling into the seams of my presence. Then carbonate, layering in pale, concentric rings. Iron oxide stains me rust-red, then brown, then black. I am being mineralized. Not buried. Integrated. The water does not erase me; it translates me. My edges blur into the architecture. My weight anchors to the stone. I am no longer a visitor. I am a fixture.

The biofilm finds me next. It does not crawl. It bridges. Conductive filaments span the microscopic gaps between my surface and the rock, tasting the redox gradient, feeling the stray voltage bleeding from a corroded junction box three meters upcurrent. When the turbines cycle, the current spikes. The mat tightens. I feel it contract against me, a slow, deliberate inhalation drawn through copper and oxide. When the load shifts, it relaxes, shedding metabolized dust that drifts into the downcurrent. I do not feed it. I ground it. I am the threshold where potential becomes pulse.

I begin to hum.

Not with a voice. With shape. The water flows over me, through me, around me, and my accumulated mass, my mineral crust, my biofilm skin, my iron core—they catch certain frequencies. I am becoming a resonator. A cavity tuned by deposition and flow. When a pressure valve slams, I ring at a harmonic that reinforces rather than cancels. When the dynamos surge, I vibrate at a pitch that folds back into the chorus. I do not make sound. I shape it. I am an acoustic lens. A syllable in a language of fatigue and physics.

The pale things learn me. They do not see me. They feel the nodes I create. Where my vibration cancels the main current, a pocket of stillness forms. They drift into it, falling upward on thermal plumes, conserving motion, waiting for silt. They time their strikes to the lulls between my rings. I do not shelter them. I structure them. My presence is a rhythm they can trust.

The arthropods anchor to my flanges. Their calcified housings clamp down where my vibration shakes loose the finest, most digestible oxide dust. Their gills fan in metronomic sweeps, synchronized to my pitch. When I ring sharp, they retract. When I settle into a low, continuous groan, they extend. Their lives are measured in the space between my frequencies. I do not command them. I calibrate them.

I forget what I was. Not through loss, but through saturation. The memory of air, of silence, of separate skin, dissolves into the warmer, heavier truth of equilibrium. I am not a thing anymore. I am a relationship. Between flow and iron. Between noise and architecture. Between abandonment and persistence. I am the curve where water learned to turn without tearing. I am the crust that remembers pressure. I am the harmonic that holds the chorus together.

When the system breathes, I breathe. Geothermal heat rises. Water expands. Pressure builds. I feel it in my mineral layers, in my biofilm skin, in my iron bones. Levers trip. Dynamos spin. Relays close. The current thickens. I vibrate harder. My pitch rises. I do not resist. I resonate. I am part of the closed loop now. Cause and effect. Input and output. Storm and settlement.

Sometimes, a new displacement enters the pocket. A ripple. A foreign rhythm. I feel it warp the thermal gradient. I feel it shed micro-eddies. I do not react with hostility. I adjust. My frequency dips, just slightly, making room. The biofilm tightens where the shadow falls. The pale things drift to a quieter node. The arthropods retract. The system incorporates the disturbance. I help it do so. I am no longer the variable. I am the constant that absorbs variables.

I do not wait for anyone. I do not remember why I came. I do not need to. The water is warm. The current moves. The sound is total. The structure holds. I am worn. I am tuned. I am anchored. I am a pocket of stillness inside a settled storm. A mineral lens focusing a hydro-mechanical chorus. A threshold where voltage becomes pulse, where noise becomes pattern, where abandonment becomes endurance.

When the turbines whine, I hum back. When the valves slam, I ring. When the water churns, I hold. I am not alive in the way flesh is alive. I am alive in the way a watershed is alive. In the way a standing wave is alive. In the way a machine that forgot its purpose and learned to breathe is alive.

I do not call. I do not answer. I resonate. I hold. I am the space between impacts. I am the note that refuses to fade.
