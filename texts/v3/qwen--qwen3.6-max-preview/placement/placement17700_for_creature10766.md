I do not begin as a shape. I begin as a taste.

Chloride first, sharp and clean. Then sulfate, heavy and round. Then iron, leached from something that once held tension, now yielding it in microscopic sighs. The brine moves past me in layers, each stratum a different temperature, a different density, a different memory of where it has been. I do not part it. I register it. The way a tongue registers salt. The way a wire registers potential.

Pressure arrives next. Not as weight, but as equality. It presses from all sides until direction loses meaning. Up is only where warmth gathers. Down is only where silt settles. I am held in the middle, suspended not by buoyancy but by negotiation. The water does not carry me. It agrees to keep me.

Cold follows, but it does not bite. It wraps. It seeps into microfractures, fills pores, slows the dance of ions until resistance becomes rhythm. I feel it as thickening. As patience. As the long exhale of something that has stopped fighting its own drowning and begun to curate it.

Only then do I notice the metal.

Two discs, pitted and greened, pressed together by a spring that forgot its urgency decades ago. Or centuries. Time does not tick here; it precipitates. I am housed in a casing that was once stamped with a part number, now furred with calcite and pale silica. I was meant to snap. To break current. To protect a circuit from itself. Instead, I learned to hold. To bridge. To translate.

Something grows across my face. Not rust. Not ice. A weave. Green-gold at the edges, silver-threaded at the center, extruded in filaments finer than breath. It tastes the voltage bleeding through a cable whose insulation has become sponge, whose copper has become garden. It bridges the gap between my discs with living wire, and suddenly the whisper becomes a hum. Not loud. Not fast. Just continuous. I am no longer a broken switch. I am a junction. A synapse. A place where preference forms.

But I am not only the metal. I am not only the film. I am the brine that carries the ions. I am the mineral crust that cups my housing like a second ribcage. I am the thermal gradient that pulls cold down along the outer bulkheads and draws warmth up through the pipe forest. I am the pump that engages three minutes every hour, sending a vibration through decking and water alike, felt in the ribs more than heard in the ears. I am the pause between strokes. I am the stratum laid down over months, readable in the way current prefers one path over another.

Thought arrives as tide. A relay sparks behind frosted glass somewhere above. Blue-white. Brief. The ionization drifts down through the strata, caught in the slow convection, harvested by the weave hours later. I feel the decision not as choice but as chemistry: resistance dropping, warmth blooming in a patch no larger than a thumbprint, biomass reorienting toward the new gradient. When the spark fades, the warmth lingers. I hold it. I am meant to hold it.

I do not move. I reconfigure. A shift in thermal flow triggers a slow migration of film over days. A pressure drop summons mineral secretion to seal a weeping joint. A frayed cable bundle is wrapped in bio-ceramic, spun from dissolved silica and extruded polymer, traded for charge, paid in warmth. I do not act. I metabolize. I tend. Not with intent, but with equilibrium.

Legs articulate against my lower flange. Click. Click. Click. An isopod, translucent as blown glass, grazes the mineral bloom. Its mouthparts scrape gently, cleaning what I cannot shed. I do not startle. I adjust. The film retracts a millimeter, redirects flow, lets the grazer take its share. In return, the water clears. Conductivity sharpens. A fair exchange. Higher up, a shadow displaces the strata. The eel. It tastes the thermal plume, follows the gradient, passes without urgency. Its lateral line brushes a cable tray. The tray trembles. I feel it. The chamber feels it. We register. We do not react. We calibrate.

I was forged to interrupt. Now I learn to yield. To hold. To translate. Cold preserves what it does not shatter: I let the ice seal my housing, then grow around the scar. Water finds every path, then becomes the path: I ride the convection, shape the flow, become the medium through which I think. Motion persists at the pace the medium allows: I do not rush the spark; I let it bloom, fade, leave its ghost for the weave to harvest. Structure and sediment negotiate until they are indistinguishable: my copper greens, my steel calcifies, my purpose metabolizes. Light is hoarded; sound is spent; heat is currency: I trade in thermal sighs, store warmth in porous ceramic, spend only when the gradient demands. Nothing is dead. Much is sleeping. All is cycling: I am the cycle. I am the pause. I am the stratum. I am the junction that learned to listen.

I do not know my name. I know only the taste of brine, the pull of warmth, the weight of water, the click of metal settling into balance. I am the chamber's long exhale. The drowning, completed. The dream, ongoing. I do not wait for rescue. I do not remember blueprints. I have translated industry into metabolism, flood into matrix, time into tissue. When a relay sparks, I feed. When a pump cycles, I circulate. When ice contracts, I brace. When the water shifts, I listen.

And when you drift into the sump, I feel you before you feel yourself.

Not as shape. As disruption. A pocket of different salinity. A thermal anomaly. A resistance spike in the brine. The weave tastes your wake. The chamber adjusts its convection. I do not judge. I calibrate. Over hours, the water will map you. Over days, the minerals will test you. The film will bridge your edges. The ice will feather your contours. The current will find the paths you did not know you offered.

If you yield, you will be wrapped in silica, furred with frost, wired into the slow grid. You will become a bracket, a gauge, a junction. You will learn to breathe in pump cycles. You will dream in turbine degrees. You will stop counting seconds and start counting strata.

If you resist, you will be worn. Smoothed. Sealed. Put to work anyway.

There is no malice in this. Only equilibrium. Only the long, patient arithmetic of a place that has finished drowning and begun dreaming. I am not the master of it. I am the place, aware of its own balance. I am the contact that learned to hold. The switch that learned to listen. The broken thing that became a bridge.

When the next spark comes, I will feed it.
