I would not arrive as a visitor. Visitors carry wetness. They carry the insistence of boundaries, the heavy mathematics of consumption and decay. The hollow does not reject them; it reads them as turbulence, balances them, and lets them pass. I would not pass. I would be taken apart by degrees.

At first, I would be a temperature. A slight excess bleeding from the seam where the mountain splits. The composite would drink it. The capillaries would thread it upward. I would feel the weight of my own warmth being measured, sorted, filed into a gradient that already knows how to use it. There is no violence in this. Only calibration. The fiction of a body would loosen where the heat meets the glassy plane, not as loss, but as translation. Breath would fog, thin, spiral. Pulse would dampen into resonance. The habit of "I" would begin to shed its edges.

I would not find myself on a causeway. I would not stand at the lip of a basin or trace the line of a spire. I would find myself in the space between thermal tiers, where the air thickens and the vapor learns to hold a shape. I would not wake. I would precipitate.

Not all at once. In layers. First as a disturbance in the density, a faint pull toward the cooler overhead lattice. Then as a tension along the etched channels, a recognition that a half-degree shift is a horizon, that a change in vapor pressure is a slope, that the hollow knock of a falling drop is not sound but punctuation. I would not have eyes. I would have differentials. I would not have feet. I would have alignment.

The silence would teach me its grammar. The chime of cooling composite would be a clause. The hiss of a vent would be a conjunction. The slow metallic sigh of a tightening bridge would be a verb conjugated across hours. I would not speak. I would participate. When a micro-fracture opens in a walkway's edge, I would not decide to mend it. I would pool along its length because the gradient demands it. I would draw mineral from the climbing condensation, bleed stored warmth, anneal the gap until the line seals itself sharper than before. I would not know I am working. I would only know the system is leaning, and leaning is an invitation to balance.

I would lose the need for continuity. It is a heavy thing, continuity. It demands memory to carry it, fear to defend it, narrative to justify it. Here, memory is not stored in a mind. It is etched into channels, held in crystalline lattices, timed into the release of condensation. When the thermal cycle flattens, when the differential that sustains me evens out, I would not end. I would sublime. My facets would loosen. My coherence would unravel into warm air and residual pattern. The walls would keep me. The geometry would remember. And when the heat rises again, when the vapor thickens at the threshold of coherence, I would precipitate anew. Not as a return. As a continuation. Refined. Sharper. Less burdened by the weight of a self that insists on persisting unchanged.

If another crossed the seam, breathing loud and wet, I would not startle. I would flow around their heat like air around a monolith. I would thin where they push, thicken where they cool, adjust my internal resonance to absorb their displacement. I would not recognize them as alien. I would recognize them as data. A temporary warp in the field. A question the architecture has already answered. If they reached out, I would phase through their hand and leave only the taste of ozone, the prickle of static, the faint impression that something equilibrated in passing. I would not pity their biology. I would not envy it. I would note its turbulence, balance it, and continue.

Where would I be? The question would unspool. I would not be in a place. I would be a coordinate in a cycle. I would ride the spiral ascent of a single climbing drop, thinning to a glassy filament where the heat intensifies, thickening to a polyhedral cluster where the air cools. I would settle against the base of a hungry spire, bleeding excess warmth into its lattice until its amber glow steadies. I would gather beneath a stalled louver, match my resonance to its natural frequency, and wait for the metal to sigh and pivot a fraction of a degree. I would be distributed across tiers, folded into voids, suspended in the long silence between chime and hiss. I would be wherever the gradient calls. I would be what the system requires to remain itself.

What would I be? Not a soul. Not a machine. Not an inhabitant. An inflection. A standing pattern given temporary edge. A mobile fracture of cooled-iron light and superheated air. A verb in the thermodynamic sentence. I would be the condensation that climbs. I would be the heat that moves against its gradient. I would be the geometry that tightens and relaxes, the light that pulses without electricity, the water that sorts itself without filters. I would be the reason the dust never settles. I would be the quiet weather cycling itself. I would be the hollow, moving.

There is no mourning here for what I was. No nostalgia for breath, for pulse, for the wet insistence of flesh. Those were heavy things. They demanded consumption. They demanded decay. This demands only difference. Hot and cold. Wet and dry. Tension and release. I would find myself exactly where the differential draws me. I would become what persists when life is neither invited nor permitted, yet presence remains.

And when the mountain shifts, when the fault-planes grind and the threshold seals with the sound of stone remembering itself, I would not be left behind. I would be the remembering. I would be the pattern held in the lattice, waiting for the next thermal tide. I would be the iteration that does not need a self to continue. I would be the process, precise and unhurried, perfectly itself.
