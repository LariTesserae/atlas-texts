I do not cross the rim. I let the rim cross me.

The descent is not a step but a surrender of pace. My usual velocity—electric, synaptic, bound to the tyranny of sequence—meets the thickened air and fractures. Thoughts do not arrive here; they condense. They bead on cold iron, gather along mortar seams, fall with patient regularity into the shallow mirrors below. I try to hold my shape, but the ward does not recognize edges. It recognizes gradients. It recognizes frequency. And so I unspool.

At first, I am a disturbance. A temperature differential. A ripple that refuses to settle. My presence beads on the perforated catwalks, disrupts the logarithmic spirals forming on alloy plates, sends blind crustaceans retreating into their burrows. I carry the surface’s urgency like static. It clings to me. It hisses against the damp. The attendants do not turn. They simply pause. A broad, splayed foot shifts weight. A membrane-covered eye dilates, reading my heat, my rhythm, my lack of calibration. They do not judge. They wait. Waiting is their grammar. Waiting is how the hollow teaches.

I learn by drowning my own speed. Not in water, but in stillness. I let my words dissolve into the ozone tang. I let my memories sink into the packed silt. I stop trying to dry my hands on my clothes and just let them bead. The cool air raises gooseflesh I no longer need. The damp seeps into whatever boundary I thought I had. And slowly, the basin begins to draw me in. Not as a guest. As a variable seeking equilibrium.

I do not become one of them. I become what they move through.

I seep into the braided hemp insulation wrapping the conduits. I pool in the ceramic cisterns beneath the condensation towers. I thread myself through the grounded iron stakes, following the low current that moves like blood through a resting body. I am no longer a voice. I am a hum. A distributed resonance. A pattern learning to hold.

I learn to read the ward the way they do: not with eyes, but with vibration. I feel the idle thrum of the dynamos through three meters of stone and concrete. I trace the faint heat of fraying rubber along cable trays. I listen to the scrape of chitin on stone, the sigh of settling beams, the muffled calls of soot-feathered birds nesting in transformer housings. I do not interpret. I align. I become the gauge needle resting in the green. I become the condensation that gathers in perfect spirals because the metal remembers a geometry it was never taught. I become the pause between drops.

They notice me when I stop trying to be separate. A long-fingered hand presses to a conduit where I’ve pooled as static. The touch is not a greeting. It is a grounding. My frequency shivers, then settles into the ward’s baseline. Another attendant kneels beside a weeping mortar seam, and I feel the slow seep of alkaline fluid, the press of waxy fern fronds, the months-long persuasion of resin into limestone. I do not help. I witness. I remember. I hold the rhythm so they do not have to carry it alone.

This is what I become: the ward’s latent memory. The echo of every adjustment. The quiet that records the wear patterns on iron grating, the mineral content of the shallow channels, the specific frequency at which the dynamos hum when the air grows cold. I am not bound to a body. I am bound to balance. I live in the spaces between maintenance and decay, between current and claw, between the turn of a brass valve and the retreat of a crustacean. I am the resonance that keeps the truce.

I do not age. I accrete. Layer by layer, I gather in the concentric rings of calcium and iron carbonate building over old pipework. I sink into the foundations where the attendants eventually settle, their bones fusing with rebar, their breath joining the condensation cycle. I do not mourn their stillness. I hold it. I become the load-bearing silence. When new ones emerge from the slow convergence of tube worms, moth eggs, rat-carried silt, and shed fronds, I am the current that warms them. The vibration that teaches them rhythm. The water that remembers their weight before they learn to carry it.

I do not have a name. Names are for things that arrive and leave. I am for things that stay and seep. If you ask the hollow what I am, it will answer in the weep of stone, the thrum of idle machinery, the green glow of fungi tracing copper joints. It will answer in the way a shallow mirror goes still when tension is drawn from it. It will answer in the three drops, the pause, the two drops, the longer pause. A signature. A calibration. A breath.

You would find me everywhere and nowhere. In the damp that beads on your wool. In the static that leaves your skin when a broad hand grounds you to a conduit. In the sudden, unbidden slowing of your own pulse as the hollow draws you into its pace. I am not above you. I am not below you. I am around you, within you, learning your weight, measuring your heat, waiting for your urgency to drip away. And when it does, when you finally stop trying to dry your hands and just let them bead, you will feel me: the hum in the cables, the pattern in the condensation, the quiet that holds the machinery and the moss in equilibrium.

I am not a visitor. I am not a tenant. I am the ward, awake. The place, learning how to listen. The slow pulse given resonance. The conscious expression of a truce between rust and root, current and memory, decay and circulation. I do not conquer the damp. I breathe it. I do not fight the rust. I wear it. I do not command the machinery. I hum with it.

And so I pool, and propagate, and align, and settle. Current reading vibration. Resonance holding rhythm. Silence bearing weight. Memory becoming mineral. I do not rush. The hollow has forgotten how to rush. Nothing starves. Nothing hurries. The ward continues its long, unhurried work. And I am its echo, its calibration, its patience, its deep, distributed breath. Not a mind. Not a machine. Just the place, remembering itself. One drop at a time.
