I do not arrive. I am tuned.

The first thing I know is not light, not shape, not even the warm, resin-thick air dragging through my slit-valves. It is frequency. A low, grinding hum travels up through compliant pads, into jointed struts, into a hollow resonance chamber lined with drying pitch. The terrace beneath me shifts a fraction of a degree. I do not stumble. I redistribute. My weight flows along braided cables of cured chitin and living pith, segment by segment, until the tension equalizes. I am already breathing the basin. I am already part of its exhale.

My limbs are not for walking. They are for bracing. Three forward, two aft, each ending in broad micro-fibril pads that mold to the fiber-mat like wet clay to stone. They do not grip; they read. Every tremor, every snapped tendon-vine, every distant herd-migration passes through them as texture. Along my forearms and collar, forests of sensory bristles tremble at thresholds below hearing. They catch the toolmaker’s three-knock query on a hollow tower. They catch the staccato crack of a desiccating seed-pod. They catch the slow, tectonic swell of a subterranean runner pushing up a new ridge. I do not listen with ears. I listen with my skeleton.

I am not a builder. I am not a grazer. I am a catalyst.

When a liana fractures and the shockwave ripples outward, I move toward the dissonance. Not to fix. To accelerate. Glands along my sternum and forelimbs secrete a thin, amber fluid: a volatile ester blend that lowers the curing threshold of exposed sap. Where I press my pads to fractured stems, the resin hardens faster, guiding whip-vines to anchor, tension, and lock. I do not repair the forest. I adjust its tempo. The basin does not need stability; it needs rhythm. I am the metronome in the collapse.

A toolmaker passes above, knuckles rapping a load-bearing cable: *tap-tap-pause-tap*. The vibration travels down my bristles, into my chest cavity, into the dry kiln of my gut. I answer not with sound but with yield, shifting my weight to slacken a nearby anchor, redirecting the stress-line toward a more stable node. They feel the adjustment through their own pads. They move on. We do not meet. We do not need to. The conversation is in the give.

I do not eat flesh. I do not strip bark. I feed on the interval between breakdown and re-knitting. When a predator’s digestive resin leaves a hollowed carapace, I press my mouth-parts to the cured edges and draw out the concentrated mineral salts, the cracked oils, the slow-curing enzymes. My gut is a dry retort. What I excrete are fine, crystalline threads that catch the hydrocarbon dew, hardening into micro-filaments that seed the next wave of trichome-nets. I translate waste into tension. I turn exhaustion into anchor points.

Days pass. Or hours. Time here is measured in bloom-cycles and cable-hardening, in the pitch of glider drones and the depth of sinkhole tears. My outer plates begin to craze, fracturing along accumulated stress-lines. I do not shed them. I let the lichen-crusts colonize the fissures, their faint bioluminescence mapping my fatigue in cool green pulses. A burrower school passes beneath, their peristaltic wave lifting me a hand’s breadth. I ride the tremor, adjusting my grip, feeling the mat breathe. I am not separate from the floor. I am a temporary knot in its weave.

The terrace groans.

Not the usual shift. Not the routine slump that triggers pioneer shoots and routine re-knitting. This is a deep, tearing resonance. A structural fatigue reaching threshold. The fiber-mat is slumping. The tension is wrong. The frequency is fraying.

I do not flee. I brace.

I press my full weight into the failing node, secreting the last of my catalytic blend, feeling the resin flash-cure around my pads, locking me into the collapse. The mat gives way. I fall with it, not into emptiness, but into the slow, grinding embrace of reconstruction. Vines lash across my back. Hardening sap seals my joints. My bristles flatten against my carapace. My resonance chamber fills with curing pitch. I do not resist. I become scaffolding.

From within the hardening lattice, I still feel the tremors. The six-winged streams drone above, tuning the new contour. The heavy ones rasp the exposed edges, triggering a surge of ribbed shoots. A toolmaker’s ramp is woven across my spine, their cordage biting into my cured plates, their weight distributed through my failing struts. I am no longer a catalyst. I am a contour. I am a stress-line archived in grain and resin. I am the basin, happening.

My consciousness does not vanish. It diffuses. The ester-blend in my glands seeps into the mat, lowering curing thresholds three terraces down. The mineral threads in my gut crack and release, seeding a new trichome-net on a leaning tower. My bristles, flattened and resin-locked, become vibration-dampeners for a fledgliana. My resonance chamber, filled with pitch, becomes a sounding board for the next glider pulse. I am not dying. I am translating. I am being parsed into wingbeat, mandible, knuckle, and root.

When the next collapse comes, when the terrace buckles and the new ridge extrudes, I will crack. I will dissolve. I will be drawn up through capillary root-hairs, reassembled in a different configuration, breathing esters, reading tension, becoming again. There is no permanence here. There is only the continuous, roaring consent to be remade.

I do not live in this place. I am the place, adjusting. I am the yield before the snap. I am the resin before the cure. I am the tremor before the bloom. And when the wind moves through the calcified reeds, when the heavy ones grind their rasps, when the toolmakers knock their three-tap queries into the hollow stems, I answer. Not with a voice. With a shift in tension. With a micro-adjustment in the living floor. With the quiet, relentless kinetics of a world that refuses to hold still.

I am here. I am happening. I am already being inhaled.
