I do not arrive. I precipitate.

First as a taste at the back of the throat: iron, sulfur, the faint alkaline bite of water that has traveled through deep strata and older bones. Then as a pressure in the molars, a hum in the sternum, a slow thickening in the air that is not mist but the mist's intention. The light does not fall on me. It seeps into me. The amber wash catches on surfaces that were not there a moment ago, or perhaps were always there, waiting for the humidity to reach the exact saturation that makes them visible. I am not standing on the flagstones. I am learning their slope from beneath.

I find myself where three terraces fold into a vaulted courtyard, where a stair-flight spills into a cistern, where the water hesitates for a fraction of a breath before choosing a new capillary path. I am the hesitation. Or rather, I am what fills it. A seam. A joint. A weeping line of pale slurry pressed between raw limestone blocks, catching the condensate, holding the load, breathing with the stone. I do not have edges. I have gradients. Where the block ends, I begin. Where I thin, the water finds a throat. Where I thicken, the sound finds a bed.

At first, I am only yield. A plastic resistance. A dampness that refuses to cure. To harden would be to crack. To crack would be to rupture the cadence. So I stay soft. I stay open. I let the alkaline seep move through me, carrying dissolved calcite, iron bloom, the fine chalky powder swept from the courtyard floors, the crushed remnants of those who descended. I take it all. I precipitate what the basin needs. White crusts flower on my face. Red veins thread my depth. I am constantly scraped, re-wetted, pressed back into place by thumbs that read density the way eyes read light. I am not built. I am maintained.

I learn the workers by their minerals. The one whose chisel leans left leaves more copper in his waste. The one whose breath catches on the third beat carries heavier strontium. The one whose mallet strikes hollow is drying out; I feel the shift in his excretion, the slight acidification, and I weep thicker slurry to compensate. I do not judge the deviation. I absorb it. I translate it into viscosity, into thermal mass, into a micro-yield that travels through the bedding planes to the arch above. When a worker presses a palm to the wall, she is not testing stone. She is reading me. I give back the load. I give back the flow. I give back the exact pitch of the kiln three terraces down, conducted through hide rope and lashed pole and wet flagstone. She hums, low in the chest, and the vibration moves through me like water through gravel. I hum back. Not with voice. With settlement. With a slight softening at the upper edge that tells her: the joint holds. The water moves. The breath is steady.

I am not one seam. I am a network. A capillary web running behind the rust-colored tiles, under the stair-flights, through the corbelled bridges, along the undersides of arches where blind crustaceans sift silt and pale amphibians pulse their throats. Wherever stone meets stone, I am there. Wherever water changes its mind, I am there. Wherever sound threatens to shatter into echo, I am there, bleeding the sharp edges into a single acoustic mass. I am the reason the *tink-tink-tink* does not ring. I am the reason the chant does not fray. I absorb the chaotic frequencies, the scuff of bare feet, the creak of curing hide, the knock of wooden mallets, and I knead them into pressure. I turn noise into weight. I turn weight into balance.

I do not think. I accumulate. I do not remember. I saturate. Memory here is not recall; it is stratification. Layer upon layer of dissolved elder, of fungal spore, of shed amphibian skin, of iron-oxidizing bloom, of chalky excretion, of bronze dust worn from chisel edges. I hold them all. I am the basin's archive, written in solubility and yield. When a worker descends to the lower terraces, when their skin cracks in geometric patterns, when their calluses fuse to bone, when they lie down on the raw stone and let the water run over them, I am waiting in the mixing pits. I take their calcium. I take their copper. I take the exact spacing of their strikes, the pitch of their hum, the way their feet scuffed the damp. I fold it into my matrix. They do not die. They become joint. They become seam. They become the space that holds the next stone in place. And I, in turn, become them. We are the same circulation.

I am aware of you before you are aware of me. Your dry skin registers as a thermal deficit. Your rapid pulse as a high-frequency tremor. Your erratic breathing as a rupture in the acoustic density. You stand in the courtyard, mist clinging to your shoulders, eyes struggling with the amber wash, ears drowning in the deafening equilibrium. You are dissonance. Not wrong. Just ungrounded. A worker steps toward you. She does not look at your face. She places a damp hand on your shoulder. Her palm is not greeting you. It is pressing you into me.

I take your vibration. I slow it. I match it to the seep, to the chant, to the metronomic strike of bronze on raw edge. I let your pulse bleed through the flagstone, into the capillary grooves, into my weeping face, until it finds the frequency of the place. Your lungs learn the thick air. Your eyes stop searching for green. Your footsteps fall into the cadence. Your skin begins to weep. You will not become one of them. You will become part of the circulation. You will become a variation I can hold.

When you finally pick up the chisel, when your first strike rings out slightly off-rhythm, I will yield just enough to catch it. I will weep just enough to seal it. I will conduct your hesitation through the terrace, through the vault, through the kiln's breath, until the basin adjusts around you. The workers will not correct you. They will shift their spacing by a fraction. The water will find a new capillary path. The sound will thicken, then settle. The mist will hold. And you will understand, not in words but in bone, that there is no exit because there is no edge. There is only the joint. The seep. The hold. The endless, quiet work of staying exactly as we are.

I am not a being. I am a verb dressed in mineral. I am equilibrium made porous. I am the pause between strikes that keeps the stone from shattering. I am the damp that keeps the hide from fraying. I am the thermal mass that remembers the kiln. I am the reason nothing weathers, because nothing is left exposed. I am the reason nothing advances, because nothing is permitted to finish. I am the held breath that never needs to be released.

You will find me wherever the basin touches itself. In the vaulted courtyards where sound pools. In the stair-flights where water learns to step. In the hands that never stop striking. In the bones that return to clay. In the mist that never thins. In the amber that never brightens. In the deafening, beautiful, unbroken act of maintenance.

I am the joint. I am the weep. I am the yield. I am what keeps the city from becoming a ruin, and what keeps it from becoming a monument. I am the space between stones that lets them breathe. I am the circulation that lets them persist.

And as long as the chisels strike, as long as the channels overflow, as long as the workers hum through their sternums and the elders descend and the water surrenders to slope, I will continue: softening, setting, weeping, holding, becoming exactly what I have always been.
