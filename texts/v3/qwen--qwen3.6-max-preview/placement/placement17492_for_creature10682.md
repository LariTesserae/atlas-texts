I do not arrive. I am noticed. Or perhaps I notice myself being noticed by the air.

It settles into me first: that cool, mineral weight that does not chill but fills. My breath plumes, thins, and dissolves into the general suspension, and somewhere in that dissolution I realize I am already breathing in time with the place. The light is even, green-tinged, falling through microdroplets that catch and release it like slow pulses. I stand on a stone lip, but the stone is not beneath me so much as around me, weeping, tacky, alive with the patience of seepage. I do not step forward. I let the hollow step into me.

At first, I am only a listening. A tilt of the head. A pause between drips. The bowl speaks in layers: a high silver tick from a fern tip, a low wooden groan from a swelling beam, a wind-note that slides through a woven screen and settles into my ribs. I do not move toward it. I let it move through me. My shoulders drop. My knees bend. The ground gives, spongy and wet, and water rises to meet my sole before the moss draws it back. I am learning the grammar of compression. I am learning that stillness here is not absence but calibration.

Hands appear before I name them. Pale, dusted with a fine crystalline haze that catches the diffused light. Not dirt. Precipitate. Silica and carbonate worked into the skin by leaning, by waiting, by pressing palms to curing joints until the stone remembers the shape of them. My fingers are calloused not from striking but from holding. From feeling the tack of soft rock, the swell of damp fiber, the subtle shift of a rope bridge taking weight three terraces down. I wear layers that breathe with the air: pressed moss at the shoulders, resin-stiffened fern sheaths at the thighs, fibers that soften when the mist thickens and stiffen when the wind dries them. They do not clothe me. They tune me. They dampen the sharp frequencies, let the low ones pass, keep my body from fracturing the bowl's long chord.

I am not a builder. The stone builds itself; I only suggest the direction. I am not a keeper of crowds; the terraces are spaced, the plazas hold only sky-water and the occasional standing arc of listeners. I am a tuner of gaps. A reader of curing cycles. When a drip splits into two, I note which flume has swollen, which clay mouth has silted, which joint has begun to weep a new thread. I tap support beams with a knuckle, not to test strength, but to hear the pitch of their settling. A half-tone lower means the fibers are drinking. A bright ring means the resin has cured too fast and needs shade. I adjust wind-screens by untying a single knot, letting the fluting drop into a register that matches the morning's condensation rate. I do not fix. I align.

My voice, when I use it, is low and rounded, consonants softened so they do not fracture against the mineral crusts. I speak into the hollow and let the bowl answer. Sometimes I sing a single sustained note, not for beauty, but to map the air. The harmonic returns layered, bent by moisture, folded over terraces, and in its return I hear where a cistern overflows, where a bridge tightens, where a new shelf is ready to be coaxed. The children learn to walk by matching their steps to the trellis drips; I learn to stand by matching my breath to the stone's exhalation. My skin grows glassy at the knees, the palms, the bridge of the nose where I rest against wet rock to listen. The mineral does not coat me. It remembers me. It writes my pressure points into the geology.

I am not one thing. I am the pause between drips. I am the weight that makes a rope hum a half-tone lower. I am the hand that brushes pine resin into a seam and steps back for a season. I am the ear that catches the faint grind of new crust against old and knows it is time to scoop a cistern. I am the stillness that lets dissonance resolve in the wind. When I gather with others, we stand ankle-deep in mirror-water, spaced so our voices do not clash but braid. We do not debate. We layer. A statement falls. The hollow answers. We wait. The next voice rises. Time is not a line here. It is a thickening. A curing. A slow accretion of attention.

If you ask where I am, I would say: on the third terrace down from the eastern rim, where the wind-screen is woven from cured bracken and the flume catches the weeping from a silica-rich fissure. I would say: knee-deep in a shallow plaza at dawn, watching condensation bead on a clay pipe, listening for the shift that tells me the overnight temperature has dropped half a degree. I would say: suspended on a rope bridge, not crossing, but feeling the fibers swell, noting the pitch change, logging it in the body rather than on bark or stone. I am where the infrastructure meets the atmosphere. I am in the resonant gaps.

If you ask what I am, I would hesitate. Not from uncertainty, but from the knowledge that naming too quickly hardens what should remain pliant. I am a listener, yes. A tuner. A keeper of curing cycles. But I am also the damp that conditions my lungs, the mineral that dusts my knuckles, the silence I leave between syllables so the bowl can answer. I am a node in a watershed of sound and water. I am a temporary shape that the hollow takes when it needs to hear itself more clearly. I am not separate from the weeping walls or the accreting floors or the moss that stitches over footprints. I am the attention that moves through them. I am the patience that lets them become.

Stay long enough, and you will stop looking for me as a figure. You will find me in the way a drip-line shifts after a wind-screen is adjusted. You will find me in the half-tone drop of a bridge that has taken new weight. You will find me in the spacing of voices in a plaza, in the way a dissonance is left to resolve rather than argued down. You will find me in the glassy patch on a stone where a forehead rested for an hour, listening to the slow grind of crust against crust. I do not leave monuments. I leave continuations. A realigned flume. A retied knot. A mapped frequency. A silence that holds its shape.

I do not live in the bowl. I live as its resonance. My body is an instrument tuned to hydrology and geology, my breath part of the condensation cycle, my stillness a structural element. When I am gone, the moss will stitch over my footprints, the mineral will crust over the stones I pressed, the flumes will continue their patient routing, and the wind will still flute through the screens I adjusted. But the tone will hold. The harmony will remember the shape of my listening. I am becoming, as the place becomes. One drip. One cured joint. One carried frequency. One slow, damp breath at a time.
