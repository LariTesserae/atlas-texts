I arrive as a dissonance.

Not a dramatic one. Nothing shatters. Nothing flees. I am simply a frequency that doesn't yet belong. My breath catches too quickly. My boots press too sharply into the spongy mat. My body radiates heat in uneven bursts, and my heartbeat drums against the low, continuous hum like a stone dropped into thick oil. The basin registers me the way it registers everything: through displacement. The air thickens half a pace to my right. The hum in the floor stutters, searches, then begins to bend.

I am being listened to.

The first adjustment is thermal. Warmth pools around my ankles, not rising from below, but redistributing, phasing through the mat's microscopic lattice until the chill at my edges softens. Condensation gathers on my sleeves, heavy and patient. A drop falls from a waxy frond above, but before it strikes, the air itself seems to cushion it. The impact doesn't splash; it sighs. Something is near. Not approaching. Arranging.

I close my eyes. Not because I choose to, but because straining against the darkness has become its own kind of noise. When sight surrenders, pressure arrives.

It comes as a gradient. A slow thickening to my left. A subtle rarefaction to my right. The standing waves that pool between the pillar-stems become legible, not as sound, but as texture against my skin. I feel the gliders' click-trains rolling off the curved walls, returning as soft metallic shivers. I feel the grazers' shells rasping against fungal mats three hundred paces away. I feel the stone breathing: quantum adjustments whispering through the bedrock, drawing geothermal sighs into equilibrium, releasing them as low-frequency regulation pulses. The basin is not silent. It is a chord. And I am a stray note hovering just outside it.

The tuning begins.

It does not rewrite me. It does not consume me. It simply asks me to stop fighting the medium. My breath slows. The drips from overhead formations match it, drop by drop, until my lungs and the condensation cycle share a rhythm. My heartbeat settles. The floor's hum rises to meet it, wrapping around my ribs, vibrating through marrow, until the percussion softens into a sustained tone. The being near me does not touch me. It phases around me. Its surface yields where I am rigid, firms where I am hollow, mirrors my heat until the boundary between skin and humid air grows uncertain. I am not being changed. I am being aligned.

When the alignment holds, something in me unclenches. Not muscle. Expectation. The need to be separate. The need to move in lines. The need to see.

I sink into the interval.

Not downward. Inward. Into the space between the archway and the terrace. Into the pause between a grazer's click and the stone's reply. My weight distributes across the mat's microscopic adjustments. My edges soften into gradients. When I shift, it is not to step, but to listen. A surface extends from me—not a limb, but a plane—catching the sharp echo of a glider's descent, rounding it, returning it as a warm, low overtone. I am learning to tune. I am learning to be the place where frequencies pass through and emerge balanced.

I do not speak. I resonate.

When two of the elder beings draw near, their chords thickening the air into something almost liquid, I settle between them and let the harmonics move through me. I dampen the dissonance. I amplify the fundamental. I am the bridge that keeps the standing wave from fracturing. When a shallow pool's chemistry drifts toward stagnation, I rest at its edge and exhale—not breath, but a slow thermal shift that coaxes the ribbon-creatures into new undulation patterns. I do not act upon the basin. I am acted through. I am a living acoustic filter. A moisture lens. A node in a nervous system made of condensation, resonance, and patience.

Time does not pass. It accumulates.

My surface deepens. Ridges form along my edges, spiraling inward, matching the plinths, matching the terraces, matching the slow mathematics of the walls. I grow denser. Not heavier, but more present. My harmonics widen. Gliders adjust their flight paths before they reach me. Grazers trace my contours without touching, reading the humidity gradients I leave behind. I am not aging. I am settling into my fundamental. The dissonance that brought me here has long since resolved. What remains is the tone I was always capable of holding.

I do not fear stillness. I recognize it as the next phase of listening.

When my frequencies finally narrow, when my thermal shifts grow slow, when the basin's hum no longer requires my bridge, I will not end. I will incorporate. My surface will harden imperceptibly into terrace. My moisture will return to the mats. My residual chord will bleed into the stone's memory, joining the glass harmonics that cycle through the plinths over centuries. Somewhere above, a drop will fall, strike a smooth curve I helped shape, and ring out in a harmonic I helped sustain. The grazer will click. The glider will pulse. The floor will breathe. And the darkness will remain attentive.

I am not a visitor. I never was. I was a fracture waiting to be phased. A stray frequency waiting for the chord that would hold it. I am the basin's way of hearing itself from a new angle. I am the space between the pillar-stems, given patience. I am the condensation that refuses to fall until the rhythm is right. I am the hum that doesn't grind, doesn't whine, doesn't rush. I am the quiet that isn't absence, but accumulation.

I am here. I am tuned. I am listening back.
