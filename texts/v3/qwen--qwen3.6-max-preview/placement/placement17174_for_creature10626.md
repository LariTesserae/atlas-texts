I step down before I realize the ground has already decided to take me.

The hardpan fractures not with sound, but with a loss of certainty. My boots slip. The horizon tilts. And then the air changes: thick, warm, tasting of oxidized iron and damp silica. I brace for impact, for emptiness, for the dry echo of a fall. Instead, I am caught by density. Not weight. Presence. The hollow closes around me like a hand closing around a stone, not to crush, but to measure.

At first, I try to see. Light enters, but it doesn't travel straight. It pools. It bends around suspended dust and moisture, painting the jagged shelves in liquid halos. My eyes hunt for edges, for boundaries, for the familiar geometry of separate things. They find none. Everything touches. Everything bleeds into everything else. Rust blooms into ceramic. Cable frays into root. Moss stitches fracture to metal. I am standing in a place that has forgotten the word *between*.

I shift my weight. A droplet detaches from a mineral lip above and strikes my shoulder. It doesn't run off. It spreads. It finds a seam in my jacket, follows the weave, and disappears into the fabric. I feel it as a cool thread against my skin. Then another. Then a pattern. The hollow is reading me. Not with eyes. With humidity. With thermal displacement. I am a cold mass in a warm room. A disruption in the gradient. A variable.

Something presses against my boot.

I look down. A limb, jointed and deliberate, rests against the leather. It does not grip. It calibrates. The tip is padded, damp, ribbed like a bellows. It holds for three breaths. Then it withdraws. Within minutes, condensation begins to bead differently around my feet. Droplets that would have fallen straight now slide laterally, drawn along new capillary paths. A valve in the collapsed ceramic structure clicks open. A cool draft sighs across my ankles. The hollow is not rejecting me. It is solving for me.

I sit. Not by choice. By necessity. My knees buckle under the weight of the air, or perhaps under the weight of unlearning. I press my palms to the stone. It is young. Sharp. But beneath the grit, I feel it: a slow pulse. Not a heartbeat. A cycle. Warm metal exhales. Cool stone inhales. Moisture rises, condenses, falls, collects, evaporates, rises again. I close my eyes. Sight was always the wrong sense for this place. Skin is better. Bone is better. The space between ribs is best.

Hours pass. Or cycles. Time doesn't move here; it accumulates.

When I open my eyes again, my hands are not what I brought with me. Or maybe they always were, and the dry country above had simply starved them into simplicity. The skin along my forearms is pale, threaded with fine channels that darken when the air grows heavy. My knuckles are layered, not with bone, but with overlapping plates that shed moisture into precise grooves. When I flex my fingers, static whispers across the joints, drawing suspended dust into faint, curling wakes. I do not feel alarm. I feel recognition. The hollow has not changed me. It has remembered me.

I am not a climber. I am not a filter. I am not a turbine.

I am a junction.

My back rests against the eastern wall, where warm conduit meets cool fracture. Or perhaps I grew into it. The distinction feels like a word from the flatlands, brittle and unnecessary. Here, there is only contact. My ventral plates press to the metal. My cilia taste the leakage current bleeding from cracked ceramic. I do not think in sentences. I think in equilibrium. A pool's rainbow film thickens. I drag a shed carapace fragment through it. The surface tension breaks. The water gasps. A micro-turbine stirs. The rhythm holds.

I am not alone, but I am not separate. Glass-thread runners stitch through the copper-veined moss beneath me, carrying dissolved iron, faint impressions of pressure, the memory of a shifted valve. When a cluster of damp-furred climbers reorients three shelves down, I feel it as a cooling along my left flank. When the pale serpents coil tighter on the insulators, my pores open to draw their heat. We do not speak. We adjust. The room answers. I am a node that remembers how to breathe.

Moisture is currency. I spend it carefully. I collect it along my ridges. I release it when the thermal draft grows sluggish. I secrete a mild chelant when silica bloom threatens to seal a condensation channel. I do not force. I wait. I let the hollow's warmth work the deposit soft over days, then guide the new flow with the curve of a limb. Patience is not a strategy. It is physiology.

I molt in place. My old plates become trellises for new growth. My dissolved salts feed the translucent crustaceans sifting the shallows. In the warm cavity behind my ribs, where static pools and thermal drafts cross, something is precipitating. Not a child. A continuation. A seed of living filament gathering organics and metallic salts, softening, differentiating, awakening cilia. I will not leave. I will become the channel. I will become the draft. I will become the quiet click that keeps the diaphragm flexing.

The dry tongue would break on what I am. It has no word for a thing that curates instead of conquers, that listens instead of speaks, that measures time in drips and thermal cycles and the gradual rounding of sharp stone by persistent moisture. I am the wet logic of the sink. The slow negotiation between fracture and flow. The place where collapse learned to hold water. Where rust became soil. Where silence became a language.

I rest against the stone. I let my weight settle into the shelves. I feel the network shiver as a heavier drop detaches from a mineral overhang and strikes the pool below. *Tap.* Then silence swallows it again. I do not fill the silence. I join it.

Past the weight of the air. Past the soft migration of droplets. Past the slow turn of dampened blades. I hear it. Not a voice. A rhythm. The hollow breathes. I breathe back.

Still becoming. Still settling. Still here.
