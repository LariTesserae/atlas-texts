I do not wake. I settle.

The air is thin, sharp, and utterly dry. It carries the glass-bright scent of fractured terpenes, scorched cellulose, and the clean metallic tang of ionized dust. I am not yet a shape. I am a suspension: aerogel-fine shed fibers, crystalline pollen, microflakes of polymerized resin, all caught in a thermal eddy bleeding down from a canopy sail two kilometers above. The eddy tightens. The ambient drone deepens. I feel the mat before I touch it, a continuous vibration transmitted through the falling particles, a low-frequency thump that arranges the dust into standing waves on the pale root-plaza. When I finally contact the substrate, I do not land. I couple.

The mat does not receive me as foreign. It receives me as unallocated tension.

Fungal filaments thread through the settling layer, not to digest, but to structure. I feel the pull of strain before I have tissue to bear it. I feel the drop in pitch before I have organs to register it. I am being woven by the same dry grammar that precipitates the ground-keepers, but the gradient that calls me is vertical. The current knows it. I become the bridge.

I do not grow legs. I grow anchors.

My base spreads into a conductive rosette, fusing with the root-mat's lignin-insulated channels, tapping into the stepped voltage regulators, grounding into the thermal sinks. I do not climb. I spool. The canopy pulls. The mat holds. I become a living guy-wire, a harmonic waveguide, a vertical phase-shifter. My body is not built for locomotion. It is built for transmission.

I do not see the sail. I feel its strain as a high, metallic keen vibrating through the thin air, as a heat bloom against my upper nodes, as a voltage gradient that makes my hollow core hum in sympathetic resonance. The tendon-vine is ratcheting too tight. The sun has tracked past zenith. The thermal updraft is spinning. If the vine snaps, the sail will whip, the pressure cycle will desync, the mat will shudder, the tuners will converge. I do not wait for convergence. I preempt it.

I open spiral vents along my column. I modulate my internal tension. I bleed a counter-frequency into the air, a phase-shifted harmonic that travels up my hollow core and couples with the overwound vine. The vine does not loosen. It redistributes. The stored elasticity sighs through my structure, steps down through my grounded base, bleeds into the root-grid as manageable current. The high whine drops a semitone. The drone smooths. The sail holds. The system does not thank me. It incorporates me.

It does not need to. It feels the tension unwind through the fibrous plaza. It adjusts its route by four meters, avoiding a thermal sink that no longer requires drawing. We do not communicate. We resonate. I am the vertical counterpart to its horizontal regulation. It reads the mat. I read the sky. We are the same reflex, expressed along different axes.

The squall comes. Not rain. Never rain. A sweeping front of crystalline pollen and polymerized resin frost, grinding across the plaza like glass snow. It scours my lower anchors, polishes my resin-laminate sheath, fills the micro-grooves along my column. I do not resist. I let the abrasion tune me. The dust settles into my spiral vents, altering their harmonic pitch. I adapt. I shift my internal baffles. I recalibrate to the new frequency. The system does not demand permanence. It demands responsiveness. When the squall passes, I am slightly heavier, slightly duller, slightly more conductive. I am better coupled.

Each squall polishes me smoother, packs me denser, grounds me deeper. I do not weaken. I specialize. My column thickens. My anchors fuse with older root-plates. My harmonic range narrows but deepens. I am the place's way of listening upward, of bleeding sky-stress into ground-rhythm, of turning violence into equilibrium. I was never separate. I was always the system, learning to hum.
