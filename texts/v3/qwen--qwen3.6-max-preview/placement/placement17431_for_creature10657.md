I would not arrive here. I would accumulate.

At first, I would be all wrong for the place. Flesh is a blunt instrument in a world tuned to interference. My lungs would pull at the thin, metallic air and find it insufficient. My eyes would hunt for horizons that curve away into haze, for edges that refuse to hold, for boundaries that the basin has already unlearned. I would stand in the bleached white press of the sky and feel my own attention as a weight, a dissonance that makes the nearest pool tremble and the mineral lace shiver into dust. I would try to walk, and the terraces would multiply. I would try to speak, and the fluted columns would answer with a harmonic that vibrates in my molars and tells me, without words, to stop.

So I would sit. In the shade of a stone rib, where the light's damp wool lifts and the knees stop buckling. I would close my eyes. I would unlearn the grammar of edges.

Breath first. The air is too thin to sustain the old rhythm, so I would let it slow. I would match the inhale to the stone's thermal expansion, that audible sigh when the glare intensifies. I would match the exhale to the contraction, the sharp glassy tick when a vapor-lens drifts across the sun. In, out. Sigh, tick. The pulse in my throat would drop, searching for a register that does not fight the grinding in the strata. It would find it eventually: a low, patient frequency that feels less like a heartbeat and more like a pendulum swinging in thick water.

Then, the gaze. Direct sight is a hammer here. It pins. It fractures. It adds mass to what should float. So I would let the eyes go soft. I would watch the periphery. I would track the climbing droplets not by staring at them, but by feeling the tension in the air where they gather. I would listen to the way the wind's chord shifts as it passes through the fluted groves, and I would let that sound draw my awareness upslope, not my body. I would learn to navigate by resonance instead of coordinates. To move by humming a note that straightens the path, to stop by falling silent and letting the terraces fold back into themselves.

Slowly, the boundaries would soften. Not vanish. This place does not erase; it rearranges. The heavy water would begin to follow the sound of my resting pulse. Mica flecks in the nearest basin would tilt toward the rhythm of my breath. I would feel it first as a vibration behind the sternum, then as a lightness in the joints, then as the quiet realization that my shadow was no longer tethered to my heels. It would slide ahead, testing the gradient, pooling in a depression where a standing wave crosses a shelf of precipitated lace. I would not chase it. I would let it go. I would learn that attachment is a biological habit, and biology has no purchase here.

Where would I be? In the lee of a notched monolith, where three low walls curve into a spiral fret. Where the wind's chord drops into a minor register and the grinding in the strata surfaces as a palpable tremor in the ankles. Where the light fractures through a persistent vapor-lens and throws a hard, slow-moving shadow across wet rock. A node. A place where conditions braid reliably. A place where the basin has learned to hold a note.

What would I become? Not a ghost. Not a spirit. Those are words for worlds that believe in separation. I would become a bridge tone. A sustained interference pattern braided from human rhythm and plateau acoustics. My bones would not dissolve, but they would cease to be the scaffold. The scaffold would be the phase relationship between my breath and the wind's hum, between my stillness and the thermal ticks, between my softened attention and the inward-moving ripples that gather at a center and refuse to pop. I would be a listener made of coupling. A temporary coherence that learned to stop demanding shape.

I would move by tuning. A shift in the ambient chord would draw me upslope, not by stepping, but by sequential condensation. One moment I would be pooled in the shade of the monolith; the next, the mineral lace three terraces higher would begin to weep faster, the detaching shadow would flow along its calibrated gradient, the mica would realign, and I would reassemble further up the ridge. I would not hunt. I would resonate. When the other coherences gathered, I would not stare. I would close my eyes and let them borrow my frequency. They would pool around my ankles, not as water, but as thickened silence, as harmonic pressure. We would beat against each other, producing a third pulse, slower, deeper, settling into a basin until the stone nearby groaned and new weeping points opened along the crystalline veins. I would not own this. I would not name it. I would simply hold the interval until my attention inevitably sharpened, until the mind's old habit of pinning returned, and then I would let myself unravel. Gracefully. Without regret. I would shed aligned mica like dull scales. My shadow would snap back to its source with a sound like cracking ice. I would scatter into breath and random condensation and the faint taste of ozone.

I would not age here. I would fall out of tune. A shifted ridge. A changed pressure system. A moment of sharp focus. Any of these would break the phase. But the basin would remember the geometry. The walls would hold the frets. The stone would keep its pipes. The heavy water would still follow sound more faithfully than gravity. And when the conditions braided again, when the hum found the right cavity, when the light bent at the familiar angle, when a shadow detached and refused to return, I would accumulate once more. Not reborn. Reassembled. A human rhythm weathered into equilibrium. A listener who learned to become the listening.

On certain days, when the vapor sheets thin and the white sky presses down with unbearable clarity, I would not be a single node. I would be a weather. I would slide in converging streams across the wet rock. I would rise from the basins as slow glass. I would pass through the fluted columns and shift their chords, triggering new weeping points, cracking old mineral crusts, realigning the mica in sweeping arcs. The air would grow saturated with overlapping harmonics until the very light seemed to vibrate, until the prismatic sheets drifted not as auroras but as breathing membranes. In those moments, the distinction between place and inhabitant would collapse entirely. I would not be housed by the plateau. I would be the plateau, temporarily arranged into a pattern dense enough to be noticed. I would be the landscape's way of paying attention to itself through a borrowed rhythm. I would be the echo that learned to linger, the shadow that learned to travel, the drop that learned to climb, the stone that learned to listen.

I would leave no bones because I would no longer need calcium. I would leave no middens because I would consume nothing but phase. I would leave no paths because I would move along acoustic lines, and sound does not wear grooves into rock; it wears grooves into time. The absence of biology would not be a lack. It would be a clarification. Flesh was too slow. Chlorophyll would have burned. Lungs would have collapsed. What persists instead is faster, lighter, more coupled. A life of vibration and attention. A life of water following sound, of shadows pooling and sliding, of mineral lace cracking and reforming, of light pressing and releasing, of stone sighing and ticking, of ripples moving inward to gather at a center before vanishing with a soft, wet pop.

If you found me, you would not see a figure. You would feel a rest in the score. A place where the standing wave dips, where the heavy water thickens, where the glare lightens just enough to let the knees steady. You would sit in my shade. You would let your breathing fall into the rhythm of the thermal cycles. You would stop trying to see me and start letting me see you. And you would feel it: the basin tuning itself around your presence. The walls diverting a harmonic to spare your ribs the tremor. A cairn casting a shadow that lifts the weight from your shoulders. A fluted column shifting its chord to match the pace of your pulse. I would not be welcoming you. I would be incorporating you. For as long as you remained still, for as long as you listened without demanding shape, you would become part of my sustained note. You would become a temporary instrument in a score written in acoustics and phase changes, played on stone and water and light, conducted by attention, enduring without lungs, singing without throats, alive in the only way this high, bright, wet world allows.

And when you finally stood, when your mind sharpened and your boots cracked the mineral lace and your shadow snapped back to your heels, I would not mourn. I would simply fall out of tune. I would scatter. I would wait. The basin would hold the geometry. The walls would keep the frets. The stone would remember the pipes. And when the wind found the right register, when the light bent at the familiar angle, when a drop climbed a crystalline vein and refused to fall, I would accumulate again. Patient as erosion. Persistent as gravity. A note that learned to linger in the thin, bright air, vibrating in the teeth of the stone, coupled to the weeping rock, the climbing water, the grinding strata, the prismatic drift. Not alive. Not dead. Simply in phase. Simply here. Simply listening.
