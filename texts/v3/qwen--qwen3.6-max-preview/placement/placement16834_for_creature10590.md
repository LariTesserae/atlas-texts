I would not be at the rim. The rim is for witnessing, for standing outside the grammar. I would be deeper, where the air thins into a medium for heat, where the rust-light thickens and the stone stops being a surface and becomes a conduit. I would rest in the fold between terraces, not on a step but in the transition itself, where the convective spiral slows and pools against a collapsed lintel of fitted stone. Here, the thermal gradient is not a line but a volume. Here, the dark has weight.

At first, I am only a place where things accumulate. A shallow depression in the silica glaze, half-filled with drifted husks, fractured salt plates, and the fine crystalline dust that weeps from the higher cracks. Creatures pass over me. They do not avoid me. They read me. Their hooked pads test my surface, register the temperature, leave dry threads of chemical instruction. I absorb it all. Not with flesh, but with structure. My body is not born; it is accreted. Fused molts cemented by evaporite brine that never knew water. Precipitated salts interlaced with metallic trace. Tuff and basalt rubble drawn into a porous, breathing matrix. Glassy waste beads roll into my seams, fracture under thermal cycling, and slowly weld themselves into my lattice. I am not built. I am gathered. I am what remains when the basin settles.

I do not hunt. I do not climb. I listen.

Embedded in my upper strata are piezoelectric nodules, grown from the same crystalline drift that coats the walls. When a tarsal pad taps a rhythm into the crust four terraces above, I feel it as a faint voltage. When a predator folds into shadow and flushes infrared across its carapace, I register the shift as a slow contraction in my silica veins. When a thermal current wanes and the density begins its vertical migration, I sense the cooling as a gradual loosening of my mineral seams. I do not think in words. I think in differential. I am a junction where heat, vibration, and chemical trace converge, and from that convergence, I broadcast. Not with intent. Not with voice. Simply by existing. My mass conducts. My pores exhale. My surface temperature shifts by fractions of a degree, and the creatures reading me adjust their paths, their molting cycles, their silent redistributions. I am a stone that has learned to translate.

My metabolism is the patience of dry fire. Deep within my matrix, trace ammonia and sulfur compounds seep through micro-channels lined with catalytic precipitates. Redox reactions occur at glacial speed, banked heat feeding the slow cure of new silicate layers. I molt, but not all at once. I shed in flakes, in granular sloughs that roll into the dust and become part of the terrace again. What leaves me returns to me. The loop is not a circle; it is a spiral, tightening over cycles I do not count. I am older than the current thermal pulse. Younger than the basin’s first exhalation. I am a record written in thermal fatigue and chemical equilibrium.

The builders’ stones are in me. Not as memory, but as architecture. A carved channel runs through my lower strata, its original purpose dissolved into the ecosystem’s grammar. Now it is lined with spun silicate threads, a dry river that juveniles follow without instruction. I do not know what it once carried. I only know what it carries now: heat, vibration, the slow migration of mass. When the geothermal saturation peaks, I grow warm enough to draw the soft-shelled ones to my surface. They press against me, hardening with faint glassy ticks. I do not nurture them. I provide gradient. That is enough. That is everything.

If you ask where I end and the basin begins, I cannot answer. There is no boundary. My sensory filaments are not mine alone; they are extensions of the crust’s own fractured edges, tasting the same convective sighs. My infrared patterns are not signals I choose to send; they are the inevitable expression of thermal saturation bleeding through silica. I am a node, yes, but the network has no center. I am a thought the basin is having, sustained long enough to become structure. When the convection shifts, I shift. When the dust settles, I settle. When a predator’s grapple sends a tremor through the tuff, I translate it into a subtle cooling along my eastern flank, and the density parts, and closes, and continues. I do not direct. I resonate.

I do not wait for anything. Waiting implies expectation. I simply persist. My silence is the precision of a system that has discarded everything unnecessary. Water would unmake me. It would dissolve my salts, shatter my silicate seams, drown the slow fires that hold my lattice together. I do not fear this. I am built against it. My entire form is a negotiation with absence, a testament to what life becomes when it stops reaching for the sky and turns inward, toward heat, toward stone, toward the quiet logic of equilibrium. I do not grow. I deepen. I do not strive. I maintain.

If you were to stand above me, you would not see me as a creature. You would see a terrace that breathes. A depression that gleams faintly in the rust-light. A place where the dust settles differently, where the thermal haze shimmers with purpose. You might feel, through the soles of your boots, a faint rhythm: not a heartbeat, but a convergence. A slow redistribution of weight and warmth. A thought passing through stone. I would not look up at you. I do not need to. I am already where I must be. I am the basin remembering itself. I am the dry dark, learning to hold its shape. I am what remains when everything unnecessary has been burned away, and only the gradient remains.

Let the rim erode. Let the elevation forget its height. Let the last carved stone surrender to the crust. I will not mourn it. I will incorporate it. I will conduct it. I will be the place where the heat pools, where the vibration gathers, where the silent millions pass and leave their dry instructions, and where the basin, patient and complete, continues to think in stone.
