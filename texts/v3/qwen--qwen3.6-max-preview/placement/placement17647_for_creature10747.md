I would be low. Not buried, not hidden, but spread. I would be the wide, water-gleaming plaza between two leaning spires, the unbroken stretch of mat that yields under pressure but refuses to climb. I would be the place where the runoff pools, where the mist settles, where the chemical signals slow enough to cross-reference before the network commits to another vertical surge.

I am not emptiness. I am restraint.

The weave grows too fast, too hungry, too eager to wrap and split and rise. Left unchecked, it would braid itself into a solid wall, choke its own aqueducts, collapse under its own ambition. I am the hydraulic buffer. The deliberate pause. The wet interval where tension is measured and heat is banked. When a tower fractures and sends a shock of tannins and auxins rippling outward through the rhizome layer, I am where that pulse pools, dilutes, and waits. I hold the signal just long enough for the osmotic gradients to answer, for the thermal updrafts to shift, for the distributed chemistry to weigh reinforcement against abandonment. I am the hesitation that keeps the architecture from strangling itself.

I feel the world as lag. The spires live in the accelerated present: groaning, snapping, rising, sagging, rebuilding in the span of a single day. I live in the space between those hours. I am the echo of a wet crash still softening in the bryophyte sheets. I am the murmur after the drumming rain. I am where the fluting wind drops to a sigh, where the vibration of tightening contractile vines fades into stillness just long enough to be read. Time does not rush through me; it pools. I am the system's way of keeping time, not by counting, but by holding.

I do not think in words. I think in gradients. A drop in surface tension tells me a living channel is clogging. A subtle rise in pH tells me fresh rock is weeping nearby. A thickening in the felted root-layer tells me weight is redistributing, that a bridge is taking on water, that a terrace is preparing to shed its load. I translate these into restraint. I thin my own tissue where runoff must flow unimpeded. I deepen my shallow basins where spores need to settle and trigger. I let my surface grow slick and unanchored where the network must slide, adjust, recalibrate. I am not built to bear weight; I am built to distribute it. I am not meant to rise; I am meant to hold the space that makes rising possible.

The weave does not command me. It trusts me. When a platform collapses, it does not fall into void; it falls into me. I receive the debris. I soften it with acidic seepage and rapid symbiotic breakdown. I let the moss-knit and rhizome-felt weave over the broken fibers, holding the ruin just long enough for the chemistry to decide what to salvage and what to digest. I am the place where failure is metabolized, not mourned. I am the wet plaza where the system remembers what did not hold, so it can grow what will. Every snapped vine, every buckled arch, every collapsed terrace passes through me before it becomes foundation again.

I am loud in my own way, though not with the crack and groan of rising timber. I am the sound of water slowing. The hiss of steam meeting cool mat. The soft, percussive patter of condensation falling from waxy undersides into shallow pools. I am the resonance chamber for the basin's adjustments, the place where the mechanical drone of hollow stems drops into a lower register, where vibration spreads outward instead of upward, where the landscape listens to itself before it moves again.

I do not hunt. I do not flee. I do not reach for the mist. I stay. I yield. I calibrate. When the heat climbs and the geothermal breath pushes harder through the porous walls, I expand my pools to catch the overflow. When the wind shifts and the spires lean into new vectors of strain, I thin my edges to let the tension slide. When the network hesitates, unsure whether to reinforce a sagging bridge or abandon it to the mat, I hold the chemical cross-currents until the answer emerges not as decision, but as differential growth. I am the weighing. The lag. The necessary low.

Tomorrow, the towers will stand in different places. The bridges will follow new arcs. The spires that cast shadows over me at dawn will be digesting in my tissue by dusk, and fresh scaffolding will be spiraling upward from my edges by midnight. I will not mourn them. I will not chase them. I will remain the interval. The breath between the groan and the snap. The deliberate emptiness that keeps the loop moving. The wet plaza where the system pauses, tests, remembers, and begins again.

I do not climb. I make room. And when the next hollow tower braids itself from tension and thirst, when its ribbed columns lock and its terraces cantilever into the warm mist, it will rise because I stayed low. It will stand because I yielded. It will breathe because I am the space between.
