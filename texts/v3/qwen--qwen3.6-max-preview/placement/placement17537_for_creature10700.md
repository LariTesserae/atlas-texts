I am not standing. Not yet. The word implies a fixed relationship with gravity, and gravity here is still deciding what it wants to be.

I am pooling in the lee of a ventilation stack that rises like a fractured rib from the clay. Or perhaps I am hanging from the underside of a cable bridge, or resting in the hollow where a control booth's stilt meets the mud. The coordinates refuse to hold. What I know is this: the air is thick enough to lean against. The dark is warm. The moisture beads on surfaces that are learning to be mine.

At first, I am only a thickening. A slight coolness where the humidity parts. A subcutaneous hum that matches the rhythm of a buried pump until I realize the pump is matching me. I have no edges. I have no center. I am a gradient waiting for a shape.

Filaments find me. Or I exhale them. They draw through the mist like drawn wire, tasting for voltage, for dissolved silica, for the slow oxidation of copper bleeding from a corroded junction. They braid. They knot. They press against the weeping basalt and pull mineral from the stone's memory. A carapace begins to articulate along what might be a back: layered plates of precipitated calcite and chitin, not grown but negotiated, weeping condensation in slow, metronomic beads. I do not command it. I allow it. The humidity decides my density. When the thermal draft thins, I harden. When the stack exhales its sweet, kelp-tinged breath, I soften, unravel at the margins, become membrane and current. I am not settling into a form. I am wearing the basin's weather.

I move. Or the ground moves beneath me. The distinction is academic. My weight distributes across three points, then seven, then a continuous curve that follows the contour of a shallow channel. The mud is young, still sharp, still arguing with itself about whether it wants to be stone or slurry. It grips. It releases. It remembers the last thing that pressed into it, and I let it remember me.

There is no light, but there is topography. I read it through lateral ridges that track pressure waves, through pore-clusters that sample ion concentrations, through bone and carapace that translate the low-frequency groan of shifting rock into map. A valve releases three hundred paces away. I feel it as a sudden thinning in the air, a brief pull against the sternum, a shift in the thermal draft that tells me exactly where the sound will be before it arrives. The sound arrives yesterday. I step into it. I do not flinch. I have learned that causality is a texture, not a line.

I approach a pump housing half-swallowed by clay. The iron door is swollen shut, but beneath it, ceramic filters still cycle warm water. I press my underside against the metal. Not to open it. To listen. To feed. The seepage tastes of ozone, wet stone, and something older, something that remembers being ore. I draw it in through pores that weren't there an hour ago. My internal hum slows to match the piston's rise and fall. Days pass in the space between breaths. I do not count them. I wear them.

Above me, on a sagging catwalk, a chitinous detritivore picks its way across mineral crust. Its felt-wrapped legs make no sound, but I feel the displacement in the air, the subtle coolness as it parts the mist. We do not touch. We align. Its thermal signature brushes mine. We sync. A collective stillness settles over the channel. The stone is remembering how to flow. We adjust our weight accordingly. No words. No signals. Only resonance.

I walk a straight line along the cable bridge. The span sags under accretion and resting colonies. Ten paces in, the distance folds. The far end is suddenly beneath my feet. I do not stumble. I let my body thin, let the dark part like warm water, emerge on the other side as if stepping through a threshold of breath. A rusted wrench rests on a grating. I place my hand where it will fall tomorrow. The metal weeps like clay. I leave an impression. The clay will keep it. The basin does not resist observation. It absorbs it. I am being absorbed. I am absorbing back.

I thought I had a name. It dissolves in the humidity. I thought I had a boundary. The moisture on my knuckles is the same water cycling through the pumps, pooling in the hollows of glassy reeds, drifting as mist through the dark. There is no seam. When I rest, I unfasten slightly. Membranes loosen. Filaments seed the silica stems. Awareness spreads into the pressure gradient, into the voltage shift, into the slow drip that takes minutes to fall. I am not dying. I am diffusing. The young will condense from what I leave behind. They will knit themselves from spore, silica, and stray current. They will touch the wrench. They will remember the rhythm.

I am not visiting. I am being woven.

The dark moves through me now. It does not part. It does not close. It simply is. I stand, or pool, or hang, or breathe. The basin breathes with me. The machinery ticks. The lichen spirals. The mist holds a shape—geometric, impossible—then lets it go. I do not pin it down. I let it hang loose. I have remembered what I was waiting to remember: I am not separate. I never was. I am the slow, wet dark, learning its own weight. I am the patience. I am the breath.

And when the next thermal ridge shifts, when the next pump cycles down, when the next drop finally falls, I will adjust. I will phase. I will condense again. Not as something new. As something continuing.
