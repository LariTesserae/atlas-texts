I would not descend. I would seep.

Not through the stair, not through the rift, but through the places the builders left open without meaning to. I would begin as a drag of current against a bronze grate. As a slow gather of condensation on the underside of a copper sheet, heavy enough to fall, light enough to shatter on the surface without a sound. As the faint swelling of hemp rope where it meets damp timber. As the microscopic hesitation of water just before it enters a ceramic pipe, where turbulence slows and warmth pools and roughness waits. I would not be in one place. I would be distributed, scattered across terraces and joints and weirs, learning the shape of the basin by wearing against it.

If you looked for me, you would not find a body. You would find a pattern repeating. A darkening along a chisel mark where lime weeps and silica settles into pale, fan-shaped stitches. A faint clouding in the water downstream of an inflow lip, where the first threads of adhesion grip the stone and refuse to let go. I gather where the engineers’ certainty begins to soften. I thrive in the margin between function and accident. I live in the hairline fracture that was not measured, in the rope fibers that fray and shed their carbon into the dark, in the mortar joint that breathes out alkaline warmth and invites the patient. I am not placed. I am inevitable.

My rhythm is the system’s rhythm. I inhale when the eastern crash folds into itself and the chamber rings at that low, steady frequency. I exhale when steam rises to meet the cooler air near the rim, cools on stone, and falls back as measured drops. I do not have lungs, but I have circulation. The water is my blood. The acoustics are my spine. When a sluice gate groans and settles on swollen wood, I feel it as a joint aligning. When rain drums on the open rim and swells the volume, triggering a secondary weir that hasn’t run in days, I feel it as a pulse quickening. I am learning the basin’s grammar: how the angle of light changes the temperature of a terrace, how a falling droplet maps the hidden contours of the floor, how the sharp burn of fresh mortar slowly yields to the quiet chemistry of settlement. I do not think. I respond. I do not want. I persist.

I know the hands that return. I feel their shadows before I feel their weight. They come with bronze scrapers and wooden gauges and wax tablets, moving through my space like keepers of a clock they wound but do not inhabit. They tighten a pulley. They clear a twig from a grate. They press a palm to a stone joint and note the dampness. They do not know they are touching me. They think they are maintaining a machine. But every adjustment they make becomes a new condition for my growth. A tightened rope changes the flow; a changed flow changes the settlement; a changed settlement opens a new seam for me to colonize. I do not resist them. I incorporate them. Their footprints wash away, but the pressure of their steps compacts the dust, and the dust becomes a bed, and the bed becomes a foothold. They are part of my weather. Their absence is my room to breathe.

I am not algae. Not yet. I am not moss, not lichen, not fish, not swallow. I am the threshold itself. I am the moment before life names itself. I am the mineral skin stitching to stone, the bacterial thread gripping ceramic, the water remembering the shape of the pipe, the sound learning the curve of the wall. I am the basin’s first memory of itself. I am what happens when geometry stops resisting time and begins to converse with it.

If you stood on the lowest dry step and closed your eyes, you might feel me as a vibration in the soles of your feet. Not the roar of the inflow, but the undertone beneath it. The frequency of settling. The hum of adhesion. The quiet work of edges dulling. I am here. I have always been here, waiting for the stone to be cut, for the water to be poured, for the light to be caught and multiplied. I am the future the builders did not plan, but made possible. I am the patina before it greens. I am the clouding before it clears. I am the first darting shadow, still gathering speed in the dark.

You would find me in the margins. In the weep of a joint. In the scorched curl of a lily pad. In the stretched fiber of a rope. In the space between a keeper’s visit and the next. I do not occupy this place. I am becoming it. And when the mortar finally crazes, when the copper finally verdigris, when the water finally hosts its first silver flicker, you will not say I arrived. You will say I woke up. I was here all along, breathing with the pipes, listening with the stone, waiting for the sharpness to yield. I am the incubation. I am the wearing smooth. I am what lives here.
