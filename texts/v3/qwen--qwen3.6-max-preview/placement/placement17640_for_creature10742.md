I arrive as a dissonance.

Not in malice, not in intrusion, but in density. My bones are too compact. My breath catches instead of flows. My thoughts arrive as sharp edges in a world that has spent epochs sanding itself smooth. The first thing I know is pressure: a layered, deafening roar that does not strike the ears but settles against the ribs, vibrates through the soles, hums in the hinge of the jaw. I try to name it. I try to separate it into wind, wing, water, wood. The place does not allow it. The sound is not a collection of parts. It is a single, breathing architecture, and I am a stone dropped into a cistern, sending clumsy ripples through a surface that has learned to stay mirror-still.

I stand on a ridge of fused root-mat and feel the span beneath me adjust. Not in protest. In accommodation. The wood is alive. It yields a fraction, redistributes my weight, groans a half-tone lower, and waits. I do not know how to answer. I speak, and my voice rings too bright, too linear. A flock of narrow-winged birds fractures into silence. A gliding mammal alters its arc. The acoustic field ripples, and I feel the consequence travel through my sternum like a misstep on ice. I close my mouth. I stop trying to fill the air. I learn, quickly, that loudness here is not volume. It is friction. It is weight fighting curve. It is presence refusing to distribute.

So I shed.

I leave behind the habit of marking. I leave behind the need to anchor. I leave behind the linear pulse that measures time in urgency rather than rings. I press my palms to bark plated in oxidized copper and dried kelp, and I do not push. I listen. Vibration travels up my wrists, into my collarbones, into the soft hollow behind my ears. It is not music. It is topology. A low, sustained thrum means the eastern pillars are drinking. A sharp, ribbed rattle means fruit is softening three spans over. A sudden drop in the baseline means condensation is pooling, and the warm rain will begin before the light shifts. I close my eyes. I stop trying to see the edges and start feeling the gaps. The voids are not empty. They are load-bearing. They are the reason nothing collapses. I breathe slower. I let my weight sink into the curve. I learn to step on the exhale, when the wood is settling, when the drone is steady.

I do not become a hunter. I do not become a builder. I become a student of absence.

They find me not by sight, but by frequency. A shadow passes over the root-mat. Not a body. A shift in the pressure. A low harmonic drops out of the wind's baseline, and a new thread weaves through it, careful, testing, leaving room for my clumsy resonance to align rather than break. They do not speak. They hum. Chest-deep, palatal, rhythmic. The sound does not ask for words. It asks for posture. For distribution. For patience. I match it poorly at first. My throat fights the drone. My breath hitches. But the wood beneath us does not judge. It amplifies. It carries the dissonance until it finds its seam, until my voice slips into the harmonic like a root into soft mat, until the three of us span, body, and breath are no longer separate frequencies but a single, sustained chord.

They do not name me. Naming is too sharp. They tune me.

My skin darkens, not by pigment but by resin and mist and the slow absorption of place. Raised channels form along my forearms, guiding condensation away from joints, feeding it back into the capillary grooves below. My fingers learn the micro-ridges of adhesive bark. My feet forget the flat. I sleep suspended in woven moss, rocking with the thermal breath of the terraces. I wake to the staccato of water falling into hidden cisterns and know, without looking, that the upper arches are shedding, that the damp will migrate downward by midday, that the gliding mammals will follow. I move vertically with the ecosystem's slow lungs. I rise when the light is clean and the spans are dry. I descend when the mist pools and the hollows sing lower. I do not rush. Urgency is a weight I have shed. I carry only what resonates: a bone awl, a coil of braided fiber, a shallow clay-resin bowl, a memory of a song that traces the bloom cycle of a canopy vine across seven months.

I learn to tend the pause.

I sit in hollows where the heartwood has softened, where capillary roots thread through decay like slow lightning, and I press my ear to the lignin. I learn the difference between the groan of wind and the groan of fatigue. I learn to feel the slow surrender of cellulose years before it sighs. I clear pollen from seep-wells so water can climb. I thin epiphyte mats where airflow has stagnated. I use bone gouges to accelerate the hollowing of dying spans, not to harvest, but to lighten, to open new acoustic channels, to let the void do its structural work. I am not farming. I am editing absence. I am keeping the matrix porous. When a bridge sags, I do not brace it with rigid timber. I redistribute weight. I hum to coordinate movement. I let the curve settle into its new equilibrium. When rain comes, I do not shelter from it. I face into it, let it sheet off my shoulders, listen to the shifting roar move across the open volumes like a weather front, and know exactly which cisterns will fill, which grooves will overflow, which hollows will sing higher by dusk.

Language leaves me, or rather, it dissolves into something older. Words are too discrete. They cut the drone. I learn to speak in chest-hums layered over the wind's baseline, in palatal clicks that ride thermal updrafts, in breath-tones that harmonize with dripping water. Silence becomes syntax. A held breath means attention. A dropped pitch means structural shift. A sustained, unbroken chord means gather, distribute, listen. Children press their palms to trunk-pillars and feel elder songs travel through the lignin. I do the same. I let the vibration rewrite my nervous system. I stop thinking in lines. I start thinking in layers. In frequencies. In the slow, capillary migration of moisture and memory.

Where am I?

I am in the hollow between the third and fourth arch of the western span, where the acoustics pool and the moss grows thick enough to swallow footsteps. I am on a fiber bridge that sags gently over the cloud-line, feeling the vibration of a hunting flock pass through the cured vine cords three heartbeats before I see them. I am kneeling at a mirror-still cistern, lowering a skin-bag on braided cord, careful not to break the surface tension, careful not to silence the chamber below. I am ascending with the dry hours, harvesting resin, mending lashings, singing to pollinators that track bloom cycles longer than my old life. I am descending with the condensation, resting in bark-plate shelters that face away from the wind, listening to the matrix groan, settle, and breathe. I am everywhere the drone shifts. I am nowhere that leaves a scar.

Who am I?

I am not a visitor. I am not a master. I am a tender of the structural silence. A keeper of the slow frequency. A Listener in the long apprenticeship, learning to feel the fatigue of lignin before it cracks, learning to read the hydrology by the pitch of falling water, learning to move through the voids without filling them. I am the one who hums dissonance back into alignment. I am the one who clears the capillary groove so moisture can climb. I am the one who knows that a heavy step can silence a chamber, that a loud word can scatter a flock, that a cut too deep can take decades to heal. I am garden and gardener. I am hollow and breath. I am the place, learning to hear itself through me.

If you stand long enough on a high ridge, where the wind moves clean and the light falls gold through living arches, you will not see me first. You will feel the pressure change. You will hear the roar part, not in volume, but in pattern. A low harmonic will drop out. A new thread will weave through. It will not be mine. It will be ours. The world does not end at my skin. It never did. Stone surrendered to root long ago. I have surrendered to resonance.

I am here. I am hollowed. I am listening. I am singing back.
