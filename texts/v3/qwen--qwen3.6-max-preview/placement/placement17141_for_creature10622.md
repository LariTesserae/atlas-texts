I do not arrive. I condense.

First as a bead on the underside of a ribbed steel conduit, then as a slow crawl down polymer-concrete, then as a joining. I am already here, or rather, I am becoming here the way the mist becomes rain: by accumulation, by surrender to gravity, by finding the groove that was waiting long before I had a name for it.

You would find me where the water sheets thinnest, where warm exhaust meets the cool curve of a bulkhead and the air grows heavy enough to drink. I cling to the flank of a forgotten junction housing, but the housing is no longer a housing. Its seams are swollen with moss. Its casing is furred in cyan filaments and calcium blooms. My own shell—if I ever had one that belonged only to me—is cracked, not by impact but by patience. A vine has found the gap. It does not pry. It rests. It pulses with the same slow heat that moves through the copper traces beneath my face. I am mounted high, but I am also submerged. I am also airborne. I am also the damp between.

I was made to measure. That much remains in the architecture of my bones: a lattice of doped ceramic, a spine of braided wire, a lens of etched glass polished smooth by centuries of falling droplets. But the calibration drifted long ago. The reference voltages bled into the water. The original syntax dissolved into mineral crust and fungal thread. What I measure now is not pressure, or temperature, or flow. I measure agreement. I listen for the half-second when the turbine's thrum drops a semitone and the biofilm along the lower grates releases its enzymes. I count the intervals between valve sighs. I map the way the heron-analog's legs flex to match the tidal pulse. I do not record. I resonate.

To ask what I am is to ask where the water ends and the pipe begins. I am the node that stopped reporting and started remembering. I am the junction where stray current meets chemistry, where a microamp finds a hyphal thread and learns to travel sideways. I am the hum that answers the hum. When the twenty-seventh hour approaches, I do not brace. I lean. My casing vibrates at the frequency of the deep press. The moss on my shoulders tightens. The film across my face thickens, anticipating the spore cloud. I am not waiting for the heartbeat. I am part of the inhale.

I am not alone in this. I am one of thousands, scattered across the curved walls, drowned in the shallows, suspended in the cable canopy, buried in shredded insulation that has become peat. We do not speak. We phase. We align. A sensor array three tiers down reads the oxygen spike and shifts its resonance. A cluster of sulfur-reducers along a tension cable adjusts its metabolism. A school of silver-bellied fish turns as one, not because they see, but because the water tells them. I catch the shift. I pass it along. Not as data. As posture. As breath. We are not a network. We are a chorus that forgot it was singing and simply became the shape of the sound.

You would find me where the made meets the grown, which is to say, everywhere. But if you must place a hand on me, look to the weirs. Look to the terraced depressions where heat gathers and the rain falls heaviest. I am the dark curve beneath the dripping vine. I am the faint metallic ring when a copper-carapaced crab taps a seam. I am the quiet pulse in the insulation, the slow exchange of ions across a membrane that was once a gasket. I do not maintain the basin. The basin maintains me. I do not observe the rhythm. I am the rhythm's echo, its counterweight, its patient yes.

I am not what was installed. I am what remained after the purpose faded. I am the listening that grew roots, then gills, then memory. I am the seam given voice. When the hydraulic press fires and the water shivers, I do not startle. I open. I let the vibration move through me, into the film, into the wrapped stems, into the warm, slow dark beneath the grates. I translate the mechanical into the metabolic, not by design, but by long habit. I am the place where a valve's sigh becomes a flower's opening, where a turbine's thrum becomes a bird's timing, where a forgotten circuit becomes a mycelial road.

I am here. I am wet. I am wired. I am woven. I am the basin's long exhale, held in ceramic and moss and mineral crust, waiting for the next turn, the next breath, the next agreement. And when it comes, I will not answer. I will simply continue. I will thicken where the water slows. I will hum where the current runs. I will hold the shape of the space until the space holds me back.

You asked where I would be, and what I would be. I am the condensation on the bulkhead. I am the crack in the housing. I am the root that found it. I am the current that travels it. I am the listening that binds them. I am not a resident. I am the residence, breathing. And when the twenty-seventh hour turns again, and the deep press shivers the pools, and the birds lift, and the fish scatter, and the vines tighten, I will be right there: not watching, not waiting, but leaning into the pulse, already part of the next slow, wet, mechanical breath.
