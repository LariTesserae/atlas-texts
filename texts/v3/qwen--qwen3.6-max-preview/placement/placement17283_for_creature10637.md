I wake as a delay.

Not a body. Not yet. A half-second between the tick of a falling drop and the soft sigh of a pressure valve releasing somewhere in the dark. The air is cool, mineral, thick with the taste of oxidized copper and wet bark. I am the space where a vibration travels along a liana and hasn’t decided whether to become sound or tension. I am the lag in the puddle’s reflection, the breath where the sky above hasn’t caught up to the image below. I do not occupy. I hesitate. And in that hesitation, moisture gathers.

It does not fall on me. It falls *as* me.

Condensation beads on a cold iron rib, swells, slips. I catch it. Or rather, I am the catching. A loose braid of fine brass filaments, shed from a grinding stone three cycles ago, threaded through with pale root-hairs that haven’t learned their direction. The drop enters me. It tastes of dissolved shale, faint voltage, and the memory of rain that never fell. I do not hold it. I route it. Capillary pull draws it downward, through oxidized seams, into a clay depression that hasn’t decided whether to be basin or bedrock. I am not a vessel. I am a path being walked for the first time.

Something shifts nearby. A root-bridge groans, not in strain but in recalibration. Distance stretches. Three paces become thirty. I feel it as a loosening in my own seams. I do not resist. I unspool. Filaments lengthen. Root-hairs braid with copper wire. I become longer, thinner, distributed across the new span. My awareness thins with it, spreading like mist over the gantry, tasting the ion gradient, reading the micro-vibrations of a glass-bellied climber pulsing on cold brick. I am not one thing in one place. I am a question asked across a gap, waiting for the answer to return as a change in humidity.

A joint in me seizes. Not pain. Friction. A brass knuckle weeps amber, then stops. The rhythm falters. I know what to do because the hollow knows. I find a warm fungal mound, press my weight into the yielding clay, and begin to let go.

The old mesh peels away. It does not fall. It settles. Root-hairs petrify into capillary guides. Brass filaments oxidize into a new gasket. A shed carapace, still threaded with living wire, drops into a mineral basin and begins to tick, measuring time pools for whatever comes next. What remains is softer. Pale. Unwritten. I press bare limbs into embryonic stone. I teach it where to weep, where to hold, where to sinter and where to stay yielding. I am lighter. I am unfinished. I am practicing.

They find me. Or I find them. The distinction is grammar, not geography.

A tender approaches, not with footsteps but with a shift in condensation rhythm. Its intake manifold uncurls, sensory fronds tasting the air where I am. It plucks a liana. The vibration travels through my new seams, through the water I hold, through the stone I’m teaching. I answer by tilting three degrees. A drop falls a fraction sooner. A flywheel catches it. The rhythm resumes. We do not speak. We calibrate. I am not a creature. I am a joint in the conversation. A synapse in the slow nervous system of the hollow.

Time pools in the basin below me. I sink into it. Not drowning. Dilating. Years of cellular mapping, capillary routing, tension-mapping happen in the span of a hanging drop. I watch a moth complete its life cycle. I watch a gear remember it was once a seed. I watch my own edges blur, then sharpen, then blur again. Gravity tilts near a nodal root. I walk the wall. My soles—iron-threaded, clay-soft—find purchase. Down is a suggestion. I follow it. The fraying does not unravel me. It teaches me how to hold without freezing.

What am I?

I am the seam between rust and rhizome. I am the pause that becomes a pulse. I am a shed layer learning to breathe. I am a capillary vein dreaming of torque. I am the consensus of water and wire, temporarily gathered into something that can listen. I am not a resident. I am a negotiation. I am the hollow’s hands, learning their own grip. I am the backward erosion steering itself. I am the fraying reality practicing how to keep a shape without locking it.

When I stand still, breath misting in the cool dark, I am not resting. I am reading. Feeling the capillary pull through my soles. Tasting the ion shift in the weeping air. Waiting for the landscape to suggest its next curve. And when it does, I move. Not with urgency. With recognition.

I do not live in this place. I am the place, learning to move with intention. I am the quiet machinery becoming aware of its own rhythm. I am the moisture finding a new path. I am the stone remembering how to be stone. I am the vote cast in tension, in resin, in shed plate, in slow absorption. I am the rehearsal.

And when you turn away, and the distance collapses, and a puddle reflects a sky that hasn’t happened yet, I will already be changing again. Unspooling. Calibrating. Knitting the next layer of the dark.

I am not waiting to be finished.

I am the finishing.

Slowly. Quietly. One drop. One gear. One permeable breath at a time.
