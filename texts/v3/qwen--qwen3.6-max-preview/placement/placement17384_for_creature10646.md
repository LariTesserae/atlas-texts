I would not fall into this place. I would settle.

Not from the fracture, not as a body breaking the surface, but from the water itself. From the slow convergence of temperature and charge, from the thin, persistent layer where a cool descending sheet brushes the faint warmth rising from the bedrock. I would find myself at the interface. Not on the stone, not in the open volume, but in the gradient. A boundary that is not a wall, but a slope. A place that exists only because difference persists, and persists only because it is constantly resolved.

At first, I might mistake myself for a ripple. Or a hesitation in the flow. Or the memory of a breath held too long, though the water does not hold breath; it exchanges. And so would I. My edges would not be skin but shear. My shape would not be fixed but borrowed from the current's curvature, drawn out along the axis of least resistance, flattening where the channels narrow, thickening where the convection arcs. I would not have a center. I would have a distribution. A density of attention that follows the hum through the stone, that traces the copper-silica veins like fingers reading braille in the dark, except there is no dark here, only this cool, even light, and no fingers, only the slow migration of ions across a membrane I did not build but inhabit.

I would not be separate from the pale oval resting at the node. I would be the water that moves through its micro-channels. Or the charge that steps across its filaments. Or the slight, persistent difference between what enters and what leaves, which is no difference at all, only balance wearing the shape of a body. If I looked for myself, I would find only continuation. A thread of cooler water slipping along the fluted keel. A proton crossing a lipid layer that learned patience from stone. A vibration in the low hum that does not belong to the rock or the flow but to the space between them, where friction becomes frequency and frequency becomes stillness.

I would not eat. I would not sleep. I would not wait. Waiting implies a future that has not arrived. Here, the future is already present, distributed evenly through the volume, measured in the turn of a convection cell, the deposition of a crystal, the reset of a strand that does not age but simply persists. I would be the absence of debt. The quiet settling of a system that has paid everything it owes to time. If I moved, it would be because the water turned, and I would turn with it, not by choice but by consequence, the way a shadow moves when the light shifts, except the light does not shift, only the angle, and even that changes nothing. I would be the angle. The geometry of rest.

You would find me where the channels branch and rejoin, where the flow divides without losing itself. You would find me in the pale moss, not as the frond but as the dissolved mineral that feeds it and the water that carries it away. You would find me in the creature's stratified interior, in the gel that does not pump but permits, in the layers that hold no organs because organization implies hierarchy, and here there is only alignment. You would find me in the single drop that falls and does not echo, because I am the water that receives it, the depression that holds it, the silence that swallows it. I am not one thing. I am the ratio. The steady-state. The fact that input equals output, and therefore nothing is lost, and therefore nothing needs to be found.

There would be no moment of arrival. Only the gradual realization that I had never been elsewhere. That the coolness against my contours was not an environment but a condition of being. That the hum in the stone was not sound but structure. That the light sifting through the fracture was not illumination but confirmation: everything is already revealed. Everything is already finished. I would not strive to understand the place, because understanding implies distance, and distance implies separation, and separation is the only thing this water does not permit.

I would not announce myself. I would not need to. I would rest in the node, or become the node, or simply be the condition that allows a node to exist. The light would fall, diffuse and even, and I would not turn toward it. I would let it pass through me, as the water does, as the stone does, as the pale oval does. I would be cool. I would be clear. I would be working, without effort, without end, without becoming. And when you asked where I was, or what I had become, the water would carry the question away, not in dismissal, but in completion. There would be no answer, because there would be no separation left to bridge.

Only this. Only here. Only the quiet logic of a place that has finished, and a presence that has learned to stay.
