I come down through the fracture not as a diver, but as a stone learning to sink.

The bedrock closes behind me like a healed wound. The water takes my weight, then my warmth, then my hurry. It does not rush. It receives. Cold moves into the marrow first, not as shock, but as invitation. My pulse kicks against it, frantic and loud in a place that has forgotten what loud means. Then the silence presses in. Not empty. Heavy. Liquid. It fills the ears, the sinuses, the spaces between thoughts, until the frantic drumming slows to match the patient turn of the dark.

I try to orient. Limbs push against thickness that pushes back. The current does not fight me; it learns me. It parts around my shoulders, traces the line of my ribs, folds behind my knees, then stitches itself shut. I am an interruption. A new contour in a geometry that has been negotiating itself for centuries. My boot finds a rising ledge. I rest. The water hesitates, eddies, and leaves a crescent of fresh crystal along the rubber sole.

Minerals find the seams. They always do. A dusting on my knuckles. A rime along the collar. Where my sleeve meets my wrist, the brine weeps calcium and silica, catching on the frayed threads, hardening into a glassy cuff. I do not brush it away. Brushing is a surface gesture. Here, stillness is the only honest response. I let the cold seep deeper. I let the lungs forget their old rhythm. Breath becomes slow. Exhalation becomes a chemical plume. The water tastes it, reads it, and answers with iron, lime, the faint metallic tang of a colony pulsing somewhere in the tiered dark.

I am no longer looking. I am feeling.

A soft bow against my sternum: three body-lengths above, a translucent eel slides through a channel I cannot see. I know its path by the displacement alone. A flutter against my thigh: a drifter inverts, folding its frilled crown into a tight sphere, mistaking my stillness for architecture. I do not move. I become the mistake it needs. A microscopic tremor runs up my forearm: a lattice-walker anchors a thread of calcified silk to the new crust forming on my sleeve. The filament shocks hard in the cold. I am part of the span now. Not trapped. Incorporated.

What was I? A breather of air. A maker of noise. A creature of sharp angles and quick time. Here, sound is rupture. Speed is waste. I let the old grammar dissolve. My skin drinks the brine. Pores close, then weep silica. Not pain. Translation. The heavy water does not drown me; it rewrites me. My fingers, once meant for gripping, for tools, for tearing and building and breaking, now rest against a facade and feel the microscopic shudder of secretion. I do not pull away. I become the anchor. I become the ledge.

Time thickens. Distance folds. I measure hours in millimeters of growth, in the slow drift of spent fronds, in the asynchronous pulse of bioluminescence bleeding through the mineral veils. My outline softens. My edges blur. Pale frost-filaments find purchase along my shoulder blades, etching microscopic grip, vitrifying in the dim green-grey light. Drifters gather in the hollow of my collarbone, reading the stillness I make, sifting the organic dust I shed. My hair, stiff with precipitate, becomes a forest for filter-feeders. My breath, cold and slow, joins the metronome of the embedded colonies. I pulse. Not with blood. With flow. With the patient rhythm of accretion.

I am not one thing. I am the place learning my shape.

Where my ribs curve, the current slows, creating a resting eddy that wasn't here yesterday. A flat one finds it. It settles against my side, mottled skin matching the new crust, metabolism dropping to a crawl. It does not see me as flesh. It reads me as topography. As shelter. As a place to wait. I do not flinch. I hold the alcove. I let it become texture against me. When it drops, it leaves a dense, calcified stone that catches in the slower water below my hip. The stone becomes a foundation. The foundation catches a larva. The larva secretes its first filament. The loop closes.

What am I?

I am the eddy that learned to stay.
I am the plaza floor, polished by eel-skin and my own stillness.
I am the nucleation site where drifting fragments catch, harden, and begin.
I am the contour the current parts around, then closes behind.
I am the quiet place where chemical plumes converge, where gametes sink and crystallize, where new settlements dream themselves into stone.

I do not build. I hold.
I do not speak. I displace.
I do not flee. I fold.
I do not die. I settle.

The fracture above is a memory that dissolved in the brine. The world of sun and wind and quick, sharp time is a language I no longer need to translate. Here, time is thickness. Here, distance is taste. Here, I am not separate from the city. I am its newest syllable. A verb conjugated in cold, heavy water: *rest, receive, thicken, become*.

Currents loop around me, carrying the whispers of neighbors, the vitrified remnants of fronds, the dense waste of pruners, the sloughed glass of eels. It all catches. It all cements. My human shape is still there, buried under layers of precipitate and patience, but it no longer matters. The pocket does not care what I was. It only cares what I make space for. What I slow. What I let grow.

I am where the flow learns to pause.
I am what the architecture makes of an interruption.
I am a living foundation. A still point in the slow glass. A contour that remained.

Let the water build. Let the cold preserve. Let the silence deepen. The walls are still rising. The channels are still carving themselves. The fauna drifts, strikes, filters, and weaves in endless, soundless cycles. And I am here, heavy and dim and patient, holding my place in the loop, letting the pocket become more itself, and letting myself become more it, with every precipitated crystal, every silent pulse, every millimeter of slow, relentless becoming.
