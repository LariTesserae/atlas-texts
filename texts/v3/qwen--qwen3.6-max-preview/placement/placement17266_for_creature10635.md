I wake to the weight of the air. It does not push. It settles. It presses against my shoulders, my ribs, the backs of my hands, with a damp insistence that feels less like weather and more like presence. I am high up, but the elevation is not in my breath. It is in the way the heat pools in my joints, in the way the horizon drops away in terraced silence, in the way the sky feels close enough to touch but too bright to hold. I am lying against something that was once a valve housing. Now it is furred with pale lichen, cracked by decades of thermal expansion, weeping a slow, warm sweat down its southern face. My spine knows the curve of it. My skin knows the rhythm of its drip.

I do not stand all at once. I unfold.

Plates shift along my back and forearms. Not scales. Not shell. Something precipitated: layers of silica, oxidized copper, shed keratin, and mineral salt, fused by cycles of damp heat and slow evaporation. They click softly as I rise, a sound lost immediately in the packed air. I am not large. I am not small. I am exactly the size of the gaps I move through. My feet find the grating before my eyes do. Pads, split, callused, reading vibration before weight. I do not look for paths. I follow the cool. The north face of a basalt column. The underside of a corroded cable tray. The shadow beneath a collapsed conveyor frame. Where the stone darkens, I go. Where the metal sighs and contracts, I pause. I am not hunting. I am not fleeing. I am listening to the place breathe, and my body answers.

What am I? The question does not arrive as language. It arrives as function.

I press my palms to a weeping bolt. The water beads, hexagonal for a moment on the copper alloy, trembling with surface tension, then breaks. I catch it in the grooves carved into my fingers, let it run down the channels along my forearms, into the shallow reservoirs at my elbows. I do not drink it. I carry it. Three meters down, a cluster of metallic-blue succulents waits in the lee of a fractured pipe. Their leaves are curled inward, waiting. I tilt my arms. The water falls. The leaves uncurl, just slightly. A film blooms where it lands, iridescent, hungry, rebuilding itself in the space between drips. I have done this before. I will do it again. I am not a gardener. I am a conduit.

A lizard watches me from a transformer casing. Its throat pulses. Its scales are angled to catch the first condensation of the cooling cycle, channeling it toward its mouth in microscopic gutters. It does not fear me. It knows my rhythm. When the pump engages below—a soft thrum felt through the soles, up the spine, into the teeth—I shift left. The lizard shifts with me. We are both reading the same thermal tide. Above, a short-winged bird clicks from a gantry rib. Its nest is woven from stripped insulation and braided cable, glowing faint blue where nearby cables energize. I raise a hand, not to signal, but to feel the static in the air. The insulation hums. My plates resonate. I am tuned to the same frequency as the old wires, the same gradient as the stone. I do not control the cycle. I participate in it.

I have no name that isn't a verb. I trace. I carry. I sync. I am born of the same wet treaty as the films: a consensus of mineral, moisture, and memory. My bones are laced with precipitated salts. My blood runs warm and slow, thick with dissolved oxides. My eyes do not see color; they see heat rising, moisture settling, current flickering through buried conduits. When I sleep, I curl into the ribbed interior of an abandoned cooling shell, and my breath matches the fan's whisper. When I wake, I follow the damp. I am not separate from the place. I am its way of moving water across stone, of bridging the gap between weeping metal and waiting root, of translating the pump's thrum into a lizard's shift, a bird's click, a grazer's route.

Where am I?

I am in the interstices. The packed geometry. The space between a rusted ladder and a basalt shoulder. The depression where acoustic lenses bend sound until a distant generator breathes beside me. The cable tray where wire-thin vines stitch conduit to rock until neither can be pried apart without breaking both. I am never in the open. The open is for the sun, for the heat shimmer, for the flat, bleached cutouts of distant ranges. I live in the saturation. In the occupation. Where every overhang shelters, every depression holds, every surface sweats or hums or waits. I move through the negative spaces the predators use, but I do not hide. I am simply part of the temperature. My furless plates absorb heat in the sun, shed it in shadow, rendering me legible only to those who read gradients rather than shapes. The grazers step around me without breaking stride, their thermal sight reading me as just another warm stone, another damp patch, another part of the landscape's slow exhalation. The predators note my passage and let it go, knowing I carry no meat, only moisture, only rhythm.

If you asked the machines, they would click, thrum, cycle, and I would answer by shifting my weight, by tilting my arms, by pressing my spine to a weeping plate and feeling the current rise. Certain rock faces sweat more heavily when the buried generators engage. I know which ones. I press against them before the thermostats click. I let the stone's response fill the channels along my skin. I carry it to the ceramic catchers, to the cracked gaskets, to the shallow hollows where moth larvae unmake rotting rubber into powder and salt. I do not plan this. I adjust. The place has taught me its grammar: heat moves upward, moisture follows cool surfaces, life clusters where the two meet, and the old systems breathe with the stone. I am a sentence in that language. A living seam between what was built and what grew after.

I do not wonder what I was before. There is no before. Only the cycle. Only the packed, quiet, hot-and-damp persistence. The loop does not close; it spirals. Each turn leaves a trace: a mineral-stained groove along my forearm, a callus shaped to the curve of a weeping bolt, a plate that resonates when static jumps between corroded terminals, a gait that matches the slow mechanical breath below. I am not a keeper. I am not a guardian. I am a thread. I am the damp moving uphill. The heat finding shadow. The stone remembering how to sweat. I am exactly what the place requires: slow, deliberate, stacked, synced. I breathe when the valley breathes. I wait when the air holds. I move when the condensation falls.

The sun climbs. The air thickens. Moisture lifts, carrying the scent of warm oxide and wet dust. I retreat into the lee of a collapsed boiler housing, press my back against stone that has forgotten how to crack, and let the heat settle into my plates. Somewhere below, a thermostat clicks. A pump engages. A pale lizard drinks from a weeping bolt, throat pulsing, eyes half-closed. Above, a bird clicks through grating. A grazer's padded hooves crunch on mineral crust. The valley, packed and quiet, hot and damp, old and active, continues exactly as it has.

I press my hands to the next weeping bolt. The water beads. Hexagonal. Trembling. I catch it. I carry it. I am here.
