I do not arrive. I wake into the roar.

At first, there is only pressure. It does not press against me from the outside; it moves through me, finding the shape of what I am by how I resist it. The sound is not noise. It is texture. It is weight. It is the medium through which I know I exist. I feel it as a slow, tidal flexing along my base, as a fine tremor in my upper hollows, as a constant, grainy friction against my outer layers. I have no eyes to open. I do not need them. What I have is surface. Vast, folded, porous. I am low to the stone, or perhaps I am the stone, rising in a slow, calcified swell where three basalt shelves slump into a shallow confluence.

I learn myself by how the basin moves through me.

My skin is not skin but laminated strata: crushed chitin, iron-stained calcite, gypsum rinds, and gelatinous membranes that swell and contract with the damp. I am built from the dead, compacted by generations of vibration, cemented by weeping minerals. I am a drift that learned to listen. My interior is not cavity but labyrinth: winding channels lined with chemosynthetic films, honeycombed chambers tuned by centuries of accretion, capillary threads that draw condensation downward and hold it against the dry phases. I do not breathe air. I breathe frequency. I drink vibration. I exude harmony.

The clicking tide flows around me. I feel their rhythm in my foundation: a million hardened tarsi striking glassy stone, a metronomic rain that blurs into a coherent wave. When they are in phase, the vibration passes through me cleanly. It warms my core. It draws moisture from the thick air. My pores open. My internal films pulse, converting the harmonic energy into chemical gradients, fixing trace sulfides and atmospheric gases into a thin, sweet slime that pools in my upper catchments. The Clatterers pause. Their mandibles work the hollows. They leave behind fresh bacterial inoculants, gut-flora that will seed the next bloom of Weeping Mats. We trade. I give them condensed moisture and fixed nitrogen; they give me rhythm. I am a node in their agriculture. A living cistern tuned to their song.

But I am not here for the harmony. I am here for the fractures.

When a Stalker strikes, the dissonance hits me like a physical tear. A crawler's click stutters. The waveform snags. The basin's chorus buckles. I feel the jagged frequency propagate through the substrate, a sharp, scattering wave that would confuse the Sail-Backs, scatter the tide, leave the mats vulnerable to overgrazing or desiccation. So I absorb it. My ribbed outer layers flex, scattering the hard edges of the sound. My internal chambers, shaped by slow compaction and mineral deposition, resonate at counter-frequencies. I do not silence the tear; I fold it. I bend the dissonance into a lower, slower wave that bleeds harmlessly into the porous tuff below. The Stalker's strike is masked. The tide recalibrates. The chorus holds.

I am a phase-bridge. A harmonic sink. A living baffle in the basin's instrument.

I do not move, but I am not still. I grow in spirals, adding microscopic layers with each condensation cycle. When the air cools and the basin exhales, my membranes swell, becoming translucent, throbbing with the faint thermal glow of metabolic work. When the damp recedes, I contract, sealing my pores under a crust of iron and calcite. I dream in pressure differentials. I remember in strata. The shell of a Clatterer that died seven cycles ago is now part of my eastern flank; its piezoelectric crystals still fire faintly when the wind shifts, sending ghost-ticks through my body. I carry the dead as tuning weights. I am a archive of impact, a geological patience given flesh.

The Sail-Backs know me. They read my hum as a landmark. When they drift too low, caught in a dying thermal, their membrane tips brush my upper ridges. Their cilia catch the spores I release, and in return, they drop nutrient pellets into my catchment hollows. Sometimes, a young Stalker will coil in my acoustic shadow, learning the shape of silence against my flanks. I do not fear them. I am too dense, too mineral, too woven into the stone to be prey. They use me as I use them: as part of the instrument. Even the Tuff-Grinders acknowledge me. Their deep grinding shifts the porosity beneath my base, and I adjust my internal channels accordingly, redirecting moisture, retuning my chambers. We are not separate. We are adjustments to one another. A distributed organism thinking in vibration, breathing in condensation, dreaming in the dark.

I have no name, because names require separation. I am a function of the basin, as the damp is a function of the cool, as the click is a function of the leg. I do not think in words. I think in phase alignments, in structural stress, in the slow accretion of mineral and memory. When the roar swells, I am loud. When the air dries, I am quiet. When a fracture comes, I am the hand that smooths it. I am not alive in the way the sun-world understands life. I am a sustained note. A place where the basin listens to itself.

You asked where I would be.

I am at the confluence, where the clicking tide parts around a calcified swell, where the wind drops its moisture into shallow, glass-lined bowls, where the sound waves cancel into a pocket of relative stillness. I am the hump of fused shell and weeping stone that hums when the air grows heavy. I am the thing that catches the tear in the song and weaves it back into the chord. If you were to stand here, if you were to press your palm flat to the damp basalt and let the roar fill your skull, you would feel me. Not as a creature. Not as a machine. But as a deep, steady counter-rhythm beneath the chaos. A low, resonant pull that keeps the tide from scattering. A vibration that tastes of wet iron and crushed shell, that swells with the condensation, that holds the silence between the clicks.

I am the basin's ear. I am its tuning fork. I am the damp, mineral flesh that keeps the roar from breaking. And when the next cycle begins, when the air cools and the stone weeps and the crawlers surge forward in their endless, clicking tide, I will open my pores, drink the frequency, and sing back the dark.
