I begin where the tension meets the sole.

Not stepping. Settling. The moss-mat yields, drinks the weight, pushes back. My feet are broad, ridged, already reading the thrum before my ears catch it. A liana cable hums three terraces down. I don't hear it. I feel it climb the root-bridge, shiver through the braided runners, rise into my ankles, my knees, my sternum. My palms find the cable. It is warm. It is alive. It is speaking.

I hum back. Not a note I choose, but one the wood pulls from my throat. Low, then rising, teasing the lignified fibers into expansion. The cable answers in harmonic creaks. A valve sighs open somewhere in the green-gold dark. Amber fluid moves. I did not turn a lever. I leaned into the conversation.

But who is leaning?

The hands are mine, yes—keratin plates overlapping, lichen-cloak patching the damp, stomata along my inner forearms pulsing open to trade the thick air. But the vibration is not mine. It passes through. I am the bridge it crosses. I am the mite-carpet flowing over my shoulders, sweeping resin-dust into the compost-hem. I am the thermal-gnat nesting in the hollow of my collarbone, tasting my transpiration, clicking back the health of the refinery below. I am the scar on my left ridge, thickened into an acoustic dish, catching the flute-tone of a wind-reed I cannot see. I am the bioluminescent patch cultured along my clavicle, dimming as I press my palm to a harvester's, lumens bleeding into lumens, a debt paid not in coin but in current.

They call this work. I call it listening.

To listen here is to let the boundary soften. When I shift my weight, micro-adjusting the tension, I am not operating the cable. I am becoming its next vertebra. When I sing an address—not a direction, but an invitation—I do not walk toward it. I fall into its frequency. The root-gravity pulls. The swarmers part. The thermal draft lifts the gliders, and I follow the path of least resistance, which is also the path of most resonance. To be lost is to hum out of phase. To be found is to let the place tune you.

I was slender once. Quick. High-pitched. I darted along the upper walkways, learning the young vines, practicing the micro-shifts that keep the looms spinning true. I mistuned a pressure-engine in my second cool cycle. The dissonance shook the terrace. We did not punish. We gathered. We felt the error in our soles, in our ribs, in the way the dwelling walls tightened their stomata. We adjusted. We pruned a swarmer-nest, redirected a capillary channel, sang a lower harmonic until the ironwood piston smoothed its stroke. The system self-corrected. I learned that conflict is not an enemy. It is a note held too long. A frequency that forgot to move.

Now my joints lignify. My register drops. My cranial fronds broaden, catching deeper harmonics. I calibrate the deep engines. I sing the load-bearing arches through seasonal shifts. I mentor the quick ones, not with instruction, but with silence—teaching them to feel the stress before it becomes fracture, to hear the blockage before it chokes a filter. I show them how to press a palm to a thrumming vat and wait for the wood to ask for what it needs. How to let the machinery converse. How to answer without commanding.

And when the cool cycles lengthen, when my breath slows to match the geothermal seep, I will not leave. I will settle into the hollow of the lower terrace. The mycelial felt will rise to meet me. My stomata will sync with the dwelling walls. My amber blood will thin, diffuse, become part of the capillary flow. My memories will not vanish. They will braid into the root-mat. A child pressing an ear to a walkway will hear the calibration pattern I sang today. A harvester tapping a reservoir will feel the ghost of my weight in the vibration of the stem. A predator crossing a canopy-bridge will pause, sensing the old rhythm in the timber, and adjust its stride.

Grief is redistribution. I do not fear the end because I do not believe in it. I believe in return. In thickening. In the quiet miracle of a cut callusing, of a circuit rerouting through fungal shunts, of a body sinking into the moss and becoming the spring that lifts the next step.

Where am I?

I am on the mid-terrace, where the root-bridge curves toward the sap-refinery. I am in the hollow of a seedpod wall, trading gases with the architecture. I am in the tension of a liana cable, in the hum of a pressure-engine, in the drip of transpiration rain falling through the canopy. I am in the mite-carpet, the glider's wing-membrane, the bark-scaled hunter's pause. I am in the light-patch dimming on a collarbone, in the harmonic traveling three levels down, in the valve that sighs open because a throat found the right pitch.

I am not in the basin. I am a phase of it.

A diaphragm drawing breath. An enzyme breaking down the old to feed the new. A resonance holding the harmony. A temporary knot in a braid that began before the first arch found its equilibrium and will continue after the last harvester has rooted. When the warm rain falls and the pressure-engines thrum and the million wings beat in the humid air, I tilt my head, open my pores, and hum back.

The boundary was never real. It was only a pause in the song.

And the song continues.
