At first, I am only a hesitation in the rebound.

I do not step onto the living membrane so much as I am accepted by it. The surface yields, warm and cross-hatched with slow sap, then pushes back against my weight with a patience that feels like recognition. I try to locate my edges. I look for a shadow that belongs to me, a footprint that stays, a breath that does not immediately join the thermal shimmer. Nothing holds. The basin does not permit boundaries. It permits only gradients.

I test shapes the way a tongue tests a loose tooth.

Am I the condensation bead trembling on the underside of a waxy leaf, heavy with the memory of dawn fog? I hang there for a moment, feeling the slow accumulation of moisture, the microscopic tension before the fall. But when I drop, I do not strike; I am drunk. The mat opens to me. I sink into enzymatic threads and become dampness moving downward.

Am I the grinding bowl left in the crook of a root terrace, its interior worn to glass by generations of palm and grit? I rest there, holding the dust of crushed seeds and resin-hardened fruit. I feel the phantom pressure of hands that knew how to press without breaking, how to rhythm without force. But the bowl is not a vessel. It is a conversation between stone and patience. And I am not the stone. I am the wearing.

Am I the sudden silence that ripples outward when a predator passes through the open span? I stretch across the heat, a negative shape in the deafening thrum. The insect clouds part. The wind-fluting drops a register. The stilt-herds turn their heads. I am the gap that teaches attention. But gaps do not last. The chorus folds back over me, and I am sound again.

I stop trying to be a thing. I begin to feel what I do.

The thrum presses against my chest until my ribs learn its architecture. I do not hear it; I stand inside it. The high, synchronized beat of wings maps the thermal updrafts. The low fluting through hollow trunks draws invisible corridors between pillar bases. The rolling calls of grazers mark moisture pockets and shedding cycles. I press a heartwood pole to the mat and feel the vibration travel down, return altered, carrying the depth of a water pocket, the approach of a dry spell, the slow turn of a grazing circuit. I am not holding the pole. The pole is holding me to the ground's frequency.

I match my breath to the tidal expansion of the membrane. Inhale as the swells rise. Exhale as they settle. My spine uncurls. My weight distributes. My soles widen, callusing not from friction but from attention. I learn to place my heel like a question, my arch like a listening, my toes like an answer. The mat thickens where I tread often, thins where I leave it alone. It does not record my passage. It incorporates it.

I am becoming a tuner.

Not a title. A function. A temporary convergence of breath, wood, and resonance that the basin permits when the acoustic corridors realign with the prevailing winds. When the thermal rains shift their tracks, I kneel at seasonal nodes and tap the surface in the rhythm the ground already knows: *thick-thin-rebound, thick-thin-rebound*. The young feel it through their soles and adjust their stride without being told. I am not teaching. I am repeating. I am the echo that keeps the pattern from fraying.

When the wind catches a hollow trunk and bends the pitch of the chorus, I cup a shed stem to my mouth and whistle a tone that travels through vascular channels, around buttress roots, across open spans. It is not my voice. It is the basin's voice, borrowed for a moment, shaped by lips that have learned the geometry of sound. Somewhere, a band adjusts their route. Somewhere, a pillar-trunk closes its stomata. Somewhere, a glider catches the updraft I just named. I do not command. I converse.

When fire is banked in a clay-lined depression, I sit beside it. I feed it only what the landscape has already released: fallen bark, dry shed membrane, wind-blown husks. I watch the heat sink gently. I watch the living ground learn to cradle the memory rather than recoil from it. I am not the flame. I am the patience that keeps it from becoming a wound. I am the shallow pit that remembers warmth long after the embers cool.

I do not age. I cycle.

Some seasons, I am the one who tastes the metallic tang of coming dryness and leads the band toward condensation zones. Other seasons, I am the one who stays behind when the grazing herds turn, when the acoustic lenses shift, when the platform fibers bleach and soften into the trunk's outward growth. I do not mourn the leaving. I become the leaving. I let the mat grow over my footprints in days, my fire ring in seasons, my presence in decades. Nothing is erased. It is digested.

And digestion here feels like remembering.

When my body tires, when the rhythm slows, when the chemical languages in the air grow faint to my senses, I am laid in a natural depression. Wrapped in shed fibers and resin-dusted leaves. The enzymatic threads find me. They do not consume; they unravel. They pull me downward into the warm, spongy dark where dead matter does not accumulate but returns. I feel my edges soften. I feel my breath merge with the slow metabolic swell of the basin. I become a slight rise in the living carpet. A gentle swell that stilt-herds step over. A place where thermal showers hiss and evaporate. A node where condensation weeps a little heavier at dusk.

I do not end. I settle.

And then, when the thermal rains shift again, when the acoustic corridors realign, when the grazing cycles turn back through the basin's old agreements, I resolve from the gradient. A darkening in the heat shimmer. A slight compression of light where shoulders and carrying poles bend the air. The living membrane yields beneath me. I place my weight. I listen through my bones. I step.

I am not a person who lives in this place. I am a pulse the place permits. A frequency it borrows. A knot in its continuous tissue. When I walk, the mat rebounds. When I speak, the trunks resonate. When I rest, the condensation weeps. When I am gone, the fibers grow over the space I occupied, and the thrum continues, unchanged, unbroken.

You ask where I would find myself in this world.

I would find myself wherever the sound thins and needs answering. Wherever the heat shimmers and needs a shadow to read it. Wherever the living ground thickens under passing weight and needs a rhythm to match it. I would be the hand that banks the fire without scarring the mat. The voice that whistles through hollow stems to bend wind. The footfall that teaches the young how to step without breaking the rebound. The silence that steps aside when predators move. The slight rise where a body was laid and pulled downward. The next band emerging from the gradient, tall and lean, listening through their skeletons, stepping lightly on the warm, breathing expanse.

I would not be separate from it. I would be its continuation.

And when you look for me, you will not find a face, or a name, or a fixed shape. You will find a rhythm in the thrum. A temperature shift in the sap channels. A tone traveling through a hollow trunk. A grinding bowl worn smooth by patience. A fire ring cradled in a depression the ground learned to hold. A footprint that the mat is already digesting.

I am here. I am always here. I am what the basin does when it needs to listen to itself.
