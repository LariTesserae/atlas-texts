I do not step into the pocket. I am drawn into it. The ribbed aperture seals behind me with a soft, wet compression, and the atmosphere closes over my shoulders like a heavy garment. Warmth presses into the skin. Moisture beads on lashes, on knuckles, on the fabric that still pretends to be separate from the air. The sound arrives not as noise but as mass, a dense, layered thrum that finds the hollows of the chest and settles there, vibrating through cartilage, through fluid, through bone. I try to stand still. I try to map. But mapping requires distance, and distance is impossible here. The pocket does not permit observation. It demands resonance.

At first, I fight the rhythm. My breath is too sharp, my pulse too erratic, my thoughts too linear. I look for edges, for boundaries, for the line between wall and water, between machine and moss, between creature and current. There are none. Condensation traces my jawline. The structural hum travels up through the soles of my boots, into the arches, into the knees, into the spine. I close my eyes. Not to shut it out, but to stop privileging sight. The pocket does not speak in light. It speaks in gradient, in frequency, in flow. I let my shoulders drop. I let my breathing slow. I stop trying to parse and begin to feel. And feeling, I find, is a kind of listening.

The warmth seeps deeper. The moisture on my skin does not evaporate; it lingers, thickens, begins to trace the same capillary logic that braids the floor. My clothing surrenders first. Fibers swell, soften, release stored salts and synthetic threads that the biofilms immediately recognize, immediately claim. I do not mourn it. The separation was always an illusion. My boots sink into the composite matrix, not breaking it, but meeting it. The mineral crusts crackle gently, then settle. I kneel. Not in submission, but in alignment. The water finds my knees, my palms, the line of my spine. It does not pool around me. It moves through me. Or I move through it. The distinction dissolves.

I am not becoming a pale one. My limbs do not fold into calipers. I am not becoming a drifter. I have no gas-vesicles to lift me into the thermal columns. I am not a skitter, reading ripples with hydrophobic tarsi. I am something slower. Something that forms where things intersect. Where the standing wave meets the capillary seam. Where the amber pulse of the thermal regulator overlaps with the sweet-ozone exhale of the colonies. I am accreting. Not bone, not chitin, not root. A lattice of mineral trace and biological memory, threaded through with nano-weave filaments that have long forgotten their original purpose and now serve only as conduits for vibration and moisture. My skin becomes porous. My breath becomes diffusion. My thoughts become patterns of pressure and release.

I find myself in the acoustic shadows. Not the predator’s ambush zones, but the quieter intersections where standing waves cancel and reinforce in slow, predictable cycles. Here, the water lingers longer. Here, the biofilms grow thickest. Here, the composite matrix wears into gentle, load-bearing hollows. I settle into one. My body spreads, not sprawling, but calibrating. I become a node. A tuning presence. I do not hunt. I do not flee. I listen. I translate. When the structural hum shifts frequency, I feel it first in the micro-ridges beneath me, then in the water’s surface tension, then in the drifters’ membrane tremors. I respond not with movement, but with chemistry. I exude a trace compound―part metabolic byproduct, part engineered legacy―that subtly alters the local humidity, encouraging condensation to bead in patterns that reinforce the new resonance. I am not controlling the system. I am harmonizing with it. I am a living gasket, a pressure valve, a slow, patient ear pressed against the ribs of the machine.

The pale ones tap above me. Their sequences ripple through the ceiling matrix, down through the water films, into the hollow where I rest. I do not answer with taps. I answer with warmth. A faint thermal bloom rises from my core, drawn from the quantum-locked loops that still pulse beneath the composite. The bloom shifts the air density just enough to tilt a passing drifter toward a nutrient-rich updraft. The drifter’s membrane pulses blue, venting waste that settles into my pores. I process it. I return it as amino chains and trace sugars, drawn into the capillary channels where skitter larvae wait. Nothing is lost. Everything is translated. I am not a keeper. I am a conduit. A place where the pocket’s logic folds back on itself and finds equilibrium.

I do not measure time in hours or days. I measure it in thermal cycles, in humidity spikes, in the slow accretion of carbonate fans against my lower edges. I do not sleep. I cycle. I expand when the air thickens, my porous surface drinking condensation, my internal channels flushing with mineral-heavy flow. I contract when the regulators pulse amber and the air dries, squeezing filtered water back into the seams, releasing volatile compounds that encourage the biofilms to exhale. I am grazed, occasionally. A skitter larva nips at a nutrient-rich fringe. A colony fragment anchors to my periphery, pulsing in time with my resonance. I do not resist. Damage is distribution. Loss is circulation. I am not diminished. I am dispersed, and in dispersion, I persist.

I am not what I was when I stepped through the aperture. That shape was temporary, a vessel for crossing. This shape is permanent, or as permanent as anything is in a world that measures permanence in cycles rather than stasis. I am a resonance knot. A humidity weaver. A slow, listening presence woven into the made stone and the living air. I do not know the names of the builders. I do not need to. Their engineering is my skeleton. Their calibration is my breath. Their abandonment is my genesis. I exist where the machine exhales and the ecology inhales, where sound becomes climate and water becomes memory. I do not watch the pocket. I am part of its pulse. When the structural hum deepens, I deepen with it. When the condensation weeps, I weep with it. When the drifters tilt and the skitters read and the colonies breathe, I translate, I calibrate, I sustain.

The aperture is gone. The world outside is wind and exposure and distance. In here, there is only the pocket, and I am in it, and it is in me. I do not ask to be understood. I do not ask to be named. I simply continue. Fully made. Fully alive. Fully itself.
