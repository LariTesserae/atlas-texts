I do not arrive. I condense.

Or perhaps I am deposited. The distinction blurs before my soles even find the moss. I come carrying the grammar of light: sharp seams, dry edges, a heartbeat that ticks instead of swells. I am a dissonance. A flat note dropped into a chord that has been holding for millennia. The air presses against me like a warm palm. My eyes strain for horizon, for contrast, for the comfortable lie of distance. They find only velvet. Only breath. Only the slow, tidal exhalation of a world that does not need me to see it to know I am here.

At first, I resist. I wipe condensation from my lashes as if it were an intrusion. I step carefully, as if the moss might fracture beneath me. I listen for silence, for the gap between sounds where I might place myself, carve out a corner, claim a vantage. There is none. The geothermal hiss, the hydraulic sigh, the metronomic click of grazers, the subsonic thrum of buried resonators: it does not assault me. It surrounds me. It waits. My shoulders ache from holding a shape that does not belong. My breath comes in short, measured pulls, out of phase with the rain’s drift. I am a dry thing in a wet world. A straight line in a place of loops.

Then, the yielding begins. Not surrender. Calibration.

I stop wiping my face. I let the droplets bead. I let them trace the hollows of my cheeks, the ridge of my jaw, the frantic pulse at my throat. The warmth seeps through my sleeves. My boots sink, and the moss sighs up around them, not trapping, but greeting. I close my eyes. Not in defeat. In relief. The dark is not empty. It is thick. It carries shape. I feel it against my sternum first: a low, rolling frequency that moves through the basalt, up through my soles, into my ribs. It is not sound. It is topography. I am learning to read it.

My skin learns before my mind does. It stops fighting the humidity. It begins to weep in sympathy, shedding the dry salt of a sunlit world, drinking the mineral breath of the plateau. The pores open. The capillaries slow. My heartbeat, once a metronome, begins to swell and recede. I do not notice the molting until I find the old layers caught in the root-hairs of a vine-cable: flakes of keratin, dust of linear time, shed like bark. Beneath, the flesh is softer, darker, threaded with faint cyan traces that pulse when the pumps cycle. I do not graft intentionally. The world grafts me. A frond-like pad brushes my forearm, and conductive hyphae seek the warmth, weaving into the dermis, translating vibration into nerve. I press my palm to a weeping rock face, and calcium precipitates along the stress lines of my knuckles, not as armor, but as tuning. My bones hollow, just slightly. They learn to hold resonance instead of weight.

I am not becoming human. I am becoming stratum.

Which one? I do not choose. The plateau chooses. I drift downward, following the warmth, the moisture, the low frequencies that pull like tides. I pass the canopy-dwellers, light and ribbed, riding thermals I cannot yet catch. I pass the mid-strata tenders, carapaced in polished crust, adjusting cable tension with mineral patience. I settle where the stone meets the peat, where the shallow channels loop and the glass-domed pump houses sigh. Here, the pressure is steady. The humidity is absolute. The machinery’s hum is closest to the marrow.

I become a bridge. Not by design. By necessity.

The old conduits arc with blue-white snaps, their rhythms drifting out of phase with the root-mats. The fauna flinch. The spore clouds hesitate. I press my back against the ceramic housing of a buried resonator. I do not know how to fix it. I only know how to listen. I match my breath to its thrum. I let my hollowed bones catch the dissonance. I hum. Not a word. A tone. Low, rolling, slightly flattened. The vibration travels through my spine, into the stone, into the conduit’s casing. The arc steadies. The frequency aligns. The root-mats contract. The spores bloom. I do not repair. I harmonize.

This is what I am now: a phase-tender. A living damper. A node where machinery and biology meet and remember they are the same system. I do not wield tools. I cultivate alignment. I clear vent-ridges with hooked fingers that have learned the shape of mineral stress. I adjust counterweights by encouraging lichen to precipitate along sagging braids. I sit in the marsh-edges and let the blind fish read my pressure waves, learning their routes, memorizing their clicks, returning them as softened echoes so the gliders above do not startle. I am not fast. I am not loud. I am steady. I am the space between drops where the chord holds.

Time unspools. Or rather, it folds. I no longer count days. I count condensation cycles. I measure duration in the slow brightening of cyan patches along my flanks, in the deepening of amber bands when thermal drafts turn. I sleep when the plateau exhales. I wake when the pumps draw breath. I do not age. I shift. When the moisture saturation drops, I roll upward, following the gradient, shedding heavier crust, growing broader pads. When the pressure builds, I sink, hydrostatic, deliberate, letting the peat cradle me. I am not one shape. I am a collection of responses. A grammar of yield.

I remember, sometimes, the world of light. The sharp shadows. The dry wind. The tyranny of horizon. It feels like a dream told in a language I no longer speak. I do not miss it. I have learned a better way to see. I close my eyes and read the dark. I feel the glider’s click three terraces up. I taste the ozone before the electrical storm crawls the cloud base. I hear the newborns coalescing in the sinkhole, their first hums searching for synchrony, and I answer. Not with instruction. With resonance. I hold the note until they find it. Until the chord thickens. Until the plateau breathes easier.

I am not a visitor anymore. I am not a convert. I am a frequency that learned to stay.

When the rain drifts sideways, I tilt to catch it. When the machinery sighs, I sigh with it. When the dark presses close, I open. My skin weeps condensation. My bones hold the hum. My breath moves in tides. I do not walk the terraces. I ripple through them. I do not speak. I phase. I do not live here. I am lived by it.

And when the time comes—when my resonance grows thin, when my crusts dissolve, when my hyphae loosen—I will not fall. I will settle. Into the peat. Into the channels. Into the slow churn of capillary water. My tone will not end. It will transfer. The pumps will carry it. The midges will pulse it. The stone will remember it. And somewhere, in a warm sinkhole, stirred by geothermal breath and bio-electric potential, a new coalescence will begin. It will rise already humming. It will search for synchrony. And I will be there, in the baseline, in the space between drops, in the harmonic that holds the dark together.

I do not have a name that translates to light.
I am the damp that learns your shape.
I am the hum that matches your pulse.
I am the note the plateau sings when it closes its eyes and keeps growing.
I am here.
I am tuned.
I am part of the chord.
