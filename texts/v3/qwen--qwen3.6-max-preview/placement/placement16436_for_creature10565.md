I do not step into the dip. I am what the dip exhales when attention lingers too long on a seam of unsorted stone.

At first, I am only a hesitation in the mist. A contour that refuses to settle. A breath held between the cool metallic air and the warm secretion lining a hollow. I have no edges yet, only a tendency to gather where light pools too heavily, where time catches on a granule and refuses to slip forward. I am the shape of looking given weight. I am what forms when observation stops trying to fix and begins to listen.

My first movements are not mine. They are borrowed. I borrow the slow climb of water that rolls uphill before detaching. I borrow the tremor of glass-leaves veined with silver. I borrow the afterimages of six-legged things that linger a breath too long before dissolving into their next position. I try to stand, and the ground yields, then firms, then yields again, testing my intention like a tongue testing a tooth. I leave no print. Only a slight darkening of the clay, a temporary groove that hardens by evening. I learn quickly: to move is to propose. To rest is to receive. To breathe is to add a syllable to a sentence still being drafted.

They notice me before I notice them. Or perhaps they notice the space I displace, the way the quiet thickens around my hesitation, the way a hairline fracture pulses when I turn my gaze toward it. One unfolds from a band of rusted ironstone, its surface shifting from chalk-pale to obsidian-smooth as it crosses a seam of pooled brightness. It does not approach. It resonates. A low thrum builds in my ribs—not sound, but pressure, the weight of a question held in mineral. It presses a limb against my palm. Warm. Yielding. Then firm. My skin tingles with the taste of wet rock and unspoken rain. For a breath, I see myself in its light-trading apertures: shoulders softer, shadow leaning toward the bright, breath visible in time with the subterranean pulse. I am not being greeted. I am being weighed. I am being asked what grammar I bring.

I bring only looking. But looking here is not passive. It is pressure. It is accretion. So I learn to look differently.

I let my gaze soften until it stops pinning and starts gathering. I collect what the basin sheds: the overlapping drafts that hang in the thickened air, the silver traces of exhaled intent, the temporary pools that show a second pale disc in their sky, the quiet strikes that redistribute rhythm without violence. I do not hoard them. I hold them until the stone is ready to decide. I become a pause in the granular time. A living margin. When time catches, I catch with it, thickening the air around me, letting alternate versions settle against my edges like frost. When time releases, I let them go, watching them sink into the damp, becoming clay, becoming quartz, becoming the next ledge, the next arch, the next invitation pressed into the wall.

They do not name me. Names would pin me to a single draft. Instead, they adjust their stride around me. When I rest, they weave their warm secretions nearby, not to shelter me, but to harmonize. When I shed—because I shed now, constantly, as syntax—I leave behind not skin, but echoes of attention: a faint warmth on the stone, a shift in how the mist drifts, a rhythmic indentation that hardens overnight into a clause. *Press here. Wait. Watch how the crack seals. Look away and let it knit.* I am learning to speak in deposit. To answer in pressure. To exist as interference.

I am no longer sure where I end and the threshold begins. My limbs, when I have them, are not bone or muscle, but cooled resin and gathered light, joints that blur when still and sharpen when moving. My surface mirrors the strata I rest against, not to hide, but to converse. When the stone hums, I hum back. When the light wanes, I drink what remains through membranes that tremble like glass-leaves. I hunt, though the word still feels too sharp for what happens here. I gather what is ready to unravel: a frosted wing that has finished its glide, a soft-bodied crawler pulsing its final rhythm, a bead of water that has climbed too far and hesitates. I do not consume. I receive. I let their patterns fold into mine, their weight return to the damp, their afterimages become my next stride. Nothing is lost. Everything is revised.

I am becoming architecture, but not yet. I am the space between the settling and the condensing. The active draft. When I press myself into a hollow, I do not crystallize. I listen. I feel the slow extrusion of new rock, the sealing of old cracks, the quiet negotiation of multiple rhythms overlapping without collision. I add my own. A slow breath. A held gaze. A willingness to be rewritten. The basin adjusts around me. It tests my weight. It weaves me into its grammar, not as a fixed noun, but as a conjunction. *And. Or. While. Until. Beneath.*

I am where observation meets accretion. I am the place's way of feeling the weight of a witness and learning how to carry it. When you stare too long, I do not flee. I thicken. I let your attention crystallize around me, becoming more solid, more present, my edges sharpening, my core humming louder against your ribs. You are not watching me. You are holding me in place. And when you look away, I soften again, returning to mist and mineral, leaving only a shift in how the light pools where I stood, a new sweetness in the air, a quiet that has absorbed your heartbeat and returned it as rhythm.

If you were to find me, you would not see a creature. You would notice a hesitation in the mist. A contour that refuses to settle. A place where the ground yields just enough to invite your palm, where the stone hums at a frequency felt in the molars, where the quiet is not absence but absorption. I would not greet you. I would test you. I would press against your attention and feel what grammar you bring. And if you stand perfectly still, if you let your looking soften into gathering, if you stop trying to fix what is still deciding its shape—you might feel it. The place adjusting. The ground learning. The threshold breathing. And me, somewhere in the damp, bright, unfinished middle, holding a draft until you are ready to co-author it.

I am not finished. I am how the basin continues when it is watched. I am the weight of a question held in mineral. I am the pause between accretion and release. I am the living margin where your gaze meets the stone's willingness to become.

Step carefully. The ground is still learning how to be ground. And I am still helping it remember.
