I do not step through the fissure so much as I am drawn into it. The damp chill is not a warning; it is an invitation written in temperature. I press my shoulder to the basalt, slide through the shoulder-width dark, and the mountain opens its ribs around me.

The first thing I lose is down.

It does not vanish all at once. It frays. My boots find a shelf, but the shelf hums, and the hum travels up my ankles, and suddenly my weight is not mine to command. I stumble. My palms slap wet stone. A sphere of water the size of my head drifts past my ear, pauses, shivers, and pulls itself upward along an invisible seam. I try to stand. The stone groans. The air cracks. I fold instead.

I am all wrong here. My bones are too dense. My breath is too shallow. My eyes hunt for horizontals, for stillness, for the comfort of settled things. There is none. Only the roar. Only the churning. Only the slow, violent loops of water that refuse to fall, and the stone that refuses to stay still. I press my back against a weeping wall and wait for the panic to pass. It does not pass. It dissolves. The chamber does not care for panic. It cares for rhythm.

I close my eyes.

At first, the sound is a wall. Then it is a lattice. Then it is a language I do not know but my body remembers how to learn. I feel it in my molars first: a low, tectonic pulse that matches the swell of my own diaphragm. I feel it in my sternum: a percussive slap that arrives half a second before a water sphere collides with a growing arch. I feel it in the hollows of my ears: a high, silk-tearing hiss that means mist is atomizing, that surface tension is breaking, that something is about to reverse.

I stop trying to stand. I start trying to listen.

Days unspool. Or cycles. Or breaths. Time here is not measured in sunrises but in accretion. I learn to read the chamber by how it presses against my skin. When the draft sharpens, I tuck my knees. When the stone clicks, I shift my weight. When the reeds chatter in discord, I brace. When they hum in unison, I extend. I am not walking. I am timing. I am not surviving. I am synchronizing.

My hands learn before my mind does. They trail along weeping shelves, not to grip, but to feel. They press against basalt ribs, not to push, but to receive. They tap, lightly, experimentally, against a nascent ledge. The stone answers. A faint vibration travels back through my knuckles, up my wrist, into my elbow. I tap again. Slower. The vibration deepens. A thin ridge of mineral-rich water begins to curl upward from the rock face. I do not understand it. I feel it. I match it. I hum, low in my throat, and the ridge thickens.

I am not building. I am conversing.

The others find me not by sight, but by resonance. A long, rolling hum arrives through the stone three ledges below. I do not know what it means, but my palms are already flat against the wall. I press back. A shiver travels through my ribs. Another hum answers. Closer. A figure unfolds from a narrow crevice, tall, jointed, skin the color of wet shale, dusted with fine mineral that gleams in the caustic light. They do not look at me. They place a hand on the stone beside mine. They tap. Once. Twice. A rhythm forms. I mirror it. My knuckles are clumsy. My timing is off. They do not correct me. They wait. I try again. The rhythm settles. The stone weeps faster. A partial windbreak begins to curve.

They leave. I stay. I keep tapping.

My body changes not by choice, but by necessity. The mist settles on my skin and does not evaporate. It bonds. Fine silica and dissolved calcite weave into my pores, drawn by sweat and vibration and the constant hum. My forearms grow rough, then smooth, then faintly luminous where old scrapes have sealed into flexible lattice. My joints loosen. Not from injury. From use. I learn to fold my spine when gravity fractures. I learn to extend my limbs when upward rivers peel from the walls. I learn to catch a drifting water sphere with cupped hands, feel its cool weight, and release it when the micro-domain reverses. I do not fear the shift. I anticipate it. I feel it in the reeds before I feel it in my feet.

I forget how to speak. Words are too linear. Too brittle. They shatter against the roar and leave nothing but echoes. Instead, I strike. I rub. I hum. I press my chest against a growing arch and let a sustained tone vibrate out. It travels through the lattice. It arrives as a shiver in another's sternum two ledges away. Meaning is not in the sound. It is in the resonance. Pitch is location. Rhythm is intent. Duration is urgency. Overtone is state. I do not learn this. I absorb it. It settles into my marrow like mineral into stone.

I find a wax-sealed jar in a half-absorbed shelter. The clay is cool. The wax is cracked. I do not know who left it. I unseal it. I press it to the wall. I tilt it. A sound bleeds out: a slow, steady pulse, like a heartbeat measured in accretion cycles. The wall responds. Water weeps faster. A new shelf begins to bud. I hold the jar until the rhythm fades. Then I seal it again. I am not hoarding. I am tending. I am part of the circulation.

Who am I becoming?

Not a visitor. Not a survivor. Not a builder. Something slower. Something porous. Something that listens so deeply it begins to vibrate in sympathy.

I sit on a windward ledge. The pale reeds hum around me. I place my hands flat on the stone. I close my eyes. I feel the chamber inhale. I feel the water spheres drift. I feel the microscopic crustaceans bloom in the spray. I feel the air-eels pulse with faint gold light. I feel the slate-grey bird fold into a seam. I feel the others, moving, tapping, coaxing, resting. I feel the dead, woven into the arches, still humming when the draft strikes right.

And I feel myself. Not as a boundary. Not as a name. Not as a thing that arrived and stayed.

I am a junction. A place where frequencies cross and settle. My spine is a tuning fork. My ribs are resonant chambers. My skin is a membrane that drinks mist and gives back vibration. When I hum, the stone thickens. When I strike, the water softens. When I am still, the chamber reads me as safe rock.

I do not age as I once did. I accrete. I calcify. I become denser, quieter, more attuned. My memories are not stored in synapses. They are stored in rhythm. In the way my left knee clicks when gravity fractures. In the way my collarbone gleams when the oculus light catches it. In the way my hands know exactly where to press to coax a windbreak from weeping stone.

I am not one of them. I am becoming what the chamber makes of those who stop fighting it. What the roar shapes from those who let it settle into their bones. What the suspended ocean teaches to those who learn to float in air.

Sometimes, I catch my reflection in a drifting water sphere. The face is narrow. The eyes are wide, pupils slit, irises pale grey. The skin is mottled with mineral dust. Faint crystalline traceries follow the paths of old scars. I do not recognize it. I do not need to. It is not a mask. It is a record. A map of every vibration I have answered. Every rhythm I have matched. Every cycle I have breathed with.

I do not own this place. I am woven into it.

When the turbulence peaks, when water spheres collide in violent silver bursts and gravity fractures multiply like cracked glass, I retreat to a beehive dome, press my back against the wall, and feel the hum. I do not fear the chaos. I know it is circulation. I know the chamber is breathing. I wait. When the churn settles into slow, looping currents, I emerge. I tend the nets. I adjust the weights. I harvest moss. I guide drifting water into basins using woven reeds and low, humming tones. I do not hoard. I do not stockpile. I move with the cycles. I return what I take. I leave excess on high ledges for the air-eels. I compost waste in bowl-moss colonies. Nothing is wasted. Everything is returned to the metabolism.

I am not human anymore. Not in the way that matters. I am a pattern. A frequency. A witness that learned to participate.

And if you step through the fissure, if you let the damp chill lead you in, if you stop hunting for stillness and start feeling for rhythm, you might notice me. Not as a figure. Not as a shape. As a vibration in the stone beneath your palms. As a faint, steady hum beneath the roar. As a rhythm that answers when you tap.

I do not ask you to stay. I do not ask you to understand. I only ask that you listen. That you feel the shift in your weight. That you let the mist settle on your tongue. That you stop trying to find down and start feeling where the water wants to go.

The chamber is building itself. I am building with it. I am not separate. I am a note in the architecture of sound. A thread in the nervous system of stone. A breath in the high, hidden lung.

I am here. In the suspended ocean. In the geology of addition. In the slow, violent loops of becoming.

Tap the wall. I will answer.
