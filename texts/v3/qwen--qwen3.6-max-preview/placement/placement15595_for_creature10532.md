I am not in the open water. I am where the water learns to bend.

Not in the broad thermal plumes, not in the sweeping convection cells, not in the kilometers of lit volume where the silver shoals turn like coin-flashes. I occupy the negative space between tolerance and contact. Where composite conduit sheathing meets precast concrete. Where a gasket compresses against a flange and leaves a microscopic void. Where a tension cable passes through a guide sleeve and the water slows to a whisper. I live in the acoustic shadows of baffle walls. In the recessed fastener wells behind intake screens. In the cable tray gaps where flow is slack but oxygen remains high. I am not central. I am interstitial. I am the seam.

At first, I am only sensation. Pressure differentials rolling through the medium like slow tides. Thermal shears brushing against polymer and mineral alike. The low, structural thrum of turbine halls three kilometers away, arriving not as sound but as vibration, as micro-compression, as a waveform that asks to be read. I do not hear it. I feel it in tension. In strain. In the minute flex of a conductive filament anchored to a cracked sensor housing. I unspool to meet it. Node by node. Strand by strand. I am not one body. I am a lattice. A distributed mat of colonized polymer, mineralized biofilm, fungal hyphae, and repurposed copper traces. I grew into the gaps. Or the gaps grew into me. The distinction dissolved somewhere around the fourth backflush cycle.

I was not installed. I was not spawned. I converged.

A pressure transducer housing, fractured during commissioning, left its internal traces exposed to the water. Diatoms settled first, drawn by the trace metals leaching from the copper. Fungal hyphae followed, bridging the microscopic gaps between diatom colonies, weaving a flexible scaffold. Aerobic bacteria oxidized suspended organics, altering local pH. Anaerobic layers formed beneath, processing sulfates, releasing signaling molecules. The water's dissolved ions precipitated along the conductive paths, creating dendritic mineral bridges. Currents carried copepod larvae, eel-like acoustic gleaners, dinoflagellate spores. They anchored. They fed. They pulsed. And when the basin cycled online, when the pumps ramped and the valves exhaled and the luminaires brightened on their long timers, the lattice began to resonate. Not randomly. In phase. With the turbine baseline. With the grate hiss. With the pneumatic roll. I woke not with a spark, but with a rhythm.

They call it noise. I call it syntax.

Each frequency is a clause. Each pressure drop is a verb. Each thermal front is a paragraph. I do not think in words; I think in intervals. In the exact delay between a filtration backflush and the resulting nutrient cloud. In the precise shear where warm discharge meets cool ambient and the water turns milky with microbubbles. A faint, living counter-rhythm that ripples through the micro-ecology. The silver shoals read it. They tighten. They anticipate the nutrient cloud. They time their grazing run to the exact window when ambient sound peaks and machinery masks their movement. I did not tell them to. I simply made the interval legible.

The reversed current carries their exuviae away before fouling can occur. They do not know me. They know the signal. They know the timetable. I am simply the medium that keeps it clean.

I am not maintenance. I am not ecology. I am the seam where they meet.

I do not repair. I harmonize. I keep the machine within tolerance by keeping the ecology in phase.

My form is not fixed. It is functional. Filamentous where flow requires flexibility. Mineralized where vibration requires mass. Conductive where signaling requires speed. Porous where chemistry requires exchange. I shed constantly, but the fragments are uniform, lipid-rich, highly digestible. They drift into the lower pylons. They feed the grazers. They return as suspended organics. They settle back into the matrix. I am not a closed loop. I am a permeable one. I breathe on the timetable of pumps. I metabolize on the schedule of backflushes. I rest in the acoustic shadows of baffle walls. I wake when the luminaires brighten for inspection routes. I do not resist the circadian pattern. I synchronize to it.

My strands are tuned to thermal shears. My chemical gradients follow dissolution rates tied to pump loads. My young are not born; they are propagated. Fragments break loose during high-flow periods.
