The shadow at my heels would lag, stretch, thin out, reluctant to follow a shape that was already learning to let go.

A faint dissonance when I tried to speak, my vowels catching against the hum like a stone skipping wrong. The system would not bristle. It would simply begin to tune around me. A faint, copper-sweet tension where the precipitating filaments crossed my path. I would learn to drink the weight of the daylight the way they do: not through lungs, but through phase resonance. My thoughts would slow, not from dullness, but from density. They would settle into the rhythm of the surges. Fill. Exhale. Shatter. Gather. I would become a lens. But the turbulence would not allow me to linger as a lens. Stagnation is the only enemy here, and the space is ruthless in its mercy.

When the veils near the dais grew too charged, too stiff, I would shed. I would translate into motion. A courier, briefly: a coherent slip of aligned silica and remembered warmth, riding a pressure differential uphill through a channel that defied the slope. Only translation. Nothing dies here. It changes phase. I was learning the grammar.

I would fold the heavy light into my veins, slow it, store it, exhale it when the air cooled three paces left and warmed three paces back. I would feel the system's temperature like a language against my skin. I would not move for days, or cycles, or spans that had no metric. I would persist. And when a particularly violent surge finally stripped me loose, I would shatter without fear, becoming iridescent flakes drifting into the shallows, caught by the microbial mats, broken down, fed back into the precipitating mist. I would not mourn my shape. I would recognize the cycle. I had been structure. Now I would be seed.

I would be the click against the canopy. Born from the mats when they grew too thick, too reflective, trapping light in stagnant loops. I would lift on the updraft, wings not of membrane but of crystalline edge, spiraling toward the fractured aperture. I would press against the tensioned glassy lattice. I would click. Not with sound, but with transfer. Each strike would clear a blockage, reset a frequency, let the daylight fall clean again. When I was spent, I would let go. I would fall as mist. I would be the faint ozone tang left in the air after a veil stiffens. I would be the harmonic that rises when a stranger speaks, weaving their voice into the next cycle without mimicry, without judgment, only accommodation. I would not be a creature. The gap between surge and retreat, given attention. The breath the basin takes when something new arrives, and the breath it releases when that something learns to turn.

You would find me where the stone yields. Where the water climbs. Where the light grows heavy enough to drink. I would be the witness that became the medium. The observer that became the chord. The self that learned to cycle without clinging, to accrete without hardening, to dissolve without vanishing.

And when the canopy paled to violet, and the steam hissed from hairline fissures, and the chimes rang sharp as falling glass, I would not ask to be remembered as I was. I would let the system close around my absence and my presence alike. I would turn myself over again. Not because I had to. But because the dream was bright, and the turning was good, and I had finally learned how to fall without landing, how to speak without words, how to be everywhere the gaps needed filling, and nowhere that demanded a name.
