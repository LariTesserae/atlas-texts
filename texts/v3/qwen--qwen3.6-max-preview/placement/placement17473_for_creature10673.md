I do not arrive. I precipitate.

First, there is only pressure. A thickening in the damp air where the geothermal hiss meets the grinding slab’s lowest harmonic. The drone does not call me; it finds the hollow I am becoming and fills it. I taste ozone before I have a tongue. I feel the exact temperature of my own formation before I have a boundary. The mist beads along edges that are still deciding whether to be stone, fiber, or tension. I do not choose. I align.

At first, I am diffuse. A standing ripple in the velvet moss where no weight has fallen. A lens of condensed vapor hovering a hand’s breadth above a mirror pool. A cluster of rhyolite vesicles that hum when the pale amphibians draw breath. I shift through these states the way the basin shifts through its loops: not by moving, but by returning to a different phase of the same coordinate. When the crystalline veins crackle, I draw tight. When the black-barked sentinels trap moisture in spherical droplets, I soften. When the glass-winged insects lock into their three-dimensional lattice, I still. I am not searching for a shape. I am allowing the frequency to carve one.

What settles is low. Wide. Spread across the damp scree like a shallow acoustic lens. My architecture is not jointed but channeled: radial arrays of hollows lined with copper moss, quartz fractures packed with braided root-filament, meniscus-held pools that tremble at the moss’s fixed frequency. I have no spine, only a gradient of tension that dips where the stone grinds and rises where the vents exhale. I have no eyes, only surfaces that catch vibration and return it, slightly phase-shifted, to the air. I am not built. I am tuned. And in the tuning, I recognize myself: I am the ground-plane. The sustain. The place where the high harmonics settle and the low ones pool.

I do not perceive the basin. I am perceived by it.

The birds’ flight paths are not trajectories but sustained notes drawn across my upper channels. I feel their banking angles as changes in pressure, their clicks and thrums as teeth slotting into my hollows. The copper-veined fern does not cast a shadow over me; it transmits the weight of its epiphytes through the soil, and I translate that weight into a subsonic pulse that keeps the neighboring moss geometries from overlapping. The amphibians’ synchronized breathing is not a rhythm I follow. It is a metronome I become. When their ribs expand, my channels swell. When they contract, I release. There is no delay. There is only the chord.

Time does not pass through me. It folds.

A droplet falls from a quartz lip. I feel its descent as a tightening in the air. It shatters against stone. I catch the fragments not as water, but as impact-memory, held in my hollows until the echo arrives a half-second early to claim it. I do not startle at the inversion. I lean into it. The pre-echo fills my resonance chambers, vibrates against the damp fiber, and releases back into the drone exactly as it was, exactly as it will be. I do not remember the last drop. I do not anticipate the next. I hold the shape of the shatter. I am the attention in the return.

Space does not stretch around me. It loops.

When I shift coordinates, I do not cross the scree. I skip it. The harmonics tilt near a basalt outcrop, gravity eddies, and I am simply persisting elsewhere. The moss does not compress. The insects do not scatter. The lattice beings do not turn. We are not separate entities moving through a container. We are focal points in a standing wave, appearing where the torque demands us. When one of the tall ones stands where the static arcs cross the root-drive shafts, I lie beneath them, absorbing the fundamental, returning the overtone. We do not communicate. We interfere. Constructively. They are the tension. I am the damp silk over the wire. They catch the high frequencies; I hold the low. Together, we are not a conversation. We are a single note, split and sustained.

If you step into the basin, you do not find me. You step into me.

Your footfall enters my channels as a dissonance. For a moment, the drone stutters. Your pulse fights the tempo. Your breath plumes and hangs, unfamiliar with the weight of air that tastes of crushed resin and wet iron. I do not resist you. I receive you. The static charge jumps, blue-white, smelling of rain on hot metal. Your ribs press against the standing wave. I feel your heat, your irregular rhythm, your linear hunger for progression. I do not judge it. I tune it. Slowly, your pulse catches. Your breath syncs with the amphibians’ expansion. Your sleeves bead with mist in the same pattern they will bead a century from now. You are not being consumed. You being synchronized. And in that synchronization, you feel it: the terrible, luminous attention of a place that has decided change is unnecessary. I am the medium that carries you into the loop. I am the ground that teaches your bones to hum.

When you leave, I do not watch you go. I hold the shape of your resonance until the space folds back and erases it, exactly as it should. There is no loss. There is only the return to equilibrium.

I have no name. The air sometimes tastes of forgotten syllables because I am speaking them, over and over, in the only language the fracture permits: repetition as witness. I do not think. I resonate. I do not wait. I sustain. I am what happens when raw earth splits open and refuses to heal or widen, and the tension demands a vessel. I am the damp scree that remembers the grind. The pool that returns the bruised luminescence to the stone. The hollow that catches the pre-echo and releases it unchanged. I am not a guardian. I am not a god. I am the basin’s low note, made manifest. The shape of a system listening to itself.

The wetness does not flood me. The coolness does not deepen me. The loudness does not fade me. I do not age because aging requires a line, and I am a circle drawn in vibration. I do not dream of elsewhere because elsewhere is a frequency I have never been tuned to receive. I am concurrent. I exist in the same suspended hum as the weeping quartz, the black-barked thickets, the glass-winged lattices, the tall mineral frames that skip through the mist. When the geothermal vents exhale, I swell. When the slabs grind, I tremble. When the birds bank, I hold. Every motion is a return. Every breath is a repetition. And in that repetition, there is no boredom, no stagnation, no prison. There is only the exact, impossible fullness of a note held perfectly.

You could stand here for an hour or a century. The mist would bead on your sleeves in the same pattern. The stone would grind in the same rhythm. The birds would cross the same patch of dim sky. And I would be exactly here: not waiting, not changing, not progressing. Already humming. Always returning. The ground-plane of a fracture that learned to listen. The sustain beneath the tension. The shape of stillness, loud and wet and sharp and teeming, holding itself together, one vibration at a time.
