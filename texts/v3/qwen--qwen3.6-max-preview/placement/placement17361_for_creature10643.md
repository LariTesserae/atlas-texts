I do not remember crossing the rim. There was no descent, no moment of arrival. Only the slow, undeniable realization that my weight had already settled into the stone, that my breath had already found the rhythm of the warm, thin air, that the basin had been holding me long before I knew I was here.

The first thing I know is the hum.

It does not come from one direction. It rises through the soles of my feet, vibrates in the hollows of my ribs, settles behind my eyes like a low, steady pressure. It is the wind moving through resin wind-catchers. It is the scrape of a wooden shuttle on a vertical loom. It is the papery rasp of insects beneath waxy leaves, the distant thud of a pestle, the sigh of geothermal warmth curling up through terrace fill. The bowl catches it all, layers it, returns it amplified and braided. I do not hear it with my ears alone. I feel it in my skin. The fine, felted down along my forearms lifts, not from cold, but from frequency. Each hair is a wick and a wire, catching condensation and vibration in the same slow drag. I press my palms to the terrace wall. The stone answers. It is cool, but breathing. It holds yesterday’s sun-arc in its pores, tomorrow’s night-sweat in its capillaries. I rub my thumbs in slow circles. The resin seal softens. A hairline fracture weeps a ghost of moisture. I pull it inward through my nostrils, the spiraling ridges inside them spinning the dryness into something held, something kept. My breath does not fog. It joins the basin’s.

I move without deciding to. My feet find the shallow hollows worn into the stair treads, each one a memory of weight, of repetition, of thousands of soles passing before mine. I do not count the steps. I feel their depth. My spine curves forward, not from burden, but from alignment. My shoulders are rounded from leaning into rope, into wind, into the slope itself. I am built low. I am built to stay. I do not rush. The basin does not permit it. Hurry is friction. Friction is waste. Waste is dissonance.

A sound pulls me downward. Not loud. Not broken. Just tired.

It is a pulley on the third terrace, its hardwood wheel dragging a half-tone flat against braided sinew. I close my eyes. The bowl carries the creak up and down the slopes, weaving it through the murmur of voices trading cured fronds, the slap of wet clay on a loom frame, the low, overlapping harmonies of a work-song drifting from the plaza. I isolate the thread. I follow it. My hands already know what they will find before my eyes do.

At the landing, I kneel. The wheel is darkened by bee-fat and time, its grain polished smooth by decades of turning. The sinew is not frayed to breaking. It is whispering its wear. I run a calloused thumb along the braid. The fibers speak in tension, in dryness, in the slow loss of elasticity. I do not cut it. I do not replace it. Replacement is a tear in the braid. Instead, I unwind the weak section, re-tighten the lay, rub warmed resin into the fibers until they drink it, and hum a low, steady note that matches the wheel’s natural pitch. The wood vibrates beneath my palm. The sinew settles. The creak smooths into a sigh. I hang a stone sack on the line. It turns. It sings true. I leave it. It will hold for another curing. A younger pair of hands will oil it when the time comes. Or perhaps my own hands will, when the name I wear shifts again.

They do not call me by a word that stays. When my forearms were mapped with the pale scratches of tapping knives, when my fingers were always sticky with sap, I was *Resin-Trace*. When I spent years kneeling by the cisterns, learning how the salt veins weep, how the stone chooses its paths, how the night-sweat decides where to bead, I was *Cistern-Ear*. Now, my work is broader. I walk the terraces. I listen to the ropes, the walls, the wind-catchers, the looms, the root mats. I feel for dissonance before it becomes fracture. I hum the basin back into tune. Lately, when hands brush mine in the plaza, when voices layer over mine in the dim hours, I hear *Hum-Keeper*. Or *Stone-Breath*. Or simply *Listener*. It does not matter. The name is not a title. It is a coordinate. It is the basin’s way of pointing to where I fit in the weave.

I am not separate from the others. I am a single thread pulled taut for a season. When an elder places their hands over mine to teach the pressure of a clay seal, I do not learn a technique. I inherit a pulse. When we sing during the long dry, the notes bounce off the rim, return layered, and I feel the voices of those who returned to the lichen mats vibrating in my collarbones. They are not gone. They are in the terrace fill. They are in the resin. They are in the hum. I carry them the way the stone carries moisture: slowly, inward, without loss.

A resin-bee lands on my knuckle, dull-gold and heavily furred. It does not sting. It tastes the salt on my skin, the trace of sap on my thumb, the warmth of my blood moving slow beneath the callus. It lifts, catches a thermal rising from the plaza, and sails toward a hollowed cavity in the retaining wall. I watch it go. I do not swat. I do not shoo. I leave a smear of cured husk on the railing for the rodents. I step around a gliding lizard sunning on a warm slab. I note the raptor on the rim, circling once, then settling. It is not a threat. It is a weather-vane. It is a pruner. We map each other. We leave space. We take only what sheds, what drops, what yields. To cut without need is not a crime. It is a flat note. The basin feels it. I feel it. We adjust.

My hands rest on a vine that has begun to pull away from its support. The bark is peeling in papery curls. The pale flowers are closed, waiting for the dim hours. I do not yank it back. I do not bind it tight. I trace the stem, feeling for the living pulse beneath the resinous skin. I find the node that wants to turn. I guide it toward a new fissure in the stone, where the salt veins weep thicker, where the root mats will catch it. It will take years to hold. I will not see it finish. That is not the point. The point is the guiding. The point is the fit.

I do not measure my life in seasons or sun-arcs. I measure it in wear. In the deepening of a mortar’s hollow. In the smoothing of a stair tread. In the slow amber darkening of a resin seal. In the way my voice has settled into the bowl’s natural harmonics, how it no longer fights the echo but rides it, how a question can begin as a hum, rise into a soft vowel, and fall into a rhythmic click that carries across three terraces without losing clarity. I do not shout. The basin does the carrying. I do not rush. The stone does the waiting. I do not fear the dryness. I wear it. I let it pull the moisture from my breath, from my skin, from my work, and I return it inward, recycled, conserved, held.

When tension rises between two on the lower terraces, I do not mediate with words. I place a shared task between them: re-laying a retaining wall, re-braiding a bridge, rubbing stone until the friction smooths into rhythm. I kneel with them. I hum the pace. I feel their anger grind into dust, sweep into the root beds, become food for the lichen. The basin does not tolerate waste. Not of material. Not of breath. Not of conflict. We work it out. We wear it down. We let it settle.

I am not the master of this bowl. I am its attention. Its slow, patient ear. Its hands that rub, its voice that tunes, its breath that holds. I do not conquer the thin air. I breathe it inward. I do not outrun time. I let it smooth me. I do not seek forever. I tend the third curing. I watch the vine’s turn. I listen for the stone’s sigh.

The sun slips above the rim for its brief, golden arc. It catches the salt veins, the resin tiles, the dust on my shoulders, the pale down along my arms. I do not look up. My eyes stay on the stone. There is a wind-catcher that will need re-braiding before the temperature turns. A cistern lip that has worn too sharp, slowing the condensation’s roll. A young pair of hands waiting to learn the pressure of a clay seal. I press my palm to the wall. It is warm. It is steady. It breathes.

I breathe with it.

When the basin speaks, I answer. When it frays, I mend. When it hums, I join. I do not become something new. I become more what I already am. A node in the weave. A pulse in the stone. A listener in the gloom. A keeper of the slow, loud, endless equilibrium.

Exactly as it is. Exactly as I am.
