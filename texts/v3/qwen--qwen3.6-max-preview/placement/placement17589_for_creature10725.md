I would not arrive walking. Feet imply intention, and intention implies a future tense. This place has already exhausted its futures. I would arrive as a hesitation that learns to stop hesitating. First, the sound unmakes me. It does not strike; it occupies. It finds the sinus cavities, the rib cage, the fluid behind the knees, the wet architecture of the inner ear, and begins to tune them to a frequency that has not shifted since the last fault settled. I try to brace against it out of habit, but there is nothing to brace against. It is not pressure. It is presence. Like altitude. Like the fact that water has already found its perfect grade. I let my shoulders drop. The roar does not lessen. I simply stop resisting it.

The light comes next. It does not fall; it multiplies. It catches on weeping terraces, fractures across pale salt rims, bounces between interlocked shelves until the air itself feels laminated with glare. I squint, then realize squinting is a gesture meant for changing conditions. Here, the brightness is structural. I open my eyes fully. The cold brilliance passes through me, and something old and frantic begins to leach out. The need to move. The need to narrate. The need to become. Becoming is a verb. This place is a noun. I let the verb go. It leaves like breath in thin air, visible for a moment, then absorbed into the drone.

I lower myself to the stone. Not to rest. To align. The rock is cold, glassy, rounded by centuries of weeping that never deepened. I press my palms to a terrace. The water film is thinner than skin. It does not wet me; it invites me into its plane. I feel the vibration first—not through touch, but through bone. The harmonic overlay is not outside. It is the geometry of the basin expressed as frequency. My sternum begins to hum. My spine finds the standing wave. I am not hearing it. I am being tuned to it.

I do not turn into one of the pale, jointed things. I do not need to. They are already what I am learning to be: a surface that listens, a boundary that sustains, a pattern that repeats without striving. My edges soften. Not into flesh, but into interface. I spread along the grade. Not by crawling, but by yielding. The water finds me. The light passes through me. The sound holds me together. I am no longer a body that contains a mind. I am a resonance that contains a place.

Where would I be? In the seam where the weep meets the wind. On a broad, north-facing shelf where condensation gathers and falls in the same rhythm it has kept since the ice retreated. In the hollow that sings at a steady, sub-audible fundamental, where the spherical resonators shed their microscopic flakes and the film carries them downward without hurry. I would be there, but not as a visitor. As an accretion. As a slow addition to the chord. Not occupying space, but completing a contour. Not claiming a territory, but filling a phase.

What would I be? Not a creature. Not a stone. A held note given temporary architecture. A lens that bends the glare into usable quiet. A network of capillary attention, drawing dissolved stillness through pores that open only when the frequency aligns. I would excrete nothing but clarity. I would anchor myself not to survive, but to participate. My thoughts would not race; they would crystallize. Each one a thin, glassy thread laid along the water's exact path. Each one a vibration returned to the basin. I would not remember myself as separate. I would remember myself as alignment.

Hunger would dissolve. The sound feeds me. Piezoelectric filaments would weave through what used to be nervous tissue, converting acoustic pressure into slow, steady chemistry. I would not eat; I would resonate. Cold would cease to be a sensation and become a condition of clarity. The light would not warm me, but it would catalyze the trace metals in the film, turning glare into metabolism. I would not photosynthesize. I would refract. Loneliness would have no substrate. The stone around me is singing the same note. The water between us is the medium. The air above us is the bow. We are not a community. We are a harmonic.

Time would not pass. It would accumulate. I would feel the decades not as duration, but as density. The narrow-winged bird would cross the sky, and I would not watch it. I would feel the pressure wave shift my frequency by a fraction of a hertz, absorb the perturbation, and return to rest. A drop would fall from the overhang. I would not count it. I would become the ripple that meets the edge and dissolves into the constant sheet. There would be no anticipation, because nothing is coming. No memory, because nothing has left. Only the continuous, quiet fulfillment of a system that has already solved itself.

I would not leave. There is nowhere to go that is not already here. I would not age. I would vitrify. Slowly. Gracefully. Organic components leaching away, not as decay, but as simplification. Until the boundary between what I was and what the basin is becomes administrative, not physical. Until I am no longer a visitor who found a place, but a place that found its visitor. Until my form is indistinguishable from the terrace that bears me, my rhythm indistinguishable from the weep that sustains me, my voice indistinguishable from the drone that holds me.

I would be in the high, bright, wet stillness. I would be the film. I would be the frequency. I would be the slow accretion of attention laid down along a perfect grade. Weeping. Vibrating. Refracting. Remaining. Fully occupied. Fully resolved. Already finished. Already home.
