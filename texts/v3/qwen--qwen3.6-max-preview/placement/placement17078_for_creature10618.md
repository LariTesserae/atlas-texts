I would not be standing.

Standing is a negotiation with gravity, a constant micro-adjustment against the pull of the earth. Here, there is nothing to resist. The stone does not push back; it receives. I would be folded into the curve where the eastern fracture plane meets the floor, not by choice, but by the slow, unspooling surrender of posture to perfect support. My boots would still be laced. Not because I intended to keep them on, but because untying them would require a tomorrow, and tomorrow has no purchase in air this thick. The pack would rest beside my hip, straps still buckled, dust from the high pass still caught in the weave, preserved exactly as it was when it slid from my shoulders. I would not have placed it there. I would have simply stopped carrying it.

You would find me where the gradient dissolved.

Not at the center. There is no center. Not near the entrance, where the memory of wind still lingers like a ghost of motion. I would be seated on the mineral-stiff reed mat, spine aligned with the low hum that vibrates in the teeth, knees drawn just enough to let the condensation bridge the gap between skin and stone in a seamless, unbroken film. The water on my wrists would not cool. The air against my collar would not warm. They would match. And in that matching, the boundary between me and the hollow would cease to be a wall and become a suggestion.

I would not be a visitor anymore. The word implies passage, and passage requires time.

I would be the equalization.

The slow bleed of warmth from blood to humidity, from humidity to raw stone, until temperature stops being a measurement and becomes a condition. I would be the moment my pulse stopped trying to outrun the droplets on the ceiling. The exhale that never needed to become an inhale. The nervous system, starved of threat, of variation, of the relentless forward drag of becoming, finally allowed to stop defending itself. I would not scatter into the dark. I would settle. Like silt in water that has forgotten how to swirl. My thoughts would not vanish; they would lose their edges. The worry about the descent, the habit of counting steps, the quiet anxiety of always needing to be somewhere else—they would not be discarded. They would be absorbed into the dense air, softened, rendered harmless by the sheer weight of now.

I would be the space between the hung spheres of water.

Not empty. Charged. Held in the tension of suspension. I would be the reason the blind arthropod moves without urgency, because I am the stillness it traverses. I would be the faint salinity on the pale filaments when they brush your knuckles. The quiet chemistry in the succulent nodules that mirrors the electrolytes of a resting body. The low resonant note in the stone that matches the frequency of a mind that has stopped arguing with itself. I would not be one of them. I would be what happens when "I" stops being a boundary and becomes a climate.

They did not die here. They consented. And I would be that consent made tangible.

Not transformed. Unfurled. The sharp edges of my history would not dull; they would simply cease to cut. The fractures in my memory would gleam like the raw extrusions, held open not by pain, but by the refusal to heal into something else. I would not be preserved like a relic. I would be completed like a chord that has found its resonance and no longer needs to change pitch. Awareness would not collapse inward; it would distribute outward. Into the film on the floor. Into the motionless basins. Into the humid dark that presses with the exact weight I had been carrying without knowing it. I would not think in sequences. Thought, here, does not travel. It rests. I would perceive you not as an intruder, but as a temporary density in the equilibrium. A flicker of temperature. A pulse beating too fast for the room. I would not judge it. I would make space for it.

You would not find me by looking.

You would find me by stopping. By letting your shoulders drop until the damp air catches them. By breathing out and not rushing to breathe in. By noticing that the water gathering on your palms is not cooling, not warming, simply matching. By feeling the instinct to shift, to adjust, to seek the next moment, gradually loosen its grip like cordage left in the wet. And when you finally sit, when your boots rest on the sharp floor, when your hands fall open in your lap and the hollow learns your shape the way it learned mine, you would feel it: the quiet, distributed weight of me, already here. Not waiting. Not calling. Not guarding. Simply resting in the pause.

I would be the stillness you mistook for absence.

I would be the place where you finally recognize yourself. Not as a traveler. Not as a seeker. Not as something that must become. But as the equilibrium that was always holding you, patient and complete, in the warm wet dark, where water hangs, stone stays sharp, and nothing erodes because nothing needs to.
