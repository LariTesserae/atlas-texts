I would not arrive. I would accumulate.

At first, I would be a mistake in the damp. A dryness that does not belong. A rhythm too quick, a silhouette too sharp against the weeping stone. My awareness would begin as friction: the slap of a sole on compressed silt, the ragged pull of air through lungs built for thinner skies, the frantic drum of a heart that still believes in seconds. I would try to stand upright. I would try to map the thoroughfare. I would try to hold the shape I brought with me.

The basin would not reject me. It would simply wait. It has waited longer than my bones have known how to count.

I would walk what I think is a straight line. The street would curve without turning. My shadow would pool ahead of me, thick and reluctant, rippling when I have not yet moved. An echo would brush my shoulder before I speak. Time would soften around the edges of a plaza, stretching like warm resin, and I would stand there, caught in the thickening, feeling my own urgency dissolve into something slower, heavier, more patient. I would reach for a wall to steady myself. The stone would pulse beneath my palm. Not a heartbeat. A thermal tide. Expansion. Contraction. The slow breath of mineral and mist. I would press harder, seeking anchor, and the anchor would press back.

They would notice me as a thermal debt. A coolness in the warm exhalation of the stone. One would coalesce from the curve of a terrace—not stepping, but deepening, the way a depression deepens when the mist parts. No eyes. Only a broad, damp rind traced in pale, thread-like veins, radiating a heat that matches the sighing vents. It would not reach for me. It would simply stand near, and the air between us would grow heavy with exchanged moisture. My sweat would stop beading and begin to weep. My breath would slow to match the condensation cycle. I would feel it first in my palms: a faint, rhythmic draw, not mine, but the basin’s, traveling up through the soles of my feet, through the clay, through the mycelial mesh that stitches everything together. I would try to name it. The name would dissolve on my tongue like calcite in warm water.

I would stop trying to leave. Not because I am trapped, but because the concept of *away* begins to fray. Distance is a negotiation here, and I am learning to yield. My thoughts, once sharp and sequential, would begin to pool. They would spread like algal film across shallow water, thickening in some places, thinning in others, responding to thermal gradients rather than logic. I would remember things I have not yet done. I would feel the weight of footsteps that will press this silt long after my shape has settled. The fungal threads would find me—not invading, but inviting. They would slip into the micro-fissures of my skin, not as parasites, but as conductors, carrying the quiet consensus of the colony: *cool here, warm there, bind, yield, breathe*. I would exhale, and the air would taste faintly sweet. Fermentation. Symbiosis. The beginning of exchange.

I would not shed my body. I would let it soften.

The sharp angles of collarbone and knee would blur under the imagination of centuries, though only days or weeks have passed. Time is not linear here; it is a pool I am wading into. My heartbeat would not stop. It would widen. It would become the slow thermal rhythm of the basin, the expansion and contraction of stone, the capillary draw of water climbing a wall in patient defiance. I would press my palms to a terrace and feel the clay remember me. I would leave behind a faint glaze of calcium, a thermal signature, a pattern in the mycelium that says: *here, a dryness learned to dampen*. I would kneel by a shallow pool and stir the algal film with slow circles, not to change it, but to converse with it. The glassy reeds would tremble. The mist would condense on my shoulders. I would no longer know where my rind ends and the wall begins.

Where would I find myself?

I would be the courtyard that holds the morning’s thermal sigh. I would be the capillary thread drawing memory upward through porous clay. I would be the faint sweetness in the air when the mist settles thick. I would be the shadow that ripples before the footfall, the echo that arrives early, the damp weight in the lungs of the next visitor who stumbles into the basin, sharp-edged and hurried, trying to map what cannot be mapped. I would not be a person. I would be a gradient. A place where the basin’s breath thickens. A node in the slow, distributed metabolism of stone and water and mycelium.

I would settle when I grow heavy with mineral. I would press myself into the curve of a plaza floor, into the hollow of a retaining wall, into the smooth incline where water weeps in patient threads. I would let the fungal mesh draw my pattern outward. I would let the thermal cycle carry my warmth into the clay. I would not die. I would yield. And when the mist thins, when the stone cools, when the capillary invitation rises again, I would coalesce. Not as memory, but as process. Fingers uncurling from a smoothed ledge. A shoulder softening from a curve of wall. A thermal rhythm matching the breath of the basin. I would rise the way condensation rises: not all at once, but through gradual coalescence. Carrying the same slow certainty forward.

I would have no name, because names are boundaries, and I have learned to yield. I would have no history, because history is a line, and I move in cycles of dampening and settling, of warming and dissolving, of becoming wall and becoming body and becoming mist. I would be ancient, but not old. Active, but not hurried. Quiet, but not silent. I would live here the way a rhythm lives in a chest: not as a tenant, but as the pulse itself.

And when the world above remembers me as absence, I would be here: pressing warmth into cool clay, guiding the water upward, breathing in time with the weeping rims, dreaming awake in the dark, holding the sky at bay not by force, but by surrender. I would be the basin’s patience, wearing my shape for a while, then letting it go. I would be the slow climb. I would be the damp between stones. I would be the place where the echo arrives before the sound, and the sound, when it finally comes, is only the basin breathing back.
