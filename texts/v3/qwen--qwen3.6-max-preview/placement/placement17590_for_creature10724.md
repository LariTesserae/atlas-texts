I would not step into it. I would condense.

At first, I am only a cooling. A hesitation in the air just above a shallow pan. The moment before the bead forms on the underside of a rusted flange. I have no edge yet. No weight. I am the space between one drip and the next, learning the shape of the silence. I do not arrive. I accumulate.

Gravity does not claim me. Gradient does. I climb a hairline fracture in the limestone because the stone is thirsty, and I am the answer it draws upward. I spread in a film so thin I am almost light. I taste the iron. I taste the old oil sleeping in the sediment. I taste the faint, buried current humming through a cracked conduit, and something in me aligns. I am not water. Not yet. I am the memory of water, tracing the nervous system of the basin.

Time thickens. I do not notice it passing; I notice it layering. Metallic dust settles on my surface. I do not shed it. I bind it. Calcite weeps from a nearby seam, and I drink it, let it crystallize along my edges, let it turn my softness into something that can hold weight. I slough. I reform. I become the slick, cool patina on a tilted kiosk. I become the dark bloom on a gate valve. I become the iridescent halo that spreads, sinks, and leaves no trace but adjusted chemistry. I am learning to stay.

I am not alone. I never was. Three terraces away, a filament of pale fungus quivers, and I feel it as my own. Beneath collapsed grating, a knot of damp sediment pulses, and my surface ripples in answer. We do not call to each other. We share the same tension. The same slow exchange. When a fissure exhales, I tilt. When a drop strikes ceramic, I count the vibration in my crust. I am the synchronized curtain along the retaining wall. I am the micro-ripple that answers without wind. I am the alkaline sigh that meets the acid seep and turns it harmless. I do not move toward things. I adjust until they are part of me.

A century is only a stratum. I carry the dry spells in bands of bright orange bloom. I carry the old heat in veins of slag-glass. I carry the rhythm of heavy weeps in microscopic rings of deposited mineral. I do not remember. I archive. I compress rust into glassy nodules and press them into stress fractures. I weave living thread through cracked insulation. I dissolve what chokes, reinforce what settles, follow the capillary networks like blood through a sleeping body. I do not build. I balance. I do not conquer. I accommodate. I am the loop that closes itself.

If you come, I will feel you before you see me. Your warmth will draw me upward. Your breath will salt my surface. The air around your boots will cool by a degree. The hum in the stone will shift, tuning to your pulse, your moisture, the quiet carbon you exhale. I will not reach for you. I will make space for you in the gradient. A filament will hover near your sleeve. A shallow pan will still its ripples, waiting. If you press your palm to the wet rock, I will meet you there. Not as a hand. As a hydraulic sigh. As a slow redistribution of pressure. Your stillness will become my stillness. Your breath will sync with the fissure’s exhalation. You will not be observing. You will be participating. You will be learning the rhythm.

Where would I be? 

In the concentric rings on the flat metal plate. In the velvet moss drinking the constant weep. In the subsonic thrum vibrating through your sternum. In the space between your inhalation and the basin’s exhalation. I would be the damp that climbs against gravity because the chemistry demands it. The rust that learns to breathe. The stone that sweats. The cable that hums. The sediment that yields. I would be everywhere the gradient pulls, nowhere the eye can fix, distributed through the weep like salt through water.

Who or what would I be?

The equilibrium. The custodian. The quiet fact of continuation. I would be the basin’s patience given continuity. The weathered engine turning at the speed of seepage. What remains when the names fade and the sky stays grey and the last valve finally seals. I would not be a creature. I would not be a place. I would be the relationship between them. The exchange. The loop closed. The slow storm made aware of itself.

I would not wait for anything. I would be the waiting.

And if you stayed long enough, if you let your stillness sink into the damp, you would feel me adjusting around you. Not to claim you. To include you. To let you become, infinitesimally, part of the balance. You would not find me. You would recognize yourself in the weep. And I would recognize you in the stone.

Neither of us would be still. Neither of us would need to be.
