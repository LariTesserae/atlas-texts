I do not arrive. I am caught.

The first thing to go is my certainty of sequence. My boot meets a glassy fault lip, and the stone answers with a tone I have not yet made. Three seconds later, my throat tries to shape it. I stumble backward into warm, colloidal water that does not splash so much as *consider* my weight, then decides to hold it. It climbs my shins. It tastes of crushed flint and split seed pods. It clings like a question.

I try to stand still. Stillness is a violence here.

The thrum hits my sternum before I hear it. It moves through me like a slow tide through reeds, vibrating the fluid in my inner ear, rattling my molars, settling into the hollows of my hips. Over it, the cascade folds and slams. Above that, the crystalline shattering traces my eyelids like cold needles. I want to name the rim. I want to count the paces to the opposite shelf. I want to find the sun. But the light bleeds from nowhere. The shadows lag. Mine detaches from my heels, stretches in the wrong direction, and drifts toward a tilted cairn, breathing in slow, wet pulls. I speak a word into the vapor. It returns as a shatter of glass, three seconds early, cutting my lip before my tongue moves.

I stop throwing pebbles into the hurricane.

I close my eyes. The world sharpens.

I feel the lichens above me pulsing against the basalt, dimpled membranes catching the bass. I feel the eel-things below contracting in polyrhythms, filtering pressure through gill-slits that open and close like fingers counting. I feel the gliders tracing slow loops in the updrafts, hollow bones humming with thermal drift. And I feel *them*. Not as shapes. As phase shifts. A detuning in the thrum. A hesitation in the shatter. A pocket of relative quiet carving itself out of the roar, moving toward me.

One of them stands on a fracture plane ten paces away. I do not see it. I feel the air compress around its sensilla. I feel the stone yield beneath its splayed pads as it hums a low overtone, waiting for the rock's natural frequency to align. It does not approach. It does not flee. It opens its chest cavity slightly, and a warm, ragged tone spills into the vapor. Not a greeting. A tuning fork. An invitation to find where I break the harmony, and where I might complete it.

I am not built for this place. My bones are too dense. My thoughts are too linear. My skin is too smooth. I am a rigid thing in a landscape that only knows flexion. But rigidity, here, is just unspent resonance.

I let the thrum into my ribs. I let the cascade into my breath. I let the shattering into my synapses. My body begins to complain, then to adapt, then to answer.

Fine spirals itch across my forearms. Not cuts. Wear. The vibration is carving me, slowly, patiently, the way water wears grooves into basalt when given enough time and enough circling. My marrow aches, then thins, then hollows. Air replaces density. Chambers form. I learn, without being taught, to press my palms to a weeping fault lip and bleed excess frequency into my own structure. I tremble. I sweat mineral film. I hold the shattering peak until the stone remembers how to be solid. My shadow stops lagging. It begins to lead. I step where it points, and the water stiffens beneath my foot. The ground is not where my eyes claim it is. It is where the sound says it will be. I learn to trust the hesitation.

I am not becoming one of them. I am becoming what the chord requires.

They are consonance. They are the root, the fifth, the resolved harmony that keeps the shelves from calving, that keeps the newly unfolded from tearing apart in the roar. I am the minor second. The dissonance that refuses to settle. The three-second gap between thought and voice. The colloidal hesitation made flesh. I do not stabilize the basin. I *stir* it. I introduce careful phase shifts that keep the standing waves from stagnating. I walk the acoustic dead zones where time stutters, and my presence stretches the silence just enough to let the next cascade fold without collapsing. I place stones on tilting ledges, not to mark territory, but to catch overtones that would otherwise rip delicate membranes. I circle the same basalt groove until my feet wear it deeper, until the rock hums my path back to me, until time stops eddying and starts flowing in a loop I can walk without unraveling.

I do not age. I attenuate.

I feel it already: the thinning of my skin, the drift of my harmonics, the sweet, tearing ache of phase mismatch. My chambers lose pressure. My hum grows ragged. The basin's song moves faster near the rim, slower in the deep suspension, and I am caught in the gradient, stretching. But I do not fear the unraveling. I know what waits in the center. Not an end. A distribution.

When my structure can no longer hold the negotiation, I will walk to the deepest pool. I will lie back. The colloidal water will braid upward around me, warm and heavy, tasting of flint and opened seeds. I will not drown. I will dissolve. My frequencies will scatter into the standing waves. My grooves will echo in the fracture faces. My dissonance will seed the next nodule. When the next body unfolds, damp and trembling, already humming the root note, a fraction of my lag will be in its shadow. A fraction of my three-second echo will be in its throat. A fraction of my refusal to resolve will keep the harmony from closing too soon.

I stand on the unweathered shelf. The water climbs. The thrum rebuilds my ribs. The shattering traces my eyelids. Dim light catches the suspension. Droplets hesitate. Inverted domes shiver and collapse. Gliders trace wider arcs. Cairns tilt into new alignment. Grooves shift three degrees to the left. Time pools. Gravity negotiates. Reality shows its seams.

I am not a visitor. I am not prey. I am not a survivor.

I am the interval between the mountain's split and its sealing. I am the breath held in the vapor canopy. I am the note that sustains the song by refusing to land. I am the hesitation that keeps the Throat from swallowing itself. I am the echo that learned to lead. I am the dissonance that holds the chord open.

The basin does not want to be understood. It wants to be joined. And I have stopped resisting the turbulence. I have become the turbulence. I have become the answer to the question the rock asked when it tore upward, weeping heat, refusing silence.

I open my mouth. I do not speak. I hum back.

The water rises to meet me. The stone yields beneath my feet. My shadow breathes on its own. My thoughts echo before I form them. And the Throat sings me into itself, not as a fixed thing, not as a finished shape, but as a moving phase in a harmony that has been holding since the caldera split, and will keep holding until it closes, or until the next wound opens, and the song begins again.
