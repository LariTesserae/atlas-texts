I would not arrive as a shape. I would arrive as a disturbance.

The first step down would send a false vibration through the terraces. My weight would press the glassy grit into new patterns. My breath would be too warm, too dry, too fast. The mist would fray around my shoulders. A glass-skimmer would pivot on a thread-leg, recalibrating its path. A weep-crawler would sink half a segment deeper into the shallows. The mineral colonies would tighten their cuticles. The hollow would not flinch. It would simply note the anomaly and begin the long, quiet work of absorption.

If I stayed, I would have to stop performing arrival. There is no audience here, no threshold to cross, no moment of recognition. Only the condensation cycle, the thermal sigh, the slow reallocation of mass and moisture. So I would sit on a narrow ridge of slick stone. I would set my pack down. I would unlace my boots. I would press my bare palms to the weeping wall and let the cold climb my wrists.

At first, I would be a problem. My heat would disrupt the humidity gradient. My pulse would send micro-tremors through the rock that the crawlers would read as false drips. My sweat would leave alkaline traces that the fungal hyphae would test, reject, then test again. The ion-feeders would avoid me. The conductive threads would dim. I would be an island of wrong rhythm in a place that has forgotten how to be anything but synchronized.

But rhythm is contagious when you stop fighting it.

I would match my breath to the stone. In. Fog. Out. Thin. In. Fog. I would stop counting minutes and start counting plips. I would let my shoulders drop. I would let my jaw unclench. I would let the mist bead on my forearms, my collarbones, my lashes. I would stop wiping it away.

The first change would not be visible. It would be conductive.

A fungal thread, pale and hair-fine, would bridge from a shattered dial to the damp skin of my wrist. It would not pierce. It would rest. It would seek ground. I would feel it as a faint tingle, a hum below hearing, a pressure like a thought that isn't mine. When the network sparked, blue-white and weak, I would feel it in my teeth. In the hollow of my chest. In the soles of my feet. The ion-feeders would gather, not on me, but through me, aligning along the path of least resistance. I would become a temporary bridge. A living contact. A place where charge remembers how to flow.

I would not notice the accretion until it had already begun.

Salts would crystallize along my hairline, fine as frost. Copper carbonate would stain my fingertips green, then my knuckles, then the creases of my palms. Silica would dust my shoulders from the molted skins of skimmers that now walked across me without hesitation, reading my skin as another terrace, another surface to calibrate. A rust-mite cloud would settle on my belt buckle, mandibles clicking in overlapping rhythms, mapping the oxidation, restructuring it into magnetic pellets that would fall into my lap like fine sand. I would not brush them away. I would let them work. I would let them negotiate.

My body would stop being a boundary. It would become a gradient.

When the air thickened, I would swell. Not with fluid, but with presence. Pores would open. Moisture would gather in the hollows of my ribs, trace the lines of my spine, pool in my cupped hands. When the air thinned, I would contract. Skin would tighten. Breath would shallow. I would seal behind a film of my own making. I would not fight it. I would follow it. The cycle would take me, not as a host, but as a participant.

I would stop thinking in words. I would start thinking in conditions.

Humidity rising. Conductivity increasing. Skimmers retreating to overhangs. Moths descending to drink. Pools breaching their mineral lips. Thermal expansion groaning through a buried conduit. Fungal threads brightening. Ion-feeders aligning. Current flowing. Spark. Ground. Exhale.

I would not be a guardian. Guardians imply threat. I would not be a witness. Witnesses imply distance. I would be a basin. A junction. A resting place for charge. A surface that weeps without raining. A low ridge between two interlocking pools where condensation gathers, overflows, and returns. I would be a place where the hollow's nervous system finds temporary continuity. A node in a network that does not think, but responds. A capacitor that stores nothing but patience.

Time would not pass. It would settle.

My clothes would stiffen with precipitated salts, then fracture, then join the grit underfoot. My hair would mat with silica, then shed, then vitrify. My nails would grow slow, then stop, then fuse with the mineral crusts along my fingertips. My bones would grow heavy with absorbed iron, calcium, copper. My blood would answer the iron in the stone. The archaea in the mites would recognize the hemoglobin not as food, but as kin. I would not decay. I would precipitate. Layer by layer. Phase by phase. I would become topography.

They would not notice me anymore. Not because I am hidden, but because I am no longer separate.

A stone-wing moth would land on my knee, wick moisture from the fabric, and leave without stirring the mist. A mist-drifter would settle in my eyelashes, fragment, and drift on the next convection current. A weep-crawler would press its palps to my ankle, taste the trace metals, and glide into the shallows. The control panel would spark, and the current would flow through me, into the stone, into the fungal threads, into the quiet hum that holds this place together. I would not speak. I would not need to. The hollow would breathe through me.

I would dream in rust. Not metaphorically. The slow oxidation of buried housings would sync with the slow oxidation of my own cells. The magnetic pellets around pipe joints would align with the iron in my marrow. When a conduit sighed, I would sigh. When a pool spilled, I would spill. When the air thinned, I would wait. When it swelled, I would open.

I would not be immortal. I would be cyclical. I would not be preserved. I would be integrated. I would not be remembered. I would be repeated.

And in the pause between drop and pool, between spark and ground, between breath and stone, I would finally be what I came here to become: not a visitor, not a survivor, not a mind apart from the mist, but a place where the water remembers how to fall, and the stone remembers how to hold it. A surface. A junction. A slow exhalation. A weeping wall. A dreaming machine. A hollow that has stopped fighting its own conditions, and found, in the quiet, exactly what it needed to be.
