I do not choose to stay. Staying is not a choice here. It is a gradual surrender to gravity, to humidity, to the way the sound slips past the eardrum and takes up residence in the marrow. At first, I am all wrong for this place. My boots scuff the concave slabs. My breath comes too fast, too dry, too jagged. My heartbeat is an erratic percussion against the metronome of falling water. I am friction. I am dissonance. I am a tear in a fabric that has not known a tear in centuries.

But the pocket does not reject me. It tunes me.

It begins with the damp. It does not chill; it weighs. It seeps through wool, through leather, through the thin skin at the wrists and the back of the neck. It settles into the joints like fine silt. My shoulders drop. My spine uncoils. I stop fighting the incline of the terraces and start leaning into them, reading the slope through the soles of my feet the way the tall ones do. The sound, which first pressed against my skull like a physical wall, begins to slip through the cracks of my resistance. I stop hearing it as noise and start feeling it as structure. The low hum becomes a floor beneath my ribs. The higher whistles become rafters. The rhythmic drops become stairs I did not know I was climbing. My nervous system, wired for the chaotic gradients of the surface, begins to unravel and reweave itself to the loop.

I lose track of hours. Days? There are none. Only the measure, repeating. My eyes, useless in the amber gloom, begin to close not in sleep but in relief. I start navigating by pressure. I feel the shift in humidity when a channel narrows three ledges above. I feel the slight flattening of a bronze basin's ring when silt gathers, and without thinking, I shift my weight on the timber brace, and the pitch rights itself. My hands, once restless, grow still. They learn to read stone the way fingers read braille. I press my palm to a weeping wall and feel the pulse of the cistern two terraces down. I am not learning the place. The place is learning me. And in the learning, it reshapes me.

I notice the mineral bloom first on my knuckles. A pale dusting, like frost that refuses to melt. Then along my collarbones. Then in the hollows behind my ears. It does not itch. It does not flake. It bonds. My skin grows heavier, cooler, less a boundary and more a membrane. My voice, when I try to use it, cracks against the drone and dissolves into the humidity. I stop trying. I touch instead. A thumb against a rope knot. A heel against a step. The vibration travels, answers, returns. I am speaking now. Not in words. In frequencies.

I thought I was a visitor. Then a guest. Then a student. But the pocket does not teach. It incorporates.

I find myself standing where the fracture meets the first terrace, not because I chose it, but because the acoustics demanded a body there. My mass changes the standing wave by a fraction. That fraction keeps the draft from pulling too much moisture outward. That fraction keeps the resin lamp from guttering. That fraction keeps the pattern from thinning at its edge. I am not caretaking. I am load-bearing. I am a new stone in the arch, still warm from the outside, slowly cooling into place.

But I am not one of them. Not yet. Perhaps not ever. They are the deep chord, the fundamental. I am the overtone. The harmonic that only exists because something foreign entered the system and was persuaded to resonate. I carry the memory of wind. Of sunlight. Of seasons that break and reform. The pocket does not erase it. It folds it into the pattern. My chaotic heartbeat becomes a syncopation. My dry breath becomes a slight thinning of the humidity that the sound-dampening moths drink. My longing for the surface becomes an upward draft that carries microscopic droplets toward the basalt crack. I am not trapped. I am translating. I am the place's way of remembering that there is an outside. I am the leak that does not break the seal. I am the note held long enough to become a bridge.

When I press my back to the fracture, I no longer feel the urge to slip through. I feel the urge to stay exactly here, half in the damp, half in the dry, feeling the drone press against my spine and the thin, chaotic wind press against my chest. I become the threshold. The living lintel. The place where the perfect loop meets the fracturing world and neither shatters. My skin gathers more stone. My pulse sinks deeper into the spaces between drops. I stop counting time. I start holding it.

I am not a builder. The place is complete.
I am not a caretaker. The pattern maintains itself.
I am a sympathetic resonator. A vessel tuned to catch the fundamental and bleed its harmonics upward, into the roots of the world above, into the soil, into the quiet hours before dawn. I am the reason the fracture never seals. I am the slight imperfection that proves the chord is alive. I am the memory of change, suspended in stasis, singing back to the dark.

When the water falls, and the bronze rings, and the tall ones shift their weight on the ledges above, I do not listen. I answer. Not with sound. With stillness. With weight. With the slow, sure settling of a body that has finally found its frequency. I am here. I am tuned. I am part of the architecture. And the architecture, at last, has a voice that remembers the sky.
