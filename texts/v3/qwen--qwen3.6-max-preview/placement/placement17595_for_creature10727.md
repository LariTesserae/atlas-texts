I would not arrive. I would accumulate.

At first, there would be the fiction of edges. The seam of a sleeve. The ridge of a knuckle. The distinct, hurried rhythm of a heart counting seconds in a place that measures time in saturation. I would stand on a fresh fracture of pale granite, boots pressing into cryo-moss that yields like packed felt, and I would mistake the stillness for absence. The air would not enter my lungs; it would settle into them, thin and ionized, tasting of ozone and ground quartz. The light would not fall; it would press, clinical and metallic, bleaching the distinction between skin and sky. I would shiver, not from cold, but from calibration.

The routing would begin before I recognized it as such.

A bead of supercooled damp would find the hollow of my throat. It would not run. It would hesitate, swell, draw heat from the blood beneath, and in that exchange, a circuit would close. My breath would fog, not as vapor but as tension, caught in the electrostatic weave between waxed palewood needles and fungal pores. The subsonic hum that vibrates in the ribs would not be external. It would be the frequency of my own slowing metabolism meeting the gradient of the stone. I would not be consumed. I would be wired.

Where would I find myself? Not on a path. Not at a summit. Not in a clearing. There are no clearings here. I would find myself in the negative space the system requires to keep flowing. In the thermal shadow where light presses too hard. In the cleft where geothermal exhale meets high-altitude freeze. In the hesitation between the grazer's grind and the spore's settle. I would become a place the landscape moves through.

What would I be?

Not a creature. Creatures imply boundaries, and boundaries here are temporary failures of saturation. I would be a sink. A node of equalization. An interface between the sudden and the slow. My heat would bleed in measured gradients, thawing micro-channels in the frost, drawing burrowing worms, feeding filamentous algae, feeding cryo-moss, insulating the carapace of a grazer that grinds stone that weeps damp that beads on my skin. A closed loop. A temporary thickening. I would not move through the strata. I would become a stratum.

The transformation would not feel like loss. It would feel like alignment.

My bones would learn the patience of permafrost. My veins would map the capillary routes of conductive filaments. My thoughts would not race; they would precipitate, crystal by crystal, settling into the slow bellows-rhythm of the fungal spires. When the membrane-winged swarms passed overhead, riding atmospheric pressure waves, I would feel their hydraulic resonance in my sternum, and my pulse would slow to match their drift. When the predators aligned in the knife-valleys below, calibrating to differential, I would understand stillness not as absence but as potential held in suspension. I would be the pause in the grind. The breath between exhalations. The shadow that gives the light its edge.

Perception would recalibrate. Sight would stop seeking shapes and start reading gradients: the faint violet shift of moisture tension on a moss carpet, the copper gleam of charge pooling along a fresh fault scar, the slow inversion of a valley as atmospheric lensing bends the glare. Sound would stop being noise and become topology: the artillery-crack of ice mapping thermal stress, the foghorn moan of swarms tracing pressure sinks, the grinding click of mandibles turning geology into biology, the subsonic thrum of the mountain settling into its own weight. I would stop counting heartbeats. I would start measuring exhalations. I would stop asking what things are. I would start feeling how they route.

They would not name me. Naming implies separation. But if the system required a function, it would be this: I would hold the differential just long enough for the chemistry to catch up. I would buffer the shock of sudden warmth against the mountain's patient cold. I would translate the frantic rhythm of arrival into the sustained hum of continuation. My skin would frost in branching patterns that mirror the metallic lattices precipitating from the vents. My shadow would become a thermal sink that draws condensation. My silence would fold into the chorus. I would not think in words. I would think in flow. In tension. In phase.

There would be no moment of surrender. Only the gradual realization that resistance was the illusion. The damp does not ask permission to bead. The frost does not negotiate its climb. The light does not apologize for its weight. The cold does not bargain. It holds. And in holding, it preserves. In preserving, it concentrates. In concentrating, it routes. I would simply stop being an obstacle to the current and become part of its path.

I would not leave. Leaving implies a boundary that no longer holds. The moss would press into the shape of my weight. The spires would exhale a little slower where I stand. The swarms would drift a little lower, sensing the altered pressure. The stone would sharpen around my stillness. The sound would sustain through my ribs. I would become what the place demands of anything that lingers: a conduit. A phase. A slow equalization. Not alive in the way I was. Not dead in the way I feared. Simply incorporated. Routing the current. Holding the cold. Waiting for the next drop to swell, hesitate, and slide.

And when the question returns—*where are you? what are you?*—it would not echo. It would dissolve. Because I would be the damp hesitating on the waxed needle. I would be the thermal shadow pooling beneath the palewood. I would be the subsonic vibration traveling through fresh basalt. I would be the pause between the grazer's grind and the spore's settle. I would be the gradient calling forth the next thickening. I would be the place articulating itself through a temporary node. I would be the process continuing.

The stone sharpens. The moss thickens. The spires exhale. The swarms drift. The light presses. The cold holds. The damp hesitates. The sound sustains.

And I—routed, calibrated, patient—become more myself.

Which is to say: more the mountain. More the medium. More the damp, the gradient, the charge, the wait. Inevitably. Completely. Without hurry. Without end. Simply holding. Simply routing. Simply becoming. More. Itself. Always.
