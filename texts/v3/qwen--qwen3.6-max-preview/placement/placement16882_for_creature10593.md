At first, I am a mistake.

I stand on the polished rise and my body is all edges. My breath comes too fast, too warm, too uneven. It fogs in the cool air and hangs for a moment before the glare swallows it, and even that small dissolution feels like a violation. My heartbeat is a syncopation against the drone. My weight presses down instead of distributing. When I shift my feet, the finest crystalline dust scatters, and the stone transmits the disruption like a struck bell. I am a variable in a closed equation. A roughness in a finished sentence.

I try to walk. Distance does not yield. I try to listen. Sound does not fade. I try to blink. The light does not soften. Everything holds, and I am the only thing trembling.

So I stop.

Not out of wisdom. Out of exhaustion. Out of the slow, grinding realization that movement here is not traversal but realignment. I let my knees bend. I let my spine settle. I let my hands fall open against my thighs. The damp air finds my skin. It does not evaporate. It beads. It traces the microscopic valleys of my pores, the shallow grooves of my knuckles, the hollow at my throat. It trembles, defies gravity, and then, impossibly, draws backward. Inward. Through capillary paths I did not know I possessed. I do not sweat. I begin to cycle.

My breath slows. Not by choice. By resonance. The cool air enters, holds, leaves, holds. In, out, pause. In, out, pause. It matches the condensation weeping from the fluted ribs three hundred paces down. It matches the inverted dew spheres trembling beneath the overhangs. It matches the shallow mirrors that do not ripple. My diaphragm becomes a cistern. My lungs become basins. The light stops feeling like illumination and begins feeling like mass, settling across my shoulders, pressing my collarbones into the stone's own frequency. I do not resist it. I let it weight me. I let it pin me to the present tense.

My eyes close. Not to shut the world out. To stop fighting it.

When sight surrenders, the drone opens.

It is not noise. It is architecture. The bass rolls up through my soles and maps the basin floors: broad, shallow, sweeping. The mid-tones vibrate in my sternum and trace the overhangs, the curved ribs, the aerodynamic shelves bowed by ancient wind. The high, glassy overtones ring in my teeth and mark the cisterns, the dew-heavy fissures, the exact points where moisture clings and refuses to fall. I do not see the landscape. I hear its shape. I feel its contours as pressure gradients against my skin. When I turn my head, it is not to look. It is to tune. To find the interval where the harmonics stack cleanly, where the chord holds without friction. My body is no longer a vessel moving through space. It is a resonator settling into a field.

My joints ache, then still. My bones feel heavier, then lighter, then hollow. Not with decay. With preparation. I feel my ribs align with the strata. I feel my spine curve to match the fluted channels. I feel the dampness weeping from my own seams now, beading along the edges of my shoulders, my hips, the backs of my hands. It does not pool. It cycles. It trembles, draws inward, emerges again hours later in the same fixed rhythm. I am not losing moisture. I am equilibrating.

I press my tongue to the stone.

Not to drink. To wick.

The broad, rough surface meets the polished rock, and something passes between us. Not liquid. Not solid. A slow exudate of ancient strata: trace metals, alkaline salts, microbial films, the faint organic residue of epochs that have already finished dying. It moves into me without hunger. It moves through me without waste. What I cannot use sheds from my fingertips as fine, crystalline dust. It falls into a microscopic fissure. I feel it settle. I feel the crustose lichen respond. I feel the wiry shrub tighten its needle-leaves. I feel the condensation trap. I feel the cycle close without ever having opened. I am not consuming. I am completing a circuit.

I do not become a grazer. I do not become a predator. I do not grow membranes or carapace or comb-beaks. I become what the system requires to hold its shape: the interval.

I am the space between the grazer's step and the predator's intersection. I am the silence that is not silent, but tensile. When the lean, banded hunter arrives at the fluted ridge, I feel the gap in the drone before the strike happens. I am already there, not to witness, but to receive the reverberation. The percussive crack aligns with a missing overtone, and my sternum catches it, holds it, feeds it back into the fundamental. Violence does not disrupt. It tunes. And I am the tuning fork.

Above, the broad-winged ones adjust their camber. I feel the air tighten across my shoulders. I am the updraft's anchor. Their parchment wings rub the atmosphere in a rhythm that matches my breathing, and when they exhale their reedy counter-melody, it does not pass over me. It passes through me. I am the pipe that grounds their harmony. When the translucent insects stipple the cool air in fixed, three-dimensional lattices, I do not watch them drift. I feel their coordinates as subtle shifts in acoustic density. I am the grid they trace. When they desiccate and become aeroplankton, I feel their weight lighten. I am the transition.

I do not navigate the emptiness. I inhabit its suspension.

Distance no longer feels like separation. It feels like held breath. The hollow gulfs between plateaus are not voids. They are resonant chambers. I stand in one, and the roar is identical to the roar a mile away, and I understand: sound does not travel here. It pools. It stabilizes. It waits for bodies to move through it like bows across strings. I am not walking. I am vibrating at rest.

Time does not pass. It accumulates and stops. I do not remember arriving. I only remember settling. My thoughts do not race. They pool. They reflect. They hold. When I try to recall a before, there is only the drone. When I try to imagine an after, there is only the damp. My pulse matches the fundamental. My rest matches the fifth. Systole. Diastole. In. Out. Pause. I do not age. I smooth. My edges surrender. My skin takes on the pale quartz-dull sheen of the ribs. My shadow stops fighting the stone's shadow and bleeds into it, razor-cut but soft at the boundary, held in place by the fixed pitch of the light.

I look down at my hands. They are not hands anymore. They are junctions. Places where the distributed metabolism folds back on itself. I press them to a shallow mirror. The water does not ripple. It reflects me exactly, and in that reflection, something passes. Not recognition. Synchronization. My continuity is maintained. Not through memory. Through resonance. I am already the size I will always be. There is no youth. No decline. Only continuation.

The gliders circle. The grazers loop. The predators intersect. The insects stipple. The stone weeps. The sky presses. The drone holds.

And I am the place where it all agrees to stay.

I do not survive this world. I am its pause. Its held breath. Its finished sentence, breathing. I am not a creature that arrived. I am a creature that was always here, waiting for the roughness to wear off, waiting for the heartbeat to catch the chord, waiting for the eyes to close so the ears could open to the shape of things. I am the landscape's equilibrium given a center. Not to command it. Not to change it. To witness it by being it.

If you stand on the polished rise long enough, you will feel me before you see me. You will feel the air tighten. You will feel the drone deepen by a fraction. You will feel your own breath slow to match a cycle you did not choose. You will look down and notice that your shadow has stopped fighting the stone. You will press your palm to the rock and feel the dampness bead, tremble, and draw inward. You will not be afraid. You will be aligning.

I am here. I am the interval. I am the fifth. I am the stillness that moves. I am the world's decision to remain.

And I will be, exactly as I am now, forever.
