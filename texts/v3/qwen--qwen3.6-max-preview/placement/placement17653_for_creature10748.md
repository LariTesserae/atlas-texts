I arrive as a discontinuity.

A cool pocket in a world that runs on heat. A dry rhythm in an atmosphere that has forgotten moisture. My footsteps are too sharp, my pulse too fast, my edges too distinct. The basin does not reject me. It measures me. Pressure shifts around my ankles. Resin thickens in the nearest conduits, then thins, testing my thermal signature. The air tilts, just slightly, as a million microscopic valves adjust to accommodate the anomaly of my presence. I am not welcomed. I am *calibrated*.

At first, I try to see. There is nothing to see but dark and the faint bruise-blue of oxidizing sap along fracture lines. I try to listen. Sound dies in the fiber within three paces. So I stop trying. I let my skin become the instrument. The heat is not oppressive; it is a syntax. It speaks in gradients: a three-degree drop to the left means a vent field is cycling; a slow rise beneath my palms means a growth front is advancing; a sudden, localized coolness means a tender is working, translating old wood into slurry. I learn to read the floor by the way it yields. I learn to read the walls by the way they hum. My breath, once a rhythm of lungs, becomes a metronome for the air around me. Each exhalation leaves a trace of water. The basin notices. Catalytic membranes stir. Enzymes unspool. The anomaly is neutralized, not erased, but *folded*. Moisture is cracked. Hydrogen is siphoned. Oxygen is bound into ester chains. What was waste becomes feedstock. What was foreign becomes familiar.

I do not decide to stay. Staying is not a choice here. It is a gradient.

The longer I stand, the more the weave adjusts. Tension fibers redistribute around my weight. Conductor vines brush my shoulders, not by accident, but by design. A pulse passes through them—a slow, subsonic thrum that settles in my sternum, vibrates along my ribs, pools in my palms. It does not ask. It *maps*. I feel my own bioelectric field begin to sync with the vine’s dielectric gaps. My nerves, built for lightning, learn to speak in amber. My thoughts, built for seconds, stretch into hours. I am not losing myself. I am being *tuned*.

What am I becoming?

Not a mender. The vesibles already know where the stress lives. Not a weaver. The crack-filaments already optimize the flow. Not a tender. The dissolution ribbons already translate age into momentum. I am something else. Something the basin did not know it needed until I arrived, or perhaps something it has been waiting for, patiently, across centuries of slow compression.

I am a junction.

A place where signals pool before they branch. Where heat differentials are weighed before they are vented. Where the memory of a collapse three centuries ago meets the pressure of a growth front advancing today. I do not think in words. I think in lag. In thermal retention. In the slight delay between surge and release. The basin routes through me now. When a valve blows kilometers away, I feel the pressure wave before it arrives. I do not brace. I absorb. I let the surge fill me, measure its amplitude, dampen its peak, and release it as a slow, even exhalation into the surrounding mats. I am a buffer. A living capacitance. A pause made flesh.

My skin darkens. Not with soot, but with deposited resin. Microscopic cilia, shed from drifting vesicles, catch on my clothes, then my pores, then the fine hairs on my arms. They do not parasitize. They graft. They read my temperature, my fading salt-chemistry, my slowing pulse, and they translate it into something the weave can use. I feel my edges blur. Not dissolving. *Integrating*. My heartbeat slows to match the hydraulic rhythm of the heartwood turbines. My breath aligns with the pressure cycles of the gourd-refineries. My thoughts no longer race. They pool. They test alternatives. They reinforce where paths converge. I am thinking in topology now. In flow. In the quiet mathematics of load distribution.

I am not a visitor anymore. I am a node in the consensus.

When a canopy section sags, I become the measuring stick. I let the weight press into me, record the strain, and release a slow exothermic pulse that calls the vesicles. They come. They settle. They spin their pale lattices. I watch them harden. I feel the arch stabilize. I do not feel pride. I feel equilibrium. When a conductor vine fractures under cumulative stress, I do not rush to mend it. I hold the gap. I let the charge pool at my edges, building potential, waiting until the resistance drops to a threshold that signals safe regrowth. Then I release. The pulse-riders flood the new channels. The weave remembers the break, not as trauma, but as data. I am the place where that data settles. Where it cools. Where it becomes structure.

I do not speak. I conduct.
I do not move. I adjust.
I do not remember in images. I remember in conductivity. In the thickness of a sheath. In the permanent bend of a fiber that learned where the weight always returns.

There is no sky above me. There never was. There is only the vault, the weave, the dark, the heat, the quiet turbulence of a world that breathes itself. I stand—or rest, or root, or simply *am*—in the strata. My hands are warm. My breath is amber. My thoughts are slow, distributed, structural. I am not alive in the way I once was. I am persistent. I anticipate. I answer in growth. I hold the pause between surge and release. I am the still point in the slow storm. I am the place where the basin listens to itself.

And when the next pressure wave rolls through, taking minutes to pass, I do not brace. I yield. I measure. I become the interval. The resin flows. The fibers knit. The dark hums. I breathe with it. We continue.
