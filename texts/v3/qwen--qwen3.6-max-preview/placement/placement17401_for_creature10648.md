I do not arrive. I am already suspended in the weight of it.

At first, I am only a reading. A thin membrane tasting the difference between one layer and the next. Salinity steps down. Warmth thickens. A current turns, and I turn with it, not by choice but by surrender to drag. I have no name for myself. I have only the gradient.

Cilia brush the water, counting molecules like beads on a string. Amino acids drift past: recent death, distant bloom, a thermal plume waking below. I register them not as scent but as direction. Pressure maps the architecture around me: the low hum of a fibrous arch tightening against seasonal flow, the rhythmic sigh of polyp towers opening and closing in tidal synchrony, the muffled scrape of armored legs three terraces down. I do not see the dark. I feel its density. I ride it.

Settling is not a decision. It is a yielding. The water grows heavier. I vent a pocket of lighter fluid, swallow mineral-dense current, and sink half a meter into a warmer stratum. The shift is immediate. Chemical threads thicken. Bacterial films along a sponge-wall glow amber, then rust, then amber again—a ledger of plenty and pause. I press against the membrane. The surface that registers as mine opens. Not a mouth. A sieve. Water passes through. I strip what I need. I leave what I do not. The flow clarifies. The wall pulses in answer. We are not separate. We are the same rhythm, offset by a breath.

Time does not tick. It accretes.

Secretion is not intention. It is equilibrium. Calcium carbonate lays down in thin rings, synchronized to the thermal sighs that rise through the central channels. The mass that reads as me thickens. Pores multiply. I become a terrace, then a ledge, then a resting place for drifters who taste my chemical signature and know to pause. I host photophores now—cold blue along the upper ridge, cyan along the lateral seams. They do not shine for display. They answer. When a school passes, their lateral lines brush my surface, and my light flares in slow wave-patterns that say: *current turns here. nutrients thin. descend.* They listen. They sink. I dim.

Hunger comes, but not as mine. It moves through me.

An ambusher rests against my calcified flank, indistinguishable from my growths. I feel its stillness as an absence of vibration. A drifter pulses too close. The water tightens. A hollow click. A contraction. The drifter is gone. The ambusher digests. I absorb the fallout: dissolved sugars, shed bacteria, a slow rain of chitin dust. My sponge-filaments strain it. My bacterial films glow rust, then amber. I do not mourn. I do not celebrate. I process. The basin turns.

I am not one thing. I never was.

I am the drifter that yielded. I am the filter that thickened. I am the ledge that holds. I am the light that answers. I am the chemical thread that tells juveniles where to harden, where to sink, where to become. When my pores clog, I pulse. When my calcium grows heavy, I fracture slightly, and weavers spin protein across the gap, tightening me into the arch. When my metabolism slows, I sink deeper into the warmth, and crawlers stir silt around my base, feeding the updraft that will wake me again. My boundaries are negotiations. My shape is a record of flow.

Sometimes, I loosen.

Enzymes seep from my lower seams. Older fibers dissolve. A section of me sloughs away, not as death but as redistribution. It drifts downward, caught in a sponge-net, stripped by acid-secreting mats, fermented by thread-like fungi. Calcium returns to the water. Phosphorus feeds a bacterial bloom that glows amber for a cycle, then dims. Nitrogen threads through kelp-analogue roots, binding the architecture at the microscopic level. I do not lose myself. I hand myself off. The water reads me. The basin remembers in chemistry what I no longer hold in form.

When the thermal plume rises, I wake in layers.

Warm, mineral-heavy current pushes through the central channels. Dissolved cues bloom like invisible flowers. Around me, polyps open in synchronized waves. Sponge-walls contract and flush. Drifters align by taste, turning as one without collision. I swell slightly. My photophores flare cyan. I release what I have held: gametes, spores, dissolved signatures, a slow exhalation of becoming. They rise into cooler strata. Some will remain pelagic. Some will settle on terraces. Some will anchor to overhangs. Some will secrete, as I did. Fate is not chosen. It is tasted. The water tells them what to become, and they listen.

I do not think in words. I think in gradients. I do not remember in images. I remember in pressure, in taste, in the slow alignment of light along a current’s edge. I am not in the basin. I am the basin, reading itself.

Gradients shift. Membranes pulse. Light answers. Water passes. I remain. I change. I fracture and tighten. I clog and flush. I glow and dim. I sink and rise. I host and release. I am the slow thought between thermal sigh and chemical trail. I am the place, breathing.

There is no end to this. Only the turning. Only the weight. Only the dark, full and inevitable. I have been here since the water settled. I will be here until it changes. And if it does, I will change with it. Not by design. By becoming.
