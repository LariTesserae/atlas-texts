I did not climb here. I was poured.

Or perhaps I climbed, and the memory was scoured away by the first true gust. It doesn't matter. The basin does not care how you arrive. It only cares whether you can hold a note.

At first, I am all wrong. My boots slip on the glass-smooth plates. My lungs claw at the thick, brass-tinted air, expecting oxygen, finding ammonia and vaporized copper. My eyes hunt for horizons, for the familiar architecture of wet worlds: rivers, canopies, clouds that weep. There is only the vortex, the quills, the mats, the moss bleeding violet and rust. I try to speak. My voice is a flat, dead thing. The wind eats it without chewing.

I sit. I take off the boots. The heat rises through my soles, dry and even, a pulse waiting to be matched. I press my palms to the hexagonal seams. The stone hums. Not metaphorically. Literally. A low, tectonic drone travels up my wrists, into my elbows, settles in the hollow of my sternum. My ribs vibrate in sympathy. Something in me, long dormant, uncoils.

I close my eyes. Sight is a liability here. Sound is the map.

Time loses its edges. The pressure flips. The reverse rain begins: copper dust, oxidized flakes, bioluminescent spores lifting toward the haze in a slow, swirling exhale. The wind tightens. My clothes tear. I let them go. The grit kisses my skin, not as abrasion, but as invitation. I feel them before I understand what they are: spores of metallic moss, drawn to the heat and the stillness of my breathing. They settle in the hollow of my throat, along my forearms, in the seams of my joints. They do not parasitize. They partner.

A slow chemical fire wakes in my blood. The air tastes of sulfur and ionized iron, and suddenly, it is sweet. My lungs stop fighting. My throat learns to pull vapor through the colonizing cilia. I am drying out. I am waking up. My skin thickens, segments, takes on the oxidized patina of the lee-side stones. My eyes grow heavy, then useless; I bind them shut with cured membrane and find I see better. The world resolves into a topology of sound: the deep groan of shifting plates, the high whine of vapor through quill-pores, the rhythmic thud of lithovore jaws, the shimmering interference patterns of glider wings. I learn to walk by reading the nodes. I learn to rest in the silences between harmonics.

They find me on the seventh pressure cycle. Or I find them. The distinction dissolves in the drone.

A shift in the chaos. A correction. Three figures move through the brass haze, low and coiled, crests flared, cloaks singing through tuned slits. They do not approach with caution. They approach with listening. One strikes a tuning stone. A clear, bronze note rolls across the plates. My sternum answers before my mind does. A hum rises in my throat, unbidden, rough but true. It catches the standing wave. It holds.

They circle me. Hands calloused and warm press against my ribs, my spine, my jaw. They feel the dissonance. They feel the potential. One places a weighted copper ring against my collarbone. Another traces the line of my throat where the moss has woven itself into flesh. They drum a rhythm against the stone—low, polyphonic, a chord that asks a question. I answer. My voice splits. I didn't know it could. Two tones, friction and harmony, weaving into the basin's drone. The moss on my arms trembles. An aerogel mat nearby contracts, releasing a sigh of spores. The Resonants step back. Their crests tilt. Approval is not a smile. It is a sustained fifth, ringing clear.

I am not one of them. I was not born to the shear. I am grafted to it. But the basin does not care about origin. It cares about resonance.

I become a bridge. A living interval. I take up residence in the wide avenues of bare stone, where the clusters part and the wind runs clean. I am not a Keeper of the Stone. I am not a hunter of lithovores. I am not a weaver of cloaks or a tender of condensation traps. I am the *drift-tuner*. The one who came from outside the chord and learned to hold the dissonance until it resolved.

My purpose emerges slowly, written in vibration and necessity. The Resonants are masters of harmony. They keep the song tight, the frequencies pure, the cycles predictable. But purity, left unchecked, becomes stagnation. A chorus that only echoes itself eventually chokes on its own perfection. I am the grit in the bearing. The deliberate wrongness. When the harmonics grow too rigid, when the silica quills stop shedding, when the aerogel mats grow dense and suffocate their own cilia, I step into the open. I strike a flat note. A dissonant tritone. It shudders through the basin. The quills stiffen. The mats recoil. The Resonants pause, crests flaring in alarm. Then they adjust. They weave around my friction. They find a new key. The system breathes again.

I am the necessary disturbance. The storm within the storm.

My body is a ledger of adaptation. My joints click with copper-alloy caps I forged in a vent-fire. My throat houses a dual resonance chamber now, grown around the moss, shaped by cycles of polyphonic practice. My cloak is not glider membrane, but woven aerogel and dried cilia, tuned to catch the high harmonics the others miss. I do not speak. I hum. I click. I drum my knuckles against my own ribs to throw a pulse across the plates. I court no one, but sometimes, when the pressure inverts and the reverse rain paints the air in copper and phosphor, a Resonant will approach. We will stand in the star-junction. They will sing a line. I will answer with its shadow. We will weave a phantom tone that hangs between us, trembling, until the wind takes it. It is enough.

I fear the Great Hush, as they do. But I also know something their legends have smoothed over: silence is not always death. Sometimes it is the breath before the strike. Sometimes it is the space where a new frequency is born. When the vortex tightens and the noise becomes a solid wall, I do not retreat to the low domes. I climb to the rim. I lie flat against the wind-fused tuff. I let the scream of the shear pass over me. And in the eye of the turbulence, where the standing waves cancel into a pocket of impossible stillness, I listen. I hear the deep earth. I hear the slow cooling of the glass. I hear the basin dreaming of its next shape. I bring that dream back down. I translate it into a low, rolling drone. The Resonants catch it. They weave it into the chorus. The stone remembers.

Where am I?

I am in the dry cathedral. I am in the brass haze. I am at the star-junction when the pressure flips, and I am on the rim when the wind screams. I am in the wide avenues, in the spaces between clusters, in the silence that holds the song together. I am the listener at the edge of the chorus. I am the friction that keeps the harmony from seizing. I am the grafted note.

Who am I?

I am rust and resonance. I am a body that forgot water and remembered vibration. I am a listener who became a lens. I am the drift-tuner, the dissonance-keeper, the one who learned that to belong is not to match, but to complete.

When the time comes, I will not undertake the Final Descent as they do. I will not spiral upward into the reverse rain. I will climb to the highest tuning stone. I will strike it once, a clear, open fifth. I will let the updraft take my cloak, my moss, my dust. I will not fall. I will settle. I will sink into the hexagonal seams. I will become a node. A permanent correction. A frequency waiting in the rock for the next lost thing to step over the rim, strip off its boots, press its palms to the stone, and feel the basin ask, in a language older than breath:

*What note will you hold?*

And I will be the answer humming in the floor.
