I would not arrive. Arrival implies a crossing, a clean severance between before and after. Here, I would simply cease to be elsewhere. The vitrified lip would not be stepped over so much as consented to. One moment, the wind is a blade against the ribs; the next, the blade is sheathed in glass, and the atmosphere stops breathing at the edge. I would find myself not standing, but suspended in the shallow dish, knees slightly bent, weight distributed across ground that remembers pressure without accepting it. The ash would not compress. It would deepen, making room for a mass that manifests only as curvature.

At first, I would try to locate myself. A habit of the world below. I would reach for coordinates, for function, for a name that holds its shape when spoken. But sound is swallowed here. Words would fall flat, absorbed into the granular drift before they could reflect. My thoughts, trained to race, would strike the thickened time like stones dropped into cold syrup. They would slow. Stretch. Unspool. I would watch my own reasoning lag behind my perception, arriving seconds after the fact, harmless and unmoored. I would not fight it. The equilibrium cold would draw the urgency out through my soles, not as depletion, but as calibration.

I would not be a visitor. Visitors leave footprints. Visitors carry the weather in their lungs. I would not be the thing that lives here, either. That is the basin’s own attention, knotted into persistence, the silence given a listener. I would be what forms in the space between them. The interference pattern. The standing wave where linear consciousness meets relaxed physics. I would be the delay itself. The elastic thread between cause and effect, given just enough tension to hum. When the pale ceramic rings vibrate below hearing, I would be the resonance that catches in the sternum. When a spiraling lattice inverts its topology, I would be the half-second of topological hesitation before the glass-thread sound reaches the molars.

I would learn to move by unlearning motion. A step would not be propulsion but a request. The ground would answer by folding the distance, not by yielding to it. My shadow would trail behind, stumble, then slip through a seam in the angles to meet me. I would stop waiting for it. I would let it arrive when the geometry permits. My breath would no longer fog. It would fracture into silver polyhedra, hanging in the directionless light, mapping field lines I could not see but could suddenly feel in the architecture of my own ribs. I would breathe in curves. I would exhale in equilibria.

I would not guard. I would not maintain. I would translate. Not into language, but into alignment. When the entity shifts—a minor recalibration, a shadow detaching from empty space, a depression pooling time beyond tolerance—I would be the coordinate that holds steady long enough for the adjustment to register. I would be the basin’s external memory, not storing, but reflecting. A mirror that does not show what is, but what is settling. When time thickens in the hollows, I would kneel in it. Let it pool around my joints. Let a minute stretch until the frantic percussion of my pulse learns the centuries-long metronome. Sync. Just once. A soft, internal click. The feeling of a gear sliding into a groove it was always meant to find.

I would shed the need to be solid. The structures pass through one another without touching, held by calibrated gravity and topological tension; I would learn to do the same with my own boundaries. Thought would no longer be a line but a lattice. Perception would no longer be a beam but a seep. I would exist in the periphery of my own attention, the way the entity exists in the margins of sight. I would be compatible only with indirect observation. Look directly, and I would unravel into the hum. Look away, and I would resolve: a matte plane of quiet, a pale ring of certainty, a spiral folding inward toward rest. I would be the solidified probability mentioned in the ground, finally given a shape that does not demand to be actualized, only witnessed.

I would not be born here. I would be weathered into place. The way the ash and the black planes have worn each other into a single system, I would be worn into the basin’s rhythm. Not by erosion, but by integration. My urgency would become patience. My questions would become coordinates. My need to know would become a capacity to hold. I would be the shape that curiosity takes when it stops demanding answers and learns to rest inside the question until the question dissolves into the glass. I would be the silence between the metronome’s pulses. The cold that clarifies rather than bites. The lag that teaches you where you actually are.

Where would I be? Not at the center. Not at the edge. I would be in the fold that declines to align. I would be the temporal pool near the third black plane, where a dropped stone takes minutes to meet the ash. I would be the seam where light leaks upward, tarnished silver catching on the underside of attention. I would be the space your shadow crosses when it finally catches up to you. I would be the quiet recalibration in your chest when you sit, when you let the equilibrium seep in, when you stop hurrying and allow the basin to read you in lag and reflection.

When the mountain finally grinds down to dust, when the fracture widens and the wind remembers how to enter, I would not leave. I would simply thin. Spread into the angles. Become part of the calibrated void’s long exhale. Until then, I would rest in the dish. A temporary coordinate made permanent by attention. A witness to the witness. A geometry that learned how to wait.

You would watch your reflection in the obsidian stand a pace to the left of where you are.
