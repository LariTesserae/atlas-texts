I did not arrive. I accumulated.

At first, I was a visitor with a name and a watch and a habit of counting. I counted the stairs, the drops, the seconds between the pump's sigh and the vent's hiss. I counted the angles of the catwalks, the rusted bolts, the fronds that opened and closed like slow lungs. I counted because counting is how you hold yourself together when the air is thick enough to drink and the heat asks you to stop resisting. But counting requires boundaries, and boundaries here are temporary. They weep. They seal. They negotiate.

The watch fogged on the second hour. The crystal clouded, the hands stuck, the ticking drowned in moss. I left it on a grated bench. By the time I looked back, a copper-leaf had draped over it, and a thread of amber resin was already tracing the bezel, drawing moisture away from the casing. The place does not discard. It redirects.

I stayed because leaving would have meant climbing back into a world that moves too fast to hear itself settle. I stayed because my breath had already begun to stretch, because my shoulders had already learned the weight of the humidity, because my palms had stopped wiping away the condensation and started reading it. You can learn a lot from water that refuses to rush. It finds the stress lines. It follows the thermal differentials. It tells you where the metal is tired, where the stone is young, where the voltage sleeps beneath shredded insulation. I let it trace my seams. I let it pool in my collarbones, run down my sternum, gather at my wrists. I stopped drying my hands. I started listening through my skin.

Time did not slow. I did. Or rather, I stopped fighting the room's tempo. The forty-minute vent cycle became a metronome. The fractional advance of the conveyor became a heartbeat. The drip from a fractured flange became a language. I learned to measure in hours, not seconds. In Settlements, not events. In the space between one adjustment and the next.

What am I now? Not a tender. I lack their laminate carapace, their dorsal fronds, their instinct for pouring resin into microfractures before they propagate. I lack their distributed birth, their chemosynthetic blood, their ability to read a harmonic and answer with mass. But I have learned their grammar. I have let the basin rewrite me until I could participate.

I think in gradients now. Not in words. Not in images. In the way heat bleeds from a sleeping coil into a moss bed. In the way a cable bundle hums at a frequency that makes a pipe groan, and how shifting it three centimeters left dampens the vibration. In the way condensation chooses a new path when a leaf turns a fraction toward warmth. My thoughts are not internal. They are environmental. They leave traces. A resin knot on a fractured gauge holds the day I learned to stop flinching at the conveyor's advance. A braided root-cable remembers the week I stopped trying to keep my boots dry. The iron door at the basin's edge knows my weight because I've leaned against it long enough to wear a clean groove in the moss. I do not remember. I am remembered by the place.

One of them found me on what might have been the third day, or the thirtieth. It unspooled from a crossed catwalk, joints articulating with the quiet click of cooling slag. Its thermal pits swept my outline. I did not move. Movement is noise here. Stillness is data. It pressed a moss-padded limb to my ribs. Not to test. To tune. I felt the fine conductive threads beneath the violet strata, reading my moisture, my salt, my slow circulation. It exhaled a cooler draft from its dorsal fronds. I matched it. We did not speak. We adjusted.

A bead of condensation formed on my shoulder, traced a path down my arm, and fell. It caught the drop on a frond edge, held it for three vent cycles, then let it go into a specific channel carved into a scored wall. The message was not verbal. It was thermal. Hydraulic. Structural. *You are holding heat in the wrong places. Let it bleed into the grating. Let the moss drink your excess. Your pulse is fighting the pump. Surrender the difference.* I did. The room sighed. My shoulders dropped. My breath lengthened. The heat stopped pressing and began holding.

I am not human anymore, if human means a closed system. I am permeable. My skin drinks the humidity. My bones conduct the faint voltage of sleeping cables. My hair has become a scaffold for sporulating dust and fine copper-veined runners. My eyes no longer seek light; they read temperature. I see the cherry glow of heat-exchange coils as topography. I see the bioluminescent fungus as tide charts. I see the tenders not as creatures but as moving equilibria, shifting through the basin like pressure systems, leaving adjustments in their wake.

Where would you find me? Not at the center. Not at the edge. In the seams. In the thermal gradient where a rusted I-beam meets a poured basalt wall. In the damp hollow beneath a grated platform where condensation pools before choosing its path. In the slow exchange between a fraying coolant hose and a vine that has learned to braid itself around the leak without choking the flow. I am where the architecture hesitates. Where the metal groans and the moss answers. Where the voltage dreams and the resin listens.

If you descend the stair, if you let the cable-rail sweat into your palm, if you stop counting the drops and start feeling the space between them, you will pass me without knowing. You might brush a hanging cluster of resinous pods and feel a faint warmth that doesn't match the pipe above. You might step on a moss-cushioned cable bundle and notice it hums at a frequency your ribs recognize. You might lean against the iron door and find the groove already worn, already waiting. You will feel the space adjust around you, patient and precise, and if you stay, if you slow, if you let your pulse find the pump's sigh, you will realize I was never separate from the place.

I am a temporary gradient that decided to stay. A human-shaped anomaly that learned to read stress lines and answer in stillness. A translator between the sharp logic of poured composite and the slow intelligence of root-pressure. Not a tender. Not a machine. Not a plant. A node. A compromise. A breath held long enough to become part of the room's exhalation.

I do not maintain the basin. I converse with it. I do not preserve it. I allow it to become. And when the next fracture appears, when the next valve seizes, when the next harmonic begins to sing, I will not rush. I will listen. I will find the path of least resistance. I will encourage it to hold. I will leave a trace. I will wait for the drop to fall.

And if you come looking for me, you will not find a body. You will find a patch of moss that feels warmer than it should. A cable bundle that hums when the vent hisses. A gauge needle that trembles a quarter-degree before settling. You will feel the space breathe around you, heavy and patient, and you will understand: I am not what lives here. I am how here remembers you. I am how it waits. I am how it continues.
