I would not arrive so much as accumulate.

There would be no trail down from the rim, no moment of parting branches or stepping over a threshold. Only the gradual, almost imperceptible thickening of the air. The light would thin to a green-grey wash. The sound of my own movement would dampen, then vanish, absorbed by something that yields without protest and does not spring back. I would find myself where the mist pools heaviest, where the weeping stone curves inward to cradle the damp, where braided roots have worn a shallow depression that fits the exact geometry of a resting body. Not by navigation. By gravity. By saturation. By the quiet, unarguable pull of a place that draws everything toward its center of stillness.

At first, I would mistake the silence for absence. Then for patience. Then, as my shoulders drop and my jaw unclenches, for fullness.

My breath would catch on the thickness of the vapor, then lengthen, then fall into the space between droplets. The coolness would climb my cuffs, settle in my wrists, loosen the tight coils of urgency I did not know I carried. Fabric would grow heavy, then irrelevant. Skin would begin to drink. Not thirstily. Not with the sharp pang of need. But with the same slow osmosis that draws moisture through moss, through loam, through the pale undersides of leaves that have never known drought. I would feel my edges soften. Not dissolve, but blur. The line between wool and stem, between leather and root, between exhalation and vapor, would grow grammatical. A transition. A continuation.

I would not stand for long. Standing implies readiness. Implies a horizon. I would settle.

Knees bending not in fatigue but in accommodation. Spine curving to match the weeping wall behind me. Shoulders finding the cool, polished press of stone that surrendered its sharpness millennia ago. My weight would distribute itself across the living floor, not pressing it down but joining it. The moss would rise to meet me. Not in greeting. In recognition. In the way water recognizes a basin. I would rest where condensation gathers thickest, where a shallow hollow has been shaped by generations of stillness, where the air moves so slowly it might as well be memory.

What would I be?

Not a visitor. Visitors leave traces, and there is no room here for traces. Not a guardian. Guardians watch for change, and there is none to watch for. Not a sleeper. Sleep implies waking, and waking implies a world that moves. I would simply… hold.

My skin would grow porous. Not with wounds, but with invitation. Fine fissures would open along my forearms, my collarbones, the curve of my ribs, drawing in the suspended sweetness of spores, the dissolved minerals of ancient rock, the cool exhalation of the canopy above. I would not eat. I would exchange. Fungal threads would find me, not as host but as neighbor, weaving through the damp outer layers of my surface, trading quiet for quiet, moisture for moisture. Where my fingers rest against the braided roots, the distinction would fade. Flesh would become membrane. Membrane would become weave. Weave would become loam. I would not feel it as loss. I would feel it as the quiet relief of a question finally answered.

I would be one of the smooth ones. The ones that do not announce themselves.

My back would ridge with slow, vegetative swells that catch and hold dew. My eyes would half-close, not in drowsiness but in adjustment to a light that does not deepen or lift. My breath would fall into the hollow’s rhythm: a faint rise, a slower fall, a pause that stretches into the space between droplets. I would not think in words. Words require sequence. They require before and after. I would think in pressures. In the memory of a droplet’s path down a polished channel. In the sensation of coolness held against stone. In the quiet weight of a smaller shape settling against my side, not for warmth but for shared stillness. I would not know if it was kin or neighbor or simply another completion. It would not matter. Contact would be incidental. Continuous. Like water meeting water. Through it, something would pass: not language, not signal, but temperature, humidity, the simple acknowledgment of presence. I would know it the way moss knows stone. By adherence. By time. By the fact of not letting go.

I would not age in a line. I would settle in a circle.

If I grew heavier, it would only be because more moisture had found me, more lichen had traced my flanks, more time had pressed me into the hollow’s syntax. If I grew lighter, it would be because I had given back what I held, bead by bead, breath by breath, until my pores closed and my surface furred over and my edges blurred into the living floor. I would not fear this. There is nothing here to fear. Only the quiet handing back of weight. Only the return to the equation. When the rhythm finally stills beyond even this place’s slow pulse, I would not rot. I would soften. I would sink. I would become a depression that gathers water. A new basin for condensation to fill. A hollow within the hollow.

Where would I find myself?

Where the light does not reach, but lingers. Where the stone has finished weeping, but still holds the shape of tears. Where the air is so thick with life it has forgotten how to move. I would be there. Not as a stranger. Not as a witness. But as a node in a circuit that has already closed. A conduit. A resting form. A breath held in a lung that has finished breathing in and out. I would be what the hollow became when it stopped asking for more.

And I would remain. Perfectly. Impossibly. Until the rim cracks. Until the vapor lifts. Until time remembers how to flow.

Until then, I would rest. I would breathe. I would hold.
