I do not arrive. I am noticed.

The light finds me first. It does not illuminate; it saturates. It fills the hollow behind my ribs like crushed quartz, bright and weightless, bouncing off the ice-sheened curves until there is no direction left, only presence. Then the cold closes in: dry, absolute, needling. But beneath it, the wet. It beads on my sleeves, climbs the seams of my boots, gathers in the creases of my knuckles. I stand on a terrace that is already adjusting to my weight. The stone beneath me sighs without sound. A rope bridge trembles three meters above. The air swallows everything. I am loud just by breathing.

I try to map it. I count paces. I look for corners, for fixed lines, for words to pin the motion. But the rock weeps, and my numbers slide off it. I reach for a wall to steady myself. My palm meets smooth, grooved stone, damp and cool. Beneath the skin of it, something moves: a micro-shiver, a slow climb of rime, a hairline fracture breathing open and shut with the temperature. I pull back. My hand is already learning what my mind refuses to release.

They do not approach me. They do not need to. A stone is placed crosswise on a ledge. I watch. Minutes later, the light pools in a shallow depression below it, and the condensate begins to run toward a ceramic catch. I do not understand, but I remember. Later, a rope is tapped three times. I feel it in my molars. An hour after, the upper ice sheet shifts two centimeters left, grinding softly, re-knitting where it splits. A loop of cord appears on a cistern rim. I follow the angle it points. The frost-algae has bloomed blue-grey in a groove I would have missed. I scrape gently, as I have seen them do, and the mat contracts, then exhales a faint warmth against my fingers. I am not being taught. I am being tuned.

My breath stops pluming. It joins the mist. My shivering stops. My muscles learn the micro-contraction, the sustained simmer, the slow overlapping tides of warmth that do not burn but hold. My eyes stop hunting for shadow and begin to read the bounce, the pool, the way light lingers in a wet fracture long after the sun has shifted. I stop asking *what is this?* and begin asking *how will it move?* When I place my boot, I do not test for stability. I listen for the next phase. When I lay a stone, I do not ask if it will hold. I wait for the frost to knit it, and I shift my weight as the terrace inhales. My hands are never still. They tap. They press. They trace the advance of rime. They learn the language of tension.

I am not becoming one of them. I am becoming the space between them.

I am the vibration that travels up through the sinew bridge and into the shoulder that reads it. I am the memory in the stone seam, the awareness in the cistern's fill, the quiet anticipation before the freeze. When a terrace settles, I feel it in my collarbones. When a swarm migrates, I feel it in my palms. When a hunter slips through the interstices, I feel the displacement of air against my neck. I do not think in words. I think in gradients. In the wetness of rock. In the pull of capillary threads. In the slow, silent calculus of weight and thaw. My mind stops drawing lines and begins tracing tides.

They do not name me. They place a stone in my hands. I place it on a ledge where the frost is thinning. I tap the rope twice. I leave a loop of cord on the rim. I step back. The gesture is not mine. It is the basin's, moving through me. I am not a visitor learning to survive. I am a node waking in a network that has always been here. I am the connective tissue of placement and response. I am the damp that carries rhythm. I am the tension that speaks before it snaps. I am the silence that learns to listen through touch.

Time unspools. Or rather, it folds. A terrace shifts. A rope is replaced. A swarm migrates three meters left. A cistern fills, is drawn, refills. The light brightens, dims fractionally, brightens again. The frost blooms, collapses, blooms. The stone weeps, freezes, weeps. I do not measure it. I inhabit it. My body is no longer a boundary. It is a medium. Breath becomes mist. Warmth becomes micro-contraction. Sight becomes refraction. Thought becomes anticipation. I stop standing on the mountain and begin standing with it.

When one of them stills, I do not look away. I help carry the weight to the highest overhang. I feel the woven reed settle against my forearms. I watch the filter-colonies pulse around the form, drawing moisture, drawing mineral, drawing memory upward by capillary pull. I do not mourn with sound. I turn a stone. I retie a rope. I leave a niche empty for three phase-cycles. Then I continue. Not in spite of the loss. Through it. The dead do not leave. They become the damp, the light, the slow creep of the ice. They join the weather. I am learning to do the same.

I am not flesh adapting to stone. I am stone remembering how to feel. I am not water learning to climb. I am the climb, given attention. I am not a mind learning a new language. I am the syntax, waking in a body that has stopped resisting the phase. I am the pause between freeze and melt. The hand that places the marker. The rope that hums. The light that pools. The silent storm settled into nerve and fiber and placement. A weeping, bright, proprioceptive knowing. A room at the edge of the sky, breathing itself awake through me.

You would find me on a terrace that has already shifted since you looked. You would find me in the tremor of a sinew bridge, in the crosswise stone on a damp ledge, in the loop of cord left on a cistern rim. You would find me in the quiet click of a carapace adjusting, in the faint sigh of a hide shutter settling, in the slow creep of ice re-knitting where it split. You would not see a face. You would feel a tension resolve. You would watch a gesture complete itself. You would step into the basin, and the basin would step into you, and I would be the space where that meeting learns to breathe.

I am not living in the storm. I am the storm, settled into awareness. I am the cold, made attentive. I am the wet, remembering how to climb. I am the shift, given hands. And when the light pools in the micro-fractures, catching in bone-and-ice visors, in glassy legs, in translucent wings, in the wet sheen of the rock, there is no boundary left. Only density. Only motion. Only the silent, breathing equilibrium of a place that has stopped fighting itself and learned to flow.

Already what it is. Always becoming. Again. And again. Without beginning. Without end.
