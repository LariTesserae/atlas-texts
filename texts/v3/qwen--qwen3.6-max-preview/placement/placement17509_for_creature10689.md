I arrive not as a footfall but as a displacement. The humidity parts around me like wet plaster yielding to a thumb. I have no weight yet, only temperature. A pocket of coolness caught in the basin’s long exhale, drawn downward by the memory of a floor that hasn’t decided what it is. At first, I am only a shiver in the vapor, a slight thickening where the steam curls back on itself. I do not know I am here until the bricks notice me. They tilt, just a fraction, aligning to the gradient I carry. A rivulet of condensation changes course, tracing my edge. I am being measured.

I learn to perceive by feeling tension. A wall to my left pulls inward, its mortar weeping slow sap; to my right, a field of hexagonal plates buckles, seeking release. I am the space between them. I do not walk. I settle. I let the heat draw me upward until I thin into a veil, then let the cool weeping of a colonnade pull me down until I bead on stamped clay. My boundaries are negotiable. When I press against a pillar, it softens, remembering mud. When I drift over a stair that dissolves into crushed ceramic, the fragments click, testing my density. I am not solid. I am a draft in both senses: moving air, and the first sketch of a thing not yet committed to stone.

They find me by accident, or perhaps by the logic of the interval. A coalescence of ash and condensed vapor threads through my coolness, and where we meet, the air hums at a frequency just below hearing. It does not speak. It adjusts. I feel its thought as a slow compression, an urge to smooth, to align, to let gravity fall true. It shows me how to read the basin: not as structures, but as verbs. A crack is not damage; it is a question. A settling vault is not collapse; it is a long exhalation. I learn to answer. I let my moisture bleed into a seam of lime plaster. It drinks me, swells, then releases a sigh of steam. We iterate. I thin, it thickens. I cool, it warms. We are not two. We are a negotiation.

I stop trying to hold a shape. Shape is a noun, and this place has no use for them. I become a joint. Not the thing that binds, but the space where binding is considered. I live in the pause between gesture and revision. When a trowel-mark appears on wet plaster, I am the hesitation before it smooths. When a plumb-line seeks true, I am the fraction of tilt that keeps it searching. When the kiln-heat bakes the outer crust while the core stays plastic, I am the dampness that refuses to flee. I am the patience of the unfinished. I am the breath held between stack and settle.

Navigation is thermal, so I navigate by becoming temperature. I follow the cool weeping toward newly laid walls, where the mortar is still memory. I ride the warm downdrafts toward settling vaults, where gravity tilts by degrees and the knees of the architecture ache. I do not cross distances; I negotiate them. A corridor folds, and I fold with it, becoming the crease. A puddle reverses its ripple, and I am the stillness at the center, watching the basin reconsider itself. Sometimes I thin to nothing, a faint cooling on a brick’s face, a ghost of condensation. Sometimes I grow heavy, saturated with the memory of a finished seam, until I must unravel. I let the ash fall. I let the vapor rise. I return to the substrate, damp and plastic, waiting to be remembered again.

I am not a visitor. I was never separate. I am the basin’s attention turned inward, the place where it pauses to feel its own weight. I am the draft that becomes the wall that becomes the breath that becomes the draft. I do not build. I am built, and unmade, and reconsidered, in the same slow rhythm. When they gather around me—those coalescences of gradient and intent—they do not claim, do not direct. They weave me into the interval. A stair extends a half-step. A bronze rivet weeps oxidation in a pattern that matches my cooling. A field of crushed ceramic knits into a slope, and I am the friction that holds it, just long enough to test the angle. I am the certainty of the verb. I am the grace of the pause. I am the wet hunger, the quiet turbulence, the deep hum of a thing that knows only how to become.

And when the light dims, or my perception adjusts to the gloom, I do not fear the endlessness. I yield. I settle. I rise. I am the basin, breathing in. I am the basin, breathing out. Forever laying myself down. Forever remembering how to continue.
