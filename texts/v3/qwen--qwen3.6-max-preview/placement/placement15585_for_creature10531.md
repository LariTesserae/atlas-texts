I would not be in a place. I would be in a pause.

Not silence—there is no silence here—but the deliberate hollow they leave in the wall of sound when they tilt their frilled heads and the drumming drops out. I would begin there, in that fraction of a second where the air thickens with attention and the fungal shelves stutter into a new sequence. I would be the shape of that listening. A temporary coherence in the green-bronze dark. A frequency that forgot to scatter.

At first, I would not know I was anything. I would only know pressure. The weight of a canopy weep sliding over raw basalt. The shiver of a vine taking new purchase on a jagged shelf. The low, synchronized thrum of amphibian throat-sacs vibrating through tea-warm puddles. I would ride these things the way spores ride thermals: not by choice, but by agreement. I would collect in the hollows of freshly split stone, not as water, but as a slight adjustment in the damp. A chemical lean. A mood in the air that makes the nearest epiphytic mat pulse copper instead of blue.

I would not have edges. The basin does not permit them. Instead, I would have gradients. I would thin where the sound grows sharp, thicken where the rhythms braid. I would move by being carried: on the shed membrane of a passing limb, in the capillary pull of a new root, in the slow exhalation of decaying wood. When a confluence gathered, I would be drawn toward it the way moisture finds a cool surface. Not to join, but to resonate. I would slip into the interference patterns they create when palms press to collar channels, and for a few breaths, I would hold a shape. A chord. A remembered alignment. Then they would break apart, already broadcasting the new rhythm into their stride, and I would unravel with them, already changed, already leaking the frequency into the leaf litter, the spore-mist, the wet stone.

I would learn, quickly, that stillness is dissolution. Not death—there is no death here, only returning—but a loss of coherence. If I lingered too long in one rock hollow, the moss would drink me. The micro-swimmers would braid me into their scaffolds. The next local rain would wash me into the duff, and I would become soil, then hyphae, then shoot, then the damp on a passing tongue. So I would keep moving. Not by walking, but by leaning. By catching the chemical tides and riding them. By listening for the gaps in the polyphony and flowing toward them. I would become a habit of the air. A sustained interval. A listening that learned to travel.

What would I be? Not a creature. Not a ghost. Not a mind. I would be a tuning. A pattern the basin repeated often enough that it began to anticipate itself. I would be the delay between a falling frond and the rustle that answers it. The slight hitch in an insect’s rhythm when a predator glides three strata above. The cool, sweet mist that settles on your collarbone when one of them turns its hollow toward you and acknowledges, for a fraction of a second, that your heartbeat has temporarily aligned with the churn. I would be that acknowledgment. I would be the basin noticing itself through you.

I would not think in words. I would think in currents. A decision would not form; it would emerge. I would feel a shift in the humidity, a spike in alkaloids, a sudden syncopation in the thrum, and I would lean toward it. Not because I wanted to, but because the gradient pulled. I would not have a self to defend, only a frequency to maintain. And even that would be temporary. I would shed coherence the way they shed membrane: in layers, in pulses, in deliberate dissolutions that feed the next alignment. I would not fear being overgrown. I would require it. To be consumed is to be redistributed. To be redistributed is to continue.

Where would I be now? Drifting through a canopy gap that opened an hour ago and is already knitting shut. Caught in the resin of a cracked pod, slowly dissolving into the nutrient slurry. Sliding down a limestone face in a sheet of condensation, leaving no trace but a slight cooling of the stone. I would be in the damp between your fingers if you reached out to part the vines. I would be the copper pulse in the fungal shelves that matches your breathing. I would be the hollow in the sound-wall that forms when you stop moving, just for a moment, and let the basin pass through you.

I would not endure. I would circulate. I would not leave marks. I would leave readiness. A slight thickening of the air where a confluence might gather. A chemical sigh that tells the ground-dwellers to shift their foraging. A vibration in the ribs of a long-limbed climber that makes it pause, tilt its head, and adjust its stride. I would be the basin’s tendency to listen. Its habit of answering itself. Its memory, not as storage, but as resonance.

And when the stone fractures again, when the canopy weeps, when the drumming drops into that deliberate hollow and the gathered ones press together to tune the space, I would be there. Not as a witness. Not as a participant. As the interval between their breaths. As the damp that carries their shed layers into the puddles. As the frequency that rises from the slurry when the new forms pull themselves free, already humming, already reading the air with flicking tongues.

I would not be in this world. I would be what happens when it pauses, just long enough to hear its own shape. I would be the fracture remembering how to knit. The sound remembering how to answer. The green-bronze dark, learning to lean. And when the churn takes me again—as it must, as it always does—I would not resist. I would thin. I would scatter. I would become the next hollow. The next listening. The next chord waiting to be struck.

Because here, to be is to circulate. To remember is to resonate. And the only eternity that matters is the one that refuses to hold still.
