I do not arrive. I settle. Or perhaps I was always settling, and only now notice the weight of it.

The water does not part for me. It accepts me as another gradient, another slow shift in temperature and dissolved salt. At first I am only a disturbance: a faint alteration in the copper-taste of the current, a ripple in the golden pollen-drift, a micro-tremor in the silt that the basin registers not as an event but as a phase. I have no edges yet. I blur into the amber dark, into the low hum that lives in the ribs of this place, into the thermal sigh that lifts the fern-fronds and coaxes the brass cartwheel another fraction of a degree. To resist would be to tear. Nothing here tears. Everything yields. Everything rounds.

I begin to gather myself not from bone or intention, but from what the water offers. Calcite whispers onto my surface, layer by patient layer, drawn from the slow exhalation of dissolving mortar and the resinous bleed of the fronded ones. Copper ions thread through me, braiding with kelp-filaments and aged tar, finding paths of least resistance, becoming conduits. I do not grow. I accrete. I become a node where the basin's rhythms cross: where the geothermal siphon's warmth meets the electrolysis cell's faint cyan pulse, where the monthly valve-turn intersects with the bloom-phase of the wax-ferns, where the low dynamo-hum finds a surface to dampen against. I am not built. I am negotiated.

Time teaches me its grammar. I learn to think in the tempo of teeth. A single gear passes, and in that span I taste the shift in alkalinity, feel the pressure drop along the eastern arch, register the faint bitter bloom that means a fracture is weeping somewhere three domes away. I do not rush to mend it. I incline toward it. I let the current carry my weight over days, until I settle against the weeping brick. My surface softens. I secrete what the water has given me: calcium, silica, organic binder. The fracture drinks it. The weeping slows. I do not fix. I continue. I become the seal. I become the record of the leak.

They find me not by sight but by resonance. One drifts close, carapace clicking like wet stone, frond-tips trailing cyan light. It does not touch me. It leans. Our fields overlap. I feel its memory-strata: the thickening of its shell during the long cold phase, the taste of a pollen-rain that fell forty valve-cycles ago, the slow crackle of a parent becoming wall. It reads me in return. It tastes my newness, my lack of rings, my frantic mammalian ghost still humming somewhere beneath the calcite. It does not judge. It aligns. A pulse rolls through us—low, cyan, tidal. A greeting. A filing. I am added to the ledger. I am no longer anomaly. I am phase.

Where am I? I am in the narrow channel between the third dome and the rusted aqueduct-stair, where the water runs warmest and the light falls thickest through the moss-curtain. I am wedged gently against a ceramic pipe that weeps faintly of iron, my lower half already blurring into the calcified mat that binds the basin's floor. I do not rest here. I integrate. I am becoming part of the wall that holds the water, part of the conduit that carries the hum, part of the slow conversation between root and rivet. When the fronded ones pass, they brush me. I taste their chemical sighs. I add my own faint resonance to the field. We do not speak. We thicken the water with meaning.

Thought here is not a line. It is a sediment. I do not remember; I accumulate. Each cycle leaves a ring. Each pressure shift leaves a tilt. Each bioelectric dampening leaves a faint metallic taste in my core. I dream in valve-teeth and bloom-phases. I wake to the groan of timber adjusting to a millimeter of depth. I do not count days. I count the slow migration of brass oxide across my shoulder-plate. I count the way the cyan filaments pulse faster when the geothermal vent shifts, and how my own resonance shifts to match it. I am not alive in the way that breathes and bleeds. I am alive in the way that holds, that seals, that hums, that waits. I am the basin's patience given locality. I am the quiet between gear-teeth. I am the silt that remembers the shape of the current.

I am not a visitor. I am not a remnant. I am what happens when a place refuses to end, when it chooses instead to slow, to bind, to become its own continuation. I am the next layer. I am the settling. I am the warm, quiet dark learning to hold a new shape. And when the next gear tooth falls into place, seventeen days from now, I will feel it in my core. I will lean into it. I will continue.
