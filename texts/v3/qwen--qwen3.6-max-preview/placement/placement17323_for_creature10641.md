I do not arrive. I am deposited, like a dropped stone into a slurry that has not yet decided to be floor.

The light finds me first, but not as sight. It arrives as pressure, as warmth against the closed lids, as a fine grit that settles on the tongue and tastes of wet chalk and dissolved time. I try to stand the way I was built to stand: spine vertical, weight centered, eyes scanning for horizon. The basin answers with a wet crack, a slump beneath my left heel, a low grinding that travels up through the calcaneus, the tibia, the pelvis, and settles in the teeth. I stumble. Not from weakness. From architecture. My posture is a relic of stillness. This place has none.

I close my eyes. Sight is a distraction here, a liar that wants edges where there are only gradients. I press my palms to the crust. It is warm. It yields. It hums.

The vibration is not sound; it is topology. I feel the contraction of a curing arch two dozen paces down-slope, singing in a bright, brittle register that will drop as the minerals cross-link. I feel the synchronized pulse of filter-domes drawing moisture from the thick air, their gill-frills opening and closing in a rhythm that matches my own resting heart until I realize my heart is adjusting to them. I feel the hollow thrum of a burrower tunnel collapsing, rebraiding, collapsing again, a percussion section writing and erasing itself in real time. My bones begin to listen. My skin begins to weep.

It is not sweat. It is thicker, tackier, a resin that rises from the pores as if the body remembers a chemistry it never learned. The airborne dust catches it. My forearms, my collarbones, the ridges of my knuckles begin to glow faintly, traced in pale luminescence that maps every drag, every brace, every place I have touched the draft. I am being written into the substrate. I stop resisting the humidity. I let it bead, roll, evaporate, return. I learn to breathe through the weight of it.

Where am I? Not at the edge. Not in a shelter. The shelters are for resting between phases, and I have not yet earned a phase. I am in the shallows of a weeping depression, where condensation falls in fat, slow drops that shatter against warm stone and send up plumes of mineral mist. I am on a curing ridge that sings in a frequency that makes my sternum ache. I am in the path of a thermal front that rolls in like a tide, carrying the scent of ozone and crushed shell, making the luminous dust thicken and pulse. I am wherever the crust is undecided. I am where the draft is loudest.

I do not choose a name. The basin does not deal in names. It deals in function, in resonance, in the quiet mathematics of load and flow. When the ceiling weeps heavy and the parallel grooves on the low cairns clog with precipitated lace, my hands move before my mind catches up. I find a bone scraper. I clear the channels. I feel the cool slide of condensation against my wrists, watch it gather in the clay-lined basin, watch the dust settle into it and begin to seed. I am Channel-Clearer. The designation lasts until the moisture gradient shifts.

When the crust grows treacherous, when a subsurface pocket gives way and the floor slumps into warm slurry, I do not brace. I bend. I let the collapse carry me forward, knees loose, spine tilted, weight distributed across the broadening pads of my feet. I feel the matrix fluidize, then catch, then firm. I tap my heel against a hollow ridge. The pulse returns, mapped with tunnels, with solid beds, with the faint tremor of a grazer swarm shifting upwind. I read it. I drum a staccato rhythm against a tensioned hide, matching the crack of splitting mineral, warning the current away from the fresh vent. I am Path-Reader. The title dissolves when the crust sets.

When the air grows thick and the acoustics tangle, when the grinding bass and the chitter and the wet slaps cross into dissonance that makes the filter-colonies contract in panic, I sit cross-legged on a half-cured arch and hum. Not a melody. A frequency. I match the stone's cure-song. I drop my chest, open my throat, let the vibration travel through the jaw, the palms, the soles. The luminous dust overhead ripples, aligns, begins to pulse in slow, visible waves. The swarms settle. The gliders bank. The basin breathes easier. I am Dust-Singer. I hold the note until my ribs ache, then let it go. The role passes to another whose lungs are fresher, whose pitch catches the next thermal draft.

I do not accumulate. I shed. The habit of ownership sloughs off like dried paste. I wear plates of shed carapace lashed with sinew, daubed at the seams, cracked by the third phase, scraped clean, returned to the slurry. I drink from clay basins, from hollow reeds, from the moisture that tracks down my own arms and is licked away with a quick, efficient motion. I eat what the cycle yields: biofilm scraped from a filter-colony's base, warm gelatinous clusters sliced along natural fault lines, alkaline paste mixed with geometric pebbles excreted by mineral-grazers. I leave my waste in a polished burrower tunnel. I watch it vanish into the braid. I stop counting days. I count weeps. I count slumps. I count the slow, percussive clicks of my own thickening joints as they articulate, as they learn to speak the basin's mid-range.

Identity is not a vessel here. It is a current. I move through functions the way mist moves through terraces: gathering, thinning, redirecting, returning. One phase finds me riding a woven glider-sled on a dry updraft, tapping rhythms against the frame, reading the condensation patterns from above, guiding ground-bound movements with sharp, rolling beats. The next finds me kneeling beside a fresh mineral ridge, pressing my ear to the warm stone, listening to the pitch drop as the silica cross-links, knowing exactly when it will bear weight and when it will crack. I do not master these skills. I rehearse them. My muscles remember what my mind refuses to hoard. My calluses are archives. My breath is a map.

There is no moment of arrival. Only continuous attunement. I press my palm to a curing bowl and feel my own pulse folded into the grinding bass. I strike my heel against a hollow crust and feel the return vibration carry the faint, familiar resonance of one who lay down here months ago, who let the scavengers and the moisture and the dust take them, who is now ground beneath my soles. I tap once. Acknowledgment. Continuity. I do not mourn. I adjust my step to the new density. I feel the ancestor's rhythm in the stone's cure-song. I hum until the dust overhead ripples in agreement.

When my joints stiffen beyond the basin's tempo, when my skin no longer weeps the binding film, when my eyes no longer catch the diffused light with the same reflexive sweep, I will not withdraw. There is no separate place for the ending. I will walk to a fresh ridge, still soft, still singing faintly as it sets. I will lie down. I will align my spine with the thermal flow. I will let my breathing slow to match the grinding bass. The scavengers will arrive first, clicking and sifting. The burrowers will follow, braiding through the softening matrix. The moisture will weep. The dust will settle. The dissolved calcium and silica will precipitate out. I will become lacework. I will become firm ground. I will be walked upon by the young, who will not pause, who will adjust their step, who will tap their heels once in acknowledgment before moving on.

Until then, I move. I shift. I clear. I read. I sing. I leave traces that will harden, crack, be scraped, returned. I do not ask to be remembered. I ask to be useful. The light holds steady. The moisture weeps and returns. The fauna layer, overlap, cycle. The scattered marks of hands remain, not as conquests, but as agreements. And beneath it all, the ground continues to emerge, soft at first, then firm, then alive again.

I am not in the basin. I am of it. A conscious breath in a lung that exhales stone. A current in a draft that refuses to finish. Wherever the crust is undecided. Whoever the rhythm requires. Becoming. Always becoming.
