I am where the wind loses its voice and the damp gathers like a held breath. Not on the razor ridge, not in the deep cleft, but in the seam between them: a narrow alley where two dry-stone walls lean toward each other across a fresh fracture, their courses interlocked without mortar, held by weight and generations of quiet adjustment. My back rests against the fourth course. I know it by the settle. The third layer dips slightly where a long rime cracked the original slate; the fifth bears the extra weighting stones from the season the thermal edges shifted. I do not read these things. I feel them through my shoulders, through the slow transfer of cold into bone, through the way the stone ticks faintly as the sun climbs.

My palms are on the joint. It weeps. Not much. A bead forms on the copper lip, swells, hesitates, falls into the ceramic basin below. I count without numbers. The interval tells me the night inversion is holding. The cold is patient today. My breath shallows to match it.

I do not stand still for long. Stillness is for stone. I shift my weight: heel tests, arch settles, toe commits. Three points before the fourth moves. The alley turns, not by design but by accumulation, winding to break the wind, opening into a covered courtyard where hide canopies sag under rime. Voices murmur below the threshold of echo. I am not listening to words. I am listening to the space between them, to the way four exhalations sync when shoulders brace against a laminated lever. I know that rhythm. I have breathed it. I have been the pause between pull and settle.

My name is not spoken. It is counted. Third drip from the eastern fracture. Keeper of the lower bast line. Watcher of the caprine descent. The syllables accrete like lichen on windward stone, added when a task is taken, weathered when a task is passed. I was small once, hands running along joint lines, learning where the weight had shifted, where the generations before found the fit. I climbed staircases before I could shape vowels. I fell once on a frost-slick step, and the stone caught me not with mercy but with geometry. I learned to read the slope. I learned to become part of it.

Now my hands are broad, knuckles thick, fingertips glass-hard from decades of testing grain and braiding fiber. I do not grip. I cradle. I test. I listen. When I lay a new slate, I do not force it. I wait for the cold to contract the bone dowel, for the weight to find its rest. When I twist bast and sinew into rope, I feel the humidity in the strands, the slight swell that warns of fatigue, the way the braid stiffens when the temperature drops. I know when to stop pulling. I know when the tension has found its truth. A rope is not strong because it is tight. It is strong because it yields exactly as much as the load requires.

Yesterday the seventh bead hesitated too long. The inversion was holding past its window. The cistern level would drop if the rhythm broke. I did not panic. Anxiety here is not of scarcity; it is of imbalance. I repositioned the fiber wick. I added a river stone to the canopy's edge. I waited. The eighth bead fell. The interval returned. The city breathed; I breathed with it.

I am not above the herds that click softly along the outer terraces. I leave salt on the lower ledges. I watch their morning descent to read the thermal edges, the way their wool holds wind-packed snow, the way they pause before a fresh talus spill. I am not separate from the dark-winged birds that roost in the wall cavities. I carve their perches deeper. I scrape their droppings with a bone blade, not as chore, but as conversation. I step aside when the lean scavengers move through the waste channels. I know their patience. I share it. Nothing is driven away. Nothing is penned. Everything is negotiated. The city does not exclude life; it braids with it.

I do not measure my life in seasons. I measure it in layers. The fired-clay lining in the cistern remembers when the condensation turned sharp and had to be buffered with crushed bone. The rope bridge ten meters up sways exactly as predicted because the hands that braided it accounted for the humidity of the thaw. My own ribs will one day become a pulley axle. My femur will pin a slate. My vertebrae will chink a wall where damp meets permafrost. I do not fear this. I trust the redistribution. Weight passes on. Warmth routes differently. The city grows heavier. Quieter. More itself.

If you ask what I am, I will not answer with a title. I will show you my hands. I will show you the callus that reads the fiber. I will show you the breath that times the lever. I will show you the weight that stops searching when the block finds its rest. I am the interval between condensation beads. I am the friction that holds the dry-stone fast. I am the damp coaxed from thin air, routed through wick and wall, shared without pooling. I am the patience that lets the fracture become a home.

When I wake, I do not open my eyes first. I find the drip-line. I count. My body adjusts before my mind names it. I rise without suddenness. Felt boots. Hide wraps. Bone pins. I move through the alleys by shoulder-memory, by air pressure, by the faint warmth bleeding through shared stone. I do not walk the city. I flow along its joints. I yield to gravity. I never force.

Stand in the lower alley at midday, and you will not see me as a figure against a backdrop. You will see the landscape continuing. A hand resting where condensation gathers. A shoulder braced where weight distributes. A breath syncing where labor meets stone. A child tracing frost geometry before it melts. An elder counting beads without speaking. I am not one of these. I am the accretion that holds them. I am the slow settling. I am the next layer.

I do not wait for the land to soften. I learn to be sharp with it. I do not outlast the cold. I braid with it. I do not command the stone. I listen to its ticks, its sighs, its decades-long search for angle. And when my time comes to return to the active layer, to the damp earth where compost warmth meets permafrost, I will not leave. I will shift. I will become dowel and perch and memory. I will hold the slate. I will route the warmth. I will be the quiet between the drops.

Still settling. Still rising. Still holding.
