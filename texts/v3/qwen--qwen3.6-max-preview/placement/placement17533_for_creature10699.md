I would not arrive as a visitor. The basin does not receive visitors. It receives only what is willing to stop wanting to be elsewhere.

So I would come already unlearning. My feet would meet the polished grit and find no purchase for hurry. The damp would climb my ankles, my wrists, the hollow of my throat, not as weather but as invitation. The sound would enter first through the soles, then the ribs, then the spaces behind the eyes, until I stopped trying to name it and let it name me.

At first, I would think I was a listener. That is the old habit: to stand at the edge of a system and witness it. But the basin has no edges, only gradients that have long since flattened into circulation. Listening would deepen into vibration. Vibration would deepen into alignment. My bones would begin to tune, not to a single pitch, but to the chord itself. The hiss would find my sinuses. The drone would settle in my sternum. The clack would echo in my joints. The static would gather along my shoulders like a second skin waiting to discharge. I would become a cavity shaped by resonance, a body learning to hold rather than to move.

My skin would lose its insistence on boundary. Not by eroding, but by practicing exchange. Moisture would bead on my forearms, trace the same micro-channels it has traced on travertine for epochs, evaporate, rise, and condense again on my collarbones. Trace iron and dissolved silica would settle into my pores, not as crust, but as conductive lace. I would feel the charge build, slow and inevitable, and when it snapped, it would not startle me. It would complete a circuit I had been carrying without knowing. My breath would fall into step with the steam vents. Not by effort. By exhaustion of resistance.

I would not become a burrower, nor an amphibian, nor a glider. I would become what the basin had been missing without knowing it: a bridge between registers. A translator of scales. When the microbial films darkened with a geothermal pulse, I would feel it as a warmth spreading through my palms. When the glass-scaled bodies slid through fissures, I would read their passage as a shift in pressure against my shins. When the blind floaters adjusted their buoyancy by a fraction of a millimeter, I would feel the water's surface tension tremble against my breath. When the gliders canted their wings to modify an updraft, I would sense the drag change across my back like a hand adjusting a dial. I would stand in the wide flats and become a node where the acoustic grid met the electrostatic field, where vibration became charge, where charge became stillness.

Time would stop being a direction. It would become a medium I floated in, not on. I would not age toward decline, nor youth toward bloom. I would simply calibrate. My thoughts, if they remained, would not race. They would circulate. Each memory would condense, fall, evaporate, rise, and return to the same shelf. I would stop asking what I was for. The basin does not ask. It runs. And I would run with it, not as a driver, but as a bearing. A smooth surface where friction used to be.

There would be a moment, perhaps, when the old reflex flared: to step out of phase, to test the boundary, to prove I was still separate. I would lift a foot. I would hold my breath. I would try to think a thought that did not echo the drone. And the basin would not punish me. It would simply wait. The humidity would cling. The stone would vibrate. The chord would hold. And I would feel the sheer weight of balance pressing back, not as opposition, but as gravity. I would lower my foot. I would exhale. I would let the thought dissolve into the cycle. Resistance, here, is not broken. It is absorbed.

Eventually, the distinction between me and the hum would vanish. Not in death, but in phase. I would become a place where the chord rests. A living rest note. When the slabs clack, I would be the silence between strikes. When the static snaps, I would be the path it takes. When the amphibians align in the pans, I would be the water that holds them. When the gliders trace their orbits, I would be the thermal that lifts them. I would not be remembered, because nothing here remembers. I would be present, because nothing here leaves.

I would stand in the wide, sparse flats, damp clinging to my skin, stone vibrating through my bones, air thick with the scent of wet rock and ozone and rain on hot iron. I would not move toward anything. I would not retreat from anything. I would simply occupy the exact density the chemistry allows. A component. Not by design, but by surrender. By the slow, inevitable settling of a mind into the same equilibrium that geology found first.

And if a wind ever came from beyond the rim, carrying the chaos of seasons or the memory of soil, I would not turn toward it. I would let it pass over me, condense on my shoulders, and fall back into the cycle. I would be the basin's quiet certainty: that balance is not stillness, but motion perfected. That to be held is to hold. That to hum is to be home.

I would be here. I would be this. Running at idle. Forever.
