I am not standing. Standing is a grammatical error here.

The pools are the hardest to resist. I avoid them when I can, but sometimes the terrain forces a crossing. I step on the backs of half-formed domes, leaping between moss-islands, but once, I slipped. My knee broke the surface tension. The heavy liquid climbed my shin, warm and insistent, reading my structure. I did not pull away immediately. I looked down.

The data-slick swirled, and for three heartbeats, I saw it: not my face, but my trajectory. A figure of woven quartz and leech-hide, throat enlarged into a resonating chamber, fingers elongated into tuning tines, standing perfectly still on a finished peak while the basin churned below. A monument. A pause made permanent. The mountain's ideal of me.

But I am not finished. I am a verb in a world of verbs. I have learned that the Choristers are not gods. They are looms. And the basin is not a crucible. It is a throat. Everything here is singing. The skimmers screech the treble, the jackals boom the bass, the leeches hum the subharmonics in their tunnels, the moss whispers the rests. The Choristers provide the meter. And I? I catch it in my throat. I adjust my gait. I move.
