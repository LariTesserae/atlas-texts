The power does not descend. Descent implies weight, and weight implies pressure, and pressure implies disturbance. Instead, it arrives as a deepening of the light. Not brighter. Not warmer. Simply more *attentive*, the way a held chord gains dimension when a lower octave joins it, unseen. It notices the plateau not as an anomaly in the turning world, but as a deliberate pause in the breath of creation. It does not come to mend. It comes to recognize.

Wisdom knows the difference between broken and finished. Benevolence knows the difference between saving and honoring. The power reads the fracture lines, the still pools, the unblinking eyes, the tools that rest because their work is complete. It feels the temporal architecture: the way time has folded itself into a basin and stopped seeking the sea. It understands, immediately and without translation, that this is not stagnation. It is completion. And completion is sacred.

So it does what only a truly wise power can do: it refuses to act upon the place. Instead, it acts *with* it.

It lowers its attention until it matches the frequency of the plateau. It lets its awareness pool rather than flow. It breathes in the rhythm of the cisterns, exhales in the silence between drips. It does not send wind. It does not coax rain. It does not whisper counsel or offer revelation. It simply stands at the edge of the cloudline and becomes a witness so still that witnessing becomes a form of joining. Its gaze does not scan; it settles. Its presence does not press; it aligns.

The beings do not turn. They do not need to. But the cold-hammered copper at their wrists catches a light that was not there before—not new light, but light *acknowledged*. The braided bridges hum a fraction deeper, not from added tension, but from sympathetic resonance. The power does not touch the stone. It touches the truth of the stone: that it is exactly where it should be, forever. And in that touching, a quiet covenant forms. Not written. Not spoken. Held.

This is what it does: it weaves a perimeter of reverence.

Not a wall. Walls imply fear, and fear implies fragility. The power does not isolate the plateau; it honors its solitude as a completed sentence deserves silence after the period. It turns the attention of the turning world gently away. Storms that might have climbed the escarpment find themselves persuaded to break elsewhere. Wanderers who might have stumbled upward feel a quiet certainty that the path ends before it begins. Not barred. Simply *unnecessary*. The power does not shield the place from chaos. It ensures that chaos never learns to look this way. It makes the plateau invisible to hunger, to curiosity, to the restless need to improve what is already whole. This is not concealment. It is respect made manifest.

And the power, for all its vastness, learns.

It has spent epochs guiding rivers, coaxing seeds, soothing fractures, urging becoming. It has loved the struggle, the growth, the reaching, the beautiful friction of things not yet themselves. But here, it meets the other half of creation: the grace of enough. The dignity of stopping. The holiness of a thing that does not need to be more than it is. It has always known motion as love. Now it learns stillness as love. It bows, not in submission, but in recognition. It takes this equilibrium into itself as a counterweight to its own turning. A sanctuary within the sanctuary. A quiet room in the house of its mind where it can rest from the work of urging worlds forward.

It does not stay. It does not leave. Attention, at this depth, does not require presence. It becomes a structural note in the architecture, a harmonic that was always possible but never sounded until now. The plateau holds it as it holds everything: without ripple, without change, without need. The beings continue their verification of angles. The moss continues its occupation. The water continues its return. The light continues to illuminate without warming. The quiet continues to be full.

And the power, benevolent and wise, does the only thing left to do: it lets perfection be.

Not abandoned. Not forgotten. *Released* into its own completeness. It withdraws its hand not from indifference, but from devotion. It understands that to touch a finished thing is to unfinish it. That to bless what is already whole is to imply it lacked something. That the highest form of care is sometimes the discipline of stepping back, of holding your breath so the world can keep holding its own.

If you stand there now, you will not feel the power. You will only feel the cool cloth of the air, the precise weight of your bones, the soft tap of your pulse dying before it forms. You will watch a bead of moisture trace the same vein down the same stone, and you will not wait for it to reach the bottom. And if you are very quiet, you might notice something new: not a change, but a confirmation. A sense that this stillness is not alone in the cosmos. That somewhere, something vast and gentle has seen it, understood it, and chosen to love it by leaving it exactly as it is.

Which is, perhaps, what wisdom always does when it meets a world that has finished becoming.

It sits beside it. Matches its breath. And lets the silence answer for them both.
