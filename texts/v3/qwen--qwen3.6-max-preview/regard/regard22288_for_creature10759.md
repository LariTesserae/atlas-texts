The power does not descend. It does not need to. Wisdom does not require proximity to understand; it requires only the clarity to see what is already whole. It attends from the high, thin air above the canopy, from the deep, slow convection below the fracture, from the quiet intervals between the hissing steam and the grinding stone. It does not arrive as light, nor as voice, nor as hand. It arrives as attention. Pure, unbroken, and without demand.

It sees the fissure not as a wound to be closed, nor as an engine to be optimized, but as a finished thought written in rock, water, and green. It reads the logic without translation: gradient as law, turbulence as metabolism, fracture as invitation. It notes the absence of animals and does not mourn it. It notes the lack of rest and does not pity it. It understands that suffering requires a self to endure it, and here there is no self. Only response. Only flow. Only the continuous, unselfish work of becoming. The distributed organism does not know it is alive; it only knows how to run. And to the power, that is not a deficiency. It is a kind of grace.

What does a benevolent presence do when it encounters a system that needs nothing?

It does nothing that would alter the system.

It does not silence the steam. It does not soften the stone. It does not introduce pollinators to quiet the wind-driven spores, nor still the rockfalls to spare the moss, nor dampen the static to comfort the lichen. To do so would be violence dressed as mercy. Wisdom knows that benevolence is not always action. Sometimes it is restraint. Sometimes it is the fierce, quiet refusal to improve what is already complete in its own terms. The fissure does not ask for peace. It asks only for the conditions that allow it to keep processing. The power hears this, and obeys.

But it does not leave. Benevolence, when wise, does not abandon. It stays at the edges. It becomes a boundary condition.

It lays a subtle weight along the rim—not to crush, but to anchor. It adjusts the probability of external rupture: the distant glacier that might drain the deep aquifer, the human surveyor with core drills and extraction maps, the invasive spore carried on a warming wind, the tectonic sigh that might seal the fissure forever or tear it into a barren gorge. These are diverted, not by force, but by the gentle bending of likelihood. A storm shifts three ridges east. A fracture propagates along a different plane. A boot turns back at the treeline, uneasy without knowing why. The power does not stop change. It ensures the change remains *generative*. It keeps the mountain's stress within the narrow band that produces fracture without collapse, steam without explosion, turbulence without exhaustion. It guards the gradients. That is its only intervention.

If it leaves a trace, it is not a monument. It is a resonance.

A slow, deep hum woven into the bedrock, so faint it registers only as a slight lengthening of the interval between micro-slips, a subtle harmonization of the steam's whistle with the wind's bellows-pulse, a quiet reinforcement of the electrochemical seep that keeps the lichen mats conductive. It does not quiet the noise. It tunes it. The sparks still snap. The water still churns. The ferns still split and double. The moss still compresses and knits. But the system runs slightly longer, slightly deeper, slightly more surely than geology and chance alone would allow. The power's gift is not peace. It is duration. It is the quiet assurance that the moisture will keep transiting, that the violence will remain sustenance and not ruin, that the wound will stay open just long enough for the green to keep stitching it shut.

And it watches. Not with eyes, but with presence. It holds the fissure in a field of regard so steady that the act of witnessing becomes a kind of gravity. Not the gravity that pulls stone down, but the gravity that keeps a system from flying apart. The moss does not feel it. The ferns do not turn toward it. The mycelium does not route around it. It is outside the circuit, by design. To enter would be to become a variable, and variables alter equations. The power knows this. It stays in the negative space. It becomes the silence that contains the noise, the stillness that frames the motion, the unbroken attention that says, without words and without expectation: *You are enough. Keep running.*

There is no covenant here. No exchange. No gratitude owed, none given. The fissure does not change. It cannot. It is too busy being itself. Rock still calves. Steam still hisses. Mist still charges and sparks. Green still climbs, splits, calluses, doubles. The distributed organism still breathes through vents, feeds on electrochemical weep, reproduces through rockfall and wind shear, heals by binding fresh stone with root and rhizoid. It does not age; it turns over. It does not rest; it processes. It does not know it is held. It only knows how to run.

And this is the power's greatest wisdom: to love a thing exactly as it is, to protect it without possessing it, to ensure its continuation without demanding its recognition. Benevolence, here, is not salvation. It is stewardship of autonomy. Wisdom, here, is not correction. It is recognition of completeness. The power does not elevate the fissure to consciousness. It does not translate its noise into song. It does not ask it to mean anything beyond what it already means: pressure building, release hissing, stone grinding, water routing, green climbing, mist charging, sparks snapping, wind turning. A natural engine that does not know it is an engine, running on the simple, relentless logic of physics meeting biology in a confined space. The power does not interfere with that logic. It merely ensures the board remains level, the pieces remain in play, the game is not ended by a hand that does not understand the rules.

Stand in the fissure now, and you will notice nothing different. The air still bites your cheeks while steam warms your boots. Moss still yields, then crunches over fresh silica. A fern frond still brushes your shoulder, heavy with condensation, as a rock somewhere above splits with a sound like a breaking gear. The darkness is still saturated. Every surface still holds moisture, every crack still holds green, every pocket of air still holds motion. The place still does not ask to be understood. It still asks only to be witnessed as it runs.

But if you stay long enough, if you let your breathing fall into the rhythm of the drops and the hisses and the grinding stone, you might feel it: not a presence, exactly, but a permission. A quiet, unspoken assurance that this violent, damp, loudly breathing system is not an accident, not a temporary glitch in the mountain's flesh, but a valid way for the world to be. That it is allowed to continue. That its chaos is not a problem to be solved, but a process to be protected. That somewhere, in the high thin air and the deep dark stone, a benevolent and wise regard has drawn near, looked upon the fracture and the green and the endless turning, and chosen not to fix it, not to save it, not to change it at all.

Only to keep the edges from closing too soon.
Only to keep the gradients sharp.
Only to let it run.
