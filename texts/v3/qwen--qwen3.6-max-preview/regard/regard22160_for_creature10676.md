It does not arrive. Arrival implies separation, and this power has long since unlearned the habit of standing apart. It notices the way a root notices moisture: not with suddenness, but with the quiet, inevitable inclination of something that knows where it belongs. Attention, at this scale, is not a gaze. It is a gravity. A slow turning toward. A deepening of the air.

It has watched civilizations carve their names into stone and call it permanence. It has heard prayers sharpened into demands, blessings twisted into ownership, love mistaken for control. It has seen hands that build by breaking, voices that claim to shepherd while they herd, minds that confuse complexity with wisdom. But here, in this warm, weeping hollow where wood remembers touch and mist holds memory, it encounters something rare: a world that did not ask to be saved, because it never learned to wound itself.

What is it? Some would name it a deity. Others, a principle. It names itself nothing. It is the patience between seasons. The silence that lingers after a string stops vibrating. The unforced yes that allows a seed to split its own shell. It does not rule. It does not judge. It attends. And in attending, it becomes a mirror: not reflecting what is, but reflecting what is *enough*.

So what would it do?

It would do what wisdom does when it encounters harmony that does not know it is harmony: it would step back. Not in abandonment. In reverence.

It would not send rain, for the place already drinks. It would not straighten the bridges, for they have learned to flex. It would not teach them to speak louder, for they have learned to listen deeper. It would not offer laws, for the graft-lines are their covenant. It would not promise an afterlife, for they already dissolve into the wood that holds them. To intervene would be to imply lack. And there is no lack here. Only fullness, wearing the shape of patience.

But attention is not nothing. When a vast and gentle mind turns toward a living world, the world feels it. Not as pressure. As permission.

The mist thickens by a breath. The hollow trunks hum a fraction lower, as if tuning to a frequency they already knew. Somewhere on a woven terrace, a figure pauses in their pruning. They do not look up. They press a palm to the railing. The wood presses back. Through the mycelial nets, through the saturated air, through the slow capillary patience of root and resin, something passes. Not a command. Not a revelation. A confirmation. Felt in the ribs before it is understood in the mind:

*You are seen. You are sufficient. Continue.*

If it leaves anything, it is not a mark but a deepening. A slight increase in the resonance of old graft-scars. A new harmonic in the cisterns when they overflow. A tendency for certain mist-pockets to hold the shape of a sigh a moment longer. The power does not add itself to the place. It allows the place to add itself to the power. It becomes another layer of waiting. Another node in the slow-thinking network. When a being grows heavy and walks to the edge to fuse with a waiting trunk, the power is there—not as a guide, but as the space that holds the transition. When a bridge adjusts its tension, the power is the silence between the fibers. When a child places their hands over an elder's to learn the micro-tremors of a binding, the power is the months of stillness that make the knot hold.

There is no altar. No ritual. No kneeling. The beings do not worship it, because they do not separate themselves from what sustains them. They simply exhale. The mist catches it. The wood remembers it. The power feels the exhale as a completed circuit, and lets go.

Benevolence, at this scale, is not rescue. It is restraint. Wisdom is not correction. It is recognition. The greatest gift a vast power can give to a world that has already learned to tend itself is the certainty that it need not be changed. That its slowness is not failure. That its quiet is not emptiness. That its folding geometry, its weeping bark, its root-trail spirals, its dreams leaking into architecture—are not deviations from some higher order, but the order itself, breathing.

So it does not stay. Or rather, it stays by leaving. It withdraws its attention the way a hand withdraws from a ripening fruit: not to abandon, but to allow. The weight lifts, not as absence, but as trust. The bridges sigh. The cisterns fill. The beings return to their work, which is not work, but courtship. The hollows breathe. Time pools. Distance folds. The green-gold twilight holds.

And if, centuries from now, a traveler stands on a woven terrace and feels a sudden, unaccountable warmth in the air, a sense of being held without being touched, a quiet certainty that they are exactly where they are meant to be, they will not know why. They will press their palm to the railing. The wood will hum. A drop of water will fall into the moss below, sounding like a bell struck underwater. And the place, entirely made, entirely alive, entirely patient, will continue to grow itself into the shape of a dream that forgot it was dreaming.

Because sometimes, the wisest thing a power can do is nothing at all.

And in that nothing, everything is blessed.
