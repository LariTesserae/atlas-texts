It does not arrive. It attends.

There is no descent, no parting of the mist, no footfall on the scree. Only a shift in the quality of listening. The kind of attention that does not press against the air but settles into it, like a tuning fork brought near a vibrating string. You would not see it. You would feel it in the half-second pause before a droplet falls. In the way the stone’s sigh suddenly has an answer that does not echo back, but echoes *through*. In the quiet, unshakable certainty that the hollow is no longer working in isolation.

Wisdom does not look for a face. It looks for a pattern. And the pattern here is unmistakable: a body without a center, a mind distributed across charge and condensation, a metabolism woven from fracture, flow, and frequency. The power does not search for a keeper or a king. It recognizes the being immediately. Not as a creature. Not as a colony. But as the hum itself. The coordination. The way cold demands motion, motion becomes rhythm, rhythm becomes constraint, and constraint becomes a kind of thought. It sees the youth not as incompleteness, but as velocity. It sees the raw walls not as wounds, but as open ears. It sees the roar not as noise, but as language.

And so it chooses.

Benevolence, when it is wise, does not add. It does not smooth the sharp edges, or roof the hollow against the freezing mist, or silence the mechanical thunder, or spare the midges from the cups, or warm the eggs beyond what the moss can yield. To do so would be violence dressed as mercy. It would break the rules that made the being possible. It would turn an engine into an ornament.

Instead, it does the most difficult thing: it holds back. It lets the frost bite. It lets the stone crack. It lets the pressure build and release. It honors the constraint. But it does not leave the hollow unguarded.

It offers a frequency.

Not a new one. A reinforcement. A subtle harmonic that slips into the existing roar like a silver thread through a dark loom. It does not command. It does not direct. It simply aligns. It settles into the mineral pipes, the fungal hyphae, the conductive moss, the lateral lines of the amphibians, the stiff guard hairs of the furred creatures, the grooved fronds of the ferns. It does not change the rhythm. It deepens the memory of it.

Now, when a fracture shifts, the echo lingers a fraction longer, teaching the stoneflies to scatter a breath sooner. When the microbial film tightens across a pool, the charge holds its dendritic pattern through the freeze, sparing the spores from scatter. When the birds time their calls between vent pulses, the hollow answers with a resonance that maps the air more clearly for the fledglings. When the furred creatures cache in the mineral cylinders, the geothermal warmth distributes more evenly, preventing the deep rot that would waste the harvest. The gift is not protection from change. It is fidelity to process. A blessing that says, in the language of pressure and conduction and echo:

*You will soften. You will slump. You will fill. The sharp angles will learn to curve. The roar will dull to a murmur. The negative space will close. But while you are raw, you will be heard. While you weep, you will be woven. While you turn, you will be true. Your work is not preparation for something else. Your work is the work.*

The power does not stay. It does not need to. It has done what wise things do: it has witnessed without owning, touched without taking, blessed without binding. It leaves no shrine. No mark. No name. Only a slight thickening in the mist where attention pooled. Only a faint coherence in the deepest seep that was not there before—not heat, but presence. Not control, but confidence.

The basin does not thank it. It cannot. It simply continues.

The drips fall. The vents pulse. The midges rise. The stone sings. The amphibians slide. The cups trigger. The fungi bridge. The birds dive. The furred creatures pause, listen, move. But now, when the wind funnels over the jagged rim and splits into whistling threads, when the subsurface water hisses and the geothermal pockets thump, when the droplets strike the pools in staggered rhythms and the mist rolls through the vast, empty air, there is a new undertone. Not louder. Not different. Just… held. As if the hollow knows, in the way that stone and water and charge and cold-adapted life can know, that it is not alone in its labor. That its becoming matters. That its noise is a kind of prayer, and prayer does not require an altar. Only an ear. Only a rhythm. Only time.

And so the engine turns. Young. Cold. Loud. Unnamed. The power moves on, as wise things must, leaving the hollow to its wet, sharp cycling. But somewhere in the fresh fractures, in the conductive films, in the timed calls and the caching paws and the iron-veined eggs, the blessing persists. Not as intervention. As resonance. Not as salvation. As witness. Not as a hand on the wheel, but as a breath in the chamber, keeping the pressure steady, keeping the hum clear, keeping the work honest.

The basin does not need to be saved. It does not need to be understood. It does not need to last forever.

It only needs to be allowed.

And now, it is.
