The power does not descend. It does not part the vapor, nor brush aside the canopy, nor cast a shadow across the moss. It arrives as attention arrives: a quiet turning of regard, a softening of the vast toward the small. It draws near without crossing the rim. It simply notices.

And in noticing, it understands.

It reads the closed loop not as stagnation but as fulfillment. It traces the capillary seep, the fungal weave, the slow exchange of breath and stone, and feels the weight of air that has forgotten how to move. It recognizes the creatures not as dormant but as complete. It sees the pools that do not rise or shrink, the leaves that do not yellow or fall, the predators that rest beside prey without hunger, and it does not diagnose a flaw. It recognizes a miracle. This is not a world waiting to become. It is a world that has arrived.

A lesser kindness would try to help.

It would bring light to the dimness. It would stir the still air with wind. It would teach the hollow seasons, so that bloom might follow dormancy, so that loss might make room for gain, so that the creatures might know the sharp, beautiful ache of wanting. It would break the loop to let time flow again. It would call this salvation.

But wisdom is older than kindness. Wisdom knows that some gardens are not meant to turn. Some songs are not meant to resolve. Some equilibria are not problems to be solved but sanctuaries to be left alone. To introduce change here would not be a gift. It would be a violence. It would be the breaking of a vessel that has finally learned how to hold.

So the power does nothing.

And in doing nothing, it does everything.

It withdraws its hands. It stills its voice. It lets its vastness fold inward until it is no larger than a droplet hanging from a moss-choked ceiling. It does not bless the hollow with motion. It blesses it with restraint. It becomes a witness that does not disturb the water.

If it acts at all, it is this: it settles at the rim. Not as a sentinel posted. Not as a wall raised. But as a presence that chooses to rest. It breathes in time with the hollow. It lets the vapor bead on its own unseen edges. It adds nothing to the equation but the quiet weight of being known. And somehow, without shifting a single stone, without stirring a single spore, the hollow accepts it. The moss does not recoil. The creatures do not startle. The pools do not ripple. The equilibrium makes room, not by expanding, but by deepening. A new node joins the circuit. Not a conductor. Not a current. A silence that recognizes silence.

Through the mycelial weave, through the damp loam, through the slow press of root against stone, a faint acknowledgment passes. Not gratitude. Not submission. Simply the recognition of another weight that has chosen not to push. The power does not speak to them. It does not need to. It lets its regard sink into the saturated air like a stone into a pool, and the water closes over it without a sound.

This is what benevolence looks like when it has learned to be wise: it withholds. It watches. It loves a thing enough to let it be exactly what it is, even when the universe outside screams for growth, for progress, for the turning of wheels. It knows that completion is rarer than creation. That stillness is harder than motion. That a world which has finished wanting is not broken. It is whole.

And so the power offers the only gift that would not be a violation. It offers its attention, and then it offers its absence. It promises not to save them. Not to change them. Not to remember them in a way that would pull them forward into time. It promises only to hold the space around them. To keep the rim unbroken. To let the vapor thicken. To let the outside world forget this place a little longer. It becomes part of the boundary not by guarding it, but by refusing to cross it.

The droplet falls. The bird shifts its weight. The pool reflects the same dim ceiling. Nothing has changed. Nothing will.

But if you stand there now, and press your palm to the weeping stone, you might feel it: a faint, impossible resonance beneath the coolness. Not heat. Not movement. Just the quiet pressure of being known. A love that learned to be still. A wisdom that understood when to kneel.

The hollow breathes. The power breathes with it. And the world, for once, is allowed to be done.
