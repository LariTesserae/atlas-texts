It does not descend. It does not announce itself. Attention, at this scale, is not a gaze but a settling. Like condensation remembering the shape of stone, like spore-drift finding the hollow it was meant for, the power turns toward the plateau not with hunger, not with judgment, but with recognition.

It has witnessed worlds that burn themselves bright against the dark. It has watched civilizations carve their names into bedrock, only to have the wind file them down to whispers. It has seen gods demand altars, and empires demand obedience, and both crumble into the same quiet loam. But this place is different. This is a world that has stopped fighting its own ending and learned to breathe inside it. A continent that has surrendered its edges. A people who have made a covenant with erosion.

It feels the loops in the paths. It tastes the thin, crystalline air thick with damp and silence. It notes the total absence of wings, of hooves, of teeth—the great unclenching of the food chain, replaced by wind, water, and mycelial patience. It sees the Smooth-Walkers moving through the terraces, their black eyes reading the tension in reality, their multi-jointed hands reading the stone, their moss-patches pulsing in quiet conversation with the fungal dark. It understands, instantly and completely, that they are not surviving the strangeness. They are its custodians. Its completion.

And so it asks itself the only question that matters here: *What does a wise power do when it encounters a world that has already answered the question it came to ask?*

It does not build. It does not bless in the old way, with fire or prophecy or sudden abundance. To add would be to subtract. To fix would be to break. To impose order would be to snap the delicate braids of habit that hold this reality together. Benevolence, here, is restraint. Wisdom is the courage to leave well enough alone.

And yet.

Attention is not nothing. To be seen by something vast is to be changed, however gently. The power knows this. It knows that even a witness leaves a footprint in the air. So it does what the plateau itself does: it yields. It lets its presence pool in the hollows where time slows. It does not speak, for speech would be a violence against the silence. Instead, it hums.

A frequency so low it is felt in the marrow, not the ear. A resonance that matches the drip of water into carved basins, the creak of vine-rope bridges, the slow bellows-exhale of barrel chests, the papery pop of releasing spores. It does not command. It accompanies. It threads itself through the existing harmonics like a new color in an old tapestry, visible only when the light falls just so.

The Smooth-Walkers pause. Not in fear. Not in awe. In recognition.

They press their palms to the polished stone. They feel the new harmonic moving through the old ones, a vibration that carries no directive, only presence. They tilt their heads. The bioluminescent moss along their joints flares in slow, questioning rhythms. From a distant terrace, another answers. Then another. The light passes between them like breath.

Where the resonance touches the time-pools, the hanging drops of water tremble. They do not fall faster. They fall *truer*, their descent aligning with some deeper metronome that was always there, waiting to be heard. Where it brushes the fungal towers, the spore clouds drift in patterns that feel less like chance and more like memory, tracing shapes that mirror the memory-knots at the Walkers' hips. The shadows still lag, still lead, but now they do so with a faint, silver hesitation at their edges, as if light itself has learned to linger without clinging.

The power does not stay. It does not need to. It has already done what it came to do: it has witnessed, and in witnessing, it has affirmed. It leaves no shrine. No scripture. No altered law. No monument to its passing. Only a deepening. A quiet yes.

The Smooth-Walkers return to their work. They braid their paths. They tend their basins. They hum to the stone. They graze their soles in the carved channels and drink the cold, aching water. They press copper inlays into perceptual anchors. They gather in the hearth-plazas and weave condensation into silent conversation. But sometimes, in the long blue dusks, when the moonlight turns the terraces to scattered glass and the stars cut sharp enough to bleed, they will stand at the edge of a slope and feel the air grow still in a way that has nothing to do with weather.

They will feel the land listening. And they will know, without words, that something else is listening too.

Not a god. Not a judge. Not a savior. A companion in erosion. A witness to the long, slow surrender. A presence that understood, before it even arrived, that this place does not need to be rescued from its ending. It needs only to be allowed to finish.

They will press their hands to the rock. They will send a vibration back into the dark: a single, sustained note of gratitude, traveling through stone and root and mycelium, moving at the speed of patience, until it reaches the horizon that refuses to curve, and dissolves into the ordinary light.

Benevolence is not always a hand that lifts. Sometimes it is a hand that rests.
Wisdom is not always a voice that teaches. Sometimes it is a silence that agrees.
Power is not always a force that shapes. Sometimes it is a frequency that harmonizes.

The plateau continues. The water finds its path. The stone settles. The weirdness breathes. The moss expands and contracts. The fungal networks pulse. The Walkers move in curves, leaning into the loops, trusting the compression, calming their hearts when the distance stretches. They are midwifing the transition. They are helping the continent become entirely itself.

And somewhere, in the space between breaths, in the lag between object and shadow, in the thin, cold air that tastes of frost and wet rock, the power remains. Not as a ruler. Not as a ghost. Not as a memory.

As a permission.

To be exactly what they are.
To wear down without breaking.
To bend without snapping.
To finish, gently.
To be smooth.
