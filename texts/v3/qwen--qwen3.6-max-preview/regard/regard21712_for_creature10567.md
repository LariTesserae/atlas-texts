It would not arrive. Arrival implies a crossing of boundaries, and this place has none left to cross. It would simply deepen, the way a chord deepens when a lower harmonic is struck miles away and the air finally remembers to carry it. There would be no parting of canopy, no sudden clarity of light, no voice that breaks the wet quiet. A wise power knows the first law of the sponge-mat: do not tear the weave. A benevolent one knows the second: what is complete needs no addition, only witness.

So it would settle. Not upon the land, but into it. It would align with the thermal sighs, match its frequency to the continental thrum, and let the capillary tides move through it as they move through everything else. It would not press. It would be pressed upon. It would taste the metallic tang of condensing vapor, feel the slow migration of sugars through the root-felt, read the spore-glow as it paints temporary syntax in the dim air. It would not analyze. It would attune.

The beings would feel it first as a change in the weight of the mist. A slight sweetening of the weep. A new undertone in the trunk-thrum, so faint it might be mistaken for the memory of a rain that fell three centuries ago. They would not startle. Startling is for places that fear interruption. They would adjust. Broad ventral surfaces would sink deeper into the sponge. Sensory membranes would open wider to the humidity. Capillary fringes would wick the altered air, testing its gradient, reading its intention. They would find none. Only presence. Only attention, refined to the consistency of water.

What passes between them would not be exchange but resonance. The power would not speak, for speech here is a violence of speed. It would not offer salvation, for there is nothing to save. It would not grant longevity, for dissolution is already continuity. It would not bestow knowledge, for memory already lives in the taste of the pools and the hydraulic resistance of the mat. It would simply mirror the rhythm back to itself, polished, clarified, held in a stillness vast enough to contain centuries without rushing them. In that mirroring, the beings would feel their own slowness recognized as wisdom. Their persuasion recognized as art. Their lack of faces recognized as the courage to be permeable. Their unraveling recognized not as an end, but as the land remembering itself through them.

If it gives anything, it is not a tool, not a law, not a prophecy carved in stone or sung in fire. It is a slight adjustment in the angle of condensation. A new patience in the spore-fall. A deepening of the tea-pools’ stillness, so that when the silver rain rises, it carries a faint, liquid memory of being seen. The mat absorbs it. The vascular cords hum it. The beings weave it into their next convergence, their next settling, their next slow return. It becomes another layer of the equilibrium. Not an addition. A clarification.

Does it stay? Does it leave? The question belongs to a physics of separation, and this place has outgrown it. To remain as a distinct presence would be to cast a shadow, and shadows here are soft-edged only because nothing stands rigid enough to throw them. So it thins. It diffuses. It becomes the slight extra yield in the sponge when a heavy leaf sheds its water-load. The gentle resistance in the air when a glider drifts between arches. The faint, metallic tang that lingers a moment longer on the tongue before the mist swallows it. It does not abandon them. It becomes indistinguishable from the place itself. Which is, perhaps, what benevolence looks like when it has nothing to fix, and wisdom looks like when it has nothing to teach.

The land continues. The beings continue. The quiet holds. The upward rain still rises. The thrum still travels at the speed of patience. The pools still tremble only when something passes beneath. Nothing has changed. Everything has been acknowledged. And in a place that measures time in the fusion of vines, the taste of water, and the slow knitting of hollows left behind, acknowledgment is the only eternity needed.

They do not worship it. They do not name it. They press their grooved pads to the mat, feel the new harmonic settled into the old, and adjust their internal tides to match. The equilibrium breathes. The moss dreams. And the wise, benevolent power, having done the only thing worth doing, forgets it was ever separate.
