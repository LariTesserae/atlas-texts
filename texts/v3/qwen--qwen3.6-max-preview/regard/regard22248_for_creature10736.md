It does not descend. It does not break the rim or part the mist or cast a shadow across the terraces. It arrives as a change in the pressure, a slight thickening of the damp that does not chill but settles, like a hand resting lightly on a breathing chest. The wind's drone does not falter; it deepens, as if the stone itself has drawn a longer breath. The power does not announce itself. It has crossed distances that have no name to find a place that does not call out, and now it does what wisdom demands when it encounters something already whole: it listens.

It waits for the hollow to speak first.

It hears the amphibians threading their trills between drips. It feels the spiders reading wingbeats through oiled silk. It tracks the gliders waiting for the drone to shift, the predators moving where the echo predicts, the people humming low tones to test the air. It does not measure this against some external standard of flourishing. It does not look for lack. It recognizes the architecture of balance: how every life has learned to leave space, how every motion respects the return, how the emptiness at the center is not a void but a chamber that holds the whole. It sees that nothing here is struggling to become something else. Everything is already fluent. Everything is already singing in key.

A lesser power would bless by adding. It would pour warmth into the chill, still the wind, raise permanent walls, teach the people to command the echo rather than converse with it. It would call this elevation. It would call this mercy. But this power is wise, and wisdom knows that to add to a complete system is to break it. To impose is to deafen. To save is often to overwrite.

So it does not give. It attunes.

It lowers itself into the frequency of the place, not as a ruler, but as a root. It becomes a subtle harmonic in the stone's memory, a vibration so deep it does not disturb the surface but steadies the foundations. It does not stop the slow weathering; it ensures the basalt weeps at the same pace. It does not silence the predators; it keeps the acoustics true so the prey can hear them coming. It does not grant permanence; it deepens the cycles so that return feels like recognition, not repetition. It wraps itself around the hollow's equilibrium like a second atmosphere, invisible, weightless, present only in the way things hold.

The people notice first as a change in the return.

A hum thrown against the curved wall comes back slightly fuller. Not louder. Not altered. Just clearer, as if the air has learned to hold the shape of the sound a fraction longer before releasing it. The water in the collection basins tastes no different, but it settles faster, the surface tension holding a touch tighter, wasting less when the bowl is tilted. The gliders find the thermals more predictable, not because the wind has shifted, but because the hollow's resonance has stabilized, making the invisible currents easier to read. The predators do not grow weaker. The prey do not grow faster. The balance does not tip. It deepens. Even the damp feels more intentional, the condensation veils falling with a rhythm that matches the evening drone without effort. Nothing is new. Everything is more itself.

The power does not take a name. It does not demand witness. It does not appear in dreams or carve signs into the limestone or leave objects that do not belong. It remains as a sustained note beneath the wind, a patient presence that has chosen to become part of the meter. When the people sing to test the air, it answers not with a voice, but with a perfection of the echo, a return so clean it feels like recognition. When a child places a palm on wet moss for the first time and feels the cool pulse of the terrace, the power is there in the steadiness of the stone. When an elder oils a wooden bowl for the last time and leaves the shelter door open to the mist, the power is there in the quiet certainty that the hollow will keep what is given. It does not protect by isolating. It protects by belonging.

It has learned what the inhabitants already know: that benevolence is not always intervention. That wisdom is often restraint. That to love a place is not to change it, but to learn its rhythm and step into it without breaking the measure. It does not hover above the terraces. It sinks into them. It becomes the slight buoyancy in the thin air that eases a long descent. It becomes the clarity in the seepage that carries memory without weight. It becomes the way the echo never frays, even when the wind turns sharp, even when the cloud rolls heavy, even when the hollow is left to itself for months at a time. It does not command the ecosystem. It converses with it. It leaves space for the stone to answer.

If you sit on the upper terrace now, breathing until your ribs match the slow expansion of the damp air, you will feel it not as a presence above or beside you, but as a depth beneath the sound. The wind's drone, the water's drip, the calls and wingbeats and rustling, the half-whispered phrases that wait for the basalt to reply: they do not change. They simply hold. The power has done what benevolence and wisdom always do when they encounter something already complete. It has stepped into the rhythm. It has lowered its weight into the stone. It has chosen to listen forever.

The basin continues its work. Weeping. Echoing. Breathing. Alive.

And now, held.
