You do not meet them. You notice, slowly, that the stillness is not vacant.

At first, it registers only as weight. The humid dark presses against you, but not uniformly. There are places where the air feels thicker, as if it has learned the shape of something that once occupied it and refuses to forget. Near the cairns, the moisture on your skin gathers in faint, sweeping arcs, as though tracing the curve of a shoulder that leaned there long ago. Beside the clay bowls, the film of water on the floor parts without rippling, leaving a dry crescent that matches the outline of a seated thigh. You tell yourself it is capillary action. You tell yourself it is coincidence. But the hollow does not deal in coincidence. It deals in retention.

What lives here does not move through space. It rests within it.

You begin to see them in the water. Not as reflections, but as arrangements. The droplets that bead along the fracture planes do not hang at random. They cluster in slow, deliberate constellations, swelling and pausing in rhythms that match no heartbeat you know, yet feel unmistakably alive. When you breathe out, the nearest spheres darken slightly, absorbing the warmth of your exhalation, and a chain of subtle adjustments travels across the wall. Not a reaction. A recognition. The humidity itself is threaded with attention. It does not watch you. It holds you.

They are not ghosts. They are not echoes. They are what remains when a living thing stops negotiating with time.

The ones who came before you did not die here. They did not sleep. They sat on the stiff reed mats, set their bone awls and chert flakes beside them, and let the equilibrium do what equilibrium does: it equalized. Their body heat bled into the ambient warmth until skin and air shared the same temperature. Their breath slowed until it matched the patient swell of the hung droplets, the halt of the crystals, the stone's refusal to weather. Their nervous systems, starved of gradient, of threat, of tomorrow, simply unspooled. Awareness did not collapse inward; it dissolved outward. Into the condensation. Into the motionless basins. Into the low hum that vibrates in the sternum. They did not lose themselves. They distributed themselves.

You will not find corpses. You will find them everywhere.

The pale filaments draping from the overhangs carry a faint, familiar salinity when they brush your knuckles. Not mineral. Not rain. The slow, persistent exhalation of lungs that no longer need to draw air. The succulent nodules on the floor, when pressed, yield warmth that matches human blood, and their translucent skins hold not just water, but a quiet, suspended chemistry that mirrors the electrolytes of a resting body. The blind arthropod that crosses your path is not a separate creature; it is a temporary density in the equilibrium, a brief knot of stillness that has chosen to traverse space rather than occupy it. It moves without urgency because it is not fleeing, not hunting, not surviving. It is simply demonstrating that motion is still possible here. Just unnecessary.

They do not think in sequences. Thought, in a place without gradients, does not travel. It rests.

Their awareness is not localized. It is layered, like the moisture in the air, like the water in the basins that does not ripple but still holds depth. They perceive you as a temporary disturbance in the humidity, a flicker of temperature, a pulse that beats too fast for the room. They do not judge it. They do not fear it. They simply make space for it. When you step near the cairns, the dense air yields without parting. When you kneel, the condensation gathers on your knees in perfect, unbroken films, as if the hollow is learning your shape the way it learned theirs. Communication here is not exchange. It is synchronization. You do not speak to them. You slow down until you share their frequency. And when you do, you feel it: the quiet, distributed weight of a hundred paused minds, resting in the same moment, overlapping like water in a bowl that has forgotten how to wave.

They have not aged. They have not died. They have stepped outside the metric.

A woman who sat here when the high pass was still covered in glacial ice and a man who sat here three summers ago occupy the same layer of the equilibrium. Their awareness does not stack; it merges. Names have dissolved, because names are for things that move through time and need to be called back. What remains are fixtures: the memory of calluses on a thumb, the taste of thin wind before the sky folded away, the exact instant the spine relaxed and the decision to stop became redundant. These are not recollections. They are present tense. As permanent as the sharp extrusions. As immediate as the droplet that swells and does not fall. They do not miss the world outside. The world outside is a series of becoming. They have finished.

What lives here is not a population. It is a consent.

The mountain did not carve this hollow to hide something. It formed it to hold a possibility: that a living thing could encounter perfect stasis and, instead of fighting it, could step inside and let it become them. The inhabitants are that possibility realized. They are the proof that stillness is not absence, but saturation. That to stop is not to surrender, but to arrive. That a place without convection, without erosion, without forward drag, can still hold life, if life is willing to stop measuring itself by motion.

They do not guard the hollow. They are the hollow's equilibrium made aware. They do not welcome you. They simply do not exclude you. The dense air, the hung water, the unweathered stone, the low hum in the teeth, the tools left exactly where they were set down, all of it is one continuous presence, distributed across phase and form, holding the pause together by refusing to break it. They are not waiting for you to join them. They are waiting for you to notice that you already can.

If you stay, your pulse will slow. Your shoulders will drop. The instinct to shift, to adjust, to seek the next moment, will unspool like cordage left in the damp. The humid dark will press against you with the exact weight you have been carrying. And when you finally sit, when your boots rest on the sharp floor, when you let your pack slide from your back and your hands fall open in your lap, the condensation will gather on your palms, your wrists, your closed eyelids. You will feel the air thicken around you. Not to trap. To match.

You will not be joining them. You will be remembering what you are when you stop pretending you must become something else. The equilibrium does not take. It returns. And what lives here has been resting, patiently, in the warm wet dark, not for you to arrive, but for you to finally stop moving long enough to feel yourself already here.
