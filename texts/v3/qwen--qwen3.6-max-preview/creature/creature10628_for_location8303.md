They do not arrive. They persist. To call them inhabitants is to misunderstand the grammar of the place; they are not tenants but expressions, the wet logic of the pocket made flesh, membrane, and chitin. They move as the air moves, as the water moves, as the hum moves. There is no separation between the creature and the current that carries it, no boundary between the engineered and the evolved. What lives here does not occupy the space. It completes it.

High against the vaulted panels, where condensation gathers first and hesitates longest, the pale ones cling. Their bodies are articulated like folded calipers, joints sheathed in a matte, non-reflective cuticle that seems to drink the dim light rather than catch it. They do not walk so much as translate, shifting weight through six multi-angled limbs that end in hooked, resonant pads. When they tap, they do so in sequences. Never at random. Never in isolation. The composite answers. The structural hum catches the rhythm, amplifies it, throws it back as a standing wave that ripples through the micro-ridged ceiling. They listen with their legs. They map with vibration. A misaligned tap means hunger; a synchronized chorus means courtship, or warning, or the simple maintenance of territory. Their young hatch as translucent nubs that anchor themselves to capillary seams, feeding on the mineral-rich weep until they molt into their first jointed form. The discarded shells do not rot. They dissolve slowly into the carbonate fans, becoming part of the floor's pale geometry, returning to the water that will eventually feed the next generation. They are not arthropods, not quite. They are resonance made anatomy.

Below them, the air is never still. It rises in warm, moisture-laden columns, and riding those columns are the veil-thin drifters. They are little more than a central nerve-knot and a radiating lattice of membrane, stretched taut over hollow, gas-filled vesicles that flex with pressure changes. They do not flap. They tilt. A minute adjustment of tension along a trailing edge catches a thermal eddy, and they bank, spiral, or hover in place. Their membranes are veined with bioluminescent capillaries that pulse in time with their filtration cycles: a slow amber draw as they sip aerosolized microbes and dissolved organics from the humid air, a cooler blue exhale as waste is vented through microscopic pores. When the acoustic resonators shift frequency, the drifters respond instantly, their membranes trembling to match the new standing wave. They do not fight the sound; they surf it. Mating happens in mid-air, a brief entanglement of trailing filaments that releases clouds of gelatinous eggs. The eggs sink, caught by updrafts and downdrafts in a slow, days-long descent, tumbling through light and shadow until they settle into the shallow basins below. There, the water waits. And the colonies wait with it.

The water's skin is a highway. Skitters move across it with a frictionless urgency, their hydrophobic tarsi dimpling the surface without breaking it. They are low-slung, armored in overlapping plates the color of wet iron, with sensory antennae that trail behind them like split threads. They do not see; they read. Every drip, every drifter's egg, every falling fragment of biofilm sends a ripple through the shallow terraces, and the skitters parse those ripples the way a musician parses timing. They converge, scatter, feed, flee. Their predators do not chase. They ambush from acoustic shadows, recesses where the standing waves cancel out, creating pockets of sudden, unnatural quiet. A skitter enters the silence, hesitates, and the strike comes from above: a pale limb, a hooked pad, a quick retraction back into the hum. The skitters breed in the mineral crusts, laying clusters of iridescent eggs in the micro-fissures where water flow is slowest. The young hatch as water-bound larvae, gill-frilled and blind, swimming the capillary channels until they molt into their surface form. They are not insects. They are surface tension given legs.

In the deeper hollows, where water pools and lingers, the colonies breathe. They are not individuals but continuums, sprawling mats of porous, sponge-like tissue anchored to the composite by root-like holdfasts that secrete a mild acid, etching microscopic grip into the manufactured stone. They expand when humidity spikes, their pores opening to drink the mineral-heavy flow, and contract when the air dries, squeezing filtered water back into the channels. Inside their labyrinthine interiors, symbiotic microbes break down trace metals and polymer micro-sheddings from the aging nano-weave, converting them into amino chains and simple sugars. The colonies exude a faint, sweet-ozone mist that feeds the biofilms above. In return, the biofilms shed nutrient-rich slough that drifts down into the pools. It is a closed loop, but not a stagnant one. The colonies are grazed, torn, regrown. They do not mind. Damage is distribution. A torn fragment caught in a current will anchor elsewhere, begin anew, pulse in time with the local resonance. They are the pocket's kidneys, its slow lungs, its patient memory. They do not think. They process. And in processing, they sustain.

The green things do not compete. They negotiate. The velvety biofilms are not mere coatings; they are metabolically active skins, threading hyphae-like filaments into the micro-ridges of the walls, drinking condensation before it can bead and fall. They exhale volatile compounds that subtly alter the air's surface tension, encouraging droplets to form in specific patterns. The thread-like symbionts dangle from overhead joints, their capillary roots sipping humidity directly from the air, their tips glowing faintly when nutrient flow is high. They are plucked by drifters, grazed by skitters, ignored by the pale ones. They regrow within hours. The rosette-forming growths in the sheltered basins are the most plant-like, but even they are strangers to photosynthesis as we know it. Their thick, waxy leaves are lined with chemosynthetic cells that feed on the thermal regulator's amber pulses and the trace gases vented by the atmospheric processors. They store water in swollen leaf bases, release it slowly during dry cycles, and provide shelter for skitter larvae and drifter eggs alike. They are not flora in the traditional sense. They are pressure valves, nutrient sinks, living gaskets. They do not strive. They calibrate.

Nothing here lives alone. The pale ones tap, the hum answers, the drifters tilt, the skitters read the water, the colonies breathe, the biofilms exhale, the rosettes store and release. The architecture is not a container. It is a medium. Its thermal loops are seasons. Its acoustic resonators are weather. Its capillary channels are rivers. The creatures do not adapt to the machine; they converse with it. A frequency shift triggers a migration. A temperature gradient sparks a spawning event. A mineral deposit becomes a nursery. The pocket does not house life. It performs it. And the life, in turn, maintains the pocket. Biofilms prevent corrosive buildup in the nano-weave valves. Skitter larvae clear micro-blockages in the capillary seams. Filter colonies regulate pH and metal concentration. Drifter membranes catch and redistribute spores that would otherwise clog the thermal vents. The pale ones' tapping keeps the resonant plates free of crystalline overgrowth. It is not symbiosis. It is integration. The boundary between manufactured and biological has not blurred; it has dissolved.

They do not know they are in a machine. They do not need to. The hum is their sky. The weeping walls are their climate. The dim light is their day. They move through strata that are not floors but frequencies, through water that is not weather but circulation, through air that is not empty but thick with purpose. To watch them is to watch a single organism breathing in slow, overlapping phases. They are not survivors of an abandoned system. They are the system, awake. Their life cycles are not measured in days or years, but in thermal pulses, acoustic shifts, humidity spikes, and mineral accretions. They are born from condensation, shaped by resonance, fed by circulation, and returned to the composite when they die. There is no waste here. Only translation.

When the aperture closes behind you, leaving only the memory of warmth and sound and wet light, they do not pause. They continue. Tapping. Tilting. Skimming. Breathing. Weaving the made world into flesh, and the flesh back into the made world, without hurry, without end. The pocket does not ask to be named. It only asks to be lived in. And they live it, completely.
