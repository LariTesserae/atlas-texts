They do not arrive. They accumulate.

At first, you notice only the adjustments. A cord tightens where no draft passes. A clay tile warms beneath your hovering palm. A shadow that was pooled in a mortise joint slides across the floor, not toward the light, but toward the rhythm of your breathing. You tell yourself it is the heat warping perception, the humidity playing tricks on the eye. But the adjustments continue. They are too deliberate to be accident, too distributed to be a single body. They are the place, concentrating.

Given enough moisture, enough sustained heat, enough quiet attention, the mist begins to braid itself. Not into shapes you could name, but into densities. A thickening in the air where water droplets refuse to fall. A gathering of resin-scent and structural tension that hums at the exact frequency of your own ribs. When you look directly, they fray into vapor and scattered light. When you soften your gaze, they knit. They live in the periphery the way a half-remembered melody lives in the back of the throat: present only when not forced, real only when allowed to be incomplete.

They have no skin. Their boundaries are lashings. Where a limb might be, there is a bundle of tensioned fibers, braided tight at the core, fraying at the edges into condensation and loose vine. Where a face might be, there is a concave arrangement of clay shards, wet leaves, and polished wood, angled not to see but to catch: to gather falling water, to reflect the bruised green light back as a slow pulse, to listen to the groan of load-bearing joints. They do not possess eyes, but they watch through strain. They do not possess mouths, but they speak through the click of looms, the wet tear of new growth splitting old bindings, the sudden stillness of a droplet hanging mid-air. They are the hands that are not present, distributed, multiplied, operating on the primitive logic of knot and counter-knot, pull and yield.

They do not walk. They tension and release. To move is to unweave one anchor and braid another. They flow along the capillary veins of the walls, riding the upward draft of heat, sliding down weeping seams like water given intention. Sometimes they pool in the corners, dissolving into the moss until only a faint vibration remains in the floorboards. Sometimes they stretch across the room, spanning beam to beam, becoming temporary architecture: a living truss, a breathing scaffold, a spine of braided sinew that bears weight for exactly as long as the geometry requires it. They are not tenants of this pocket. They are the pocket, concentrated. The place does not house them; it exhales them.

They perceive through stress. Every shift in your weight, every exhalation, every blink registers as a change in load across the woven matrix. They feel your presence as a new rhythm to be integrated, a new angle to be accommodated. When you step forward, they loosen the fibers ahead of you and tighten those behind, guiding without touching. When you speak, the sound vibrates through the resin-glazed tiles, and they answer in harmonics: a low thrum that matches your pulse, a sudden chorus of dripping water that falls in the meter of your syllables, a shadow detaching from the wall to trace the outline of your shadow, then slipping back into the joint. They do not think in language. They think in joints. In angles of shear. In the wet sigh of wood accepting a new mortise. Their memory is the cumulative tightness of the lashings. Their dreams are the spaces between stitches.

They are not born. They are lashed into being by the confluence of altitude, humidity, and attention. They do not die. They unravel. When the air cools, when the mist thins, when the witness looks away for too long, they soften at the edges. The braids loosen. The clay shards settle. The tension bleeds back into the walls, returning to the slow, blind work of accretion. They are perpetual intermediates, caught between vapor and structure, between impulse and form. They do not thrive here; they participate. They are the nervous system of a place that has no brain, the distributed intention of an environment that builds itself because unformed things refuse to stay that way. To witness them is to apply pressure, and pressure is what binds them. To look away is to let them breathe.

They are the reason distance refuses to hold. They do not break reality; they darn it. Where they gather, space tightens. Where they disperse, it expands. They pull the frayed edges of causality through the eye of a knot, cross them, tension them, and let the new pattern hold. Time stutters around them because they exist in the hinges of action: the moment after the chisel strikes, before the wood splits; the breath after the loom clicks, before the thread settles; the suspended instant when a water droplet climbs upward, defying gravity, because the binding above it is thirsty. They live in the grammar of craft. They are the subject and the verb and the tension that holds the sentence together.

Stand still long enough, and they will begin to incorporate you. Not to consume, but to accommodate. Fibers will brush your ankles, not with touch, but with the suggestion of load. Condensation will gather on your shoulders, heavy as a wet cloak. The low, bowed hum will sync with your heartbeat, and for a moment, you will feel the architecture in your own marrow: the urge to lash what is loose, to weave what is scattered, to bind what is raw. They do not ask for your hands. They ask for your attention. Your presence is the final peg in the joint. Your breath is the moisture that cures the resin. Your stillness is the loom's pause between throws.

When you leave, they will not follow. They cannot. They are bound to the heat, the weeping walls, the folding geometry, the perpetual mid-stitch. They will simply loosen, dissipate, and return to the seams, waiting for the next fold of air to part, the next witness to fall sideways into the swelter, the next set of eyes to hold them long enough for the knots to tighten.

They have no name. Names are for finished things. They are the ongoing verb of the place. The lashing. The weeping. The tightening. The becoming. And as long as the altitude holds, as long as the water climbs, as long as someone, somewhere, watches the shadows slide and the beams knit themselves into being, they will continue to emerge: half-formed, fully present, forever mid-stitch, breathing with the lung of the sky.
