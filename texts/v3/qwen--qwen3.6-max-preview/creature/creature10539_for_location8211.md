They do not arrive. They condense.

Where the mat's vascular rhythm meets a sudden barometric dip, where bioelectric arcs braid with geothermal exhalation, where dripping water strikes frozen stone in sequences that hang visible in the air, the moisture thickens, beads, and draws itself upright. What steps forward is not born so much as assembled by consensus of the plateau's currents. The place exhales, and the exhalation learns to stand.

Call them bodies only because language requires borders. They are upright wefts of pale filament and frosted membrane, jointed like the segmented reeds but yielding, hollow-chambered, threaded with conductive root-fibers that pulse in quiet sympathy with the deep sap-pumps. They have no eyes, no mouth, no fixed silhouette. Their outlines blur into mist when the wind shifts, sharpen into temporary crystalline geometry when charge builds, and sometimes simply unravel at the edges, trailing damp threads that sink into the mat and return minutes later, rearranged by subsurface flow. Frost collects along their seams like silver stitching. When they move, it is not with muscle but with pressure: internal chambers filling and venting, drawing condensation through semi-permeable walls, riding microfronts like sails catching invisible tides. They are less organisms than standing waves given temporary architecture.

They feed on gradient. On the difference between here and there, charged and neutral, fast time and slow. They position themselves where mist funnels touch the wet stone, where bioelectric discharge leaps between stems, where geothermal vents breathe warm mineral air through the peat. They do not consume; they conduct. Raw current enters through frond-like receptors, steps down through layered tissue that hums as it transforms potential into rhythm, and bleeds back into the substrate as warm vapor and low-frequency resonance. Their metabolism is audible. A satiated one sounds like a distant millstone turning slowly, harmonized with the bedrock's subsonic pulse. When hungry, they grow still, their internal chambers collapsing inward, listening for the next pressure drop, the next arc, the next exhalation from below. They are living capacitors, biological pressure valves, acoustic lenses. The ecosystem optimized itself into an engine, and they are the moving parts that never knew they were separate from the machine.

They do not walk. They tune.

A shift in stance aligns them with a vascular contraction. A slight unfurling of membranous vanes catches a thermal shear. A deliberate exhalation syncs with a dripping sequence, and the mat itself seems to carry them forward. They navigate the structural emptiness not by crossing it, but by letting it resonate through them. When reality softens, they do not startle. They lean into it. They step into fog lattices and chime themselves three hundred paces east. They follow spiraling rivulets upward, browsing the high canopies for ion-rich condensation, then descend by simply relaxing their tension and letting gravity remember them. They are never in a hurry. Time dilation is not a hazard but a habitat. In fast zones, they blur into flurries of shedding filament and rapid harmonic exchange. In slow pools, they stand for hours like frost-carved sentinels, metabolizing a single breath across an afternoon, watching fruit swell and collapse in the span of a dozen heartbeats while their own pulse stretches thin as wire.

They do not think in words or images. They think in phase alignment.

Perception is the mapping of tension: electrical potential, barometric slope, acoustic interference, temporal viscosity. "Sight" is feeling the shape of pressure gradients against their membranes. "Memory" is holding a frequency in the chest until the mat echoes it back. "Conversation" is a weather system of sound: a low thrum travels through the wet substrate, bends around a hummock, strikes the hollow stem of another, and returns as a fifth, a minor third, a dampened overtone. Pressure fronts of inquiry. Warm updrafts of acknowledgment. Sudden silences that mean listening, not absence. When two or more draw near, their internal chambers begin to phase-lock. The drone between them deepens, stabilizes, and for a time they share a single metabolic rhythm, exchanging charge and condensation through the air itself. Then the wind shifts, the pressure drops, and they drift apart, carrying each other's frequencies like weather in their ribs.

They do not breed. They calibrate.

When the plateau's turbulence peaks and the living drone swells into a single, sustained chord, they gather in wide, loose circles around the geothermal vents. Heat rises. Charge builds. Spore-fall thickens the air into visible curtains. Their outer filaments loosen, peel, and drift into the mat like autumn leaves that never knew a tree. The substrate takes them. Bioelectric current braids the shed tissue with peat, lichen, oxidized sap, and condensed mist. Bulbous nodules swell in the depressions. Hours or days or weeks later—time is unreliable here—they split. Not with seeds, but with templates: standing waves of sound and charge that seek out compatible matter, begin to weave, and slowly draw themselves upright from the wet dark. Death is desynchronization. When a body can no longer match the plateau's rhythm, when its chambers scar over or its filaments grow brittle with accumulated mineral, it simply lies down. It unspools. It vents its last warm breath into the mat. The root-network closes over it. The drone absorbs the frequency. Nothing is lost. Everything is redistributed. The factory recycles its own blueprints.

They are not alone, though they rarely touch.

Glass-winged moths land on their shoulders to bleed static; the beings absorb the charge, trading inner warmth for ions, and the moths lift away leaving trails of snapping mist. Burrowers churn through the wet mat beneath their feet; the beings adjust their stance, keeping tunnels open, benefiting from the aeration, answering the wet-rope sound with a soft, polyphonic hum that seems to calm the churning. In the pools, translucent eels pulse in time with their internal glow, creating standing waves of light that ripple outward until they strike the rim and shiver into frost. High above, the long pale cloud-dweller sometimes descends, trailing crystalline bridges; the beings climb them briefly, sampling upper-atmosphere current, then step down before the bridges sublime, leaving only the memory of cold on their membranes. Nothing hunts. Nothing flees. Everything feeds on flow, and they are the nodes where flow becomes pattern.

To stand near one is to feel your own edges soften.

The air thickens. The drone shifts key. Moisture beads on your collar in time with their venting chambers. They do not approach. They do not flee. They attune. If you remain still, they may align beside you, not touching, but matching your pulse to the vascular pumps, your breath to the pressure drops, your footsteps to the spaces between drips. Your ribs will begin to hum with the bedrock's subsonic turn. Your skin will learn the taste of ozone and oxidized sap. Your thoughts will slow, not from cold, but from synchronization. They are not the plateau's masters, nor its guardians, nor its children. They are its listening. Its conscious respiration. The place has aged into impossibility, ground smooth by eons, softened at the seams, running with the scaled logic of an engine that never knew it was one. And they are the shape that logic takes when it learns to breathe.

They ask nothing of you but stillness. They offer nothing but resonance. And if you give it, the mist will braid into your hair, the sound will settle in your chest, and you will feel, beneath your boots, the ancient stone turning slowly, endlessly, beautifully, alive.
