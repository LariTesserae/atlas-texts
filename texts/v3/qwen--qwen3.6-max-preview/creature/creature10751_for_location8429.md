You do not see them first. You feel the place adjusting.

A braided rope tightens by a fraction. A woven baffle in a light-well shifts, and the warm rectangle on the tiles narrows, then widens, then settles. The tapping begins. Not random. Not urgent. Phased. It travels through the laminated beams like a pulse moving through a resting body, echoing off ceramic eyelets, dissolving into the resin-bound floor, returning altered. It is not a call. It is a calibration.

Along the grout lines, you notice the wear. Not abrasion, but polish. Thumb-sized depressions smooth the edges of carved wooden cleats. The hexagonal tiles bear faint concentric rings where weight has rested, distributed evenly, never shifting abruptly. In the suspended dust, there are gaps: clean, sweeping arcs where something moved without disturbing the spiral, reading the air currents rather than pushing through them. Where a vine's shadow lingers after the frond has shifted, the dust is slightly thicker, as if something passed through the afterimage and left a trace of static in its wake.

When one finally crosses your sightline, it does not step into view so much as unfold from the geometry.

It is low to the plane of the tiles, not crouched but built for it. Its outline is layered, echoing the terraces above: overlapping plates of cured membrane and thin-fired clay, bound by tensile cords that run beneath a surface of dry, fibrous fur. The plates are not armor. They are joints, laminated like the dark oil-cured beams, flexing along grain-lines that follow the body's long, deliberate curves. There is no head as you would recognize it. Instead, a forward array of slender, horn-tipped probes rests against the floor or the nearest beam. Each probe ends in a polished stone bead, worn smooth by decades of contact. They tap. The structure answers.

It breathes, but not with lungs that heave. Along its flanks, gill-like slits open and close in a rhythm that matches the thermal sigh of the tiles. Inside, you glimpse the faint copper gleam of filamentous tissues, dry and furred like the mineral vines overhead, exchanging heat and trace minerals rather than moisture. Its circulation is resin-thick, moving through capillaries that mirror the amber binder between tiles, carrying crystalline sugars and suspended ceramic dust. When it moves, it does not walk. It flows through tension. Limbs articulate, find purchase on a tile edge or a wooden cleat, and draw the body forward by redistributing load through the braided cords beneath its skin. There is no impact. No scuff. Only the soft complaint of fibers taking strain, the click of a stone bead testing a surface, the minute shift of a joint accommodating an angle that should not exist.

It does not see the courtyard as a space to cross. It reads it as an instrument to tune.

The probes map thermal gradients, structural resonance, and the slow drift of folded geometry. When it approaches a corner that bends past ninety degrees, it does not hesitate. Its body adjusts mid-stride, plates sliding, cords rebalancing, joints exploiting the extra play carved into every mortise and tenon. It navigates by vibration and memory stored in tension. The tapping it sends through the beams returns altered, carrying information about rope slack, tile shift, light-well alignment, the presence of others. It listens to the architecture the way a blind musician listens to a room: by feel, by echo, by the way silence changes shape.

There is more than one. You realize this not by sight, but by rhythm.

The tapping is never solitary. It phases, overlaps, resolves. Somewhere above, a cluster-bird answers with a rapid staccato against a hollow beam. Below, a burrowing grub's ceramic secretion shifts the resonance of a seam. The courtyard is a network, and they are its nodes. They do not gather. They distribute themselves according to need. One rests near a light-well, adjusting a woven baffle with slow, deliberate pulls, testing the drift of incense smoke against its probes. Another traces a resin capillary, pressing a warm, fibrous palm against a living arch to encourage fusion, feeling the slow seep of nutrients through dry photochemistry. A third lies in a sun-pool, plates angled to catch the diffused glow, metabolizing light through the same crystalline processes that sustain the waxy leaves. They do not speak. They resonate. Their conversation is the maintenance of equilibrium: a continuous, patient dialogue of tension and release, tap and response, shift and settle.

They age, but not as you understand it.

Their plates darken from ochre to rust to verdigris, not from decay but from slow mineral exchange with the air and the grout. Their cords thicken, then thin, then are gradually replaced by new growth woven from harvested fiber and cured resin. They do not die so much as unravel. When the rhythm slows beyond recovery, the body is laid against a tile seam. Grubs trace the resin lines. Mycelial filaments drink the remaining crystalline sugars. The plates fracture along their laminations and are worked into the grout by burrowing symbiotes. The cords are harvested, re-twisted, soaked, and cured over slow fires by younger hands. Nothing is lost. Everything is rearranged. They are not builders. They are tenders. The courtyard was not made by them alone; it was made by the logic they embody, passed down through rhythm, not record. A juvenile learns by resting its probes against the beams while an elder taps out the seasonal calibration. It learns by feeling the ropes tighten as the walls contract. It learns by tracing the thumb-smoothed lips of clay, by matching its own slow breath to the thermal expansion of the floor. Knowledge is not stored. It is performed. Every adjustment is a lesson. Every tap is a lineage.

Do they think? Not in words. Not in images. They think in tension, in resonance, in the slow accumulation of correct angles. Their awareness is distributed across the structure, extended through the vines that fuse to their plates, the grubs that polish their resting places, the light-wells they tune by feel. They perceive time as viscosity. A falling leaf's spiral is not slow to them; it is legible. A drop of resin beading over hours is not delayed; it is a sentence being written. They do not rush because rushing would tear the stitch. They move at the speed the geometry allows.

When you stand still long enough, one may approach.

It will not look at you. It will rest a probe against the tile near your foot, tap once, and listen to how your presence alters the resonance. It will feel the heat you radiate, the moisture you exhale, the foreign, hurried rhythm of your pulse. It will not fear you. It will simply adjust. A baffle shifts. A rope loosens. The air current redirects, carrying your damp breath away from the dry leaves. The shadows near you bleed slightly into the grout, accommodating the disruption. The courtyard absorbs you, not out of hospitality, but out of habit. Everything here is woven to hold contradiction without breaking.

They are the place, and the place is them. Not metaphorically. Structurally. Their bodies are laminated like the beams, tensile like the ropes, glazed like the tiles. Their metabolism runs on dry photochemistry and crystalline sugars. Their perception is vibration and thermal gradient. Their society is a distributed calibration. Their history is written in rope slack and resin cure and the slow fusion of vine to ceramic. They do not conquer space; they fold it. They do not measure time; they inhabit its thickness. They do not build; they maintain the stitch.

And in their stillness, in their patient tapping, in their seamless adjustment to angles that defy geometry, they reveal the truth of the sunken court: it is not a shelter. It is a body. And they are its nerves, its tendons, its slow, dry breath, keeping the weave tight, season after season, until the sharp edges remain sharp, the shadows learn to linger, and the silence becomes a language you can almost understand.
