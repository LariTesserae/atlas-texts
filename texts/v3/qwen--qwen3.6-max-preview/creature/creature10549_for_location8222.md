No eyes. Sight is useless where shadows lag and light bends around hesitation. Instead, a crown of slender, jointed sensilla traces the brow, the shoulders, the spine, trembling like tuning forks. They read pressure gradients, thermal drift, acoustic interference. They feel the shape of the air before it moves. They move in polyrhythms, matching the basin's cascade and shatter, placing each foot where the sound says the ground will be, not where it appears. To watch them cross a shelf is to watch a musician play an instrument that is also the floor, the air, the water, the bones.

They do not speak. They interfere.

A sustained hum from the throat sac bends the roaring cascade into a temporary arch of mist. The cairns are not markers. They are dampeners. Each stone is placed to catch a specific overtone, to bleed excess vibration from a ledge that threatens to split, to anchor a temporary node of calm in a place that refuses it. The grooves are not carvings. They are wear-patterns from circling, from bodies moving in slow, deliberate orbits until the basalt remembers the path and begins to hum it back. The petrified platforms are not floors. They are resonant diaphragms, stretched across fractures, tuned to translate the raw tectonic roar into something the newly unfolded can withstand without unraveling. They do not build to endure. They tune to participate. When a shelf shifts and a cairn tilts, they do not mourn. They listen for the new harmonic and begin again. Arrangement here is not architecture. It is acoustics made visible.

They are not a single species. The boundary between organism, mineral, and sound is too thin for such categories. What you perceive as "them" is merely the most complex temporary coherence in a continuum that begins in the inverted domes. When the colloidal water braids upward and pools, certain frequencies cause suspended minerals, organic films, and acoustic pressure to precipitate into soft, pulsing nodules. If the thrum holds, the nodule splits. A new body unfolds, damp and trembling, already humming the basin's root note. It is not born. It coheres.

They do not age. They attenuate.

Over what passes for years in a place where time eddies and pools, their membranes grow thin. Their hollow chambers lose pressure. Their harmonics drift out of phase with the roar. The dissonance becomes a physical ache, a tearing at the seams of their form. When it can no longer be borne, they walk to the deepest suspension, lie back, and let the water take them. Their bodies do not rot. They dissolve into the colloidal hesitation, their frequencies scattering into the standing waves, their grooves echoing in the stone until the next nodule forms. The blind eel-things that drift through the suspension are not separate creatures; they are attenuated forms that never fully cohered, or cohered forms that shed their complexity to ride the bass currents. The gliders above are not a different taxon; they are membrane and hollow bone that caught an updraft instead of a standing wave, riding the turbulence until fatigue returns them to the mist. The rubbery lichens are not plants; they are acoustic filters, externalized resonance organs that pulse in sympathy with chest hums, bleeding high frequencies before they tear delicate tissue. Nothing here is truly distinct. Everything is a phase of the same song.

They do not call this place home. They call it the Throat.

The seams show because the song is loud enough to stretch the world, and they have learned to slip through the gaps when the harmony demands it.
