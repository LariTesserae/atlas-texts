You do not see them first. You feel the air adjust.

Not a draft. Not a displacement. A subtle reweighting of the coolness, as though the damp has found a new surface to rest upon, or a shadow has remembered how to hold its shape. A bead of condensation pauses mid-runnel on a fresh rock face. It does not climb. It does not fall. It simply acknowledges a presence that does not disturb the stone. They are here before you notice them, and they remain after you look away.

When they step into the open, they do not arrive. They resolve.

Or perhaps they were always arranged, and your eyes are only learning the pattern. Their silhouettes echo the horizontal patience of the plateau: low, broad, layered like the overlapping slate roofs, shouldered like the terraced benches. Their surfaces carry the color of wet stone without the slickness, the texture of felted moss without the yield. Silver-veined fibers or fronds or hair drape in deliberate falls, catching the clear light without reflecting it, drinking illumination the way the shrubs drink damp. Limbs are jointed with the same clean angles as the dry-fit walls. Movement is not a crossing of space but a completion of it. When they walk, the soft tap of sole on flagstone dies before it can echo. They leave no impression. They displace no air. They are heavy without weight, present without pressure.

They breathe, but not to draw in.

Their chests rise and fall in the rhythm of cisterns filling, of moss drinking, of dew returning to itself. Exhalation does not warm the air; it joins it. Stand beside one and feel your own pulse hammer against the quiet, while theirs is a slow, deep resonance that matches the tension in the braided bridges, a harmonic you feel in your molars rather than hear. Their eyes are open, always open, the color of still water over low-fired clay. They do not blink. They do not need to. The world does not change, so there is nothing to miss. When they turn their heads, it is not to follow motion but to verify an angle. When two stand near each other, they do not face one another. They align. Shoulders match the slope of a retaining wall. Hands rest where tools rest. Fingers trace the V-joints of flagstones not to read them, but to confirm they are still true.

They do not think in sequences. Thought here is not a current but a basin.

Awareness pools, fills, rests. Memory is not stored; it is embedded. Every gesture is a repetition of the first gesture, which was also the last. They do not remember raising the city. They are the city remembering itself. The hands that shaped the stone are these hands. Not preserved. Not aged. Unchanged because change was never required. Calluses are not scars of labor but topography, mapped to the grip of wooden mallets, the pull of braided fiber, the exact weight of slate. Thin bands of cold-hammered copper rest at their wrists, their throats, their brows: stress points, yes, but also conduits. The patina does not spread because oxidation is a process, and processes have ended. They wear the craft as skin. They carry the tension of bridges in their sinews, the capillary pull of aqueducts in their veins, the right angles of plazas in their posture. They do not maintain the settlement. They are its equilibrium given stance.

The silver hares do not startle at their approach. The ash-grey birds do not alter their glide. The long-legged grazers pass through the spaces between them as though passing through cool air, though there is only form and patience. Predators watch from high ledges with the same unhurried regard they turn on the stone itself. The inhabitants do not hunt. They do not gather. They do not store. They occupy. They are the pause between the drip and the pool, the breath between the step and the flagstone, the green that holds its height without striving. When they sit, they do not rest. They complete a terrace. When they stand, they do not wait. They verify a sightline. Their stillness is not the absence of motion but motion resolved. Every possibility has been spent. Every need has been met. Every question has been answered by the geometry.

If you approach, they do not turn. If you speak, your words fall at your feet and stay. If you reach out, your fingers meet coolness that does not yield, does not resist, simply exists at the boundary of your warmth. They do not judge your restlessness. They do not pity your pulse. They have no concept of intrusion, only of addition. You are a ripple that does not spread, a note that does not harmonize, a shadow that lengthens when none should. They allow it. The city allows it. Equilibrium is not fragile; it is absolute. It can hold your trembling without breaking. It can hold your aging without noticing. It will hold your leaving as easily as it held your arrival.

They are not a people. They are not spirits. They are not echoes of builders who walked away.

They are what remains when becoming stops. The finished thought. The settled dust. The water that has found its level and refuses to seek lower ground. They are the hands that laid the last stone and never lifted them again, because lifting would imply something left to do. They are the breath of the plateau, the damp in the moss, the tension in the fiber, the green that does not compete. They live here because living here requires no effort, only presence. They are distributed without being divided, singular without being alone. A bridge cable hums; a spine answers. A cistern fills; a chest rises. A shadow falls true; a gaze confirms it. They do not inhabit the terraces. They are the terraces, attentive.

When you finally understand them, you do not name them. You simply stop moving.

You let your breath slow until it no longer fights the cool cloth of the air. You feel the precise weight of your own bones. You notice how the light falls, clear and unfiltered, illuminating without warming. You watch a bead of moisture trace the same vein down the same stone, and you do not wait for it to reach the bottom. You let your pulse become a quiet thing, a soft tap that dies before it forms. And in that settling, you touch the edge of what they are: not dwellers in a finished world, but the finished world, awake. Holding its green and its stone and its quiet life in perfect, unchanging balance. Asking nothing. Offering nothing. Simply being, completely, until even your need to understand dissolves into the stillness that has already understood itself.
