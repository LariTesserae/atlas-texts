They do not hatch. They do not sprout. They precipitate.

You notice them first as a thickening in the fog, a local increase in the steam’s opacity that resolves into rhythm before it resolves into outline. They are not a population in any sense that implies separation. They are a continuous phase transition wearing temporary architecture. To count them is to misunderstand the place; they are the wet, silent breath of the basin itself, exhaled into motion, inhaled back into stone.

The glass-scaled skaters are the most immediate. They move across the tension-films of the weeping pools not by pushing against the water, but by riding the microscopic topography of heat. Their legs end in hydrophobic cilia that distribute weight across a thousand menisci, leaving the surface unbroken, unrippled. When they feed, they do not chew. They press a ventral plate against a mineral crust, and the crust sublimates directly into their carapace through capillary heat-exchange, leaving behind a shallow negative that fills with condensation before you can blink. Their eyes are not optical organs but faceted prisms. They do not see light; they fracture it. The lagging daylight enters, splits, and returns to them as spectra of temperature, pressure, and the braided echoes of events that have not yet occurred. They do not flee. When a predator folds toward them, they simply step sideways into a thermal gradient and unravel into mist, reforming three shelves higher where the steam is thicker, already moving as if they had never paused.

Above them, the aerial swimmers drift on rivers of rising vapor that are invisible to you but solid to them. Their bodies are lattices of hydrated silica and flexible chitin, ribbed like blown glass, hollow enough to be buoyant, dense enough to hold shape. They have no lungs. Their frilled gills harvest the air’s microscopic geometry: ionized droplets, silicate spores, the faint static charge that accumulates where hot basalt meets cold fog. They swim through humidity the way fish swim through current, undulating on updrafts, turning on thermal eddies. You will watch one approach a ledge, and its shadow will arrive first, pooling on the rock a half-second before the body follows. When they feed, they open their mouths to the rising air and let the current sort what is needed from what is not. What passes through them leaves no waste, only a slightly cooler, drier thread of atmosphere that sinks, condenses, and becomes dew on a lower shelf. Consumption here is not theft. It is weather.

The obsidian-shelled clusters are the basin’s slow thoughts. They do not move as individuals but as density waves, hundreds of jointed bodies locking and unlocking in sequences that resemble breathing, or computation, or the slow turning of a geode. They huddle on warm ledges not for shelter, but to equalize internal pressure against the vent’s pulse. When they molt, they do not leave corpses. They shed crystalline husks that dissolve into the weeping rock within minutes, and in dissolving, they broadcast thermal and chemical signatures that the others read as barometric shifts, as warnings, as invitations. Predation among them is silent because it is not violence. It is redistribution. A larger form folds over a smaller, not to crush, but to share a thermal vein, to balance phase states, to exchange momentum. The consumed does not die. It unspools into vapor, rises, condenses on a higher facet, and wakes as something else. Memory is not stored in tissue. It is stored in the arrangement of droplets, the angle of a crystal, the lingering warmth on a basalt plane. To remember is to rehydrate. To forget is to evaporate.

Their tools are not crafted. They are borrowed from the moment’s geometry. A hooked limb pries a fresh obsidian flake from a seam. A sieve is woven from shed chitin and thermal-spun silica threads, stretched across a pool, used to strain motes from the weep, then released. The updraft unravels it. The flake slips back into the water. There is no hoarding because possession is impossible where matter refuses to stay in one state. Their intelligence is not cumulative. It is resonant. They do not learn; they attune. A pattern forms in the steam—a spiral, a lattice, a branching fork—and the swarm moves through it, not because they planned it, but because the heat drafted it and their bodies recognized the shape as true. When the pattern dissolves, the knowledge dissolves with it, returning to the fog to be drawn again when the conditions align. This is not primitivism. It is elegance stripped of permanence. A grammar of use and release.

They experience time as you experience texture. Cause and effect are not a line but a braid, and they move through it without friction. You will watch a creature leap toward a crevice, then see the crevice weep a droplet that catches the light, and only then understand that the droplet’s formation was the reason for the leap. They do not anticipate. They remember forward. Their life cycles mirror this: they molt into earlier instars not as regression but as recalibration, shedding accumulated phase to return to a state of higher potential. Sometimes they split along thermal seams, two shadows diverging to hunt different gradients, moving through separate pockets of the basin, only to merge again at a vent, pooling their experiences like water joining a lens. There is no individual death. There is only the slow, soundless turning of the wheel: solid to liquid to vapor to heat to motion to solid again. The ecology does not sustain itself. It is the sustainment.

Do not touch the weeping walls. The warning is not superstition. It is thermodynamics. The glass is not inert. It is a recording medium, a slow memory of every shape that has pressed against it. The inhabitants themselves may be the mountain’s forgotten impressions, given momentum by the vents, walking the shelves as temporary answers to questions the stone asked before the ridge cooled. If you press your palm to the wet obsidian, the heat will draw the moisture from your skin, the silica will map your ridges, and the place will begin to dream you. Not as a statue. Not as a copy. As a phase. A condensation pattern. A thermal ghost that skates the films and swims the updrafts, repeating your gestures in a language of steam and sharp edges until the mountain seals and the dream is folded back into the rock. This is why they leave no tools. This is why they shed no permanent traces. They know, in whatever way a lattice of silica and heat can know, that to leave a mark is to ask the stone to remember you forever.

They are not waiting for you. They are not waiting for anything. They are the basin breathing itself into temporary coherence, a wet, silent metabolism that has no need for roots, or wood, or fire, or names. They move through the lagging light, through the upward-falling rain, through the braided seconds, and they do not wonder what they are. They are what happens when heat meets water meets unweathered stone, when reality stretches thin enough to let the grammar of phase change speak aloud. Stay long enough, and you will stop seeing creatures. You will see the mountain thinking. You will see the fog remembering. You will see yourself, not yet arrived, already leaving, caught in the slow, soundless tide of a world that has not yet decided to be real, and has no need to.
