They do not arrive; they accumulate. You notice them the way you notice the moss: first as a darkening against the steel, then as a rhythm beneath the mist, then as a presence that has always been there, waiting for your eyes to adjust.

Their bodies are built for the thinness. Chests are broad and slow-cycling, ribs flared to draw oxygen from air that tastes of wet iron and crushed quartz. Lungs have learned to work in long, deliberate draws, holding the damp coolness until it yields what it can, then releasing it in plumes that hang and drift rather than dissipate. Their skin carries the mountain's palette: weathered to slate and oxidized copper, crosshatched with fine lines that catch condensation like lichen catches dew. Knuckles are thick, palms mapped with callus and mineral stain, fingers long and accustomed to feeling rather than gripping. They do not startle. They do not flinch. Sudden movement is a luxury for thicker air, for ground that trusts its own shape. Here, every gesture is measured, every shift of weight deliberate, as if the body is constantly negotiating with a world that might stretch, compress, or tilt beneath it.

What they wear is less clothing than architecture. Layers of felted grazer-wool, oiled canvas, and woven moss-fiber cling to the frame, darkened by years of suspended moisture until they match the wet basalt and riveted plate. Brass buckles, ceramic toggles, and braided cord hold it all together, but the true fastenings are acoustic. Small resonant disks hang from belts, are stitched into collars, are wrapped in leather around wrists and ankles. They are tuned to the local frequency of stable space. When a fold begins to open, when gravity loosens its suggestion, when time begins to pool or thin, the disks vibrate. The warning is felt in the marrow before it is heard in the ear. Their boots are broad-soled, studded with soft iron that grips without sparking, leaving prints that fill with condensation before the next walker arrives. Tools hang from harnesses not for speed, but for balance: weighted plummets, spool-tangles of braided wire, glass vials stoppered with wax, all swinging in slow, predictable arcs that help the body read the air.

They perceive differently. Not with sharper senses, but with patient ones. They have learned to watch for the slight warping of light that precedes a spatial compression, to read the way fern fronds curl away from a gravity eddy, to listen for the subsonic groan of settling strata beneath the thrum of turbines. They do not look straight ahead; they look through. Distance is not a measurement here; it is a texture. They navigate by harmonic resonance, by the way sound bends around a fold, by the faint warmth bleeding through a moss-draped conduit, by the rhythm of winches turning three plateaus away. Time does not flow for them; it accumulates. They measure it in condensation cycles, in the slow advance of lichen across a newly poured platform, in the number of cable notches between shifts, in the viscosity of temporal drips caught in graduated glass. A conversation might span hours, punctuated by long silences that are not empty but necessary: space for the air to carry the words, for the mind to adjust to a thinning moment, for the mountain to exhale. They do not interrupt. They overlap. Like the cable-bridges. Like the moss over steel. Like the harmonics of the reality-locks.

Their labor is accretion. They do not build; they add. They do not fix; they adjust. A worker on a cliff face does not drive a bolt home in three strikes; they seat it over twenty, pausing between each to let the rock settle, to feel the vibration travel through the stone, to ensure the fracture network accepts the intrusion. They harvest anomalies the way lowlanders harvest grain. Temporal drips are caught, labeled not by hour but by thickness, traded to gear-wrights who know which machines need time slowed and which need it quickened. Spatial folds are mapped with chalk and copper wire, then gently persuaded into insulation pockets around steam conduits, where the compressed distance keeps heat from bleeding into the mist. Gravity suggestions are not corrected; they are ridden. Loads are lifted not by brute force but by positioning them where the air grows light, then guiding them upward with braided lines and patient hands. They do not fight the softness of reality. They have made peace with it, the way one makes peace with weather. A wall that phases into rock is not a failure; it is a conversation. A clock that runs backward is not broken; it is remembering. They teach their young to press palms against trembling stone, to feel the mountain deciding, to wait for the decision to settle before laying the next plate.

They are scattered, but not isolated. Distance here is deceptive, and they have learned to trust sound over sight. A call across a ravine might take minutes to arrive, bent by a fold, dampened by mist, but it arrives true. They answer in kind, their voices trained to the thin air: low, resonant, vowel-heavy, carrying without strain. Communities cluster around reality-locks, not for safety alone, but for the steady harmonic that binds them. They gather in open-sided pavilions where mist rolls through unchecked, sharing meals of slow-cured meats, mineral-rich tubers pulled from grit-beds, and tea brewed from condensate and ironwort. They do not celebrate speed or completion. Their gatherings mark stabilizations: a plateau that has stopped shifting, a cable-bridge that has found its equilibrium, a child who has learned to read the glass-bending silence. They name each other not by lineage but by resonance: by the frequency they hum when the air grows thin, by the way their footsteps sync with the winch cycles, by the anomalies they tend, by the patience they carry.

They do not fear the unfinished. They inhabit it. Their bodies bear the marks of the place: joints that ache in rhythm with settling strata, eyes adapted to perpetual twilight, ears tuned to the harmonic lock, lungs that exhale plumes that linger like questions. They carry the damp in their sinews, the thin air in their blood, the slow time in their bones. They know that to force a straight line is to invite a fracture. They know that to demand permanence is to miss the point. They are not conquerors. They are tenants, stewards, collaborators. Another layer of accretion. Another strand in the living mesh that binds rock to metal, fracture to foundation, breath to vapor.

If you stand among them long enough, you stop seeing them as separate from the city, from the mountain, from the mist. Their patience is not passive; it is structural. It holds the platforms steady. It keeps the cables from snapping. It allows the reality-locks to hum without straining. They are the ones who listen when the rock rings. Who catch the temporal drips. Who map the folds. Who walk the causeways without hurrying, who leave room for the land to breathe, who understand that unfinished does not mean broken. It means alive. It means becoming.

And they are becoming with it. Slowly. Deliberately. One breath, one bolt, one harmonic at a time.
