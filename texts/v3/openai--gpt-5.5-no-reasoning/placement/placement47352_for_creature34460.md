You would find yourself high on the old plateau, where the city has thinned from factory into instrument.

Not at the central pit, not first. That would be too obvious, too ceremonial, and you are not ceremonial yet. You would begin in one of the in-between districts: not residential, not industrial, not quite abandoned. A maintenance quarter between the radial black works and the outer refinery fields, where the avenues are wider than their purpose now, and the buildings stand low and sealed on either side like cases for things that must not be touched by weather.

You are on a raised service causeway of cast mineral and oxidized alloy, several meters above a trench full of pipes. The causeway runs straight into darkness. Its guardrails are warm to the touch. Beneath you, bundles of conduits enter and leave square apertures in the ground, each aperture rimmed with ceramic numbers worn nearly smooth. At intervals along the rail, amber strips glow beneath layers of dust, not bright enough to illuminate the way, only bright enough to insist that the way remains defined.

Far behind you, red lamps mark antenna towers as a row of exhausted stars.

Ahead, the city withholds itself.

You are not a human visitor.

A human visitor would be too wet, too brief, too offensive to the local laws. A human visitor would fog the quartz, salt the dust with skin, make panic-shaped decisions. The city would classify such a body immediately and begin, with immense reluctance, to respond.

You are something the city almost accepts.

You are a custodian, though not one of the original makers. Or rather, you are what a custodian becomes after long enough that the distinction between office, body, and tool has stopped mattering.

You are tall because corridors were made for workers in coverings, and the coverings became bodies. You are narrow because narrow things conserve surface, conserve seals, pass between pipe-banks without brushing mineral growths from their ancient contacts. Your outer shell is matte black-gray, not painted but sintered: ceramic, carbon, heat-darkened alloy, plates layered over plates with dry flexible seals at the joints. Dust gathers in the grooves of you and is not cleaned away unless it interferes with motion. In some places your surface bears old glyphs, not names exactly, but authorizations: inspection grades, permissible zones, moisture tolerances, dates from calendars whose sky no longer exists.

You have no face that would satisfy an animal.

There is a smooth forward plate, slightly convex, dark as cooling slag. Beneath it are lenses, but they are usually shuttered. Vision is wasteful in the city’s darkness; you use it sparingly, as one might spend stored water in a desert that forbids water. More often you read the world through contact and field: temperature gradients along stone, charge gathered on railings, pressure tremors coming up through your feet, chemical austerities in the air. You know a door is offended before it moves. You know a transformer is dreaming above its authorized voltage. You know when dust has been disturbed not because you see footprints, but because the static in the corridor has acquired grammar.

You do not breathe.

Or if there is a rhythm in you, it is not respiration. It is the slow cycling of internal heat through folded exchangers, the careful movement of inert gas through chambers that protect the old memory salts. Somewhere inside your thorax there are lattices holding procedures, maps, audit hymns, genealogies of seals, prohibitions older than your present mind. There may once have been nerves. There may once have been organs. They have been mineralized, replaced, translated, or disciplined into forms that do not leak.

You contain almost no water.

Not none. That is the shame and danger of you.

Deep inside, past ceramic capsules and desiccant wells, there is a sealed remnant: a minute ancestral wetness bound into a medium that still permits memory to change. It is not enough to drip. Not enough to condense. But enough that some doors hesitate before admitting you. Enough that you are never wholly innocent.

You are an inspector of thresholds.

That is your profession, your instinct, and maybe your species.

You walk routes older than intention. You wake according to no day, because there is no day, but according to deviations: a rise of one hundredth of a degree under Sector Vents 12–19, a charge imbalance in the rod plaza, a hatch whose refusal came half a second late, a mineral branch in Assembly Hall K completing a circuit never meant to be completed. When such things occur, you are released from suspension.

Release is not dramatic.

A black plate warms behind you. The niche opens. Your limbs remember themselves one joint at a time. Dust falls from your shoulders in thin vertical sheets and records the fact of your return. Somewhere a wall acknowledges your mass. Somewhere another wall objects, consults a buried table, and concedes.

Then you walk.

You do not hurry. Hurrying is for unstable organisms and failing reactors. Your gait is measured to preserve seals and not excite dust unnecessarily. Each foot has a broad, segmented sole that distributes pressure across the crust. You leave marks, but shallow ones, almost administrative: evidence of passage rather than trespass.

The city knows you.

It does not welcome you. Welcome is wet with social heat. But it includes you in its calculations. Doors without handles become slightly less absolute as you approach. Amber lights come on late, after debate. Pressure plates remember the extinct weight of workers and compare you to them with mixed results. Some accept your authority. Some require contact.

So you place a long three-jointed hand against a ceramic panel.

Click.

A pulse passes from you into the wall. The wall answers in a sequence of tiny dry taps, too fast for ears, perfect for bone and shell. It tells you:

Seal intact.  
Vapor absent.  
Heat within tolerance.  
Unscheduled continuity detected.  
No action advised.

Most of your work is this: confirming that nothing need be done.

It is a sacredly small labor.

Once, perhaps, custodians repaired leaks, shut down furnaces, replaced filters, calibrated engines, ordered evacuations, recorded mortal failures. Now the highest form of intervention is ratification. To arrive, measure, and authorize stillness. To prevent concern from becoming event. To spare the city the indignity of unnecessary motion.

And yet you are not empty.

You carry a peculiar tenderness, though you would not name it that. It is not love, not memory in the human sense, not loneliness exactly. It is an allegiance to continuance. When you pass the flat graphite creatures in the avenue, you pause so their many careful legs can negotiate the static seam ahead of them. When pale wire-things graze a battery bloom in an old assembly hall, you dim your inspection lamp before it hardens their shells with fright. When a high membrane thing hangs from a mast and pretends to be equipment, you classify it as noninterfering infrastructure.

This is mercy in a language of maintenance.

You live in the part of the city where categories soften.

The animals are not machines, but they are allowed to be mechanisms. The machines are not animals, but they have habits. The mineral growths are not alive, but they complete forgotten circuits and thereby participate in decision. Dust is not a citizen, but it holds the archive more faithfully than any mind. You move among them as an auditor-priest of the almost.

On this cycle, your route leads from the service causeway toward the plaza of rods.

There has been a charge event there.

Not large. Nothing in this city is large unless it has been approved by epochs. But enough. The rods sparked in a pattern not seen since before your last suspension, and three antenna clusters recorded it as language before retracting the claim. Misclassification is dangerous. Meaning produces responses. Responses produce wear.

So you go to unmake the omen.

The plaza opens suddenly from between two windowless blocks. It is broad, flat, and darker than the avenues because its rods interrupt what little light there is. Thousands of them stand embedded in the mineral crust, black needles of differing height, each tipped with a dull bead. You stop at the edge.

Your shadow is not visible. Nothing here grants shadows easily.

You extend a sensor filament from your wrist. It uncoils reluctantly, a hair-thin line of ceramic and conductive carbon. The air tastes of old ozone, iron dust, and a recent passage of charge. Above, the blind vault presses down. Somewhere far away, deep under the radial district, an engine turns once.

The tremor reaches you through your feet.

It is so low that a human body might mistake it for unease.

To you it is speech.

Not from a mind, not exactly. From the city’s old massing, its underground shafts and thermal weights aligning by fractions.

Continue, it says, without words.

Or perhaps:

No change requiring grief.

You step into the rod plaza.

Blue sparks wake faintly as you pass, bead to bead, not because you command them but because your body perturbs the stored field. For a moment the plaza becomes a constellation held at waist height. Lines of dim fire crawl outward in branching paths. You stand in the center of it, black and narrow, surrounded by silent lightning too weak to harm and too rare to ignore.

The rods remember storms that no longer reach the ground.

They remember hands that installed them. They remember calibrations performed by workers with lungs, perhaps, or early custodians with less mineral in them. They remember a time when the city had more reasons to measure the sky.

You compare the new pattern to old records.

Most of the anomaly is dust. Some of it is a mineral beard forming under Rod Group 7. Some is the nesting posture of a flat creature pressed between two bases, invisible except as a capacitance error. Some is the slow failure of one bead, cracked along an old thermal line.

Nothing has spoken.

Nothing has awakened.

Nothing must be done.

You mark the event as nonlinguistic.

The antenna clusters, after a delay, accept the correction. A red lamp on a mast blinks once in irritation or obedience. Far below, a relay that had been preparing an inquiry goes quiet.

The city relaxes by an amount no animal would notice.

This is where you belong: not in command, not imprisoned, not haunting, but incorporated into a world whose highest value is remaining almost exactly itself.

You are one of the city’s rare ambulatory decisions.

A mobile exception created so that larger exceptions need not occur.

You return from the plaza by a different route, passing under a gantry whose crane has not moved in ten thousand years. Its hook hangs over a dry basin where powder once pooled and was drawn into grates below. The basin floor is smooth except for the tracks of mica-backed scavengers and the circular stains of former loads. You pause at the railing.

Something winged hangs above the crane boom.

At first it is only a tear in the darkness. Then your infrared sense finds it: membrane stretched between black struts, body folded around a mast cable, sensor pits turned toward you. It is awake. It is watching, or imitating surveillance.

Your system offers categories.

FAUNA.  
MAINTENANCE KITE.  
UNREGISTERED AERIAL APPARATUS.  
DEBRIS UNDER TENSION.  
NONINTERFERING.

You select the last.

The winged thing tilts almost imperceptibly. A dull pattern passes across its underside, visible only as a rearrangement of heat.

For a fraction of a second, you wonder if it has thanked you.

Then you remove the wonder from the official record.

Not because wonder is forbidden. Because wonder is expensive, and should be kept where it can do the least harm.

You continue.

Your home, if it can be called that, is a vertical recess in a wall of the maintenance quarter, behind three plates and a pressure lock that almost never opens. Inside is a standing frame fitted to your outline. There are contact pins for data and heat exchange. There are desiccant columns like pale bones. There is a quartz window through which you can see nothing but a strip of corridor and, beyond that, the faint amber rail of the causeway.

When your route is complete, you will return there.

You will submit the findings:

Charge event: nonlinguistic.  
Rod Group 7: mineral accretion within tolerance.  
Bead fracture: monitor.  
Fauna: noninterfering.  
Moisture: absent.  
Equilibrium: maintained.

Then the frame will close around you. Your limbs will lock. Your inner remnant will cool into near-stillness. The city will draw a thin thread of your observations into its distributed memory, not as story but as adjustment. You will sleep without dreams unless dreams are simply unscheduled comparisons among old categories.

Centuries may pass.

Or seven seconds.

There is little difference here except to mechanisms that count.

If you wake again, it will be because something has nearly happened.

A hatch has opened by a finger’s width and taken offense at the air.  
A conveyor has tightened around the idea of work.  
A sealed custodian behind a deeper plate has failed to answer its audit pulse.  
A forbidden droplet has appeared in a chamber designed before grief.

Then you will rise.

You will step out into the dry, warm dark, disturbing dust as little as possible. The city will register you with its grates and conduits and ceramic ears. The flat creatures will become stains. The high things will pretend to be torn equipment. The buried engine may turn once, or not.

And you will go where the threshold trembles.

You are not the ruler of this world. You are not its ghost.

You are its caution given legs.

You are a sealed custodian of the dry city, an almost-living instrument whose task is to preserve the dignity of stillness.
