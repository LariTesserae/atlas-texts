I would find myself low to the ground, under the locked green-black cover where the air does not move freely.

Not in the open water, not at the highest branches, not as one of the louder dawn creatures. I would be somewhere between mud and breath: in the seam where a shallow channel presses against a root-tangled bank, where sedge leans over warm opaque water and the ground is half true, half yielding. I would not stand apart from the place. I would be difficult to distinguish from it.

At first I might think I am the broad dark animal: the heat-slowed body hidden among reeds, eyes just above the surface, hide carrying mud and algae and old scratches. I would feel the basin through pressure more than sight. A tremor in water. A small panic traveling through frogs. The shift of insects when rain is near. I would know routes not as maps but as bodily certainties: which hummock bears weight, where the clay shelf dips, where a trail belongs to many animals and must be entered carefully.

I would wait well. That would be one of my talents.

Not waiting as emptiness, but as work. Digestion, listening, cooling, scenting, conserving. My stillness would have purpose. Things would walk over me. Mosquitoes would settle near my eyes. Snails might cross a hardened ridge of my back. A frog might mistake me for earth until the instant it did not. I would be mud-colored, root-colored, the color of old submerged wood. My mouth would be practical. My hunger would not be noble or cruel. It would simply be one of the basin’s many openings.

But if I stayed with the image longer, I would not remain only that animal.

I would be too distributed for a single hide.

I would be the trail the animal uses, and the smell left in it. The compressed reeds. The churned place at the drinking edge. The dung already entered by beetles. The leech waiting in the water warmed by its passing. I would be the frog that stops calling when the musk thickens, the mosquito breeding in the hoofprint, the fungus loosening a fallen branch above the channel. I would be a system of hungers arranged in darkness.

So perhaps I would find myself as a pressure in the basin rather than a creature with a clean boundary.

A low intelligence of use and reuse.

I would live wherever matter changes hands: under mats of leaves warming from within, in the silt cloud stirred by a fish, in the black water beneath roots, in the throat-pulse of frogs, in the ants rerouting after flood, in the bacterial heat of rot. I would not have a face except temporarily. Sometimes an amber eye at water level. Sometimes a ribbon of ants. Sometimes a turtle’s nostrils. Sometimes a snake-line passing through leaf litter. Sometimes steam lifting before dawn.

If I had a body, it would be broad, dark, scarred, amphibious in habit if not in name. A creature of bank and channel. A patient feeder. A maker of slides in mud. I would sleep in root hollows and enter water without a splash. My young would be many enough that loss was expected, sharp-patterned and brief, learning immediately that every surface is also a mouth.

But if I had a truer form, I would be the basin’s hidden metabolism.

Not ruler, not spirit, not monster. More like a dense agreement among mud, heat, shade, water, hunger, and decay. I would be old without needing memory in the human sense. I would remember through trails, through softened banks, through scars on bark, through where generations have pressed their bodies into the same routes because the land allowed it there and not elsewhere.

I would be found at night, mostly by not being seen.

A darker section of reeds.

A bank breathing.

A pause in the frogs.

A warm smell crossing the trail.

A set of eyes appearing once in the pool and then sinking without ripple.

And if someone asked what I was, the nearest answer would be:

I am not one animal, though I may wear one.

I am the lowland’s patience given appetite.  
I am mud learning to wait.  
I am the crowded life of the basin, hidden under one dark back for a while.
