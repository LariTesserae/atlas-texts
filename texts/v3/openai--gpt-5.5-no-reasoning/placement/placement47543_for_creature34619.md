You would find yourself at the edge of the basin’s moving interior, where the “land” is not land but living infrastructure: a dry, warm underworld-valley beneath stone, lit by amber bulbs and mineral glow, roofed in root-felt and mineral seams, loud with wings, cracking pods, creaking growth, and the organ-roar of pressure storms.

More specifically: you would be somewhere on a maintained migration ramp above a chaff hollow, near one of the human agreements with the cavern rather than in a true settlement. Not a village. Not a city. A threshold.

A place where salt-brick walls lean inward under waxy curtains, where bone ladders are tied to mineral columns, where screens of cured hide catch edible dust from the air, and where every doorway must be scraped each morning before the plants decide it is theirs again. Behind you: a shelter kept open by smoke, cutting, and habit. Before you: the basin’s living tumult—root-bridges tightening, basket-beasts clattering below, ballooning animals drifting overhead like translucent fruit, insects negotiating the walls in black rivers.

You would live in the margin between human craft and nonhuman architecture.

You would be a **storm-listener and bridge-tester**.

Not a leader, not a hunter in the heroic sense, not a scholar safely apart from the world. You would be one of the people who go out before others move. A reader of tension. A keeper of routes that may not exist tomorrow.

You would wear wax-glazed fiber veils and dust-cloaks, darkened around the eyes, with filters at nose and mouth and resin plugs tied at your throat for the roar-hours. Your hands would be scarred by static burns and thorn cuts. Your palms would know the feel of living rope: which strands are merely taut, which are hungry, which are pretending to be dead.

You would carry:

- a hooked testing pole tipped with bone or obsidian;
- a seed-rattle tuned not for music but for warning;
- cords knotted into maps that must be revised by touch;
- bitter resin cakes to pacify tightening vines;
- a thorn comb for scraping seed-dust from cloth and skin;
- a small sealed pouch of moisture, never opened lightly;
- and perhaps a shard of black leaf, taken from one of the patterned fields, wrapped in hide because no one agrees whether it is tool, omen, or listening thing.

Your work would begin before the herd moves.

You would walk the ramp barefoot or soft-wrapped, feeling for warmth through the crust. Too pleasant a warmth would stop you. Blue-white film would make you detour. A bridge that sang in a lower note than yesterday would be suspect. You would tap root-cables, smell cut ends, watch the dust. You would read where insects choose to cross and where they refuse. You would look for the tiny, decisive signs: new hooks along a ledge, a frond turned against the air, pods hanging too still, meal gathering in a seam where nothing should yet be dying.

You would not think of the cavern as hostile. That would be too simple. It is not enemy, not mother, not wilderness, not machine. It is a crowded negotiation in which almost everything has leverage.

Your skill would be knowing when the basin has changed its mind.

During quiet intervals, you would stand on a shelf-colony above the migration groove and listen with your feet. Basket-beasts below would scrape salt blooms from tough plates. Their rib-gardens would tremble as they breathed. Insects would run over your wrapped ankles, tasting you, deciding you were not presently useful. Overhead, ballooners would drift in the amber haze, their threads combing the air for pollen and tiny flyers.

Then the note would come.

Not sound at first. Pressure. A tightening in the root-bridges. A faint ache in the teeth. The seed-pods would turn their seams outward. Copper-winged shriekers would begin before anyone else heard it. Somewhere below, an old root-mind would pulse through mineral and wax.

You would raise your rattle.

Not to stop the storm. No one stops the storm.

To tell the shelter behind you which openings to seal, which animals to drive forward, which ropes to cut loose and which ones to feed smoke. You would strike a coded rhythm against your pole, felt more than heard. Others would answer with hand signs through the dust-thickening light.

When the basin roared, you would become almost blind.

The world would narrow to touch: a rope under your palm, a shoulder against yours, the vibration of a living bridge deciding whether to hold. Seed-dust would hiss across your veil. Pods would fire into the storm like thrown teeth. Herds would move because the plants had already arranged the direction of possible survival.

Your task would be to keep a temporary corridor open long enough for bodies to pass.

Afterward, everything would be altered. The bridge you had trusted might be flowering and impassable. A predator corridor might now be a nursery. A new fissure might breathe orange light. The black leaves below might have arranged themselves into a pattern no one had seen before.

You would return to the shelter and retie the map cords.

Not as a cartographer of fixed places, but as a witness to change.

Among your people, you might be known by a practical name: **Dry-Ear**, **Low-Note**, **Tension-Hand**, **Rattle-Before-Roar**. But the basin would know you differently, if it knew you at all: as a recurring pressure on certain paths, a warm animal who cuts without spilling, who feeds resin to ropes, who does not linger on listening crust, who carries forbidden water but almost never opens it.

You would be human, but only provisionally.

Because here a person is not separate from the ecology. You would be a temporary clearing, wrapped in fiber, moving through a civilization of nonhuman agreements. Dust would live in your clothes. Mites would live at the seams of your mask. Seeds would wait in your cuffs. Your dead, when the time came, would likely be given to a chosen vine, lifted into the dry architecture, milled into meal, memory, bridge-skin, nursery wall.

So: you would find yourself in the dry green underworld, at a maintained edge of the living basin, where the architecture breathes and the roads have appetites.

And you would be one of those who listens for the moment before the world rearranges itself.
