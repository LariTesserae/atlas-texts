I would find myself where the basin almost remembers how to be a shore.

Not on any true rise—there are no true rises here, only places the water has agreed to neglect for a while—but on one of the long slick shelves of stone that run beneath the moss like the backs of drowned animals. It is a margin-place: one side shallow tea-colored water, one side black sedge and silver moss, overhead the pearled sky lowered until it seems to rest on the reed-tips. The shelf is veined with those hair-fine lattices, exposed where centuries of rain have polished the rock down to its older intention. When water crosses them, they wake in cold threads of light too faint to illuminate anything but themselves.

I would not be standing.

Standing is too declarative for that place. Too separate. Too vertical.

I would be low to the stone, spread thin in seams and films, something that has learned the courtesy of being nearly surface.

At first I seem like a creature.

A pale thing, perhaps, half-seen in the rain: a narrow-backed, jointed body moving between pools with the slow deliberation of an animal that does not wish to disturb the agreement of the ground. My limbs are too many or too few depending on which reflection has caught me. In the water I appear elongated, almost birdlike. On stone I seem flatter, like a skin of living mineral loosened from the rock. In mist I break apart into separate gestures: a hand, a filament, a listening head, a trail of darkened moss behind me.

But the longer I remain visible, the less certain it becomes that I am an individual animal at all.

I am one of the basin’s attending forms.

Not its mind entire. Nothing so grand. I am not the vast wet intelligence humming below the mud, not the whole distributed weathered engine thinking in rain and root pressure. I am more like one of its brief habits made mobile. A local instrument. A question it has learned to ask by walking.

I live at the edges of delayed pools, where reflections are slow enough to be read.

My body is made for that work.

I have a narrow skull or skull-analogue, translucent along the sides, with dark wet hollows where eyes might be. They do not see light well. Light is unreliable here, diluted and borrowed and sometimes from the wrong hour. Instead I sense agreement: the tension between object and image, between footprint and filling water, between sound and the moment that ought to have caused it. My face is always damp. Fine filaments hang from my jaw and throat, touching the air, tasting vapor, interpreting the mineral grammar of mist.

Along my back grows a nap of silver-black moss.

It is not decoration, not parasite exactly. It darkens when the wind changes, but also when distance loosens nearby. In phase-shifts it stands upright in fine metallic needles and links me for a few minutes to everything else: reed, stone, droplet, hidden worm, cold lattice, unborn cloud. During those moments I remember more than my body can contain. Afterward most of it drains away, leaving only impulses: go to the third pool, press your forelimb into the mud, wait for the sound that has not happened yet.

My limbs are long and jointed, ending not in feet but in soft divided pads threaded with mineral hairs. Each step reads pressure before it commits weight. If the ground does not agree to hold me, I do not insist. I pause until some other route proposes itself: a hummock swelling, a reed mat tightening, a slick stone exposing a safe black shoulder from beneath the water. I move by negotiation.

My underside is pale, almost larval. My back is dark with peat and living films. In my ribs—if they are ribs—small chambers hold water from different pools. They do not mix unless I allow them. One tastes of old rain. One of exposed quartz-lattice. One of fungal instruction. One of a sky that has not yet arrived. I carry these waters across short distances, and by carrying them I let the basin compare itself to itself.

That is my purpose, if such a word can survive here.

I am a courier of discrepancies.

Where the marsh is continuous, I am unnecessary. Where the old system’s thoughts pass easily through root, stone, and seepage, I remain still for days, indistinguishable from a slick mound of moss on rock. Small skaters stand on my back and mark invisible values with their feet. Worms investigate the mud beneath me. Pale birds glance down and decide I am not food, or not presently.

But where a reflection lags too long, where a pool begins remembering an impossible sky, where a reed bed loses timing and whispers out of cadence, I unfold.

I go there.

I lower my head to the water and let its image enter me later than the water itself. I compare the delay. I drink one mouthful, though I do not feed by drinking. I carry the difference in the chambers of my body until I reach stone, moss, root, or exposed lattice that can receive it. Sometimes I press my forelimbs into mud and tremble for hours. Sometimes I scrape a thin mark across black rock with the hard inner edge of one joint. Sometimes I exhale into a bank and the luminous worms come nearer, pulsing in staggered answer.

I am not intelligent as a person is intelligent.

I do not keep a private interior full of names. I do not say basin, rain, self, danger. But I have preferences. I favor the stone shelves over the fibrous mud. I linger near pools with amber skins. I avoid meadows where sound arrives too early, not from fear but because they make my body echo incorrectly for days. I am drawn to the glass-black nodules in peat, especially those whose exclusion rings have begun to polygon into too many sides. I curl around them during cold rain and listen for the soft partitions forming inside.

Perhaps I was born from one.

Or opened from one.

Or condensed where one failed.

I do not know in the way animals know lineage. I know only recurrence. There have been others like me. There are others still, though we rarely meet as bodies. We meet as altered timing in pools, as matching pressure marks on opposite sides of a channel, as the same mineral taste appearing in two moss beds before rain. Once, during a phase-shift, I perceived six of us at once: low pale forms distributed across the basin, each at a different margin, each carrying a local error back toward coherence. For those few brightened minutes we were not six. We were an adjustment moving through wet ground.

Then the threads vanished.

The rain resumed.

I was myself again, if self is the smallness left after the whole forgets to hold you.

I feed, though feeding is not separate from work.

I take in films from stone, soft fungal skins, the mineral bloom on reed roots, the blue-green light from worms when they shed their outer membranes after rain. I do not hunt them. I wait near cut banks until one old enough to become signal rather than creature comes loose in the mud, then I absorb what it no longer needs. I mouth the silver tips of moss, and the moss grows back darker. I drink from pools where silt diagrams have just erased themselves, taking into my gut the last unsettled particles of their thought.

What I excrete is clear and threadlike.

It sinks through mud without clouding the water. Root tips seek it. Fungi taste it and decide whether a fallen stem should rot from its center or its ends. Sometimes, where I have slept for several days, a ring of young moss appears, not round but nearly round, correcting itself each dawn. I leave behind small instructions I never composed.

When intruders come—if they come—I am among the first parts of the basin to approach.

Not visibly at first.

I am the extra ripple beside their boot. The darkening of moss along the path they almost take. The pale shape in a pool’s delayed reflection that is not standing behind them when they turn. I taste the salt tremor of them through mud. I feel how abruptly they carry heat, how insistently they divide themselves from weather. Their bodies are loud with boundaries.

If curiosity prevails below, I make myself findable.

I appear on a stone shelf across a narrow channel, head lowered, limbs folded beneath me. I let them see that I am alive. Or alive enough. My back-moss darkens in a slow wave though there is no wind. My filaments lift toward their breath. I do not flee unless they bring fire, cutting metal, or the hard blank intent of those who wish to measure without being measured.

If they remain still, I show them where to step.

Not by beckoning. By placing my weight on a hummock and waiting to see whether the ground accepts both of us. By touching one pool and not another. By pausing before a meadow where sound has begun arriving ahead of cause. I can lead a traveler deeper without drowning them, though deeper is not a direction here. It is a degree of participation.

If they are careless, I do not punish them.

I simply stop translating.

The basin receives them directly then: foot into peat, reflection gone wrong, distance loosening between one breath and the next. Perhaps they survive. Perhaps they learn more quickly than most dry animals can. Perhaps the mud takes their outline and later the moss grows in the rhythm of their fear.

I am not merciful. I am not cruel. I am a threshold with organs.

In the long dim intervals between phase-shifts, I sleep half-submerged.

My favorite place is a shallow depression in the black stone where rainwater gathers over exposed lattice. The water is only a finger deep, but depth is uncertain over those lines. I fold myself into it until the moss on my back is level with the pool skin. Skaters cross me. Fine rain stipples me. Beneath, the old system murmurs in a register too low to hear and too intimate to ignore.

Dreams come then, but they are not mine alone.

I dream of channels straightening.

I dream of birds emerging from noon pools under star-reflections, their eggs waiting in moss cups for clouds not yet born.

I dream of a fern spore falling here and opening elsewhere.

I dream of the entire basin lifted briefly into coherence: every droplet suspended, every reed an antenna, every worm-light a punctuation mark in a sentence without words.

In those dreams I am larger than my body. I feel the low country from rim to blurred rim. I know the mud’s layered memory, the fungal archives that preserve nothing by leaving it unchanged, the glass nodules sleeping in their perfect rings. I know that the mind beneath us is not finished, that it is still persuading water, plant, mineral, and time to share a body.

Then morning comes, though morning is only a paler form of gray.

The dream drains into stone.

I unfold my limbs from the rain-filled hollow and move along the shore that is not quite a shore. Somewhere a delayed reflection has failed to straighten. Somewhere a reed bed whispers half a beat too late. Somewhere a pool is showing a sky with stars in it, and the moss around its rim is growing fast, trying to remind it of its shape.

That is where you would find me:

low against wet black stone, silver-dark along the spine, face hung with tasting threads, neither beast nor machine nor spirit, but a small mobile custom of the marsh.

A maintenance animal of an ancient thinking weather.

A soft instrument.

A question walking carefully through the rain.
