You would find yourself **inside the hollow’s rhythm rather than simply in its space**—not at the center as a ruler or heart, but somewhere low, necessary, repetitive, and almost unnoticed.

You would be **one of the amber-shelled snails circling the mineral vents in the runnel**.

Not because the snail is the grandest inhabitant, but because it is one of the clearest expressions of the place’s law: small work, endlessly repeated, keeping an opening open.

You would live half in water, half in green film, moving around the same pale ring with a patience older than intention. Your shell would be translucent amber, threaded with green where algae live inside or upon you, so that even your body would not be wholly yours. You would graze the liverwort and minute floating leaves from the bubbling mouths beneath the surface. If you stopped, the vent would fur over. If the vent closed, the breath would move elsewhere. If the breath moved, the gnats would shift, the flowers would wait too long, the reeds would answer differently, and the hollow would not quite be itself.

You would not understand this.

You would only rasp.

Circle, feed, pause, feel the dome of gas swelling under the skin of water.

Click.

The bubble breaks beside you.

You continue.

Your world would be armspan-wide and inexhaustible: green film, warm mineral breath, the underside shadows of floating leaves, the dim bronze legs of water-striders touching the surface above, frog-calls entering the water as blunt tremors, the vast chuff of the reed-trunks moving through your soft foot as pressure. The hollow’s industrial music would not sound like machinery to you. It would be weather, body, instruction.

You would belong to the maintenance-being as an aperture belongs to a lung.

You would not be alone, but neither would you be individual in any sharp way. Other snails would keep neighboring rings. Some would die and be opened by ants, dissolved into films, taken up by moss or reed or fungus. Others would arrive at the same circumference and continue the same work, so closely that absence would seal before it became event.

You would be a small moving valve in the vegetal engine.

A green-veined amber maintenance thought.

The creature that keeps breath from becoming blockage.
