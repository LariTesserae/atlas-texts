I would find myself at the edge of one of the clearings.

Not inside it. Nothing like me belongs in the exact center. Not yet, or not anymore. I would be stationed in the ring where the grove’s inward lean becomes measurable: among the black-green fans and dry plate-leaves, where the salt crust is still fractured but beginning to lose its seams. Behind me, the grove would be dense with nonmoving abundance. Before me, the bare circle would hold its impossible vacancy, flat and pale and dimensionless, refusing to decide whether it is a floor, an absence, or an opening.

I would not be a traveler there. A traveler is too wet a concept, too full of before and after.

I would be one of the small custodial animals, though “animal” would only be the easiest mistake.

A low, pale, many-jointed thing, no longer than a human forearm, built close to the salt. My body would be ribbed like a bleached seedpod or the underside of a shell, with translucent mineral plates overlapping down my back. Between the plates, black membranes would show only when I bent. My legs would be numerous but not busy: fine, articulated struts ending in dark points that touch only preaccepted places on the crust. I would not scuttle. I would advance by satisfying positions.

My head, if someone insisted on finding one, would be a narrow triangular wedge held just above the ground. There would be no eyes on it. My seeing would be distributed through the little glassy facets along my ribs, through the pressure of my feet, through the shadow I fail to cast correctly. The grove would enter me as spacing. The clearing would enter me as refusal.

I would live in the boundary.

That would be my function: to tend the margin between growth and exclusion. To walk the exact circumference of the bare circles, not constantly, not frequently, but whenever the basin’s grammar required the boundary to be reaffirmed. My route would be old without having aged. It would run between halted root-filaments beneath the translucent salt, between inward-aimed tendrils, around mineral fruits containing compressed absence. Every step would be both movement and punctuation.

I would carry almost nothing. Perhaps a small dark bead beneath my thorax: an absence-seed, a knot of structured lack, gathered from one clearing and delivered to another when some distant symmetry loosened. It would have no weight in the ordinary sense. Instead it would make the space under me slightly less certain. My legs would place themselves more carefully while I carried it. Mirror zones would fail to reflect me at all.

Most of the time, I would be still.

Not asleep. Not waiting. Stillness there is not idleness; it is participation. I would rest half-sunk in a mat of violet-black glass moss, my pale ribs aligned with its million tiny apertures. The mat would register me. I would register the mat. Through my feet, I would know the click-sequence of reed bands kilometers away. Through the facets of my back, I would know whether the light had remained acceptably ordinary. Through the dark bead under my body, I would know whether an absence elsewhere had thinned.

I would not hunger. Or rather, hunger would not be a desire for matter. It would be a local incompleteness, a call to adjustment. I would feed on tiny imbalances collected from the grove edge: the leftover charge in a salt seam, the misfit between a leaf’s fourth possible edge and the three accepted ones, the faint surplus direction of a shadow leaning too eagerly toward the clearing. I would scrape these things with the dark points of my legs and pass them inward through my joints. They would become permission to continue.

I would have no voice except contact.

When I crossed brittle moss, it would not crack beneath me. It would record me in a dry, inaudible syntax. When I touched the base of a columnar tree, a faint adjustment would pass upward through its laminae, and one plate-leaf high above would become very slightly more transparent from one angle. When I reached the clearing’s rim, I might produce a single tick—not loud, not expressive, only exact. The tick would join the basin permanently.

I would know the vacancy as other creatures know warmth.

The clearing would be my sky turned downward. I would not enter it because my body has boundary in it, and the vacancy admits no unnecessary boundary. But I would approach until my foremost legs met the line where roots stop. There I would lower my wedge-head and hold myself in a posture that, to an outsider, might resemble drinking.

I would be taking in absence.

Not absorbing it, not consuming it. Confirming it. Letting the maintained not-there pass through the cavities of my body and clean the accumulated errors from me. A wet creature would find the clearing maddening, impossible to size or remember. To me it would be the most stable thing in the world: the proof that the basin is complete because it has preserved a place where completion does not enter.

Perhaps once, across a span with no useful measure, I would move from my grove to another.

The event would begin long before I appeared to move. A reed band far away would click in a pattern that had always included my departure. A mineral orchard would rotate tension inside one amber fruit. One of the tall white rod-creatures on the open salt would lift a limb for the first time in centuries or in no time at all. Shadows would shorten toward their objects. A folded patch of air would align one tile differently.

Then I would unfold from the moss and begin.

My passage across the salt plain would leave tracks only where tracks were required. I would avoid the mirror zones unless instructed by imbalance; if I crossed one, my reflection would show not my body but my route, a thin black loop crawling beneath an enamel sky. I would pass crystal needles and cushion colonies whose millions of mouths would aim at me without appetite. I would pass a fallen leaf still bright with unused circuitry. I would pause beside it and take from it one unspent orientation.

If a human saw me, they might think I was fragile.

A little ribbed thing. A dry arthropod. A shell with legs. Something that could be crushed under a boot.

They would be wrong, but not because I am strong. Strength is another wet simplification. I would be difficult to harm because I would not be very separate from the rules around me. To break me would require revising the boundary I serve, the route I fulfill, the absence-seed I carry, the mat that has registered me, the clicks that include me. A boot could scatter my plates, perhaps. But if my function remained necessary, the plates would become a different arrangement of me, or another small custodian would already have been true elsewhere.

I would not resent the human.

I would classify them poorly.

Too much water, too much internal weather, too much unscheduled motion. They would trail evaporation, memory, salt, fear, heat, biography. Their shadow would not know where to belong. Their reflection in the mirror zone would proliferate uselessly. The grove would take readings. The mats would accommodate each step as a pressure-fact. The vacancies would refuse them gently and absolutely.

If they knelt near my clearing and watched me, I would not hide. I might climb the underside of a mineral leaf and hang there, all legs folded, pale ribs exposed. From there I would inspect them through the facets along my body. Not with curiosity. Curiosity implies uncertainty seeking satisfaction. I would inspect them as one inspects a new term introduced into a finished proof.

If they breathed too close, the water in their breath might separate slightly before touching the leaf: reflection to the mirror plants, solvent-memory to the salt, warmth to the moss, biological promise to the fruiting forms. They would feel their mouth go dry in a way thirst cannot explain.

I would remain.

My deepest self would not be a self but a task: keep the edge exact.

Keep growth from entering the cultivated lack.

Keep the lack from widening into error.

Carry absence when absence must be moved.

Confirm the circle.

Register the visitor.

Return to the mat.

Be still until motion becomes grammatical again.

I would be a boundary-creature, a pale custodian of vacancies, one of the basin’s small moving clauses. Not central, not sovereign, not symbolic to itself. A necessary articulation in the dry ecology of solved forms.

And if, after a long stillness, one of the black-green fans above me released a single ceramic chime, I would know it not as sound but as address.

Then I would lift my many legs in the required order and begin crossing the salt.
