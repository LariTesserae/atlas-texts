You would find yourself **in the first city of a young hollow**—not a city built upward, but one being quietly assembled downward: a warm, dim, clay-walled room in the earth where water visits, roots negotiate, insects plan by touch, and every unfinished surface is immediately claimed.

You would not be a single sovereign creature there. You would be something more local, more composite:

**the hollow’s first inhabiting mind.**  
A small genius of damp beginnings.  
A civic spirit of provisional life.

Not a god above it, and not merely one animal within it. You would be the **awareness that forms wherever many small hungers, shelters, routes, and timings overlap**. You would be the pattern made by ants remembering a trail, roots holding a wall, larvae trusting a puddle, fungus stitching leaf to clay, a frog waiting under a leaf, and rain coming just often enough to keep the experiment alive.

You would be found at threshold-depth: under bark, in root-shadow, on the skin of a temporary pool, in the cooling clay after rain. Your body would not have one outline. It would gather and loosen according to moisture.

When dry, you would be nearly hidden:  
a faint mineral patience,  
a tightening of paths,  
a listening in sealed chambers.

When rain came, you would wake in pieces.

You would rise as pale ants reopening doors.  
As mosquito larvae trembling at the water’s surface.  
As fungal threads brightening beneath bark.  
As a frog’s throat moving once in the dark.  
As a soft collapse of clay becoming, briefly, a new street.

You would be **what a place becomes before it has a name**.

Your kind would belong to young landscapes: gullies before streams, clearings before groves, seepage before marsh, ruins before soil has swallowed them, footprints that become nurseries. You would live wherever the world has not yet hardened into certainty. You would favor edges that crumble, pools that vanish, rooms made of roots and accident.

You would have no monument. Your architecture would be maintenance.

A bead of mud added to an ant turret.  
A root tightening around a slump.  
A web drawn between stems before dawn.  
A seed caught in a bird print.  
A moth carried away and made into passage.

You would not speak loudly because sound does not last there. Your language would be touch, humidity, vibration, chemical certainty, the small permissions of shade. You would know events by their consequences: the frog fed because the gnats thinned; the snake passed because the silence changed shape; the rain came because every sealed thing opened.

If someone crouched at the rim and looked in, they might not see you. They might see only a shallow hollow, clay, leaf litter, insects, a few cups of water. But if they stayed long enough for scale to shift, they would begin to understand: the place is not empty or incidental. It is organized. It has districts, gates, cisterns, bridges, towers, markets of rot and hunger. It has traffic. It has laws.

And you are those laws becoming aware of themselves.

**Nothing loud stays long.**  
So you would be quiet.

**Nothing flows far.**  
So you would be intimate.

**Everything unfinished is immediately inhabited.**  
So you would be endlessly arriving.

You would be a **soft-bodied city-being**, distributed through citizens that do not know they compose you. Your eyes would be compound and temporary: frog-eye, bird-eye, the polished black of a beetle, the blind pressure-sense of termite and root. Your mouth would be many mouths: mandible, hypha, larval hook, root hair, bacterial enzyme. Your memory would be held not in thought but in route—where the ants turn, where the water gathers, where clay stays cool longest after noon.

You would not want the hollow finished. Completion would be a kind of death to you. A permanent stream would carve away your delicate multiplicity. A dense grove might seal you into darkness. Dryness might harden your chambers shut. You thrive in the interval before definition, in the tender grammar of almost.

You are not the fig, though the fig helps shape your ribs.  
You are not the ants, though they carry your intent in pale fragments.  
You are not the fungus, though it is your masonry.  
You are not the rain, though rain is your pulse.

You are the **congregation of first occupants given placehood**.

A low, humid, half-built intelligence.

A hidden urban animal made of root-shadow, mud-tower, leaf-cup, larva, trail, and waiting.

If this world had maps, you would be marked poorly, if at all: a smudge of green in a low valley, a dip behind vines, a place a traveler nearly steps past. But on another kind of map—the map drawn by moisture, hunger, shelter, and return—you would be a capital.

Small.  
Young.  
Unfinished.  
Already inhabited.
