The power watched for a year before doing anything.

It saw how quickly a hard rain could fill the cisterns, and how little of that water remained in the ground a month later. It saw the worker move a shoot away from the hot pipe rather than cut it. It saw that the bird nested in the weak part of the roof, where people were careful with their props.

At the end of the dry season, when the channels were empty enough to walk in, the worker with the wrench found a seam of damp sand beneath a fallen stone. She called two others. They dug only far enough to learn that an old branch of the stream lay buried there, stopped with silt where it turned beneath the bank.

The power had not put water in the seam. It had loosened the stone.

The townspeople cleared the buried turn over several days. They laid back the stones whose roots had not taken hold and left the others alone. When the first storm came, some of its water entered the old branch. It moved slowly through the bank instead of racing past the road. Downstream, the storm still arrived; here, the roots stayed damp a little longer.

By spring, grass had taken in the new wet ground beyond the last rack. Insects gathered there, and the bird under the roof no longer called alone. The west windows still needed freeing. The threshold still rose. There were still weeks when the motors turned slowly and stems were left standing.

The power remained attentive to the place. It did not keep the rain falling or prevent the roof from aging. Once, during a storm, a prop slipped, and the worker with the wrench climbed up to set it right. She found the nest above her head, waited for the bird to settle, and chose another place for the prop.
