The power would come in the dry hour, when the fan had pushed the damp border almost to the wall and the springtails were gathered in the last shaded rust. It would know how little a place like this needs to be lost: a broomful of dust, a splash of cleaner, a truck tire a few inches off its usual line.

It would not quiet the fan. It would not take the spider away. The spider lives here too.

Instead, when a worker came around the shed with a bucket and a stiff brush, the power would draw their eye to the bearing. A drop would fall just then. The worker would see the water tremble, then notice that what looked like pale grit along its rim was moving toward it.

They would leave the brush against the wall and carry the bucket around to the drain.

By morning the bearing would be empty again. The spider would still wait beside its chip, and the springtails would wait in the rust for the pipe’s next drop. Nothing in the crack would know it had been spared.
