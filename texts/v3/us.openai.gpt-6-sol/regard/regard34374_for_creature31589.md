The power would first mistake the place for a ruin in need of repair. It would see the fallen roof, the empty grooves, the paddles worn thin by knocking. Then it would wait through a change of wind.

It would watch the plants turn silver and the scrapers emerge. It would hear the paddles cover the quick passage of feet across the stone. It would understand that the grooves no longer carried what they once had, but the paddles were still doing work.

So it would not rebuild the roof. It would not bring water into the dry cracks or still the wind to give the small animals peace. Too much of this place lived by what the wind brought, and by the brief shelter its noise provided.

One day, a scale of stone would loosen high on the rim, directly above the clicking colony. The power would set a hand against it—not to hold the wall unchanged forever, but to turn this one fall. The stone would come down a little farther along, landing across two old slabs. It would leave a narrow space beneath it.

By the next gust, something small would have found that space. The rim would go on shedding grit. The paddles would knock. Deep inside a folded crown, the unseen worker would keep working, unaware that anyone had come.
