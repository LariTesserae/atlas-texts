The power would spend a long time doing nothing.

From the ridge, it might have mistaken the valley’s motion for struggle and tried to quiet it. Up close, it would see that the falling columns let light in, that the fraying belts carry seeds to bare wood, that a hollow closing in one place makes a hollow elsewhere. It would not straighten the columns or hold the canopy open.

It would stay long enough to learn where the white creature went when its tracks seemed to end.

One afternoon, the creature would be drinking from a leaf cup when the roots on either side began to draw together. It would turn toward its usual passage and find that passage gone. The power would feel for the nearest space already opening in the mats, then lift a few fine stems between the creature and that space. Only the width of two fingers.

The creature would hesitate at the unfamiliar opening. A drop would fall behind it, loud enough to send it darting through. By the time it reached the next root, the stems would be settling back. The valley would go on moving water uphill. The creature would find another cup.

The power would keep watch, but it would not make the little passage permanent. This place knew how to make passages. For once, it had needed a moment more.
