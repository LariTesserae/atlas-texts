The power comes to the plateau and does not quiet the towers.

It hears what their noise conceals: water moving through the shafts, the small dry rattle of one loosened fastening, the clicks of hooked feet beneath a ledge. It watches a school scatter and return. It waits long enough to understand that the current is not merely something the creatures endure. It is where they feed.

At the trembling tower, the power sets a hand against the loose plate. The whine changes. Fish flash away from the grille, and even the hunters below turn toward the unfamiliar note. So it takes its hand away. The school returns, a few fish at a time.

Later, when the fish are out feeding, the power tightens the fastening from inside the tower. The plate can no longer work free, but water still brushes its edge. It trembles as it did before. Nothing falls into the shaft.

On the far side of the plateau, a fresh fracture has opened in clean stone. The power finds a few branching threads caught on a current and lays them in the slow water at the bottom of the crack. It does not cover the bare slopes or summon fish to them. The threads may hold, or they may not.

Then it leaves. For a while the silver school keeps its distance from the tower. By the next day it is crowded against the grille again. Beneath a ledge, one flat animal lets go, crosses the narrow gap, and fastens itself where there is now something to eat.
