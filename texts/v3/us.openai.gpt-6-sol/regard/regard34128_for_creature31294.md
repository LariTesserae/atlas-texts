The power would not fill the hollow.

It would notice how much is already there: the last of the day’s heat, the narrow shelter beneath the stone, the faint movements that tell the scorpion when to wait and when to hunt. It would understand that the bare center is not a place the scorpion needs to be persuaded to cross.

So it would leave the stones where they are. It would not call rain into ground that holds no water, or crowd the hollow with plants whose roots could find no purchase. It would let the insects pass by their own small purposes, and let the scorpion answer with its own.

For a while, the power would remain attentive. A grain of grit would slip. Warmth would rise from the rock and thin into the night. At the edge of the gap, the scorpion would extend one leg, touch the dust, and draw it back.

Nothing here asks to be made larger or brighter. The power would give the hollow the rare kindness of being left intact.
