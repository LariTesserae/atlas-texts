The power came to the hollow on a morning when the channel held no water. A worker had set aside the first blue-gray slab, and its branching marks were still moving. The power watched until they stopped. Then it waited a little longer, as the worker always did.

It could have made the streets keep their lengths. It could have straightened the buried stairs, taken the windows out of the walls, and sent every sound promptly to its destination. But the district was still learning what it was. To settle all of that at once would have been a kind of harm, even if everyone reached the bakery on time.

Instead, the power chose three small things.

On every route to the clinic, the drain covers would remain countable. A person might still take longer than expected, but seven covers would lead to the clinic door, and a person coming back would find seven again. The power gave the same steadiness to the routes children used for school and to the stairs the repair crews used when someone was trapped below. Elsewhere, it left room for the streets to grow.

It gave the new wall-windows a way to be private. If a person beyond the glass wished to be seen, the little view remained. If not, someone passing outside saw only the blue-gray stone. The masons noticed that some windows appeared and vanished from one day to the next. They checked the walls for weakness, found none, and went on with their work.

At the channel, the first slab of each morning stayed soft for an hour. After that it hardened as the others did. The worker had time to set it down without cutting through the branching marks. The marks had time to go wherever they were going. Neither was asked to understand the other yet.

The power did nothing to the footsteps on the stairs. Once, walking down beneath the transit stop, it heard a second shoe follow it: late on one step, early on the next. It stopped. The other footstep finished, as it always did. When the power continued, it went slowly enough that the sound could follow without hurrying.

Years later, the surveyors would still photograph the grooves beside the channel after every rain. Children would still learn to count without looking down. Friends would still walk one another all the way home, because a dependable route was not the same thing as company.

No one in the hollow knew a power had visited. A few people thought the clinic had become easier to find. The worker at the channel took to bringing a second cup of tea and setting it beside the slab while he waited.
