The power would not begin by straightening a street.

It would spend a long while in the city without taking a shape: in the grit beneath a cornice, in a wall’s brief warmth, in the space between an animal’s forelimbs and the stone. It would watch the small ones wait at entrances that did not yet admit them. It would watch a chamber remain sound while some change of slope made it too cold to hold the young. The city, it would learn, was not careless. It was changing on a scale that could not always attend to a single shell.

When it acted, the alteration would be slight. In a few sheltered rooms, the stone would keep its warmth through the end of the dim hours. Not every room, and not forever; enough that an animal seeking shelter could find one before its young grew cold. At the foot of each such room, a narrow band of grit would remain undisturbed by the wind. The animals would come to know it as they knew a pressure behind a wall.

Then the power would turn to the distances. It would leave the districts free to draw near and away, but give certain passages a gentler habit. A traveller who had gone out by an arch might still face a long walk home, yet the arch would not vanish altogether while they were on the road. For the jointed animals, this would mean a few more safe returns. For the city, it would be only another habit, if it accepted the change.

The power would not stop the grain falling through the courtyard. Something waited below it. Perhaps the landing mattered; perhaps only the waiting did. It would let the grain reach the paving in its own time.

Years later, little would show of the visit. A few rooms would be sought more often at the coldest hour. Some corners would gather less grit. On a roof, a watcher might notice that an arch once used was still visible across a great distance, pale against the violet-grey sky.

The city would go on making streets and taking them away. The animals would go on touching each step before trusting it. The power would remain long enough to see whether its kindness had helped, and quiet enough to change it if it had not.
