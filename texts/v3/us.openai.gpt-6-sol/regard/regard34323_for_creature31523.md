The power would come in the hour before a gate opened, when the water went strangely still.

It would feel the pressure gathering beneath the channels and the small animal feeling it through the ground. It would see the burrow above the usual tide and the fresh gravel laid over peat, and understand why the animal had chosen it. A good place, until tonight.

It would not hold the gate shut. The people beyond the road needed it to open, and the plain needed the water to pass. Nor would it fasten every bank in place. The flood’s fresh cuts made room for fish, for willow roots, for birds feeding on mud that had not been mud yesterday.

Instead, it would spend its strength on a few small heights.

Here and there, among the willows but away from the deepest channels, gravel would settle more firmly into the peat. Roots would find it. The rain and tide would leave narrow rises that looked like ordinary work of the flood, except that they would endure its worst hours. The power would make no continuous wall of them—only places close enough for a creature with wet feet to reach.

Tonight it would make one beside the animal’s burrow.

As the wrong stillness deepened, the animal would climb. Its whiskers would trail over stones, then living roots. Behind it, the bank would go with a little sound no one in the pump house could hear. The water would take the tunnel and leave the new rise alone. There the animal would wait, cold and breathing fast, while the gate’s pulse passed through the ground.

The power would tend to the pump keepers, too, though less visibly. A stubborn fault in one pressure reading would become plain before it mattered. Someone behind the amber glass would check it, then choose a release that gave the water a little more time to spread. Some banks would still disappear. None of the people would have to guess blindly at a danger they could have known.

By morning, birds would be running the new mud. The animal would go down to feed and, perhaps, dig beneath the willow roots. Its next tunnel might last no longer than the first.

The power would leave it that uncertainty. But when the water rose again, there would be somewhere to climb.
