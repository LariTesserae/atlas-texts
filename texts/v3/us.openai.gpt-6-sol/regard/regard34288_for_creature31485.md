The power would not make the town larger. There is nowhere for it to go, and widening it by force would break the joined wood that keeps its oldest inhabitants alive.

It would come first in the rain, when the walls gave off water and every fracture in the black stone could be felt. Deep beneath the last arch it would find a crack beginning to open under the weight of the crowns. There it would set the two faces of stone gently against each other, leaving room for the roots already inside. The water would still pass. The cliff would still be a cliff. But it would not take the outer courts with it that winter.

Then the power would wait.

In a high court, one old stem had stopped carrying sap. It stood because its neighbours held it, and below it a dozen young plants had turned their leaves toward the same narrow piece of sky. The power would not choose one of them to live. It would let the dead stem soften as dead wood does, until a storm could lay it down without tearing its neighbours loose. A little more light would reach the court. Some shoots would grow toward it; others would not.

Before leaving, the power would touch the newest growth at the outer arch. Those shoots were reaching across the opening, as shoots must. It would give them no fear of the drop and no command to stop. It would leave them the strength to join where they could, and the freedom to fail where they could not.

By spring, there would be an unfamiliar pale patch on the floor of the high court. Birds would find insects in the fallen wood. Water would keep its old, untraceable course through the walls. Anyone entering the town would still have to part the fine roots at a doorway, and they would still settle back behind them.
