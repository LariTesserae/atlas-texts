The power would not bring rain.

It would see how much of the plain depends on having none: the resin sealed in the leaves, the dust gathered from their edges, the old courses that would swell if water entered them. It would not straighten the rows, either. The row set a little off true might be the one that lets a leaf turn freely.

For a while, it would do nothing but listen with the workers.

Far below a shaded root bed, it would feel a pressure they could not yet feel. An old channel had slowly narrowed around a living root. In a few years the root would lift the course above it, as roots often did. But this channel ended beneath a factory track, where no tile could rise.

The power would not break the floor open. At the next dustfall, while the leaves were closed and the combing machines stood still, it would loosen a single ceramic fitting at the channel’s far end. Not enough to move it. Only enough that the pressure below made a faint, uneven tick.

A worker resting with cords at their wrists would wake to it. They would put a hand on the floor, wait through three slow turns of the leaves, and call another worker over. Together they would find the channel and decide where to open it. They would set a new socket beside the track before the root needed it.

The six-legged animals would arrive when the first tile lifted. The workers would wait for them to leave before lowering it again.

By evening, the fitting would be quiet. No one would have reason to think the plain had been visited. The power would be content with that, and would go on listening.
