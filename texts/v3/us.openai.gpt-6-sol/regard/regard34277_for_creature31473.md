The power comes without a lantern.

At the first post it rests a hand on the warm shaft and waits through several of its slow pulses. It feels the pressure moving beneath the stone, the roots holding the soil, the tiny feet tapping at its hand. Nothing asks to be remade. Even the darkness is useful here: in it, a footstep announces itself long before a hand can close around a small animal.

A stranger’s next step lands close to a root hollow. The callers stop. When the foot lifts, the power feels how slowly the root eases back.

So it changes the path, and almost nothing else. Along the narrow line of firm stone between the posts, it raises a few shallow ridges—too low to trip over, plain beneath a bare foot or a walking stick. They lead around the hollows and away from the rim. At each post, one ridge ends where a traveler can stand without pressing on the leaf mat. Rain will wash over them; roots can grow across them. Anyone who loses the line can still find the old warmth.

The power waits while a broad-backed animal crosses. Its many feet take their accustomed holds. A caller rasps from a crack beside the new path, and another answers a drop of water. Nothing has to learn a new way home.

Then the power goes. It leaves no light behind, and no promise that the rain will lessen. Only this: the next careful stranger may find a place to put their feet, and the small lives beneath them may go on calling.
