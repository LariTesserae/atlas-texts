The power would come without a body. A body would have to stand somewhere, and here, standing somewhere can change where *somewhere* is.

It would wait above a fresh split and attend to the plateau’s slow turning. The shells are not tearing the stone apart; they are feeding from a stone that has always opened. The ferns know how to follow water, and the grazers know when not to cross. Even the cliffs, shedding their teeth of rock into the cloud, have not finished becoming what they are. There is no wound here for the power to close.

But one morning a calf reaches the ledge where it drank yesterday. The pool is gone. It calls into the ground, and an adult answers from a distance the calf cannot travel alone. Between them, a new crack is widening.

The power does not join the edges. It lets a little moisture gather in the crack, enough for the moss on either side to darken. The leader of the herd, waiting beyond it, puts down its muzzle and finds the damp place. By evening, the herd has taken a longer path around. The calf walks in the middle of the line, placing its feet with care.

When the power withdraws, the plateau is still rising. The crack widens; roots enter it. Six-winged creatures find the warm seam and leave their eggs there. Far below, the great dark back passes under the cloud, and its call reaches the new pool beside the herd’s path.

The power listens until the water stops trembling. Then it goes.
