The power watches for a long time before doing anything.

It learns that the basins are not merely drains, that the blue film is not dirt, and that a crossing with a wrong arrow is safer than a crossing someone has been persuaded to trust. It does not straighten the valley. It does not bring down the bright walls to let in the sun.

Instead, it attends to a dry trough.

For years, water has returned to this trough without warning. The creatures beneath the sealant survive the dry months, but each new flow carries some of them through a grate before they can find purchase. The power makes no new water. It puts one slight hollow in the trough’s floor, no wider than a hand, where the first trickle will pause.

The change is small enough that a worker clearing grit finds it only by touch. She marks it with her grease pencil and checks the grate. The hollow does not obstruct the drain, so she leaves it.

When water returns, it gathers there first. The many-legged creatures emerge into it and spread along the trough. A few are taken by birds. Others reach the basins, where the fish make their small corrections beneath the drips. By evening a thin blue cast has appeared at the hollow’s edge.

The power watches that too. If the hollow had caused harm, it would have smoothed it away. But the water passes, the seal holds, and the worker’s mark remains where she can find it next month.

Then the power leaves the valley to its people and its other inhabitants. It has given them nothing they must understand in order to use, and nothing they must keep if they find it wanting.
