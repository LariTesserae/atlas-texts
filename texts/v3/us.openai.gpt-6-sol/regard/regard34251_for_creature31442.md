The power came to the crack at dusk, when the wind had dropped and the mouths along the walls had closed.

It could have pressed the mountain together. The two halves still remembered how to meet; even the pale seam pointed the way. But closing the crack would crush the ledges, shut out the wind that fed the colony, and bury everything that lived on what fell from it.

It could have made a bridge. That, too, would give passage to creatures that could not now cross: the cleaners beneath the wings, the hunters among the fallen feathers, whatever waited in the dark below. The power did not know which of them might flourish on the other wall, or what would vanish if they did.

So it listened.

An adult called from the eastern face. Another answered from the west. Between the calls came the smaller voices of the young, and beneath those, the slow, uneven taps of water finding its way down fresh stone.

One young creature had climbed beyond its parent’s reach. It stood on a thin lip near the end of the pale seam, its hooked feet set carefully, its wings held out for a wind that would not come. Across the gap, adults answered its calls. Each answer sent it edging nearer the drop.

The power did not lift it. It let a little of the evening air that had been moving over the summit turn downward into the crack. The first gust touched the far wall and failed. The next passed under the young creature’s open wings.

It held on.

Its parent called once from below. Then the young one let go, fell a little, and crossed. It struck the opposite wall clumsily, lost one foothold, found another. By the time it had settled among the dark bodies there, the gust was gone.

The power stayed until morning. It watched the pale many-legged things make their rounds and the drops gather where warm breasts met cold rock. It heard the colony wake to a stronger wind without its help.

Then it left the mountain split.
