The power would not bring rain.

It would see the broad leaves and the crowded roots, and understand that water was not what they lacked. Nor would it plant a grove in the empty miles. A small growth there would live just long enough to spend itself.

For a long time, the power would watch. It would learn how much charge the stands gathered and how much they needed to survive the dark. It would notice that the bare passages were not wounds in the growth. They were places where the current ended.

Then, at the outer edge of one stand, it would make a small change far below the stone. Not a new seam, not yet: only a little more charge along a seam already active, enough to reach a crack just beyond the last roots. No stem would be pulled toward it. Nothing would be made to grow.

Through the bright day, the stand would turn and store its charge as before. At its margin, a frond might lean over the crack. In time, a thin root film might follow. If it did, the stand would gain a narrow strip of ground. If it did not, the extra charge would fade into the stone, and the power would leave that edge alone.

It would do the same nowhere else until it knew what the first change had cost.

The distances between the stands would remain. The power could not tell whether they wished to meet, or whether a path between them would let one draw too much from another. It would not mistake a feeling of loneliness in the shade for a need the stand itself had shown.

Perhaps, after many bright days, there would be one new blade in the crack. The power would watch it share the light and the current with the others. That would be enough to begin with.
