The power would first go to the eastern basin and wait through a night.

It would watch the crews mark a new boundary at dawn. It would see them leave a warm hollow alone, even though the soil there was ready for the trees. It would understand that the city had not defeated wear. It had learned to bring wear somewhere it could be seen, measured, and treated with care.

So it would not stop the passage beneath the streets. Nor would it make the basin inexhaustible. An endless supply of old soil would let the city forget where its years went.

Instead, it would make one small change, and make sure someone noticed it.

After the next rain, a little water would remain in the shallow canal. Not enough to flood a road or trouble the roots, but enough to join the warm pools by a thin, continuous thread. The animals could travel beneath the bridges without crossing dry stone. Children would find it in the morning, following its glint from one district to the next.

Near the basin, where the crews had marked out the sleeping hollows, the thread would spread into a patch of damp ground. Reeds would take hold there. They would catch some of the brick dust before it reached the hollows, and the small animals would nose among their stems. The crews would see that this new ground lay in the way of tomorrow’s work. They would draw their outline around it.

The power would leave them to decide what to build after that. It had given the city a little more room for its water and its animals, and a reason to keep looking closely at the place where its years arrived.
