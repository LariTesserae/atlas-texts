The power first thinks of making the water reliable.

It could bring a clear stream down through the limestone, fill every basin, and spare the people their evening work. Then it watches a little longer. It sees the animals emerge at the first trace of damp, the fish pass through hidden connections, the film grow and recede. A permanent stream would make one part of the city flourish and leave other parts dry for good. The people, too, would have to abandon much of what they know in order to live beside it.

So it leaves the water free.

What troubles it instead is a bridge in the district beyond the terraces. One of its supports has been settling for years. The bridge has always shifted a little with the seasons, and its newest wedge looks sound, but a crack has opened behind the wedge where no one can see it. The next hard rain will loosen the clay packed into the support. The bridge will stand through the rain and fall afterward, when the stones have begun to dry and someone thinks it safe to cross.

The power does not hold it up forever. It cannot make a failing bridge sound by wishing it sound.

On a dry morning, it sends a thin fall of grit through the hidden crack. The grit gathers on the pale floor in a narrow line, directly beneath the support. An elder sees it while carrying stores home. She looks up, calls to someone across the street, and together they close the bridge with a shutter laid sideways over its first step.

For three days, people who might otherwise have tended channels take apart the damaged support. They find the crack. They carry out the loose clay and fit new stone beneath the span. Rain comes before they finish, and they wait it out on opposite sides of the water. Afterward they finish the work.

No one has much to say about the grit. Some think an animal disturbed the seam. The elder says she is glad she looked up.

The power remains for a while. It watches a woman move a bank by two handspans and watches the water ignore her, turning instead into an old basin. She laughs at her mistake and follows it with her stones. Before she reaches the basin, the pale animals are already there.
