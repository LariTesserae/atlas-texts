The power would arrive at the rim during a dry hour and stay there.

At first it might mistake the town’s quiet for a need. It could bring rain enough to fill every roof cup, or straighten the roads, or pull the roots from the bearings so the wheels could turn freely. But it would see the beetles waiting for condensation, the hunters waiting for beetles, the small bodies riding a belt that moved only a little at a time. It would see a moth pass through a window and come back by a route the power could not follow.

So it would wait through a rain, and through the hour in which the leaf drank it. It would wait until the ridges rose again beneath the new grit. It would not yet know whether the town was one living thing, or many, or whether that distinction mattered to anything inside it.

Near morning, a roof scale would close early. A moth beneath its edge would fail to move with the others and be held there, one wing caught in the seam. The power would ease the scale by less than the width of a beetle’s leg. The moth would free itself and settle beneath a press. Then the scale would close.

That would be all for now. The power would remain at the rim a while longer, attentive to what followed, and leave without making a path down into the town.
