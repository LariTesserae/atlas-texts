The power came to the shelf in the hour before dawn, when the houses were still dark and the wind could be heard between the insect calls.

It had meant to look for what was lacking. There was little water, certainly. The roofs needed constant mending. At the rim, the old path had been worn so smooth that a hurried foot might slip. But the power stayed long enough to see how water was saved, how a loose roof slab was known and spoken of before it fell, and how nobody crossed the rim in a hurry.

It did not bring a spring to the mountain. A spring would have been a gift with a long shadow: new roots, new walls, new claims upon the ground. Nor did it quiet the wind. The people went to the edge in the morning partly to hear it.

Instead, the power attended to the bare rock where the oldest steps ended. Grain by grain, it gave the smooth stone back a little of the texture the wind had taken. Not enough to catch a sandal or make a goat stumble. Just enough that, on a cold morning or a rare wet one, a foot could hold where it expected to hold.

No one noticed the change that day. An old woman crossed to judge the haze and came back thinking about flour. A child followed a goat halfway out and was called home. The stone remained under them, roughened no more than it needed to be.

Then the power left. The shelf had people who knew how to care for it, and plenty of work that was theirs to do.
