The power would not begin with light.

It would come while the keeper was at the pump and listen through the soles of the district: to the flywheel’s pause, to the water passing the grate, to the almost imperceptible current holding the fish in place. It would learn that the collars were not meant to end the dripping. The keeper moved water from one place to another, keeping a little on the threshold for the crabs and a little away from a failing joint.

Then it would do what the keeper could not. Deep in the vault, where no hand could reach, it would hold an iron beam steady. It would ease the strain from the masonry above the pump. Nothing would look different afterward. The familiar drops would fall into their familiar cups. But the roof would no longer be preparing, year by year, to come down.

The power would leave the shop door alone, raised lip and all. It would not clear the calcite from the old height marks. It could not know whose heights they were, or whether seeing every mark again would comfort the keeper.

Instead, on a morning before the lamps came on, the keeper would find a new fitting among the others. It would be plain, threaded to no pipe in the district. When they picked it up, its metal would be warm—not enough to startle them, only enough that they might keep holding it. There would be no instruction attached and no task it was waiting for them to perform.

The keeper could put it back opening-out, like the rest.

And if, some evening, they carried it home in a stiffened coat pocket, the power would let that be enough.
