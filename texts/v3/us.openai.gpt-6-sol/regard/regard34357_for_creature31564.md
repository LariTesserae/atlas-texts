The power would arrive during a fine rain and, at first, do nothing.

It would watch a water crew lift a grate that had been dry the day before. It would watch a surveyor put her hand against a wall and wait to feel whether it moved. It would watch the birds rise ahead of a train, and the grazers wait at a place where the stone sometimes gave them water.

The plateau was rising. To make it still would take away the thing on which the city had learned to stand. To make its water run plainly over the surface might leave the hillsides below dry. The power would not know every consequence merely because it was powerful, so it would ask questions. It would travel with the crews, listen to the people in the newest blocks, and sit through a long winter evening with those who kept the trains running.

Then it would make one small change. Wherever water passed under the plateau, a dark line would appear on the stone above it after rain. The line would last only until the surface dried. It would show where the water was going now, not promise where it would go tomorrow.

The crews would test it for months before trusting it. Children would follow the lines across schoolyards; their teachers would call them back from the fences. One morning a line would run beneath a street where no one had expected water. The crews would close the street, and by afternoon a portion of it would give way. No one would be there when it fell.

In time, the marks would be added to the water maps. Some would lead to grates the grazers used, and those places would be left open. Others would show where a new foundation needed a different footing. The city would still need its surveyors, its guesses, and its daily work.

The power would stay for the first winter, in case its gift had done harm it had failed to foresee. It would help repair what it could. When the thaw came, it would leave.

Years later, someone waiting for the last tram would notice a line appearing at the edge of the shelter. She would move her bag out of the wet, then look to see where the water went.
