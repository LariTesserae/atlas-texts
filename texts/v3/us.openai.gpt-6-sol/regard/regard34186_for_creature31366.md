The power does not try to make the gap smaller.

It watches a grain fall between the walls and understands that the distance is real to the creature, however little room it occupies in the bank. Lifting the creature out would be easy. So would setting it down on the far side. Neither would help it live there.

The power listens to the tap. Deep behind one wall, a pebble has begun to loosen each time the river presses against the broken clay. It will not fall today, but the shelf above the pocket is new and sharp-edged, and more of it will give way.

So the power turns its attention to the roots already in the cracks. It does not force them through the creature’s country. It helps them take hold farther back in the bank, where the clay is firm, and lets their fine branches knit slowly toward the break. A few grains still loosen and drift down. The leaves still fold against one another. There is no sudden change for anything living in the pocket.

The tap comes again. The creature closes its seam and lies still. This time the pebble holds.

In the hours that follow, one of the creature’s hairs catches a shed skin and draws it in. Another part of its body releases the unseen surface. It settles a little farther along, so slowly that a grain resting on its back keeps its place.

The power stays long enough to see that the roots have found their hold. Then it leaves the creature to its walls and to the dark water between them.
