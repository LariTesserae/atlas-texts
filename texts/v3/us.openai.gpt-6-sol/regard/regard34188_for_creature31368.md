The power would not begin by touching anything.

It would stand where the rail enters the chamber and wait through a full turn of the district’s work: the cart’s advance, the gate’s long hold, the shutters closing over the vents when water fell. It would wait through the dry interval too. A place that answered itself so carefully might feel an unexpected adjustment as an injury.

At last it would offer a question in the only language it had heard. When the next drop was due to strike the sleeper, it would catch the drop and hold it for one breath. The shutters would remain open. The gate would settle. The cart would wait beneath the hopper.

Then the power would let the drop fall.

It would do this no more than once. If the district made no answer beyond the one its workings required, the power would leave its rhythms alone. It would mend only what it could mend without asking: a crack in the rock above the hottest pipe, still too small for any valve to feel, which would otherwise open years from now.

But if, after the drop fell, the cart waited one breath longer than it needed to, the power would understand that something had listened.

It would not offer to carry the district away, or fill the chamber with daylight. The streets had been fitted closely for good reasons, and the darkness was part of how the heat was kept. Instead it would show, gently, what lay outside the opposite doorway: stone with room enough for one more gutter, a cooler shelf where water could rest, a ledge on which the thin stalks might spread.

The district could make its own way there, or not. The power would leave the doorway clear and go.

Long afterward, if a new pipe crossed that threshold, it would be by the district’s measure. If no pipe came, the crack above the hot metal would still be sound. And whenever rain reached the seam, the two sleepers would darken together, exactly as before.
