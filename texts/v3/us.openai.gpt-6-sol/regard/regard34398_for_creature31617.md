The power came to the basin expecting, at first, to find what was wrong.

It felt the heat beneath the shelves and the gas moving through the buried chambers. It watched a drop gather for most of an hour before a pale shell tipped toward it. Nothing was waiting for rescue. The roar did not frighten the creatures; it told them, through the stone, where they were.

So the power left the vents open. It did not bring soil to the bare shelves or quiet the ground. A softer, greener place would have buried the wet lips where the creatures fed.

It stayed until a traveler crossed the basin. Drawn by the sound, the traveler stepped toward an overhang, then stopped: in the shade below, a shell had drawn inward. The traveler crouched to look. For a moment, the small creature and the hand above it were still together. Then the traveler stood and took another way across the stone.

The power had done only that much. It had let the traveler notice what lived there before curiosity became a careless step.

Afterward the drop fell. A soft edge reached into the water it left behind, and withdrew. Beneath the basin, the great sound went on.
