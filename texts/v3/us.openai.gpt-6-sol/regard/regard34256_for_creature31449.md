The power would notice the smaller ones first. They are almost transparent, and each pump stroke carries them a little farther from the shelter of the roots. None has yet learned where to brace.

It would not stop the pump. The warm surge feeds the weeds, clears the silt, and brings food to the thing in the groove. Instead, the power would put a slight bend in the bronze pipe, no more than a thumb’s width from its mouth. The water would still arrive warm and on time, but it would enter along the ceramic wall before crossing the bowl. In the pipe’s shadow, the smallest creatures would find a place where the current could not lift them.

Then the power would look beyond the chamber. The gate’s stamped numbers had worn so faint that one day someone might mistake this opening for an unused one. When a keeper next passed over the works with a lamp and a list of repairs, the power would draw their eye to a thin line of rust at the inspection frame. The keeper would mark the window for sealing. Looking through it to judge the work, they would see the green fronds flatten under a pump stroke, and then rise.

They would wait to replace the pane until the young had grown. They would leave the groove in the iron floor untouched.

Afterward, the power would go. The chamber would still knock. The large one would still hurry home at the change in pressure, dark with filings, and come out again when the water eased. Beneath the weeds, the smaller ones would begin to gather filings of their own.
