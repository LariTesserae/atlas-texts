The power came at dawn, when the district was still wet and the first worker had not yet stepped onto the boards. It had come for the youngest willow on the river shelf. The tree had rooted where the gravel thinned, close enough to the new bank that its leaves trembled when the water pressed against the far side.

The power could have held the bank in place. It could have made the willow grow tall overnight, or kept the river from ever changing course again. Instead it stayed until the light strengthened and watched where the water went. A little escaped through the gravel hollows. Most ran safely past. At one bend, though, it slipped beneath the bank and carried away a pinch of earth.

Before the shift whistle, a worker crossing to the shops saw a wet mark on a stretch of gravel that should have been dry. It was small enough to miss. She stopped, tested the edge with her boot, and called someone over. They moved the walking boards farther back that morning. In the evening, after the machinery fell quiet, several people came with spades and set a row of stones where the water had begun to cut.

The power did no more to the river. It could not know every course it might take, and the people who lived beside it would need to notice its changes for themselves.

At the bridge, it drew one loose plank firmly against the next as a cart passed. The altered knock was plain to anyone accustomed to hearing it. The cart stopped; the plank was fastened down.

By dusk the willow still stood on uncertain ground. Its roots had a little more time to take hold. Behind it, swallows crossed the yard and beetles remained under the timber; ahead, the river went on testing its bank.
