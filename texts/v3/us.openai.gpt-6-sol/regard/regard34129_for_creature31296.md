The power did not bring light.

For a while it listened: to the wings overhead, to the scraping along the seams, to the deeper blow that made every small body pause. It watched a flat-shelled crawler cross three ridges for a fresh deposit and lose its place to another. It watched a hunter take a swimmer from the water. There was hunger here, but hunger was also how the basin stayed alive. The power left the hunter alone.

Far below the floor, a fallen slab had begun to turn in its passage. Each surge of water pressed it closer to a narrow throat. One day the slab would lodge there. The basin would not go dry at once. The seams would thin first; the winged creatures would gather over fewer troughs; the crawlers would scrape the same clean stone until there was nothing to find.

The power set a hand against the slab. It did not push water into the basin or widen the cracks that fed the pale film. It shifted the stone just enough that the next surge would carry it past the throat.

When the surge came, the familiar blow traveled underfoot. The animals fell quiet. Droplets struck the roof, then the ridges, and water threaded its way through the troughs. A crawler lifted itself out of the rising flow. A hunter stepped down toward it. Above them, the flock turned together in the dark.

No creature knew that anything had been done. The water still sank away. Some were fed, and some were eaten, and by the end of the long wet hours the stone was picked clean. But another surge could come after this one, and another after that. The power left the passage open and let the basin make its own sound.
