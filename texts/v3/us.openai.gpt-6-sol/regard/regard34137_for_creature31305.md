The power would not begin by making the crack gentle. The animals know its heat and narrowness. They know when to cling, when to hide, when to come out for what the wind brings. To cool the whole ridge or fill it with food would make a different place, and might leave no room for the lives already fitted to this one.

It would wait through several cycles of the knocking. Then it would notice what the animals cannot: behind the wall, the heated pocket has begun to press against a thin shelf of stone. One day soon the shelf will break across the holes where they shelter.

So it would open a pore on the far side of the ridge, no wider than a root. The gas would find that easier way out. The knocking would slow as it always had, but the shelf would hold.

For a while the animals would remain still, antennae tucked close. Grit would settle on their backs. Then one would feel its way out beneath a lip, and the others would follow when the stone cooled.

The power would leave the wind to bring what it brought. It would leave the ridge unfinished. But now the mountain could go on making its little interior without closing it, all at once, over those who lived there.
