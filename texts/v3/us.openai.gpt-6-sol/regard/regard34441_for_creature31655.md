The power would come first to the bank, because that is where the smallest life is holding on.

It would watch the creature draw water through its clear fringes. It would see the midge taken and the shrew pass overhead, and leave both alone. The hollow has room for them all, though not safety for any of them. Nor would it seal the split in the peat. The seep is what lets the creature live there.

But the hard strike travelling through the ground has another source. The power would follow it beyond the low ground, into the plant, where a mounting foot knocks against its frame each time a heavy machine cycles. On the next round, a mechanic would notice a little movement at the bolt. She would stop, look closer, and put in a repair. It would take time; someone would have to agree to the stop. The power would make the fault plain, not make their decision for them.

Afterward the engines would still run. The bass note would still reach the hollow. But the separate blow would cease, and the water between the sedges would no longer shiver before the sound came.

At dusk, the creature would work a grain of clay loose, carry it to the opening under the moss, and press it into place. The power would not ask to be known for this. It would stay long enough to see the seep run clear through the little door, then leave it to the creature.
