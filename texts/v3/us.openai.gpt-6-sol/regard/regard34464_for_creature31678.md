The power would come in a lull and wait for it to end.

At first it would want to quiet the towers. Their sound hurts the people who cross the bowl, and a sudden turn can send grit over the path faster than anyone can run. But the power would see the grazers gather at each change of pitch, the hunters waiting beyond the rim, and the trees opening their leaves in the wind the towers divide. It would leave the shafts alone.

It would go instead to the three-walled shelter. There, where people had scratched their waiting into the stone, it would press a hand against the back wall. The wall would grow a little deeper—not enough to roof it or shut out the sky, but enough that someone caught far from the rim could stand inside while grit passed across the entrance. The old scratches would remain.

Then it would set small, pale stones beside the path, no higher than the worn markers already there. On ordinary days they would seem to be part of the rock. When a tower began to turn, each would tremble against the sole of a foot a moment before its note changed. A stranger might not understand the warning at once. Anyone who knew the bowl could teach them.

The power would leave the trees their wind, the grazers their swift, dangerous crossings, and the colonies their narrow cracks. It would not try to make the plateau safe in every hour. It would give those who lived there one more moment to notice what was coming, and one better place to wait.
