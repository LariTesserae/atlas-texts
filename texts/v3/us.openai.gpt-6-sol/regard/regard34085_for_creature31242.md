The power came to the plain without a light. Light would have sent the animals running, and there was nowhere they needed to run.

It listened long enough to learn that the tearing in the reeds was not distress, that the cries in the crowns were often argument rather than fear, and that the ground’s low, intermittent groaning belonged to a place still becoming itself. It did not quiet any of these sounds.

Near dawn, rain began. Water gathered in the old hollows and ran down the black folds of stone. At one pool, the smaller animals came to turn stones, watched from the thicket by a hunter that had learned their habits. Farther off, a herd stepped through the reeds, leaving a passage that would serve other feet by nightfall. The power saw how much depended on what did not last.

It found a shallow hollow where rainwater was already slipping through a hairline crack. Beneath it, deep in the rock, one narrow edge had not quite settled against another. The power eased it into place.

The pool held for three days instead of one.

Frogs called there. Insects finished their change into winged things. A leaf-eater drank, and later a hunter drank from the same edge, finding only the deep prints the herd had left. The small animals discovered the pool on the second morning. They turned its stones, ate what they found, and went away without carrying the stones.

On the fourth day the water was gone. Nothing had been made permanent. The crack below still took the next rain, and the plain went on opening, closing, growing over, and being laid bare.

The power stayed a while longer. When no further touch seemed wise, it left.
