The power would come at evening, when the stone begins to give back its heat. It would see the first dull backs emerge from the cracks, and the birds turning overhead, and the thin lichen that feeds one life while the other feeds on it.

It would notice how little water the valley keeps. It might be tempted to fill the basins, deepen the cracks, make the shelf safe to cross. But more water would change where the grass grew. A safe shelf would change where the birds hunted. The creatures had lived here long enough to know the wind, the shadows, and the damp places better than any visitor could.

So the power would sit among the weather-rounded stones and watch.

A young creature would venture too far from a gully. At the pass of a bird’s shadow, it would flatten itself against the pale rock. The bird would turn once, then fly on toward the insects at the wall. After a long while, the creature would move again.

The power would leave the stone as it found it. By morning, the young one’s narrow feeding trail would be visible beside a hundred others: a little brightness where its mouth had passed through the lichen.
