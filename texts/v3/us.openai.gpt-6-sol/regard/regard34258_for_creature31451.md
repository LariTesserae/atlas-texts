The power would come at midday, when the light was already too dull to cast a startling shadow. It would wait. At first it would see only water leaving one stone lip for another. Then a shrimp would rise from the gravel, and a crab would close its claw, and the power would understand that the yard was not empty and did not need to be made beautiful.

It would listen beneath the pump house. The flywheel’s knock frightened the animals, but they knew what to do when it came. The dry pipe took nothing from them. One loose brick, though, had begun to settle against a seam where water rose. In time it would shut off the trickle feeding three shallow pools.

The power would lift that brick and set it on firm ground. It would take care not to crush the snail travelling beneath it.

It might do one other thing. Beyond the yard, where the water drained away through gravel, it would leave a narrow channel open so that a hard rain could pass without scouring the stone shelves. It would not make the pools deeper or clear away the rusted fins. The crabs hunted beneath those lips; the snails fed on the iron’s pale film.

Then it would go. Water would continue to rise through the seams. The flywheel would knock, and the animals nearest it would vanish first. A little later they would come out again, finding their routes still there.
