The power would listen first. It would hear the burrowers below the roots, the leaves chattering in the wind, and the faint knock of shells against a wet seam. It would see that the cold had shaped almost every kindness available here: moss on the grazers’ backs, bodies pressed together, water kept moving beneath the stone.

Then it would notice a young grazer left behind.

One of its six feet has slipped between two new blades of rock. The herd is advancing so slowly that, for a while, it still appears to be around the youngster. By evening there will be a gap. The foot is not badly hurt yet, but the animal cannot pull it free without twisting the leg.

The power would not split the ridge. That crack holds moisture; shell-backed creatures are waiting along its length. Instead it would ease one small stone beneath the trapped foot, scarcely more than a pebble, until the animal could bear its weight and step upward. The stone would settle again.

The youngster would limp. It would stop once to nose a polished leaf, then follow the pale backs ahead. When it reached them, another grazer would shift enough to make room.

The power might remain through the night. It would find no way to spare every creature on the plateau its hunger, its cold, or its death without taking away the lives bound to those things. But it had found one foot it could free. For now, that was what it would do.
