The power came in the hour when the pool was deepest. It could have made a spring there. It could have laid soil across the black stone and filled it with green. Instead it watched until evening, when the water went down and the clear threads drew themselves into the seams.

On the next hot day, one of the posts failed to open above the rising water. Silt had caught in a moving space and dried there. The pool climbed more slowly. At the farthest clump, the outer leaves did not lift.

The power did not touch the post. It held a patch of cloud between the sun and that part of the plateau for a little while. In the cooler air, the silt softened. The space opened; the caught grains fell into the pool. Water reached the leaves before evening.

Then the cloud moved on.

The power stayed long enough to see where the grains settled. A clear thread found them at dusk. By morning they were part of a small knot beside the shelter, and the post had closed cleanly behind them.

Nothing on the plateau knew it had been spared a difficult day. That seemed right. The power went away without marking the stone, leaving the posts to open and close, and everything in the cracks to make what life it could from the water they gave.
