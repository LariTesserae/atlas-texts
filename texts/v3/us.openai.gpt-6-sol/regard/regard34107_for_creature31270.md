The power would first learn what the people already knew.

It would follow a guide line through dark water with one hand, feel where a knot meant *home* and where another meant *turn*. It would watch a woman cut mineral crust from a binding without cutting the fiber beneath. It would listen at the square, where a visitor’s account of a distant rise passed from hand to hand. Only then would it touch the mountain.

A lower shelf was going to break. The change was still deep in the rock, too slow for anyone living on it to feel. There were three houses there, and a garden whose oldest ribbons had just begun to shade the doors.

The power could have made the shelf firm forever. But the stone beneath it was unfinished, and holding that one piece still would put strain elsewhere. Instead, it held the shelf for a little while and let a narrow crack open across the bare rock beside the nearest street. Warm water came through. By morning the crack had widened enough to catch a fingertip.

The household found it while tending its garden. They called their neighbors. Hands went along the ledge, testing places no one had thought to test the day before. At the square they decided to take the houses down.

They complained about the labor. They saved the poles. Children carried coils of binding up to a firmer shelf and, for once, tied no one’s ankles together. When the lower ledge finally slipped away, its walls were gone and most of its plants had been fastened above it.

The power let the rock fall.

Afterward, it did one more thing. Along the guide lines leading toward the empty rises, it made the stone’s faint movements perceptible where a line was tied to rock. A hand resting there could feel a steady pull, or the small, irregular tremor of a foothold beginning to fail. The people would have to learn what those feelings meant. Some would disagree. They would move a line, test it again, and tell each other what they had found.

The power did not build streets on the distant rises. It left that work to the hands already reaching toward them.
