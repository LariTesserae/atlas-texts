The power did not stop the root.

It watched the pale crest rise, and the grazers run over it, and the soft-bodied creatures work their way out from beneath it. It watched the shoots enter the new ground. To hold the valley still would have been to take away the ground it was making.

But it saw one of the wall-builders caught below the enclosure. The being had gone down a narrow passage to retrieve a blade. Now a root was lifting across the way back, and the stalks on either side were being pressed together. The blade was in one hand. With the other, the being felt along the wall, searching for a gap wide enough to pass through.

The power had not made these creatures. It did not know whether the being would trust a sign from it. So it made no voice, and put no thought into the being’s mind.

Instead, it let a little more light through one place in the porous ceiling. The warmth reached a joint in a stalk that had been locked in shadow. Slowly, the stalk bent. Behind it lay a passage the being could use.

The being saw the movement and drew back. Then it saw the gap. It slipped through with the blade held close against its body, while dust fell from the shifting leaves behind it.

That was enough for the day.

Later, when the root had risen and the noise had settled, the power turned its attention to the wall. There were ledges in the rock above it—too steep for a grazer to run along, too bare for the broad-bodied creatures, but within reach of careful hands. They had always been there. A little dust had hidden the lowest foothold.

The power sent a gentle current of warm gas across the stone. The dust lifted and drifted away. It would return; the roots would continue to rise. But a wall-builder looking uphill might now see another place to stand, and have time to decide whether to go there.
