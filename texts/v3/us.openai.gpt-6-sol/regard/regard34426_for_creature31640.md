The power would come to the town in a season when the clouds kept passing below it.

No rain fell on the roofs. Each morning the people went out to the bridges and watched the white banks move through the valleys, too low to touch a gutter. The cisterns were not empty, but their voices had changed. Children calling down the stairs heard their words return a little sooner.

The power could have lifted a cloud over the summit. Instead, it listened to the mountain.

Deep beneath the oldest streets, water was still finding its way through the stone. Much of it passed close to the cisterns without entering them. The power followed those narrow courses, feeling where a wall had shifted over centuries and where roots had opened a seam. It widened one passage no more than water itself might have widened it in a hundred years. A thin thread began to run into an unused basin.

For several days, nobody noticed. Then a child carrying a bucket stopped on the stair and called down. The answer came from a place that had been silent all her life. She called again, to be sure.

The keepers found the new trickle. They tested it, measured it, and argued over which old gate, if any, should be opened. They made a mark beside the waterline and returned the next morning to see how far it had risen. No one hurried to put the new water to work. They knew a seam could close as easily as it opened.

The power stayed long enough to hear their caution. It heard the tank tenders let a pump rest between strokes, though the mills could have used more water. It saw a worker kneel by a cistern with a lamp and wait until the pale fish came up, then lower the lamp without disturbing them. It left those chambers untouched.

By the time rain finally crossed the roofs again, the unused basin held a little water. The town built a narrow channel from it to the nearest storage tank, with a gate that could be shut by hand. The child who had heard the first change was allowed to call down from the stair before they opened it. This time her voice came back from farther away.

Afterward, the power did nothing more. It had given them a passage, not a promise of rain, and they knew how to listen for what happened next.
