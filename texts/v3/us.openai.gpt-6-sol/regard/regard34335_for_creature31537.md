The power would not open the plain to let in light. It would not bring water to roots that have never needed wet earth, or fill the empty streets with creatures merely because the streets are empty.

It would listen long enough to tell the wind in the tubes from the strain in a pillar. Then, where that pillar stood near giving way, it would let a buried crystal offer a little more purchase to the root pressed against it. The plant would thicken there at its own pace. The ceiling would still descend, but not because one support failed before the rest were ready to bear it.

It would leave the old cuts alone. They were made by someone, and the plant has lived with them; neither fact gives a visitor the right to preserve the town against its own growth. A leaf would still close a doorway. The square would slowly lose its edges. The thin breath of gas would still find the seam.

If the power returned after many years, it might rest its attention on the smooth place in the floor, then on the living strand inside a dry wall. It would know it had helped not by finding the town unchanged, but by finding the plant still able to grow through it.
