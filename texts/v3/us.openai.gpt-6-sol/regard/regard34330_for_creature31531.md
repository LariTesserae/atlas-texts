The power would come down through the clouds and wait at the rim until it could tell one motion from another: mist entering a crack, a root filling, a grazer moving with it beneath the moss.

It would notice the grazer caught by a winged hunter. It would feel the urge to intervene and let the hunter eat. Later it would watch a dark creature beside a pool miss its prey and go on waiting. Benevolence, here, could not mean sparing every life from every other life.

The power would walk the bare stone without leaving a track. Where the blue points appeared, it would kneel, not to make them brighter, but to understand how long each root held warmth before giving it back. It would find the old hollows full of ice and the new cracks taking water, and leave them as they were. Nothing in the basin had asked for a better design.

There was one thing it could do. At the far rim, where a way up from the lower mountain might someday be found, it would keep watch. If something came that could break the basin without needing it—something that would drain the seams or strip the moss from the rock—the power would meet it there.

For now, a bed lifted. A blue point shone through the leaves. Beneath them, a small pale animal fed, unaware of the watcher or the wings descending through the dark.
