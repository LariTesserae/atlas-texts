The power would come at dusk, when the grit began to lift, and wait.

It would feel the scrapers leaving the deep holes, the hunters taking their places, the young packed inside the old stone rings. It would understand that a scraper’s escape and a hunter’s meal could not both be assured. It would leave that passage to them.

Above one ring, though, a slab had begun to loosen. No animal had caused it, and none could move it. Another season of heat would send it down across the low wall. The power would put a hand to the slab and settle its weight into the rock behind it. There would be no flash, no sound greater than the click of a shell finding a new hold.

Then it would turn to the visitor at the entrance. Not to drive them away, but to let them feel, through the soles of their feet, how many small hooks held the stone before them. The visitor would stop reaching for a place to stand. They would back out slowly, while the cleft made room and closed again.

By full dark the cracks would glow, the mineral crusts would be warm, and hunger would still move quietly from ledge to ledge. The ring would hold.
