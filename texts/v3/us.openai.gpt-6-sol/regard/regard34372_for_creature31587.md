The power would wait through more than one flood.

It would learn that the thud is a warning, not a failure; that the standing pieces give light early enough for the last animal at the rim; that the water’s violence feeds what survives it. It would notice the dark rims on the old shell and understand that this arrangement has held for longer than any visitor could know.

Then it would look beyond the chamber, into the hidden workings. If everything there were sound, it would do nothing to the water. It would not smooth the channel, plant the bare stone, slow the surge, or lift the animals into some gentler home. A gentler home might starve them.

It would tend only what could fail without their knowing: clear grit from a distant bearing, ease a strain behind the ceiling seam, keep the warning note true. It would make those repairs so quietly that the next flood arrived on time.

Afterward, it would remain a little while. The water would drain. Drops would become audible again. From the cracks above the old waterline, the pale animals would emerge, carrying their smallest beneath them, and go to the rim.
