The power came to the plateau in rain. It had no reason to arrive as a figure, and so it did not. It paid attention first to the water: how long it stayed in the moss, which cracks carried it after a storm, where it rose warm from the stone.

For a while, it did nothing.

The grazers had enough to eat, though some would be caught. The hunter’s young were warm, though not all of them would live. The beetles fed at the seeps, and the birds found them. To spare every creature its danger would be to take away another creature’s life. The power watched long enough to understand that it could not make the plateau kinder by choosing winners.

Then, during a hard frost, a slab shifted in one of the sheltered clefts. It sealed the narrow outlet where warm water had risen for longer than any animal could remember. The ground cooled. Two young hunters, still in their thin coats, pressed against their mother in a hollow that no longer gave off heat.

The power could have warmed the hollow. Instead it went beneath it, into the broad crack where the water was gathering against the fallen stone. Farther down, a root had kept another passage open, though mineral crust had nearly closed its mouth. The power loosened that crust. Water came through slowly at first, carrying grit, then with its old, steady tremor.

By morning the hollow was wet and warm again. One of the young woke and moved away from its mother’s side to drink. On the lower shelf, water spread into moss that had been dry through the last several frosts. No new spring had been made. The water had found a course that the root and stone had almost left ready for it.

The power stayed until the mother returned from hunting. She paused at the cleft, feeling the familiar tremor through her feet, and went in. Then the power left the plateau to its rain, its hunger, and its many narrow paths.
