The power would not begin by stopping the pumps. It would listen to one through an entire turn, and to the water after the turn was done.

It would learn that a tender knows when a motor is failing by the beat through the floor. It would learn that the fish can live for a little while on the new banks, but not always long enough for water to find them. It would learn that no one walking between stations is afraid of the dark itself. They are afraid of losing a companion in a distance that changes without warning.

So it would do a small thing first.

The next time every living creature fell quiet, each tender out on the plain would feel a gentle pressure around the wrist that held a tool. Not a pull toward a station. Just the unmistakable touch of a hand holding fast. It would last until the water could be heard entering water again. Anyone who wished to stand still could stand still; anyone who needed to move could follow the cable. No one would be carried somewhere against their will.

At the stations, it would give the logbooks a way to keep what their columns could not. A tender opening a book after a changed walk would find the outward journey still there, in their own handwriting, beside the return journey. The hours would not agree. Neither account would be erased. In time the tenders might discover a pattern, or decide that some changes had none. The power would leave that work to them.

For the stranded fish, it would make the dry gravel hold a little rain between its stones. Not enough to soften the bank or keep it from sinking when its time came—only enough for gills to go on moving until a channel reached them, or a tender lifted them back into the shallows.

It would do nothing to the birds during the silence. Their stillness belonged to them.

Then it would remain, attentive but difficult to find. A wise power would know how easily help could become another force rearranging the plain. If the tenders wanted more, they could ask by knocking on a concrete stair before they climbed it. The power would learn their knocks, and answer, when it could, with one beat through the floor.
