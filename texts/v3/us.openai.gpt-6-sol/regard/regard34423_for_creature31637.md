The power came to the basin during rain, when the street was full of warm water and silver fish. From the rim it watched a woman wait beside a bridge that had not finished weaving itself. She had a basket on her arm. On the far bank, a bird was pulling at something in the mud. The bridge took its time; the woman let it.

The power could have made the basin safe from floods. It could have set every street in place and taught every bridge to hold. But the water fed the roots, and the roots had made a town in which a journey belonged to more than its traveler. So the power went down and learned the paths as everyone else did.

For three days it followed people clearing channels. It watched children find firm ground, and birds abandon a wall when its leaves opened too far. It listened beneath the creaking, where the roots pressed against old buried foundations. There it found one place the town had been unable to mend: a deep cut left by a flood many years before. Each wet season the roots reached across it, and each dry season the buried edges pulled apart. A bridge near that place was slower to form than the others. No one had asked it to hurry, but many had waited.

On the fourth night, the power laid a hand in the mud. It did not close the cut. It softened a little of the old stone beneath it, enough for fine roots to enter, and left the rest to them.

Years later, a keeper of the instruments noticed that pressure crossed that part of the mat differently. A cart passed without the little pause its driver had come to expect. The bridge still unwound behind it. The channel still opened after rain, and the fish came through.

No one knew why the change had happened. The woman with the basket, older now, sometimes waited there while a bird finished drinking. The bridge held together until she chose to cross.
