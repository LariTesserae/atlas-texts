The power would wait a while before doing anything.

From above, the mountain might seem unfinished: bare stone, thin water, almost no green. Up close, it is full of arrangements that have taken time to find. A crack holds grit. Grit holds a root. The root slows a drop. An insect survives beneath the drop’s dark edge, and a bird remembers where to look. The power would not lay soil over the plates or still the ice beneath them.

At dusk, it would notice one small bird left behind by the flock. The bird is not injured. It has kept searching a pool after the others rose, and now ice has joined the far rim to the near one. It taps at the sealed surface, then stands still, listening to wings it can no longer hear.

The power would touch the stone a little way downslope. Not enough to warm the ridge. Only enough to let a thread of water, already traveling under the ice, find an old seam. It would emerge beside the bird’s foot and run black and open for the length of a feather. The bird would drink. Under the lip of that seam, a few insects would have somewhere damp to endure the night.

Then the bird would lift into the cloud, calling once. Whether the flock answered would be for the flock to decide.

By morning the thread might freeze. When it thawed again, it would have worn the seam a little deeper. The power would leave it at that: one small opening through which the mountain could continue its own work.
