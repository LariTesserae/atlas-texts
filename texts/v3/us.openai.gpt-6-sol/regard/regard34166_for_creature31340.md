The power would wait.

It would be easy to mistake the dry reports for cries, or the leaning stones for a plea to save them. But it would feel the long pressure beneath the plateau and find no injury there. The movements were not trapped. They were making their way through stone at the only speed stone allowed.

So it would not quiet the fissures or hold down the rising ground. It would not make a garden in the shelter of the old wall. Water and roots might come someday; bringing them now would be an answer to a question no one here had asked.

It would go to the doorway and stand beside it while the ridge approached. The shelf would tilt. One gray stone would shift against another. For a moment the power would bear their weight, not to keep the doorway upright, but to let the stones settle without breaking. Then it would take its hand away.

By the time the ridge passed, the doorway might open onto higher ground. Beneath it, the slow movement would continue, scarcely altered. That would be enough.
