The power came without a light. Light would have helped a visitor and told the people who lived there nothing they could trust.

It listened from the stone beneath the paths: to the rivers, the engines, the small feet pressed against the service-house floors. For a long time it did nothing. Most of what moved on the plateau knew how to live with what moved beside it. A runner crossing a path was not an enemy. Neither was the thing in the shallows, though the power could feel its great weight passing through the water and could have followed it under the ledge.

Then the animals abandoned a housing while its bearing still sounded sound to human ears. The power heard what they had heard: a thin, irregular strike, buried between two ordinary beats. At the next rise of the river, the bearing would seize. Its housing would break toward the speaking tube where a crew member usually stood to call for tools.

The power did not stop the engine. Water backed up at a stopped station could take a different path across the marsh, and no one—not even this power, after all its listening—knew every place that path might go.

Instead, it loosened one pebble from the old paving outside the door. The pebble dropped into the warm groove beneath the route used by the cable-cart crews. Each turn of the engine now sent a small, distinct knock through that stretch of stone.

The first crew member to step off the cart stopped. She crouched in the rain and laid her palm flat. She listened for several turns, then called the others over. They found the empty nests, shut the intake, and opened the housing from the side.

Afterward, the power lifted the fallen handrail out of the deepening pool and laid it on dry rock. Someone finding it there could set it properly or carry it away. Until then, no hand searching for a root would follow it down into the water.

In the shallows, the large thing passed once more. The power felt its passage and let it pass. It had not come to make every sound on the plateau answer to a name.

When the crew left, the repaired station settled into its old uneven pounding. The frogs began calling again. Beneath the wet stems, the runners continued their slow work across the stone.
