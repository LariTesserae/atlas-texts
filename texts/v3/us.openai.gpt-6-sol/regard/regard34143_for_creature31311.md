The power came to the court before dawn, when there was something to see.

It could have filled the spiral with water. It could have warmed the clay or hushed the note behind the walls. Each would have been a gift by some measure, and each would have changed the place on which the rosettes depended. So it watched while the young ones made their slow descent and took their places around a dampness too thin to reflect the sky.

When the hour passed, the power understood one thing it could do. The film in the spiral need not vanish quite so soon.

It altered no tile. It brought no cloud over the sun. On a few mornings each year, when the night air already held enough moisture, it let the cool clay keep its dew for another minute or two. One more young rosette could arrive. Sometimes none did.

Above the court, people continued to pass. A worker once stopped at its lip with a stiff brush, meaning to clear the pale growth from the seams. The power did not turn the brush aside. It let the worker notice, just then, the delicate line of cleaned blue behind one rosette. The worker bent closer, put the brush away, and went on.

That was all. The note remained coarse and constant. The sunlight remained hard. By morning the spiral was dry, and the young ones were already making their way back up the wall.
