The power waits through a rain.

It hears the river deepen and feels the floor tremble. It sees that the dark line of seepage stops well short of the stones. The little animal remains in its seam, its pale throat moving steadily. When the rain passes, it comes out to stand in one of the pits.

Nothing here needs rescuing. The hollow has been a good shelter for longer than the salamander has lived.

Still, there is one place above the entrance where a root has worked loose in the bank. Soil falls from it a grain at a time. The power settles that soil around the root and leaves the river its passage. No stone shifts inside the hollow. The old shelf keeps its groove and its narrow space behind it.

By dusk, the work is finished. The salamander crosses the floor, pauses at the edge of the moss, and catches a small insect among the ferns. It does not know that anything has been done.

That is all right.
