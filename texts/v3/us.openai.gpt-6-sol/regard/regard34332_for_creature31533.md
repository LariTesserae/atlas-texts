The power would come expecting to find someone hidden in the sedge. It would wait until it understood that the hollow itself was the being: not one body, but many lives holding one another in place.

It would be careful where it stood.

There was no wound here that asked to be closed. The machines beyond the trees were loud, but their sound did not press the moss flat or empty the rainwater from its hollows. To silence them might trouble lives the power could not see from here. To bring back the river would wash away the intricate life that had grown in its absence.

So it would do little, and do that firmly. It would turn aside the first plan to drain the low ground or level it for storage. It would keep heavy wheels from the rim and let any future path pass elsewhere. It would leave rain free to arrive and depart at its own pace.

Then it would visit sometimes, quietly enough to notice whether the pressed sedge rose again. If it did, the power would leave without a sign. For this being, that would be a kindness: to have room to go on making room.
