The power would not drain the plain.

It would arrive during a clear interval and hold itself still beside the cut cable. There is too much life here, and too little certainty about where any one life ends, to begin by repairing things. It would watch the crease cross the stems against the current. When the engines struck together, it would watch for what did not return.

It would find one such place: a stand of narrow stems that went dark whenever a new slab rose beneath it. Most times light came back through the roots. Once, it did not. Loose leaves kept growing in the water nearby, but no pulse passed from them into the stand.

The power would ease its hand under the slab and hold it an inch above its next settling place. Not forever. Just through one reversal of the current.

Roots entered the gap. A faint light crossed from the loose leaves into the stems, stopped, and crossed again. The power waited until the roots had fastened to the fresh underside. Then it let the slab settle.

At the cut cable, it would be slower. The taut copper might be a wound, a tether, or part of a work it could not understand. It would not splice it to the nearest station simply because two ends appeared to belong together. Instead it would place a small, inert ring around the exposed tip, leaving the copper accessible but keeping it from striking the gantry when the water pulled hard. If the growth closed over the ring, it would leave it. If the ring troubled the rhythms, it would take it away.

After that, the power would return at intervals. It would learn which darkenings were pauses and which did not end. Where a slab settled too soon, it could sometimes hold it. Where a station’s broken casing cut a living cord, it could blunt the edge. It would do these things one at a time, then wait long enough to see what followed.

The engines would continue. New ridges would rise, and leaves would turn toward them. The plain would not become safe or orderly. But in a few places, when the water cleared, a pulse that might have stopped would still be traveling across the stems.
