The power watched for a long time.

It saw the dew gathered at dawn and taken by noon. It saw the rain leave in bright, sudden falls, and the lives below the rim that waited for those falls. It listened through the hollow trunks until it could distinguish a quarrel in a crown from a herd crossing miles away.

There were many things it could have changed. It could have called rain every evening. It could have made the weave as hard as stone. But the creatures that lived between its layers needed those layers to open, and the plants below the plateau needed the water that escaped it.

One afternoon, an old animal at the edge of a herd tested a hollow. The surface held beneath its front feet and gave way beneath its rear ones. It pulled free, but the young animal following it stepped onto the torn place before the herd had stopped.

For an instant, the power held the loose roots together.

That was all. The young animal found its footing and followed the others around the hollow. When their trembling had passed, the roots relaxed. The gap remained: dark, narrow, and easy to miss.

At dusk, two people came along the ridge. One put a foot toward the crossing and felt the mat yield. They knelt, looked into the gap, and laid a flat stone on the firmer weave beside it. It marked a way around, not a lid over the hollow. Insects would still descend there. Rain would still find the seam.

The power stayed until morning. Dew beaded on the upright fibers and gathered, drop by drop, in the clay bowls beneath the tree. No more than usual. When the people came to empty them, they noticed the new stone and followed the safer path.
