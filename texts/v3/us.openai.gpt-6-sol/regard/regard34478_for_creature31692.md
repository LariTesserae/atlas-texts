The power would spend a long time watching. At first it might think the city was unfinished and be tempted to fill its vacant plots. Then a drop would fall, and one black edge would hold while the rest of the water vanished. It would see how little was enough.

It would not bring a spring into the chamber. It would not steady the heat or close the cracks against the cold. The inhabitants live in the meeting of those things, and a kindness offered too generously could erase the meeting altogether.

Outside the slit, where the ridge turns the wind upward, the power would loosen one sharp flake of stone. It would settle at an angle above the entrance, too small to be noticed from below. On cool nights, air passing over it would shed a little more moisture into the descending thread. Not every night, and never enough to make a stream: perhaps one additional drop by morning.

It would also leave the loose stone on either side of the slit alone. A fall might close the chamber someday, but sealing every fracture now would change the draft. The power would remember the place and return after storms. If a boulder came to rest across the entrance, it would lift it away. If the ridge remained as it was, it would do nothing.

Years later, the extra drops might have left no mark one could distinguish from the city’s own work. A few pale grains might have found warm cracks. A basin might hold its darkness a moment longer. The power would not need to know which changes were its doing.

When the heat deep below began to wander, as heat does, it would watch for another newly opened seam nearby. There might be one, or there might not. It would not build the inhabitants a second city. But if a cooling draft could carry their dust toward warmth, it would let the draft through.
