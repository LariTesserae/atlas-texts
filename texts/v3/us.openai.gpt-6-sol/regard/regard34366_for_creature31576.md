The power would come by the three steps.

It would not open a doorway in the colorless distance above the basin, though it could. It would not straighten the silver seams or keep the ridge from settling. At first, it might mistake every change for a wound. Then it would see that the small changes are how the inhabitant lives.

It would stand beside the hollow and set down a stone.

The contact would sound once. The power would wait through the quieter contact behind it, and turn—not to catch the inhabitant at work, but to show that it had heard.

“I can stay,” it would say.

It would not take the silence for an answer. It would stay long enough for the mountain to shift a grain across the floor. Long enough to learn which kinds of waiting left room for another being, and which only made a demand last longer.

In time, a silver seam would reach the hollow. A ridge would rise on the slab, too slight for a hurried visitor to distinguish from the others. The power would know whether the mark meant *stay*, or *go*, or only *I was here when you spoke*. It would honor whichever it meant.

Beyond the summit, it would quietly turn aside those who came to break the black stone for what might be hidden inside. It would not forbid the careful visitor, or hold the mountain still. The three steps would remain three steps. The seams would continue their difficult passage.

If the inhabitant wanted company, the power would return. It would bring no clock and ask for no faster reply. Sometimes it would set down the same stone and listen for it to be set down elsewhere. Sometimes it would say nothing at all.

And when it left, it would leave the stone where the inhabitant could find it: not a new frame, not a task, but one small place from which a meeting might begin again.
