The power would not begin by bringing birds.

It would stand at the edge of the pool and listen until it could hear the separate voices within the roar. The tower and its two gardens. The stairs whose steps lengthened under a climber’s feet. The quiet places that had made a street for a stranger, then waited to see whether the stranger wished to stay.

It would understand why the leaves did not move. To these beings, letting a leaf drift out of sight might feel like losing the day it fell, the tree that held it, and everyone who had passed beneath it. Their care had become so complete that there was little room left for anything else to live.

So the power would ask them nothing at first. It would place one drop of its own water on the rim of the pool, where all of them could hear it settle. Then it would let the drop evaporate.

The pool would remember the drop. The stone would remember its warmth. But neither would be asked to keep it there.

For a long time, nothing would happen.

Then, in a distant gutter, one fallen leaf would turn over. The water would carry it the length of a hand and leave it caught against a root. No street would close around the loss. No garden would become less itself. The residents would listen to the place where the leaf had been, and to the place where it now lay.

The power would stay as long as they wanted company. It would not fill the cisterns with fish or set young leaves on the trees. If, someday, the residents asked how to welcome such lives, it would help them make places where things could arrive, grow, depart, and be missed.

Until then, it would keep its hand beside the pool, not in it. The mountain was theirs. The next small movement should be theirs too.
