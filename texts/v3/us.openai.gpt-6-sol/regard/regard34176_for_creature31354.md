The power comes at dusk and does not at first understand what it is looking at.

It could see every thread beneath the clay at once. Instead, it stands beside one pool and listens to a note so low it feels it through the soles of its feet. When the note changes, it waits. The change might be a greeting, or the passage of rain far off, or a stone settling into its hollow. The power has been welcomed in many places and mistaken for weather in others. It knows better than to decide too quickly.

It returns through several rains. It learns that the pools answer one another without always agreeing. A tone may travel along a ridge and find no continuation on the other side. Sometimes the water falls silent around a newly exposed root, while the threads work there for days. The power begins to recognize that silence as attention.

One evening, a bank gives way near the weeping stones. Silt pours into a narrow channel. Beneath it, a mesh of threads parts; a few ends catch in the falling clay, and others draw back toward wetter ground. The power could put the bank where it was. It could bind every ridge against erosion.

It does neither.

A stone is coming down with the silt, heavy enough to crush the threads where they are gathering. The power holds it still for the time it takes a raindrop to reach the pool below. The threads slip around its edge. Then the stone falls and comes to rest in the new channel, where water begins, already, to find a way past it.

For a while the nearby pools make no sound. The power cannot tell whether its help was wanted. It leaves the changed bank alone.

By morning, a thin strand has grown across the stone’s wet face. Water gathers there, bright in a crack the power had not noticed. Farther off, a low note passes from pool to pool. It is not quite the note that sounded before the bank fell.

The power listens until the rain overtakes it. Then it goes, leaving the marsh its new channel, its old work, and the time to decide what to make of both.
