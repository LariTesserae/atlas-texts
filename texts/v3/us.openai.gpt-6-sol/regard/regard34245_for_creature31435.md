The power does not begin with the broken walls.

It spends a long time learning what uses them. It watches sand gather against the eastern wall when the water runs west, and watches the wall-dwellers come out after a cold current to comb through what has gathered. It follows one along a channel that takes a few strokes in one direction and much longer in the other. When the animal pauses at the mouth and feels the stone before entering, the power understands that the delay is not, by itself, a thing to mend.

It leaves the distances as they are. It leaves the broad shadow to its passage over the rim. It does not raise the walls or bring the surface closer. These would be changes too large to make on the strength of a first impression.

Then a hard current lifts the loose stone from the eastern wall. It rolls across bare rock, with a wall-dweller following it on its many small limbs. At the edge of an old channel, the stone begins to slip into the faster water.

The power turns it once. No more than a careful hand might, if a hand could have reached it. The stone catches against the lip of the channel.

The wall-dweller arrives, rests its foremost limbs on the stone, and waits while the current passes. Then it grips the piece and carries it home. By the time it reaches the enclosure, sand has begun to collect where the stone was missing.

The power stays a while longer. The next current bends the pale fans flat; the next one lets them rise. Beneath them, the small clear creatures resume feeding. Nothing on the plateau needs to know it has been visited.
