The power does not quiet the mountain. Its note is part of what holds the thickets alive, and perhaps part of how the stone will learn its shape. Nor does it pull the blade from the seam. The listener has judged that well.

Instead, it comes at the shelter while the listener sleeps and sets one small thing beside the grinding stone: a pebble, smooth as if carried a long way by water. When the listener wakes, the pebble is warm. It stays warm until the next sleep, no matter how many times they wake between. After that it cools, and slowly warms again.

The listener turns it over, looking for a crack that might explain it. Then they make a mark on the wall. The next time they wake and find the pebble still warm, they leave the blade where it is.

For a while, that is all the power does.

The listener begins to carry the pebble to the cliff. They set it on a flat chip while they watch the green tip in the seam. Once, as dust falls into their cupped hand, the great note swells so suddenly that they cannot tell whether the stone is moving faster or their own heart is beating harder. The pebble rests against their knee, warm and unchanged. They wait. The note passes.

Much later, when the seam has opened enough that the trapped blade can no longer reach both sides, the listener cuts it at its flattened base. They grind the silver rings, scatter the grit into the new fracture, and watch for a curl. One appears on each side.

The power does not show itself to praise them. On the walk home, though, the listener finds that the loose chips have been cleared from the footholds ahead. They stop and look back along the path, puzzled. Then they put the pebble in the bowl for the night, where it can be found easily in the morning.
