The power would not put a hand through the roof. A hand, even a gentle one, would be an earthquake here.

It would begin with the bead of water. It would let the young one drink, and let the bead grow smaller.

For a while this would seem like a loss. Then moisture from the wider earth would find its way along the crack, and another bead would gather. The root tips would lengthen by a measure too small to see. The clear creatures would take their fill, and the roots would go on growing.

The power would loosen one wall, grain by grain, into unoccupied ground. Not enough to bring it down; enough that the beetles could scrape a new path, and the worker could turn without brushing her rough back against the clay. It would leave the sealed cells alone. She had made them carefully.

At first she would be frightened by the change. A dent in a cell would remain. A crack would widen overnight. One of her young would press against its cap, and this time, when she put her head to it, she would hear something she had never heard before: a small jaw working steadily from within.

She would help only when the cap began to give.

There would be more room, but not endless room. Water would sometimes run, and sometimes be scarce. The worker’s jaws would not become new. The power could spare them the cruelty of being held forever at the edge of living, but it could not make a life without edges.

When the first young one emerged, it would stand trembling in the milk-white light. Its shadow would move across the wall. The worker would touch it once with her antennae, then turn aside so it could find the passage upward.
