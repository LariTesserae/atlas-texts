The power would not fill the six stalls.

It would come when the streets were empty and listen. The vents would sound one note in the wind, then another; beneath them, the slow drip would go on striking its jar. The power would follow that water through the stone. It would learn which seams had fed the cisterns all season, and which new fracture might carry the next hard rain under a foundation.

At the thin strip of earth where roots lay against pale rock, it would press nothing shut. The roots held more of the slope than anyone could see. Instead, it would draw a little of the water away from the loose foundation blocks and toward the vacant plot. The seedlings there would have a better chance; the wall, when it rose, would stand on drier ground.

The change would be small enough to notice only through work. One morning the jar beneath the wall would fill more slowly. Someone would follow the damp line, find water gathering where the house stakes stood, and move the stakes a few paces uphill. They might decide to plant there instead. The power would let that be their decision.

It would do one more thing before leaving. In the first fierce wind of autumn, when a shutter came loose above the upper stair, its wooden peg would hold until the person carrying stems below had passed. Then the shutter would fall on empty steps with a crack that brought everyone running.

They would repair it themselves, with a stronger peg. For years afterward, the wind would still make its uneven music through the vents. The jar would still need emptying. The town would grow at the pace of the hands that lived there, with one sound less for them to fear.
