The power would wait.

From the entrance, the chamber sounds like one creature making one endless noise. Inside, it would listen until it could hear the pauses: a caller yielding to another, a soft-backed young one tapping from the roots, a traveler testing a seam before it crossed the sharp floor. It would learn that the steadiness of the place is made by lives taking turns.

It would not bring rain. Water would spoil the dry shelter and change what the roots draw from the stone. It would not quiet the rattle, though it is loud. The creatures are speaking to one another.

Near the black bowl, it would find a young shell-backed creature trapped beneath a fallen chip. Its shell has not hardened enough to bear the weight. The power would lift the chip and set it down a little way off. The creature would remain still for a time, then make its unsteady way toward the roots.

That evening, the power would ease a few loose fragments away from the narrow passages the young use. It would touch nothing else. The old shelter could remain a shelter for whoever fitted inside it now; the pegs and bowl could keep their unanswered history.

Before leaving, it would listen once more. The rattle would go on, but not quite unchanged: one small caller, newly safe beneath a leaf, would have found its place in it.
