The power came at dusk, when the birds and bats crossed in the narrow air. It waited until the last wings had passed before descending to the floor.

There was no empty place to stand. Beetles moved through the grit; a spider held a line between two ledges; a frog watched a rain cup whose water had sunk below its reach. The power could have filled every hollow, softened every hard edge, made the crack green and quiet. It understood that the bats needed the upper walls, the birds needed their quarrelsome perches, and many of the small lives below depended on what fell from both. A green, quiet crack would be somewhere else.

For a while it only listened. A drop struck stone. Far above, a bird called once from outside, and the sound came back thinner.

Then the power touched a loose pebble beside the frog’s cup. It moved the pebble no farther than a beetle could have pushed it. The next drop from the wall struck its rounded side and ran into the hollow instead of disappearing into the grit. The frog did not move. It would have a little longer there.

The power left the other cups as they were. It left the spider hungry and the cricket wary. Before departing, it looked up through the pale seam and saw, on the open mountain, a line of boot prints leading toward the rim. It laid a faint warmth along a safer path of stone, enough that a cold walker might choose it without knowing why.

By nightfall the crack was loud again. Nothing in it knew it had been visited.
