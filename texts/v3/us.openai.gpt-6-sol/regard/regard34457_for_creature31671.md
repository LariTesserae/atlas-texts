The power would come in the hour when the stone was hottest, when even the lizards held their mouths open and the fern tips had begun to curl.

It would see how little water the hollow kept, and how many lives depended on that little. It would see, too, that the posts still did their work. When one sounded, a few beads gathered on its dark surface. The small creature came out from beneath a root, touched them with its narrow mouth, and followed them toward the ferns.

The power would not call down a lasting rain. Water spread across the bare stone would wash beetles from their shelter, fill the lizards’ hunting rims, and make the hollow into a different place.

Instead, where the shallow cut passed a crack no wider than a hair, it would ease one grain of stone aside. The next beads would still travel slowly enough for the small creature to drink. A few more would reach the fern roots before the heat took them.

Then the power would wait through a full turning of the light. The ferns would hold. Flies would return, and birds after them; the lizards would keep their rims. When the low call rose from below the ridge, everything would fall silent for a breath, as it always had.

The power would leave the hollow to its noise, its thirst, and the water now finding its way through.
