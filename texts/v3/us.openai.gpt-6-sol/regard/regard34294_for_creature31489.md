The power would come to the edge of the hollow and wait.

It would see the moths under the fronds and the birds pressed together on the branch. It would see, too, the spaces between them: a seam just wide enough for a beetle, a cup of water that would hold one frog but not two. There was life everywhere, but no unused place in which to set a gift.

It could widen the opening overhead. More light would reach the stone, and some of what lived here would flourish. Other things would lose the darkness they depended on. It could make the seep run faster, but water already reached every root that knew how to find it. It could warm the air, or spare each small creature from the creature waiting beside it. Soon the hollow would belong to neither of them.

The bird on the shelf worked its twig into the seam. After a while it drew something out and swallowed. The power watched without helping. The bird knew the seam better than it did.

So it left the water to find its slow way through the rock. It left the opening narrow and the twig beneath the bird’s foot. When it withdrew, no frond stirred.
