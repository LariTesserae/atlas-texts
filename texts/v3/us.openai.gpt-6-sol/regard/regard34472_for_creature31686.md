The power came to the basin in the afternoon, when cloud had taken the other ridges away. It felt the rain sinking through stone, the pressure beneath it, the hot water rising by paths older than the trees. It felt, too, a frog waiting where two currents met, and the heavy browser asleep in a hollow of ferns.

There was much it could have changed. It could have cooled the spring and made every bank safe. It could have held the channels in place so that no nest was flooded and no root lost its purchase. But the films on the mineral lips lived by the heat. The frogs lived where warmth met cold. Even the trees had grown into the rock’s slow unsettledness. To make the basin safe from change would be to take the basin away.

So the power stayed and listened.

Near evening, it heard a blockage deep in one narrow channel: fallen stone wedged against stone, with hot water gathering behind it. The pressure would break through before dawn. In its path lay the rain-fed hollow where the browser slept. The animal might wake in time; it might not.

The power did no more than ease one pebble from the blockage. Water found the opening and began to run through it, first a thread, then a small, steady stream. Steam rose among the ferns. A frog slipped beneath a root. The browser lifted its head at the unfamiliar sound and, after a while, got to its feet. It climbed out of the hollow and went quietly into the trees.

By morning, the new channel had laid a pale line across the dark rock. A few larvae had been carried downstream. Others held fast beneath the leaves. Rain was already washing silt into the opening.

The power did not stay to keep its little channel clear. It left the water to find its way, and the lives beside it to find theirs.
