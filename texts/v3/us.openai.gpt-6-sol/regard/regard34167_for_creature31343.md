The power would come to the rim and wait through a full cycle of the hum.

At first it might mistake the bare stone for a lack: room where another frond could grow, another dark edge could begin. But it would watch a leaf turn toward a seam that had not yet opened, and watch the darkness beside it gather only after the three clicks. It would see how much of this life depends on an interval—on not being touched too soon.

It would not fill the hollow. It would not cool the stone or draw the hidden body into a shape it could recognize. Even a kindness laid across those few paces might become a wall.

There is one thing it would do. Where loose rock above the rim is poised to fall, it would settle the pieces into a place where they could weather without tumbling into the hollow. No mark would show from below. The sun would still be white, the floor still hot, the seams free to open at their own pace.

Then it would leave. It would remember the place, but not return to count what lived there. If the hollow grew wide enough, perhaps one dark edge would someday reach another. If it did not, the distance between them would remain theirs.
