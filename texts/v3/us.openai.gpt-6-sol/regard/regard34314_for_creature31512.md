The power would not come down through the clouds. It would not cut a path to see what lived beneath them, or bring birds to a place that had made no room for birds.

It would wait through many seasons of rain. From above, the highland might seem to turn its leaves all at once. Beneath the cover, the power would learn how slowly that answer traveled: chamber to chamber, through old leaves that still carried water and new stems that could not yet bear weight. It would learn, too, where the rising stone pressed hardest against the living layer, and where the layer pressed hardest back.

Then, deep under one inland hollow, it would make a small change. Not a cavern, not a channel that stole the water, but a little more give in the young rock. When the wet cover grew heavy, the stone there would settle by grains instead of breaking in a single slip.

The plants would receive no message. They would grow across the hollow, fill its gaps, and guide the next rain where it was needed. They might never distinguish the changed stone from any other part of the height.

The power would leave it so. A thing that answers its own drought across such distances need not be taught to live. It may still be helped where it cannot reach.
