The power does not mend the roof.

For a while it considers doing so. It could close every white crack and bind the ice into a single unbroken vault. But the plants have grown toward those cracks. The small pale creatures feed on the faces of new breaks. Water reaches the floor where the roof gives a little. To make the hollow perfectly still would be to change it into another place, and perhaps one in which nothing here could live.

So it listens.

There is one crack whose sound has deepened over several days. Beneath it is a dependable pool. The woven screen is still there, and fresh marks in the litter show that people have come for water. Above it, a slab has caught in the fans of a great stem. It will fall soon.

The power does not fasten the slab in place. It lets a narrow fracture run through it at dawn, when the pool is empty. The sound travels farther than such a small break ought to travel. The pale creatures vanish into their joints. The long-bodied animal lets go of the curtain it was crossing and climbs back toward the stem. Far away, people filling their cups at another drip hear the report and wait.

The slab falls in two pieces instead of one. Branches bend and break; leaves come down. One piece lands beside the pool and drives stones into the litter. The other remains caught above, where its weight no longer bears on the stem below. By the time the people arrive, the air is still. They lift their screen from under the fallen leaves and see what happened.

The pool has moved. Water now follows the edge of the fallen ice before dripping into a shallow place a few steps away. It will take them time to learn this new place. The animal will find it first.

That is all the power does. It does not leave a sign, or a safe road through the plants. It cannot give the hollow a season without change and still leave it alive. When the people return, they set their screen beneath the new drip. One of them looks up for a long time before placing the first stone.
