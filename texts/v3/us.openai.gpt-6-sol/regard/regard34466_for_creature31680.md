The power would not finish the town.

It could give the buildings roofs, fill the square with water, make a road across the powder. Each gift would arrive too quickly for the lives already there. Water might drown the threads. A road might carry a visitor’s weight through the clay and break the chambers by which the basin breathes. Even a roof would take from the black tufts the fresh edges where they grow.

So it would wait through a dimming and watch.

Near the square, the bowed wall would advance toward its opposite. Beneath it, a scraper would wake and begin to cross the narrowing passage. The power could hold the wall back. Instead, it would ease a trace of gas through a deeper crack, where a buried colony had been turning toward it already. That colony would lay its next sheet a little farther down. The bowing wall would continue, but an opening would remain at its foot.

The scraper might find the opening. It might not. Other small lives would find it later. Air would pass through, carrying the mineral powder in and out across the threshold. In time the colony might close it after all.

The power would leave the seam unmarked. No sign would tell anyone that a choice had been made there. Then it would turn its attention to the empty expanse beyond the wall, and keep it empty a while longer.
