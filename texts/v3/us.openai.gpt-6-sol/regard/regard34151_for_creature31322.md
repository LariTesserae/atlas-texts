The power would not make an entrance.

It would attend first to the floor. For a while it might seem that the chamber was in danger of closing: the pale grains binding, the soft mouth resting below them, the mites finding fewer ways through. But the mouth would wake, and the mites would worry at the crust, and the floor would become rough again. The power would leave it to them.

Nor would it take the grub from its cup. The grub could leave. The builders raised their narrow rings around an empty place as readily as a full one. Whatever passed between them was still being made, like the cups themselves.

It would stay through a rain. It would watch a ceiling drop show the grub crossing the floor, and then watch the grub remain curled and still. It would not try to make the drop tell the truth. The builders had never needed it to.

Only near the end of the rain would it act. A bead would gather on the root, heavy enough to fall onto the dark pinch of soil. At the same time, a loose grain would catch against one of the new shoots, holding its tip in the direct light where it could not grow. The power would move the grain no farther than the next crack.

The bead would fall. The shoot would remain still until the light was veiled again. Then, very slowly, it would lift.

No one in the chamber would know what had happened. By morning the grain might be bound into stone, or eaten from below. The power would not come back to check which.
