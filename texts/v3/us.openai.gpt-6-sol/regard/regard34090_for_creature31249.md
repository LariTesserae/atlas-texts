The power did not stop the ground from growing.

It watched the masons leave room beneath their walls, the children find their way by sound, the crews count one another at dusk. This was not a city failing to hold still. It was a city learning how to live beside something that had only lately begun to exist.

Out beyond the paving stones, the power found the source of the whistle. It had no mouth. The sound came when a long seam in the new height opened a little and air passed through it toward the city. The seam was not calling for anyone. It was finding out what it could do.

So the power began with a small kindness. On evenings when distance stretched without warning, the survey stakes shone faintly—not enough to light a road, but enough that a worker could count them on the way home. Where a voice seemed near and its owner was far off, the air carried a second sound: the scrape of boots on gravel, arriving from the true direction. The crews still counted one another. They simply had better odds of counting everyone.

It did nothing to the extra steps. It did not make the measurements agree. Such help would have made the city easier to govern and harder to understand.

At the cisterns, it let a few drops escape each night into the shallow dishes. Along the cliffs, it eased open narrow cracks, slowly enough that no ledge broke beneath a nesting bird. The birds found them in their own time. The workers found the damp dishes and repaired the pipes around them, leaving those particular drops alone.

Last, the power went to the seam and stayed through a long interval of silence. The air moved; the height gave its strange whistle. Across the plateau, a foundry answered with its shift call. Then another sounded, farther away.

For the first time, the whistle from the new ground followed the others at the same interval.

The power could not tell whether the height had meant to answer. It thought there was time to find out.
