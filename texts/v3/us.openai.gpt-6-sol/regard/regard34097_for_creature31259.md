The power would wait through a full cycle of the room: the hot hour, the cooler hour, the fall of water, the click of the relay. Nothing here is asking to be rescued. The roots are growing toward water; the pale threads are growing where they can survive. Even the fracture, though new, has not moved while anyone watched.

Then it would learn why the slab had split.

It would follow the heat below the concrete and the strain through the walls. If a pipe were failing, it would mend the pipe. If the ground were shifting, it would brace the chamber from outside, where a repair need not grind through the narrow inhabited space beneath the floor. It would leave the crack open unless the crack threatened to become a collapse. The root tips have only just found it.

It would turn the needle back from its stop. It would give the condenser a way to shed its heat without baking the roots dry or stopping the cool hour in which water reaches them. Above, it might place a guard around the broken lips of concrete, so that anyone entering with a lamp would not put a foot through them. Nothing more conspicuous.

The next drop would gather under the pipe and fall. Some of it would run into the fracture. The growth below would thicken by an amount too small to see, and the roots would advance into a space that was, for now, safe enough.

The power would not make the room a garden. It would keep it from becoming a grave by accident, then leave the living things to make what they could of it.
