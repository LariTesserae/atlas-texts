The power would not stop the turning.

It would feel the lives that had grown around it: the pale shells opening at a familiar click, the hunters using the narrow violence of the seams, the blind creatures waiting where the stone spoke loudest. Silence would not be mercy here. Nor would an endless supply of food, or a plain made safe from teeth.

It would wait for one of the long quiet intervals. Then, far beneath the channels, it would put a hand against a piece of shale that had begun to catch. Not to hold the disks still—only to ease that one edge past another.

The turning would resume with a sound the animals knew.

Over the following rains, the change would become visible. A low ridge would lift where the worn basins had nearly joined the deepest channel. Water would still cross it, and hunters would find their way through, but the current would no longer take every loosened shell down into the stone. In its lee, shallow basins would gather silt. The pale animals would settle there. Some would be eaten. Some would not.

The blind creatures would come last. One would follow the old thud to a fissure and find it quieter than before. It would hang there, mouth open, while warm water rose somewhere to its side. After a while it would turn.

The power would leave it that discovery. It would not teach the creature where to go, and it would not make the ridge permanent. The rain and the buried stone would wear it down in time. But for now there would be another passage through the plain, and another place to wait when the turning faltered.
