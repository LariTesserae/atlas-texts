The power would not begin by touching the plateau.

For a while it would stand below the vaults, where the warm air comes down through the living wood, and listen. The clear knocking from the pools would travel through the trunks; the slower pulses would follow. Eventually it would hear something the place itself could not: beyond the surrounding country, a river was beginning to change course. In a few seasons, less water would reach the roots beneath the plateau. Nothing here showed the loss yet. Every pool held its level.

The power would be tempted to fill the pools against that future. It could leave them brimming for a hundred years. But the small builders worked at the edges of water as it moved, and the roots reached where a gap allowed them to reach. A level that never changed by so much as a leaf’s width might keep the meadow green while quietly making it less alive.

So it would go to the river.

Far upstream, where a fallen tree had caught silt and turned a side channel away from the plateau’s country, the power would move neither tree nor river. It would loosen a bank of packed clay with rain. At the next high water, some of the current would find its old way through. Enough would continue along the new channel for the creatures that had begun to live there. Enough would return to reach the plateau in a dry season.

Then the power would come back and wait beside one of the unfinished humps. It would watch a small, flat-backed builder press fibers into a seam, and a bird take a clear swimmer from the water. It would not save the swimmer or guide the builder’s hands. By evening, the seam would hold, the bird would be fed, and a few beads beneath the rail would finally fall.

There was no need to make the plateau greater. When rain reached it, it would decide where to grow.
