The power would not widen the square or soften the steps. It would not fill the spaces beside the houses with trees. Those spaces are used, and the people know where to put their feet.

It would wait for rain.

While water crossed the paving, it would make a small hollow deep in the stone beneath the basin, with a narrow passage leading back to its floor. The basin would empty as it always had. The channels would still need clearing, and afterward the square would still look dry. But some of the rain would remain below, cool and out of the sun.

A person scraping silt from the basin would find the passage with the tip of a tool. The townspeople would decide whether to open it. If they did, they could lift a stone, lower a cup, and cover the opening again. There would be enough water to ease the long carry up the paths during a dry spell, not enough to make them forget where rain came from.

The power would leave the rest alone. A shutter would still tap against stone. Someone would still have to choose a block that fit an old wall. And when rain came, the sound of it running through the square would still be worth stopping to hear.
