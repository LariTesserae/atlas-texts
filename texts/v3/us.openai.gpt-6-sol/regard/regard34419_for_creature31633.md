The power came down through a crack in the roof and, for a while, mistook the noise for distress. The wind roared. Birds cried from broken walls. Something small rattled inside a seed case. It listened long enough to hear each sound begin and end, and did not quiet any of them.

It could have brought water. It could have filled the streets with green and raised the fallen roofs. But the plants had made their lives in dryness, and the animals knew which walls held shade, which doorways kept their young cool. A swift kindness would leave many of them with nowhere to live.

Near one doorway, a slab had begun to slip. Each time the wind rose, grit sifted from beneath it. In the room behind it lay three young, gray-backed animals, asleep in the dust. The power held the slab through the next gale. When the young had grown and followed their mother out, it let the stone settle. It came down slowly, against another fallen wall, leaving the doorway open and a deeper patch of shade beside it.

Then the power withdrew. The wind still crossed the hollow. Dust gathered on the new face of the stone. In time, a lizard found a seam there and disappeared into it.
