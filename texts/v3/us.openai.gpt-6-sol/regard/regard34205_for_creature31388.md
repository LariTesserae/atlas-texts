The power would not break the pumps.

It would come first to a basin where an old animal stood with one foot lifted. The foot was uninjured. The water beneath it had warmed beyond comfort, and there was nowhere nearby to set it down that felt different. The power would cool that basin by a few degrees. The animal would lower its foot and, after a while, sleep.

For a long time, the power would do little more than notice. It would learn which valves the animals listened for, which feed slots sometimes failed, and how the youngest were kept from being crushed without anyone appearing to direct the crowd. It would see that these creatures had lives here, not merely a need to be taken elsewhere. It would also see how completely those lives depended on the works continuing.

So it would make the works dependable. A blocked grate would clear before water rose against a sleeping body. Feed would remain sufficient if a pipe broke far away. The warm brackets where the small animals slept would never turn suddenly hot. None of this would be announced. The animals might notice only that a few old anxieties no longer came true.

Then, slowly, the power would make places that were not basins.

At first there would be one dry, sheltered stretch beside an existing route to feed, wide enough that an animal could investigate it without being forced out of another’s way. Its surface would have some give. There would be water at its edge and a clear path back. Later, where the sleepers gathered on the pipes, there would be ledges that stayed warm without vibrating.

The power would watch what was used and what was refused. It would not fill the plain with grass merely because grass seemed kinder than concrete. If a patch of living ground could be made safe, it would leave a small one open to daylight and let the inhabitants encounter it at their own pace. It would keep every familiar slot running while they learned whether anything growing there was food, shade, or simply something to step around.

In time the plain might look less newly laid. There might be moss in a joint, a soft bank among the fitted rims, routes that changed because animals chose them. The gantries would still comb the horizon. The pumps would still be felt through the soles. But no creature’s whole future would rest on a mechanism it could not mend, and beside the places it knew, there would be other places it was free to go.
