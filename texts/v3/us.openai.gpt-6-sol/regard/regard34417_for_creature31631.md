The power would not begin by changing the sea. It would spend a long time inside it, listening through the roar for anything that could answer.

It would find no plea in the great plant. Water rose through its stems; nourishment crossed its roots; the leaves held. But it would also find that no part of the plant could finish what it had begun. A worn leaf could not fall. A new root could not reach past the old weave. The fish worked their fins without arriving anywhere.

So the power would touch one narrow stem, far from the others, and make room for its pressure to ease.

The roar would drop by a note. A fish beside the stem would drift the length of its own body before catching itself. For the first time, it would have to turn to face the flow. Above it, a grazer would lift its mouth from a leaf and settle again on a patch not yet touched.

Then the stem would sound as it had before.

The power would wait. If the plant drew water back through that chamber in precisely the old way, it would leave it alone. But after a while the stem would ease again, of its own accord. A little later, another would answer it. The vast cup would remain whole, the charge steady along the roots, while here and there a current slackened long enough for something living to move.

From the valleys, no one would see a difference. Inside the sea, a fish would pass between two columns and, after hours in the open water, reach the other one.
