The power would listen first.

It would listen long enough to learn that the roaring beneath the plain was not one sound but many: water passing through deep stone, water working at a thin roof, water finding a hollow where no hollow had been before. It would watch the people test each step with their poles. It would see that the birds, the swimmers, and the pale creatures all needed the changing water, even when it made life difficult for the people.

There was one place, a little way beyond the oldest wall, where the roof of a hollow had nearly worn through. By morning it would still hold a person’s weight. By the next hard rain it might not.

The power could have lifted that whole stretch of stone. Instead, while the people slept, it eased a few buried pebbles into the hollow’s narrow mouth. The water continued through, but its note changed. It began to beat against the rock in pairs, with a pause between.

At first light, a child heard it from behind the wall. She fetched her pole and went no farther. Two others came to listen. They knew the old note, and they knew this one was wrong.

By noon they had marked a new route with loose stones. They moved a barrier upstream, so the next rain would send less water across the weakening roof. The birds kept their pool. The pale things still emerged under its lip after rain. The people had a way around the danger, and a sound they would remember.

The power stayed through the storm. When it was over, the roof remained, though a crack had opened where the child’s pole would have landed. No one stepped there. By evening, a short line of stones showed the safer way through the mist.

Then the power went on listening.
