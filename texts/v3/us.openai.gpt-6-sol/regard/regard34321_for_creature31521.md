The power would come close enough to see the tracks in the film.

It would be tempting to cover the bare stone with moss, to still the turbine, to give the little creatures the whole ledge. But the bare stone is still becoming a place, and the turbine’s warm water has helped make the narrow world they know. The power would leave both alone.

Instead, it would attend to the seam behind the housing. Each rainy season, a little grit washes from it toward the open edge. The power would settle three grains of black gravel against a lip of concrete, where they would catch more grit without blocking the water. A fern root would find purchase there. Moss would follow in its own time. The youngest creatures would have another sheltered inch to cross when the seam grew crowded.

It would do nothing about the valve’s hiss. They already know what to do when it comes.

Before leaving, the power would watch one of the older creatures reach the end of a wavering track. It would feel ahead over the wet iron, find the new patch of film on the far side of a support, and go on grazing. Below the ledge, the cloud would drift without parting.
