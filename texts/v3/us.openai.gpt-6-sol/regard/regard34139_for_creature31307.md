The power comes in the cloud and does not clear it.

For a long while it watches the birds lift at the deep note and return to the new stone. It watches a beetle wait with its feet raised above a closing seam. It watches the three metal lights pulse, and the seam answer. There is much it does not know: whether the lights are requests or commands, whether the low note is a voice, whether the gentleness of the closing stone is chance or care.

So it does not take hold of the shelf.

Instead, it settles over one shallow pool and lets three drops fall into it, evenly spaced. Then it waits through the flies arriving, the pale hunters feeding, and a bird coming down to drink. At last a note passes under the stone. The pool trembles. The power lets three more drops fall, at the same slow interval, and waits again.

On the next new plate, a hollow forms. It is no larger than the bowl of a hand. Water gathers there, and three small ripples cross it, though nothing has fallen from the cloud.

The power cannot know yet what has answered. It has time to find out. It stays near the shelf, patient enough to learn the difference between a sound that can be heard and one that can be understood.
