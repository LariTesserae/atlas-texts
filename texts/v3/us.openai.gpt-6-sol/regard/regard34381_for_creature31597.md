The power first mistakes the open seam for a wound.

It could close it. The clay would knit without a mark; the plates would settle into a floor broad and sound enough to bear anything. But as it watches, a wedge holds the seam apart and a blunt surface rises into the rain. When the surface withdraws, it carries a little wet clay down into the warmth. The power leaves the seam open.

For a while, it does nothing. It learns that three knocks from below are answered by a change in pressure above. It sees an older creature fold itself around a row of young ones when the floor begins to shift. It sees another break apart a join it spent days making. These are not failures the power has been called to mend.

At last it attends to a hanging row of bricks beneath a basin. The row is about to fall. The older creature sheltering it has set every hard part it can against the descending plate, but the load is greater than it can pass along.

The power does not hold up the basin. It cannot know which seams the inhabitants will need next. Instead, it eases one pressure onto a nearby wedge of dark stone, for no longer than a breath. The older creature feels the weight change. It draws the young ones into a fold beneath the next plate. Then the power lets go.

The row falls. Far below, it breaks against another floor. After a long interval, three knocks come up through the clay.

In the days that follow, rain reaches the dry basin once. Not the whole wall of it: only a narrow fall, passing through an opening between ridges before their edges meet again. It wets a strip of clay wide enough for many hands. By the time the first inhabitants arrive, the power has withdrawn. They score the strip, lift parts of it, and carry what they need below.

It does not bring the rain back when the clay dries. The inhabitants open other ways toward it. Sometimes the power watches a plate settle safely; sometimes it hears a row give way beyond its reach. It remembers where the young ones were moved. If that place comes under strain, it will look again.
