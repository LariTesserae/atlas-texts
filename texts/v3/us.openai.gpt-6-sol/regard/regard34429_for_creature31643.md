The power came to the plateau and could not tell, at first, where to put its kindness.

It could have quieted the falls. It could have opened the limestone like a hand and lifted every small, clear body into the sun. But the falls brought their food, and the stone held the shapes of their lives. An old animal, freed from its channel, would not know how to be free.

So the power stayed through a season of water and learned the difference between a flood that swept the channels clean and one that gave those within them no time to fold.

Far under the plateau, at a meeting of two unseen passages, it eased a thin seam in the stone. The seam was too narrow for an animal to enter. When pressure rose sharply in the lower channel, a little water now reached the upper one first. No current changed direction. No shaft went dry. But before the next hard surge, the anchored bodies felt a small, unmistakable push.

The first time, many folded against the limestone before the flood arrived. Some did not. The power did not widen every passage or catch every young one carried away. It had learned that it did not know where all the young could live.

For a while it remained, attending to the pressure in the rock. Then it left the seam as the water would leave it: subject to wearing open, or closing with silt, over years beyond its keeping.

Aboveground, the roar did not change. The bare pockets stayed bare, and the stone shone fiercely in the sun. Deep inside it, a clear fringe drew back an instant before the water struck, then reached out again when the flow grew gentle.
