The power would first be still.

It would watch long enough to learn that the clouds of silt were not only a danger. They brought food. The current that buried a shelter also cleared it. The crab needed to eat, and the small animals needed the narrow openings the crab could not easily enter. To still the water or drive the crab away would be kindness given too quickly.

But one stone lay badly. At each turn of the current it settled a little farther into the clay, closing a gap where three of the small animals lived. They had worked upward twice. Soon there would be nowhere to work.

The power would put a finger beneath that stone and lift it no more than the thickness of a shell. It would slip a flat pebble under one edge, where the current could not dislodge it, then lower the weight again.

Silt would still blow through. A crab could still pass overhead. For a time the animals would remain shut, feeling the unfamiliar pressure through their gripping limbs. Then, in the steady water after a swirl, one would open its threads. The others would follow when their own gaps cleared.

The power would leave before they did.
