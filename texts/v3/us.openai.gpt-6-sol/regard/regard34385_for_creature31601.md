The power would wait through several risings of the mountains before doing anything.

From far above, the sea might look like something in need of calming. Close to the folds, that would seem a mistake. The roar is what keeps them alive. Stillness would leave the green threads intact for a while and erase everything living out in the water.

So the power would not still the sea. It would watch until it could tell one fold recurring from another—not by its shape, which changes, but by the way it turns an incoming wave. It would notice, too, how a small difference at one meeting of currents can travel for miles and reach a young ridge as a blow.

At the next splitting of that ridge, the power would do one small thing. It would turn the first hard pressure wave a little aside. The stone would split anyway; the mountain must go on becoming a mountain. But some threads would remain fastened to the new edge, and the fold beside it would have time to come apart in the cold sheet and gather again downstream.

Then it would leave the water to settle into its own motion.

It might take a long time to learn whether the fold had survived, or whether another had formed in its place. The power would return to watch. If there were a way to help here, it would have to be found in that difference: not how to keep a shape unchanged, but how to let what lives through change have another chance.
