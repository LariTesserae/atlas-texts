The power would spend a morning doing nothing.

It would watch the mesh gather water and the little animals wait beneath it. It would follow a root along the buried cable, and a seed from the hair on an animal’s side to the crack where it might grow. It would look inside the silent housings before deciding that silence meant neglect. The green lamps were steady. The pipes were cool. What lived here had made a life with these things, not merely in spite of them.

By noon it would know one thing needed doing. The broken inspection stair had not finished breaking.

The power would hold its remaining steps firm for a while, and leave a clear warning on the sound landing: a bright line across the concrete, impossible to mistake for a shadow or a stain. Anyone who came to inspect the works could choose another way down. The small pale animals would still be able to cross.

Then it would turn to the collectors. It would not make them yield more water, or summon a rain the ridge had never known. In one frame, though, a loosened wire had begun to draw away from its fastening. The power would set it back. The change would be no greater than the work of a careful hand, but drops would continue to gather there through the dry mornings ahead.

It would leave the roots where they were. It would leave the concrete cool and the lamps burning, the leaf cups full when dawn allowed it, and the exposed paths dry by noon. It would not bring birds to fill the quiet.

Toward evening, beneath the mended frame, a drop would swell on the lower wire. One of the little animals would wait with its mouth close to it. When the drop let go, the power would be gone.
