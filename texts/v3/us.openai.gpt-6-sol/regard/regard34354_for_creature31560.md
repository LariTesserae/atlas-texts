The power would arrive without taking a shape the birds had to fear.

It would listen first. The noise beneath the stone sounded like an engine, but listening long enough revealed a pause within it, too brief for an ear to catch unaided. In that pause the thing under the shelf attended to the water, the cracks, the small feet on the moss. It held them all with immense care. The work was not failing. Nor had anyone asked it to stop.

So the power would not open the rock or still the gears. It would lay a hand on one sharp lip of stone and let the being beneath know that another attention was there: steady, and asking nothing.

For a while, nothing would change. Beetles would carry their beads to the crack. A bird would drop its seed and cock its head. The hiss would rise when the thrum dipped.

Then one beetle, jostled out of line, would keep the bead it carried. It would lower its mouth and drink. The bead would grow smaller. A new drop would gather under the overhang to take its place, trembling as before.

The power would leave its hand there until the beetle had finished, and then withdraw.
