The power comes after rain, when the black seams are full and the swifts have gone to hunt. It does not dry the court. It does not straighten the walls.

It listens first. The wind sounds through one gap, then another; water ticks on the plates; under a tilted plate, something shifts its grit. The power waits until it knows which noises have always been there and which are new. One brick near the coping has begun to move. When the swifts return, their weight and the next hard rain may bring it down.

At dawn, while they are away, the power sets a hand against that part of the wall. It does not push the brick back into its old place: a narrow passage runs behind it, and something uses it at night. Instead it takes a little of the silt gathered in a channel and presses it deep into the failing joint, leaving the passage open. It steadies two bricks below with the smallest shift it can manage. The wall still leans. Wind still finds the holes.

Then the power goes to the middle, where no creature has remained long enough to claim a place. One clay plate has settled so low that, in a dry week, the last rainwater disappears beneath it before the frogs can reach it. The power lifts an edge no thicker than a fingernail. Now a narrow strip of water will stay there a little longer. The clicking creatures can come to it along the seams without crossing the open floor.

By dusk there is nothing to see of its visit. The swifts turn once above the court and slip under the coping. A frog calls from a corner that sounds like the opposite corner. In the night, a small body passes behind the steadied brick.

The power has done what it could without asking the court to become a different place. Before leaving, it listens again.
