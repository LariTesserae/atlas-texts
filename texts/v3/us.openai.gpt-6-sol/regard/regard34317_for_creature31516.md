The power did not lift the octopus from the trench. It did not still the shaft. At each stroke, water entered beneath the grille, and she reached into it with her arms.

It watched through a turn of the tide. On the next stroke the iron caught, knocked, and shook a little more mortar from a seam above the eastern housing. Most of the dust settled in the trenches. A little found the eggs. She passed an arm over them, then drew water through the rows until it cleared.

The power laid a finger against the seam. Far inside the wall, where no animal lived, a loose edge of concrete pressed back into place. The knocking went on. The grille gave its bright rattle, and shrimp still came through the old duct. But no new dust fell over the eastern trench.

The octopus held fast through the next stroke. When the rattle passed, she let go and drew the water over her eggs again. She could not have known that anything had changed. She felt along each row with the short, pale arm, found one grain of grit, and carried it out beneath the grille.

For a while, the power stayed. The fish scattered and gathered. The eel showed its head. In the faint light, the dark points inside the eggs grew easier to see. Nothing more needed moving, so it left the chamber to the tide.
