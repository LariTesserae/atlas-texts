The power would come with the rain and spend a long time doing nothing.

It would watch water cross the dark plates, pause at the new stain, and disappear through the grate. It would feel the roots reaching through their squared banks and the pipes that must remain clear. Beneath the roof, it would feel the rooms kept dry by all this careful work. The garden could grow wilder, but not by making a leak for someone below to live with.

So it would make one small adjustment. At the turn where water already lingered, a little silt would settle—not enough to block the channel, only enough to hold moisture between showers. The green film could thicken there, or dry away. The power would not decide which.

It would leave the moth its lamp housing. When the lamp came on at night, though, its light would fall a little more toward the roof and less toward the open sky. The moth might stay; others might pass without being drawn in.

Then the power would wait. It would not bring birds to a roof with nowhere for them to nest, or fill every seam with life before the seams had proved they could bear it. It would let the vines test the pipes and let the people who tended the garden see where they had reached. For now, there was room, rain, and a place at one turn in the channel that stayed damp a little longer.
