The power came to the ridge and found no one asking to be saved.

It watched a young grazer pause at a narrowing turn, then pass through sideways. It watched a tower creature wait for another to finish feeding. By evening it understood that the lanes were neither unfinished nor neglected. They were being kept open by lives that needed them.

So it did not widen them. It did not bring water to the roots that had never sought water, or soften the hard seeds so their fall would make no sound.

It did notice something no inhabitant could have seen: deep beneath the central tower, one new fracture ended a finger’s breadth from an older one. When the ridge next shifted, the two would meet. The tower might stand; it might not. The power could have fused the stone and made it certain.

Instead, it eased a little of the strain into the empty rock below the roots. The ridge settled in the night with a sound too low for even the tower sleepers to hear. Warm breath still rose through the cracks. In the morning, a thin-winged creature found a new slit of light between two leaves, and the wall beneath it began, slowly, to grow toward the gap.

Then the power left the settlement to its inhabitants. They had made a way of living on that narrow height, and the greater kindness was to give them time to go on making it.
