The power comes to the hollow by following a drop of water. It does not enter all at once. For a while it is only an added weight in the drop, which hangs from a root and does not fall.

It watches the grazers stop together. It watches the seam-runners hold the water where they need it, and the leaves make room for the large animal without knowing precisely where its back will be. It listens to the low pulse in the root beds. When a patch of moss resists it, the pulse goes on without forcing it to follow.

The power had thought, at first, to ease the cold. So much life is pressed against so little warmth. But it sees the small animals sleeping safely inside the leaf veins, with ice held at their edges; it sees water taking paths the cold has made for it. A sudden thaw would undo work it does not understand. The power lets the cold remain.

Near the newest mineral, a root reaches into a narrow crack and withdraws. Hours later it tries again. Water enters after it, freezes, and makes the crack a little wider. The root withdraws once more. Above it, a frond folds, though the floor beneath has not yet begun to rise. The power waits through this, too, until it can tell what the hollow is doing: opening a passage slowly enough for everything living there to move away.

Then it notices what the hollow cannot. Beyond the fresh mineral lies a vein of stone under strain. When the passage reaches it, the stone will break inward. The root beds will feel the break, but too late to draw back.

The power does not seal the crack. It lays a little of its attention along the strained vein and lets the blue points touch it. They pass from leaf to water to stone, then race back through the stems. The pulse quickens. One curtain tightens; another loosens. The patch of moss stays as it was, and the water finds a way around it.

For three days the hollow grows in a different direction. Roots take hold along the side of the passage, and the heavy animal sleeps elsewhere while the floor shifts beneath its old resting place. At last the stone breaks. It falls into a space prepared for it, among roots that yield and then close around its weight.

The power remains until the ticking of the ice and the rasp of feeding become regular again. By then the drop it entered has passed through the feet of several seam-runners and is hanging from a leaf farther in. When it finally falls, there is nothing left in it that the hollow needs to keep.
