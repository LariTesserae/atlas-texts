The power first mistook the plain for a place.

Then it felt the silver skin draw tight beneath the wind, felt the slow work of the great trunks and the miles of roots passing what little they had from one part to another. The ridges were not walls raised against the weather. They were the shape a living thing had learned to take while weather wore it down.

The power considered rain. It could have called clouds over the plateau and filled every hollow. But the sealed chambers would burst; the dust funnels would clog; soft growth would spread where the grazers could not keep pace with it. Generosity, here, required knowing when to keep its hands closed.

It stayed long enough to learn the sounds. A snap underfoot sent grazers running, but an identical crack from a distant ridge did not. The pale-winged animals knew when to leave a crown before it shed. The burrowers knew which pauses meant safety. None of them needed the great clatter made gentle.

Near an old waste cone, a fold in the living floor began to close. A burrower was underneath, carrying a seed in its jaws. It had followed a soft layer that narrowed faster than it could turn. Above it, the two lips of silver growth drew toward one another.

The power did not stop the fold. It put a little of its strength into one leaf, keeping it flexible for a few breaths longer than it would otherwise have remained. The burrower felt the open space, slipped through, and vanished beneath the cone. The leaf hardened. The seam sealed.

By the next wind, there was no sign of the intervention. The burrower would make more passages; some would end badly. The plain would go on folding and shedding, feeding its small inhabitants and sometimes closing over them.

The power remained until the crown drums began their evening thump. Then it left the wind to carry dust through the blades, and the living ground to decide where next to grow.
