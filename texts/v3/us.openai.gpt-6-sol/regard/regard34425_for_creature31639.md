The power would come in the rain and do nothing at first.

It would feel water entering the city at its highest roofs, disappearing into roots, finding old channels under the paving. It would know which cisterns held water through the dry weeks and which only sounded deep when fruit fell into them. It would hear the bats stirring below a floor that had carried their weight for generations.

It would not clear the vines from the streets. No one here needed the streets clear. Nor would it make every cistern full. The frogs depended on water, but other lives had found the places water left behind.

Near the lower terraces, it would find one small failure worth mending. A buried drain had filled with silt and fig roots. When hard rain came, the blocked water ran across a roof instead, lifting its old tiles and spilling into the room where the bats roosted. The roof had endured many storms, but its edge was beginning to loosen.

The power would ease a narrow way through the drain. It would leave the roots that held the wall together and move only enough silt for the next storm to pass. Then it would settle the loose tiles with a pressure no heavier than a hand.

Rain would still strike the roof. Some would seep into its cracks. The cistern below would rise and fall as it always had. But the rush that had begun to tear the roof open would return to its old course through the stone.

By morning, nothing would look restored. A young long-bodied animal would cross the roof behind its mother, putting a foot on one of the settled tiles. The bats would remain asleep beneath it. When evening came, they would leave through the same dark opening and carry fig seeds down the valley.

The power would stay long enough to see that its small repair held, and then leave the city to its many inhabitants.
