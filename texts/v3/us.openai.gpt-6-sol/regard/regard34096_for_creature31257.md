The power would not begin at the glass.

It would begin with a pump.

One morning the tremor in the water would change, so slightly that no leaf need shift. A bearing that would have failed in twelve years would last a hundred instead. A valve half hidden by roots would learn to close gently when something living lay across it. Deep in the tower, where the garden could not feel the work being done, a second way to keep the water warm would be made ready.

Then the power would wait.

It would take a long time to know whether the bodies at the far wall were drawn by the clouds or merely resting where the daylight was good. It would take longer to learn how they asked one another questions. The power would attend to the slow contractions in the root knots, to the leaves that opened after a relay clicked, to a limb held against steel until a rack had finished moving. It would not mistake slowness for lack of thought.

At last, on the glass some distance from the seam, it would place a patch of shade shaped like no passing cloud. The patch would remain until the leaves had time to turn toward it or away. The next day it would appear elsewhere. There would be no demand in it, only something the garden could notice and answer if it wished.

Perhaps the roots would leave that portion of glass untouched. Perhaps, over weeks, they would make a space around it. If they did, the power would understand at least this much: *there*.

It would build beyond that place, on the air side—a clear chamber full of warm water, with light and a little room for roots to grow. No door would open between chamber and garden. Not yet. Its water would circulate and settle; its walls would prove themselves through storms. From inside, it would look less like an escape than another part of the garden, one with a little more space between its frames.

Only when the chamber was safe would a passage appear, small enough to close, slow enough that nothing could be swept through it. The power would leave it shut unless the beings came to it. They might send a root across first. They might wait for years. They might turn their leaves back toward the lamps and never enter.

In that case the chamber would remain on the other side of the glass, kept warm against the clouds. The power would continue tending the pumps.

It would be enough to have made a future available without making the garden pay for it.
