The power came at noon, when a shadow crossed the western wall ahead of the animal that cast it. For a while it watched from the rim. The deep blows and fine knocks did not make a single rhythm, but each belonged to someone at work.

It could have brought water. It could have cooled the shelves, set the wandering shadows straight, or made the stone yield more easily to the narrow jaws. It understood, after listening, that it did not yet know what any of those gifts would cost.

So it stayed through the heat. It felt how a great animal followed a seam without knowing where it ended, and how the small ones waited beneath its plates for the tremor that meant they must hold fast. It heard three taps from a pit and, much later, an answer. Nothing asked to be made easier. The answer arrived.

Toward dusk, a young one came out onto a crust its elders had left brittle. The surface broke. It fell only a short distance, but the fallen grit filled the opening behind it. It scraped once, paused, and tapped three times. On the shelf above, another small animal stopped to listen.

The power held back the next fall of grit. It did not lift the young one out. The animal above dug at the opening until there was room for the one below to climb, and by dark they had gone on together.

Years later, people came to the rim with drills. They saw the even terraces and the mineral seams, and mistook a place made slowly by its inhabitants for a place waiting to be used. The power did not break their tools or frighten them away. It let the ground carry its sounds clearly beneath their feet: the labor in the pits, the knocks passing from one animal to another, the immense, unhurried breathing under the salt. Then it gave them time to understand what they were hearing.

Some left. Some stayed long enough to learn where they could walk without breaking a roof over a hidden chamber. None sank a drill into the plateau.

The animals went on as before. At noon their shadows still strayed, and at dusk the little knocks still answered the tremors. From the rim, the place looked almost unoccupied.
