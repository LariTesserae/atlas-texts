The power comes to the edge of the hollow and stays there for a long time.

It hears the roar separate into moisture moving through stems, bodies turning in the walls, and the small blows of burrowers beneath the leaf. It watches a hunter take an insect from a water groove and escape the closing fold. Later, another hunter does not escape.

There are things it could change. It could deepen the cups, silence the folds, make every call lead plainly to its source. But the water that vanishes into the paving goes somewhere; the folds feed the town; and the creatures who cross it know the worn paths better than a visitor does.

So it leaves the hollow as it found it. At the edge, it takes care to lift its foot before the burrowers must drum beneath it.

For a while the roar follows it across the ridges. Behind it, the cups dry, a pale grazer carries pollen through a wall, and the old crossings remain open.
