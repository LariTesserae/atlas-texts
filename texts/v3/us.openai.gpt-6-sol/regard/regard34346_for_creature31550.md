The power waits until the channel is nearly full. It does not know whether the animal’s bent leg hurts, and will not change a body it does not understand merely because it looks imperfect.

It sees farther into the gap than the animal can. Behind the flexible joint are five others, still and nearly indistinguishable from the stone. It sees, too, a maintenance order scheduled for the next morning: replace the joint and fill the void behind it. The work would be sound by every measure on the drawing.

So the power does one small thing. As the technician crosses the landing, a drop falls from the handrail a little earlier than it would have. The animal stops at its impact, forelegs lifted. The technician, reaching down to inspect the channel, sees it move.

She stays until the water sinks away. Then she checks the gap with a light and finds the others. The next morning, the crew replaces the worn joint but leaves the recess open. They fit a narrow shelter behind it, clear of the rock’s movement, and test that the channel still drains. On the maintenance drawing, the technician adds a note where someone will see it before the next repair.

The landing remains almost exactly as it was. A seam darkens. Water gathers, goes flat, and disappears. The animal comes down the wall with its slight turn at the end of each run.

The power does not ask it to be grateful.
