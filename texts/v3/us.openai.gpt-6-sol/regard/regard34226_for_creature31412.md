The power comes when the pumps start. It feels the tremor pass through the railings and watches a small plated animal cling to a stretch of open paving until the vibration ends. Only then does it follow the animal to the gutter.

At first, it considers bringing rain that would last for days. The city is thirsty in places, and there is water enough beyond the veil. But the film lives on narrow boundaries: too much water would change them, and a long cool season might kill what a brief shower sustains. It considers stopping the pumps so the buried machinery can rest. By dusk, the gutters would be dry.

So it goes below the streets.

There are valves nearly worn through and pipes whose interiors have narrowed with stone. The power does not make them new. It clears one valve seat, braces a pipe where a crack has begun to spread, and frees a shutter that has been closing a little late. It listens through several cycles before touching anything else. Above, the knocks come at their usual hour. Water takes its usual course along the walls, except that one trickle no longer fails between rains.

At that trickle, the pale film slowly extends across a joint that had been bare. The power leaves it alone.

In the old tram tunnel, well away from the inhabited seams, it finds a dead service channel leading to a cistern. It opens the channel by a finger’s width and lets a little warm water through. The first flow runs too hot; it closes the opening and waits until the stone cools. After that, the channel holds a shallow wet edge even when the gutters outside empty. Nothing is carried there. The film may reach it, and the animals may follow, or they may not.

Before leaving, the power climbs to the rim. Steam hides the plain. Behind it, rain begins, and the shutters fold over the reservoirs. Down in the streets, water spreads across the walls where the shelled animals feed. One emerges from a seam and starts along a familiar pale band.

The city continues its work. It will need attention again, and the power knows the way below.
