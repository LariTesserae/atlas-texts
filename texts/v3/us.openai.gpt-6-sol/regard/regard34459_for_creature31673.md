The power would not close the seam.

It would see that the new opening had already become a place: for moss in the dark, for insects in the cold breath, for a bird on the wall, and for the person who had set a bowl beneath it. To seal it would save no one from the ridge. It would only hide what the ridge was doing.

But it would notice something the person with the bowl could not yet see. Beneath the garden wall, a long piece of old foundation had lost its purchase. It would hold through this evening’s drip. By tomorrow’s dusk, the wall would fall into the seam.

So, before dawn, the power would loosen one stone at the wall’s foot. Not enough to bring the wall down; just enough that it rocked plainly under a hand. The person who came to check the bowl would feel it. They would call a neighbour, lift the bowl away, and take down the wall themselves, stone by stone. The hunting bird would wait somewhere else.

The power would leave the loose stone loose.

Over the following nights it would attend to the whole saddle, not by holding the mountain still, but by making its changes harder to mistake. A widening crack would show a clean line through its dust. A plank that no longer reached sound footing would shift when tested, before anyone put their full weight on it. The residents already knew to look and listen; the power would give them time to trust what they found.

When the ridge had finished with this town, the power would help them find somewhere they could carry their bowls. Until then, at dusk, water would fall where it fell. Lizards would wait beneath the bridges. Someone would move a bowl by the width of a finger.
