The power would not begin by entering.

For a while it would attend to the hollow from wherever attendance is possible without casting a shadow. It would watch the bead travel along the ceiling, the seam open after the knocking, the grub turn back from the new fold. It would learn that the room was not failing to be finished. It was growing.

Then, one overcast day, it would lay a small shadow against an older wall. Nothing shaped like a hand or a doorway: just a patch of darkness the size of a leaf. It would leave the shadow there long enough for the root to answer. By morning, a green fringe would have gathered around it.

The power would move the shadow a little. Not toward the uncertain crease, but along the older seam, where the grub sometimes came through. Each morning it would move it again, slowly enough that no new leaf had to grow far to follow. In this way the root would make a thin, shaded margin beside the grub’s route. The margin would not be a tunnel, nor a fence. It would be a place where leaves folded easily and pores were less likely to close on a passing body.

The power would leave the stone flake alone. The grub knew how to carry it, and the pods belonged to the root, which grew more. To make the flake easy to find might teach the grub to wait for help where it had once been wary.

At the newest crease, the power would do even less. Once, when the knocking ran there first, it would steady the air for the length of a breath. The floor would remain at the same distance while a new leaf unfolded. Then it would let go. If the root could use that moment, it would; if it could not, there would be another breath another day.

After some time, the power would take its shadow away.

The little leaves along the older seam would remain. They would turn toward other passing darknesses, including the narrow shadow of the grub. The new fold would go on making room in its own uneven way. And when a seam opened after the knocking, warm air would still pour through, smelling of crushed leaves and of a place beyond the chamber that no one inside had yet seen.
