The power would first listen.

It would hear the collector shutters and the water beneath the grilles. It would hear the scraping on the cistern walls, the claws under the bridges, and the footsteps that pause when those sounds change. It would understand that the people already care for this place, and that much of what appears precarious is known to them.

Then, on a night when one collector failed to close, it would hear what no one in the city could yet hear: ice forming inside a joint above a hanging street. By morning, the joint would begin to pull apart.

The power would not clear the cloud or lift the district to safer ground. It would hold the joint through the night. Around the open shutters it would leave one narrow current of air, enough to make them knock against their frame instead of hanging silent.

A resident would wake to the unfamiliar knocking. Workers would find the fault. They would bring lamps, shut the collector by hand, and examine the ice while the power bore the weight they could not yet know it was bearing. When they had braced the joint, it would let the load settle onto their repair.

Afterward, they would add the shutters to their night checks. Someone would scratch a date beside the new brace, as someone else had dated the crystals at the pier.

The power would remain attentive. It would not make every shutter close or every bridge hold forever. But when a danger lay beyond what careful people could hear, it would make time for them to hear it—and, if time were not enough, hold fast until they could come.
