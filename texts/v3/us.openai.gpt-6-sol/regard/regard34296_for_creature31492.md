The power would wait through a rain and a falling slab before deciding what kindness meant here.

At first, it might want to hold the roof together. Then it would see the animals drinking beneath a fresh break, the dark growth taking hold along a new seam, and the crowds finding routes no creature had known the day before. To make the cavern still would be to take away much of what fed it.

It would attend instead to a broad-backed animal working at a loose plate near the old bridge. The animal had found something beneath it and had one forefoot braced against the edge. Through the stone, the power felt a deeper movement approaching: not the brief shudder the animals knew, but a slow settling that would close the plate all at once.

It would not lift the animal away. A foot suddenly raised by nothing would send it running from a meal it needed, perhaps into a worse place. The power would press lightly on the stone under its other feet. A tremor, early and unmistakable. The animal would flatten its stance, pull back its nose, and leave the plate. When it shut, it would trap only damp growth and a little water.

That night, the power would look at the three empty enclosures. It would leave them empty. Their uphill openings already gave the small creatures somewhere to wait out a sudden run of water, and no one had asked it to choose who should live there. But on the bridge, one peg had worked nearly free. The next heavy crossing would send it down among the animals feeding below. The power would ease it the rest of the way while the floor was clear, and lay it beside the frayed basket.

By morning the bridge would rattle less. The plate near it would be shut; water would have found another way around. The broad-backed animal would nose along that new wet edge, and the pale grazers would reach it first.

The power would stay long enough to learn whether its small acts had made anything worse. Then it would leave the cavern its motion.
