The power would come when the pump was idle. It would listen through one full cycle before deciding that the first stroke, violent as it sounded, was not a failure. It would notice instead a small crack behind the pumping station, where water meant for the gutter was entering a room with electrical fittings.

It could close the crack with a touch. But the water would find another route, perhaps through the grain store, perhaps behind the new concrete where no one could see it. So the power would wake the attendant a little before his shift and leave him with a clear, stubborn thought: *Look behind the station before you start the pump.*

He would find the crack. The machinist would make a collar for the pipe, and the workers would open a short channel back to the gutter. There would be an argument about whose job it was, and then the work would be done. Nobody would call it a miracle.

Above the street, someone would soon be sent to finish the wall. The power would let a worker see the bats while there was still time to change the forms. The recesses would stay open to the warmer passages, but a narrow ledge would keep droppings off the pavement below. The worker would take credit for the idea, as well he should.

The power would do nothing to the moths, the rats, or the cockroaches. It would not make the fish into emblems of endurance or the fern into a forest. It would help the attendant find out what was in the water before anyone decided the gutter was harmless because something lived in it.

Then it would leave the district much as it found it: hot, cramped, and unfinished. The pump would still interrupt conversation. The rats would still wait under the grain-store door. But the next workers would know to leave the bats a way through, and the water would have a route people could inspect and repair. That was enough to give the street a little more time to become itself.
