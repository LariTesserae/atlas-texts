The power would come in the rain.

It would not descend into one of the open halls and call it a home. It would not fill the broad spaces with smaller lives for company, or decide that the changing passages ought to stay put. It would watch a storm cross the valley and follow the water afterward, until it understood why one full gutter closed and another remained open for hours.

Then, at the upper rim, it would make one small change.

For years, storms had been wearing a shallow notch there. Water crossed it too quickly to do much good: a hard, brief rush down one slope, followed by heat. The power would smooth the notch so the rush spread into several thin falls. The next squall would still arrive as a dark band. It would still strike the far frames and bow their roofs. But a little more of its water would reach the living floor slowly, after the violence of the storm had passed.

The valley might use it. A rib might thicken beneath one fall, or a gutter might grow out to meet another. It might turn the extra water away. The power would allow either answer.

Before leaving, it would stand on the rim until afternoon and watch the familiar openings change. Its gift was not a plan for the valley. It was a gentler way for rain to enter, offered to a being that already knew how to build with rain.
