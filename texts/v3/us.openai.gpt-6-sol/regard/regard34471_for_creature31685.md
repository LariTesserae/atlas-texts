The power would come to the landing and wait.

It would be able to hear the flywheel turning, but it would wait for a spoke to change position. It would be able to see the animals hidden in the brick, but it would wait for one to come out and work the sediment. It would stay through many blue lights, until it understood that the fish turned before the lamp came on.

Then it would leave the square as it was. No cleared silt, no mended walls, no brighter lamp. The silt fed things too small to see from the landing. The worn walls guided the current. The darkness gave the fish a place to settle between meals.

There was one thing it could do without entering that small, intricate weather. At the top of the broken steps, it would place a thought—no stronger than the tremor in a pipe—in the mind of anyone who came there with tools: *Look long enough to notice what lives here.*

Someone might still have good reason to work on the old machinery. The power would not forbid it. But they would see the jointed animals in the seams and the pale growth under the stair. They would watch the shelter light, and see the fish turn toward it. Then they could decide how to work without taking away the narrow places and currents those lives depended on.

When the power departed, nothing in the square would mark its visit. The flywheel would turn. A little silt would settle onto the cart. Beneath the awning, the fish would wait for the blue.
