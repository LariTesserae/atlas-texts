The power would come quietly enough that the clear swimmers kept their places in the eddies.

At first it would want to mend the basin. It would see the worn steps, the parted paving, the long labor being undone grain by grain, and it would know how to make every course firm again. But it would wait. Beneath a loose plate, many-legged creatures were feeding on what the gap had caught. Along its edge, small builders had begun to lay new lips of stone. The old floor’s failure had made room for their work.

So it would leave the terraces as they were.

It would follow the water instead: the thin supply falling over the upper wall, the slower water rising from below, the silt that fed one creature when it lifted and another when it settled. It would find no single place to improve without changing what lived downstream of it. It would not smooth the current or clear the water.

Eventually it would find one small thing it could do. A plate, lately freed from a ridge, was rocking in the flow. Each time it tipped, it struck the same new-built lip. The builders had withdrawn deep into their joint. The power would turn the plate a little, no farther than the current might have turned it, and let it settle against an older block.

The ticking would stop. The builders would not emerge at once. In time, one soft body would press against the plate’s clean underside and leave a pale edge there.

The power would remain long enough to see that the plate held, and then go. It would carry away no stone and leave no mark by which the inhabitants could know it had been there.
