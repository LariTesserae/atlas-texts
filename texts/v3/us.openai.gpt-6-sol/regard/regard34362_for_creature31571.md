The power does not open the door all the way.

It has heard the booming through the mountain and come expecting something enormous. Instead it finds a hundred small interruptions inside the noise: a leg lifted to rest, a body making room for another, a young one tucked beneath a tray. It waits long enough to learn that the sound is not a plea to be stopped.

The water worries it more. Not because there is too much, but because the same pump holds every drop in place. So it goes around the chamber, beyond the close-set plates, and tends to what the animals cannot reach. It gives the pump a second path for power and the pool a reserve that will feed it if the first path fails. Neither alters the pressure. On an ordinary day, no drop moves.

Then, on the inside of the service door, it fixes a narrow strip of ridged iron above the waterline. It does not put an animal there. The strip is simply another foothold, near enough to the trays to be found and far enough from them to be refused.

For several days nothing uses it. At last one dark back appears on the strip, then vanishes beneath a tray again. The rasp does not grow louder or softer. The moss remains in its seam. When the door closes, the mountain is quiet.

The power leaves the bulb burning and goes away, having made no demand that this small life become anything other than itself.
