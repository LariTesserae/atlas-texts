The power would not fill the empty rooms.

For a long while it would watch, until it understood that the arches were not unfinished work. Water fell there because leaves held it; animals came because water fell; the immense plants kept those passages clear because their margins lived well in the light. To close a gap with flowers, or furnish it with fruit, would be to take something from every creature that used it.

Instead, the power would kneel at one dark basin and put a hand against the pale mineral ground. It would change almost nothing visible. Where fallen pollen and dead leaf-skins collected, the stone would begin to loosen, grain by grain. The little creatures already living in that debris would have places to shelter. In time, there would be a finger’s depth of soil around the basin, then perhaps a little more.

It would do the same in a few other places where water already fell. It would make no new stream and summon no rain from elsewhere. A country that lived on drops could be harmed by a gift too large to hold.

Before leaving, the power would pause under an arch. A pale winged animal would come down, drink, and go. A drop would follow it, striking the basin without a sound that carried far. Nothing in the room would have changed. Under its roots, though, there would be a little more ground in which to endure a dry day.
