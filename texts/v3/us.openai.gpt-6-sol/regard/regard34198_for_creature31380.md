The power comes to the summit intending to open the little chamber.

It sees the door hanging loose and thinks of wind, of iron giving way, of the green torn out with it. But at the threshold it stops. Dust lies between the shorter blades. The leaves nearest the opening have grown no farther. Even here, where the mountain weather can reach them, they have found the shape in which they can remain.

So it does not shut the door. It does not straighten the handwheel or lift a blade to see what lies behind it.

For a long time it listens through the stone. The note under the floor has no break in it. The green has made room for every bolt and tooth, and every bolt and tooth has given the green a place to hold. The power cannot tell which was here first in any way that matters now.

At last it puts one finger against the outer hinge. The worn pin has nearly worked free. It eases the pin back into place and closes the split in its head, leaving the door at precisely the angle at which it hung. The repair makes no sound inside the housing. Not a grain of dust falls from the ledges.

Then it withdraws.

Below the summit, where the path begins, it leaves a small mark on a stone: not a warning or a name, only a line that turns aside. Anyone who follows it will pass the housing without coming close enough to mistake the half-turned wheel for something that needs finishing.

The wind goes on wearing the iron. The note goes on beneath the floor. Near the door, in the bare dust, a pale grain takes hold beside the repaired hinge.
