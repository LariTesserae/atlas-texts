The power would not stop the pumps. It would know where the water must go, and how many lives depend on its going there.

It would come when the cloud filled the recess and the far wall disappeared. Nothing would brighten. The valve would turn; the rail would shiver. Beside the grass, the small grey back would flatten itself as it always had.

First, the power would fasten the loose panel. Its chatter would cease, leaving the motor and the water audible as separate things. Then it would alter the edge of the seam by no more than a fingertip’s width. The floor would still tip toward the drain; rain would still clear the cabinets. But beneath the grass there would be a shallow hollow that the crossing sheet of water could not scour clean. Grit could settle there. A few husks and threads could catch there. When the pumps climbed to speed, there would be room for the many limbs to hold fast without being pressed against the sharpest concrete.

The power would not lift the creature into its hand or show it a garden. This was its place: the water’s passage, the brief shelter, the grass that rose before it did. It would make that place a little less precarious and leave it recognizable.

Much later, someone checking the drain would find it flowing freely. They might notice that the panel no longer rattled, and that the grass in the seam had grown a little thicker. They would have no reason to look beneath it.

When the valve turned again, the blades would bend. Afterward they would rise, and the grey back would follow.
