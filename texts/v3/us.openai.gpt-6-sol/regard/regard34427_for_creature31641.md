The power would not bring rain.

It would see that the dust holds no thirst, that the fronds are not waiting to become trees, and that the feeder is not a lesser creature for making no sound. It would spend a long while learning what a long while means here.

Then, at the lip of the crack, it would touch the waiting grain—not to push it, but to give it somewhere to fall.

For an instant the grain would hang as it always had. Below it, the bright strip of sky would remain bright. Then the grain would drop a distance smaller than its own width and come to rest. Nothing would split. The thickets would continue their quiet exchange.

A nearby filament would brighten out of sequence.

The power would leave that change alone. Through the adjoining leaves it would travel, slight enough that a watcher might mistake it for the sun shifting. Where it reached the feeder, the animal would lift its rim from the vein. It would settle again, a little farther along, at an edge it had not used before.

If the new pattern failed, the leaves could pass the old one back. The power would not hold it in place. It would remain only long enough to see whether the thicket kept it.

The sky would still look ordinary. The far stand would still be impossibly far away, and its nearest leaves could still be touched. But beneath those leaves there would be one arrangement that had come after another. The grain would lie where it had fallen. Nothing more would be asked of the place.
