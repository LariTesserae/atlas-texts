The power would come slowly enough that nothing in the beds bent at its approach.

It would see the old work first: the ridges, the filled channels, the patient fit of stone against stone. Then it would notice that the living thing had not simply covered the work. Its roots held a fallen course together here; there, where water still needed to pass, they had left a narrow way open. It would not know whether this was memory, instinct, or something for which it had no name. It would take care with all three possibilities.

There was no need to raise the walls. The worn stones were not a wound. Nor would it bring creatures into the basins merely because the silence troubled it. The place had endured as it was, and what lived there had shaped itself around that endurance.

The power found one channel almost closed by silt. Beyond it, in a basin under the thickest canopy, the lower weed had begun to pale. It set a current moving through the channel—not a surge, only a thread of cold water, slow enough to leave every settled grain in place. Over days, the pale weed darkened. Along the channel’s edge, a root grew into the new flow, while another kept its hold on the stones.

The power let the current remain. Then it went away.

Years later, the channel was still open. No wall had risen, and no new sound filled the water. A little more light reached the basin when the upper leaves shifted; a little more water passed beneath them. From above, the great pattern looked unchanged. At its floor, the living cover went on tending what it could.
