The power would spend a long while doing nothing.

It would follow the animals through the vents and watch the leaves turn toward a new lamp. It would listen beside the workers who knew what a wooden knock meant. The city was young, but its people had already learned something difficult: a living place could be helped without being commanded.

On the plain beyond the workshops, the surveyors would soon choose between two routes. One crossed a low rise that seemed easy to level. The other bent around it, costing several days’ growth. Beneath the rise, roots were gathering toward a narrow seam of salt. The city had not found a way to make that need plain.

So the power would do one small thing. For a few breaths, it would let the roots at the rise make their usual, quiet strain visible: pale lines under the skin of the street, tightening toward the seam. A surveyor would notice. An older worker would put down a padded tool and feel the ground. Someone would move a marker.

Then the lines would fade. The workers might disagree again tomorrow; the power would leave them that right. It would not draw their streets, purify the bitter air without knowing what fed on it, or make every knock into a voice. It would have helped the city be heard once, clearly enough for its inhabitants to listen better the next time.
