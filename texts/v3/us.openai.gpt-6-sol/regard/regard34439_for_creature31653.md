The power would come in cloud, when the far peak had disappeared and the drop sounded closer than it was. It would see the narrow places where roots held, the insects waiting under lichen, and the goat standing still on the rim. It would understand that the wind was not an enemy here. Neither was the bare stone.

So it would leave the hollow much as it found it. It would not widen the seams for the ferns or make the ledge safe to wander without looking. The hollow had room for its inhabitants because each lived within a small portion of it; giving any one of them more might take something from the rest.

The power would lift the wooden scoop from beside the seep and tuck its split handle under a flat stone, out of the path of the next hard rain. Then it would wait.

When someone came again for water, they would find the scoop where they could reach it. Before kneeling, they would notice the moss beneath their foot and step onto the bare slab instead. They would drink, hear a bird working along the seam, and set the scoop back under the stone.

The cloud would pass. Drops would begin falling from the ledges. The power would go without asking the hollow to remember it.
