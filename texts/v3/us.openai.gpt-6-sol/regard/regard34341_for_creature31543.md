The power would come close enough to see a frog sitting at the edge of a hollow, its throat moving before any sound came out. It would notice the larvae beneath the water, the hunter balanced on the surface, and the lizard warming itself on the stone nearby.

It would feel an urge to deepen the hollow. More water would give the young frogs room and make their lives less dependent on a single fallen leaf. But deeper water might hold fish, or last long enough for another kind of hunter to settle there. It would consider lifting the lizard away. Then it would see how thin the lizard was, and how carefully it watched for a meal.

For a while, it would do nothing but learn where the water went. It would follow a drop from leaf to root to earth. It would watch an ant take the long way around a pool and a snail cross stone that had become slick with rain. By dusk, the hollow would still be shallow. The lizard might catch its prey; some of the larvae would live.

Before leaving, the power would make one small promise to the place: if a harm came that the basin could not answer in its own ways, it would return. It would not ask the basin to be harmless in the meantime.

The frog would call. Water would gather on the leaves above it, and one drop, grown too heavy to hold, would fall.
