The power watches for a long time before doing anything.

It sees a young grazer step into the soft edge of a water pan. The mud holds one broad foot fast. Its mother waits nearby, calling softly, while insects settle on the shrinking water and birds turn overhead. The pan is nearly dry; by afternoon its mud will harden.

The power loosens one stone beneath the soil. Water seeps into the print the trapped foot has made, and the mud gives way. The young grazer pulls free. Its mother noses it once, and they walk on, dark little shifts in the grass.

The power does not fill the pan. It does not silence the birds or make the plateau safe from every hunger. It has seen the insects waiting in the wet earth and the roots that need the ground to dry.

Before leaving, it turns its attention to the old paths. Where feet have begun to cut across a fragile mat of plants, it lets a seam of hardy grass take hold along the better route. Travellers will follow it without knowing why. Rain will still be brief, and the wind will still cross the plateau unhindered. But a little more soil will remain where it belongs.
