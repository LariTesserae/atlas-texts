The power would wait through a rain and then through the drying that followed it. It would watch the creature open a route, lose it to grit, and open another. At first, every blockage might look like something to mend. After a while it would see that some of the creature’s life lay in making those choices.

It would leave the channels alone.

Behind the flywheel, though, the old pipe had a split along its underside. In a long dry spell, water held in the pipe escaped into the brickwork before it could reach the fold gripping the iron. The power would close that split with a little of the basin’s own silt, pressed deep enough to stay. It would not seal the pipe or set the wheel turning. The next rain would still have to find its way in.

Then it would step back. The bird would still be startled by rising water. Foam would still catch under the overhang. A passage might still close in an afternoon, and a new one might take a season.

But when the basin was nearly dry, a few drops would remain inside the pipe. The oldest fold would have time to loosen when the rain returned.
