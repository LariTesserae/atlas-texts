The power does not stop the turbines.

It stands for a long time in a cutting, among the pale stems, while water gathers on the leaves. It hears the pitch of each machine and the deeper movement that travels beneath the rock. It cannot yet tell whether the machinery is shelter, prison, or some arrangement the inhabitants made for themselves. Wisdom, here, begins by declining to guess.

In the nearest pumping house, it finds a panel marked **EMERGENCY BYPASS**. The lettering is old; the switch beneath it is clean. The power leaves it alone.

Instead, it mends a narrow channel beside the main flow. The channel can carry enough water to keep the warm pipes warm and the pressure in the stone steady, even if one turbine pauses. It takes a long time to make, because every joint must hold without asking the plateau to change all at once. When it is finished, the great machinery runs as before. Nothing has been taken away.

Then the power goes back to the cutting and sits down. On the wall of the pumping house, within sight of the stems, it hangs a small lamp. The lamp brightens when the bypass is ready. There is no demand in it, and no schedule. It means only: *There is another way for the water to go, if you need one.*

Days pass. Rain fills the beads on the leaves; clear weather makes them small again. The lamp remains lit. Far below, a gauge needle makes its slow journey beneath the bare stone.

At last, one evening, the needle stops. The power waits, listening carefully, but no turbine falters. The pressure is steady. In the cutting, a single drop leaves its leaf and strikes the rock.

The power does not call that an answer. It does not touch the switch.

It stays to learn what happens next.
