The power would not enter by a door. It would come as a pause in the pumps: long enough for the basin to settle, not long enough for the film in the warm pipes to dry.

It would watch one cycle before touching anything. Water gathers at a seam; a portion is drawn aside. A threshold rises. High above, grit passes out of a new cut in the rock. The power would notice that the cut is approaching a pocket of loose stone. It would also notice that every time the pumps falter, water drains from one of the oldest, coolest joints.

It could seal the pocket and make the pumps run forever. Instead, it would place a small brace where the rock is weakest, leaving the cut open. Then, during the next rush, it would send a pulse through the channels: one that arrives at the basin by two routes, with a space between them.

The ridges on the water would change. After several cycles, a pulse would return.

There would be no attempt to call this a name, or to ask it to leave the walls. The power would send the shape of the loose pocket, as nearly as water could carry a shape. It would wait while the pumps ran and rested. The next grains of grit would emerge from a different cut.

At the old joint, the power would make a shallow hollow in the stone beneath the pipe. Water could collect there when the pumps stopped, within reach of the film. It would not fill the chamber or alter the rhythm of the rush. For a while the living mesh would draw from the hollow; later, a thin channel of its own making would lead back to it.

Before leaving, the power would clear one upper window. Not to let anyone in. It would open onto raw rock and a little cooler air, and the power would leave its edge rough enough to hold a drop.

Many pump cycles later, water would find that edge. A drop would hang there through one rest, then fall into the dark passage below. The pumps would start again, and the city would carry it on.
