The power would not lift the creature out of the court.

It would first learn what the pumps were doing besides moving water. Their shiver told the old one where it was. The current brought food to the grille. The troughs sheltered the young. To stop the machinery, even for the sake of quiet, would be to take away part of their world.

So the power would begin behind the wall. It would mend a failing bearing, clear a drain no one had thought to inspect, and arrange for the water to keep crossing the floor when the pumps one day stopped. It would do this slowly enough that the creature at the inlet would feel no strange morning arrive all at once.

Then it would make one small change in the court: a low gap between the maintenance troughs, always wet, wide enough for the smaller ones to pass through without crossing the open square. They might use it or not. The old creature might never notice.

The power would leave the water shallow, the concrete worn, and the rail where the bird sometimes landed. It would not teach the creature to follow the outflow or give it a reason to want another life.

One day the bird would alight above it. The contact would travel down the wall. The creature would raise its head and wait. Beneath its feet, water would go on moving at nearly the same slow pace as before.

That would be enough for the power to have done, though it would keep watch.
