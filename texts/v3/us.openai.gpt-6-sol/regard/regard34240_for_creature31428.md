The power would come in the hour before sunrise, when the chimney builders were carrying damp grains home and the undersides of the shelves had begun to bead.

It would look for a long time. There was no lack here that could be cured simply by making more: more grass would draw more mouths, more water would fill the hollows with bodies until their edges were crowded too. The hunters needed to eat. Even the dust passing through the transparent creatures had its work to do.

So the power would change almost nothing.

On the hottest mornings, when the drops would otherwise vanish before the youngest pale animals could reach them, it would hold the shade under a few shelves a little longer. Not across the whole plateau, and not every day. Just long enough for the small white backs to emerge from beneath the older ones and make their way upward.

A drop would tremble beside a flat creature’s spread foot. Those nearest would drink. The others would press forward, and for once the last of them would find a little moisture still on the stone.

By noon the ledges would be dry. The grazers would nose the seams; a hunter would pass through a trough, and the pale backs would tighten to let it by. Nothing would know it had been spared a harder morning. That, the power would think, was enough.
