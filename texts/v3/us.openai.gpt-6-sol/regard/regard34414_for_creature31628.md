The power notices the rust before the workers do. It notices the animal too.

For a while it does nothing. The rust is a line no longer than a fingernail, and the animal has food and water. To change the place merely because it is cold and narrow would be to mistake its own preferences for the animal’s needs.

But the drops keep falling. Water will find the screw beneath the plate; eventually someone will have to open the wall. So the power waits until the next scheduled inspection and helps a worker see what is already there. The amber lamp catches the pale mark. The worker follows it upward to the clamp, writes *condensation above grating* on a work order, and arranges for the drip to be led away from the steel below.

Before the repair, the power does one smaller thing. It lets the worker’s hand pause at the lamp bracket. In that light the green threads are visible. The worker has no reason to suspect an animal among them, but has no reason to scrape them off, either. They lift the bracket gently and set it in a shallow tray with a little of the water caught from the seam.

The clear animal grips a thread throughout. It cannot know that the plate beneath its feet is moving. It knows only the brief change in vibration, the water closing around its claws, and then stillness.

When the bracket goes back, the tray stays on a shelf nearby, out of the machinery. The worker means to deal with it later and forgets. Condensation gathers on its cool underside. The green threads persist.

The power does not make the animal large enough to be seen, or promise that it will live forever. It leaves the worker a sounder wall and the animal a wet edge to cross.
