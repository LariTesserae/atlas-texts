The power would not keep the gap open forever. It would see how the town is built to move, and how the roots must go on growing.

It would come at night, when the last watcher was waiting beneath a swollen drop. Up on the bank, beyond the roof, it would ease a little of the rain into the sedge. The stems would bow under the water, and their roots would drink what they could. Below, the drop would still fall, but later. The watcher would have time to touch the warning fibre and get clear.

Farther along the bank, the power would find a seam between clay and root. It would loosen no more than a worm might loosen in a season, then lay one long sedge root across it. Water would follow the root’s curve away from the seam. Moss would have a place to take hold.

By morning, one of the inhabitants carrying a wall would notice the new high patch. She would test it with her broad feet, press the wall against the root, and call someone to help. They would build there because it was sound, not because they knew who had made it so.

The power would leave the beetle at the entrance and the worms beneath the streets. It would leave the rain its weight. When the bank changed again, the town would still have to listen, lift its children, and choose where to go.

But tonight the warning would come in time. And when they went looking, there would be somewhere to build.
