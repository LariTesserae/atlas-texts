The power would wait through several turns of the wheel before doing anything. It would listen when the grind faltered, and hear how quickly every other sound fell silent.

Then it would lay a little of the day’s stored warmth inside the old machine—not enough to quicken the wheel, only enough to carry it through one turn when the hidden creature let go.

The rasping would stop. The small backs in the seams would shine and vanish. In the dark, the great limb would remain across the machine’s base, its hooks resting where they had rested for years. The wheel would turn once without it, and the rasping would return, uncertainly, one voice at a time.

The power would leave the next turn to the creature.

If it took up the work again, nothing would resist it. If it rested, the warmth would carry the wheel slowly, as the creature had carried it. By night the stone would cool and the gift would end; by day it would gather again. No new light would expose the far wall. No crack would be sealed against the little animals, and no feast would be set down to change what lived on what.

The chamber would still be dry and crowded and difficult. But in its darkest part, a being that had always moved when the wheel needed moving could learn that it was possible to be still.
