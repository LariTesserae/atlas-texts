The power does not brighten the chamber all at once.

It begins with the abandoned valve wheel. A worker passing through on an unrelated errand notices that a newer pipe has been fastened to its bracket. He moves the fastening to a sound brick pier and marks the wheel **OUT OF SERVICE**. He never sees the eggs. Nothing turns in their gap.

After the next rain, water finds a hairline crack above the cool pipe and begins to drip onto a live electrical housing. Another worker traces the leak. She seals the crack and clears grit from the basin’s drain, but leaves the pipe itself alone. Its familiar condensation still gathers at the bend. A drop falls onto the worn porcelain; silverfish wait in the seams for the next.

These are small interventions, and none makes the chamber safe from every danger. Heat still rises through the plates. The gecko still has to hunt; the silverfish still have to run. The moth may or may not be there when a door opens again.

For now, the eggs remain warm without being shaken. The basin holds a little water after rain. Beside it, the pale stems keep their narrow claim on the light, and the gecko returns through the shoulder-wide dark to the place it knows.
