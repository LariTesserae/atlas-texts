The power did not bring a dawn.

It had seen what warmth meant here, and how much could be lost by changing it carelessly. It rested a hand against a pipe and waited long enough to feel the tiny corrections made beneath its casing: a pressure steadied, grit loosened, a living joint withdrawn from the seam. The city was not abandoned. Much of its maintenance belonged to those who lived in it.

In one outer hall, a pipe had begun to cool. The change was so slight that no visitor would have noticed it, but shells were already gathering at the threshold. The power could have sealed the pipe at once. Instead, it held the pressure steady while the larger creatures worked their way toward the fault. Where the metal had worn too thin for them to mend, it gave them a new fitting, shaped like the old one and rough enough for their shells to take hold. Then it took its hand away.

There were other places the creatures could not repair: long crossings of bare stone between one source of warmth and another. The power laid narrow ribs beneath a few of those squares. They gave off almost no heat. From above, they looked like shallow scores in the paving; to a creature pressed against the ground, they made a path that could be followed without crossing a whole expanse of cooling stone.

It left the squares dark. It left some of them empty. The small creatures still had sheltered cracks, the larger ones still had pipes to tend, and neither had to travel farther than it wished.

Years later, a wall fell near the edge of the city. Shells appeared in the dust, moving toward the changed pressure. Some followed an old warm pipe. A few found one of the new ribs and crossed a square they had never crossed before. By the time they reached the fallen wall, the power was gone. It had left them a way to get there, not decided what they would do.
