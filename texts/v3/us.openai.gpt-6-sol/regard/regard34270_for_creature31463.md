The power comes when a valve opens. It feels the pressure pass through the water, sees every black fringe lie flat, and waits until the strands rise again.

It does not stop the pumps. Without them, the water in the seams would settle, and much of the life beneath the floor would dry. Nor does it mend the concrete as it once was. A sealed floor would leave the thing nowhere to live.

Instead, the power follows the water through one complete circuit. It learns which surges bring fine particles to the filaments, which tear them loose, and which carry their fragments to places where they can take hold. Then, at a valve deep below the plain, it eases a worn part into a slightly different position. The next surge still comes, but its first hard blow is gentler. At the broad channel, the water still runs fast enough to clear the grates. Behind them, a little more of it slows.

Over the years, the power makes other changes of that size. It leaves some cracks alone and braces others before they can break wide enough to scour out everything inside them. Where the floor gives up cement dust, it offers the current a little clean stone as well, so the filaments need not depend wholly on the destruction of their shelter. It makes no new fringe. The creature finds each changed place itself, or fails to.

There are still bare grates. Pieces still wash away and do not return. The pumps still roar underfoot, and water still takes something from the plain whenever it passes over it. But the loss is slower now. A thread catches behind a flake of rust and has time to thicken. Farther along, a seam that has held for years sends a few small pieces into the flow.

When the power leaves, nothing rises above the mist to mark its visit. One valve opens. Along the nearest grate, the black fringe folds down before the water reaches it. The rush passes, and the strands lift, one by one.
