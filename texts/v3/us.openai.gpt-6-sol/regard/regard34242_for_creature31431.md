The power would wait through a rain before touching anything.

From the ridge, it might be tempting to see a thousand basins and one problem: water always goes down. But down among the partitions, it would find that an hour held at a high lip is not the same as an hour spent in a dark cistern. It would watch one resident leave with the first sheet of water and another remain in a basin that looked empty. It would not remake their home into a river.

After several rains, it would choose a small basin halfway up a slope. There, beneath the overflow lip, it would make a second notch. The new notch would carry no water by itself. When the basin filled, water would pass over the old lip as it always had.

But if the resident drew back from one corner and held there, a little water would find the new opening. It would travel along the wall, around a stair, and arrive in a basin above.

The power would make only that one passage, and wait again.

Perhaps nothing would use it. Perhaps the resident would spend a whole wet season testing the edge without entering. The power would leave the notch in place, so small that it changed neither basin’s ordinary filling nor its ordinary fall.

One day, though, a thin margin might open against the stone. Water would climb. In the basin above, grit would gather into a curve and disperse.

Then the power would know whether to make another passage. Not a network laid over the valleys, not a way out for every life, but a few routes chosen slowly, by the ones who lived there. It would keep rain in the covered cisterns through the driest intervals. It would leave the high lips high and the shallow places shallow.

Most days, anyone standing on the ridge would still see the same bright slopes. A basin would fill, hesitate, and pour. Only now, on rare days, an answering drop might be heard from above.
