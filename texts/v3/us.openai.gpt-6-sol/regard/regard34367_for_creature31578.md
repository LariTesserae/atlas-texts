The power comes to the hollow during a dry week. There is no water in the basins, and the old grooves are dull with dust. The lizard with the damaged foot has reached the edge of the light and stopped there, its throat moving against the stone.

There are many things the power could change. It could fill the basins, mend the foot, soften every beetle’s shell. It waits long enough to see how each thing belongs to the others.

Then it attends to a flaw in the stone. At the lip of the smallest basin, a hairline channel lets the last of the rain escape almost as soon as the water grows shallow. The power closes that channel with limestone. Nothing else moves.

The next rain comes as it always has: first the cool smell, then the darkening roof, then an hour of running water. The birds drink. The beetles climb. The lizards wait above the shining grooves.

Two days later the broad basins are empty. In the small one, under the lip of stone, a little water remains. The damaged lizard reaches it without crossing the bright floor. A bird drinks there too, though it must fold its wings close and keep watch.

By the fourth day it is gone. Dust settles over the flint flakes. The hollow keeps its dry weeks and its sudden streams.

The power leaves without marking its work. When the next rain comes, there will be one more day to drink.
