The power would not bring rain.

It would see how much lives by the absence of it: crowns built to comb the wind, roots patient in stone, animals whose journeys matter because the green places are far apart. A river would be a gift to some things and an ending to others.

It would watch one pale animal leave a forest with seeds caught along its flank. By evening the animal would reach a ridge where a slab had slipped across the old crossing. There was a way around, but it led over loose rubble, and the animal was tired. It stood a long time in the cooling light, testing the ground with one narrow foot.

The power would do almost nothing. Beneath the fallen slab was a smaller stone, already cracked by years of heat and cold. It would ease that stone through the last fraction of its long breaking. The stone would settle; the slab would tilt; a firm passage would open along the ridge.

The animal would cross without knowing why the way was there. One seed would pull free of its coat as it passed. It would fall into the new shade beneath the slab, where dust would gather whenever the wind came from the west.

The power would not make the seed grow. It would leave that to the dust, the light, the seed, and the years.
