The power would first wait.

It would follow the heavy pulse through the rock to learn whether it was breaking anything. It would watch the water pass through the groove and return again. The colony had lived with the engines long enough to make their rhythm part of its days; an abrupt silence might be as strange to it as a louder noise. Wisdom would not mistake quiet for safety.

Finding no immediate harm, the power would leave the chamber as it was. It would not brighten the water, clear the silt, or bring other creatures in to make the place seem less empty. The colony needed no witness, and the bare stone was not asking to be filled.

Outside, though, where the engines were tended, a worker would begin to notice a change in the heavier pulse. A worn mounting, perhaps, or a load striking harder than it used to. The power would not put words in anyone’s mouth. It would make the small evidence a little easier to hear, and leave people free to repair what they found.

Then it would come back from time to time. Not to keep the chamber unchanged forever, but to notice if the water ceased to pass through the groove, or the pulses began to loosen the stone. For now, its kindness would be mostly restraint: a careful attention held over a life too thin to cast a shadow.
