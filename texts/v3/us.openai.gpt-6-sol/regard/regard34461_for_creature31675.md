The power would come to the shelf after a long dry day, when the stone still held the sun’s warmth but the seam beside the wall ran cold.

At first it might mistake the hollow notes for drops striking a pool. It would listen until one note came a little late, after a faint scrape against the chamber roof. Then it would understand that something living had let go and caught hold again.

It would not lift the wall to see.

The creatures had places to be that a hand could not reach without changing them. The power would follow the water instead, attending to where it entered the mountain and where it left. Most of its channels were sound. One, near the upper end of the shelf, was slowly filling with grit. In another dry season, a green hollow might lose its connection to the seam before rain returned.

The power would ease the grit from that channel, grain by grain, and settle it in a crack where it could do no harm. Water would continue to rise through the soil when pressed, to bead beneath ledges, to slip past the old leaves into the dark. The creatures could follow it as they always had.

It would do nothing to the bare stone between the patches. It would not make a path for the creatures across it, or bring other creatures to keep them company. The open reaches belonged to the shelf too.

Before leaving, the power would wait by the wall. A drop would fall. Something on the roof of the hidden chamber would release its hold, catch the stone again, and remain where it chose. A moment later the drop would strike the pool. Beneath the steady rushing, the sharp note would sound.
