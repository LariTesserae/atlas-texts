The power came to the shelf and rested beside the third bowl.

The sprig had grown past its rim. Its newest leaves held in the current; its oldest were darkening where they joined the stem. The bowl contained a little silt, but not enough to feed it for much longer.

The power could have opened the cliff and given it a spring. It could have filled all three bowls with plants. Instead, it listened to the water passing through the slate joints and felt, beneath the shelf, a seam so narrow that almost none of what it carried reached the valley.

It loosened one grain of stone.

A trace of mineral entered the current. Most of it passed the bowls and went on into the open water. A little settled among the sprig’s roots. The power waited through the long dark while the leaf at the rim held fast, then departed.

Years later, the sprig had grown over one wall. The other bowls still held silt. At the edge of the shelf, the stone path still ended above the drop-off; nothing had come to finish it.

The new seam would run out in time. The sprig might release a fragment before it did, or it might not. For now, there was enough.
