The power would spend a long time beneath the stair doing nothing that could be seen.

It would listen with a shell pressed to the wall, if it could make itself small enough. It would learn which rattle meant a bridge needed wedging and which was simply how that bridge sounded. It would learn that the drip in the third groove arrived a little later when the stone was cold. It would not mistake the town’s constant labor for distress.

Then it would attend to what the town could not repair. Deep below the lowest wall, where the roar came from, a seam of stone was slowly narrowing. The workers could hear the water gathering force there, but no street led to it and no tool could reach it.

The power would open a narrow passage through the bedrock *beyond* the town. On ordinary days, nothing would change: water would bead on the lintels, tick through the drains, and give each doorway its familiar pitch. Only when the deep water rose too high would the new passage take it away. The power would brace the stair above as well, so that its weight would not one day settle through the upper roofs.

It would leave the mineral crust for the workers to scrape and the bridges for them to tend. When it was done, it would listen again, to make sure it had not silenced anything by saving it.

Perhaps one of the shelled builders would pause at a crossing, feel a new, distant note through the shale, and follow it as far as the lowest wall. There would be no entrance there. After a while it would turn back toward a drain that needed clearing, carrying the sound of the town with it.
