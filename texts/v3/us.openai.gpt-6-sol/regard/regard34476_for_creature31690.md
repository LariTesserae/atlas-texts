The power would not begin with the ceiling.

It would come to the platform and wait beneath the bark roof, where a drop hung over the nearly empty bowl. The place had been made with care. Whatever had stopped it might also have kept its inhabitants safe. To set everything moving at once, simply because stillness looked strange, would be another kind of carelessness.

So it would touch only the drop.

The drop would fall. It would strike the clay with a sound small enough to leave the silence intact.

For a while, nothing else would happen. Then the frog below the platform would loosen its throat and call once. The call would travel across the bare tiles and arrive at the far gardens. A bird there would turn its head toward it.

The power would listen before doing more.

It would not take away the crossing at which a frog might be seen twice. It would not pull roots from the mortar or demand that the maker return and explain the cord. But wherever something was already trying to happen, it would give that effort room: a leaf could finish uncurling, water could enter a bowl, the shoot could raise its tile another hair’s breadth. If a creature wished to remain in its shade, the shade would remain.

By evening—if evening came—the bowl beneath the shingle might be fuller. The cord might still lie where it was left. Beyond the gardens, someone might hear a bird call and know the way back.

The power would leave before finding out.
