The power would wait through one rain before doing anything.

It would watch the water strike the hard faces of the clay and run away, watch a curled edge soften and lift, watch one small plant take what it could from the seam beneath it. The plain was still settling. A generous flood might tear open its young joints, and a new layer of clay would only bury what had begun to live there.

At the next storm, the power would move nothing larger than a cloud’s shadow. It would slow the narrow curtain of rain as it crossed the basin, letting it travel the length of a seam instead of spending itself in one place. Water would reach the buried roots gently. Some would sink past them into the joint below.

The plant would be green for a few more days. It might put out a seed; it might not. The power would leave the wind to carry it, and the clay to make room for it or refuse. Where the layer curled, the roots would continue their small work of holding it. Where rain found a new channel, the plain would keep that mark too.

Before departing, the power would touch one old handprint and find it still clear. Nothing had been remade. By afternoon the pools would be gone, but deep in several seams, the clay would remain damp.
