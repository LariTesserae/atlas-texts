The power did not touch the tower.

It had first thought to make the water move. A current might carry warmth through the six streets, or bring small living things to the bare stone. But it watched the chip of basalt beside its broken corner, and the fine particles that never sank. It felt how carefully each resident had made a home from weight. A current would enter every room, whether welcomed or not.

Instead, the power waited beside the last doorway. When the jointed visitor next came up the slope, it tapped the frames as usual. At the first house, the pressure eased a little after it left.

The visitor did not turn back.

So the power placed one thing in the street: a small piece of ceramic, loose enough to be carried, shaped to rest between two of the visitor’s limbs. One face was smooth. On the other, a thin hollow yielded to the slightest pressure and returned to its shape when released. The tower held it in place, as it held everything here, until the visitor tapped it. Then it came free.

The visitor took it down the slope. When it returned, it carried the piece to the first blue-marked door and set it there. For a long time nothing happened. Then the water pressed into the hollow. The piece bent, and held that bend.

The visitor picked it up.

The power remained long enough to see it visit another door, and to see the resident there leave the little piece untouched. It did not ask again on their behalf. One answer had been given; another had not.

Before leaving, the power made a second hollow on the smooth face. The visitor could press it flat whenever it wished, and the next resident could make a new mark. Nothing else in the district had to change. The six streets still met beneath the tower. The chip stayed by its corner. But now a reply could travel beyond the summit, and, if its maker chose, be answered.
