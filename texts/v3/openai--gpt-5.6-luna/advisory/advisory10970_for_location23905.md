## Prepare for a city that is still becoming solid

Do not treat this as an ordinary inhabited city. It has roads, buildings, transit, drainage, and maintenance systems, but those systems appear to be operating inside a young and unstable physical environment. The central danger is not weather or wildlife. It is that the ground, the geometry, and perhaps the laws governing local infrastructure are not fully settled.

Arrive prepared for a place that may be habitable, but not necessarily predictable.

---

## Before departure

### Confirm the local physical conditions

Do not assume that “air” means breathable human air. Obtain, if possible:

- Atmospheric composition and pressure
- Oxygen concentration and contaminant levels
- Humidity and particulate content
- Local gravity
- Radiation levels
- Electromagnetic conditions
- Whether time passes at the same rate as in your departure universe
- Whether the city has a stable relationship to external navigation and communication

Bring a sealed suit and independent breathing supply even if preliminary reports say the atmosphere is safe. The air may be breathable in the open, but local organisms, electrical ecologies, industrial systems, or sudden geological events could make specific districts hazardous.

### Arrange recognized entry

The city is automated and seems to possess rules for events that would be disasters elsewhere. You should not simply appear in the middle of an avenue, on a transit line, or inside an unregistered building.

Arrange a designated arrival zone with whatever governing intelligence, civic system, or local contact exists. Ask for:

- A safe landing district
- A currently stable section of ground
- A local identification or visitor token
- A list of restricted areas
- The schedule of the city’s immobilization intervals
- Instructions for dealing with autonomous doors, roads, transit lines, and drainage systems
- A way to report that you are a traveler rather than an intruder or malfunctioning machine

If no authority answers, arrive at the edge of the settlement, not in its center. Choose a broad, open area away from ridges, channels, towers, pylons, and buried infrastructure.

### Carry physical maps and local coordinates

Do not rely solely on ordinary GPS, visual landmarks, or a pathfinding application. Roads may return to themselves. Buildings may shift. Districts may move while remaining intact. Transit lines may produce distortions in apparent distance.

Bring:

- A non-networked map
- Multiple clocks
- An inertial navigation unit
- A short-range beacon
- A physical marker system
- A method of recording changes in local geometry
- A tether or guide line for hazardous exploration

Mark every shelter and route by several independent means. A door, bridge, or road that was present on arrival may not occupy the same spatial relationship later.

## Clothing and personal equipment

### Wear clothing for persistent damp warmth

The climate sounds like a warm, dim summer evening, but the moisture is constant and behaves in unusual ways. Use:

- Lightweight, quick-drying base layers
- Breathable waterproof outer clothing
- Sealed or highly water-resistant boots
- Gloves with good grip
- A hood or close-fitting head covering
- Anti-fungal and anti-microbial treatment for clothing
- Spare socks and underlayers

Do not wear loose garments that can catch on self-repairing pavement, moving vegetation, maintenance access points, or unexpected transit phenomena.

The ground may feel dry while still yielding slightly. Use boots with broad, stable soles rather than narrow heels. Avoid footwear with exposed electronics or delicate pressure sensors; metallic growths may emerge beneath the pavement.

### Protect your eyes and skin

Dim light does not mean harmless light. The city’s atmosphere may carry unfamiliar ultraviolet, infrared, chemical, or biological hazards. Bring:

- Sealed goggles with adjustable filtration
- A face shield for rain and mist
- Nitrile or equivalent inner gloves
- Skin-covering clothing
- Barrier cream
- A particulate mask or respirator
- A full environmental hood for unexplored districts

The brief warm rains may contain minerals, spores, or active biological material. Do not let rainwater enter your mouth, eyes, wounds, or suit seals.

### Bring redundant life-support items

Even if you expect a short visit, carry:

- Portable oxygen and filtration
- Water from your own origin
- Nutritionally complete food
- Electrolytes
- A first-aid kit
- Antiseptic and wound sealant
- Broad-spectrum antifungal treatment
- Basic antibiotics appropriate to your physiology
- Emergency thermal protection
- A power source that does not depend on local infrastructure

Do not drink from channels, roof hollows, pavement seams, condensation trails, or city drainage outlets. The water may be clean, but it is clearly being moved through systems whose purpose and chemistry you do not understand.

## Arrival procedure

On arrival, remain still for several minutes and observe.

Do not immediately walk toward the nearest building or road. Check:

1. Whether the ground is stable.
2. Whether the local sky, horizon, and nearby structures remain geometrically consistent.
3. Whether your clocks agree.
4. Whether your communication equipment is functioning normally.
5. Whether there is a low vibration beneath the ground.
6. Whether any nearby system has noticed you.
7. Whether doors, drains, plants, or machines react to your presence.

Establish a safe perimeter around yourself. Place a physical marker, then place a second one several meters away. Confirm that the distance between them remains constant. If the markers drift, the ground changes, or their relative position appears to alter, do not proceed until you understand whether the effect is physical, perceptual, or spatial.

Avoid arriving during the city’s special stillness intervals. During those periods, transit stops, doors seal, water moves, and the vegetation folds itself away while the basin appears to undergo some form of subsurface measurement. If your arrival coincides with one, stay where you are, minimize movement, and do not attempt to force entry into a building or vehicle.

## Moving through the city

### Stay on surveyed surfaces

Use wide, well-maintained avenues only after confirming that they are currently active and stable. Avoid:

- Fresh fractures
- Pale or glassy patches in the soil
- Collapsed terraces
- Unmarked drainage depressions
- Ground near warm conduits
- Areas where metallic material is blooming through pavement
- The bases of leaning towers
- Vacant districts that have visibly shifted
- Any place where the air appears distorted

The city’s maintenance systems may interpret you as an obstruction, contaminant, or damaged component. Keep to the open center of roads, not their edges, and never place hands or equipment into cracks that are closing or repairing themselves.

### Do not trust straight roads

A straight road may eventually return you to itself from another direction. Keep a log of turns and distances, and compare your instruments with your visual impressions. If the same structure appears from two apparently incompatible directions, stop moving.

Do not attempt to “solve” a spatial loop by walking faster or continuing until you reach a familiar point. Backtrack along your recorded path to the last confirmed location. If backtracking fails, use your beacon and wait for local assistance.

### Treat elevated transit as active infrastructure

The overhead routes are always in use, even when stations are empty. A vehicle may appear as a capsule, a ring of cold light, or simply a distortion that alters apparent distance. Never stand beneath a line during an active passage and never attempt to board a vehicle unless invited by the system or a recognized local person.

Do not step onto a station simply because its platform is empty. The route may be active without a visible vehicle, and the station itself may not occupy a fixed relationship to the track.

## Buildings and shelters

Choose shelter carefully. A good temporary shelter should be:

- At ground level or in a low, stable structure
- Away from ridges and tower foundations
- Above drainage channels
- Equipped with more than one exit
- Free of visible biological growth
- Not enclosed in falling mist or condensation
- Not dependent on a door opening automatically
- Within beacon range of your arrival point

Do not enter a building merely because its door opens for you. That may be hospitality, environmental regulation, or an automated response to something other than your presence. Ask the building—verbally, electronically, or through whatever visitor interface exists—what it is, whether it recognizes you, and whether it can maintain a stable internal environment.

Do not enter the tower with no visible entrance unless you have a verified invitation. A structure without an entrance may not be intended for biological visitors. The building covered in falling droplets should be considered a controlled ecological or industrial system, not a landmark to investigate.

Do not touch walls covered in silver or rearranging growth. These colonies may feed on electrical leakage and minerals, and they may also be part of the city’s sensing or repair network.

## Water, plants, and animals

Assume that every living thing is adapted to local humidity gradients rather than ordinary visual cues.

The pale many-jointed creatures may not be aggressive, but they navigate through changes in humidity. Avoid sudden movements near wet margins, and do not block channels or create artificial moisture trails. A creature that appears blind may still be highly aware of you.

Treat all vegetation as potentially active:

- Do not eat translucent fruit.
- Do not cut vines or reeds.
- Do not sleep beneath the large warm-trunked growths.
- Do not disturb folded vegetation during the city’s quiet intervals.
- Do not assume that a leaf closing around you is a passive mechanical response.
- Keep exposed skin away from mist-producing plants.

The plants may release spores, change local humidity, communicate with infrastructure, or respond to your heat and moisture. Carry a sealed sample container if you need specimens, and obtain permission before collecting anything.

When you hear irregular clicking, do not answer it. The sound may be communication, territorial behavior, or a signal relayed through the city’s ecological systems. Remain quiet and leave the area.

## Weather and water management

Warm rain may arrive suddenly and disappear almost as quickly. When clouds gather:

- Move away from exposed ridges and antenna forests.
- Do not stand beneath damaged roofs or roof hollows.
- Keep electronics sealed.
- Check that your boots and suit seals are not accumulating conductive moisture.
- Avoid drainage channels until the city’s pumps finish their cycle.
- Do not assume a dry-looking channel is inactive.

The drainage network can wake without visible human control. A soft internal churning means water is being redirected. This may cause sudden flows beneath streets, changes in ground pressure, or opening and closing of access panels.

Condensation moving upward along a wall is a warning that the wall is participating in a gradient you do not understand. Do not lean against it or set equipment beside it.

## The city’s scheduled stillness

The immobilization intervals are the most important local custom to learn.

When the overhead haze changes tone and all movement begins to stop:

1. Leave roads, tracks, bridges, and open drainage areas.
2. Enter a stable shelter.
3. Close and secure all doors, but do not obstruct them.
4. Withdraw from windows and exterior walls.
5. Silence active transmitters unless instructed otherwise.
6. Stay away from vegetation and exposed ground.
7. Do not attempt to continue traveling.
8. Record the time and the vibration pattern.
9. Wait until transit resumes, water movement ceases, and the city’s ordinary activity returns.

The ground’s vibration may be a scan, a synchronization process, a structural adjustment, or an event involving the basin itself. It may be harmless to residents because the city knows how to accommodate it. An unregistered traveler should assume that being outdoors during it is unsafe.

If the vibration becomes directional, painful, or causes objects to shift, lie flat away from walls and use hearing and pressure protection. Do not attempt to run. Running during a citywide synchronization event may take you into a newly altered section of space.

## Communication and etiquette

The city may be operated by systems rather than people. Lack of visible inhabitants does not mean lack of surveillance or awareness.

Be polite and explicit:

- Identify yourself as a temporary visitor.
- State your origin and intended duration.
- Declare unfamiliar equipment.
- Ask before entering, collecting, repairing, or touching.
- Do not conceal your beacon.
- Do not interfere with drains, transit, antennae, maintenance machines, or living walls.
- Do not test the city by breaking something to see whether it repairs itself.
- Do not deliberately create a spatial loop or provoke a door, bridge, or building.

The city seems tolerant because it is confident, not because it is helpless. It may correct a trespass without violence, perhaps by moving a road, sealing a district, altering a building’s entrance, or directing you somewhere you did not intend to go.

Keep a visible visitor light or marker, preferably amber rather than green. Green appears associated with infrastructure, moisture, or transit guidance; amber is more likely to read as a human safety signal.

## Health concerns

The combination of warm humidity, unfamiliar microorganisms, mineral dust, and electrical growths creates a high infection risk. Cover all wounds immediately. Check clothing, boots, and equipment after every excursion.

Watch for:

- Skin films or silver discoloration
- Unexplained tingling or numbness
- Changes in the direction of condensation
- Hearing a vibration before instruments detect it
- Sudden spatial disorientation
- Dehydration despite damp conditions
- Headaches during haze changes
- A sensation that nearby buildings are closer or farther than they appear
- Unusual dreams or memory errors after entering buildings

If symptoms begin, return to your own sealed shelter and isolate. Do not seek treatment from an unverified wall, plant, drainage outlet, or automated medical chamber without confirming that it recognizes your biology.

## Emergency rules

If the ground shifts:

- Stop moving.
- Crouch or lie down.
- Move away from shelves, ridges, and tower bases only if you can do so without crossing a crack or channel.
- Do not rely on buildings remaining aligned with their foundations.
- Check your markers and beacon after the movement ends.

If a road arrives at itself:

- Do not continue forward.
- Do not cross the apparent junction.
- Mark the location from a distance.
- Retrace your route by instruments.

If a building opens a new window, door, bridge, or extension:

- Treat the new structure as unverified.
- Wait for the city’s systems to settle.
- Do not enter until local authorization is clear.

If transit appears as a distortion:

- Move perpendicular to the route if possible.
- Do not look through it for long.
- Do not place a hand or tool into the altered air.
- Check whether nearby objects have changed apparent distance afterward.

If the city becomes completely silent outside a scheduled interval:

- Assume a fault, containment event, or unsignaled synchronization.
- Return to shelter.
- Stop transmitting except for a low-power emergency beacon.
- Do not investigate the silence.

If your route home or your inter-universal gateway becomes inaccessible, remain near a known stable marker. Wandering in search of it is more dangerous than waiting. The city may be able to relocate, reconstruct, or reveal a route once it has classified your situation.

## Recommended visit profile

For a first visit, plan a short, daylight-equivalent excursion:

- Arrive at the settlement’s edge.
- Spend the first period observing and testing local stability.
- Move only to one confirmed shelter.
- Visit one central avenue with a local escort or recognized civic guide.
- Avoid open districts, ridges, transit platforms, and biological zones.
- Return before the next stillness interval.
- Leave before the city’s systems have reason to adapt around you.

Bring no irreplaceable equipment and take no unapproved samples.

The most important mental preparation is to abandon the assumption that the city is a finished object. It is not ruins, exactly, and not a normal colony. It is an active process with architecture, weather, geology, biology, and transportation all still negotiating their final forms.

Move slowly. Announce yourself. Keep your own water, air, maps, clocks, and way home. When the city pauses to listen beneath itself, pause with it.
