## Preparing to Visit the City in the Hollow Stem

Treat this as a visit to a living wetland organism, not a conventional city. The settlement is inhabited, but the stem is not under human control. Its walls, floors, passages, weather, and animal life are all part of an active biological system. You are entering a nest, a lung, a root canal, and a partially domesticated ecosystem at once.

Your first priority is to avoid being mistaken for prey, a disease source, a source of fire, or an invasive object.

### 1. Arrive with the right body and clothing

The interior will be cold, humid, dark, and constantly wet. Expect water on every surface, mud at the lower levels, and condensation inside your clothing.

Wear:

- A close-fitting waterproof outer layer
- Soft, flexible clothing underneath
- Waterproof gloves with good tactile sensitivity
- Boots with quiet, nonmetallic soles and excellent grip
- A hood or close-fitting head covering
- Eye protection against dripping water and insects
- Clothing that cannot snag easily on roots or tendrils
- No dangling cords, loose straps, bells, metal ornaments, or hard protrusions

Avoid heavy armor or rigid equipment. The platforms and bridges are living structures, not engineered load-bearing floors. They may support a person, but not necessarily a person carrying a large pack and metal frame.

You should also avoid exposed skin wherever possible. The city contains biting, stinging, parasitic, and unknown organisms, and the water is unlikely to be safe for ordinary human wounds.

Bring several changes of dry base layers in sealed waterproof pouches. Once wet, the interior offers very few ways to dry anything. Do not rely on fire.

### 2. Do not bring fire

Fire is likely to be dangerous, socially unacceptable, or both.

The air is damp, the plant is living tissue, and the city is composed almost entirely of combustible or vulnerable plant material. Smoke could panic the inhabitants, attract predators, damage the stem, or cause the plant to seal passages and suffocate a district.

Bring:

- Shielded, low-intensity electric lights
- Several independent light sources
- Waterproof batteries
- A dim red or amber mode
- Glow markers that can be removed without piercing or cutting the plant

Use light sparingly. Sudden bright illumination may frighten the inhabitants and may attract animals. Never shine a powerful beam upward into an unobserved darkness. The city’s people understand the dark better than you do, and they may interpret an illuminated ceiling as an immediate threat.

A tactile navigation system is more important than illumination. Bring cord, soft tags, or removable bands for marking your own route, but ask permission before attaching anything to living growth.

### 3. Carry no sharp tools unless absolutely necessary

The inhabitants have a strict rule against piercing the outer wall. You must adopt the same rule immediately.

Do not carry visible knives, picks, ice axes, climbing spikes, drills, saws, or anything that could be mistaken for a wall-cutting tool. If you need a tool kit, keep it compact and sealed, and explain every item before entering.

Never:

- Test the wall with a blade
- Drive anchors into roots
- Cut a tendril to clear a path
- Remove plant tissue as a souvenir
- Widen a doorway
- Open a sealed pod
- Break a leaf-record or knot-marker
- Pull on a root that appears to be part of a bridge

The stem is young and under tension. A tiny injury may become a rupture, flood, collapse, or sudden defensive growth. The local people may regard an accidental puncture as an act of murder or sabotage.

### 4. Protect yourself from the water

The water may be biologically active, contaminated, or capable of carrying unfamiliar organisms. The black stream should be treated as unsafe for drinking, bathing, washing wounds, or placing bare hands into.

Bring:

- Your own sealed drinking water
- A compact filtration and sterilization system
- Waterproof wound dressings
- Antiseptic supplies
- Anti-inflammatory medication
- Broad-spectrum antibiotics if medically appropriate
- Antifungal treatment
- Oral rehydration salts
- A way to test basic water chemistry
- A means of drying and isolating wet clothing

Do not assume that filtration alone is sufficient. Some organisms may be too small to remove or may release toxins into the water. Ask whether the inhabitants have a trusted drinking source. Their water may still be unsuitable for you, but they will know which pools are associated with illness, predators, or plant cycles.

Do not cross the stream casually. The local rule against crossing when white petals are present should be regarded as an absolute biological warning. The petals may indicate breeding, chemical release, flooding, or the movement of something that hunts in the water.

If an animal touches you, wash and isolate the area as soon as possible. If you suffer a bite or puncture, do not wait to see whether it swells. Tell your hosts immediately; they may recognize the animal and know whether its venom, eggs, or scent attracts other predators.

### 5. Prepare for darkness and vertical confusion

The hollow is narrow but effectively immense. Height will be difficult to judge, and darkness will make ordinary spatial instincts unreliable. A bridge may seem to end in open air when it continues above or below you. A stair may descend into water. A sound may travel through plant tissue and appear to come from the wrong direction.

Before entering, practice:

- Moving slowly without looking directly at your feet
- Climbing with three points of contact
- Navigating by touch
- Remaining still when you lose the path
- Distinguishing a living surface from a loose object
- Following a guide without grabbing them abruptly

Keep one hand available for feeling roots, walls, or guide-lines. Never rush after a person who disappears into the dark. If separated, stop where you are and use the agreed signal. Searching blindly is more likely to lead you into a pool, predator territory, or an unmarked passage than back to your group.

A simple tether may be useful, but it must have a quick-release mechanism. If an animal grabs the line, you must be able to abandon it immediately. Do not tie yourself to the plant.

### 6. Learn the local rules before you enter

You should not enter alone. Arrange for a resident escort, preferably someone accustomed to guiding outsiders. Their guidance will matter more than maps, because the city is continuously growing and changing.

Learn the following before arrival:

- How to announce yourself
- Which gestures mean peaceful intent
- How to indicate that you are lost
- How to request shelter
- How to signal injury
- How to ask whether a passage is safe
- What sounds are forbidden
- Which spaces are private
- How to tell a real person from an animal or echo

The warning about voices from above is particularly important. Never answer a voice merely because it sounds human. First confirm that you can feel or see the speaker’s feet on a bridge, or have your guide identify them. Something in the upper darkness may imitate voices, or a person may be calling from a dangerous position where helping them would expose you.

Do not touch people without warning. Physical contact may be interpreted as aggression, contamination, or an attempt to pull someone from a bridge. Announce yourself verbally and with the locally accepted hand sign.

### 7. Respect the plant as a citizen, not scenery

The inhabitants do not merely live inside the plant. They understand themselves as living with it, through it, and partly because of it. Their roads, houses, records, graves, and boundaries are all biological.

Do not describe the plant as a building or mine it for resources. Do not ask whether the plant “belongs” to the city in a way that assumes ownership. A better approach is to ask what the plant permits, provides, remembers, or refuses.

Watch for signs of stress:

- Tightening tendrils
- Sudden contraction of a wall
- Silvering leaves
- Red flushing
- Rapid water movement
- A newly opened seam
- Widespread silence among insects
- Platforms trembling without visible cause

If the people tell you to leave a district, withdraw immediately. They may be responding to pressure, flooding, a predator, a chemical change, or a movement within the stem that you cannot perceive.

### 8. Understand the animals’ importance

Do not expect the inhabitants to eliminate pests. Many creatures are food, pollinators, cleaners, warning systems, or parts of a predator balance. Killing animals casually may create a larger problem.

In particular:

- Do not crush masses of mites or insects unless they are attacking you.
- Do not reach into wet seams.
- Do not put hands near the underground stream.
- Do not approach motionless amphibians.
- Do not stand beneath unobserved overhead growth.
- Do not investigate clicking, scratching, or breathing sounds alone.
- Do not leave food waste behind.
- Do not sleep near water, warm roots, or open wall pores.

If a harmless creature enters your clothing, do not panic and thrash. Move toward a resident or a well-lit, inhabited platform. Sudden movement may attract predators even when the original animal is harmless.

The local rule about dead creatures is practical: carcasses draw larger animals, and those animals may learn the route to human shelters. Dispose of biological waste only as instructed.

### 9. Bring food, but do not assume you can share it

Bring sealed, low-odor provisions for yourself. The city’s food—moss cakes, fungus eggs, inner bark, beetles, and other organisms—may be nutritious to the residents but poisonous or indigestible to a human from another universe.

Do not eat local food until you have had a controlled test under medical supervision. Do not offer imported food to children or animals. Sugar, meat, strong spices, fermentation products, and artificial scents could attract organisms that the city normally does not encounter.

If you bring gifts, choose useful nonbiological objects:

- Waterproof containers
- Soft fasteners
- Durable cloth
- Simple ceramic or glass vessels
- Medical supplies
- Low-energy sealed lights
- Fine cord
- Noncorrosive tools
- Writing or recording materials that do not require cutting leaves

Avoid seeds, soil, insects, untreated wood, living plants, and anything that can reproduce. You should arrive in quarantine-clean clothing and equipment, with all mud, spores, eggs, and organic debris removed.

### 10. Be careful with sound

The city’s quiet is not peaceful in the ordinary sense. Sound travels through the stem and may reveal your position. A dropped metal object, a shout, or repeated footsteps could be noticed throughout a district.

Use soft equipment. Keep voices low. Do not whistle, imitate animal sounds, drum on walls, or call into the darkness for amusement.

The inhabitants may use specific rhythms or tapping patterns to communicate through roots and bridges. Do not copy these until you understand what they mean. An innocent imitation could be a warning, a distress call, a territorial challenge, or an announcement that a predator has entered.

When the city goes silent, become silent too. Widespread silence is likely to mean that something dangerous is nearby.

### 11. Plan for rapid environmental change

The stem grows quickly. Your route may change during your stay. A familiar passage can narrow, thicken, flood, or become part of the wall. A fresh seam may open into the marsh and close before everyone has passed through.

Establish:

- A fixed meeting point in an inhabited district
- At least two return routes
- A maximum time away from your guide
- A signal for immediate evacuation
- A way to identify the current “outside” direction
- A written or tactile log of route changes
- A departure deadline before the plant’s weather cycle changes

Do not assume that a map drawn on arrival will remain accurate. Have your guide inspect every route before you use it, especially after rain or a major contraction.

If the walls begin moving around you, do not fight the movement. Flatten yourself against a stable platform if possible, protect your head, and wait for instructions. Trying to wedge open a closing passage could injure you and cause the plant to grip or seal around you.

### 12. Prepare psychologically

The greatest danger may be your own reflexes.

You will repeatedly experience situations that feel impossible or threatening:

- A floor flexing beneath you
- A doorway closing like a mouth
- Hair or fingernails appearing in new plant tissue
- A voice coming from an empty shaft
- A large body turning below the stream
- A wall breathing or changing temperature
- A house becoming part of the stem
- An entire population of insects moving over the walls
- Long periods in which no one speaks and nothing visible moves

Do not interpret every movement as an attack. Do not interpret every silence as safety. Learn to distinguish alarm from ordinary growth, and trust residents who have spent their lives reading those distinctions.

Sleep only in an assigned pod or protected shelter. Keep your gear packed and your exit clothing accessible. Do not sleep with your hand outside the membrane. Do not leave a door-flap open after the second condensation, even if the room feels stuffy.

### 13. The safest arrival procedure

On arrival, do not enter during heavy rain, a visible contraction, a period of mass insect movement, or a fresh wall rupture unless the residents specifically request it.

A good arrival sequence would be:

1. Wait outside until a resident confirms the opening is stable.
2. Remove all mud, plant matter, and foreign organisms from your clothing.
3. Declare your number, purpose, and length of stay.
4. Allow your equipment to be inspected.
5. Enter with both hands visible and no sharp tool drawn.
6. Stop at the first inhabited platform.
7. Remain there until your guide explains the local signs and current hazards.
8. Drink only from your own supply.
9. Learn the day’s water, animal, and growth warnings.
10. Travel no farther until you can identify the city’s basic markers.

### 14. What to leave behind

Do not bring:

- Firearms unless there is an extraordinary necessity and the residents explicitly permit them
- Open flames
- Aerosols or strong chemical sprays
- Loud machinery
- Drones
- Unsealed food
- Live soil or plants
- Unsterilized biological samples
- Bright floodlights
- Climbing hardware that pierces living tissue
- Scented products
- Pets
- Anything that can reproduce or burrow

Most importantly, do not bring the assumption that a city must be dry, illuminated, permanent, or separated from the wilderness.

This city is alive in the literal sense. Its streets are growth patterns. Its weather is circulation. Its walls are skin. Its records are leaves. Its graveyards become architecture. The residents survive not by conquering the ecosystem, but by noticing it early enough to cooperate with it.

Visit quietly, stay close to your hosts, keep your hands away from warm roots and black water, and remember that the darkness is not empty.
