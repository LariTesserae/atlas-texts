## Preparation for Visiting the Highland Basin

Treat this place as a **high-altitude industrial greenhouse and water-treatment facility**, not as a garden or a natural wetland. It is small, wet, enclosed, mechanically active, and only partly under human control. You can visit safely, but only if you arrive prepared to be cold, damp, short of breath, and surrounded by equipment that may react to your presence.

### Before departure

#### Confirm that your body can tolerate the altitude

The basin is above a sea of cloud, and the description suggests genuinely reduced atmospheric pressure. Even though the air is warm and humid, it may not contain enough oxygen for an unacclimatized person.

Bring or arrange:

- A portable oxygen monitor
- A compact emergency oxygen supply
- Medication for altitude sickness, if medically appropriate
- A way to descend quickly
- A travel companion trained to recognize confusion, stumbling, unusual fatigue, blue lips, or loss of coordination

Do not assume the warmth means the altitude is harmless. The heated grilles may keep the plants alive without making the air comfortable for human exertion.

Spend at least several hours, preferably a day, acclimatizing at a lower altitude before entering. Avoid alcohol, sedatives, and strenuous activity immediately beforehand.

#### Establish a return anchor

Because this is a multiverse journey, do not rely on the visible service door as your only route home. The basin has low visibility, confusing reflections, repeated walls, and infrastructure that may change its configuration during pressure events.

Bring:

- A redundant return beacon
- A tether or homing signal that functions through concrete, steel, mist, and cloud
- A second independent navigation method
- A physical marker that does not shed material or contaminate the site
- A departure window with generous extra time

Your return anchor should be placed outside the machinery’s drainage cycle and well away from grates, gutters, warm vents, or active maintenance platforms. Do not leave anything in a channel or planting bed. If the facility treats foreign objects as obstructions, it may cut, drain, heat, or sterilize them.

If possible, arrive at a known access platform rather than materializing directly inside the basin. Appearing inside a sealed planting bed, behind a panel, or over an open drain would be dangerous and could damage the water system.

### Clothing and equipment

Wear clothing suited to **constant mist, rapid temperature changes, and slippery artificial surfaces**.

Recommended clothing:

- Waterproof outer shell with a hood
- Warm insulating layer beneath it
- Moisture-wicking base layer
- Waterproof trousers
- Waterproof boots with aggressive, non-marking soles
- Thin thermal gloves beneath tougher work gloves
- Eye protection or sealed safety goggles
- A headlamp with a red-light setting
- A respirator or at least a high-quality particulate mask

The respirator is not necessarily needed for ordinary breathing, but the site contains sterilizing fluid, mineral deposits, biological growth, warm vapor, and possibly aerosols from the filtration system. Avoid inhaling mist directly, especially near the processing tower, overflow housing, and pressure vents.

Your equipment should be:

- Sealed against water
- Resistant to condensation
- Mechanically simple
- Free of loose straps, dangling cords, and exposed metal hooks
- Clearly labeled with your name or team designation

Do not wear fabrics that shed fibers, glitter, loose dyes, seeds, burrs, or synthetic fluff. Do not carry open food. Do not bring ordinary soil, houseplants, untreated wood, feathers, or insects. This installation is an artificial ecology, and a single introduced organism could become part of it.

Use a sealed medical kit, not one packed in paper or cardboard. Bring electrolyte tablets and water from your own world, but do not refill your bottle from the runnels or filtration tower unless the facility’s operators explicitly permit it.

### What to expect on arrival

Your first impression may be bodily rather than visual.

You may notice:

- Mild breathlessness
- Dampness on exposed skin
- A sense of warmth near the floor and chill at the walls
- Condensation on lenses and instruments
- The smell of wet stone, hot metal, leaves, and disinfectant
- A faint vibration through your boots
- Difficulty judging distance through mist and wet reflections

The basin may appear to float above the clouds. Do not approach the parapet immediately. The combination of altitude, cloud movement, refraction, and wet glass can make the horizon look inverted or displaced. A visitor who leans over to inspect distant ridges may misjudge the edge.

Stay near the arrival point until your breathing, balance, and vision settle. Check that your return beacon is transmitting before moving farther inward.

### Understand the facility’s cycle

The place operates according to a recurring water and pressure sequence:

1. Condensation is gathered.
2. Water enters the filtration tower.
3. The water is cleaned.
4. It is distributed through the planting beds.
5. Heat is exchanged beneath the floor.
6. Water drains back below the installation.

Do not cross a channel merely because it looks shallow. A runnel may be part of a timed flow system, and the water could become suddenly warmer, more chemically active, or faster-moving.

Observe the machinery for one complete cycle before exploring. Watch for:

- Valves opening
- Transparent columns filling
- Pumps starting
- Fans changing angle
- Blue pathway lights activating
- Red pulses beneath grates
- Pressure equalization
- Mist suddenly thickening

The safest period is likely the quiet interval after filtration and distribution, before the next pressure adjustment. Avoid standing near the tower when it fills, and never place your hand near a valve, pipe joint, belt cover, or transparent column.

If a low vibration passes through the floor and the leaves begin flickering without wind, stop moving until the vibration ends. It may simply be a turbine changing load, but it could also indicate a pressure transition.

### Movement rules

Use only designated walkways and platforms. The clean, pale surfaces are deceptive: they will be slick with condensation, moss, mineral film, or plant residue.

Observe the following rules:

- Do not step on planting beds.
- Do not place weight on drainage covers.
- Do not climb over parapets or service rails.
- Do not use pipes as handrails.
- Do not enter a maintenance hatch.
- Do not squeeze behind panels or into cable trays.
- Do not walk beneath suspended equipment.
- Keep clear of warm floor grilles.
- Keep your hands away from roots entering cracks.
- Do not touch green films, eggs, insects, or the amphibian.
- Do not block sensors, vents, gutters, or access doors.

The roots are not harmless decorative growth. They are already exerting pressure on the structure and exploring buried pipes. Disturbing them could release water, dislodge a slab, expose a hot line, or trigger automated pruning.

The moss beneath stairs may be soft but extremely slippery. The artificial soil in the circular vats may conceal irrigation hardware, heating loops, or biological material. Treat every apparently empty cavity as occupied.

### Water, plants, and animals

Do not drink from the runnel, transparent column, condensation gutters, or overflow housing. “Filtered” does not mean safe for an unadapted visitor. The system may be removing minerals or organisms for the plants rather than producing human drinking water.

Do not eat the red flowers, fruit, leaves, moss, or insects. Some may be harmless; some may contain concentrated nutrients, disinfectant residues, or compounds evolved under conditions unlike those in your world.

Do not collect specimens. This includes:

- Seeds
- Spores
- Roots
- Mineral crusts
- Eggs
- Insects
- Water samples
- Pieces of moss
- Sludge from drains

The pale insects may be native to the installation, and the amphibian may be associated with the filtration ecology. It is possible that its “silver eggs” are not eggs in the ordinary sense, or that removing one would disrupt a pipe-based life cycle.

If you encounter the amphibian, do not pursue it. Record it from a distance and let it retreat into the overflow housing.

### Dealing with the machinery

The equipment is quiet enough to encourage carelessness. That is dangerous. Large machines warn you with noise; this place warns you with small changes:

- A click of relays
- A soft hiss
- A change in fan direction
- A pressure tremor
- A status lamp changing color
- A plant moving without visible wind
- Mist forming suddenly along the floor

Consider every such change a possible operational signal.

Do not attempt to “help” the facility. Do not prune a vine, clear a gutter, wipe a sensor, turn a valve, or remove a mineral deposit. The apparent disorder may be part of an adaptive correction process. A plant crossing a sensor may already be scheduled for cutting; an obstruction may be holding a pipe in a particular position; a leak may have been deliberately left as an overflow.

Never stand over a grate when the red light is pulsing. Never look directly into a pressurized transparent column at close range. Keep your face away from vents, especially when mist begins to erase the far walls.

If a hidden vent opens and visibility collapses, stop immediately. Do not attempt to navigate through the whiteout. Lower yourself if necessary, activate your beacon, and wait for the mist to clear. The walls are close, but the site can still become disorienting enough to send you toward a drop, hot grille, or restricted area.

### Biological and cross-world precautions

Your greatest threat to the basin may be contamination from you.

Before entering:

- Sterilize boots and equipment.
- Wear clean outer garments.
- Seal hair and exposed skin as much as practical.
- Keep food, soil, paper, and organic waste in sealed containers.
- Do not sneeze or cough toward planting beds or water channels.
- Avoid touching your face, then touching railings or plants.
- If injured, contain all blood and leave promptly.

After leaving:

- Quarantine clothing and equipment.
- Do not carry mist, water, plant fragments, or insects back through the return gate.
- Inspect bags, boot treads, cuffs, and equipment joints.
- Decontaminate before entering another world or habitat.

The same applies in reverse. If your multiverse transit system has an established sterilization protocol, use it before arrival. The basin is already developing a new ecological identity from the meeting of machinery and life; an organism from another reality could alter that process catastrophically.

### Psychological preparation

The basin is likely to feel alive without being inhabited in a familiar way. Its infrastructure breathes, its plants respond to hidden schedules, and its few animals seem to belong to pipes and drains rather than to an outside ecosystem.

You may experience:

- The sensation of being watched by sensors
- A false impression that the room is expanding or contracting
- Unease at the lack of birds and ordinary animal sounds
- Confusion from reflections in wet walls
- The feeling that the plants are following water rather than light
- Anxiety when the mountain becomes visible beneath the clouds
- A strong urge to investigate cracks, conduits, and hidden cavities

Do not let curiosity override procedure. The basin is not ruined wilderness waiting to be explored. It is a functioning system in the early stages of becoming something else. Your role should be that of a careful observer, not an archaeologist, gardener, rescuer, or repair technician.

### Best visiting protocol

A sensible visit would be brief and structured:

1. Arrive at a stable external platform.
2. Confirm oxygen levels, pressure, beacon function, and route home.
3. Acclimatize for several minutes.
4. Observe one complete water cycle from a safe walkway.
5. Photograph or record without touching anything.
6. Inspect only the central public or service route.
7. Avoid all active maintenance zones and biological cavities.
8. Leave before dusk if possible, before the blue pathway lights and red drainage indicators dominate navigation.
9. Decontaminate before returning through the multiverse transit point.

Do not visit alone. Bring one person for navigation and one for environmental monitoring if circumstances allow. Keep the party small; the basin has limited floor space, few safe resting areas, and no evidence that it was designed for visitors.

### The most important rule

Remember that the place is neither a garden nor a factory in isolation. It is a controlled experiment that has begun to acquire its own ecology.

The machinery is still in charge, but less completely than before. Water is finding unplanned routes. Roots are entering cracks. Moss, mineral deposits, insects, and hidden organisms are occupying the seams between design and accident.

Respect those seams. Keep to the dry marked surfaces, stay out of the cycles, bring nothing living in or out, and leave before the installation has time to incorporate you into its next one.
