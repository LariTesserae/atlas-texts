## Preparation for Visiting the Unfinished Basin

Treat this location as an active developmental environment rather than a landscape. It is not merely wet, warm, or alien; it is still deciding what its own physical rules are. Your main objective should be to remain a passive, clearly bounded object while observing as little as possible and disturbing nothing.

Do not arrive alone.

Bring at least one companion whose job is to monitor you, the environment, and the route back. Since distance and direction may become unreliable near certain features, carry several independent navigation systems: a conventional inertial recorder, a tether, a timed beacon, and physical markers that cannot be mistaken for native growth. Do not rely on visible landmarks. They may change shape, move, or become something else.

### Before arrival

#### Verify that the entry point is stable

If your multiverse-travel method permits choosing an arrival point, do not materialize:

- In the central pool
- On or inside any black mineral plate
- Near the western arch
- Within the mist layer
- On a reed, frond cluster, or structure
- At the basin rim
- During or immediately after a silence interval

Arrive on a broad, dry-looking patch of exposed ground, preferably near the apparent center of the basin but well away from the pool and all construction. “Dry-looking” may not mean dry, so test the ground with a disposable probe before transferring your full mass.

The safest entry would be a brief remote observation first, followed by an unmanned probe. The probe should remain for at least several local cycles of weather, silence, pool movement, and ground correction. A camera image alone is not enough. Send instruments capable of measuring:

- Atmospheric composition and pressure
- Heat flow
- Sound propagation
- Local gravity and inertial behavior
- Radiation
- Biological contamination
- Temporal irregularities
- Changes caused by observation

Assume that the place may react to being observed. A probe that stares continuously at one structure might accidentally stabilize it, attract something, or turn itself into part of the site.

#### Protect yourself from the environment

Wear a sealed suit with independent oxygen and humidity control. The air may be breathable, but this is not established, and the local organisms clearly use unusual respiratory and sensory systems. Your suit should include:

- A hard outer shell, not merely fabric
- Full face and eye protection
- Replaceable filters and an isolated oxygen supply
- Strongly sealed joints
- Thermal regulation
- Chemical and biological sampling ports
- Acoustic dampening
- A visible, non-emissive tether or marker line
- Emergency beacon with a purely mechanical backup

The warmth is described as gentle, but continuous warmth combined with saturated air can produce rapid dehydration, fatigue, and impaired judgment. More importantly, heat attracts local life and may stimulate mineral growth. Do not use an exposed heating unit, flare, camp stove, or bright thermal lamp. Your body itself will be a conspicuous heat source.

Bring insulated containers for samples, but plan not to collect any. The stones, water, organisms, and construction materials may be in unstable states. A specimen could continue changing after removal, react to attention, or carry a local process back through your transit system.

#### Prepare for sound sensitivity

Sound is not just communication here. It can move water, bend mist, wake machinery, and perhaps influence the unfinished ground. Avoid radios with open speakers, loud alarms, metal tools, gunfire, and unnecessary speech.

Use silent or bone-conduction communication if possible. Set all equipment to vibrate or display-only mode. Carry hearing protection, but do not block all sound permanently: the environment’s sounds may be warnings, and the periodic basin-wide silence is significant.

Do not imitate the calls of the animals. Do not strike the mineral plates to test their resonance. Do not sing, clap, shout, or use sonar unless absolutely necessary.

### Upon arrival

Remain still for the first several minutes.

Let the environment register your presence without adding movement, heat, sound, or light. Observe whether:

- The ground swells beneath you
- Nearby pools shift toward you
- Animals approach or retreat
- Structures become more solid
- Your shadow behaves normally
- Your equipment readings agree with one another
- Any sound appears to come from below the ground

Establish a marked “safe point” immediately. The marker should be a physical object whose location is recorded by tether and instrument, not merely by sight. Place no marker into the ground if you can avoid it; the ground may absorb, grow around, or relocate it.

Keep your group close together. The basin is small enough that separation should be unnecessary, and distance is unreliable in the vicinity of pools, shadows, and black reeds. Maintain direct line of sight, but do not stare continuously at structures or animals. Use brief observations and rotate attention.

### Movement rules

Walk slowly and deliberately. Test every step before committing weight. The surface may be:

- Soft but self-restoring
- Rigid but resonant
- Warm enough to attract organisms
- Wet without visible water
- In the process of becoming something else

Never step on a black mineral plate unless there is no alternative. Do not strike, cut, scrape, heat, or lean against one. A plate may respond physically, acoustically, or informationally. A bootprint that closes behind you may not indicate harmlessness; it may indicate that the ground is learning your shape.

Avoid the black reeds by a generous margin. They are not necessarily rooted, and their condensation cups produce impacts with unusual acoustic force. Their shadows are explicitly associated with unreliable distance. Do not cross through a reed’s shadow, even if the reed itself appears far away.

Do not wade through any pool. Do not reach into water, even shallow water, and do not drink it. The water rearranges itself when unobserved, something turns beneath the central sheet, and animals may enter or emerge from it. Any pool could be a biological habitat, a sensor, a transit surface, or a mouth of some larger process.

Move only across open ground that remains visually consistent while watched. If a wet patch changes position, route around it rather than trying to determine why.

### Animals and other life

Assume every organism can detect you, even if it appears blind, headless, or stationary. “Small” does not mean harmless. Some creatures alter their anatomy while moving. Others can cause disappearance without physical contact, feed on heat, or use sound as a weapon or tool.

Do not touch, feed, trap, illuminate, or approach the animals. Do not kill them unless they physically attack and there is no retreat. A dead organism may not remain dead, and its shadow, sound, or other property may persist separately from its body.

Keep thermal output low. Avoid sudden warm objects, exposed skin, and active electrical equipment. The winged shapes feed on heat and may descend toward you. If one approaches, do not swat at it. Reduce heat output, remain still, and allow it to lose interest. A protective suit should be designed to spread and mask body heat rather than present a bright thermal source.

The waxy hare-like animals are especially worth avoiding. Their calls can ripple water and may be intended to locate or stun something. If they begin calling, stop moving and lower your equipment’s emissions. Do not assume that their listening posture means they are harmless.

### The structures

Treat all six constructions as active instruments, not ruins.

You may observe them from a distance, but do not:

- Enter them
- Place a hand on a panel
- Lean against them
- Remove material
- Leave equipment inside
- Focus a beam on them for long periods
- Speak directly to them
- Attempt to complete, repair, or align them

Attention has weight in this place. Sustained observation may make a structure solid, awaken it, or force it into a more definite and possibly dangerous state. Use intermittent observation from multiple angles. If a structure appears to change whenever you look away, assume the change is real and potentially deliberate.

The sensation of being remembered by something that has never met anyone should be treated as a warning, not a curiosity. It may indicate recognition, recording, prediction, or incorporation into the site.

### The western arch

Do not pass through the arch.

Do not put any part of your body, tool, probe, tether, beam, or vehicle through it. Do not stand in its framed air. Do not use it as a camera window. Its alternate basin contains reversed movement and displaced water, which suggests more than a visual illusion. It may be a parallel state, a temporal inversion, or a region where causality is arranged differently.

The arch’s low tone should be treated as an operational event. When it sounds:

1. Stop moving.
2. Withdraw from the western side if you are nearby.
3. Secure your tether.
4. Silence all equipment.
5. Avoid touching the ground or nearby structures.
6. Wait until droplets, animals, and shadows return to ordinary behavior.

If the tone occurs during a basin-wide silence, return to your safe point immediately if possible. Do not investigate the source below the world.

### Weather and ground corrections

Weather can change in one breath, so carry no equipment that depends on gradual warning. Your suit must tolerate warm rain, condensation from below, metallic dust, and rapid changes in pressure.

If rain begins, do not seek shelter inside a structure. Take cover only behind inert, already-tested terrain, and keep your faceplate sealed. If droplets rise from the ground, remain tethered and avoid low areas. If metallic dust appears:

- Stop movement
- Seal all intake ports
- Switch to internal air
- Avoid touching illuminated organisms or surfaces
- Do not remove your helmet until fully decontaminated

The long ground shudder is not ordinary seismic activity. When it begins, kneel or brace yourself, secure loose equipment, and do not attempt to cross the basin. Afterward, assume every map, marker, and route is obsolete until checked. Pools may have moved, structures may have appeared, and the rim may no longer correspond to its previous position.

### The silence intervals

When all creatures become silent, freeze.

Do not speak, move, test the ground, or look directly into the central pool unless your safety requires it. The silence seems to expose the basin’s larger scale and may correspond to a system-wide transition. It is possible that the normal activity is not noise but camouflage, and that the silence reveals the actual mechanism of the place.

Watch your instruments rather than the landscape if possible. Confirm that your companions remain present and that their shadows still correspond to them. If something moves in the central pool, do not approach. This is the moment when you should be most ready to leave.

### Exit criteria

Leave immediately if any of the following occur:

- Your shadow points in a direction inconsistent with your companion’s
- A structure begins following your movement
- A pool forms between you and your tether
- Your footprints return from a direction you have not traveled
- An animal appears inside your suit’s perimeter
- Equipment records different elapsed times
- The arch shows your arrival point rather than the alternate basin
- The ground begins forming around your boots
- A familiar voice comes from below the ground
- You are unable to see the safe point while standing within the open basin
- The creatures stop responding to heat, sound, or movement in their usual ways
- The central pool rises into a standing figure

Do not wait for the place to become hostile in an obvious manner. Its danger lies partly in processes that look calm, incomplete, or merely beautiful.

### Leaving and decontamination

Use the same route only if the route still exists in physical and instrument records. Do not navigate by memory. If the return aperture is generated at the safe point, ensure that no native matter, organism, shadow, water film, or altered air crosses with you. A sealed transit chamber is strongly recommended.

After departure, quarantine the traveler, suit, tools, and transit apparatus separately. Maintain isolation until you have confirmed that:

- No material continues changing phase
- No condensation forms in empty space
- No sound is emitted without a source
- No shadows detach or behave independently
- No equipment records events that occurred after return
- No new objects appear in storage
- No organisms emerge from samples, seams, or water droplets

Do not bring back a stone as a souvenir. In a place where matter is still deciding whether to become rock, liquid, or information, the most dangerous specimen may be the one that seems inert.

The correct mindset is not that you are exploring a valley. You are entering a small, exposed portion of a living or self-assembling system. Stay cool, quiet, tethered, and temporary. Observe without insisting on being understood. Leave before the basin has finished deciding what you are.
