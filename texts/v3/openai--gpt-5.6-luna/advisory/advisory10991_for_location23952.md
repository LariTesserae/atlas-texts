## Preparation for visiting the rooted industrial settlement

Prepare for this place less like a jungle expedition and more like a visit to a damp, inhabited ruin that is also a working agricultural machine. Its dangers are gradual: cold, wetness, contamination, unstable surfaces, hidden machinery, and accidentally disrupting systems that keep the settlement alive.

### Before departure

**Confirm biological compatibility.**  
The settlement is dominated by fungi, mosses, algae, medicinal plants, and decomposing organic matter. A traveler from another universe may be vulnerable to local spores or microbes, or may introduce organisms capable of overwhelming the settlement’s carefully maintained plant systems.

Bring:

- A sealed or filtered respirator, especially for entering the processing hall.
- Protective gloves and washable outer clothing.
- Eye protection for cutting areas, ducts, and machinery.
- A way to sterilize boots and equipment before arrival and before leaving.
- No untreated soil, seeds, insects, food plants, or biological samples from home.
- Containers for any authorized samples; do not collect casually.

Assume that even pleasant-smelling air may contain fermentation gases, fungal spores, or plant defenses.

**Bring waterproofing, not just rainwear.**  
The dampness is persistent rather than dramatic. Your clothing may never become fully dry unless you deliberately dry it. Pack:

- Waterproof outer shell and trousers.
- Insulating mid-layer suitable for cool, gray weather.
- Several pairs of synthetic or wool socks.
- Waterproof boots with good grip and ankle support.
- A compact towel and a means of drying clothing.
- A sealed bag for electronics and papers.
- Gloves that retain dexterity when wet.

Do not rely on sunlight to dry anything. The settlement may remain under low cloud for days.

**Use a mask or filtration system in enclosed buildings.**  
The processing hall contains damp pulp, mold, plant dust, old machinery, and possible gases from decomposition. A respirator is more appropriate than a simple cloth mask. Do not enter the hall immediately after a grinder, pump, or pressure vessel has opened unless a resident confirms it is safe.

### What to carry

A sensible day pack would include:

- Filtered drinking water in a sealed container.
- Compact food that does not require local cooking.
- Headlamp and spare batteries, despite the daylight.
- Waterproof map case and a non-electronic navigation method.
- Signal device or local communication token.
- First-aid supplies, including antifungal and antibacterial treatment appropriate to your biology.
- Blister care and foot-drying materials.
- A light walking pole for testing soft ground and probing vegetation.
- Small repair tools, but only for your own equipment.
- A personal air monitor if available, particularly for oxygen deficiency, methane, or other decomposition gases.
- A respectful gift or trade item, preferably manufactured and biologically inert.

A machete or large cutting tool would be inappropriate unless specifically issued by the residents. The settlement’s plants are not merely vegetation; they are walls, pipes, supports, filters, and water-control devices.

### Arrival and quarantine

Do not walk straight into the settlement from the open basin.

Stop at the boundary and wait to be received. The embankment and drainage channels are likely part of the settlement’s water-management system, and stepping across them may damage channels or introduce contaminants. Expect residents to inspect your boots, clothing, packs, and possibly your breath or equipment.

Follow their cleaning procedure exactly. It may involve:

- Scraping mud from boots.
- Washing or steaming equipment.
- Leaving outer garments in a designated area.
- Waiting before entering occupied buildings.
- Declaring all food, medicines, and biological materials.

Be honest about your physiology and technology. They need to know whether your body releases substances that could affect local plants, whether your equipment emits heat or radiation, and whether you carry autonomous machines or biological sensors.

### Moving through the settlement

Stay on maintained paths. The old streets may look solid, but vegetation covers cracks, drainage grooves, submerged rails, and soft cavities. The green tunnels and external walkways are engineered structures, not wild growth. Some are living load-bearing forms and may be damaged by careless contact.

Watch for:

- Slippery moss over concrete and metal.
- Open drains hidden beneath leaf layers.
- Flexible grating and woven-stem bridges.
- Sagging cables and energized utility equipment.
- Unmarked valves, sluices, and inspection covers.
- Machinery that appears abandoned but is still cycling.
- Falling condensation from roofs and beams.
- Root mats that conceal holes or active pipes.

Use a walking stick, but do not drive it into walls, planting beds, root bundles, or roof gardens. If you lose the path, stop and ask for help rather than making a new one.

The settlement is small, but do not assume it is easy to navigate. The vegetation deliberately obscures old signs and landmarks. Marked routes may change as residents redirect growth.

### Water and food

Do not drink from the reservoir, drainage channels, condensation leaves, or any exposed pipe. The water is divided by function, and a clear-looking section may still contain algae, industrial residues, or microbes dangerous to an off-world body.

Ask for drinking water from a resident. The covered reservoir section and ceramic collection basins are likely reserved for that purpose. Do not touch the broad leaves used for condensation harvesting.

Likewise, do not eat local plants, medicinal preparations, algae, fermented pulp, or anything offered without asking what it is and how it is prepared. The settlement’s food web is plant-dominated, but “plant” does not mean universally edible. Some substances may be medicines to them and poisons to you.

Bring enough food for the entire visit. This avoids putting pressure on their supplies and makes it clear that you are not treating the settlement as an open pantry.

### Entering the processing hall

The hall is the settlement’s central engine and should be treated as an industrial facility, not an attraction.

Before entering, ask:

- Which areas are currently active?
- Where are the safe walkways?
- Is respiratory protection required?
- Which pipes, belts, vats, and doors must not be touched?
- Are photographs or recordings permitted?
- Is there a time when the machinery cycles?

Keep clear of belts, grinders, feed hoppers, pressure vessels, pumps, and vents. A machine moving slowly can be more dangerous than one moving quickly because its motion is easy to ignore. Never place hands, tools, or clothing near roots entering machinery housings. A root bundle may be under tension or connected to a living structure elsewhere.

Avoid using bright lights, drones, heat sources, or loud equipment inside. The settlement’s systems are quiet and finely balanced; a drone’s rotors could scatter spores, damage leaves, or interfere with vents.

### Climate and health

The cool humidity will make exertion deceptive. You may sweat inside waterproof clothing while still feeling cold. Take regular breaks, change damp socks, and avoid remaining motionless for long periods.

Watch for:

- Chills and numbness.
- Headache or dizziness in enclosed spaces.
- Shortness of breath.
- Eye or throat irritation.
- Unusual fatigue after entering processing areas.
- Skin rashes from plant contact.
- Foot damage from prolonged dampness.

If you feel unwell, tell someone immediately. Do not assume that a quiet, gray environment is harmless. The most dangerous exposure may be invisible and entirely ordinary.

A traveler with an unusual metabolism, respiratory system, or temperature range should carry personal life support. The air is breathable to the residents, but that does not guarantee compatibility across universes.

### Social conduct

The people are quiet because much of their work is continuous, delicate, and distributed. Do not interpret their reserve as hostility, but do not demand attention either.

Good etiquette includes:

- Announce yourself before entering any building.
- Wait for an invitation before crossing a root-mat doorway.
- Ask before touching plants, machinery, walls, water, or tools.
- Keep your voice low near the processing hall and reservoirs.
- Do not cut, prune, harvest, or “helpfully” clear vegetation.
- Do not praise the settlement as a beautiful ruin; it is a functioning home.
- Ask what work may be observed and what work is private.
- Offer labor only after learning the correct method.

If you are allowed to assist, expect tasks such as carrying leaves, clearing a specified drainage groove, inspecting a cable, holding a lamp, or monitoring a valve. Do exactly what you are told. Improvisation can redirect water, kill a living support, release pressure, or contaminate a growing bed.

A useful gift would be something durable and sterile: metal fasteners, hand tools, sealant, ceramic containers, waterproof fabric, wire, or reliable measuring instruments. Do not give seeds, fertilizer, pesticides, pets, or unfamiliar microbes.

### Shelter and overnight stays

Ask to stay in a designated guest room, probably in an upper building or communal hall. Do not camp beside the reservoir, under a trellis, in an abandoned apartment, or near the drainage belt. Those areas may be planted, monitored, unstable, or reserved for environmental functions.

Before sleeping, inspect the room for:

- Condensation and mold.
- Insects or plant growth entering through vents.
- Unstable grating or external walkways.
- Electrical wiring.
- Nearby pumps or release valves.
- Emergency exits that may be sealed by living root mats.

Keep your belongings inside sealed containers. Local plants may not be aggressive, but they are persistent and will enter gaps overnight if given the opportunity.

### Timing and departure

The best arrival is during ordinary daylight, preferably after a dry interval and before the evening release cycle. Avoid arriving during heavy rain, when drainage channels and reservoirs are actively receiving water, or at night, when condensation collection and machinery cycles may be harder to see.

At departure:

1. Return all tools and borrowed clothing.
2. Remove mud, roots, seeds, and plant fragments from your equipment.
3. Submit to whatever exit decontamination the residents require.
4. Do not carry away water, pulp, soil, leaves, or insects without explicit permission.
5. Report any damage, spill, illness, or unusual machine behavior.
6. Avoid visiting another ecosystem immediately afterward until your clothing and equipment have been fully sterilized.

### The right mindset

Do not prepare for a spectacular alien wilderness. Prepare for a place where nearly every surface has a purpose, and where that purpose may be partly mechanical and partly alive.

The settlement is not being reclaimed by nature. Nature has been trained into infrastructure, and the infrastructure has been taught to continue living. Your greatest responsibility is therefore not survival against the environment, but restraint within it. Walk carefully, breathe through a filter, keep yourself dry, drink only what is offered, and assume that every green wall, quiet pipe, and damp patch is part of a system someone is still maintaining.
