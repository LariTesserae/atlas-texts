## Preparation for visiting the hollow

Treat this destination as a **small physical site containing a much larger, partially realized environment**. Do not assume that “smaller than a room” means it can safely be entered by an ordinary body. Its dimensions are not stable, and the distinction between a cavity, a landscape, and an observation inside a droplet is already unreliable.

The safest visit is not a conventional expedition. It should be performed by **controlled translation, remote embodiment, or temporary reduction of the traveler**, with the traveler’s original body remaining outside in a stable universe.

### 1. Arrange a reversible form of entry

Do not excavate an entrance. Do not widen a crack. Do not try to force the stone to accommodate you. The hollow may regard physical enlargement as a change to its own development, and the result could be a cave, a wound, a tunnel, or a mouth.

Use one of the following:

- A projected or remote body with limited sensory bandwidth.
- A temporary body adapted to uncertain scale and local gravity.
- A controlled reduction in size, performed before arrival and reversed only outside.
- A translation method that places you in the cavity without requiring a permanent doorway.

The original body should remain under observation in a stable location. The return mechanism should not depend on a cable, rope, marked path, radio signal, or precise coordinates. All of those may become unreliable inside. Instead, establish a **nonlocal external anchor**: a destination, identity, or state that exists outside the hollow and can pull you back even if local distance and time stop behaving normally.

Have an independent observer initiate extraction after a predetermined interval. Do not rely on your own judgment about how long you have been inside.

### 2. Dress for sharp stone and uncertain matter

Wear a seamless, flexible protective suit with no loose straps, knots, buckles, exposed fasteners, or rigid straight edges. The hollow appears capable of altering or softening manufactured forms. A garment that begins as clothing may become part of the mineral, a root, or an arrangement of stones.

Use:

- Soft, close-fitting protective layers.
- Flexible gloves and foot protection.
- Eye protection against mineral fragments and sudden changes in brightness.
- A hood or sealed face covering.
- A respirator or closed breathing system, preferably one that does not require delicate moving parts.

Do not bring metal tools, worked wood, blades, climbing gear, tripods, or rigid measuring instruments. Even if they function initially, they may lose their intended properties. A knife may become dull, a ruler may no longer remain straight, and a knot may simply cease to be a knot.

Bring as little as possible. Anything carried in may become uncertain, and anything left behind may be incorporated into the hollow.

### 3. Assume the air is not safe

The air is circulating, but circulation does not establish habitability. Its changing scents suggest that it may draw material or information from other environments. Oxygen concentration, pressure, spores, microscopic organisms, and local chemistry may all vary from one handspan to the next.

Keep your breathing system sealed until testing confirms that the local atmosphere is safe. Do not taste the pools, drink condensation, or inhale deeply to identify the scents. Do not trust a pleasant smell such as rain, greenery, snow, or animal fur.

The hollow may contain organisms that are harmless in their own universe but dangerous to human tissue, or vice versa. Bring no living plants, animals, food, or biological samples. On return, undergo isolation and decontamination in a universe where your body’s identity and chronology can be verified.

### 4. Prepare psychologically for attention-dependent space

The most important equipment is disciplined attention.

Distances change when you try to measure or cross them. A wall may be near while observed casually and far away when approached. A drop of water may contain more apparent space than the entire cavity. Time may pass at different rates in neighboring patches.

Before entry, practice:

- Looking without trying to possess or define what you see.
- Moving without fixing your attention on a destination.
- Maintaining awareness of your own body and the external return anchor simultaneously.
- Accepting that an object can be present without being reachable.
- Remaining calm when familiar geometry fails.

Do not attempt to force the place into a stable description. Mapping it will probably make the map less accurate. Counting steps, measuring distances, and using statements such as “the wall is exactly there” may invite the environment to contradict you.

A soft, distributed gaze is preferable to intense scrutiny. Do not stare at every unusual creature. Do not repeatedly look away and back to test whether something has changed. Your attention appears to participate in the hollow’s behavior.

### 5. Use simple communication

Travel with no more than one or two companions. More observers may produce competing versions of the space.

Establish silent signals before entry. Keep speech minimal. Spoken names may attach to the wrong object, creature, or location. Avoid naming things in the fashion of an explorer—“the predator,” “the exit,” “the shrine,” “the pool”—because the hollow may respond to names as if they were instructions or identities.

If speech is necessary, use descriptions rather than declarations:

- “Something is moving near the left-hand ridge.”
- “The air has changed.”
- “I am returning now.”

Do not make promises, issue challenges, recite personal names, or ask the place questions. There is no evidence that it understands language, but there is evidence that language may become physically consequential.

### 6. Respect the living environment

The cavity is not empty. It is a developing ecological system, or perhaps the earliest stage of one. Its organisms are small, but their significance is not.

Do not touch:

- The dark green thread or its translucent fronds.
- The pale rootlets.
- The ceiling moss.
- Hanging droplets.
- The crescent of arranged stones.
- Creatures that become transparent, vanish, or move through solid matter.

Do not collect stones, water, eggshell-like fragments, seeds, or dead organisms. Do not place objects in the pools. Do not “improve” the arrangement of the flat stones, even if it resembles a shrine, shelter, or trap. Its changing arrangement may be a process rather than a structure.

Step slowly and keep your body close to the ground if necessary. Sharp mineral ridges may form or dissolve without warning. A surface that looks solid may briefly become water-like, and a gap that looks impassable may be the actual passage.

### 7. Treat the black shape as a serious hazard

The narrow black presence is the clearest indication of a predator or predatory process.

Do not try to photograph it, trap it, illuminate it, or follow its lane through the grit. Do not assume it cannot see you merely because it only appears when unobserved. Direct observation may conceal it without making you safe.

If nearby animals freeze, flatten, disappear, or abruptly cease their normal behavior, stop moving. Do not approach the area. If the black lane appears, retreat in the direction of your external anchor without crossing the lane if possible.

If water rises toward the shape, or a pool reflects a larger wilderness than the cavity could contain, terminate the visit immediately. Do not enter the reflection, touch the water, or investigate the trees, stars, or running water shown within it. That may be an image, a connected region, a lure, or a view of what the hollow is becoming.

### 8. Do not trust apparent exits

An expanding corridor may be a genuine route, a consequence of attention, or the interior of a pool or droplet. If the opposite wall recedes when approached, stop pursuing it. The correct exit is probably not the most visible opening.

Return by maintaining contact with the remembered state of arrival rather than by retracing physical steps. Physical footprints and object markers may change. A piece of chalk, thread, beacon, or placed stone may no longer be the same object when you return.

The departure command or procedure should be initiated from outside whenever possible. If you must initiate it inside, do so at the first clear sign of:

- A change in shadow direction.
- A second set of shadows around your body.
- Local time behaving differently.
- A suspended droplet becoming a fixed landmark.
- Your own name feeling unfamiliar.
- The cavity becoming larger when you look away.
- A sound entering your ear without seeming to travel through air.

### 9. Set a strict visit limit

Do not remain to watch a complete life cycle of a moss island, a hanging drop, or one of the small animals. You may experience hours, days, or an entire local generation while only moments pass outside.

The place is quiet enough to encourage lingering. That is dangerous. Its processes are subtle and may not announce when you have become part of them. An expedition member might gradually become:

- A new mineral ridge.
- A shadow-feeding organism.
- A root searching for a pool.
- A mark on one of the stones.
- A memory preserved in a droplet.

Set the extraction limit conservatively. Leave while the environment still seems uneventful.

### 10. After returning

Do not immediately describe the visit in precise terms. Confirm your identity, body state, chronology, and memories against records made before entry. Check that you still recognize your companions, your original universe, and the purpose of the expedition.

Remain isolated until biological and informational contamination has been ruled out. Pay particular attention to:

- Unfamiliar moisture under the skin.
- Small transparent organisms in clothing or wounds.
- New mineral deposits.
- Shadows that do not match your movements.
- Dreams of larger underground forests.
- A persistent smell of wet stone or distant snow.
- Objects in your possession that you do not remember bringing back.

Most importantly, do not return merely because the place was beautiful or because you believe you understood it. The hollow does not seem hostile in the ordinary sense. It seems **unfinished, fertile, and capable of incorporating observers**.

Visit it as one would visit the first moment of a world’s formation: quietly, briefly, without taking anything, and with a reliable way to become absent again.
