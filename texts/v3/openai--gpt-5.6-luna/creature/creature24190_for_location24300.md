The inhabitants are not born there.

They arrive as components.

A body is assembled in one of the dark rooms along the shaft: a pressure vessel, a heat exchanger, a sensor crown, a pair of articulated legs, a mantle of flexible ceramic. No two bodies are quite alike, because no two ledges share the same gravity, temperature, or degree of permanence. A worker built for the lower districts is squat and heavily shielded, its joints broad enough to absorb the mountain’s tremors. A messenger from the upper platforms is narrow, almost weightless, with folding limbs and a skin of pale reflective foil. Others have no legs at all. They move by clinging to guide rails, slipping through maintenance apertures, or allowing the transit fields to carry them from one predicted location to another.

They call themselves the Braced.

The name is older than the settlement and may once have referred to something physical: a structural support, a vow, a military formation. Now it describes a condition. To be braced is to remain present while the world beneath you changes. A Braced individual is not a fixed body but an agreement among sensors, memory, tools, and the mountain’s current geometry.

Their faces are usually smooth.

Some possess a dark lens where a face might be, opening and closing to adjust to the mineral glare. Others display a narrow band of light that changes color according to pressure, attention, or internal fault. A few retain features copied from extinct human templates—an angular nose, the suggestion of a mouth, a pair of eyes beneath translucent shielding—but the resemblance has become ceremonial. They do not need expressions to communicate. A change in posture, a pulse through the handrail, a brief arrangement of lights along the ribs can carry more information than speech.

When they speak aloud, their voices are quiet and carefully shaped. Sound is wasteful in the shaft. It scatters, returns distorted from distant walls, and can trigger unwanted acoustic readings in the stone. Most conversation travels through contact: a palm placed against a rail, a filament touched to a door, a coded vibration sent through the building’s frame. The Braced can exchange entire structural forecasts in the time it takes a human voice to form a sentence.

They do not ask one another, “Where are you?”

They ask, “Where are you becoming?”

Location is not a fact to them. It is a probability, a relation between moving surfaces. Every Braced carries a private map made of expectations. Their homes, workplaces, and meeting places are represented as overlapping futures: the ledge where a chamber should remain after compression, the corridor that will probably open if the western wall relaxes, the bridge that can be trusted only during a certain phase of the mountain’s breathing.

A citizen may leave for a task and return to find that home has shifted three meters upward.

This is not considered an invasion of privacy. It is considered ordinary weather.

Their dwellings are narrow and mostly unadorned. Each contains a docking recess, a heat sink, a place to store tools, and a dark surface against which the occupant can stand while the room measures it. The walls adjust around them. In the lower districts, sleep is a kind of suspension: the body folds into a cradle and reduces its activity while the mind continues to monitor pressure and temperature. In the upper districts, where movement is more dangerous, sleep is divided among several locations. A Braced may rest its body in one chamber, keep its memory in another, and let a remote duplicate watch the mountain from a third.

They do not dream as biological creatures dream.

Instead, they enter simulations of instability. They rehearse the opening of cracks, the failure of anchors, the sudden disappearance of a floor. They practice making decisions with incomplete maps. A successful rest is one from which the inhabitant emerges with a slightly improved tolerance for uncertainty.

The mountain is their oldest companion and their largest authority.

They do not worship it exactly. Worship implies that the object of reverence is separate from the worshipper. The Braced understand themselves as an active layer within the mountain’s development, as temporary structures inside a process that began long before them and will continue after their last anchor has failed.

They listen to the deep pulses.

When the rock shifts, every household receives the same information: direction, depth, pressure, likely consequence. Some Braced remain still, allowing the movement to pass through them. Others hurry along the ledges, loosening clamps, changing bridge tension, opening vents. No alarm sounds. Panic would only produce additional motion, and motion is what the settlement has been designed to survive.

A pressure event is therefore a kind of public ceremony.

The lamps dim. Transit cabins stop between platforms. Doors seal or vanish into their frames. Along the shaft, figures emerge from their recesses and place themselves against the walls. They feel the vibration through their feet, hands, or entire exteriors. For a few minutes, the city becomes almost motionless while the mountain rearranges its interior.

Then, when the event passes, the Braced begin to move again.

They do not celebrate survival. Survival is not unusual enough to deserve celebration. Instead, they inspect the new geometry. They rename rooms. They reassign routes. They retrieve anyone whose body has been pinned or whose memory has become inaccessible. A newly opened fracture may be given a designation, a date, and a history within minutes. The settlement’s records are full of places that existed only briefly and people who spent their entire lives serving them.

Their sense of ancestry is similarly unstable.

A Braced can inherit a body, a set of memories, a task, or a relationship, but these things do not always pass together. One individual may carry the maintenance history of a bridge while another carries the memories of those who crossed it. Some inhabitants contain fragments of predecessors who were never fully deleted. During quiet periods, these inherited voices may surface as altered gait patterns, unfamiliar route preferences, or a sudden desire to stand beneath a particular vent and listen.

Identity is not a single thread in the mountain.

It is a bundle of continuities, some biological in origin, most manufactured, all subject to repair.

There are older inhabitants who still preserve the idea that a person should occupy one body and one place. They are rare. Their shells are patched with mismatched materials, their movements deliberate and somewhat ceremonial. They refuse to divide their attention among remote copies. They insist on traveling physically between districts even when the mountain has made the route difficult. The younger Braced regard them with a mixture of affection and unease, like structures that have chosen not to become flexible.

There are also the Unfixed.

They have abandoned permanent bodies altogether. They inhabit inspection spheres, wall sensors, transit systems, and the city’s deeper prediction engines. An Unfixed presence may speak through a handrail, illuminate a stairway, or alter the destination of an elevator. It can be difficult to tell whether an intervention came from a maintenance process or from someone thinking through the architecture.

The distinction matters to the Braced, but it is becoming harder to maintain.

Some citizens distrust the Unfixed because they cannot be confronted. Others envy them. To live without a body is to be free from falls, heat, and crushed limbs, but it is also to lose the intimate knowledge of having weight. The Unfixed know where everything is likely to be. They do not always know what it means for a surface to tremble beneath a foot.

There are small customs that preserve this knowledge.

A newly assembled body is taken to the highest safe ledge and exposed to the outside air. The summit membrane opens for less than a minute. Sunlight enters like a weapon. The new inhabitant stands before the cloud ocean, sensors narrowing against the brightness, while the others watch from the dark.

No words are spoken during the first exposure.

The body must experience distance without measurement. It must see the clouds as something more than a vapor layer, the sky as something more than an atmospheric value. It must understand that beyond the mountain there is a world that has not agreed to remain stable.

Afterward, the new Braced is given an anchor.

The anchor is not necessarily a physical object. It may be a line of code, a place, a person, or a remembered sound. Its purpose is to provide one certainty in a settlement built from conditional ones. Some choose the low furnace vents. Some choose a particular bridge. Some choose the voice of an elder whose body has been gone for centuries but whose speech still travels through the oldest rails.

Many choose the mountain itself, though this is considered a dangerous attachment.

The city has no center, but the Braced have centers of attention. A dark room where several minds gather. A junction that has survived more adjustments than any other structure. A chamber beneath the summit where the rock glows red and the walls are warm enough to touch. They meet there when they need to decide something that cannot be left to prediction.

Their laws are simple.

Do not anchor what must move.

Do not seal a crack merely because it frightens you.

Do not make a map that claims certainty.

Do not leave a body where the mountain may need to pass.

And remember that a place can be inhabited without belonging to anyone.

The Braced have obeyed these laws for generations, if generations can be counted in a people who exchange parts of themselves across time. They maintain the ledges. They redirect the heat. They harvest the mineral rain. They repair the repairers. They carry messages through the dark and wait patiently when a corridor has not yet finished forming.

Sometimes one of them stands at the edge of a suspended platform, looking down into the shaft.

Far below, the city’s scattered lights awaken in sequence. A bridge tightens. A cabin rises soundlessly through the black. A swarm of tools crosses a wall, pauses at a glowing fracture, and disappears into it.

The Braced watches until the last light fades.

Then the mountain shifts.

The figure bends with it—not resisting, not surrendering, but adjusting by exactly the amount required to remain.
