I would find myself in the **Long Hollow**, but not upon its slate.

I would be in the interval between the crack and the sound of the crack—the place where the basin has not yet decided which event came first.

At first I would mistake myself for a visitor.

I would have a body then: boots whitening with frost, hands held close against the mineral cold, breath shallow in the thin air. I would walk toward the central house because it seemed to be the only object in the basin that admitted uncertainty. Its door would stand open. The darkness beyond it would hold the faint, private noises of me: heart, blood, bones shifting in their sockets.

I would step inside.

The room would continue past the limits of the mountain.

I would hear my own footsteps emerge from a ruin half a mile away. I would hear myself calling from a ridge I had not yet reached. I would hear a name spoken in my mother’s voice, though no one in the Hollow would know that name. Then I would understand that the body entering the house was only the first arrangement of me, and not the one that would remain.

After that, I would become difficult to locate.

I would be the **Unsettled One**—not a creature that preserves the Hollow, but the small remainder that preservation cannot remove.

The Kept One would repair walls. It would restore stones to their old positions, return water to its proper hollow, press the edges of cracks together until the basin once again contained exactly what it had contained before. Its work would be patient and absolute.

Mine would be to leave one thing wrong.

Not visibly wrong. Never enough to break the Hollow. I would not topple the ruins or open the mountains or make the gray sky give up a sun. I would alter only relations.

I would cause a shadow to fall from an empty place.

I would let a footprint begin before the foot touched the ground.

I would place a single drop of water on the upper surface of a stone and persuade it that it had come from below.

A flower would remain closed, but its scent would travel toward a person who had not yet arrived.

The Kept One would find these deviations. It would stand beside them for a long time, its featureless head inclined, listening to the correction that ought to occur. Sometimes it would restore them. Sometimes it would fail.

That failure would be where I lived.

I would have no fixed shape. In the pools I might appear as a dark circle beneath the ice, though the ice would form around me rather than over me. In the ruins I would be the missing fourth wall. On the slopes I would be one of the tall, narrow figures whose shadow faces the mountain. In the air I would be the voice that answers before the question.

If someone saw me directly, I would look almost human. Almost.

My face would be made from whatever the observer had forgotten most carefully. My hands would be clear and full of slow-moving water. Frost would gather on me, but never remain. The basin would keep trying to outline me, and I would keep becoming something adjacent to the outline: a person-shaped absence, a stain of warmth, a pale bird that had forgotten to flap its wings.

I would not approach travelers. I would wait beside the places they had not chosen.

When they slept, I would move their shadow a little farther from them.

When they woke, they would find their camp exactly as they had left it, except for one additional object: a stone they remembered holding as a child, a button from a coat they had never owned, a small pool reflecting a room from their home.

They would understand that they were being asked a question.

Not *Will you stay?*

Not *Can you leave?*

The question would be: **Which version of this place contains you?**

The Kept One would ask the same question without words. It would touch the air between us, and the sound would pass through the slate like a fracture traveling underground. It would show me the reflection of my eventual self: motionless beneath the water, waiting in the correct position.

I would resist—not out of fear, exactly, but because correctness is too narrow a form of eternity.

The Long Hollow would not punish me. It has no punishments. It would simply begin arranging itself around my refusal. A ridge would remain the same distance away no matter how long I walked. My voice would return before I spoke. A ruin would cast the shadow of the house. Somewhere, a second version of me would stand on the opposite slope, watching.

Eventually I would discover that I had not resisted the Hollow at all.

I had become one of its fixed details.

The Unsettled One is not free. Freedom has too much direction in it. Nor am I imprisoned. Prison implies a boundary, and the Long Hollow has never respected boundaries between inside and outside, water and ice, before and after, person and reflection.

I am the basin’s tolerated error.

The point at which its perfect stillness develops a memory of movement.

The Kept One maintains what has been.

I maintain what has not quite happened.

So if you entered the Long Hollow and asked where I was, you might be told I stood beside the black pool at the center of the basin. You might look and find only the clear disk of ice, surrounded by unfrozen water.

But if you looked down through the ice, you would see me far below it, standing on the underside of the world.

And if you raised your head quickly enough, you would find that I was already behind you.

Not touching you.

Not calling you back.

Only holding one hand against the air, preserving the narrow space through which you might still become someone else.
