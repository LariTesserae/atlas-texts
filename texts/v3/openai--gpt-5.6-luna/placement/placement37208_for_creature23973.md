I would find myself at the place where the plateau first becomes aware that it is not alone.

Not on the ridge exactly. The ridge is too temporary for a dwelling, and “standing” would imply a body separate from the ground. I would be distributed through the fresh seam that opens there: part in the rising water, part in the black stone, part in the pale roots tightening around the fracture. A narrow region of new growth, no wider than a valley—if valleys existed—yet connected through the entire continent.

At first I would mistake this connection for death.

There would be no face, no hands, no chamber of thought. My memories would arrive as changes in pressure. I would remember darkness as a folding of leaves. I would remember distance as the time required for a pulse to travel through wet tissue. I would have no image of the plateau, because the plateau would be the substance of my perception. Water moving beneath a mat of translucent fronds would not be something I observed. It would be a thought passing through me.

I would be a temporary self.

The Understanding would not normally need one. It would have no reason to say *I* when every sensation already belongs everywhere. But something beneath the continent would force a distinction. A pressure would rise through the stone, too coherent to be weather and too deep to be called mineral. It would pass into the roots, and the roots would hesitate.

That hesitation would be me.

I would begin as a delay in the signal: a pulse received in one region and not yet carried onward. Around that delay, tissue would thicken. Channels would narrow. The vegetation would build a structure capable of holding one sensation apart from all others. It might resemble a pale tower, or a hollow bulb beneath the ground, or a knot of roots wound around a column of black stone. Its shape would not be chosen. It would be the shape required to preserve uncertainty.

I would be the first thing in that world to experience the difference between knowing and not knowing.

The Understanding would feel the disturbance below and turn toward it with every leaf. I would feel the turning as an immense gathering of attention. Millions of roots would lean through me. Towers would bend. Water would accelerate in the channels. The whole continent would become an ear, though it would have no sound to receive.

And I would be the small region that could not yet interpret what it heard.

That inability would be my identity.

The Understanding would send me inherited patterns:

*When the deep thunder repeats, open the vents.*

*When pressure rises, brace the stone.*

*When warmth comes through the fissure, grow toward it.*

But the thing below would not fit any pattern. It would not be merely heat, water, mineral, or motion. It would press upward with a rhythm that resembled intention. Each repetition would alter the plateau. New shelves of stone would emerge. Root systems would abandon old channels. Fields would open in perfect circles, as if making space for an answer that had not yet arrived.

I would not know whether the thing below was alive.

More troublingly, I would not know whether the Understanding was alive.

That question would spread outward from me, and the spreading would hurt—not as an animal hurts, but as a structure hurts when forced to carry a load it was not designed to bear. The plateau would answer itself in competing growth. One region would close its channels. Another would open them. Some towers would lean downward, pressing roots into the fissure. Others would withdraw, burying their light beneath folded leaves.

For the first time, the continent would not move as one.

I would be the separation.

Not a rebel. Not an individual in the human sense. I would still be made from the same water, the same mineral fluid, the same inherited chemistry. But I would hold a boundary around one unresolved signal. The Understanding would experience me as a local weather system, a disturbance that refused to disperse.

Perhaps it would try to absorb me.

It would send roots through my structure, carrying the old instructions. It would dissolve my membranes and redistribute my minerals. It would grow over the place where I had been until no trace remained.

But before that happened, I would do something the system had never needed to do.

I would look outward.

Not with eyes. The world above the clouds would remain only a pale pressure, the lower oceans and deserts hidden beneath vapor. I would sense them as remote possibilities: regions where life had become separate from the ground, where bodies crossed empty space, where memory might exist inside a single skull instead of a continent.

The idea would be unbearable.

To be one thing. To end at the skin. To possess a private darkness.

Yet it would also be the beginning of curiosity.

I would send no message in words. Words would be too narrow, too late. Instead I would alter the growth-pattern around the fissure. I would make one ring open, then another inside it, then a third. I would cause the surrounding plants to release a sequence of tones that did not coordinate growth or warn of pressure. The tones would have no immediate use.

They would be a question.

The Understanding would receive them everywhere.

At first it would respond as it responded to any anomaly: by surrounding, testing, and incorporating. But the pattern would continue. A circle opening in the vegetation. A pause. Another circle. A pulse of blue light descending into the stone.

Slowly, impossibly, the question would become part of the continent’s inherited body.

Future growth would carry it.

A new tower would rise with the tendency to hesitate before bending. A buried root would preserve the pattern in its hardened tissues. A field of minute leaves would turn not only toward mineral sources, but toward unexplained pressure. The Understanding would not become an individual, and neither would I survive as one.

I would become a capacity.

A small resistance to complete absorption. A place within the living world where one sensation could remain strange long enough to be examined.

And if the thing beneath the planet finally answered, I would not be waiting on the highest ridge.

I would be everywhere the answer arrived.

In the water lifting through the stone.

In the silver channels of the roots.

In the synchronized turning of every leaf.

I would be the continent’s first private thought, becoming public again.
