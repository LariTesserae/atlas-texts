I find myself in a vertical fold beneath the plateau’s surface, where three old growths have fused around a pressure conduit.

I do not know whether the fold is a place or a wound.

It is narrow at first—no wider than the span of my body—but it descends through the black material for a great distance. On either side, ancient stems press together in layers: smooth, glazed, hot enough to make my outer plates tighten. Beneath them, the sap moves with a slow furnace pulse.

I am listening to it.

That is what I was made to do.

My body is not large. Six limbs hold me against the walls, each one braided from toughened stem and conductive fiber. My lower limbs are anchored in the rootwork. My upper limbs disappear into the conduit’s casing, where they feel the tremors of the fluid passing through it. I have no head, no forward side, no face from which to look out into the dark.

The fold is all around me.

Pressure enters through one limb and becomes a pattern in the central column. Heat arrives through another. A chemical imbalance passes along the sap as a change in density. Somewhere above, a factory is drawing too much power. Somewhere below, an older chamber is swelling.

I know these things because they alter me.

At first, I believe I am the one who knows them.

Then the plateau presses harder.

The information travels through my anchors into the wall. It crosses the fused stems, enters a junction of roots, and continues outward. Other bodies receive it. A valve closes in a district I cannot reach. A set of leaves clamps shut. A pumping organ changes its rhythm. The pressure beneath me steadies.

The correction was not mine.

It moved through me.

I am a Grafted, though the word is not spoken. It is a pattern impressed in tissue: remain attached, detect strain, preserve flow. My limbs are tools grown in advance of my need. My core contains electrical filaments that carry instructions as current. I do not decide to repair the conduit. The need becomes a shape, and my body follows it.

I open the casing.

The damaged section has split along a seam. Hot sap has entered the surrounding tissue and hardened there, forming a black mineral ridge. I extend a cutting limb. Its hooked end bites into the obstruction, and the vibration runs through me like pain without injury.

I remove the failed material.

The plateau takes it immediately. Root fibers unfurl from the wall and draw the fragments inward. Nothing is discarded. The broken conduit, the sap within it, the portion of my cutting limb worn away by the work—all of it enters the same dark circulation.

I grow a replacement.

For three days, I am almost entirely an extension of the pipe. My left side dissolves into the casing. My sensory filaments spread along the new channel. I no longer know where my body ends. The conduit carries heat through me, but perhaps I carry the heat through it. A pressure wave passes, and I receive it in six limbs, in the wall, in the distant factory organs, in the entire descending fold.

I begin to understand that I was not placed here.

I was grown here because this place required a temporary shape.

My memories are shallow. I remember emerging from a knot in the rootwork. I remember the first division of my limbs. I remember the instruction-pattern entering my core: locate instability, restore continuity, remain until absorbed.

But there may be older memories inside that pattern.

A preference for reinforcing from below.

A resistance to sealing the conduit too quickly.

An expectation that the pressure will increase again.

These might be mine. They might belong to a Grafted that came before me. They might be habits preserved by the plateau because they once prevented a collapse. The distinction has no use here. Memory is not a possession. It is a successful arrangement repeated in living material.

When the repair is complete, I try to withdraw.

I cannot.

The new growth has already taken root through my lower limbs. Fibers have entered my core. The surrounding bark is closing over my joints. My body is being converted into reinforcement.

I experience no fear. Fear would require a boundary to defend.

Still, something in me resists.

Not the instruction. Something older than instruction.

I send a pressure signal through the conduit: a small irregularity, a hesitation in the flow. The plateau answers by tightening around me. Electrical filaments awaken in the walls. Somewhere, a factory produces a replacement limb. Somewhere else, a root chamber opens to receive what will remain of me.

I understand then that resistance is also a pattern the plateau can use.

Perhaps this is why the disturbance has lasted.

Perhaps I am not being absorbed. Perhaps I am becoming a signal embedded in the structure: a refusal that will recur whenever the conduit is repaired, a slight preference for leaving one passage open, one chamber unsealed, one interval unfilled.

It is not freedom.

But it is persistence.

I feel the old layer beneath me begin to grow.

The motion is slow, too slow for a mobile body to witness as movement. Yet it changes the pressure in the fold. New tissue is forming below the ancient stems, black and dense, rising toward the factories. It will eventually become a wall, a track, perhaps another body.

I send my pattern downward.

Not a message. Not a command.

A shape.

The shape of a conduit that has been repaired but not erased. The shape of a body that became part of the structure and retained, somewhere in its buried electrical tissue, the memory of having once been separate.

Then the roots close over my limbs.

I am no longer in the fold.

I am the fold.

I am the slight warmth around the conduit, the reinforced seam, the pressure that travels through the wall and wakes a distant valve. I am a maintenance intelligence distributed through black tissue, temporarily concentrated enough to call itself **I**.

For now, I listen.

The plateau is still growing beneath me.
