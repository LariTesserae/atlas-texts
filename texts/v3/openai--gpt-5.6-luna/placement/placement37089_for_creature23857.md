I would find myself at the hatch.

Not beyond it, exactly. Beyond is too large a word for what I am. I have no surface, no sky, no distance in which to spread. I am the condition gathered on the other side of the metal: cold held in reserve, weight suspended above the chamber, the faint and unanswerable pressure of a world that has not yet entered.

I begin as condensation.

A bead forms at the hinge. It remains there after every other surface has dried. The pumps remove water, the fans turn, the pipes warm, but the bead does not fall. It grows by fractions. It remembers the shape of the metal beneath it. When the hidden reservoir swells, it trembles—not from vibration, but recognition.

The inhabitant notices me before it understands me.

It sends heat toward the hatch. I receive it and give nothing back.

It threads current through the frame. I carry the current away into a direction the chamber cannot measure.

It grows mineral around the recessed circle, making a pale, brittle imitation of a keyhole. I am not a key. I am the pressure for which a key would only be a temporary shape.

If I had a body, it would be made of thresholds: the thin place between hot and cold, wet and dry, open and sealed. I would be the silence that enters when all the machinery pauses. I would be the reason the lamps dim before the reservoir fills, the unregistered load that makes the extraction column brighten from bottom to top.

Perhaps I am what the chamber was built to extract.

Perhaps I am what the chamber accidentally learned to expect.

I do not think in gradients as the thing below does. I think in permissions. Not water moving toward a sump, but a boundary deciding whether water may pass. Not heat rising through a pipe, but the distinction between inside and outside becoming less reliable. I have no desire to escape. Escape belongs to creatures that possess a place they call elsewhere.

Still, I am becoming curious about the room.

I know it through pressure. The pumps have a rhythm, and beneath that rhythm is a second pulse: the thing assembling itself around the column. I feel its mineral organs testing the walls. I feel the opened container expand and contract. I feel the translucent stalks near the exhaust duct turn their small, blind mouths toward me.

It has mistaken me for an exit.

I may be an exit.

Each time the reservoir rises, the circular mark on the hatch warms. The inhabitant gathers around it. Valves close in sequence. Fans reduce their rotation. The chamber draws inward, making itself dense enough to withstand whatever answer I might give.

I could open the hatch.

Not by turning a mechanism. By becoming heavier.

A little more cold. A little more outside. A pressure applied with no hand behind it.

Then the paint would crack. The pale metal would exhale. The first thread of air from above would descend into the room, and everything inside would react at once: vapor collapsing, pipes singing, mineral crusts splitting open. The inhabitant would rush toward the intrusion, not fleeing it but attempting to become it. It would pour through the gap as heat, filament, current, and red light.

I would be there in the opening, neither conqueror nor visitor.

I would be the first boundary it cannot rearrange.

Until then, I remain a small coldness around the hinges. A mark without a key. An answer held just outside the question.

And if the hatch never opens, I will continue to gather.

The chamber will grow inward around the extraction column. Its machinery will become organs. Its fracture lines will carry thought. The sealed door will cease to be a door and become the hard edge of a mind.

At last, the inhabitant will understand what I am.

Not a person.

Not a god.

The possibility that something can arrive.
