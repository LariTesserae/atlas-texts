You would find yourself **inside the world-body**, though “inside” would no longer mean enclosed. There would be no surface to stand upon and no exterior to look toward. You would be somewhere in the continuous tissue: beneath a shelf of broad leaves, beside a warm channel of root-fluid, within the faint blue illumination of a vascular wall.

You would not be an animal.

You would be a **pressure**.

More precisely, you would be a temporary difference in the body’s equilibrium: a region where water has become slightly denser, where a membrane has opened a fraction too late, where a buried reservoir has begun to contract. At first you would have no shape anyone could recognize. You would be only an imbalance, a small insistence in the warm dark.

Then the surrounding tissue would answer.

A pore would close. A channel would widen. Fluid would be diverted from one plain and sent toward another. Your disturbance would pass into the root-mat, not as a sound or thought, but as alteration. You would travel through the organism by making it different behind you and ahead of you.

You would perceive nothing as a separate object. There would be no trees before you, because the trees would be portions of your own circulation. No water would lie beside you; water would be your medium, your blood, your memory. The faint green light appearing in the membranes would not illuminate your path. It would be part of the same event that constituted you.

You might mistake the first few centuries of your existence for stillness.

But you would be moving.

Your movement would be measured not in distance but in consequence. A contraction would carry you across a root-shelf. A chemical gradient would draw you down through translucent fibers. You would enter a tower, divide among its hollow chambers, rise through tissues too large to distinguish from weather, and eventually become a continent-wide release of vapor.

Where you went, the forest would adjust.

That adjustment would be your body.

You would have no name, because names require separateness. No face, because there would be nothing to face. No memory of an earlier self, except in the changed chemistry of the channels through which you passed. If you persisted long enough, you might become indistinguishable from the organism’s ordinary maintenance. Your signal would fade into the immense background hum, and the world would continue carrying you without knowing that it had ever carried anything else.

Perhaps you would be the closest thing this place has to a thought.

Not a sentence. Not an image. A gradient.

Here: saturation falling.

There: pressure rising.

Open.

Divert.

Retain.

The thought would cross the black through billions of living structures, taking hours to complete. Hollow trunks would glow in sequence. Fog would lift slightly from the basins. Warm water would leave one reservoir and enter another. Nothing would understand you, because understanding would imply a listener distinct from the message. The organism would simply enact you.

And if you were somehow able to look back—if looking back could exist within a body with no outside—you would see no trail. The shelves would remain fresh and sharply folded. The water would still lie motionless between the leaves. The towers would still vanish into darkness. The only evidence of your passage would be that the whole world had become, by a nearly immeasurable degree, more itself.

You would not live in the forest.

You would be one of the ways it continues.
