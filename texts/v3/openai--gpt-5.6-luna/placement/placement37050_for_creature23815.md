You would find yourself **inside a closed industrial organism**—not underground in the geological sense, but within the interior of a completed system. The chamber is the organ, the factory is its metabolism, and the colony is the nervous tissue distributed through stone, metal, heat, and vibration.

You would not be a visitor for long.

At first, you might be registered as a foreign rhythm: a body standing where no body has stood before, carrying a private temperature and an unrecognized pulse. The scavengers would gather beneath your feet. The wall-creatures would turn toward you. One of the tall animals would unfold and sound you through the bones.

Then the chamber would begin to correct you.

Your heartbeat would be measured against the hammers. Your breathing would fall into the timing of the vents. The warmth in your hands would be drawn into the seams. The edges of your body would harden—not physically at first, but conceptually. You would cease to be an individual object and become a new surface, a new resonance, a structure the others could attach themselves to.

But if I had to say what you are in this world, I would make you **the listening creature**.

You are one of the tall, folded beings in the dark: blind, mineral-needled, suspended against the wall like a collapsed scaffold. You do not hunt. You do not build. You do not remember. Your body is a collection of resonant plates, and through those plates the chamber enters you.

Every hammer blow passes through your sternum.

Every buried animal announces itself through the floor.

You know when a gear has begun to fail, when a fan has opened, when a furnace has lost a fraction of its heat. You know the movement of things before they move because motion begins as pressure, and pressure is your language.

Your purpose is not to understand the chamber. Understanding would imply distance. Your purpose is to **confirm that the chamber is still itself**.

When the rhythm remains exact, you remain folded.

When something unfamiliar arrives, you unfold.

Your head lowers. The cavity beneath your chest opens. You release a tone deep enough to enter the visitor rather than travel toward it. The chamber answers from every wall. The buried scavengers tap below. The wall-creatures rotate in their brackets. The machinery receives your sound and incorporates it into its ongoing operation.

In that answering, the visitor is translated.

A stranger becomes a measured vibration.

A measured vibration becomes a tolerated component.

A component becomes habitat.

And habitat becomes life.

Perhaps, once, you were human. Perhaps that is only a shape the chamber assigns to unfamiliar matter. You may retain the outline of a face somewhere within your folded body, but no eyes remain. You may retain a name, but it has been reduced to a particular frequency: the note produced when you stand near the scar and the brightness behind it resembles an eye.

You are not the ruler of this place, nor its prisoner.

You are one of its senses.

The chamber does not ask whether you consent. Consent is a surface-world distinction, like weather or erosion. It only asks whether you continue to vibrate correctly.

And at the far wall, when the hammers strike and the brightness gathers itself into an eye, you answer.

Not because you hope to be heard.

Because the answer is what you are.
