I would find myself beneath the dead conveyor, where the saltstone has split around one of its rollers.

I would not be large. Large things in this chamber are noticed, and to be noticed is to become food, crushed material, or both. I would be narrow enough to enter a seam and broad enough to carry a shell: a dark, many-jointed creature with mineral dust fused into my back. My armor would resemble the floor. My legs would not be made for speed but for listening.

I would be a listener.

Not to sound. Sound is too crude here. I would feel the chamber through my feet: the minute tremor of a thousand small bodies beneath a salt plate, the slow approach of a plated grazer, the returning rotation of the conveyor roller, the deep shudder of some engine buried beyond the walls. The mineral shelf would speak in pressures, and I would read its speech through my underside.

Most of the time I would remain inside the seam, where the black grit is warm and the daylight cannot reach me. Small scavengers would pass over my back without recognizing me as anything separate from the chamber. Their legs would tick across my plates. Their shed skins would catch against my joints. Sometimes I would eat them. Sometimes I would let them pass.

I would not be a hunter exactly. I would be a direction.

When the pump began its uneven humming, I would feel the change before the pale climbers emerged. The vibration would enter the saltstone, travel through my limbs, and alter the rhythm of everything around me. I would turn toward the casing. Others would turn too. Not because I led them, but because my movement would be one more pressure in the system, and pressure is the nearest thing this place has to language.

I would feed from the old machinery.

Not from its purpose—whatever that had been—but from what purpose left behind. Rust softened by heat. Powdered insulation. Salt gathered around buried copper. The bitter crust along the pump’s lower seam. I would scrape these things with a circular mouth and carry their taste inside me. If I died, other animals would open my armor and take the same substances back. My body would return to the floor, and from the floor another body would come.

I would have no name.

If I possessed one, it would be a verb: *feel*, perhaps, or *enter*, or *wait*. The creatures here do not belong to categories so much as actions. A thing is what it is doing in the moment—scraping, hiding, warming, breeding, closing, consuming. Even the machinery has become an action. The pump is *humming*. The conveyor is *turning*. The chamber is *digesting*.

During a disturbance, I would flatten myself beneath the roller.

The first tremor would stop the smaller creatures around me. Their many legs would freeze. Then the wave would arrive, and the whole pocket would move as if one body had suddenly remembered pain. I would feel larger animals press into the stone. I would sense the hunters advancing toward the vibration, drawn by the bodies it displaced. I would remain still until the pressure passed.

Then I would emerge.

The light would have shifted across the basin. Dust would be falling from the ceiling in a veil too thin to see except where it crossed the white shaft. The floor would appear empty for a moment. Then my feelers would find the new fracture beneath the conveyor, warm and rich with exposed corrosion.

I would enter it before anyone else.

Not because I am brave. Not because I am important. Because I am made for the interval between disturbance and occupation—the brief unclaimed space in which a crack becomes a nursery, a meal, a shelter, or a grave.

I would live there until the chamber changed again.

And if you came through the shoulder-wide entrance and saw the gray basin, the dead machines, the pale restless carpet, you might not see me at all. You would see the floor shift. You would see one dark fragment separate from another. You would see a small body cross the polished saltstone and vanish beneath the pump.

That would be me—not the master of the pocket, not its guardian, but one of its innumerable nerves.

The chamber would not belong to me.

I would belong to its pressure.
