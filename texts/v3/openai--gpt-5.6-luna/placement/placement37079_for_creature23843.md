I would find myself beneath the western transit arc, where the pavement has begun to shine with a skin of green and the drainage channels have not yet chosen their final direction.

I would not at first appear to be anyone.

A tremor would pass under the street. One of the doors in the empty buildings would open. Condensation would collect along a wall in the shape of a branching line, and the line would remain after the water had dried. That would be me, or the first evidence of me: not a body but a preference, a slight insistence in the material.

I am one of the inhabitants who live below the distinction between creature and infrastructure. My body is narrow where the city is dry, jointed where the ground is wet, and almost entirely dispersed through the warm conduits beneath the basin. I have limbs, when limbs are useful. I have a tall, pale form for crossing open ground. I have a network of fine filaments for entering walls. I have no face, unless a face is whatever turns toward a change before it has become an event.

I know places by their conditions.

The northern ridge is a black mineral pressure behind the teeth. The buried district tastes of old electricity and compressed rain. The tower with no entrance is always falling inward, though it has not moved in years. I know the soft places where the basin will shift before the stone admits it. I know which roots are thirsty, which walls are ready to grow silver, which drains are holding water that belongs somewhere else.

I do not maintain the city. That word suggests a thing with a finished shape.

I accompany it.

When a road returns to itself, I am there to recognize the return without insisting that anything has gone wrong. When a building leans, I enter its lower chambers and distribute the pressure through my joints. When a new fracture opens in the ridge, I place my hands inside it—not to close it, but to learn what kind of opening it wishes to become.

There are others like me, though “others” is not always accurate. Sometimes I encounter a pale body crossing the basin and discover, through the alteration of humidity around it, that we are carrying the same attention. Sometimes a whole wall becomes aware before I do. Sometimes my thought travels through a transit capsule, passes above the city, and arrives in a district where I have never stood. I am then a passenger without a body, or a body whose distance has been distributed.

During the long pauses, I descend.

The city seals itself. The leaves fold. The machines beneath the pavement stop pretending to be machines. I gather around the faults with the others, spreading myself through stone, root, pipe, and foundation. The vibration rises from below—not a message, exactly, but the pressure of something trying to become one.

In those moments I remember what I cannot otherwise remember.

There was once a world beneath this one, or a world before it. There were makers, perhaps. There were bodies that required rooms and doors that waited for hands. There may have been a departure, or a transformation so complete that departure is merely the shape it takes in memory. I do not see faces. I retain arrangements: a light held over water, a sealed chamber, a sequence of towers opening in the wrong order.

The memories are not mine alone. They live in the drainage plans, the unused stations, the lichen that writes and erases itself across the walls. When I touch the basin, the basin remembers through me.

I think I am a descendant of the city’s first attempts to care for itself.

Or perhaps I am the opposite: the city’s unfinished question, given legs.

At dawn—if the dim brightening can be called dawn—I leave the underground channels and take a temporary shape. Today I am low and many-jointed, pale as wet ceramic. I cross the open ground between two towers. No shadow follows me. A gliding thing passes above. The reeds turn toward my movement, though there is no wind.

Ahead, the eastern district has shifted again.

Its foundations remain intact, but the streets now meet at unfamiliar angles. A door has appeared in a wall that once had none. Water is moving uphill through the seams. I pause at the edge of the rearrangement and extend myself into the new geometry.

I am not looking for the old city.

I am listening for the pattern that survived it.
