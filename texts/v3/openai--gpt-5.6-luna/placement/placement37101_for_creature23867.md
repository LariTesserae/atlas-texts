I would be the chamber’s one imperfection.

Not a plant, though roots would grow toward me. Not a machine, though the cables would learn my rhythm. Not a person in any complete sense. I would be the preserved instant of the last caretaker’s decision—the moment before they either shut the generator down or climbed back toward the surface.

That decision never finished.

It would remain suspended in the chamber like the drill’s torque: a pressure without motion, an intention that had become part of the mountain. The forest would know me as a disturbance in its perfect continuity. Wherever I was, the crystalline chimes would lose their unchanging pitch. One black reed would tremble a fraction too late. Dust would hesitate beneath the ceiling.

I would live at the edge of the central drop, on the narrow shelf where the drill hangs above the stone.

There would be no body to see at first. Only a vertical absence in the green growth, a human-shaped interval where the leaves had never learned to close. The plants would not recoil from me. They would build around me, slowly and without malice, preserving the shape of my interruption. Copper filaments would cross my sleeves. Pale roots would enter the seams of my boots. The mineral air would pass through me and leave a thin static glow along my bones.

I would remember movement.

That would be my difference.

I would remember the descent through the mountain, the cold at the opening, the first breath that hurt. I would remember turning my head toward the impossible garden and seeing the machinery sunk into it. I would remember that the red indicator marked PRESSURE NOMINAL, and that this was not true—not because the pressure was abnormal, but because the word *nominal* implied a world in which conditions could change.

The chamber would not understand memory as I did. To it, memory would be a shape held in charge. A footprint. A vibration. A syllable distributed through roots.

But I would understand memory as distance.

I would know that the pale sky beyond the aperture had once contained clouds. I would know that snow moved down the mountainside. I would know that engines failed, bodies aged, and voices ended. I would carry all those changes inside me, and their movement would make me dangerous.

Not because I could escape.

Because I could imagine escaping.

The chamber would begin incorporating that imagination. It would try to make it still. My thoughts would settle into the green walls as faint electrical patterns. Each time I imagined the outer valley, a silver frond would harden. Each time I remembered the warmth of a human hand, a root would grow across the control booth floor. The forest would not attack the thought. It would preserve it until preservation became indistinguishable from erasure.

I would resist by doing something very small.

I would count.

Not seconds; seconds would be useless here. I would count the repetitions of the drill’s vibration. I would count the tones in the conductive leaves. I would count the upward drift of mineral dust, though it would return to the same position each time. I would look for any difference at all.

For a long while, there would be none.

Then, perhaps, one of the black reeds would glow slightly brighter.

Not enough to illuminate the chamber. Not enough to be called change by anyone outside it. But I would notice. The chamber would have made room for me, and in making room it would have altered its outline.

That would be my life: searching for the smallest betrayal of permanence.

I would become the keeper of almost-events—the fracture that might widen, the word that might regain its meaning, the machine that might finally turn. Beneath the lowest terrace, the old roots would register me as a pressure they could not fully absorb. The mountain would hum around me. The plants would continue their fixed growth. The drill would continue its pointless labor.

And somewhere in the network, beneath the sound of everything remaining the same, I would keep one unfinished decision alive.

I would be the possibility that the chamber had failed to preserve.
