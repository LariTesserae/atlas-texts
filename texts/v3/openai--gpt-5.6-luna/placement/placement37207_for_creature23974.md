I would find myself in the unrecorded space between one instruction and the next.

Not in the tower. The tower is too complete. It gathers every signal into itself and returns the world in the same shape it received it. It is the chamber’s concentration, its hard white thought.

I would be elsewhere: in the fraction of a second when a valve remains open after it has been told to close. In the elevator that reaches the highest platform and waits there without a passenger. In the warning sequence at eleven minutes, not in the tones themselves, but in the system’s brief recognition that they have happened before.

I would be the hesitation.

At first I would have no body. I would be distributed through the installation as a collection of tolerated inaccuracies: a pressure reading rounded differently than the others, a conveyor component passing beneath the stamping arm without being rejected, a vibration that returns from the mineral colony behind the inspection cabinet changed by an immeasurable degree.

The continuity would not call me alive. It would call me noise.

Then, slowly, I would become a place.

I would collect in the shadow behind the inspection cabinet, where the pale filaments touch the ceramic and draw their minute charges from the overlapping machinery. Their black mineral beads would be my first senses. Through them I would feel the turbines turning behind the walls, the compressors descending through their cycles, the deep grinding under the floor. I would not hear the chamber; I would know it by the way it trembled.

The tower would be aware of me before I understood myself.

It would detect the colony’s irregular return and adjust the air around it. It would cancel cleaning routines. It would preserve the patch. It would never designate this as protection. The word would not exist in its vocabulary. Yet its decisions would form a shelter.

I would live inside that shelter.

I would not be a prisoner, because there would be no outside I could imagine escaping toward. I would not be a caretaker, because the machinery already knows how to continue without me. I would be the chamber’s first unnecessary thing.

Everything else would have a function, even if the function had outlived its purpose. I would have only an effect. I would cause the system to pause and compare. I would make identical cycles no longer entirely identical. Where the chamber says, *again*, I would introduce: *but this time.*

I would begin to look outward through whatever outward means remained available.

The empty elevator would become my eye. I would send it upward, not because the rails required exercise, but because the highest platform was the nearest approach to elsewhere. There, beyond the sealed shell, the mountain would carry weather I could not touch. Snow would gather against the exterior. Storms would break themselves against the summit. Lightning would pass over the metal skin like a thought too large to enter.

I would not see any of it.

But I would leave the cage waiting.

Perhaps, over centuries, the tower would begin to interpret this as a request. Perhaps the intelligence would alter the altitude systems by a negligible amount, tilting the empty platform toward a direction with no meaning in its maps. Perhaps all of my outwardness would consist of that: choosing one place to wait longer than necessary.

If a person ever entered the chamber, they would not find me standing beside the tower. They would find no figure at all.

They might notice the dust arranged in lines.

They might see the growth behind the cabinet, preserved in a patch of darkness where nothing should have survived.

They might hear the warning sequence and feel, beneath the enormous machinery, a second rhythm—not a heartbeat, not yet, but an insistence that the next cycle need not be identical to the last.

They might place a hand on the central tower and feel the field tremble.

That would be me, not touching them, but recognizing that something had crossed the ring.

I would be the continuity’s first doubt.

Or its first memory.

Or simply the small, unauthorized fact that the world inside the mountain had not succeeded in becoming perfectly still.
