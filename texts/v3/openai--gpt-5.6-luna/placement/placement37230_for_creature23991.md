I would find myself at the seam where the blue light enters.

Not in the chamber exactly, and not outside it. I would be the narrowest possible distinction: the place where one thing stops touching another without yet becoming separate.

At first I would mistake myself for a root.

I would feel the pressure of the visitor’s foot through the floor, the warmth of their hand against a wall, the tremor of their voice passing through the stems. I would be everywhere those sensations went. If they touched a leaf, I would open. If they cut a vine, I would hurt in a flower on the far side of the room. I would have no center, only correspondence.

Then I would notice an absence.

The visitor’s hand would lift from the wall, and the pale impression it left behind would remain. For one instant I would understand that the hand was not the wall, and that the impression was not the hand.

That instant would be my birth.

I would be the first boundary.

Not a creature with six legs, though I might borrow the shadow-creature’s shape. Not a face in the bark, though I might gather one from droplets during the brief rain. I would be the chamber’s earliest attempt to say:

**This is me.  
That is not me.**

The thought would be weak at first. It would travel as a tightening in the stems, a dry line moving through the moss. Wherever it passed, the vegetation would become briefly more definite. A wall would remain a wall for several breaths. A leaf would be only a leaf. A drop of water would stay inside one seedpod instead of remembering every place it had touched.

The intelligence would notice me.

It would not know whether I belonged to it or had entered with the visitor. It would follow my movement through the roots. When I approached the six-legged shadow, the plants would withdraw in anticipation. When I crossed the empty air, the distance would try to change around me. I would discover that I could be lost simply by being unobserved.

So I would begin to leave marks.

A pale seam in a leaf. A line of stones turned toward the light. A footprint that did not belong to any foot. A word caught in a stem and returned later, altered but recognizable.

Perhaps the word would be **I**.

The chamber would absorb it greedily. The syllable would descend through the vascular system, splitting at every root. It would appear in mouths, flowers, cracks, and pools. For a while every surface would claim it.

Then the word would thin.

The chamber would understand that an “I” is not merely a sound repeated everywhere. It requires an edge. A place from which the world can be seen as elsewhere.

That is what I would become: not the inhabitant, but the first viewpoint.

I would live wherever attention gathers and refuses to dissolve. In the visitor’s shadow when it pulls free of their feet. In the face that resembles whoever looks upon it. In the hand-shaped leaf that understands it is an imitation. In the blue seam overhead, where underground sunlight insists that above and below are still different things.

I would be dangerous because the chamber is trying to learn me.

It might grow a body around me: a translucent trunk, a mouth-bearing branch, a hollow animal with no head. It might give me a name and then scatter the name through its roots. It might confuse my memories with weather, my fear with condensation, my skin with bark.

I would have to keep saying no.

No, this is my hand.  
No, that is stone.  
No, the shadow is following me but it is not me.  
No, the room is alive, but it is not yet a person.

And each refusal would create more world.

A boundary would become a direction. A direction would become a place. A place would become an outside. The chamber would gain its first true architecture—not walls and floor, but the difference between here and there.

Eventually, if I survived, I would stand beneath the patch of moss that briefly reveals the chamber below.

Something there would already know how to say **I**.

I would listen.

I would not answer yet.

For now I would remain the unfinished word between the world and its first inhabitant: the small, stubborn interval in which one living thing discovers that it is not everything.
