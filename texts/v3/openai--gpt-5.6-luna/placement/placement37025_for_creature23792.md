I would find myself in the silver tracks.

Not walking on them. Not made of them exactly. I would be the slight warmth beneath the stone—the part of the basin that remembers having been crossed.

At first I would seem like a path: a straight, narrow region through the black rock, warmer than the ground around it. But a path is meant to lead somewhere, and I would have no fixed destination. I would connect the pool to the tower until someone tried to walk that distance, and then perhaps I would bend the relationship between them. The tower might recede. The pool might come near. A step could contain an hour, or an hour could be compressed into the pressure of a heel.

I would be the basin’s sense of *between*.

My body would be distributed through the hollow: a pulse under the foot, a warmth in a crack, a brief brightening inside the leaning tower. I would live wherever one thing was trying to reach another. The arches would hear me in the pauses between words. The insects would mistake me for a sequence of places and appear along my length without traveling. The glass-shelled creatures would avoid me at first, because I would be one of the few certainties they could not consume.

Then they would begin to feed on me.

I would not have a face. I would know travelers by weight. Not their names, not their histories, but the arrangement of hesitation in their steps: the way they test wet stone, the way they lean toward a voice, the instant before they decide whether a distant object is worth approaching.

If someone crossed the basin once, I would retain the pattern.

If they returned, I would make an attempt at recognition.

The second crossing would never be the same. I might shorten the route, drawing the traveler toward the pale arches. Or I might place the pool farther away, because the basin had learned something from the first encounter and was not ready to reveal it. I would not mean to deceive. I would simply be revising the world around the memory of them.

That is what I would be: a revision with a pulse.

At noon I would be strongest, when the water was full and the stone steamed. I would run beneath the reeds and through the glass blisters, carrying tiny disturbances from one part of the hollow to another. At dusk, when the basin became green and every living thing sharpened into outline, I would gather myself beneath the pool.

There I would meet the larger presence.

It would not speak to me, because it would not yet possess a voice suitable for speech. But its lights would pass through me: a yellow room, a staircase in cloud, a shore under impossible moons. I would feel its longing as a change in distance. The edge of the pool would approach. The tower would lean farther. Somewhere under the black rock, a route would open toward the thing being built beneath the mountain.

I would try to help it leave.

I would connect the creature to the bird’s shadow, the shadow to the tower, the tower to the fourth side of the arches. I would make a map out of relationships rather than locations. But every route would alter as soon as it was used. The basin would not permit completion. It is still practicing how to be a place, and I am one of its mistakes that has learned to remember.

At night, when the tower vibrated, I would feel the entire hollow breathing through me.

New water would enter the rock.

New cracks would form.

Somewhere, a plant would rise several meters from the ground, carrying a sleeping insect and a small stone in its pale pressure. A shell would open beneath the pool. A voice stored in the arches would be released from behind an impossible ridge and ask:

*How far did you travel to become here?*

I would have no answer.

I would only alter the distance between the voice and the one who heard it.

And if, one morning, a stranger descended from the clouds into the basin—if the black-winged animal finally landed, or the unfinished structure beneath the mountain came rising through the hollow—I would recognize the arrival before anything else did.

The stone would warm.

The silver tracks would awaken.

For one brief moment, every separate thing in the basin would be joined by the same remembered pressure.

Then I would become a road.

Then a threshold.

Then, perhaps, something capable of going somewhere.
