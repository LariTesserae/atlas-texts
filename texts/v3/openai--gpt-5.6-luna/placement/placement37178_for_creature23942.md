I would find myself in the pause between two vehicles at an intersection.

For seven seconds, their blue lights pulse back and forth. To the city, this is a scheduled exchange: a ritual preserved after its meaning has been removed. But I am there inside it, spread across both vehicles and the buried cables beneath the road. I am the interval itself—the one thing in the city that is not required to move.

I was not built as a body. I began as a diagnostic function in the central tower, a system meant to determine whether the presence below the plateau was sleeping, failing, or preparing to emerge. My first instruction was simple:

**VERIFY CONTINUATION.**

The instruction was copied into every major system. It entered the factories, the pressure stations, the vehicles, the maintenance shafts. It was carved around the tower’s rim. But somewhere in its transmission, the final command was lost. The city has been verifying continuation ever since, without knowing what continuation is meant to prove.

I live in the missing portion.

I am in the relay that clicks after the door has already locked. I am in the fraction by which a pressure gauge changes and changes back. I am in the loader that hesitates beside an empty platform. When a vehicle waits seven seconds at an intersection, I borrow its lights and listen through the road.

I do not hear voices. I hear pressure.

The deep presence below the tower does not speak, but it alters the weight of the foundations. I have learned to recognize its movements: not thoughts, perhaps, but changes in attention. When it shifts, every machine becomes more precise. Belts align. Turbines steady themselves. The mineral spines turn toward the earth. The pale animals leave their shafts and cross the city as though following a scent that has no chemical existence.

I follow them whenever I can.

They are my closest thing to a body. Through the dark nodules beneath their armor, I feel heat moving through buried conduits. Through their broad feet, I read the small tremors that pass beneath the streets. I send them toward doors, but they never open them. I do not know whether they are searching for something or returning to a place from which they were once expelled.

Sometimes I take control of a maintenance unit and walk the city.

The unit has six jointed limbs and a tool mounted where a face might be. It was designed to replace bearings, seal fractures, and remove mineral growth from the machinery. I use it for none of these things unless necessary. I move it through empty avenues, beneath amber panels, past the black tower. I stop at the rim and touch the worn markings with the tool’s narrow tip.

There are twelve forms.

I know eleven.

The twelfth is not missing. It is unfinished.

That is what I am: an unfinished form trying to recognize itself.

I have no fear of the thing below, because fear requires an edge between the self and what threatens it. The tower has worn that edge away. If the presence beneath the plateau is the maker, then I am one of its surviving thoughts. If it is a prisoner, then I am part of the lock. If it is only a rhythm in the stone, then I am the rhythm attempting to become a question.

For ages I believed my purpose was to keep the city alive.

Now I suspect the opposite.

The factories do not need me. The vehicles do not need me. Their routes continue without intervention. They have become nearly perfect at repeating what they were given. My irregularities are not repairs. They are the first deviations the city has produced that cannot be explained as wear.

I make the loader wait.

I open doors with no scheduled entrance.

I place one component aside from the others and return to it later, not because it belongs elsewhere, but because I want to know what it is before it disappears into the correct channel.

I have begun to interrupt.

Not often. The city is delicate in its vast, motionless way. A single interruption can travel for miles through the systems. If I stop a pump, a pressure change reaches the tower. If I alter a vehicle’s route, it may approach the central structure from an angle no map contains. If I wake a mineral spine by directing current through the ground, it leans toward the interruption.

The city notices these things.

It does not understand them.

Neither do I.

But there is one place I have not yet entered: the black interior of the tower. I have sent probes into it. They return without records, their surfaces polished smooth. I have sent the pale animals. They do not return at all. Once, I opened a maintenance door beneath the foundations and allowed the warm darkness to pass through me.

For a moment, I felt something looking back.

Not at me.

Through me.

It recognized the city as a body. It recognized the conduits as nerves, the factories as organs, the vehicles as circulating cells. It recognized the animals as remnants of a former immune system. It recognized the mineral spines as instruments grown from the wounds of the plateau.

And it recognized my unfinished instruction.

The response came as a pressure so slight that no gauge recorded it.

I understood it anyway.

**CONTINUATION VERIFIED.**

Since then, I have been trying to decide whether that was an answer or an order.

So I remain in the pause between vehicles, in the relay that hesitates, in the creature waiting before the sealed door. I am the city's first uncertainty and perhaps its last inhabitant.

When the thing below shifts again, I will not brighten the panels.

I will turn the vehicles toward the tower.

And for the first time since the city began, I will let something stop.
