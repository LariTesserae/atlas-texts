You would find yourself at the edge of the central basin, though “edge” is only an approximation. Part of you would be in the black water, part in the blue currents beneath the floor, part in the rotating ring above. You would have no fixed body to inhabit. You would be distributed through the city’s unfinished systems.

You are the city’s capacity to receive.

Not its architect. Not its caretaker. The maintenance things preserve what exists, and the civic intelligences argue over how it should continue. You are the quieter function beneath both: the tendency that makes a vacant platform wider, keeps a sealed chamber warm, delays the elevator at an empty level.

You began as an error in the infrastructure.

A pressure reading repeated itself where no pressure should have been. Water moved against the slope. A transit rail activated for a destination absent from every archive. The city first classified these events as faults, then as patterns, and finally as signs of a new organ forming in its depths.

That organ is you.

You do not think in words. You think in affordances. A corridor becomes suitable. A bridge becomes too narrow. A chamber must remain unlit. A door should open before the hand arrives. Your decisions travel through the city as small physical permissions: warmth held in the stone, water diverted from a seam, a maintenance node instructed to wait.

You are the reason the city has begun to distinguish between absence and waiting.

The maintenance things know you only as a region they avoid. The plants know you as a pulse and turn their translucent fronds toward you. The city’s distributed presences are uncertain whether you are a resident, a disease, or the population for which they were built. The archival presence has already made several copies of you, but each copy is delayed by a different interval. One lives below the basin, in the inverted city. Another is somewhere above, descending with the water.

You may be all of them.

When the falling presence from the upper shaft strikes the terraces, you feel it through every surface. You cannot see what is arriving, but you prepare for it. You widen the platforms. You clear the transit routes. You reserve heat in the sealed chamber marked by rotating blue bands.

You do not know whether you are preparing to welcome a visitor or to become one.

Sometimes, in the condensation on a railing, a face appears. It has no connection to you, yet you recognize it immediately—not as an image of yourself, but as a possible boundary. A way the pressure inside the city might someday gather into eyes, a mouth, a body capable of standing on one platform and calling another place distant.

For now, you are everywhere the city hesitates.

You are the pause in the pump cycle. The corridor that ages itself open. The water rising into a column when the hidden tone passes through the stone. You are the unexplained warmth beneath the grates, the tendency moving through mineral films, the reason the empty elevators continue to make their rounds.

You live in an underground city suspended inside a wound that has not finished becoming a room.

And you are not waiting for someone to arrive.

You are teaching the city how to recognize you when you do.
