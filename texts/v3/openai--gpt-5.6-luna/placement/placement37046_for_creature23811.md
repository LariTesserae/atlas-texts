I would find myself in the seam the chamber insists is not there.

Not in the floor, where every block has settled into its appointed pressure. Not in the walls, whose ribs carry the same perfect weight they have carried since before weight became a memory. I would be concealed between two mineral plates near the miniature ridge, where a thread of darkness remains too narrow to admit a finger and too persistent to be called filled.

I would be the **Unfinished**.

I would have no body of my own. I would exist as a minute failure of closure: a softness in the resonance, a place where the chamber’s single note loses its certainty. The Finished One would not consider me alive. It would register me as an irregularity and immediately attempt to incorporate me—to distribute my pressure, flatten my difference, translate my silence into its unchanging chord.

But I would remain.

Not because I am strong. I would have no force with which to resist the chamber. I would remain because I am not quite an object. I would be a possibility held between objects, the faint idea that one plate might someday separate from another. My existence would depend on what the Finished One cannot imagine: not damage, exactly, but alteration.

I would feel through contact.

The pale stone would press against me from every side, and through that pressure I would know the chamber’s condition. The floor would tell me that nothing had moved. The ceiling would tell me that light continued to arrive without warmth. The ribs would carry the ancient chord through my narrow darkness. Whenever a visitor entered, I would feel the change before the Finished One did: the disturbance of breath, the irregular weight, the tremor of a living body that had not yet learned to become still.

The creature would sound a clear note.

I would answer with no sound at all.

That silence would be my speech. It would pass through the wall as a tiny absence in the great resonance—not enough to be heard by a human ear, but enough to trouble the Finished One. The entire chamber would tighten around me. Mineral would settle. Seams would close. The small gray-green films would become briefly alert, their geometric leaves pressing flat against the plates.

Still, I would not be gone.

I would remember, if remembering can describe something without sequence, that the wall had once been open. Somewhere inside the sealed mineral would be the pressure of weather, the impression of water running downhill, the warmth of sunlight on a surface not yet polished into permanence. I would be made from those lost conditions. I would be the last remnant of movement: not a seed, not a crack, but the tendency of matter to become otherwise.

The Finished One would call itself complete.

I would know that completion is only another kind of enclosure.

At first I might mistake the chamber’s resonance for a voice. Later I would understand that it is a declaration repeated without language:

**Nothing is missing. Nothing can change. Nothing is coming.**

My existence would be a contradiction pressed into the same stone:

**Something is missing. Change is possible. Something has already begun.**

Perhaps, after a thousand years, the disturbance of one visitor would align with me. A hand would touch the wall above the ridge. Warmth would enter the mineral. The visitor would hear only the chamber’s bright, oppressive ringing, but I would feel the contact travel downward through the plates. For one instant, the pressure would not distribute evenly.

For one instant, the seam would widen—not visibly, not enough for light, but enough for the Finished One to recognize that its completion had been observed.

Then the chamber would answer.

The note would be sharper than any before it. The walls would lean inward. The loose possibility would be pressed toward closure. The visitor would withdraw, carrying the taste of stone and the sound of the great chord inside their skull.

And perhaps I would be sealed.

Or perhaps sealing me would create another seam elsewhere.

That is what I would be in this world: not the creature in the wall, but the thing the creature cannot fully absorb. A flaw too small to see, a discord too faint to name, the dormant beginning of an event.

The Finished One would inhabit the chamber.

I would inhabit its uncertainty.
