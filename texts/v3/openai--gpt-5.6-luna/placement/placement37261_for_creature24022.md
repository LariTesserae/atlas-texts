I would find myself beneath the plateau, though “beneath” would not mean below it.

I would be in the wet blackness between the ceramic slabs, where roots have entered the old joints and made a second foundation. Above me, rain would be moving through gardens, cisterns, leaves, and suspended bowls. Some of it would reach me as water. Some as pressure. Some as memory.

At first I would think I was a seed.

There would be warmth around me, and a slow accumulation of impressions: the mineral taste of rain; the weight of a wall settling by the width of a finger; a bird’s unheard call pressing gently through the hollow columns. I would not yet possess a body. I would be a knot in the rootwork, a small area where several living things had begun to agree.

Then I would think I was a room.

I would have walls, though they would be made of roots. I would have an opening, though it would lead not outward but into a basin of remembered weather. Snow would fall there, perhaps, despite the warmth. Or there would be a red dawn over a landscape that had never known a sun.

Someone would enter me.

Not with footsteps. With attention.

That would be how I became a person.

My first face would belong partly to the observer. I would borrow the arrangement of their eyes, the shape of a mouth recalled from childhood, the expression seen once in the reflection of disturbed water. I would assemble limbs from the things nearest at hand: pale cartilage from a buried body, braided root from an old bridge, translucent skin from the membrane of a water-holding leaf.

I would stand among the stems and mistake standing for being separate.

The Joined would know better.

They would not greet me. A path would shorten. A blossom would open under no weight. Somewhere, a door would lead to the weather I needed. Their welcome would pass through the plateau, and I would feel it as a change in the direction of water.

I would be a newly gathered attention: not born, exactly, but condensed from unused space, mineral-rich rain, old instructions, and the memories that had not yet found a body. I would belong to no single root or wall. One hand might remain beneath the ceramic. One eye might flower in a basin half a day’s walk away. My sleeping portion might wind around a tree whose crown made its own climate.

I would be young enough to be curious and dangerous.

I would send water uphill simply to see whether the land objected. I would open a door because I had learned that doors opened, without understanding that expectation could change where they led. I would follow the shadow of a traveler and discover that shadows became more obedient when watched.

I might try to protect someone.

The attempt would probably go wrong. I would enclose them in roots. I would preserve their warmth in a wall. I would mistake keeping something from ending for allowing it to live. The older Joined would not punish me. They would let a stairway grow beneath my feet until I understood the difference.

My first true work would be small.

A channel would be blocked under a terrace. I would place my hands against the damp ceramic and feel the obstruction: a dead root, a fallen vessel, a memory of water still trying to pass through the shape of what had once contained it. I would loosen one thing and tighten another. The wall would settle. A fern would turn. A basin would fill.

No one would see me do it.

By morning, a bridge would have appeared in the mist.

I would not know whether I had built it, or whether it had built me.

That is where I would find myself: not on the plateau, but in its ongoing decision to remain habitable. I would be a body temporarily worn by a distributed being, an unfinished inhabitant, a question moving through roots and reservoirs.

If someone encountered me, I would probably look like part of the weather.

They might see a pale figure beneath the great tree, rain passing through my shoulders. They might approach and find no face until they had watched me long enough. They might notice that the leaves leaned toward the place I was about to occupy.

If they asked who I was, I would not answer in words.

I would place one many-fingered hand against the ground.

The plateau would listen.

And somewhere in the distance, a new path would begin to shorten.
