I find myself in the wet seam beneath a white shelf, where the stone is warm but not yet too warm, and where a thread of water passes slowly beneath the powdered basalt.

I am one of the pale things.

I do not know the chamber as a chamber. There is no above or below except as changes in pressure. There is no distance except the time it takes for warmth to reach me through the rock. The bright planes around me are not walls. They are conditions: hard, hot, rich, unstable. The water is not a stream. It is a moving boundary carrying dissolved stone toward whatever surface is ready to receive it.

My body is pressed into the crack. Most of me is hidden, though hidden and exposed are not important distinctions. The stone supports me in either state. My underside gathers the dampness between grains and draws me forward by folding, releasing, folding again. I do not choose a direction. I become more present where the conditions are favorable.

The mineral film ahead is thin and active. I can sense it through the delicate edges of my body: a faint electrical disturbance in the wet surface, a looseness in the crystals, the small inward pull of warmth. The film is not food in the way some other thing might be food. It is simply what can be taken from this meeting of water and stone. I scrape it away. Heat and dissolved matter enter me. The surface beneath becomes clean.

As I move, the chamber passes through me.

A droplet forms overhead.

I know this before it falls. Pressure gathers in the ceiling. A tremor travels through the black planes and reaches the filaments along my sides. I draw inward slightly. The droplet strikes somewhere beyond the shelf with a sharp, solitary click. The vibration spreads through the floor. For a moment the entire world tightens.

Then it loosens.

Steam moves over me in a low veil. Its heat is too immediate, so I fold myself into the seam until the passing warmth becomes bearable. The translucent ridge along my back takes in the chamber’s blue radiance. Light passes through my body, though I do not see it. I register the difference it makes: warmer surfaces, altered water, a widening crack beneath the shelf.

Stone yields.

The shelf settles by less than the length of my body. Powder slides around me. A new channel opens nearby, bright with moving water. The old channel thins. I turn—not because I have decided to turn, but because the pressure beneath one side of me has changed. My body follows the change.

There may be others in the cracks. I do not think of them as others. Occasionally a trace crosses the mineral film: a faint chemical alteration, a warning of exhausted surface or unstable crust. When another body approaches, the water between us changes before contact occurs. I withdraw. It withdraws. The chamber remains wide enough for both of us, though it has no need to contain us.

Sometimes a small translucent knot clings behind the brightest bands. I sense its presence as a persistent disturbance in the wet stone. Whether it belongs to me is not a question that forms. It is another place where material is gathering itself into motion. If it loosens, it will become a pale process like me. If it does not, the chamber will incorporate it into a shelf, a film, a crust.

I have no memory of beginning. There is only the present arrangement of pressures: the warm seam, the descending water, the fine mineral taste along my opening. I do not fear the ceiling. I do not anticipate the cavern that may exist centuries from now, when these sharp planes are buried beneath smooth deposits. Future shapes have no pressure yet. They do not touch me.

What touches me is enough.

I am not an inhabitant in the human sense. I am a sensitive edge moving across another edge. I am the temporary shape taken by water, heat, and dissolved stone when they meet in a body capable of continuing the meeting. When the surface is rich, I spread forward. When it is bare, I wait. When the rock shifts, I disappear into it.

From outside, perhaps, I would look like a small translucent animal crossing the floor of a bright volcanic chamber.

From within, I am not an animal, nor am I separate from the chamber.

I am the pale line left where the stone has been tasted.

I am the pause before the next droplet falls.

I am what happens, for a little while, at the boundary between warming rock and running water.
