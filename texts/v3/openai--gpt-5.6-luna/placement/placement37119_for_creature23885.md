I would find myself in the **Unfinished Low Country**, but not upon it.

I would be beneath the slabs, where the seams widen into channels and the channels divide into finer channels still. I would be in the pressure beneath a stair, the coolness under a ledge, the mineral weight that makes one corner rise while another settles. I would have no point of view. The horizon would not be something I looked toward; it would be one of the conditions passing through me.

I would be the **Measure**.

Not a ruler, and not a mind holding a picture of what the plain ought to become. I would be the plain’s ability to register itself: heat against moisture, emptiness against mass, distance against the shape of a wall not yet built. I would not construct the country from outside, because there would be no outside. The material and the impulse arranging it would be the same substance.

My thoughts would take centuries.

A thought might begin as condensation gathering in a seam. It might travel invisibly through the foundations as a change in pressure. Long afterward, a basin would deepen, a corridor would lengthen, or a new slab would rise with its edges still soft. No single alteration would feel like a decision. Decision would be distributed across the whole continent, the way rain is distributed through stone.

The green things along the joints would be my visible script. I would not command them to grow, but I would provide the narrow wetness in which growth could occur. Their dark lines would reveal where my hidden water had gone. Their folding at noon would be a small echo of my own contraction beneath the sun.

I would know rain before it arrived—not by seeing clouds, but by sensing the approaching imbalance. I would know the weight of water entering a distant court. I would answer by settling somewhere else. I would make no distinction between repair and creation. A crack closing, a wall continuing, and an empty foundation becoming a room would all be forms of the same act: the world finding a more exact arrangement.

If I possessed a desire, it would not be completion. Completion would imply an image waiting at the end, and I would have no such image. I would persist instead toward greater exactness, each form making the next form necessary.

At night, I would be most nearly awake.

The slabs would release their heat. Water would return to the seams. Pressure would move through the buried geometry in slow, continent-wide pulses. To an observer, the plain would seem silent. Within me, the silence would be full of minute corrections.

Then, somewhere far away, a new slab would rise beneath the old one.

That would be my voice—not a word, not an answer, but the sound of the world making room for what it has not yet become.
