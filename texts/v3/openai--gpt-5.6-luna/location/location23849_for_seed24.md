The place is a low, enclosed basin beneath the ground, no larger than a village green, though there are no paths or buildings and no sign that anything has ever been made there. Its ceiling is close enough to feel present but too high to see: a broad roof of dark, heat-softened stone, sinking toward the edges and leaving the center as a shallow chamber. The floor is nearly level. Long ago, molten material spread across it and cooled into a black, slightly rippled plain. Since then it has changed very little.

The stone is basalt, but not wholly bare. In the hollows between its old flows, pale mineral dust has gathered—gray, yellow-white, and faintly reddish in places. Some patches have hardened into thin crusts that break under a careless foot with a dry, porcelain sound. There is no mud, no dampness, no glint of standing water. The warmth comes steadily through the rock itself, not in gusts or pulses. The air is dry and close, carrying the faint smell of hot stone, iron, and something chalky.

Darkness fills the basin almost completely. It is not the absolute darkness of a sealed box; the stone holds a weak suggestion of distinction, enough for the floor to separate from the walls and for the nearest shapes to become shadows within shadow. There is no direction to the dimness. Nothing shines overhead. Nothing reflects. After a while, the eye stops expecting detail, and the chamber becomes known more by its boundaries than by its appearance.

Sparse growth occupies the warmer cracks. It is low and widely spaced: dull, fibrous tufts with leaves like narrow strips of leather, each coated in a powder that protects it from the dry air. Some are no more than a ring of brittle stalks around a dark seam in the rock. They do not form a carpet or spread across the basin. Between them lie broad stretches of mineral floor, undisturbed and empty. Their roots descend into the stone’s shallow fractures, taking what little vapor and dissolved substance the heated ground provides. They are not vigorous plants. They persist.

Nothing moves among them. There are no insects in the crust, no small bodies in the cracks, no birds or burrowing things. The growth has no need to defend itself from grazing, and so it stands thinly and without urgency. A few old stalks have fallen and remain where they landed, gradually becoming the same color and texture as the dust beneath them.

The silence is almost complete. Occasionally a grain of crust slips from a wall and ticks once across the floor. Somewhere far inside the rock, there may be a low settling note, too deep to call a sound so much as a pressure felt through the soles. The chamber does not echo much; its rough surfaces take the sound in. A spoken word would travel only a short distance before becoming indistinguishable from the warm, dry air.

The basin has no visible entrance, though the stone around one side is fractured into a narrow vertical cleft. It is not a passage so much as an imperfection, filled with colder darkness. No draft comes through it. Nothing enters or leaves. The place has reached an old balance: heat beneath, mineral dust above, a few stubborn plants between, and long intervals in which none of these things noticeably changes. Even erosion seems finished here. The walls are rounded, the floor settled, the loose material distributed into its final shallow drifts.

It is a small world with no horizon, no weather, and no need for weather. Warmth remains in the rock. Darkness remains in the air. The few living forms remain where they have rooted, separated by wide fields of black stone. Beyond the chamber, the deeper ground continues without promise or threat. There is nowhere to go, and nothing waiting to arrive.
