### The Power Waits

The benevolent power does not descend upon the Stillworks as a sun, a judge, or an engineer.

It does not break the mountains open. It does not silence the factories. It does not turn the concrete to soil or pull the inhabitants from their corridors and carry them elsewhere.

It knows better than to confuse an unfinished thing with a ruined one.

Instead, it arrives as a change in the pressure beneath the basin.

For the first time in an age, nothing new is extruded.

No wall lengthens during the night. No staircase appears beneath a tower. No clean-edged platform rises from the fractures in the ground. The factories continue their slow work, but the work no longer generates additions. The Stillworks remains exactly as it is for one full condensation season.

At first, no one notices.

The walkers continue along their routes. Birds abandon one level and occupy another. The pale mammals gather beneath the lamps when the distant siren—still silent—ought to sound.

Then an older walker places a hand against a wall and feels nothing moving behind it.

The wall is not leaning.

A door that has always disliked her opens on the first attempt.

She enters and finds a room containing a table, two chairs, and a window. Beyond the window is not another wall, nor a distant district of the Stillworks, nor a scene from elsewhere.

There is darkness.

Not the dark of a shaft or reservoir, but an ordinary darkness, deep and soft, scattered with points of light. Stars. The walker has seen diagrams of them. She has never seen them gathered in one place.

She sits.

The Stillworks waits for her to become a chair.

Nothing happens.

Her clothing does not harden. No hinges appear at her elbows. The table does not incorporate her hands. For the first time, stillness is not interpreted as surrender.

This is the first gift.

The power does not remove the old rule. It places another rule beside it:

**Nothing that is still by choice shall be assigned without consent.**

The change travels outward slowly.

A person may stand beside a rail and remain a person. A bird may sleep on a belt without being carried away. A pool may lie undisturbed without deepening into a shaft. A tool may be left on a step and remain a tool until someone decides otherwise.

The inhabitants do not trust this at first.

They test it cautiously. They place a copper wire on the floor and watch it through an entire day. They leave a warm stone beside a drain. They sit in groups at junctions, waiting to see whether the country will build around them.

It does not.

The Stillworks is not pleased. It is accustomed to using everything. Its walls flex. Its indicator lights flicker. In distant districts, several machines stop halfway through their cycles and refuse to continue.

The benevolent power does not punish the resistance.

It understands that the Stillworks has survived by converting uncertainty into function. To ask it to stop without teaching it another way to endure would be cruelty.

So the power gives it a second gift.

It introduces a place where nothing is required.

Not a garden. Not a sanctuary enclosed behind walls. The Stillworks would only turn such a place into another department.

The place appears in the central hollow.

One of the concentric weather levels becomes calm. The mist withdraws. The frost melts without becoming water. The upward rain ceases its ascent and falls gently into the black reservoir below.

Then, through a seam in the manufactured floor, something dark enters.

Soil.

It does not arrive in trucks or descend through pipes. It gathers from absence: from the dust beneath old tiles, from the residue of lichen, from mineral silt in the gutters, from the fine powder made by centuries of ceramic wear. It collects in the hollow until there is enough to hold a root.

The first plant is small and unremarkable.

A green stem rises beside a bolt.

The walkers gather around it. The birds watch from the rails. The pale mammals come out of the service tunnels and stand in a circle, transparent eyelids opening and closing.

The plant does not withdraw from shadow. It does not grow toward a pressure gauge or into a cable housing. It grows where it is.

This alarms the Stillworks more than any failure could have.

For several days, the country attempts to incorporate it. A drainage channel bends toward the hollow. A wall unfolds a bright metal panel above it. A maintenance arm descends, pauses, and waits for instructions.

No instructions come.

The plant remains.

Then the power does something more difficult than making life.

It makes room for death.

When the first bird falls from its nesting hood, its body does not become insulation. It does not flatten beneath a threshold or reappear as a signal in a distant room. It lies beneath the new plant, and the soil receives it.

The walkers mourn in the only way they know. They stand beside the body without moving.

The Stillworks trembles.

Nothing happens to them.

Gradually, the country learns that not every ending is a conversion. Some things may simply cease, and still belong.

This becomes the third gift: an end need not be another assignment.

The power does not free those who have already become machinery—not by tearing them from their housings, which would destroy what remains of them. Instead, it gives their signals a wider language.

A pressure gauge can now say *I remember.*

A door can say *I am afraid.*

A lamp can say the name of a person who once passed beneath it.

The walkers begin visiting these places not to restore the transformed, but to listen. They discover that some machine-people wish to remain where they are. Some ask for alteration. Some ask to be turned off. Some ask only that someone sit nearby while their lights grow dim.

For each request, the Stillworks makes space.

The power never chooses for them.

At last it reaches the center ring.

The hollow’s opposite side has become clear. The shimmering uncertainty recedes, and the walkers see what lies beyond: not a promised land, not a lost civilization, but an open country under a changing sky. Brown grass bends in wind. Clouds move with no schedule. A river makes a curve because it has been allowed to.

The gateway remains open.

No one is compelled to leave.

Some do. They move slowly, carrying salt, copper wire, warm stones, and the habits of a lifetime. At the threshold, their boots hesitate, unable to understand ground that has not been poured or bolted into place.

One walker steps forward.

The soil gives beneath her weight.

She nearly falls. Then she laughs—not loudly, but enough that the sound travels across the open country and returns without distortion.

Others follow.

Many remain in the Stillworks. The factories still operate. The rails still tremble. New structures still appear, though now more slowly and only where the country has been invited to build. Birds still nest in conveyor hoods. Water still flows toward impossible places.

But the basin no longer holds its breath.

It has learned that continuation is not the same as compulsion.

And the benevolent power, having done what it came to do, does not stay as a ruler or guardian. It becomes something the Stillworks can bear:

a pause in every mechanism,

a hand not forcing the door,

a dark, star-filled window in a room that did not exist before,

and, beneath the machinery, the patient knowledge that a place may remain itself without becoming only what it has been made to do.
