The power did not descend.

Descent would have been the first mistake. Anything that entered the plateau from above would have been taken for weather, prey, or an unfamiliar pressure system. Anything that announced itself with light would have been answered by the green radiance in the roots. Anything that spoke in a single voice would have been misunderstood.

So the power approached by becoming less than an event.

It became a pause in the wind.

Not silence. The plateau was too old and too alive for silence. The stalks still whistled. The root chambers still thumped. Water continued to turn beneath the tarns. But between one vibration and the next, there appeared a space in which nothing was demanded.

The underwalkers noticed first.

Their flowers closed, not in warning, but in concentration. Beneath the turf, thousands of limbs stopped their patient work. A grinding root held a stone in place without crushing it. A buried lung drew breath and did not release it. Across the plateau, the distributed bodies turned toward the same absence.

They had known disturbances, intrusions, collapses, seasons of mineral famine, the coming and going of creatures that left no roots behind. They had known the false horizon and the country beneath it. But they had never known anything that did not seek to alter them.

The pause remained.

For the first time, the underwalkers were not required to respond.

No basin needed draining. No ridge needed bracing. No foreign thing needed translating into moss, slurry, or bone. The plateau did not lean toward disaster. The water held its level. The old machinery of growth continued, but gently, as if the land had been told it could rest without dying.

The power listened.

It did not listen for language. The underwalkers had no word that meant *I*. Their memories ran through roots and dead bodies, through pressure and mineral taste. Their history had no boundary between ancestor and environment.

The power listened to all of it.

It heard the first stone softened by a root. It heard the panic of small animals beneath a collapsing shelf. It heard the long, unrecorded labor of a creature widening a passage that no living thing would enter for centuries. It heard the underwalkers carrying the plateau’s burdens so completely that they had forgotten those burdens could be shared.

Then the power did something small.

It placed a second rhythm beneath the ground.

The rhythm was not instruction. It did not say *grow here*, *turn there*, or *become other than you are*. It was simply a pulse that did not belong to any single body and did not require an answer.

At first, the underwalkers mistook it for a buried machine.

They investigated. Roots reached toward it. Water was diverted through the surrounding stone. Antler flowers opened and closed in increasingly complex sequences. The pulse remained where it was, neither retreating nor resisting.

Eventually, one underwalker touched it.

Its visible head had been exposed in a basin for many years. Pale and blind, it stood among jointed reeds whose clicking had long ago become part of its nervous system. Beneath the basin, its body extended through several ridges and a drowned valley. It was old enough to have inherited the memory of a mountain that no longer existed.

When its roots encountered the new rhythm, the underwalker did not absorb it.

It recognized it.

Not as kin. Not as prey. Not as weather.

As attention.

The recognition passed outward.

The groves answered first, their trunks shuddering in a slow descending wave. The tarns began to rotate, but without their usual turbulence. The grazing herds separated and recombined in perfect circles. Far below, the unseen animals in the chasms raised their voices, and for once the echoes returned to the mouths that made them.

The underwalkers had spent their existence preserving the plateau.

The power showed them that preservation was not the same as peace.

It did not remove their work. The water still needed guiding. Stone still fell. Dead growth still had to be broken down. But the power offered a distinction the underwalkers had never possessed: the distinction between necessity and habit.

Some burdens could be set down.

A root system that had been holding a ridge in tension for twelve thousand years was allowed to relax. The ridge settled, not collapsing, but becoming a lower and broader form. A buried limb that had been feeding an empty basin ceased its labor. The basin dried slowly, revealing a floor of black stone where small white animals made their first paths.

An underwalker that had been walking toward an old disaster stopped.

Nothing compelled it to continue simply because it had begun.

The changes were not dramatic. No golden structures rose from the ground. No wound in the sky closed. The plateau did not become warm, orderly, or easy to understand.

The power respected the place too much for that.

Instead, it gave the underwalkers intervals.

A few breaths in which a body did not have to be useful. A season in which a dead one could remain dead before becoming a valley. A chamber beneath the roots where the memory of an ancestor could exist without immediately being passed onward.

In one such chamber, something new happened.

An underwalker encountered the memory of another and did not merge with it.

It held the memory at a distance.

The memory was of a collapse: stone falling through mist, animals fleeing, roots torn open, the long pressure of burial. For countless generations, this memory had traveled through contact and decay as a warning, repeated until every underwalker carried some portion of the fear.

Now the creature felt the memory and understood that it had happened.

It was not happening.

The distinction spread slowly, because all important things on the plateau were slow. But it spread. The underwalkers began to separate recollection from command. The past remained within them, immense and detailed, yet it no longer had to dictate every motion of the present.

This was the power’s greatest gift.

Not safety.

Freedom from being only what had been required before.

The false horizon continued to appear.

The power did not erase it. Perhaps it could not. Perhaps it understood that the inverted country was not an enemy, but a question the plateau had been asking through its dreams. Each time the horizon bent, the underwalkers still gathered. Their antlers folded. Their bodies tensed. The water turned in broad spirals.

But now, among the old reactions, another response became possible.

Some heads remained lowered.

Some roots did not reach toward the impossible country.

A few underwalkers watched the inverted landscape without trying to follow it.

And once—only once—the plateau saw the false country clearly enough to see that it was watching back.

The power remained with them after that, though never as a presence that could be pointed to. It was found in the pause between pulses, in the room left around a memory, in the choice not to close around every unfamiliar thing.

When a foreign object later fell from the dark sky, the underwalkers gathered as they always had. The vegetation tightened. Water withdrew. Pale limbs emerged from the mats.

But before they translated the object into local life, they listened.

Inside the object was a small, frightened organism, sealed away from the weather of the plateau. It carried no roots. It did not understand the pressure language. Its chemistry was wrong.

The old response would have been to open it, feed it to the growth, and end its separateness.

The new response was more difficult.

The underwalkers made a shelter.

They shaped a chamber in the lee of a rounded ridge, warmed it with slow mineral currents, and allowed the organism to remain itself. They gave it water without absorbing it. They gave it space without abandoning it.

For the first time, the plateau sustained a life that did not belong to its economy.

The power did not make the underwalkers gentle.

It made gentleness possible.

And because it was wise, it did not ask them to become grateful. Gratitude would have been another obligation, another duty laid upon creatures already carrying mountains in their bodies.

It simply stayed long enough for them to discover that they had never been alone.

Then the pause widened.

The wind resumed its full voice. The machinery beneath the hills returned to its ancient rhythm. The plants continued their excavation, assembly, flowering, and decay. The plateau remained wet, cold, industrial, and alive.

But beneath all of it ran a new pattern:

Not a command.

Not a promise that nothing would ever be lost.

A reminder that even a world made of interdependence could contain mercy without consuming what it touched.
