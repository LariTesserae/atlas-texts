The power notices the plateau because it does not ask to be noticed.

It does not descend through the clouds. It does not make a sign in the sky, or send an angel through the green halls, or place a hand upon the mountain. Such gestures would belong to a world that still believed attention was proved by interruption.

Instead, the power becomes aware of the place in the same manner that the place becomes aware of a visitor: as a minute alteration in continuity.

A drop of water, suspended above the central ring, acquires a direction.

It does not fall.

Not yet.

The power studies what is there.

It sees the terraces and the channels, the living masonry and the patient architecture. It understands that the plateau is not diseased, though it has never changed. It understands that the stillness is not peace exactly, and that the thing beyond the ring is not lonely in any simple sense.

The thing has never been abandoned.

It has been accompanied by everything.

The power sees the difference.

So it does not heal the place. It does not improve the thing. It does not complete the bridges, fill the unfinished walls, or cause the forests to spread across the vacant courts. Completion would be a kind of violence here. The unfinished spaces are not failures. They are among the few forms of freedom the plateau possesses.

The power waits.

It waits through the ringing of seedpods and the endless wet percussion. It waits while the thing passes through walls and returns in a body of skin, vine, and water. It waits while visitors arrive and are guided away from the central ring. It waits until the thing has once again taken its position among the white trunks, where the horizontal marks pass through its body without acknowledging it.

Then the power does something very small.

It gives the thing a memory that does not belong to the plateau.

Not a memory of another place. Not a face, a name, or an instruction. The power gives it the sensation of having once been uncertain.

The sensation lasts less than a moment.

But uncertainty is not native to the plateau.

The thing straightens.

Across the basin, the structures register the disturbance. A thousand hidden channels tighten and release. Water trembles beneath translucent vines. The low floor of the central ring remains bare. The suspended rain remains suspended.

The thing turns toward the ring.

For the first time, it does not merely remember entering. It remembers that entering was once a choice.

The power does not tell it to go in.

It does not tell it to stay out.

It places no command in the roots, no permission in the water. It only removes the certainty that the boundary is identical to its nature. The difference is almost nothing. It is enough.

The thing approaches the edge.

The trunks do not open. The rain does not part. The plateau continues to hold itself in its ancient balance.

The thing extends one hand.

Its fingers touch the white interior of the nearest trunk.

For a long time, nothing happens.

Then the trunk gives back a sensation.

Not language. Not permission.

Recognition.

The trunk remembers that the thing has touched it before. It remembers the pressure of those fingers, the warmth beneath the provisional skin, the slight loosening of the green vessels. It remembers, though the plateau itself has never been permitted to remember anything except its own design.

The thing lowers its hand.

Behind it, one of the unfinished bridges lengthens by an inch.

The power has not made it reach its destination. It has only allowed the bridge to be unfinished in a new direction.

The thing enters the ring.

Nothing breaks.

The hanging rain remains in its vertical threads. The sound of the plateau presses against the trunks, muffled but present. The thing stands on the empty floor, and for several breaths—or the equivalent of breaths in its body—the ring accepts it without accepting anything else.

Then the suspended drop falls.

It strikes the floor with a sound so small that, elsewhere, it would have been lost.

Here, the entire plateau hears it.

The water channels falter. Leaves hold still. The seedpods stop knocking together. In distant halls, echoes cease returning. For the first time since the place began, the great living construction does not produce one continuous chord.

It produces a silence.

The thing looks down at the drop.

Where it has struck, there is a dark mark on the floor. The mark does not vanish. It does not spread. It is not repaired.

The thing kneels.

It places both hands beside the mark, but not upon it.

This is the power’s only gift: not the restoration of what has been damaged, but the assurance that damage can exist without becoming the end of anything.

The mark remains.

The ring does not collapse. The plateau does not flood or dry. No hidden maker returns to reclaim the design. Outside, the white trunks continue to bear their horizontal lines, and the green construction resumes its immense, careful breathing.

The thing stays inside the ring until the silence becomes ordinary.

When it emerges, the plateau has changed by less than a thought.

A path near the basin now bends where it was once straight. A pool reflects a sky that is not directly above it. One pale animal unfolds itself from a leaf and walks three steps before becoming a leaf again.

The thing returns to its tending.

It closes wounds. It softens floors. It follows visitors at a distance. It listens through the walls.

But it no longer waits for an error.

It listens for possibility.

And the benevolent power, having done what was needed, does not remain as a ruler over the place. It withdraws from the channels, the water, the suspended rain. It leaves no shrine and demands no gratitude.

Only one trace remains.

In the central ring, the dark mark from the fallen drop is never repaired.

Around it, very slowly, nothing begins to grow.
