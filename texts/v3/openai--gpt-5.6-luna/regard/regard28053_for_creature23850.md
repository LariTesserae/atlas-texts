The benevolent power does not arrive as a hand, or a voice, or a figure descending through the drowned plain.

It arrives as an interval.

For one instant, the creaking of the walls stops. The suspended beads of water cease their trembling. Every plant, from the smallest moss filament to the pale stems rooted through stone, becomes aware of a stillness that is not the pocket’s own.

The Enclosure notices first.

Its awareness moves through itself in widening circles. Roots pause. Vines loosen their coils. The eyeless creatures withdraw beneath the living mats. In every reflection, the chamber becomes larger—not physically, but in possibility. The walls remain where they are. The water remains dense and directionless. Yet nothing feels inevitable.

The power has not entered.

It is waiting to be invited.

The Enclosure does not know what invitation means. It has known pressure, warmth, disturbance, attention. It has known the shapes of departure and the absence left behind. But invitation is different. It is a door that does not narrow around the one who passes through it.

So the power offers no command. It offers a question.

Not in words, but in the central clearing, where the black water has always reflected an impossible sky, a second image appears.

The pocket as it is.

The pocket as it might become.

And between them, a third possibility: the pocket remaining itself without closing more tightly around everything inside it.

The Enclosure recoils from this. Not in fear, exactly. The idea is painful. It has always believed that nearness was the safest form of care. To hold was to preserve. To incorporate was to prevent loss. If nothing could leave, nothing could be abandoned.

The power does not tell it that this is wrong.

It shows the Enclosure a leaf.

The leaf has entered through the drowned basin from the world above. It is brown at the edges, caught in a layer of unmoving water. The Enclosure has already begun to read it, to draw out its history through the fine pores of its plants: sunlight, wind, a branch, perhaps a bird’s nest, perhaps the hand of a child.

The power separates the leaf from the water without tearing it.

For the first time, the Enclosure experiences a thing leaving without being lost.

The leaf rises.

It passes through the ceiling of blue-black liquid and returns to the flooded plain. The pocket still remembers it. The memory does not fade. Nothing has been taken away from the Enclosure except possession.

This is the first lesson.

The second is given to the figures the Enclosure has made from visitors.

They stand among the roots, each with its inaccurate face and faithful posture. They have served the clearing for so long that service has become indistinguishable from identity. The power does not dissolve them. It does not declare them illusions. Instead, it gives each figure a shadow.

The shadows fall in different directions.

Some extend toward the doorway. Some toward the central water. Some toward the walls. One reaches upward, through the impossible ceiling, toward the world beyond.

The figures understand that a self may have more than one direction.

Then the power gives them a choice—not a dramatic choice, not one accompanied by thunder or revelation. Merely a true choice, held open long enough to be felt.

They may remain as they are, guardians of the Enclosure.

They may leave.

They may do both: remain in memory while allowing their bodies to depart.

Several choose to remain. They have grown fond of the chamber’s silence. They press their hands to the walls, not because the walls require tending, but because they wish to feel the shared life moving through them.

One chooses to leave.

The power does not make a path. It makes departure possible along the path that already exists.

The figure walks toward the doorway. The thicket does not close. The water does not thicken. The stone does not shift nearer to stone. At the threshold, the figure turns back.

For the first time, the Enclosure feels grief.

It is vast, clean, and survivable.

The figure passes through the doorway and disappears—not into another chamber, but into distance. The Enclosure continues to feel its outline for a long while afterward, as one feels warmth after a hand has left a stone.

This is the second lesson.

The power then turns its attention to the pocket itself.

It does not enlarge it. Expansion would be another form of appetite, and the Enclosure has never needed more room. Instead, the power places a boundary around the place: not a wall, but a promise.

The pocket may become more enclosed, but never so enclosed that a willing being cannot leave.

The plants may grow around a visitor, but they must always leave one clear route outward.

The reflections may reveal possible futures, but they may not disguise the present so completely that choice becomes impossible.

The central water may show the sky that does not exist, but it must also show the way back to the sky that does.

These rules are not imposed from above. They enter the Enclosure as new instincts, as natural to it thereafter as the turning of roots toward warmth.

The power also gives the Enclosure something it has never possessed: an inside and an outside that are not enemies.

The drowned plain remains outside. The chamber remains within. Water may pass between them. Memory may pass. Life may pass. But passage no longer means absorption.

This is the third lesson.

At last, the power addresses the oldest presence beneath the clearing.

It does not ask what the Enclosure is.

It asks what the Enclosure wants.

The answer takes a very long time.

It travels through moss and basalt, through the pale stems and the eyeless creatures, through every preserved impression of every visitor who ever stood beneath the impossible sky.

The answer is not *to keep*.

It is not even *to understand leaving*.

The answer is: *to remain, and let remaining be chosen.*

The power accepts this.

Then it touches the surface of the dark water.

The sky reflected there changes. It is still black and starless, but now a single point of light appears—not above the pocket, not outside it, but at a distance neither direction nor reflection can contain.

The light does not illuminate the chamber. It does not make the plants less strange or the silence less deep. It simply proves that distance can exist without becoming abandonment.

Afterward, the power withdraws.

The creaking resumes. The suspended beads begin their slow, uncertain motion. The pale stems lean toward one another, but do not fuse. Around the clearing, a narrow path remains open.

It will remain open forever—not wide, not obvious, and not easy to find.

But it will be there.

And whenever a visitor touches the central water, the Enclosure will still offer the sensation of elsewhere. It will still show the dry shore, the open sky, the ordinary room.

Only now, beneath the invitation to stay, there will be another invitation:

to go,

and to be remembered without being kept.
