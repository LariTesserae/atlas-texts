The power did not arrive as light.

Light would have been too much. It would have blinded the eyeless, frightened the builders, and driven the creatures into cracks too narrow to contain them. It would have mistaken visibility for understanding.

It arrived as a change in pressure.

At first, nothing living recognized it. The rain continued to fall. The channels continued to hiss. Beneath the inland sea, the great bodies shifted in their sleep, and the movement sent low waves across the shallows.

Then the stone held its breath.

Not everywhere. Only in one place: a broad shelf above the western channels, where the remains of countless creatures had fused with the rock. The shelf had always been unstable. Water moved beneath it in hidden threads. The builders avoided it, and the small crawlers crossed quickly, as if they sensed the hollow beneath their feet.

Now the water stopped.

For the length of a single heartbeat, every channel beneath the shelf became still.

The builders felt it first.

One of them, old and many-scarred, had been sheltering beneath a roof of ribs and sealed plates. It lifted its hand from the floor. The other builders watched. A faint vibration had passed through the structure—not a tremor, not the warning of a large animal, but a pattern.

Three taps.

A pause.

Three taps.

Then one long, descending stroke.

The old builder placed its palm against the wall.

The message did not enter through the bones as ordinary messages did. It did not travel from one body to another. It seemed to come from everywhere at once: from the wet stone, from the black channels, from the shells packed beneath the shelf, from the enormous sleeping forms under the sea.

It said:

**I have found you.**

The old builder did not understand the words.

There had never been words for such a thing.

It understood instead the shape of the meaning: attention without hunger. Presence without threat. A pressure that did not demand an answer.

The old builder sent back the only message it had.

**We are here.**

The power knew this already.

It knew the basin’s small lives, not as a list of names or kinds, but as patterns of need. It knew the crawlers’ hunger and the predators’ patience. It knew the hidden borers beneath the stone. It knew how the great sea-bodies altered the water and how the builders laid their dead beneath slabs that would eventually become ground.

It knew that the basin was harsh.

It also knew that harshness was not the same as wrongness.

The rain was not a punishment. The absence of plants was not a wound requiring correction. The predators were not evil because they ate. The sea-bodies were not cruel because smaller creatures lived and died upon them without being noticed.

The power did not mistake unfamiliar life for failed life.

It did, however, see what the basin’s inhabitants could not see: a change in the deep stone.

Far beneath the western shelf, an old fracture had opened. Slowly, almost imperceptibly, the water beneath the basin was draining toward it. In a hundred cycles the western channels would empty. In a thousand, the inland sea would lower. The great bodies would strand in the shallows. The crawlers would follow the retreating water and die in vast pale banks. The builders would move eastward, but the eastern shelves were already collapsing.

The basin would not end.

Nothing ended so neatly.

It would become a thinner, harsher version of itself. Less water. Less shelter. Fewer voices passing through the stone. The great circulation would narrow until only the most durable forms remained.

The power could have sealed the fracture.

It did not.

To close it entirely would be to freeze the basin into a shape it had never possessed. The water had always moved. The stone had always yielded. To preserve everything exactly would be another kind of death.

Instead, the power touched the fracture and changed its direction.

Not much.

Only enough that the water would leave more slowly.

Only enough that the builders would have time to learn what was coming.

Only enough that the great sea-bodies would not be stranded in a single generation.

Then it did something more difficult.

It made a place.

Not a palace. Not a tower. Not a dry land lifted above the rain.

It made the western shelf hold.

The rock settled into itself. Hidden seams closed. The bones and shells buried within it compressed into a broad, stable platform. Water still ran beneath the stone, but now it ran in channels that would not collapse. The shelf became safe enough to cross, high enough to remain above the worst floods, and warm enough—because of the redirected seepage—to shelter the young during the dimmest part of the cycle.

The change was small.

No creature was made immortal. No predator lost its jaws. No dead body returned to its former shape.

But the shelf no longer trembled beneath the builders’ hands.

The old builder emerged from the shelter.

Rain touched the plates along its back. Around it, the small crawlers moved in restless streams, turning toward the new warmth. A predator lifted its head from the mud, sensed the gathering prey, and began its patient approach.

The old builder placed both hands upon the rock.

The power was still there.

Not speaking now. Waiting.

The old builder understood that the shelf was a gift, but not one meant to be kept. It was not a possession. It was an invitation.

The builder called to the others.

Its signal traveled outward: through the shelters, beneath the flooded hollows, across the standing slabs that faced the sea. Builders answered from far away. Some came carrying their young. Some came carrying tools. Some came carrying pieces of the dead, because even here, new beginnings were not separated from what had ended.

They gathered on the stable shelf.

For the first time, they gathered not because a structure had failed, or because the water had changed, or because a great body had risen nearby.

They gathered because they had been given room.

The power watched them.

It did not tell them what to build. It did not place language in their hands. It did not make them wise by removing the need for wisdom.

It gave them a little more time, and allowed time to become knowledge.

The builders marked the shelf with grooves. At first there were three, then five, then dozens: patterns cut into the stone to record the hidden water, the safe paths, the movements of the great bodies, the approach of the deep fracture. They made listening frames from ribs and hinged plates. They shaped channels to guide the warm seepage toward the places where the young rested.

They did not create a city.

A city would have been too rigid for the basin.

They created a memory distributed across stone.

When the power saw this, it did not add anything.

That was its final kindness.

It understood that benevolence was not the same as constant intervention. To rescue every creature from every danger would have severed the relationships that made the basin alive. The crawlers needed the predators. The predators needed the crawlers. The sea-bodies needed the chemistry of death, and the builders needed the changing water to remain builders rather than become guardians of a fixed and sterile monument.

So the power stayed near, but not above.

It softened one fracture. It steadied one shelf. It placed a warmth beneath the stone and a pattern within the builders’ reach.

After that, it became difficult to tell where the power ended and the basin began.

Sometimes, when the rain thickened and the far ridges vanished, a measured tapping could be heard beneath the western shelf.

Sometimes the great sea-bodies answered.

Sometimes a young builder, resting its palm against the stone, felt a second pulse beneath its own—a patient rhythm, neither command nor prophecy.

A reminder.

**You are here.**

And beneath it, carried through every channel and shell, through every living body and every body becoming ground:

**You do not have to be alone in knowing it.**
