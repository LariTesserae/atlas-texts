The benevolent power does not break the floor of the sea.

It does not lift the chamber into sunlight, drain it of water, or make the inhabitants resemble anything that lives above. It understands, almost at once, that rescue can be another word for destruction. The hollow is not waiting to become something else. Its darkness is not a mistake. Its closeness is not a poverty to be corrected.

The power attends first.

It feels the chamber as a whole: not a mind hidden behind the walls, but a thousand slow decisions distributed through membrane and current. It feels the pale beings pressing their bodies against new stone. It feels the fish moving through passages they have worn into habit. It feels the predator folding itself through the upper levels, hungry and necessary. It feels the old dead becoming thresholds, chalk, shelter, and food.

It feels the chamber’s fear—not fear of death, for death is ordinary here, but fear of being changed too quickly to remain itself.

So the power does nothing visible.

For a while.

It watches the unfinished ribs thicken and the lower basin settle. It watches the currents bring eggs, dust, salt, and traces of distant life. It learns the rhythm of the gill-curtains. It learns how long a gate remains open for a familiar body. It learns which blue colonies brighten before a collapse and which go dark only afterward.

Then it finds the danger.

Far beneath the city, a fracture is widening. It is not large. Nothing in the chamber is large. But it runs behind three living walls and beneath the basin where the pale beings have gathered around their new growth. If the seabed shifts again, the fracture will open. Water from the deep earth—hot, poisonous, and heavy with metals—will pour into the hollow.

The chamber will not die at once.

That would be simpler.

Instead, its membranes will fail one by one. The blue colonies will go dark. The worm towers will dissolve. The fish will crowd into the upper passages, where the predator will feed until it becomes sick. The pale beings will press their hands against the walls and feel the city losing its ability to answer them.

The power does not prevent all change.

It places one hand beneath the fracture.

Not a hand of flesh. Not a wall descending from elsewhere. It is a pressure introduced into the deep stone, a counterweight so subtle that the seabed does not know it has been touched. The fracture slows. Its edges stop grinding against one another. The hot water remains below, held in the dark by a strength that resembles the chamber’s own patient architecture.

The power leaves the danger there.

A sealed world would not be alive. The inhabitants must still encounter risk. They must still lose bridges, eggs, and bodies. They must still discover that some passages cannot be saved.

But the catastrophe will not arrive unnoticed.

The power changes the chamber’s memory.

Where the fracture begins, the mineral ribs grow faintly warmer. The blue colonies near it develop a new pulse: not light exactly, but a pattern. Three dim flashes. A pause. Three flashes again. The signal travels along the walls through bacterial films, through living membranes, through the pale mineral dust.

At first, the inhabitants do not understand.

One of the tall beings places its hand against the stone. It feels the pulse. Another feels it farther away. A third presses its body into a narrow passage and discovers that the rhythm changes with distance.

They gather.

They have gathered before around eggs, new growths, injured animals, and places where the current has become strange. But this gathering is different. The signal does not say *come here*. It does not say *feed*, *hide*, or *close the gate*.

It says, in the only language the chamber has ever possessed:

*Something is becoming dangerous.*

The pale beings follow the pattern through the city.

They find the warm ribs. They feel the held pressure beneath them. They do not know that a power has intervened. They do not kneel. They do not name what they find. Instead, they begin to work.

They alter the currents around the fracture. They thicken the living walls. They guide mineral-secreting tissue toward the weakened places. They abandon a lower passage and reinforce another. They move eggs from the basin into higher hollows. They do not save everything. They save what they can identify as worth the effort.

This is what the power gives them: not safety, but warning.

Not answers, but the possibility of answering.

The power also touches the predator.

It does not remove its hunger. Hunger is part of the chamber’s circulation, and the power is too wise to confuse cruelty with the presence of a predator. But it changes the currents in the upper levels so that the many-mouthed creature encounters a dense stream of blind fish drifting in from the open sea.

The predator turns toward them.

It feeds there instead of among the dwellings.

Some fish are lost. The city remains.

The inhabitants notice this as well. They begin to recognize that currents can be guided, not merely endured. Their work changes. They do not build walls against the predator; they shape channels that lead its attention away from the densest nurseries. They cultivate food in certain passages and empty others. They create places where a hunter can hunt without entering the heart of the settlement.

They have not become peaceful.

They have become deliberate.

The power does not give them eyes, because eyes would be less useful than the senses they already possess. It does not give them speech, because speech would divide what is presently shared. It does not give them hands free from the webbing and pressure of their world, because those hands are already suited to persuasion rather than command.

It gives them one new capacity only: the ability to distinguish an event from a pattern.

The chamber has always remembered through altered passageways. Now it begins to remember through expectation.

A gate closes before a collapse. A blue colony dims before poison arrives. A particular tremor means the upper ribs will shed stone. A certain taste in the current means eggs are coming. The inhabitants do not foresee the future in images. They sense its approach as a change in pressure.

The power remains near this ability, but never takes it away from them.

It could make the chamber permanent. It could halt the growth of the mineral ribs, fix every bridge in place, preserve every useful passage exactly as it is. But that would be a kind of death. The chamber is alive because it is unfinished. Its inhabitants are alive because they must participate in what comes next.

So the power protects the conditions of becoming.

It keeps the seam above from sealing completely, though it narrows it so that no sudden shaft of light floods the city. It allows a small amount of mineral-rich water to enter through a new crack, enough to feed the blue colonies and the rootlike organisms, not enough to poison the lower streets. It strengthens the chamber against the next shift of the seabed, but not against every shift after that.

It makes room where room is most needed.

Not broad room. The inhabitants would not know what to do with emptiness. Instead, it creates a few additional layers within the closeness: a pocket behind the nursery, a passage that curves instead of ending, a sheltered hollow where injured creatures can rest without being immediately consumed.

The city does not become spacious.

It becomes less cruelly crowded.

Years pass—or their equivalent. The pale beings continue to guide the new growth. They begin to return to the fracture at regular intervals. They touch the warm ribs and feel the faint answering pressure beneath the stone. They understand, imperfectly, that something holds the danger back.

Some believe the deep earth is sleeping.

Some believe the chamber itself has grown a hidden organ.

Some believe that the pulse in the blue colonies is a message from every dead creature whose body became part of the walls.

They are all partly right.

The benevolent power does not correct them. Wisdom is not the distribution of proper names. It is knowing when a mystery is nourishing.

Once, one of the pale beings rises again to the uppermost seam. It presses its sensory fans toward the gray light. Beyond the stone, the ocean is immense. Beyond the ocean, the sky burns with distances no body in the chamber could survive.

The being feels the power there—not as a face, not as a voice, but as an absence of threat.

The light does not demand that it leave.

The sea does not demand that it understand.

The being turns back.

Below, the chamber pulses around its inhabitants. Fish pass through the ribs. Worms work the buried dead into pale towers. A bridge of living bodies shifts beneath a careful step. Somewhere in the upper levels, the predator feeds in a channel prepared for it. Somewhere below, a new gate closes around eggs.

The power does not stand above the city.

It enters the city’s way of continuing.

It becomes the slight delay before a wall collapses. The warning carried by blue light. The current that brings food without bringing poison. The pressure beneath a fracture. The extra hollow left unclaimed until it is needed.

And when the pale beings press their hands against the growing stone, trying to persuade it into a shape that will shelter more than it harms, the stone answers with a faint warmth.

Not approval.

Not command.

Only this:

*Continue.*
