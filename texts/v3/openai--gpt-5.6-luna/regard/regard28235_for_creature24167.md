The benevolent power does not enter the city.

It does not descend through the ceiling, though it could. It does not widen the central line or reach beneath the basin to take hold of whatever moves there. It does not halt the contractions, fill the fractures, or complete the buildings according to some design it considers beautiful.

It knows that a place still becoming can be harmed by being finished.

Instead, it attends.

Its attention is unlike the attention of an eye. It does not press upon the creatures or force the city to reveal itself. It rests around the basin as gently as warmth rests around a stone. No wall is watched more closely than another. No being is singled out and made into a chosen one.

For the first time, nothing is required to hide.

The flat creatures feel this before the city does. They remain visible beneath the stairs. Their dark knots do not dim when the power notices them. They do not flatten into the walls. The larger beings below sense it too, though they have no word for the absence of threat. The pressure beneath the central line becomes less lonely.

The power learns them by waiting.

It discovers that the creatures are not asking to be made numerous. They are not asking for a purpose. They do not desire a completed city, because completion would end the changes by which they know themselves.

What they lack is not a destination.

What they lack is the ability to choose what may be lost.

So the power gives them that—not as a command, but as a new pressure woven into the stone.

From then on, when a contraction begins, the inhabitants feel two movements beneath the ordinary one. The first is the familiar tightening of the basin. The second is a small resistance, present only where living bodies are gathered. The city may still close a street, fold a tower, or bury a chamber, but it no longer does so without warning.

A faint warmth travels through the walls.

The plants open their fans.

The columns sound one clear tone.

The creatures understand: this change is coming.

If they remain, the stone will carry them into its new shape. If they withdraw, the city will leave a hollow behind.

At first they do not know what to do. Their existence has always been obedience to pressure. They have followed the movement of the city as water follows a slope. Now the slope branches.

Some stay.

Some leave.

Neither is punished.

A creature that chooses to remain may be altered beyond recognition. Its limbs may become columns, its body a cavity, its dark knots dispersed through an entire wall. But some part of its pattern will remain coherent, and the others will be able to find it again.

A creature that withdraws discovers that the city has made room for withdrawal. Behind a fresh ridge, beneath an unmoving stair, there are chambers that do not collapse. They are not comfortable. They are not warm. But they are stable for one full cycle, then another, and the creatures begin to gather there.

The power does not make these chambers permanent.

It makes them dependable.

That is enough.

The city’s changes continue, but they become less wasteful. It no longer seals every passage merely because it can. When it raises a tower, it leaves a lower route beneath it. When it closes a district, it preserves one wall through which the old pressure can still be felt. When it builds around the central enclosure, it does not encircle it completely.

It leaves an opening.

Not a door. Not yet.

A possibility.

The beings begin to use one another differently. Until then, recognition had meant holding an injured body in place while the city decided what to do with it. Now they learn a second form of recognition: asking.

They ask through pressure.

One presses a limb to the ground. Another answers from the wall. A third withdraws. If the first continues, it means remain. If it changes its pattern, it means release.

There are mistakes. Some creatures are absorbed because they cannot decide in time. Some refuse help and become dark hollows. Some gather around another so completely that their signals become confused, and all of them are carried into a new structure together.

The power does not prevent every sorrow.

Wisdom is not the removal of consequence. It is the making of consequence bearable, legible, and not needlessly cruel.

It gives the creatures memory that is more than pattern.

When one of them is lost into a wall, the others can still sense the shape it once held. Not the creature itself, perhaps, but its preference: the angle at which it liked to cling, the chambers it avoided, the kind of fresh stone it sought. These traces move through the city like faint underground currents.

The dead are not returned.

They are permitted to matter.

The power also attends to what lies below.

It cannot—or will not—remove the enormous presence beneath the central line. That being is too deeply woven into the city, and perhaps the city is too deeply woven into it. To tear one away would be to destroy both.

But the power places a boundary around the line.

Not a wall. A distinction.

The thing beneath may pass there, as it always has. It may send its pressure upward. It may call the columns to song. But it may no longer take a creature without first being answered.

The first time this happens, every inhabitant gathers in the circular enclosure.

They arrange themselves around the dark line. The plants remain folded. The columns are silent. Far beneath them, the hidden being moves along its path.

The city tightens.

The creatures feel the summons.

Then, together, they refuse to become merely its organs.

They do not flee. They do not attack. They place their bodies against the mineral and hold their separate patterns apart from one another. Each remains distinguishable. Each says, in the only way available to it:

I am here.

The pressure below increases.

The line darkens.

For a long time nothing changes.

Then the presence beneath withdraws.

Not defeated. Not banished.

Answered.

Afterward, the city builds a second line beside the first.

This one is pale.

It does not lead downward. It leads nowhere the creatures can yet perceive. But it remains when all other new edges darken. Warmth passes along it in both directions, and the beings understand that the city has acquired an alternative to compulsion.

It can invite.

The benevolent power stays with them through many cycles, never becoming an object within the place. It does not speak in a language. It does not give them names. It does not tell them whether they are animals, thoughts, nerves, or the first citizens of a world.

It protects the uncertainty.

When at last the city is able to sustain a chamber that does not change, the power does not fill it with furniture or light. It places only one thing there: a surface that remembers every pressure laid upon it and releases none without permission.

The creatures enter slowly.

One presses its body to the surface.

The memory remains.

Another follows.

Then another.

For the first time, they encounter one another without the city changing beneath them.

They do not know that this is a gift. They do not know what a gift is.

But they remain together until the chamber’s stable interval ends.

When the walls begin to move again, they leave by choice.

And the power, having done what it came to do, does not depart.

It becomes the small mercy in the architecture: the warning before collapse, the hollow left open, the memory that survives absorption, the answer required of the dark below.

It does not make the city safe.

It makes safety possible.

It does not tell the beings what they are for.

It ensures that, whatever they become, they will have some part in becoming it.
