The benevolent power did not descend as a figure.

A figure would have been too much like an answer.

It did not arrive with wings, or fire, or a voice that could be mistaken for the roar beneath the plateau. It entered as a small correction in the world’s accounting: a place where pressure did not become fracture. A pause held so precisely that the surrounding stone could feel, for the first time, what stillness was.

The change began at the great fissure.

The upward-flowing darkness hesitated. Pebbles suspended above it ceased falling and remained where they were, neither returning nor departing. The roar beneath the plateau thinned by one almost inaudible layer. The plants felt this before the inhabitant did. Silver grasses stopped clanging. The glass growths held their angles. The blue filaments drew in their subsonic vibration until the highlands seemed to have acquired a second silence beneath the first.

The inhabitant gathered itself.

Its tall form rose from the stone beside the crack. Its limbs emerged in abrupt planes, six or seven intentions given temporary shape. The pale opening along its upper body turned toward the new stillness.

It looked.

Nothing altered.

The stone did not become heavier. The shadows did not climb. No root changed direction. For the first time since the plateau had begun to make itself, attention did not immediately become an instruction.

The inhabitant withdrew slightly.

The benevolent power understood that this was fear.

Not fear of harm, exactly. The creature had no clear boundary in which harm could occur. It feared the unfamiliar absence of consequence. It had spent its existence noticing and thereby changing. To look without changing was, to it, like reaching for the ground and finding no ground beneath the hand.

So the power did not speak.

It made a place where the inhabitant could remain without becoming responsible for what surrounded it.

The place was very small. It appeared inside the central shelter, where the walls met at impossible angles. A circle of ordinary darkness formed on the floor—not the reversed darkness of the fissure, not an abandoned shadow, but simple absence of light. Within the circle, distance behaved consistently. Near remained near. Far remained far. A stone placed at its edge did not migrate toward its proper relation to the world. It could be left where it was.

The power did not complete the circle. One section remained open.

This mattered.

A closed boundary might have become a prison, or a definition imposed from outside. The opening meant that the inhabitant could enter and leave. It meant that the place belonged to no one until chosen.

Then the power did something more difficult than giving.

It waited.

The plateau continued lifting itself. A ridge rose the width of a hand. Somewhere beyond the violet horizon, a seam opened with a sound like distant metal breaking. The tree in the basin became transparent in one column, revealing a landscape of red shelves beneath a green sky. The image vanished.

Nothing was repaired.

The power did not bring rain. Water would have been an astonishment, perhaps even a kindness, but kindness without understanding could become another form of violence. The roots had learned vapor. The plants had built their lives around scarcity. To flood them would not restore them. It would erase the knowledge they had made.

Nor did the power lower the plateau. It did not soften the cliffs, settle the hovering stones, or force the unfinished valleys into completion. The world was not sick because it was incomplete. Its incompletion was its mode of life.

Instead, the power placed one thing within the creature’s reach:

A choice that would remain a choice after it was made.

At the threshold of the central shelter, a pale line appeared in the mineral floor. It led outward toward the grove, then stopped. It did not indicate a destination. It did not pull. It did not promise safety. It was simply a direction that would remain available.

The inhabitant’s opening turned toward it.

The plateau waited.

The creature extended one limb. Its end entered the circle of ordinary darkness. No stone answered. No plant divided. No shadow detached itself. The limb remained its own.

For a long while, the inhabitant did nothing.

Then it brought the limb back.

The power did not reward this. There was no light, no music, no sign that the correct action had been performed. The ability to withdraw was left undramatized, because the power understood that a choice made for applause was not yet freedom.

After some time, the creature extended the limb again.

This time it crossed the circle’s edge and touched the pale line.

The line brightened—not with command, but recognition.

Across the plateau, the silver grasses answered with a single tone. The sound was thin and almost lost beneath the subterranean roar, yet it traveled farther than any sound had traveled before. It passed through the shelters, around the basin, into the green-black tree. One of its columns became transparent.

Inside it was not another landscape.

Inside it was a small, dark room with an open door.

The creature saw itself standing there.

Not as a mountain. Not as a fissure, a grove, a pressure, or a body assembled from unfinished intentions. It saw a form with a boundary. The form was temporary and imperfect. It did not contain the whole plateau. It did not explain the roaring depths. It was only one shape the creature could occupy without ceasing to be everything else.

The image lasted three breaths.

Then the column became solid again.

The benevolent power remained.

It had done enough to make solitude less absolute, but not enough to become the inhabitant’s master. It had offered a boundary without demanding enclosure, silence without demanding peace, direction without demanding arrival.

And because it was wise, it knew that benevolence could not consist in preventing every danger.

The creature would still sometimes incorporate what approached it. Visitors, if any came, might still feel their bones lengthening toward the ridges. Shadows might still separate from their owners. The plateau would continue to grind and rise. Some valleys would fail to form. Some possible worlds inside the tree would be abandoned.

But now, at the center of the unfinished land, there was a place where a thing could remain itself.

The inhabitant returned to the shelter and lowered its many limbs around the circle—not touching it, not closing it. The gesture resembled protection. It also resembled learning.

For the first time, the plateau changed in response to an act that was not driven by pressure.

A shallow basin formed near the grove.

It did not fill with water. Instead, it gathered sound. The grasses leaned toward it. The glass trees turned their crowns away from the sky and toward its depth. The roar beneath the land descended into a quiet, comprehensible pulse.

The creature listened.

The power listened with it.

And beneath the unfinished world, something was still deciding what the place would become.

But now the decision was no longer happening alone.
