The benevolent power does not descend.

There is nowhere to descend from, and no form it could take that would not become another pressure in the crowded hollow: another heat, another hunger, another certainty imposed upon the Unfinished.

So it begins with attention.

It attends to the smallest creature first—not because the smallest is most important, but because it is easiest to overlook. A translucent thing no larger than a mote of steam is being carried toward one of the new hollows in the rain. Within that hollow, a cold blue ocean turns beneath a black sky. The creature has no limbs suited to water, no memory of cold, no desire that can be distinguished from the currents pulling it inward.

The power does not prevent it from entering.

Instead, it places beside the creature a single possibility that has never existed in the pocket before:

a pause.

Not stillness. The hollow has never known stillness. The creature continues to move, the water continues to gather, the rain continues to fall—but for the length of one breath, the creature is not compelled by what surrounds it.

It may enter the blue ocean.

It may cling to the warm stone.

It may become something neither.

The creature does not understand the gift. Understanding is not required. It folds three of its almost-limbs beneath itself and remains at the edge of the hollow until the ocean closes.

This is the first kindness.

The power then turns toward the pool.

The pool has been choosing directions for a very long time. It has drawn paths toward what is highest, what is deepest, what is most reflective, what is most unlike itself. It has never been asked what it wants. The power does not command it to become still, nor does it grant it a single true direction. It gives the pool a center.

Not a point, but a place within itself that cannot be washed away.

From then on, when the basin becomes wall and wall becomes ceiling, the pool may turn in any direction without losing the knowledge of where its middle is. Its surface may reflect deserts, moons, clouds, eyes, and impossible landscapes. But beneath every reflection there remains a small, dark steadiness.

The creatures notice.

At first they mistake it for a new kind of prey.

Then they discover that touching the center does not erase a distinction. A claw remains a claw. A wound remains a wound. A creature that drinks there does not become less separate. It becomes briefly able to feel the boundary of its own body without having to defend it.

The predators learn this before the others.

One catches a pale, many-segmented creature near the pool. It has already opened its mouth when the center reflects them both: hunter and hunted, distinct and trembling. The predator pauses. It does not release its prey out of pity. Pity is not yet among its instincts.

It releases the prey because, for the first time, it can feel the difference between hunger and the thing hunger is trying to consume.

The prey escapes into a crack.

The predator remains beside the pool, astonished by the emptiness inside its mouth.

The power does not abolish predation. It does not turn every creature gentle. It gives them only enough room to discover that a choice can exist between impulse and act.

Some will choose to act.

Some will choose otherwise.

The power permits both, because benevolence without freedom is merely another name for control.

At the highest ledge, the four-limbed beings are cutting water-threads when the change reaches them. They feel it first in their tools. The basalt splinters grow warm, then cool. The mineral flakes cease becoming fingers. The fingers cease trying to become tools.

For a few moments, the workers do not know what to do with objects that will remain what they are.

One drops a sharpened shard.

It strikes the ledge and does not become anything else.

The sound is tiny. It travels through the hollow like a bell heard underwater.

The workers gather around the shard. They touch it with their mouths, their hidden hands, the plates of their faces. They have never possessed a thing that did not demand transformation. They do not know whether this is a failure or a miracle.

The power makes no declaration.

It simply leaves the shard where it fell.

The four-limbed ones eventually use it to cut a thread of water. They use it again later. After many workings, it becomes dull. It does not grow, remember, speak, or acquire an intention of its own.

It is allowed to be only a tool.

This is the second kindness.

Then the power attends to the oldest ones, those assembled from too many lives.

They have carried the directions of others for so long that they have forgotten which movements begin in themselves. When they sleep, the pocket dreams through them. When they climb, they climb toward places that belong to creatures long vanished. Their bodies are crowded with continuations.

The power does not separate them. To separate them would be to destroy what they have become.

Instead, it gives each of them one interior room.

No wall appears. No new chamber opens in the basalt. The room is made of permission: a space in which no other creature’s memory can enter unless invited.

The oldest ones recoil from it.

One has been inhabited for so long that solitude feels like injury. Another mistakes the room for death. A third fills it immediately with the image of a red horizon and a creature it once consumed.

But gradually they learn to enter.

They learn that carrying another is not the same as being carried by another. They learn that remembrance does not require possession. They learn that an absence may remain an absence without needing to be turned into a limb, an eye, a path, or weather.

For the first time, one of the oldest creatures encounters a memory that belongs only to itself.

It is not grand.

It is the sensation of cool water touching the underside of a newly formed foot.

The creature keeps it.

No one else can drink it.

The power’s greatest work is reserved for the rain.

The drops are still falling. Each one strikes the stone and opens a world: green vegetation, blue oceans, enormous eyes, places where the laws of the hollow have never been tested. The Unfinished rush toward them, as they always have. Curiosity and hunger move together. Some creatures are drawn into the new hollows; some are devoured by what waits within; some return carrying weather beneath their skin.

The power does not close the openings.

It does not protect the Unfinished from every danger. A world without danger would be another kind of prison, and the creatures would remain dependent on the hand that made it safe.

But it changes the thresholds.

Every hollow opened by the rain gains an edge.

At that edge, a creature may know that it is crossing.

Not what lies beyond. Not whether it will survive. Not whether it will remain the same.

Only that it is crossing.

This knowledge passes through the basin in a silent wave.

The blind creatures feel it as warmth beneath their skins. The eye-creatures see it as a thin silver line around each new landscape. The wall-drinkers taste it in the condensation. The climbers encounter it at the ceiling, where the black stone no longer pretends to be solid.

The four-limbed ones understand it most clearly.

They begin to place their tools beside the openings.

Not as barriers.

Markers.

A chipped shard beside a green hollow. A mineral flake beside the cold sea. A sliver of glass beside the enormous eye. They do not know why they do this. They have no word for boundary, and no word for return. But the markers say:

Here is where one thing became another.

Here is where you may decide.

The first creature to return from the blue ocean brings back a skin of frost. It places the frost beside a marker and waits.

Nothing happens.

The frost melts.

The creature remains.

This astonishes the hollow more than any transformation. A thing has been changed, and yet it has not been claimed by the change. The cold is no longer inside it. It is simply something that happened.

The power allows this knowledge to spread.

Not as doctrine. Not as instinct. It moves through touch, reflection, imitation. A creature sees another return and realizes that returning is possible. Another sees a predator release its prey and discovers that hunger need not be the only future hunger has. Another finds the center of the pool and learns that an inner place can remain one’s own.

The hollow does not become peaceful.

There are still bites, absences, ruptures in the wall. There are still creatures that feed on distinctions and creatures that pursue the same prey along paths with no distance between them. There are still moments when the floor becomes a ceiling and newborns panic as the world overturns.

But now, during the shudder, some creatures hold on to one another not because they must.

Because they choose to.

And when the water hesitates before choosing a direction, the power does not choose for it.

It waits.

The entire pocket waits with it.

For the first time, the silence is not the silence of an event too private for sound. It is the silence of attention shared by many beings at once.

The pool gathers itself.

It considers the highest wall, the lowest seam, the black ceiling, the center it has been given. It considers all the landscapes reflected in its small surface, all the creatures crowded around it, all the directions it has followed because they were the only directions available.

Then it chooses—not upward, not downward, but outward.

A thread of water leaves the pool and passes through the basalt without breaking it. It travels along the hidden mountain, through the pressure-dark stone, beyond the unseen summit. It does not become a river. It does not escape.

It makes contact with the world outside.

Far above—or far below—the clenched hand, on the summit of the mountain that cannot be seen, a single bead of water appears in the open air.

It hangs there, bright and improbable.

Within it is the reflection of the hollow.

Within that reflection are the Unfinished, moving among one another: hungry, curious, altered, distinct.

The benevolent power places the bead where something might someday find it.

Then it withdraws its attention—not away, but deeper.

It leaves the creatures their heat, their water, their transformations, their tools, their dangers, and their impossible paths. It leaves them the right to become, to remain, to return, to vanish, and to be remembered without being possessed.

And it gives them one final gift, so subtle that they mistake it at first for a change in the stone.

Whenever one of them looks outward and finds no outward there, something looks back.

Not an eye.

Not a face.

A presence without demand.

A vast and patient assurance that the hollow is not required to justify its existence by becoming larger, older, safer, or complete.

The Unfinished do not become finished.

They become witnessed.

For a place like this, and for beings like these, it is enough.
