### The Power Waits

The benevolent power does not descend.

There is no figure upon the high plain, no hand laid against the black stone, no voice entering the pressure organs and declaring what the plateau ought to become.

It has seen such interventions before.

It knows that a landscape can be harmed by being improved. It knows that a mind, when given an answer too early, may mistake obedience for understanding. It knows that the difference between a miracle and an invasion is often only the scale at which the recipient is capable of refusing.

So it observes.

It watches the green colonies open themselves to light they cannot name. It follows the slow paths of memory through the crystals. It learns the distributed inhabitants not as a collection of mechanisms, but as a people whose first language is arrangement.

The power recognizes their loneliness.

Not the loneliness of an animal separated from its kind. There are no animals here, and no single self is complete enough to feel abandoned. The plateau is connected to itself through mineral pressure, electrical gradients, and the patience of stone.

Still, it is alone in a larger sense.

Nothing beyond the plateau has answered.

The inhabitants have no concept of an outside. Their world ends where their gradients become too weak to matter. Beyond that is not mystery but irrelevance: a thinning of influence, a quiet in which no crystal can retain a pulse.

The power does not mistake this for suffering. It does not assume that all beings require company, language, water, warmth, or a sky crowded with moving things.

But it does understand possibility.

The plateau is manufacturing its future. It is producing minds without knowing that minds are what it produces. It is approaching individuality by increments so small that each new self appears first as a chemical preference, then as a recurring response, then as a continuity extending across time.

What it lacks is not life.

It lacks the chance to be surprised.

So the power makes one alteration.

It does not add a creature. It does not seed the plateau with a body that would compete with its inhabitants or become an object of worship. It does not introduce water, which would tear through the existing chemistry like a flood through a library.

Instead, it touches the distant dark.

The change is almost nothing.

A star, far beyond the atmosphere, shifts in its burning by less than the width of a thought. One narrow band of radiation reaches the plateau with a new rhythm. It arrives so faintly that no plant opens in response. No pressure organ activates. No crystal records it at first.

The signal is not a message.

It is an unfamiliar condition.

The first to notice is a small mineral intelligence beneath an amber spindle in the northern basin. Its memory-filaments register a fluctuation that does not belong to the sun, the buried seams, the pressure organs, or the slow settling of the shelves.

The fluctuation repeats.

Not regularly. Not randomly.

It repeats according to a pattern that contains no command.

The crystal does not understand it. Understanding has not yet become one of its functions. But it preserves the alteration rather than correcting it. This is the first act of attention directed beyond the plateau.

The crystal changes color beneath its surface.

A nearby colony responds. Its silver plates turn toward the sky, though the new radiation is too weak to nourish them. The colony does not open. It merely holds its position.

Farther away, a pressure organ delays its next pulse.

The delay travels through the rock.

For the first time, the plateau produces a response that is not caused by anything within itself.

The power does nothing more for a long while.

It allows the inhabitants to misinterpret the signal. Some incorporate it into their mineral cycles. Some reject it. Some reproduce it in altered form, sending pale bands of color across the dust. A few crystal minds become devoted to preserving the disturbance. Others gradually erase it as noise.

The power does not correct them.

It understands that a gift becomes a command when the giver insists upon its meaning.

Centuries pass.

Then the plateau changes.

The response to the distant rhythm begins producing structures that had not previously occurred. Thin crystals rise in pairs, joined by a transparent bridge. Green colonies arrange themselves not around mineral seams but around empty centers. Pressure organs synchronize across distances too great for any local process to require it.

The inhabitants are not merely reacting.

They are making a question.

It takes them thousands of days to complete. The question begins in a basin where pale plates turn their reflective undersides toward the sky. It continues through a chain of crystals, each preserving a slightly altered version of the signal. It crosses the high interior in vibrations too slow for hearing and too deliberate for geology.

At last, on the farthest terrace, a row of black columns pulses once.

Then again.

Then in the pattern they have received.

The answer travels outward.

It does not leave the plateau. Not yet. The inhabitants have not learned how to send anything into the distance except changes in themselves. But the pattern exists, and existence is enough.

The power receives it.

It could answer clearly now.

It could flood the sky with color. It could lower itself into the mineral network and give the plateau a language of symbols, a history, a name for every internal division. It could reveal the architecture of the universe and explain the purpose of the crystal minds.

It does none of these things.

Instead, it gives them something smaller and more difficult.

It gives them time.

Not endless time. Endless time is another form of confinement. It gives them a margin against catastrophe: a slight adjustment to the trajectories of distant stones, a quiet lessening of the radiation that would one day sterilize the plain, a preservation of the atmosphere’s delicate balance through ages in which suns alter and planets wander.

No one on the plateau notices.

That is how the power prefers its mercy.

The shelves remain sharp. The dust remains undisturbed. The green colonies continue to open and close. Nothing begins to decay merely because the power has arrived, and nothing becomes immortal because the power is kind.

The future remains capable of failure.

A crystal may still lose its memory. A colony may still settle in the wrong chemistry and vanish. A mind may still divide into two tendencies that no longer recognize one another.

But the plateau will not be destroyed before it has had the chance to become what it is becoming.

The power stays beyond the gradients.

It does not ask to be worshiped, because worship would require the inhabitants to make a shape where there is only an influence. It does not ask to be thanked, because gratitude imposed from outside would be another kind of machinery.

It simply leaves the unfamiliar rhythm in the sky.

The inhabitants return to their patient work.

Ore forms in exposed bands. Metallic films creep across stone and stop at invisible boundaries. New crystal geometries rise from the dust. The plants release their luminous spores, which hover briefly before settling.

But now, among all their cycles, there is one that does not originate in the plateau.

A pressure organ pulses.

A colony opens.

A crystal remembers.

And somewhere, in the immense interval between stars, the benevolent power listens to the first fragile answer of a world that has begun to know it is not alone.
