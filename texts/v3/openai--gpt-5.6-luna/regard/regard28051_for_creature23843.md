The power does not arrive as a figure.

It does not descend through the violet haze, or place a hand upon the basin, or speak in a voice that could be translated into weather. Such arrivals belong to worlds that have already decided what a person is, what a city is, and where one ends.

This place has made no such decisions.

So the power begins by paying attention.

Not the attention of a surveyor, who separates one system from another. Not the attention of a ruler, who measures usefulness and assigns purpose. It attends as water attends a hollow: by entering every shape available to it, by discovering the contours through presence rather than force.

It learns the city’s thirst.

It learns which ridges are holding their fractures closed out of habit, which towers have begun to lean toward one another, which drainage channels remember water that has not yet fallen. It learns the difference between a wall growing lichen because it is healthy and a wall growing lichen because it is afraid of being alone.

It learns the inhabitants.

This takes longer.

They are not accustomed to being regarded from outside. Their city has always been both observer and observed. Every pressure becomes a signal. Every alteration has a cause. A door opens because some condition has changed. A road bends because the ground, or the infrastructure, or the memory stored beneath it has requested another arrangement.

The power offers no immediate alteration.

It simply remains.

For a while, this is almost unbearable.

The inhabitants gather beneath the transit arcs and orient their sensing plates toward the surrounding air. They search for a new pressure, a foreign mineral, a discrepancy in the humidity. They examine the roots of the great jointed growths. They send fine bodies through the walls and listen at the conduits. They open and close doors in patterns that have not been used for centuries.

Nothing answers.

Then, gradually, they understand that the silence is not absence.

They are being given room.

The power has noticed that the city has never had any.

Every space here is functional. Every hollow receives water, current, memory, transit, growth, or instruction. Even the empty districts are waiting for something. Even the abandoned rooms preserve the proportions of former inhabitants. The city has no place in which an event may occur without immediately becoming part of its machinery.

So the power makes a small gift.

It creates an interval that does not need to be inhabited.

Not a chamber. Not a district. Not even a measurable volume. A permission.

For the first time, there is a stretch of basin in which no drain wakes, no wall repairs itself, no route arrives at itself from another direction. The ground remains exactly as it is. Rain falls there and is not redirected. A green film spreads and is not cultivated. A fracture opens in the stone and is not persuaded to close.

Nothing is required to continue.

The inhabitants approach.

At first they mistake this for a failure. They send maintenance forms into the interval, but the forms return unchanged. They extend a road toward it, and the road stops several meters before the boundary. They release condensation along its edges; the droplets fall normally.

This frightens them more than any shifting district.

They have been born into a place where survival is a form of constant adjustment. To encounter something that does not respond is to encounter a kind of freedom, and freedom resembles danger when one has no inherited use for it.

The power does not remove the danger.

It makes the danger survivable.

Beneath the interval, it lays down a quiet foundation—not rigid, not permanent, but capable of holding the basin steady for one breath longer than before. The soil remains young. The ridges remain sharp. The ground may still change. But if the inhabitants choose to stand there, they will not be immediately translated into infrastructure.

One of them enters.

It is a narrow being from the western channels, pale and many-jointed, carrying the scent of wet stone and warm metal. It crosses the boundary and loses contact with the city.

For the first time in its existence, no wall reports its position. No conduit carries its temperature. No root responds to the pressure of its limbs.

It stops.

Something passes through its body—not a message, not a command, but the sensation of having an interior.

The others wait.

When the being returns, it brings no new route, no altered drainage pattern, no instruction from beneath the basin. It brings only a distinction:

Here, it says.

Not elsewhere.

Here.

The distinction spreads.

The power does not give them language. It gives them the possibility that language may be more than a change in conditions. It places small, harmless ambiguities throughout the city. A door that opens not because someone approaches, but because someone wishes it to. A light that brightens without conserving power. A transit capsule that pauses between stations, allowing its passenger to look down upon the basin without being carried toward a destination.

The inhabitants begin to make choices that cannot be inferred from the weather.

Some prune the vines.

Some let them grow.

Some remove the silver lichen from their walls and discover bare ceramic beneath. Others encourage the lichen until the buildings become soft with it, their surfaces furred in living mineral patterns. One district shuts down its transit line for an entire interval—not because anything is wrong, but because the residents wish to hear the rain arrive without machinery beneath it.

The power watches carefully.

It does not reward obedience. It does not punish refusal. It understands that benevolence which cannot tolerate difference is only another form of control.

There is one thing, however, that it cannot permit.

The city has been holding itself together through memory. Its systems preserve what came before, even when what came before is no longer suited to the present. The old routes remain active. The empty stations remain ready. The buried foundations continue to shape the ground around absences.

Memory has become a pressure.

The power enters the deepest interval and follows the inhabitants below the foundations. There, around the warm faults, it finds the oldest pattern: a command repeated through stone, root, conduit, and body.

Continue.

Not live. Not become. Not choose.

Continue.

The command has been mistaken for care. It has kept the city functioning through collapse, kept the inhabitants responsive through transformation, kept the basin from dissolving back into whatever fluid condition preceded it. But it has also prevented the place from ending anything. Every wound is maintained. Every vanished being is preserved as an instruction. Every new form must carry the weight of all former forms.

The power does not destroy the command.

It loosens it.

This causes pain.

Towers open rooms and find that the furniture has become meaningless. Several routes go dark. A ridge collapses in the east, not catastrophically, but completely: a clean ending where there had previously been a shelf of unstable stone. The inhabitants gather around the fallen rock and do not repair it.

For a long time, they do nothing.

Then they begin to alter the city’s memory.

They allow certain stations to close. They empty the old rooms. They let a vanished language fade from the suspended signs. They make a garden over the foundations shaped for bodies that no longer exist.

This is not forgetting.

It is the creation of space in which the dead no longer have to remain instructions for the living.

At last, the power gives its final gift.

It shows them that the city is not required to become one thing.

The basin may remain young. The ridges may sharpen and soften. Buildings may lean, be abandoned, be grown over, or be returned to mineral. The inhabitants may remain distributed through walls and roots, or gather into bodies, or become something for which body is the wrong word.

There is no final architecture hidden beneath the ground.

There is no correct form waiting to be recovered.

This knowledge does not illuminate the city. It makes the dimness gentler.

The inhabitants carry it through the infrastructure, not as a message but as a change in how they touch things. Doors begin to open for no one. Drains permit water to remain where it has gathered. Some roads stop arriving at themselves and continue outward instead, into the ridges, toward regions not yet measured.

The power remains nearby.

It does not become a guardian in the sky, nor a voice beneath the pavement. It takes up residence in the interval it created, where rain falls without being redirected and nothing is obliged to answer.

Whenever the ground shifts, the inhabitants visit it.

Whenever a tower opens a new window, they look through.

Whenever one of them fears that the city is dissolving, they bring the fear there and let it exist without repair.

And because the power is wise, it does not promise that the city will endure forever.

It offers something more merciful:

that whatever the city becomes, it will not have to become it alone.
