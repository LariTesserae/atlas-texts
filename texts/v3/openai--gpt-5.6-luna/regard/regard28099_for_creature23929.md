The benevolent power did not enter through the aperture.

That would have been too much like arrival, and arrival is a form of occupation.

Instead, it placed one finger against the black stone surrounding the pocket mountain and listened.

For a long while, nothing changed. The mountain continued to climb. Water ran upward through the dark. A tower leaned against a wall that had not yet decided whether to hold it. Somewhere above, the hand tool struck rock three times.

The power heard the blows.

It heard the first as labor.

The second as obedience.

The third as fear.

So it did not answer with a fourth.

It answered with silence.

The effect was immediate and enormous.

Water stopped in its channels—not falling, not rising, simply suspended in the shape of its movement. Vines loosened. Copper plates ceased their dull conversation. Mortar held its brightness without hardening. Every Maker, whether body, shadow, footprint, or buried memory, became aware of the same impossible condition:

Nothing was being asked of them.

The mountain had not commanded a pause. The summit-dwellers had not sent an instruction. No wall was waiting to be completed. No crack required interpretation. No stone was secretly longing to become useful.

For the first time, the pocket mountain contained something that had not been built.

An empty place.

It was very small at first: a space between two unfinished figures at the lowest basin. The figures had always stood shoulder to shoulder, each waiting for a direction. Now the space remained between them, dark and warm, no wider than a thread.

The power widened it.

Not by removing anything. It did not take stone, root, clay, water, or inhabitant away. It merely allowed the space to remain a space.

The Makers recoiled.

Some tried to fill it with gravel. Some sent roots across it. One of the clay figures began to shape itself into a door. The salt figures leaned inward to restore the boundary. The copper figures listened for the mechanism concealed inside the interruption.

There was none.

The empty place did not lead anywhere.

It did not hide a room, a stair, a message, or a purpose.

It was simply unoccupied.

The power held the space open.

This frightened the Makers more deeply than any summit instruction. An empty place could not be used. It could not be inhabited, crossed, reinforced, harvested, named, or made permanent. It offered no material from which a body might be formed.

And yet the longer it remained, the less unbearable it became.

The first to approach was a Maker called Wet Shoulder. It had once been a person-shaped arrangement of clay, but most of its left side had become a retaining wall. It placed one copper toe near the empty place and waited.

Nothing happened.

It did not become a hinge.

It did not acquire a corridor.

It did not disappear into the masonry.

It was still itself, whatever that meant.

Wet Shoulder placed the rest of its body beside the gap.

Then Small Root came. Then Three-Blows. Then the charcoal ones who could travel through shadows, and the slate-bodied ones who had forgotten where their edges ended. They gathered around the emptiness without filling it.

The dormant figures watched.

They had been waiting for a direction. Now they felt something else: permission not to have one.

The power did not speak into their minds. Speech would have become a corridor, and a corridor would have chosen a destination for them. Instead, it gave them a sensation—not an answer, but the absence of pressure.

You may remain unfinished.

The thought passed through them like cool water.

Some rejected it. A few Makers fled upward, desperate to resume the work. They rebuilt a broken stair, then rebuilt the stair beneath it, then added a tower to support the stair. Their labor became frantic. They feared that if they stopped constructing, they would discover there was no self beneath the construction.

The power did not prevent them.

Benevolence was not a wall placed around every harmful choice. It was the refusal to make obedience the price of safety.

But for those who stayed, the mountain began to change.

Not outwardly. The walls did not soften. The heat did not lessen. The strange laws remained strange. Rain still rose from pools. Doors still forgot who they were made for. A name still risked becoming a passage.

What changed was the meaning of completion.

Until then, every finished surface had been a boundary against uncertainty. A wall said: this is where something is. A stair said: this is how it may rise. A basin said: this is where the water belongs.

Now a few surfaces were allowed to end without becoming anything else.

A stair terminated in a ledge.

A channel stopped before reaching its reservoir.

A wall contained a window that looked only into the same wall.

These were not failures. They were the first forms the mountain had made without being compelled to continue.

Far above, the summit shuddered.

Three blows rang through the stone.

The lower Makers froze.

The instruction traveled downward, as it always had. It entered the copper, the roots, the water, the mortar. It reached the basin and touched the dormant figures.

Turn.

The figures began to turn.

Then the benevolent power did one more thing.

It gave them weight.

Not much. Only enough that turning was no longer automatic.

The salt figures remained facing the summit.

The clay figures turned toward the empty place.

The charcoal figures turned toward one another.

The copper figures turned downward, listening to the basin.

The leaf figures turned everywhere at once.

For the first time, the dormant inhabitants did not receive a single direction. They received several. None was declared correct.

The mountain trembled with the strain of this multiplicity.

Above, stairways rearranged themselves. Towers leaned away from their foundations. Water forgot which way was upward. The summit appeared in every position at once: shrine, tooth, root-wrapped stone, unfinished brick, and a darkness immediately behind the eye.

The summit-dwellers sent three more blows.

This time the Makers answered.

Not with three blows.

With many.

A clay hand struck a basin. A root tightened against a bridge. A copper plate rang. A slate body shifted against the paving. The sounds were uneven and frightened, but they belonged to different beings.

The power listened to all of them.

It did not choose a leader.

It did not crown the one who spoke most clearly.

It did not turn the mountain into a democracy, or a garden, or a place of comfort. Such transformations would have been another kind of imprisonment: a single benevolent design imposed over many unfinished lives.

Instead, it made disagreement survivable.

When one Maker wished to raise a tower, another could brace the ground without being absorbed into it. When one wished to climb, another could remain below without being called incomplete. When a wall changed its mind, those sheltering beside it would have one breath—one true breath—in which to move.

The power gave them time.

Only that.

But time, in the pocket mountain, was a radical material.

It could not be hammered into shape. It could not be mortared between stones. It could not be made useful on command.

Time allowed a Maker to notice that it was afraid before becoming a wall.

It allowed a plant to grow in the direction of warmth without dragging its container behind it.

It allowed a name to be spoken and remain a name.

It allowed the figures around the basin to wait without assuming that waiting meant obedience.

At last, the power touched the lowest overflow.

The water rose in its column, as it always did, and reached the invisible ceiling. But instead of vanishing there, it spread across the darkness in a thin, shining sheet.

The Makers saw through it.

Not the outside world. Not salvation. Not a larger mountain waiting to replace their own.

They saw the hand that had touched the stone.

They saw no face, because the power did not need one. They saw no throne, weapon, crown, or instrument. They saw only attention—vast, patient attention, holding the pocket mountain without squeezing it.

And they understood the most merciful thing the power could offer:

They were not required to become understandable in order to be cared for.

The sheet of water fell.

The mountain resumed its motion.

Three blows sounded from the summit, but now they were only sounds among others. The rain began again, falling upward through the dark. A new stair formed beside the basin, though it did not lead toward the highest place. It descended into a chamber that had not existed before.

The chamber was empty.

The Makers called it, for a time, **Rest**.

Then one by one they gave it other names.

Some used it for sleep. Some for argument. Some for growing faces. Some entered and remained exactly as they were. A few went inside and discovered that they had been carrying an old grief shaped like a tool.

The benevolent power did not stay.

To stay would have made dependence another architecture.

It withdrew its finger from the stone, leaving behind no mark except a slight warmth at the aperture’s edge. The pocket mountain was still too large for its chamber. The inhabitants were still unfinished thoughts. The summit was still changing position. The world was still dangerous, crowded, wet, and being made.

But now, in the lowest level, there was an empty place no one had to fill.

And whenever the mountain gave its three blows, the Makers listened.

Some obeyed.

Some refused.

Some answered with a fourth blow.

And some, having learned that silence could also be an answer, simply continued in the direction they had chosen.
