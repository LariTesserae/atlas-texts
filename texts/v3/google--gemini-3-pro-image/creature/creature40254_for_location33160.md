In a landscape so dominated by stillness and geological grandeur, life does not scurry or flutter. It does not hide in burrows, for there is no soil to dig, only razor-edged volcanic glass. It does not seek water, for the concept is alien here.

The inhabitants of this high, electrified plateau are mortal echoes of the landscape itself. To the hasty human eye, they are invisible, easily dismissed as unusual outcroppings of the raw basalt or particularly complex nodes of crystalline growth.

They are the Lithovores, the geometry-dwellers.

They are sentient geodes, massive, slow-growing polyhedral entities born of the same high-energy electrolysis that shaped the forests. They have no soft tissues, no blood to pump, no moisture to conserve. Their bodies are amalgams of conductive metals and insulative silicates, grown layer by atomic layer over millennia.

Imagine a tortoise, but magnified to the size of a boulder and faceted like a rough-cut diamond. Their outer carapaces are thick, interlocking plates of dull iron and hexagonal basalt pillars, mirroring the fractured ground they rest upon. Where the "sunlight" catches them, veins of native copper and silver flash within the dark rock, looking like frozen lightning trapped in stone.

They are absolutely sessile for spans of time longer than human civilizations exist. They do not hunt; they graze on the energy of the place.

You might find one anchored near the base of a towering hematite "tree." It will look like a tumour of rock fused to the metallic trunk. Having found a strong current, the creature has extruded conductive probes—roots of pure copper—deep into the Gnarled structure, tapping directly into the planetary hum. They feed on the geothermal voltage, letting the microscopic vibrations that thrum through the landscape resonate within their own crystalline cores. The warmth of the ground is their metabolism; the electrical current is their sustenance.

Because the environment is locked in apparent stasis, so too are its people. Their movements are tectonic. A shift in posture, a turning of a faceted "head" to better align with a subterranean energy flow, might take a century to complete. They are creatures living at the speed of geology. To watch them is to watch a glacier flow; the motion is undeniable only if you possess godlike patience.

They have no eyes, for there is nothing new to see in a frozen world. Instead, their entire armored surface is a dense sensor grid sensitive to electromagnetic fields and thermal gradients. They "see" the world as a complex tapestry of heat plumes rising from vents and the shimmering magnetic auras of the metallic groves against the cold vacuum of the upper sky.

They are silent, lacking any apparatus for vocalization in an air too thin to carry much sound anyway. Yet, they are not disconnected. If you were to press your hand against the warm, vibrating flank of one of these beings, you might feel a secondary rhythm beneath the primary hum of the planet—a slow, incredibly deep modulation. It is their thought, moving through their metallic neural pathways like molasses. They communicate through the conductive ground, sharing vast, sluggish concepts across the miles of barren rock in a planetary communion of slow consciousness.

They are the watchers on the plateau, ancient and durable as the mountains, living batteries whose only purpose is to exist, slowly growing more complex as the hot windless air deposits atom after atom onto their shining, geometric forms under the unwavering violet sky.
