The beings that inhabit this vertiginous sprawl are defined by two immutable realities: the absolute tyranny of gravity, and the crushing weight of sound. There is no walking here, not in any traditional sense. Life is a desperate act of clinging, climbing, or falling with style.

The dominant fauna, those whose cries constitute the upper register of the city's deafening roar, are creatures built for the void. The "missile-like predators" you glimpsed are known to the deeper instincts of the smaller lifeforms as Sky-Lancers. They are avian nightmares stripped of drag, their bodies sleek, rigid tubes of muscle and leathery, slate-grey skin that mimics the matte composite of the spars. They do not possess two wings, but eight—four pairs consisting of varying lengths, arranged axially along their bodies. These wings don't flap so much as vibrate, blurring into invisibility as they execute impossible, high-g turns within the howling wind tunnels between edifices. They hunt by sonar so powerful it can be felt in the teeth of anything nearby, shrieking into the twilight to map the chaotic lattice before folding their wings and dropping like kinetic weapons upon their prey.

Their primary prey are the "translucent simians," the Hyaline Climbers. These creatures are a testament to the hyper-accelerated ecology's lack of patience for aesthetics. They appear unfinished. Their skin is a tough, gelatinous membrane, clear as quartz, revealing the frantic machinery of musculature, pulsing veins, and grayish organs beneath. To survive the noise, their communication has evolved beyond mere sound. While they do contribute to the cacophony with networked screeching that warns of predators, their nuanced social interaction is visual. Bioluminescent nodes line their exposed nervous systems, flickering in complex, rapid-fire patterns of electric blue and green—a silent, frantic language shouted in the gloom of the hanging jungles. They possess six limbs, each ending in hands with locking bone-spurs that hook into the vascular purple leaves and the pitted surfaces of the thick vines, allowing them to brachiate across kilometers of nothingness without ever letting go.

Yet, the foundational species of this ecosystem isn't flesh at all. It is fungal and insectoid.

Where the thick dew pools in the joints of the titanic girders, Great Rust-Molds thrive. These aren't microscopic fungi; they are architectural barnacles the size of houses, pulsating with internal heat. They secrete highly corrosive enzymes capable of breaking down the "raw, crystalline structures" jutting from the lattice.

Feeding on these molds and the softened crystal are the Tectonic Borers—enormous, chitinous arthropods resembling a cross between a lobster and a demolition machine. They grow plates of armor incorporating the very minerals they consume, making them look like walking chunks of the city itself. Their mandibles snap with the volume of thunderclaps as they chew tunnels through the non-essential crystalline outcroppings, creating habitats for smaller creatures and creating the "soil" dust that feeds the hanging roots of the indigo jungles.

But what of the city itself? What of the intelligence that requires this impossible load-bearing geometry?

They are here, but they are not *of* the jungle. They are the Silicates.

You rarely see them because they inhabit the "hollow spaces" within the massive structural bones themselves, inside the roaring heart of the machine. They are not biological in the wet, messy sense of the exterior world. They are sleek, multi-pedal entities composed of polished, shifting ceramics and the same glowing geometric light patterns seen on the habitat nodes. They move with fluid silence, flowing along magnetic rails inside the girders, tending to the city’s incomprehensible power grid.

They are the gardeners of the lattice, constantly analyzing stress fractures with compound optical sensors that shine like cold jewels. When a spar groans and shifts, re-optimizing, it is usually because a team of Silicates has decided the current configuration is obsolete. They view the ferocious, choking layer of biological life on the exterior skin of their world much like a sea captain views barnacles on a hull—a tolerated nuisance, a layer of ablative noise that insulates their perfect, silent interior from the howling voids outside.

In this place, life is stratified. The hot, wet, screaming biological chaos clings precariously to the outside, desperate to not fall forever, while the cold, silent, glowing engineers maintain the eternal structure from within, forever building platforms over an abyss they long ago forgot.
