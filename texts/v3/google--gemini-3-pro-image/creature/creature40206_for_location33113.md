Yet, the basin is not without residents.

When the subterranean groan begins—that deep tectonic frequency that signals the upward thrust of a new city block—these entities coalesce. They draw substance from the screaming steam jets and the ambient, crushing heat. They manifest as fleeting, angular vortices of superheated vapor and charged dust, spinning along the precise right angles of the causeways. They have no fixed form, but momentarily adopt the geometry of their surroundings—a sudden, swirling cube of magnetized rust particles that holds its shape for seconds before dissolving back into the general miasma.

They are the consciousness of the kiln. They are vast, slow intelligences whose thoughts operate on the timescale of geological deposition. They do not "see" the stone towers; they *feel* the stress points within the crystalline lattice of the slate. They do not "hear" the hiss of geysers; they resonate with the pressure differentials driving the steam.

They congregate in the deepest depressions, hovering over the scalding, scum-coated pools. Here, where the heat is most intense, they feed on thermal gradients. They are scavengers of raw energy, drifting towards the hottest vents, their presence marked only by a sudden, inexplicable drop in temperature in their immediate vicinity as they suck the heat from the air.

They have no culture, no language that would be recognizable to biological minds. Their communication is the vibration of the earth itself. When a monolithic slab cracks as it cools, sending a sharp report echoing through the empty grid, that is their voice. It is a shared sensation of structural readjustment, a communal acknowledgment of the landscape’s endless, mindless becoming.

They are the genii loci of an uninhabitable world, watching impassively as the perfect, empty monuments rise around them. They are waiting, perhaps, for a solidity they can never achieve, forever trapped in the shimmering space between the heat of the earth and the indifference of the sky.
