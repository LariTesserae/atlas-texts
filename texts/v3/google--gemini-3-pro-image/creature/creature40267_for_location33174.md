The first thing you understand, standing upon the vibrating lattice of the floor, is that nothing *lives* here in the way you understand living. This place is too hostile, too loud, too brutally focused on its own singular, catastrophic expansion to tolerate guests. The lonely, translucent membrane-drifters you glimpse high above are not inhabitants; they are flotsam, accidental specks caught in the draft of a biological furnace.

The true inhabitant of this hollow universe is the universe itself. But it has agents.

As the tectonic grinding sound reaches a deafening crescendo, you see the source of the movement. It is not a creature separate from the architecture; it is the architecture suddenly deciding to become mobile.

A hundred meters away, a section of the colossal leaf-wall begins to hemorrhage. The surface blisters, the veins thicker than subway tunnels pulsing violently beneath the golden-green epidermis. With a sound like a cracking glacier—the "booming snap" you heard earlier—the wall erupts.

From the wound emerges a torrent of rapidly unifying matter. It is a roiling knot of raw biology, a transient entity birthed for a singular purpose. It has no fixed shape, no eyes, no mouth. It is a thundering tumbleweed of slick, pale cambium and dark, muscle-like wood fibers that twitch and contract with Impossible speed.

It is huge, perhaps the size of a small apartment building, yet it moves with the frantic, jittery spasms of a time-lapsed insect. It doesn't walk; it flows by breaking and remaking itself. Thick, hydraulic tendrils burst from its center, slamming into the root-floor you stand upon. Where they strike, they instantly fuse with the floor, weaving themselves into the existing lattice faster than the eye can follow. The entity pulls itself forward on these anchor points, tearing free from the wall it emerged from with a sickening shriek of ripped cellulose.

As it draws closer, the heat radiating from it is intense, a fever-heat of hyper-metabolism. You can smell it: the overwhelming, coppery stench of ozone and the raw, sticky sap that coats its every surface. It drips the thick, melon-sized beads of nectar, leaving sizzling, slick trails on the root-floor.

It is bristling with weapons, though they are not meant for combat. Those obsidian-sharp thorns, some as long as spears, jut wildly from its churning mass. They are tools for gouging new channels in the superstructure, for piercing and knitting the colossal leaves together.

It pays you no mind. You are too small, too inert to register against the roaring background noise of the growing world. It is a mindless antibody, a mobile construction engine made of the very building it serves.

It reaches a gap in the floor—a chasm dropping kilometers into the humid haze. The entity throws itself over the edge. It does not fall. Instead, it explodes outwards in mid-air, its central mass unraveling into thousands of screaming, whipping cables. These cables strike both sides of the chasm, instantly biting into the flesh of the world. Tension runs through them, humming in your teeth. In seconds, the entity has become a bridge. Then, it becomes a floor. The fibers thicken, bark forms over the raw wetness, and the gap is gone.

The entity has ceased to exist as a discrete unit. It has reabsorbed itself into the totality. But nearby, the wall begins to throb again, and the grinding noise deepens as another part of the endless organism prepares to tear itself free.
