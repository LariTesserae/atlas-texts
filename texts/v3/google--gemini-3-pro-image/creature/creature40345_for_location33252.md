This is a world that does not tolerate soft things; consequently, the people who cling to its shifting lattice are beings of cured leather, hardened resin, and bone.

They call themselves the Arbor-Kin—or perhaps that is simply the vibration pattern their feet drum onto the wood when they identify themselves, for spoken language is a difficult thing amidst the deafening roar of the Root-World.

Physically, they are lean, constructed of dense muscle and heavier bone than surface dwellers, built to withstand gravity that pulls from strange angles as the pathways twist. Their skin is not supple; it is a desiccated husk, the color of dried tobacco leaves, cured cinnamon, and deepest mahogany. It is thick, wrinkled, and scarred, coated in a perpetual layer of fine, irritating dust that settles into every crease. Over time, their shoulders, elbows, and knees develop thick, bark-like calluses that look suspiciously like knots in wood, natural armor against the constant abrasion of their surroundings.

They do not bleed water. If cut by a flying splinter of super-growth, they weep a thick, slow-moving amber fluid that smells faintly of turpentine and seals the wound almost instantly, hardening into a crystalline scab. Their entire biology is attuned to the slow, heavy chemistry of the silicon saps. They carry canteens fashioned from hollowed-out insect chitin, filled not with water, but with the viscous, high-energy nectar tapped from the deeper, older root-veins—a sludge that would poison a creature built on H2O, but which fuels their frantic metabolisms.

Their faces are angular, shaped by the necessity of filtration and protection. Their noses are flattened, covered by thick bristles that act as dust masks. Their eyes are deep-set, shielded by prominent brow ridges and covered by nictitating membranes that slide across horizontally, wiping away grit like windshield wipers. Many wear goggles carved from polished, transparent amber, strapped tight to their skulls with braided root-fiber.

They are functionally deaf in the traditional sense. The geological volume of the growing world has rendered external ears useless liabilities. Instead, they perceive their world through highly sensitized pads on their palms and the soles of their splayed, clawed feet. They "hear" the groan of a timber about to snap through their shinbones; they sense the approach of a giant boring insect through the vibrations in the floor. Their communication is a mixture of sharp, guttural shouts that can briefly cut through the din, and a complex sign language composed of rapid, jerky hand movements and rhythmic stomping.

Their clothing and tools are harvested directly from the ecosystem's violence. They wear segmented armor crafted from the iridescent, dried husks of the dog-sized borers, tied together with sinewy plant fibers. They carry heavy adzes and hacking tools made of sharpened chitin and fire-hardened resin, implements designed not for war, but for the endless, desperate battle against the environment itself.

They are a people in constant motion, suffering from a collective, hyper-vigilant anxiety. They cannot sit still, for stillness means being overtaken by the pinkish, swift-growing new cellulose. You see them scrambling over the precarious rope bridges with the practiced ease of arboreal monkeys, their prehensile toes gripping the braided fibers. You see them clustered around their burl-dwellings, frantically hacking away at encroaching tendrils that threaten to seal their doors.

They are dust-caked laborers in a factory that never stops, smelling of ozone and spicy resin, their lives a frantic, vibrating dance on the back of a beast that is continuously growing larger. They are as dry, hard, and relentless as the Root-World itself.
