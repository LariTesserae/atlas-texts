The denizens of Foundry-Deep do not live *in* this environment so much as they have fused with it. They are the soft, sweating organic components integrated into a hard, hot machine.

The first thing you notice about a Foundry-dweller is their personal atmosphere. Because sweat cannot readily evaporate into the supersaturated air, they move within a perpetual, shimmering nimbus of their own body steam. Seen from a distance across a shadowed catwalk, they appear as blurred, heat-hazed ghosts trudging through the gloom. Up close, the reality is more visceral. Their skin is universally slick, coated in a persistent sheen of moisture that is half-perspiration, half-condensed atmospheric grease. They smell intensely of the place—a fermented miasma of human salt, ancient machine oil, and the metallic tang of ozone.

Decades, perhaps generations, in the absence of sunlight have leeched the pigment from them. They are uniformly pale, a doughy, subterranean white that looks sickly under the jaundice-yellow sodium lamps. Yet, this pallor is rarely visible in its entirety. They are stained people. Smudges of graphite grease, streaks of rust runoff, and patches of vibrant orange or oily black chemo-bacterial slime mark their bodies like tribal tattoos of industry. Their hands are permanent maps of their labor: thick-fingered, calloused, the nail beds stained indelible black, the skin scarred from burns and the countless small nicks of jagged metal.

Clothing is minimal and functional, worn ragged by the humidity and constant friction. They wear heavy leather aprons cured to the stiffness of wood, thick rubber boots to navigate the sump-flooded floors, and modified welding gear. Protective goggles are pushed up onto foreheads when not immediately needed, revealing eyes that are permanently squinted, the surrounding skin etched with crow's feet from peering through steam and bracing against sudden arc-flashes.

The most striking adaptation, however, is their relationship with the vermin. The horror that a topsider feels at the seething insect life has long since been burned out of the Deep-born. They exist in a grotesque symbiosis with the swarm. You will see a machinist intently watching a pressure gauge, completely ignoring the thumb-sized pale cockroach investigating the sweat pooling at their collarbone. They do not brush things off. To twitch at every crawling sensation in Foundry-Deep would be to endure a perpetual seizure.

They walk with a distinct, heavy gait. It is a slow, deliberate trudge, born of the need to conserve energy in the debilitating heat and the constant necessity of crushing the carpet of invertebrates underfoot. They don't even hear the crunch anymore; it is merely the texture of their ground. The vibration of the catwalks and the thrum of the geothermal fans are felt in their bones rather than heard; they are the rhythm they breathe by.

Communication is a physical act. The cacophony renders normal speech useless. They converse in a sophisticated language of hand signals, sharp whistles that cut through the low-frequency roar, and when proximity allows, reverberating shouts directly into an ear, faces inches apart, steam mingling with steam.

They are a stoic, insular people. Their patience is geological. In a world where a shift change is marked by the slow cycling of massive geothermal pistons, there is no urgency, only endurance. They tend the machines with a reverence that borders on religious, knowing that if the great ventilation fans seize or the sump pumps fail, their suffocating, drowning world will reclaim them in hours. They are the caretakers of the deep fire, resigned to their rattling cage, finding a grim comfort in the heat and noise that would drive an outsider mad.
