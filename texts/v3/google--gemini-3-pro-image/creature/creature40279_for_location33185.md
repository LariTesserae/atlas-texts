The dominant form of life here is not merely adapted to the environment; it is literally shaped by it, crafted from the same materials and subjected to the same brutal rhythms. They are the Lithic Watchers, though they do not watch with eyes.

At first glance, they are invisible. In the oppressive, misty twilight, a hunched form clinging to the side of a rising stone piston looks like nothing more than a protrusion of basalt and a patch of aggressive moss. It is only when the piston reaches its zenith and shudders to a momentary halt before its sickening plunge that the protrusion detaches itself and skitters sideways.

They are squat, heavy creatures, vaguely anthropoid but distorted by gravity and the necessity of extreme grip. Their hide is not flesh, but a thick, leathery membrane that feels like wet slate, perpetually slick with the condensation that coats everything. Over their backs and shoulders, they grow irregularly shaped plates of natural armor. These carapaces are cemented together with mineral-rich saliva, composed of shed obsidian flakes from the western gears and shards of basalt cracked from the monoliths. They are living mosaics of the canyon’s geology, covered in a layer of the ubiquitous, emerald-copper moss that makes them look like ancient, waterlogged statues brought to stumbling life.

They have no eyes—organs of sight are liabilities in a world of stinging mist and perpetual gray—nor do they have ears that would be recognizable. Instead, their entire bodies are tuned seismic receivers. Their broad, flat hands and feet possess thick pads highly sensitive to vibration. They perceive their world through the soles of their feet and the palms of their hands, reading the deep bass *thrum* of the pistons and the high-frequency shrieking of the quartz gears as a complex topographical map of sound. They know precisely when tectonic plates will grind together based on the subtle shift in resonance seconds before impact.

Their movements are a grotesque ballet synchronized to the industrial violence. They do not scuttle like the rusted arthropods; they move with deliberate, heavy intent. A Watcher will cling to the vertical face of a house-sized piston, riding it upward through the drenching steam plumes. As the rock reaches the apex of its churn, the creature releases its grip, drops several feet through the viscous air onto a stationary ledge, and immediately flattens itself as a massive basalt monolith slides past inches from its face, shearing the air where it had just been. They exist in the negative spaces of the grinding machine.

They are sustained by the very forces that make the amphitheater uninhabitable for anything else. They cluster around the geothermal vents, pressing their slate-like bellies against the scalding rock to absorb heat, their mossy backs steaming in the frigid air. They graze on the rapidly regenerating metallic ferns, their jaws capable of crushing the rigid, cellulose-iron fronds with a sound like snapping cables that is lost in the greater roar.

Their social interaction is as brutal and detached as the landscape. They communicate not with vocals, but by percussion. A Watcher will find a resonant flake of obsidian jutting from a wall and strike it with a hardened, hammer-like fist. The resulting sharp, crystalline *clack* travels through the bedrock, felt by others of their kind hundreds of yards away. It is a seismic language of warnings and territorial claims, a Morse code hammered out on the bones of the earth.

Like the canyon itself, they seem locked in stasis. There are no young among them, and no elders dying. They are simply *there*, weathering the endless friction, their obsidian armor slowly chipping away and being painstakingly replaced, riding the stone pistons up and down in the gray light, forever part of the churning, purposeless engine.
