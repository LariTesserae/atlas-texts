The beings that inhabit this screaming crystal sphere are not grown; they are accreted. They are thinking geology, sentient mineral formations birthed from the same agonizing slowness that formed the geode itself.

To survive the marrow-shaking sonic assault, their bodies are incredibly dense. They are squat, multiform entities, rarely exceeding four feet in height, possessing a low center of gravity that anchors them against the shuddering ground. They have no flesh that would bruise or tear, no fluids to boil away in the searing heat. They are constructed of layered silicate plates, nodules of polished hematite, and joints hinged with cold-hammered native copper that has annealed in the cavern's furnace over millennia.

They are faceless in any recognizable sense. There are no eyes, for eyes would be useless blinding points of pain in the chaotic light. There are no mouths, for they do not eat; they absorb thermal energy directly from the rock and trace minerals from the dust they perpetually inhale into porous internal cavities. Most importantly, there are no ears. The concept of "hearing" is absent; instead, their entire being is a receptive organ for vibration. They "see" the world through their feet and thick, three-fingered hands, reading the subtle differences in the foundational roar to map their surroundings. They can distinguish the high-frequency chime of an obsidian leaf brushing a copper vine from the deep, bass thrum of a distant magma vent opening.

Their movements are agonizingly slow and deliberate. In a world vibrating with ferocious energy, quick motion is inefficient and potentially destabilizing. To watch one move is like watching a stone glacier shift. They lift a heavy, multifaceted leg, pause to verify the vibrational stability of the quartz beam ahead, and then shift their weight with the grinding sound of stone on stone.

Because the air is a meaningless riot of noise, they have no spoken language. Communication is entirely conductive. They converse by pressing their copper-palmed hands against the crystal walls or floors—or directly against another’s carapace—and tapping out precise, resonant frequencies. It is a language of complex percussion transmitted through solid matter, a lithic Morse code that cuts beneath the atmospheric roar. A disagreement is a dissonant flurry of rapid strikes; affection is a slow, rhythmic thrumming shared between two individuals braced against a bridge railing.

They are master artisans, their entire existence defined by the "infinite patience" visible in their city. With tools of diamond and corundum clamped in vice-like metallic grip, they do not build so much as they surgically erode their environment into habitability. A single dwelling hollowed out of an amethyst might represent five generations of continuous, microscopic chipping. They work in shifts dictated not by day or night, but by the rhythmic pulses of the geothermal vents, retreating into deeper, thicker-walled chambers when the "screaming energy" reaches its crescendo.

Over time, the abrasive sound waves that scour the city polish the inhabitants as well. The elders are terrifyingly beautiful beings, their silicate armor smoothed to a mirror sheen that reflects the violent violet and gold light, their copper joints gleaming like ancient treasure. They are stoic, tireless, and intensely focused, living lives of deep geological time, their thoughts moving as slowly, and as inevitably, as continental drift. They are the silent, solid center of a howling, glittering world.
