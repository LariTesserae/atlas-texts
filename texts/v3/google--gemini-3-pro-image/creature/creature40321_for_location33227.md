This is a world where biology has risen to meet the challenge of geology, a place where life is fundamentally mineral, desperately dry, and overwhelmingly loud. The inhabitants of the Deep Dust Galleries are not merely living *in* this environment; they are made *of* it.

The primary citizens of this grown metropolis are the **Lithovores**. They are the architects of the deafening scrape, the countless millions clinging to the towering fungal structures. They are arthropoid but built on a scale and substance unlike anything on the surface. Their exoskeletons are not mere chitin, but biomineralized composites, essentially living stone fused with metallic trace elements harvested from the rock. They come in a staggering array of sizes, from finger-long scavengers cleaning the deep crevasses of the fungal lattices to house-sized behemoths that anchor themselves to the fluted columns, living out millennia in a single spot. They are blind, having abandoned sight eons ago. Instead, their entire bodies act as complex resonators. Their segmented antennae are feathered with crystalline sensors that pick up the minutest vibration in the rock, while broad, dish-like depressions on their thoraxes act as directional microphones, mapping the chaotic soundscape in high fidelity. Their movement is a slow, deliberate scraping of stone on stone; their feeding is the high-pitched shriek of diamond-hard mandibles rasping away at the calcified fungal architecture. They are the background radiation of the noise, a constant, billion-fold friction.

Occupying the vast, dark voids between the towers are the **Aether-Kites**. These are the sources of the piercing ultrasonic shrieks. They are not birds or bats, but colossal, flattened creatures of desiccated membrane and light, hollow bone stretched taut over spans that can exceed a hundred feet. They possess no legs, for they never land. They ride the subtle thermal currents generated by the deep earth below. Their heads are dominated not by eyes, but by a massive, fleshy concave dish, a biological radar receiver that takes up their entire frontal profile. In the center of this dish is a larynx built like a high-pressure steam valve. They navigate by screaming. They fire pulses of sound so intense they would rupture surface-dwelling eardrums instantly—the "pops and whistles" that downshift into audible range are merely the residual bleed-off of their true sensory output. They see the city in flashes of agonizing echoes, a strobe-lit world of hard surfaces defined by the time it takes for their own voices to return to them.

Down on the floor of the cavern, wading through the endless plains of fine dust, are the **Geophages**. These are the creators of the industrial grinding sound. They are monumental, armored slugs, massive multi-segmented tubes of articulated rock that can grow half a mile long. They move with glacial slowness on hundreds of broad, churning pedipalps that swim through the dust rather than walking on top of it. Their role in the ecosystem is geological processing. Their mouths are gaping maws rimmed with rotating rings of crystalline teeth. They consume everything in their path—dust, fallen fungal debris, smaller creatures too slow to move—feeding it into an internal furnace where extreme pressure and specialized bacteria break down the minerals. The waste heat from this process radiates from dorsal vents, creating the thermals that support the Aether-Kites high above. The sound of a Geophage feeding is a low-frequency, vibrating crunch that shakes the very dust you stand upon, a relentless bass note beneath the treble shrieks and the mid-range scraping.

Finally, there are the things that hunt in the din. In a world defined by noise, the apex predator is an agent of silence. The **Hush-Stalkers** are sleek, six-legged horrors built of matte-black, sound-dampening chitin. Their feet are padded with thick layers of sponge-like silica that absorb vibration, allowing them to move across the resonant stone without making a sound. They hunt not by emitting sonar, but by passive listening of terrifying precision. They can isolate the specific click of a single injured Lithovore amidst the cacophony of millions. They move through the shadows like voids in the soundscape, areas of sudden, unnerving quiet. When they strike, it is with explosive speed, their crystalline scythe-arms shearing through mineral armor before the prey can even register a change in the acoustic environment. Their presence is usually only detected by the sudden, localized absence of noise just before the end.
