The inhabitants of the Rust Hollow are less a collection of biological entities and more a localized, animate phase of geology. They are the living friction that has smoothed this industrial cavern into an organic cavity. They are the **Ferro-Carapaceae**, a sprawling, taxonomically confused phylum of litho-metallovorous arthropods that have evolved in absolute dryness to become the gears of a sealed, cannibalistic machine.

They do not possess skin or flesh in any recognizable sense. They are essentially ambulatory geodes, armored in the very substances they consume. There is no blood within them; crack one open, and you will not find fluid, but a pressurized puff of metallic dust and a viscous, grinding slurry of silicate grit and oxidized paste—the "mineral slurry" they seek in one another. Their joints do not rely on lubrication, but on the high-tolerance polish of metal-on-metal bearings, moving with the jerky precision of escapement mechanisms.

To observe them collectively under the furious violet glare of the arc lamps is to look at a tidal flow of living scrap.

At the base of this hyper-competitive pyramid are the **Grit-Mites**. These are the grain-of-sand sized creatures that compose the shifting dunes on the floor and float as an abrasive haze in the air. They are faceted crystalline mites, infinitesimally small horrors of silicon carbide and diamond dust. They do not walk so much as vibrate, flowing like liquid emery powder over every surface, performing the finest level of polishing on the chamber walls. They are the first stage of erosion, finding microscopic purchase in the iron and widening it for the larger things.

Above them, making up the vast majority of the shivering tapestries on the walls and the churning convection current of the floor, is the **Scour**.

Growing from thumbnail to fist-size, the Scour are the primary workers of the Hollow. They resemble flattened, multi-segmented isopods or trilobites armored in scavenged plating. Their backs are mosaics of materials: a segment of green-patinaed copper riveted biologically to a chunk of dull lead, followed by a band of crystallized hematite. They are a riot of industrial camouflage. Their millions of legs end not in soft pads, but in micro-chisels and stiff wire bristles.

The Scour do not eat passively. They graze on the architecture with diamond-hard rasping mouthparts—radulae that function like rotary grinders. The deafening "wire brush" roar of the chamber is the collective sound of billions of these creatures literally scrubbing the walls down to the atomic level, ingesting the metal dust, and excreting it as refined, oxidized pellets that form the dune sea.

They move in a frantic, clinging carpet, climbing over each other's backs in a ceaseless wave to reach untouched sections of the remaining "scattered built" elements. When a hanging stalactite of them gets too heavy, the bottom layer shears off, raining a knot of clicking bronze and iron bodies down onto the floor below, where they instantly reintegrate into the churning mass.

Prowling through this metallic soup are the apex predators, the **Breakers**.

These are dinner-plate sized, sometimes larger. If the Scour are clockwork lice, the Breakers are industrial salvage machinery brought to terrible life. They are heavy, squat, and powerfully armored in thick, sintered plates of cast iron that give them a matte, light-absorbing finish. They move with a heavy, clanking gait on six reinforced legs.

Their primary features are their oversized, asymmetrical claws. These are not simple pincers; they look disturbingly like hydraulic shears and crushing pistons adapted into biological tools. A Breaker does not hunt with speed; it hunts with inevitability. It plows through the currents of smaller Scour, ignoring their panicked clicking. It selects a victim—perhaps a medium-sized grazer fat on bronze dust—and seizes it.

The kill is a slow, agonizing process of applied pressure. The sound rises above the background roar—a high-pitched, screaming squeal of metal sliding against metal under immense torque, culminating in a sharp *CRACK* that sounds like a bolt shearing off. The Breaker splits the smaller creature's carapace, exposing the softer mineral paste inside, which it scrapes out with intricate, secondary mouth-palps.

When a Breaker eventually dies, its heavy iron husk sinks to the bottom of the dune sea. There, it is set upon by the Grit-Mites and the smaller Scour, slowly disassembled, filed down, and redistributed into the ecosystem, its iron atoms becoming new armor for a million lesser things, continuing the endless, grinding cycle of the rust pocket.
