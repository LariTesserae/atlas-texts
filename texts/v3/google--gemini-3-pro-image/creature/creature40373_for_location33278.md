Something exists here that is less a resident and more a manifestation of the crushing depth and stubborn machinery. It is a solitary entity, massive and ungainly, a bottom-crawler of immense proportions that embodies the slow, grinding persistence of the drowned city.

At first glance, through the murky, silt-laden gloom, it appears to be merely another shifting mound of debris—a collapsed bulkhead or a pile of discarded gears given momentary motion by a deep-sea current. But it moves with intention, a slow, tectonic slouch against the pressure.

It has no true shape, having grown asymmetrical and bulky over centuries of accretion. Its outer shell is not chitin or bone, but a fused carapace of salvaged history. Riveted boilerplate, curved sections of pressure hulls, and thick, corroded gears are integrated into its being, cemented together by barnacle-like manganese deposits and thick velvet rust. It is a hermit crab on a colossal scale, but instead of finding a shell, it has built one from the corpses of factories.

To move through the syrup-thick water, it utilizes six multi-jointed limbs that resemble hydraulic excavators more than biological legs. They are piston-driven and clumsy, tipped with crude, grasping claws made of rebar bundles and jagged steel plating. Every movement is a battle against the physics of the deep, accompanied by the shriek of metal grinding on metal, a sound that resonates through the water like a dying whale's song, felt instantly in the bone.

It is blind, for eyes are liabilities in this crushing darkness. Instead, the "head"—a dense cluster of armored sensors protruding from the front of its scavenged shell—is a bristling array of thick, whip-like antennae made of copper cabling that taste the chemical composition of the silt. Alongside them are sensitive patches of exposed, leathery flesh that detect the faint electromagnetic fields given off by the city's failing power grid.

This creature is the custodian of the mechanical undeath. It is drawn irresistibly to energy and movement. It seeks out the cracks where sulfurous heat bleeds into the freezing ocean, grazing on the thick grayish mats of bacteria that thrive there. But its primary drive is more obscure.

It migrates slowly through the industrial canyons, pausing at the shuddering conveyor belts and the agonizingly turning flywheels. With agonizing slowness, it uses its massive claws to clear debris that threatens to jam the ancient gears, or to clumsily patch a leaking hydraulic line with a slab of scavenged iron. It is a crude, instinctive mechanic, keeping the senseless machinery turning for reasons lost to time. It does not understand the machines, but it shares their rhythm—a slow, heavy existence in the crushing dark, refusing to stop.
