The emptiness is a deception, a limitation of organic perception. The city is not abandoned; it is merely inhabited by a form of life perfectly adapted to a cosmos of vibration, geology, and absolute drought.

They are the Resonants.

They are creatures of mineral, not flesh. To human eyes, they would be nearly invisible even under a floodlight, so perfectly do they mimic their surroundings. Their bodies are squat, dense agglomerations of the same calcified fungi and silicified root matter that forms the forest. They are carved from the world itself, rough-hewn monoliths that range in size from a clenched fist to a small boulder.

They have no eyes, for there has never been light. They have no mouths, for they do not eat in a biological sense. They have no lungs, for the parched air offers nothing to respire. They are beings of pure density and structure, animate stones whose "life" functions are driven by the relentless, grinding roar of the planet’s core.

That deafening tectonic gale, that low cathedral groan that would shatter human nerves, is their energy source. They are piezoelectric entities on a macro scale. The constant, violent vibration shakes their crystalline lattices, generating the internal charge that animates their rigid forms. When the roar swells, they move faster; when it ebbs, they slow. If the sound were ever to stop, they would instantly die, reverting to mere rubble.

They do not walk so much as slide, grinding along the highly polished floors on bases smoothed by centuries of friction. Their movement creates a secondary layer of sound, a high-pitched shrieking of stone on stone that is immediately swallowed by the greater roar.

Their "senses" are entirely tactile and seismic. They perceive the cavern not as a void, but as a solid matrix of vibrating waves. They "see" the intricate architecture of their city by pressing their bodies against the walls, feeling the subtle differences in resonance bounces created by archways, bridges, and vast drops. A doorway is not a visual gap, but a sudden change in acoustic pressure.

The city is their work, but it is not a dwelling place. It is a byproduct of their digestion. The Resonants "feed" on unstructured stone. They slowly, agonizingly abrade the raw surfaces of the fossil forest with their own bodies and crude stone tools held in integrated gripping notches. They consume the resulting dust—the pulverized nitrates and ancient minerals—incorporating the matter into their own ever-growing mass.

The millions of chisel marks are their teeth marks. The vast halls and soaring bridges are not spaces for living, but the negative space left behind after generations of slow, methodical consumption. They are hollowing out the world to build themselves.

They have no language but vibration. They communicate by pressing against one another, transmitting complex patterns of tremors directly through their mineral bodies. A gathering of Resonants is a silent huddle of rocks, rapidly exchanging seismic data about density peaks, structural weaknesses in the root systems, and the nuanced flavors of different nitrate veins.

Their lifespan is geologic. They do not age in a biological sense; they merely erode. The same dry, abrasive grit wind that smooths the city walls also slowly wears down the Resonants. When one becomes too rounded, too smooth to effectively grind against the world and generate a charge, it simply stops moving, becoming indistinguishable from the architecture it helped carve, waiting to be consumed and recycled by its descendants.

They are the slow, dark heart of the machine, the consciousness of the stone forest, eternally grinding in the deafening dark.
