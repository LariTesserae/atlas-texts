The inhabitants of this immense, silent mausoleum at the top of the world are twofold: the myriad, scuttling things that belong to the rock, and the sparse, wretched things that have become trapped upon it.

The primary denizens are the arthropods, the **Dust-Mites of Eternity**. To call them animals is almost a misnomer; they are animated shards of the landscape itself. Their countless segmented legs are tipped not with claws, but with microscopic abrasive pads, capable of finding purchase on the smoothest ceramic bedrock. Their carapaces are colored the exact shade of aged bone and polished sandstone, dusted with the same fine, pervasive grit that coats everything. They are blind—eyes are useless in a world where the light never changes and the geometry lies—possessing instead multifaceted sensory pits along their flanks that detect the subtlest vibrations carried through the stone.

They are a geological force hidden in the guise of biology. Their endless, frantic scuttling is the only form of erosion left in this windless place. Over billions of years, the friction of their chitinous feet has helped sand down the jagged peaks into the smooth, tired waves of rock that exist today. They flow over the bulbous monoliths like dry water, a seething, unified current of pale limbs. They do not eat in any recognizable sense; perhaps they derive sustenance from the faint radiation of the indigo sky, or perhaps they are simply automata, winding down a kinetic energy charge that has lasted for an eon. Their silence is total. A million mandibles might snap inches from your ear, and the dead, brittle air would refuse to carry the sound.

Living parasitically within this silent swarm are the **Stone-Sleepers**, the pathetic remnants of a near-human species trapped in the stasis.

They are a people warped by the impossible altitude and the crushing sameness of their reality. To survive the atmosphere that feels like breathing powdered glass, their physiology has drastically shifted. Their chests are enormous, distended into rigid, barrel-like cages to house vast, efficient lungs that labor constantly to extract oxygen from the ether. This gives them a top-heavy, hunched appearance. Their limbs are gangly and brittle, their joints swollen.

They have no moisture in them. Their skin is not flesh, but a thick, grey hide, cracked and fissured like an ancient dried riverbed, coated in a protective layer of the ubiquitous dust. If cut, they would not bleed liquid, but a sluggish, tar-like paste that instantly hardens. They are nearly hairless, save for wiry, silica-coated patches on their scalps that resemble the crystalline plants.

The Stone-Sleepers are as silent as the bugs that swarm around their feet. Speech is impossible in this air, and over generations, the concept of language has atrophied into a rudimentary set of hand signs and touches. Their existence is a series of mindless, repetitive loops mirroring the insects they live among.

You might find one sitting slumped against a quartz bulb, holding a bone hammer over a flint core. They will sit there for hours, perhaps days, completely immobile, staring into the indigo zenith with eyes that are clouded by permanent cataracts, shielding them from the maddening fixity of the stars. Then, without warning, they will strike the flint once, drop the tools, and wander off into the labyrinth, forgetting the task immediately.

Another might spend a "day" obsessively sweeping dust from one patch of bedrock to another with a broom made of crystal fibers, only to sweep it back again later. They weave baskets that hold nothing. They build walls that defend against nothing. They are caught in the grooves of a broken record, acting out the memories of purpose in a world where purpose has ended.

They navigate the warped geometry not with sight, but with a highly developed spatial sense, a kind of internal compass that understands the non-Euclidean curves of the plateau. They perceive the "wrongness" of the landscape as normal, walking in spirals to reach a point that appears to be in a straight line.

They are terrifying because they are hollow. The cold eternity of the place has seeped into them, replacing their humanity with the geological patience of the stones they dwell among. They are not waiting for change; they have forgotten that change ever existed. They are merely enduring the endless twilight, silent statues breathing glass, waiting for a darkness that will never come.
