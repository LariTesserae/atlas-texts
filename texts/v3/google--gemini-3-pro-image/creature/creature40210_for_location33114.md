The primary inhabitant of this drowned plateau is the mechanism itself.

It is a singular, sprawled entity of geological senescence, a creature made of corroded iron and tectonic fire that has forgotten how to die. It lives only in the most rudimentary, vegetative sense. Deep beneath the slick obsidian streets, thermal couplings act as a vast, slow-beating heart, pumping superheated fluids through arteries of pitted steel. The vibrations felt in the teeth are its respiration; the weeping condensation on the ziggurats is its sweat.

It possesses reflexes, but no consciousness. When internal pressure reaches a critical threshold, a valve blows—a massive, involuntary spasm echoing through the purple twilight. Conveyor belts, fused by rust into statues of industrial intent, are paralyzed limbs. The city waits, with infinite patience, for instructions that ceased coming millennia ago, existing in a permanent state of hot, wet idle.

Yet, in the margins of this colossal automaton, life in the biological sense does cling, though it has been forced to become nearly as inorganic as its surroundings.

The "rumors" of life found near the boiling runoff channels are the **Ferro-vores**, or iron-eaters. They are the sole inheritors of this scalded kingdom.

To survive here, they have cast aside almost all recognizable traits of organic life. They are hexapedal, low-slung things, rarely growing larger than a human hand. They do not possess skin or fur; instead, they are encased in dermis-thick plating that is more mineral than biological. Their segmented shells are a mottled fusion of excreted chitin and absorbed environmental metals—patches of rough cast iron, streaks of sulfurous yellow, and blooms of verdigris copper. They look less like insects and more like ambulatory geodes, chunks of the city’s own architecture that have broken free and learned to crawl.

They are functionally blind. Eyes would be useless in the perpetual steam-fog, and vulnerable to the searing heat. Instead, they navigate the treacherous, slick catwalks and the banks of scalding rivers using highly sensitive thermal pits located along their flanks, feeling the radiant heat of the environment to find cooler micro-climates or the intense hotspots where mineral-rich steam vents.

Their existence is one of excruciating slowness. In the immense gravity of the heat, movement is energy-expensive. They creep along the filtration screens of the great, dead machines, using hardened mandibles that resemble hydraulic pincers to scrape away accumulated heavy metals and crystallized salts. They do not eat flesh or plant; they consume the city itself, processing the corroded sludge and mineral deposits.

They are silent. They have no predators, for nothing else can survive here, and they have no prey. They are solitary scavengers, ignoring one another, their entire existence focused on the endless, slow consumption of a dead industrial world. When they die, they do not decompose in the traditional sense; they simply cease moving, their metallic husks fusing to the catwalks, eventually becoming just another rough patch on the skin of the greater, sleeping machine.
