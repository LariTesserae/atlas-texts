To exist in the Desiccated Lattice is to be an entity composed not of wet flesh and blood, but of dielectric ceramics, conductive filaments, and hardened, silicate epidermises. Life here is fundamentally electrical and kinetic, rather than chemical in the aqueous sense. The inhabitants do not seek water; they seek charge, vibration, and mineral slurry. They are children of the roaring machine, deaf by necessity, armored against the light, and thriving on the verge of electrocution.

The baseline for animal life here is the **Lithic Scrubber**. These are the ubiquitous grazers of the great branch-horizons, enormous arthropods the size of terrestrial bulldozers. They are low, wide, and heavily armored with segmented plates of non-conductive, chalky-white ceramic, evolved to shed the constant deluge of static discharge that snaps against the surface. They possess no eyes—the star above is too blinding, the shadows too absolute—but guide themselves through sensory bristles that detect the sub-sonic geological thrumming of the Lattice. Their mouthparts are rotating drums filled with diamond-hard teeth, which they use to grind down the surface layers of the lignified wood, harvesting piezoelectric mosses and crystalline spurs. As they chew, the friction generates a visible aura of blue sparks around their maws. They defecate bricks of compressed, inert dust.

Preying upon the Scrubbers and smaller fauna are the terrifying **Resonance Stalkers**. Lean, skeletal horrors constructed of oxidized copper tubing and tensile wire tendons, they look like kinetic sculptures gone feral. They are famously hard to see against the complex background of the Lattice, often freezing perfectly still, mistaken for a piece of rusted debris. They hunt not by sight, but by acute vibratory sense. Their "heads" are dominated by massive, parabolic dish-like structures that focus the deafening background roar of the Lattice, filtering it to detect the specific scuttling rhythm of prey. They strike with whip-thin limbs ending in pneumatic spikes, puncturing the ceramic shells of grazers and draining their residual bio-electric charge. When they move, they clatter like falling junk.

Far above the "ground," inhabiting the vast abysses between the branches, are the masters of the void: the **Auroral Kites**. These creatures have no true solid bodies; they are essentially living solar sails. Their wingspans stretch for hundreds of feet, composed of incredibly thin, translucent membranes made of spun silica and conductive foil that shimmer with interference patterns. They never land. They ride the massive thermal updrafts generated by the industrial heat vents of the branches below, soaring endlessly in the freezing vacuum. They feed on atmospheric dust charged with static electricity, filtering the vacuum like baleen whales. During the great electrical storms, whole flocks of Kites gather, their wings glowing with captured voltage as they act as living lightning rods, bleeding off the immense energies of the sky in silent, blinding flashes.

Where the Lattice is most violently active—where the internal machinery roars loudest and the growth is fastest—the **Ferro-Termites** make their homes. These are social insects, each the size of a large dog, with exoskeletons made of iron pyrite and hardened resin. They are the architects of the surface chaos. They swarm over areas where new crystalline growth spurs are erupting with shrieking metal sounds, using acidic saliva that smells of ozone to soften the material before carving it into hive structures. They are deafeningly loud creatures; a working colony produces a collective grinding screech that rivals the machinery itself. Their mandibles act as wire cutters and pliers, constantly pruning and reshaping the conductive filaments that sprout from the lattice, farming the energy flow for their queens deep within the branch-canyons.

Deepest of all, living within the roaring veins of the Lattice itself, are the **Conduit Leeches**. Rarely seen on the surface, these are serpentine creatures, meters long, with bodies like flexible armored conduit pipes. They tolerate extreme heat and pressure. They bore through the walls of the main "arteries" carrying the non-aqueous fluids—the liquid methane and pressurized silica slurry—and attach themselves like ticks to a blood vessel. Their physiology is utterly alien, entirely reliant on the high-pressure chemical cocktails intended for the Lattice’s own growth. If pulled from their host vein, they do not die like an animal; they depressurize and shatter like explosive glass.
