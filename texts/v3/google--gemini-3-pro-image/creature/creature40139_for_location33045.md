It is large, humanoid only in the loosest sense of shared symmetry, and it is huddled in the warmest corner of the atrium, where a cluster of heat-exchange conduits weave behind a bank of aggressive ferns.

It is a creature of this specific environment, a biological mirror to the room's industrial womb. Its skin is pale, almost translucent, like high-grade silicone or unbaked ceramic slip. It is completely hairless, glistening with the same omnipresent moisture that slicks the walls. Moisture beads on its surface, refusing to soak in, mimicking the oily, hydrophobic coating of the engineered leaves around it. If you touched it, it would feel cool, damp, and yielding, like wet neoprene over firm cartilage.

It has no obvious gender, and its features are embryonic, indistinct. It looks unfinished—a prototype draped over a metal framework. Where joints should be—elbows, knees, spine—the pale skin gives way to flexible, ribbed polymer cuffing, integrated directly into its flesh. These aren't prosthetics; they are structural components that have grown *with* the biological tissue, shiny and unblemished by wear.

The creature is currently plugged in.

A thick, braided umbilical cable, identical to the conduits running beneath the floor grating, extends from a recessed port in its abdomen. The cable snakes across the wet metal and couples with a heavy, brass-valved bulkhead in the wall. Through the translucent outer sheath of this cable, you can see a viscous, bioluminescent green slurry moving slowly—the same accelerated nutrient broth feeding the vines. It is not eating; it is refueling, a passive intake of optimized calories.

Its head is large, resting heavily on its knees. The eyes are enormous, taking up nearly a third of the face, covered by thick, nictitating membranes that slide across them with an audible wet click every few seconds to clear the condensation. When the membranes retract, the eyes beneath are a milky, reflective silver, devoid of pupils. They are eyes evolved not for seeing shapes in the dark, but for filtering the intense, blinding white radiance of the cloud-deck outside the glass and the harsh ultraviolet of the grow-lamps.

It does not move much. It breathes in a slow, shallow rhythm that harmonizes with the *shhh-cluck* of the nutrient pumps. Its exhalations add a faint, sweet chemical tang to the copper-and-ozone smell of the air.

It is watching the wall-printing gantry.

Its massive, silver eyes follow the slow, squeegeeing motion of the extruder mechanism across the chamber. It watches the grey slurry emerge, settle, and cure into a solid wall. There is no impatience in its gaze, only a profound, vacant absorption in the process of construction. It is a creature born of a machine that is still building itself, and it sits in the wet, humming quiet, waiting to be finished.
