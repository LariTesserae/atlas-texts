They are the wretched ghosts of this blinding machine, a people bleached by cold light and shaped by perpetual instability.

To survive in a world of screaming white radiance, they have evolved—or perhaps devolved—into creatures of necessary shadows. Their skin is not merely pale; it is the colour of skimmed milk or old gristle, possessing a sickly, semi-translucent quality that betrays the frantic, furnace-like metabolism burning beneath just to keep them alive in the sub-freezing air. Veins trace blue maps across their temples and wrists, visible evidence of their desperate warmth.

You will never see their eyes. To expose naked retinas to the unshielded luminescence of the world-plant is to invite immediate, searing blindness. Their faces are perpetually hidden behind apparatus born of scarcity. Some wear heavy, bulbous goggles fashioned from the shaved, amber-coloured knots of hardened resin, filtering the clinical glare into a manageable, jaundice-yellow haze. Others are hooded deep within cowls woven from coarse, fibrous husk, extending forward like blinkers, forcing them to navigate their precarious world in narrow slivers of vision, heads constantly swivelling in paranoid awareness.

They are lean, almost skeletal, stripped of all excess mass by the relentless cold and the demanding geography of their existence. They are climbers by absolute necessity. Their limbs are long and sinewy, ending in disproportionately large hands and feet that are permanently stained a bruised greenish-black from gripping frozen, sap-slicked branches. Their palms and soles are covered in thick, leather-like pads of callus, natural crampons for navigating the icy lattice. Their movement is not graceful; it is a hunched, crab-like scuttle, keeping their center of gravity low, always maintaining three points of contact with the shifting architecture. They do not walk; they adhere.

Their clothing is a testament to their parasitic existence, a patchwork armour against the freezing damp. They are wrapped in layer upon layer of cured, waxy membranes harvested from the colossal leaves—stiff, crinkling garments that repel the incessant condensation. These peel-suits are the same dull, off-white shade as the environment, providing camouflage not against predators, but against the crushing starkness of the light itself. At their belts hang their precious, brittle tools: jagged knives and prying hooks chipped from the super-hard, glass-like silica coatings of dead bamboo spires.

They are a profoundly silent people. In a world where the scale swallows sound and sudden noise might trigger a deluge of razor-sharp ice from above, speech is a dangerous luxury. They communicate almost entirely through a complex lexicon of hand gestures, subtle shifts in posture, and sharp, sibilant hisses that cut quickly through the thin, ozone-heavy air.

Their psychology is defined by the ground beneath their feet—which is to say, it is defined by terror. They have no concept of permanence. They are nomadic scavengers, moving in small, tight-knit family units, terrified of overstaying their welcome on any given root-mass. Their eyes behind their amber lenses are forever darting upwards, watching for the shudder that precedes a growth-spasm, ready to cut the vine-ropes of their lean-tos and flee before their temporary world shatters into glowing splinters. They are tenacious, shivering barnacles on the hull of a violently growing god, existing only in the brief, icy pauses between catastrophe.
