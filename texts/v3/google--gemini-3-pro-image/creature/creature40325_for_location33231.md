The beings who claim this steaming, roaring hollow as home are as much a product of the rot as the architecture they inhabit. They are the stewards of acceleration, the gardeners of the compost metropolis. Let us call them the Rot-Stokers.

They are towering figures, standing easily seven or eight feet tall, though their posture is perpetually crouched, curved forward to reduce their profile against the immense backdrop of the burl-hives. At a distance, through the yellow-green haze, they appear almost like mobile fungi or walking shards of wet bark. Their flesh is not skin in the mammalian sense; it is a sleek, dense rind, similar in texture to a smooth eggplant or a polished shelf mushroom. It is perpetually slick with moisture, gleaming in the diffused daylight, colored in mottled patterns of deep olive, bruise-purple, and the pale, sickly cream of new fungal growth.

This hide is constantly sloughing off and renewing. As they move, thin, translucent layers of epidermis peel away, dissolving almost instantly into the nutrient-rich slime of the forest floor. They are emerging from themselves with every step.

Because the auditory landscape is a crushing din of grinding wood and animal shrieks, vocal language is obsolete. The Rot-Stokers are silent giants. They communicate, and interface with their biological machinery, through an incredibly sophisticated olfactory and sensory system.

Where a human face would have a nose and mouth, the Rot-Stoker has a complex cluster of fanning, gill-like membranes that pulse rhythmically. These organs taste the thick air, filtering the dense currents of spore-data drifting between the habitation nodes. A slight flare of these membranes can read the genetic instructions for a new bridge extension, or detect the distress pheromones of a dying structural burl three miles away. Their eyes are enormous, multifaceted orbs set wide on their skulls, covered by thick nictitating membranes that wipe away condensation every few seconds. They see not just light, but heat signatures and the faint bioluminescent pulses that run through the city’s vascular walls.

They wear no clothing—the heat would make it unbearable, and the constant biological turnover would consume it—but they carry their status and tools embedded in their own flesh. The older among them have integrated bioluminescent mosses growing in spiraling patterns along their spines and shoulders, glowing with soft, pulsing indigo light that denotes their lineage or function.

Their limbs are long, powerful, and surprisingly delicate at the extremities. Their hands end in four multi-jointed fingers, the tips of which are not equipped with nails, but with semi-porous pads. These pads are their primary tools. By placing a hand against a wall of living cellulose, a Rot-Stoker can excrete specific enzyme cocktails or viral prompts.

You might see one of these beings pause at the edge of a chasm, where the path has just dissolved into slime. It kneels, placing a broad palm onto the raw, weeping edge of the botanical substrate. A tremor runs through its arm as it injects a catalyst. Within seconds, the groan of accelerated growth drowns out the background noise. Massive, pale tendrils erupt from the stump-floor beneath the Stoker's feet, weaving themselves violently into a new, stable causeway in a matter of minutes. The Stoker crosses without looking back, as the bridge behind it already begins to brown at the edges, its purpose fulfilled.

They move with a deliberate, languid grace that belies the chaotic speed of their environment. They are the calm eyes of the biological storm. They are masters of patience in a world of frantic haste, understanding that to control the city, they must serve the decay that powers it. They are the mold that thinks, the fungi that build, turning the grave of an ancient titan into a cradle for a violent, burgeoning civilization.
