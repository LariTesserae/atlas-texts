The life that festers in the Basin of the Perpetual Hum is a testament to evolution’s terrifying adaptability. In an environment that would boil, crush, and deafen biological entities, a complex, thriving ecosystem of metal, ceramic, and semi-conductive flesh has arisen. They are the children of corrosion and voltage, organisms whose metabolisms run not on glucose and oxygen, but on electrolysis, thermal gradients, and the direct siphoning of raw current.

They do not have eyes. In a world of blinding steam-glare and deep shadow, sight is a liability. Instead, they perceive through exquisite tactile sensitivity. The omnipresent vibration of the Hum is their medium; they read the geological-scale grinding of the city-machine through their gripping pads, distinguishing the footfalls of prey from the shudder of an imminent steam-Vent. They taste the air continuously, their sensory antennae thick with receptors analyzing the chemical composition of the omnipresent vapor, hunting for the copper-tang of a fresh coolant leak or the ozone-sharpness of an exposed conduit.

The "grazers" are the most numerous. On the vast, weeping flanks of the monolithic heat-exchangers, the entities mentioned—the ones with carapaces of spun diamond-dust—are ubiquitous. They are low-slung, multipedal things, resembling trilobites forged from industrial runoff. Their eight to twelve legs end in magnetic gripping claws that allow them to traverse vertical faces slick with dielectric oil. They move in slow, jerky rhythms, pausing only to lower their heavy, armored heads to the substrate. There, grinding mouthparts, studded with industrial diamonds, rasp away at the mineral accretions and crystalline lattices that grow like barnacles on the sweating alloys. They leave behind gleaming trails of bare metal that quickly oxidize in the hot wetness.

Preying on these grazers are things that embody the Basin’s volatile fluidity. Emerging from the deep shadows of narrow canyons are the Ferro-Stalkers. They are amorphous predators composed of a semi-liquid ferromagnetic suspension. They look like flowing mercury polluted with grit. They hold no fixed shape, flowing over the chaotic terrain like sentient sludge, immune to the corrosive brines. They hunt by ambush, pooling near a rich mineral deposit and waiting for a grazer to approach. When they strike, it is a sudden violent extrusion of pseudopods, engulfing the diamond-shelled creature. The stalker then contracts, using internal electromagnetic fields to crush the prey's armor while potent acid-batteries within its liquid body dissolve the metallic flesh.

High above, in the blinding white fog, the aerial ecosystem thrives on pure energy. The clouds of metallic "insects" are less like bugs and more like self-organizing nodes of captive lightning. They swarm around unshielded energy pylons, their translucent, silica-based wings beating rapidly enough to generate a high-pitched whine that cuts through the deeper roar of the Basin. They feed by diving directly into plasma discharges, their bodies temporarily glowing sun-bright as they store the immense energies in capacitor-like organs.

Deeper still, in the submerged lower levels where the oily fluids pool into warm, stagnant seas, live the giants. These are the scavengers of the deep, armored behemoths that resemble segmented subway trains more than worms. They are blind, lumbering processors of waste. They slither through the toxic sludge, their colossal, shovel-shaped maws filtering the fluid for rusted debris, precipitated heavy metals, and the corpses of creatures that have fallen from the heights. Their passing causes tidal waves in the enclosed, flooded chambers, and their low-frequency calls—a grinding boom that travels through the liquid—can be felt vibrating in the teeth of anyone standing miles above on the "surface."

Every creature here is coated in the Basin’s output. They glisten with oil; they drip brine; they vent steam from joints. They are integral to the machine they inhabit, a secondary layer of biology growing over the primary layer of geology-like architecture. They live quick, violent lives, born in the heat, constantly vibrating with the Hum, and eventually dying to be dissolved back into the supersaturated soup, becoming just another layer in the miles-deep accretion of the Basin itself.
