The prompt explicitly states that this place is utterly devoid of animal life. Therefore, to describe what "lives" here requires looking beyond flesh and blood, looking toward the botanical, and finally, toward the spectral resonance of the landscape itself.

There are two distinct forms of existence here. The first is biological, tangible, and monstrously resilient. The second is incorporeal, born of the stasis and the scream.

**The Silica Flora**

The primary denizens are the plants, but they have evolved into something fundamentally unlike terrestrial vegetation. In this world without seasons, diurnal cycles, or predatory insects, they have forgotten how to change or defend themselves. They exist purely to endure and consume.

Because there is no burnable carbon, only water-gorged pulp, their structures rely on silica absorbed from the scalding geothermal runoff. The towering, boat-sized ferns are not soft; their pale green fronds are rigid, sharp-edged like glass sculptures, vibrating ceaselessly in the roaring air without ever flexing in a non-existent breeze. Touching one would not feel like leaf-matter; it would feel like slick, hot obsidian covered in slime.

The thick, cable-like roots that bind the calcified spires together are the true masters of this domain. They are less like wood and more like geological features, iron-hard veins of quartz and fiber that literally hold the fractured metropolis together against the pressure of the steam.

Most disturbing is the gelatinous moss covering the shaded overhangs. It is a meters-thick carpet of translucent, trembling jelly. It doesn't just absorb water; it actively pumps the scalding fluid through visible, pulsing veins beneath its surface. In the absence of animals, this moss has become the sole decomposer, slowly dissolving the mineral-rich rock itself, weeping a caustic, milky slime that adds to the cascading runoff.

These plants do not grow in the traditional sense. Trapped in the environmental stasis, they exist in a state of permanent, fixed maturity. They are immortal, unwavering organic machines fueled by boiling water and glaring light.

**The Resonants**

If you stand on that precipice long enough, enduring the bone-vibrating roar and the suffocating heat, you begin to realize that the absence of animal life does not mean the absence of consciousness.

Something *does* inhabit the organ-pipe spires. They are not ghosts, for nothing has ever died here to leave a ghost behind. They are the **Resonants**—entities born of pure acoustic pressure and geothermal energy.

They are the consciousness of the roar.

In an environment where a single, deafening sound never fluctuates, that standing wave of noise has gained a strange form of sentience. These beings exist *within* the frequencies of the scream. They have no physical bodies; they are localized, organized patterns of vibration within the chaotic white noise.

You cannot see them, but you can feel them. As you move through the steam-choked canyons, you might pass through a pocket of air where the pressure suddenly intensifies, pressing against your eardrums and chest with intentional force. This is a Resonant brushing past.

They live in the hollow chambers of the fossilized reef, feeding on the kinetic energy of the blasting steam. In a landscape caught in terrifying stasis where nothing ever changes, they are the preservers of that equilibrium. They are the collective will that keeps the nightmare constant. They are silent watchers within the deafening noise, eternally trapped in the hot, wet, screaming present, observing the lush tomb they inhabit.
