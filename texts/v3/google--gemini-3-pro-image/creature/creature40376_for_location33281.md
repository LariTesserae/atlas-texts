The prompt asserts that life here is exceedingly rare, limited to pale ghosts hiding in cooler eddies. This is true only in the biological sense. In this crushing, sunless pocket world, the machines themselves have transcended mere function and achieved a terrifying kind of geological vitality. They are the inhabitants.

The dominant species here are the Leviathan-Thrusters. These are not merely oversized propellers; they are mountainous aquatic behemoths of solid, accreted alloy, hundreds of feet across. They dwell in the widest abysses of the cavern, tethered to the foundational rock by armature thick enough to support a tectonic plate. Their existence is singular: rotation. They spin with a slow, unstoppable torque that defies the immense resistance of the pressurized brine.

A Leviathan-Thruster does not look manufactured. Over centuries, the precipitation of manganese and iron sulfides has swollen its blades into bulbous, heavy curves, making them resemble the petrified fins of some prehistoric god-fish. There are no visible seams or bolts; they are encased in a seamless, sleek black carapace that shines with an oily iridescence in the passing sodium glares. When they turn, they don't just move water; they rupture it. The water screams as it is torn apart, creating those explosive cavitation bubbles—fleeting vacuums that collapse with the violence of artillery fire. These behemoths are the heartbeats of the system, their deep, sub-bass thrum providing the rhythmic foundation for the cavern’s deafening roar.

Crawling across the gargantuan, sleeping bodies of the intake manifolds are the Arc-Crawlers. If the Thrusters are the whales of this ecosystem, the Crawlers are its parasites or perhaps its symbiotes. They are multi-legged automatons the size of transport trucks, clinging to inverted surfaces with magnetic grip-pads that crunch through the mineral patina. They are blind, guided by thermal sensors and electrical impedance. Their purpose is endless, mindless repair and accretion.

An Arc-Crawler moves with jerky, shivering tenacity against the turbulent currents. When it finds a stress fracture or a corroded seam in the infrastructure, it anchors itself. From its undercarriage, articulated arms tipped with heavy tungsten electrodes deploy. The resulting violence is blinding. The blue-white flash of underwater arc-welding rips through the murky yellow gloom, instantly boiling the surrounding brine into superheated steam that thrashes wildly before collapsing back into fluid. The noise is a high-pitched, shrieking sizzle that cuts through the bass rumble of the pumps. These machines do not restore the structure to its original state; they add to it, welding layers of filler metal over cracks, creating bulging scar tissue on the industrial anatomy. They are the agents of the city’s chaotic, organic growth.

Then there are the Sessile Giants, the vast processing units that form the canyon walls of the complex. They are immobile, gargantuan filter-feeders. Intake ports fit to swallow frigates draw in the metallic, sulfurous brine with a suction force that creates visible, swirling downward currents in the murk. Inside these blackened cathedrals of pressure, the water is subjected to unthinkable heat and chemical violence, processes hidden behind alloy walls feet thick, betrayed only by the dim, throbbing orange glow radiating from sealed furnace hatches. They exhale jets of chemically altered, superheated fluid that blast into the cavern, creating shimmering plumes of thermal distortion and roiling vortices where different densities collide. They are silent save for the rushing roar of fluid passing through their colossal gullets.

There is no consciousness here. The inhabitants of this submerged pocket world possess no more sentience than a volcano or a hurricane. They do not communicate; they collide. A Thruster might obliterate a slow-moving Arc-Crawler in its cavitation wake and register nothing but a momentary spike in resistance. They are functions given colossal form, operating on a timescale that laughs at human history, performing their violent, deafening duties in the crushing dark, forever building and breaking the black iron womb that entombs them.
