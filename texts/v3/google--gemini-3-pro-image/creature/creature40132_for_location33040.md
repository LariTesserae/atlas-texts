The beings that claim this terminal basin as home are less inhabitants and more animate extensions of the geological machine itself. They call themselves the Ferrok (in the rare moments they bother to translate their mode of communication into something auditory), and they are creatures forged by immense pressure, paralyzing cold, and ceaseless abrasion.

To survive the crushing atmospheric weight, the Ferrok are squat and tremendously dense. They possess no exposed flesh; to be soft in this world is to be sheared away. Instead, they are encased in natural segmented armor, exoskeletons grown from the very metallic slurry they wade through. Over their lifespans, their bodies accrue layers of magnetite and hematite, fused by internal heat and polished to a high, reflective sheen by the constant bombardment of diamond-dust wind. They do not just live on the landscape; they are made of it. When they stand still, hunched against the biting chill, they are indistinguishable from the intricate, frost-rimed rock formations emerging from the fog.

In a world drowned in deafening noise and perpetual twilight, conventional senses are useless. The Ferrok are functionally deaf and blind. Eyes would be liabilities, easily damaged by flying debris and useless in the gloom; ears would be destroyed by the roaring grind of the tectonic plates.

Instead, they perceive their reality through an incredibly refined magneto-reception and vibratory sense. The soles of their heavy, clawed feet are sensitive enough to map the subterranean friction miles below. They "hear" the world through conduction, pressing their armored bodies against the groaning plates to communicate in complex patterns of rhythmic tapping and grinding tones that resonate through their metal bones.

They navigate the shifting terrain by sensing the intense magnetic fields generated by the friction. To a Ferrok, the basin is not dark; it is ablaze with swirling auroral energies invisible to the human eye. The edges of the grinding tectonic slabs glow white-hot in their magnetic vision; the rivers of conductive sludge are blinding ribbons of energy; and the sprawling ferro-fungi forests pulse with intricate, soothing bio-magnetic rhythms. They track their prey—the massive sludge-trilobites—by the disturbances these creatures create in the local magnetic field.

Their metabolism is slow, driven by the geothermal heat they siphon from the ground and the chemical energy harvested from the metal-rich ecology. They are lithovores of a specialized kind. They graze on the brittle, sparking mats of ferro-fungi, their heavy mandibles crunching through the circuitry-like growths like cattle eating dry grass. They drink the viscous, conductive slurry from the rivers, their internal biology filtering out necessary minerals and excreting purified metal dust that is immediately snatched up by the wind.

The most disturbing aspect of the Ferrok is their relationship with the basin’s broken causality. They have evolved an innate, intuitive grasp of the time delays that plague their world. A Ferrok hunting party will stop moving suddenly, bracing themselves against a ridge of iron. Seconds later, the deafening shriek of a shearing plate will arrive, followed moments after that by the actual violent lurch of the ground beneath them. They react to events *before* they physically happen, living in a constant, fluid state of pre-cognition necessitated by a reality where sound, cause, and effect have become unmoored.

They are a somber, heavy people, their movements deliberate and clanking. They build no cities, for nothing stays still long enough to build upon. Their only permanent structures are deep, communal warrens bored into the "dead zones"—rare, temporary islands of stability wedged between the largest grinding plates. These warrens are insufferably hot, vibrating chambers where they huddle together, sharing conductive warmth and magnetic songs, waiting for the next shift of the great planetary engine. They are the rust-eaters, the time-echo watchers, the final, hardened children of a dying world's momentum.
