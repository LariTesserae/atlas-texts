This is a realm of crushing weight and slow, thick heat, and the things that claim the summit city as home are wrought from the same unforgiving physics. They are the **Lithovores**, though the name is an imperfect translation of the complex vibrational hum by which they identify themselves.

To the human eye, they would first appear as geological accidents—lumps of detached pillow lava or mobile concretion of rust and ochre sludge. They are squat, incredibly dense beings, their physiology having abandoned internal air spaces eons ago in favor of incompressible fluid-filled tissues and heavy, mineral-impregnated exoskeletons. They do not swim. To move here is to push against a medium as resistant as cold syrup. Instead, they anchor themselves to the jagged substrate with six multi-jointed, piston-like legs tipped with claws of natural volcanic glass.

Their hides are rough as coarse-grit sandpaper, a patchwork of chitinous plates and dermal armor studded with actual bits of obsidian and pumice they have incorporated into their own bodies during moltings. They are colored in the camouflage of the deep vent: mottled sulfide yellows, deep ferrous rusts, and the matte black of fresh basalt. When they stay still, curled against a cooling lava flow, they are indistinguishable from the mountain itself.

They have no distinct heads, but rather a sensory cluster located on their anterior slope. Vision in the crimson twilight is specialized. Two large, multifaceted orbs, glowing with a faint, ghostly green bioluminescence, dominate their upper front. These eyes do not perceive the visible spectrum as surface dwellers know it; they see the world in thermal gradients. The superheated vents are blinding white pillars to them; the cooling lava flows are roads of fading gold; and the cold abyss beyond their summit home is a terrifying void of absolute blackness.

Below the eyes lies a complex array of feathery palps and robust mandibles. The palps are constantly in motion, tasting the chemical composition of the thick water, searching for peak concentrations of sulfur and methane. The mandibles are multifaceted tools—strong enough to crush the calcified stalks of dead tube worms for construction material, yet dexterous enough to chip delicate flakes of obsidian for blades.

They are farmers and artisans of the geothermal vents. Their primary food source is the thick bacterial sludge that coats the city. They graze upon it with slow, deliberate scraping motions, their specialized mouthparts filtering the nutrient-rich slime. Yet, they also practice a form of agriculture. They actively tend the titanic tube worms, pruning the competing leathery fronds away from the worms' bases to ensure their primary building material—the calcified tubes—grows thick and straight.

Their movements are glacial. A single step is a calculated investment of energy against the viscous water. To watch them build their city is to watch a process akin to coral growth. A Lithovore might spend an entire cycle carrying a single shaped piece of tube-worm stalk to a new construction site, wedging it into place with a slow, powerful shove, and then cementing it with a paste made from masticated volcanic ash and their own adhesive saliva.

They are a silent people, saving their energy for survival. Their communication is conducted through the rock itself. They possess specialized organs on their undersides that can drum against the substrate, sending low-frequency vibrations—complex rhythmic patterns of clicks and thrums—through the continuous geology of the mountain. The city is constantly alive with this seismic language, a subterranean murmuring of instructions, warnings about temperature shifts, and communal agreements on where the next layer of dwellings must be built as the mountain beneath them breathes new land into existence.

They are ancient souls in a newborn land, patient gardeners of a high-pressure Eden, huddling together in their stacked stone hives against the encroaching cold of an endless ocean.
