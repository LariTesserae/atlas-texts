The inhabitants of this vertical crucible are not masters of their domain, but its most tenacious parasites. They are a small, specialized offshoot of humanity that has, over generations, become inextricably woven into the bio-mechanical tapestry of the gorges. They call themselves the "Pipe-Walkers" or sometimes, in the hushed tones used near the deepest vents, the "Children of the Hiss."

Their physical forms have been molded by the twin pressures of searing heat and perpetual dampness. They are lean and wiry, lacking any excess body fat that might cause overheating. Their skin, rarely exposed to the direct sun of the upper rims, is surprisingly pale, almost translucent in places, veined with thick purple blood vessels that help dissipate heat. Yet, this pallor is usually hidden beneath layers of grime—a permanent, striated coating of rust dust, sulfurized mud, and dried algae that serves as both camouflage and a protective barrier against the mineral-rich slime dripping from above. Their hands and feet are disproportionately large, heavily calloused, with thick, almost horn-like nails curved to grip hot metal piping and slick rock faces.

They wear the landscape. Their clothing is a hodgepodge of scavenged industrial materials cobbled together with fibers woven from the tough, silvery scrub-brush. Chest plates made from hammered-out copper gaskets protect them from sudden steam bursts. Gauntlets fashioned from heat-resistant ceramic cladding allow them to grip pipes that would sear ordinary flesh. Many wear bulky, modified breathing apparatuses over their noses and mouths—filtration masks scavenged from ancient lockers, fitted with charcoal filters to make the thick, sulfurous air breathable for long durations. Over their eyes, they wear goggles with lenses cut from thick, smoked glass to cut the glare when they ascend near the rims.

Their existence is entirely vertical. They inhabit the "middle zones" of the gorges—too hot near the rims, too wet and abyssal near the canyon floors. Their homes are precarious nests built into the dead spaces behind massive, silent gear works or inside gutted boiler casings that hang precariously over nothingness. They move through the chaotic lattice of pipes with the fluidity of primates in a jungle canopy. They know intuitively which pipes are merely painfully hot and which are lethal, reading the heat shimmer and the color of the oxidized metal like a map. They swing from gantries on ropes braided from wire and metallic vines, moving silently despite the heavy industrial materials they wear.

Their diet is derived solely from the unique ecosystem. They are patient hunters of the six-legged copper lizards, stalking them along the high-pressure mains and piercing their armored scales with spears tipped with sharpened tungsten shards. In the deeper, cooler shadows, they harvest the thick mats of thermophilic algae, drying it into nutrient-dense, albeit foul-tasting, bricks. They consider the leathery avians that nest in the cooling towers sacred, or perhaps just too dangerous to hunt, though they will scavenge fallen hatchlings.

They share this precarious existence with the only other "sentient" beings in the gorges: the ancient automatons. These are bulky, corroded maintenance drones that still trundle along rusted tracks or adhere to cliff faces on magnetic treads. These machines are half-blind, operating on degraded programming loops, endlessly welding patches onto groaning pipes or clearing mineral buildup from vents. The Pipe-Walkers treat these mindless machines with a strange reverence, stepping out of their way as one might avoid a large, indifferent animal. They see the drones as extensions of the great *thump-hiss* heartbeat of the earth, the immune system of the vast machine they live within.

The Pipe-Walkers are a quiet people. In an environment where sound carries for miles, silence is survival. They communicate in a complex sign language modeled on the flickering shadows of the steam plumes, supplemented by soft clicks and whistles that mimic the lizard chatter. To an outsider standing on the rim, looking down into the hazy purple abyss, the gorges would seem deserted. But if one looks closely, a patch of rust might shift against the flow of steam, a shadow might swing between two venting stacks, revealing the tenacious, adapted life clinging to the hot iron arteries of the world.
