This high-altitude crucible, screaming with wind and metallic resonance, is not merely inhabited; it is infected with life that possesses the resilience of geology and the appetite of lightning. The creatures here are not made of soft tissues and fluids, for those would be flayed and desiccated in seconds. They are lithic, metallic, and electro-trophic, an ecology built on abrasion and voltage.

The dominant lifeforms on the ground are the grazers, squat, octopedal entities that cling to the smoothed bedrock with grip strengths measured in tons. They are low-slung behemoths, armored in segmented plates of what appears to be biological magnetite and conductive hematite, shimmering with oily iridescence in the harsh light. Their movements are slow and deliberate; to lift a limb is to invite the wind to rip you from the cliffside. They move with a heavy, ratcheting *clack-clack-clack*, their carbide-tipped claws finding microscopic purchases in the polished stone, adding their own percussive rhythm to the roaring gale.

These creatures—let us call them "Resonators"—feed directly on the ferro-crystalline forests. They lumber toward the windward sides of the fractal trees where the dendritic growth is most frantic. Extending armored, conductive mandibles, they bite down on the crystalline lattices. The feeding is spectacular: a blinding arc of blue-white plasma jumps from the plant to the creature's mouthparts, a massive discharge of the static energy stored in the crystal. The Resonator shudders as the current grounds through its heavy body, its carapace glowing briefly with internal heat before it takes another agonizingly slow step toward the next meal.

Where there are grazers, there are predators. Hiding amidst the jagged thickets of cobalt-blue formations are the "Lodestone Lurkers." They are ambush predators, perfectly camouflaged as outcroppings of oxidized bronze and rust. They are flatter than the Resonators, splayed out like living bear traps made of corroded gears. They wait for a Resonator to wander too close in search of a charge. When they strike, it is faster than the eye can track—a seismic snap of metal jaws slamming shut, piercing the heavy armor of the prey to drink the residual electrical current from its dying neural pathways.

The air above is a different zone entirely, a realm of hyper-velocity violence. The sleek, delta-winged flyers mentioned before are not birds; they are projectiles with a metabolism. They are "Slate-Shrikes," blade-thin creatures whose skin is a composite of layered silicate and iron, honing itself against the abrasive wind. They have no eyes—eyes would be sandblasted instantly—but navigate via sensory pits along their leading edges that detect the electromagnetic signatures of the aerial plankton.

This plankton, the base of the aerial food chain, consists of clouds of semi-sentient, magnetized iron dust that swarm in the turbulent eddies. The Slate-Shrikes hit these clouds at hundreds of miles an hour, their mouths open slits lined with conductive baleen, filtering the metallic life from the air. They do not land. They cannot land. To stop moving is to die. They sleep on the wing, turning half their brain off at a time, forever banking and diving in the hurricane stream, a chaotic swirl of razor-edged shadows against the coppery sky.

Yet, the strangest inhabitants are those found in the anomalous pocket of silence near the broken, sound-swallowing archway. Here, where the wind is baffled and the screaming metallic choir is muted to a dull thrum, lives something delicate.

They are the "Archivists." In this relative quiet, they are spider-like, but built of pale, translucent ceramic rather than heavy metal. They have too many delicate limbs to count. They do not hunt, nor do they graze on the lightning. They scuttle silently over the sleek, unrecognizable surface of the ancient archway, tending to it. They use fine, cilia-like manipulators to clear away dust, to trace the unseen energy conduits of the dead technology. Perhaps they are repairing it, perhaps worshipping it, or perhaps they are merely parasites feeding on the faint gravimetric field it emits. They are the softest things on the peak, existing only because the archway allows them to, a small, silent priesthood surviving in the eye of an eternal, roaring storm.
