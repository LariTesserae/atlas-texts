If there are no animals and no sentience, what remains to inherit this suffocating, shrieking catacomb is life stripped bare of consciousness, existing purely as a massive, reactive biological process. It is not a single entity, but a sprawling, aggregate organism that has achieved a horrifying scale—the apex scavenger of heat and chemistry.

It has no name, for there is nothing here to name it. It is the Phage of the Geoforge.

At first glance, it is indistinguishable from the environment it consumes. It looks like particularly thick drifts of the pale Spanish-moss slime mold, or vast, weeping patches of rust on the iron columns. But if you watch the shadows long enough, against the jittering amber light, the architecture begins to heave.

The Phage is a decentralized, semi-solid colony of archaea and polymerized fungal matter that has evolved to treat the entire Geoforge as its exoskeleton. It is a viscous, translucent jelly, striated with veins of captured rust and sulfur that give it the appearance of flayed, diseased muscle tissue. It ranges in color from bile-yellow to deep, oxidized orange.

It does not have a centralized body. Instead, it coats things. It fills the colossal, unused pipes like arteries of living mucus. It plasters itself against the groaning pistons, lubricating the metal-on-calcified-metal grind with its own oily secretions, ensuring the machinery keeps moving solely to generate the friction-heat it craves. It drapes over the bracket fungi, slowly dissolving them into a nutrient slurry that it absorbs through its immense, porous surface.

It moves, but only at the glacial pace inherent to the Geoforge. It does not scuttle; it flows under high viscosity. A pseudopod of dense, trembling jelly might take three days to extrude from a ceiling pipe and bridge the gap to a geothermal vent, attracted by the supreme heat. Once connected, it pulses slowly, a mindless, throbbing conduit drawing thermal energy directly from the rock.

It has no eyes, but every inch of its countless tons of biomass is acutely sensitive to thermal gradients and chemical concentrations. It recoils slowly from sudden bursts of steam, only to creep back as the vapor settles, harvesting the mineral condensate. It has no mouth, yet it eats everything. It slowly dissolves the iron pillars, incorporating the metal into its own structure until parts of the colony are hard as carapace, while others remain soft as sputum.

It is deaf, indifferent to the industrial shrieking around it. It is mute, adding only a wet, pervasive squelching sound to the cacophony, audible only during the rare lulls between steam blasts.

The Phage is the true gardener of this toxic Eden. As it moves, it brushes against the leathery puffballs, triggering the release of the rust-colored spores. The heavy clouds coat the jelly’s exterior, hitching a ride to new locations within the cramped chambers before sloughing off to begin new fungal forests.

It is a mindless, colossal stomach draped over a dead civilization’s engine room, forever cycling heat, rust, and rot into sheer, unthinking biomass. It is the only thing that could possibly thrive here: a life form that doesn't just endure the hellish conditions, but requires them.
