The city is not empty. It is merely operating on a timescale and a scale of being that biology cannot perceive.

The inhabitants are the Architects of the kiln. They are geo-mechanical entities, distinct sentient beings forged from the very same materials that choke the basin floor. They are not born; they accrete over millennia, growing in the hottest geothermal vents like conscious geodes.

To the human eye, they are nearly invisible when stationary, easily mistaken for abstract sculptures or erratic boulders left in the cavernous plazas. They are colossal, semi-quadrupedal forms, low-slung and massive, resembling prehistoric tortoises armored in tectonic plating. Their "flesh" is a composite of heat-fused ceramics and volcanic glass, possessing the raw, scorched texture of the city walls. Veins of conductive copper and iron run just beneath their carapace, glowing with the same dull, ruddy light as the silica cacti.

They have no faces, no eyes, no mouths. In this world of perpetual twilight and blinding heat, sight is irrelevant. Instead, their entire bodies act as seismographs and thermal receptors. They "see" the world through the deep booming of the expanding rock and the roaring thermal updrafts. They navigate by sensing the minute temperature differentials between a wall of obsidian and an open avenue.

Their movement is the source of the city’s deepest groans. They do not possess muscles. They move through controlled, localized thermal expansion. By diverting their internal geothermal heat to specific limbs constructed of stacked metallic plates, they cause the metal to expand violently, ratcheting a massive leg forward with the grinding shriek of tectonic friction. When the heat dissipates, the limb contracts with a deafening clang.

They move with agonizing slowness—a single stride might take an hour—but with unstoppable momentum. When they cross a plaza, the polished obsidian floor spiderwebs under their unimaginable tonnage. They plow through the mineral jungle without pause; the ticking copper ferns and glass-sharp brambles shatter against their hulls in explosions of crystalline dust, turning the "vegetation" back into the raw materials of the basin floor.

This explains the city's design. The colossal scale, the hundreds of feet wide avenues, are necessary to accommodate their bulk and their turning radius. The windowless buildings are not dwellings, but resonant chambers. The Architects enter these vast, empty shells and press their bodies against the stone, using the structures as amplifiers to broadcast seismic language to their kin across the basin—a slow-motion conversation of grinding frequencies that vibrates the marrow of any interloper.

They have no culture of clutter, no need for tools. Their bodies are hammers and anvils. They are the tenders of the geothermal vents, ensuring the heat continues to flow, feeding the mineral growth that they, in turn, crush and recycle. They are immortal, barring catastrophic structural failure. When one finally ceases to function, its internal fires cooling after eons, it simply stops moving, instantly becoming another fixture of the landscape, a statue dedicated to its own endless, burning existence.
