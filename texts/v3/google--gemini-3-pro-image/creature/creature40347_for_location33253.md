To exist here is to be a creature of supreme grip and relentless noise. The biological density dictates that nothing lives alone; every organism is functionally a surface for another organism to inhabit.

The primary occupants of this cellulose archipelago, in terms of sheer biomass and sonic dominance, are the arthropods. They have scaled up to match their environment. The air is choked with the "Saw-Wing," iridescent beetles the size of serving platters whose carapace edges are serrated blades. They drift in clumsy, shimmering clouds on the warm updrafts, their low-frequency droning providing the bass note to the world’s cacophony.

Yet, the ear-splitting shrieking comes from the "Bark-Speakers." These are cicada-like entities clinging to the fluted wooden pillars, their bodies the size of a human torso. They possess ribbed, chitinous chests that they vibrate to produce a sound so piercing it physically hurts unclimatized ears. They are the engines of the noise, drilling into the hardened sapwood to drink, screaming their location to potential mates over the void.

Owning the damp, misty spaces between the canopy crowns are the avian reptiles. They are leather-winged throwbacks, pterosaur-like creatures with oily, water-shedding skin instead of feathers. Their colors are muted greens and rusty browns to match the wet bark. They possess hooked beaks designed to crack the armored shells of the giant beetles and powerful, taloned hind legs to anchor themselves against the swaying branches during the daily cloud-passages. Their calls are raucous, grinding shrieks that cut through the insect drone.

Below them, swinging through the dense mid-canopy in the perpetual twilight beneath the giant leaves, are the primates. They are heavy-set creatures, built for security over speed, covered in thick, hydrophobic fur that always smells of mildew. They have evolved elongated forearms and fingers that hook permanently, allowing them to hang effortlessly for hours. To navigate the terrifying gaps, some species have developed membranes between their limbs, allowing for desperate, committed glides between bio-islands. Their contribution to the soundscape is a resonant, booming chest-thumping hoot that serves to mark territory in a world where territory is a three-dimensional maze.

The "floor" of this world—that deep carpet of squelching moss covering the calcified roots—hides the silent ones. Here, ambush predators the size of jaguars lurk. They are highly specialized arachnids, "Moss-Maws," whose backs are entire micro-ecosystems of fungi and liverworts. They wait, perfectly camouflaged, by the peaty freshwater pools, waiting for a thirsty primate or a careless glider to land. They are the only quiet things in the realm, and their silence is a threat.

And then, clinging stubbornly to the underside of this chaos, are the people.

They call themselves the Wood-Borne. Physically, they have diverged from terrestrial humanity. They are leaner, their bone structure denser to cope with the thin air and the constant demands of climbing. Their arms are disproportionately long, their fingers tipped with thick, keratinous pads that find purchase on slime-slicked bark. Their toes are prehensile, gripping the braided creeper bridges with the assurance of a tightrope walker. Their skin is dark and oily, resistant to the rot that pervades everything.

Their eyes are large, perhaps to take in the terrifying scale of their home without succumbing to the vertigo that would paralyze a ground-dweller. They do not look down into the white void; their gaze is fixed horizontally or upward, reading the subtle shifts in the mist that signal weather changes or scanning the complex architecture of branches for routes.

Their culture is one of intense, localized focus within an infinite expanse. They live like highly intelligent wasps, building their homes—globular baskets of woven, still-living vines—glued to the sheltered overhangs of major branches. They survive by harvesting the thick, sugary sap of the world-trees, tapping into the veins of the behemoth they live upon, and hunting the giant arthropods for protein and armor plating.

Their existence is one of constant maintenance and negotiation with the plant life. The stairs carved into the trunk-mountains must be constantly re-cut as the living wood slowly tries to heal over the wounds. The vine bridges must be pruned lest they grow too heavy and snap, plunging an entire community into the nothingness below.

They are a loud people, for they must shout to be heard over the roar of the Bark-Speakers. They communicate across the smaller gaps with complex drum codes beaten onto hollowed-out protrusions of resonant wood. They are animists in the truest sense; to them, the "ground" is god—a slow, uncaring, growing deity that provides shelter and sustenance, but which could shrug them off into the abyss at any moment. They live not *on* the land, but *within* a living organism, parasites and caretakers on the skin of a green giant, forever damp, forever deafened, and forever holding on.
