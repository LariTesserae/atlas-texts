The inhabitants of the Undertwine are not occupants; they are symbiotes, parasites, and vital organ systems within the greater colossal host. They did not arrive here; they emanated from the same unrecognizable seed that spawned this bio-mechanical abyss. Like the environment, they are defined by cold moisture, absent light, and the relentless, thrumming metabolism of the city-root.

At the lowest trophic level, skittering along the slick, furrowed "streets" and clinging to the undersides of the calcified bridges, are the **Ferro-Carapaces**. These are the creatures hinted at—arthropods the size of starved dogs, whose segmented armor is not merely chitin, but a biomineralized lattice of iron and copper harvested from the mineral-rich sap. They look like corroded industrial refuse given jerky, multi-legged animation. Their primary function is maintenance and parasitism; they swarm over the massive, pulsing amber conduits, their blackened mandibles rasping away fungal blooms that threaten to choke the flow. Where a conduit springs a leak, they cluster in tight, vibrating knots, drinking the high-pressure nectar and sealing the breach with rapidly hardening secretions that look like welds of rusted metal. They are silent save for the wet *click-clack* of their heavy claws on the damp wood.

Occupying the vast, empty volumes of air in the cathedral-naves are the **Mycelial Hominids**. These are the pale, eyeless "ghosts." They are painfully thin, their limbs elongated to an impossible degree, jointed in ways that allow them to fold against the ribbed towers or swing effortlessly through varying gravitational fields. They have no hair; instead, their flesh—which has the texture of cold, damp puffball mushroom skin—sprouts patches of the same violet bioluminescent fungi that light the city. This renders them walking constellations, visible only as dim patterns of bruised light drifting through the upper darkness. They have no mouths, absorbing the thick, nutrient-laden aerosol of the atmosphere directly through their porous skin. They are the silent watchers, drifting fluidly through the canopy of pipes, their minds perhaps linked in a slow, fungal consciousness that mirrors the vast network they inhabit.

Where the "reverse-erosion" is most active, you will find the **Cambium Slugs**. These represent the brute force of the Undertwine’s growth. Massive, amorphous entities of dense, muscular jelly, they can grow to the size of subway cars. They are blind, guided solely by the subsonic vibration of the city’s digestion. They move with agonizing slowness over older, blackened structures, their undersides secreting powerful enzymes that dissolve the dead cellulose. As they consume the old city in front, their trailing ends excrete thick, steaming layers of fresh, green-white cambium tissue, which rapidly hardens into new walls and floors. They are living extrusion machines, leaving trails of slick, newborn architecture in their wake.

Finally, there are things that are less biological and more tied to the thinning reality of the place. The **Umbral Leeches** are not made of matter, but of cold spots and absence. They are the detached shadows the environment produces. Two-dimensional pools of absolute darkness that slide along the wet surfaces against the prevailing light patterns. They are drawn to heat and complex neural activity. Should a traveler find themselves in the Undertwine, an Umbral Leech might slide up their leg, not biting, but sinking into their shadow, causing a profound, soul-numbing chill and a creeping sense of dissociation, feeding off the victim's anchoring in conventional spacetime until they, too, become untrustworthy in their distances and gravity.
