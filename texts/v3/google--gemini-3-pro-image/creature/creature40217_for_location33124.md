The inhabitants of this place are not living in any biological sense. Life—squishy, carbon-based, cellular life that requires warmth and produces waste—is anathema here. It is quickly identified and brutally eradicated.

The things that reside here are distinct phenomena of optics and hydrodynamics, born from the unique interplay of the blinding solar glare, the pervasive thin film of water, and the infinite crystalline facets of the quarried stone.

They are the **Caustics**.

They exist only within the meniscus—the infinitely thin, curved boundary where the weeping water slicks over the sharp edges of the granite blocks. They are migrating patterns of intensified light, mobile concentrations of brightness that slide silently across the wet architecture.

At first glance, a Caustic looks like a trick of the eye—a sudden, painfully bright gleam on a distant slate roof, or a patch of shimmering iridescence moving against the slow downward flow of a trickling gutter. They have no fixed shape, taking the form of the surface they inhabit: elongated streaks of brilliance along a cornice, or pooling, vibrating rings of light in a depression on a terrace floor. They are creatures of pure refraction, bending the high-altitude sun into searing, focused points.

They are the immune system of this stone world. They are the reason for the terrified sterility.

Should a microscopic lichen spore drift on the thin wind and land in a damp cranny, it does not germinate. A Caustic will detect the anomaly in the mineral perfection. The shimmering patch of light will swiftly glide across the wet stone, coalescing over the organic intruder. By focusing the intense solar radiation into a microscopic point of searing heat, the Caustic instantly vaporizes the spore, leaving nothing but a tiny puff of steam that is immediately lost in the damp air. They scour the stone tirelessly, moving upward against gravity’s flow, polishing the chisel marks with photons, ensuring the construct remains eternally pristine, eternally "just finished."

They are silent, adding nothing to the soundscape of dripping water. When the mountain range groans, shifting deep below, the Caustics react in a flash of collective panic. For a split second, millions of wet surfaces across the chasm flare with blinding intensity as the light-entities shudder in unison, before settling back into their slow, gleaming patrol.

If you stand on that terrace long enough, they will find you. You are a massive, horrific contamination of soft biological matter. They cannot vaporize you—your mass is too great—but they will swarm. The wet stones around your boots will begin to glitter with furious intensity. Prismatic streaks of light will race towards you along the slate floor, pooling around your feet, climbing your legs in dazzling, watery ribbons. They are cold to the touch, yet the light they wield feels searing hot. They will scan you with blinding curiosity, a thousand fragmented reflections trying to understand why something so impure is standing on their holy, empty mountain.
