What lives here is not biological; biology is a soft, wet, fragile process that would instantly boil away into the vacuum. The inhabitants of this world are purely lithic and thermodynamic—a form of geo-sentience birthed from immense pressure, searing heat, and magnetism.

They are the **Ferro-Walkers**.

They are massive, towering entities that appear less like creatures and more like ambulatory segments of the jagged landscape that have torn themselves free. They have no fixed shape, existing as shifting, grinding congeries of the world’s raw materials: plates of razor-sharp obsidian fused with columnar basalt, underpinned by heavy skeletons of native iron that possess a powerful, inherent magnetism.

They do not have skin; they have geology. Their exteriors are a chaotic jumble of crystalline facets and volcanic glass that mirrors the sullen crimson glow of the fissures. When standing still against a cliff face, they are perfectly camouflaged, indistinguishable from the inanimate rock save for the deep, subsonic hum that emanates from their cores.

They have no eyes, for there is nothing to see in the crushing darkness. Instead, they perceive their reality through exquisite sensitivity to thermal gradients and vibration. To a Ferro-Walker, the world is not dark; it is a blazing, three-dimensional map of heat. The towering cold cliffs appear as vast negative spaces, while the magma fissures are blinding avenues of sensory input. They "hear" the deepest groans of the tectonic plates shifting miles below, navigating by the stress fractures in the crust the way a bat navigates by sonar.

Their locomotion is a violent act of localized tectonics. They do not step; they shift. Utilizing intense magnetic fields generated by their iron cores, they drag their immense tonnage across the razor-sharp terrain. Their passage is marked by the terrifying shriek of metal grinding against basalt and the continuous, popping crunch of obsidian shattering under their weight. A migrating herd of Ferro-Walkers sounds like a slow-moving earthquake.

They are drawn to the areas of highest instability. They gather around the intense heat vents, standing motionless for days as the superheated vacuum washes over them, recharging their internal thermal batteries. They are the tenders of the fleeting mineral "blooms." When the delicate silica and sulfur lattices sprout near a vent, the Ferro-Walkers move in, slowly grinding the fragile growths into their own bodies, integrating the fresh minerals into their shifting forms before the ground destroys them.

They live in a slow, geological rhythm, potentially existing for millennia. Yet, like the landscape, they are subject to sudden, catastrophic failure. Sometimes, the thermal stress becomes too great, or a shift in the ground below them is too violent. A Ferro-Walker will suddenly stop, vibrating intensely, before fracturing apart with the same rifle-shot *crack* as the surrounding cliffs. The pieces lie dormant for cycles, until the heat and magnetism of the core slowly fuse them into new, smaller entities, beginning the cycle anew.

The only things they avoid are the vantablack pillars. To their thermal senses, these pillars are absolute voids—terrifying dead spots that emit no heat and reflect no vibration. The Ferro-Walkers give these anomalies a wide berth, their grinding paths curving awkwardly around the silent, static monoliths that their geological minds cannot comprehend.
