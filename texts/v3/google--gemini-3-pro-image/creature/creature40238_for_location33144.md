They are the Curators, though they do not know themselves by that name, nor do they possess language to name anything at all. They are the reason the stasis is perfect; they are the agents of this terrified equilibrium.

They are immense, ponderous entities, born not of biology but emerging from the complex interplay of exhausted physics and geology that defines the plateau. They are not built; they have accreted. Their bodies are amalgamations of the city itself—huge, smoothed nodules of ancient bronze, fused obsidian, and heat-warped girders that have coalesced into vaguely humanoid, mountainous shapes. They tower nearly as high as the lower edifices, monumental rough-hewn sculptures weighing thousands of tons.

Like everything else here, time has eroded their definition. They have no faces, only smooth, sloping surfaces of pocked iron where a head might be. They have no distinct limbs, but rather thick, columnar extensions of their mass that serve as legs, joints fused and hidden beneath overlapping plates of meteorite-smoothed armor.

They do not eat. They exist solely on the thermal gradients of the plateau. They are mobile heat-sinks, migrating with glacial patience toward the dying geothermal vents, absorbing the dull radiation into their dense cores, and then moving away again when saturated. To stand near one is to stand next to an open furnace door; they radiate an intense, dry heat that warps the brittle air around them.

They are utterly silent. Despite their immense weight, their movement generates no sound. Perhaps they glide on cushion fields generated by dying internal reactors, or perhaps their very composition absorbs vibration before it can become sound. They drift across the dead plazas like thunderclouds made of iron, their passage marked only by a slightly wider wake in the omnipresent metallic dust.

Their purpose is preservation. They are thralls to the aesthetic of stasis. They move through the silent, dark avenues, their eyeless facades scanning for imperfections—a crack in a cyclopean wall, a gear that has begun to sag from its axle, a patch of pavement threatened by a micrometeorite impact.

When they find damage, they do not repair it with tools. They repair it with presence. A Curator will position its massive, heat-radiating body against a fractured bulkhead and simply remain there. It might stay for a century, or a millennium, its internal furnaces radiating intense thermal energy into the surrounding structure, slowly, painstakingly re-welding the metal through sheer, localized heat and pressure. They sacrifice their stored energy to fuse the city back together, terrified of things falling apart.

They are few, perhaps only a dozen across the entire sprawling vista, separated by distances so vast they may never encounter one another. If they did, they would not acknowledge the meeting. They are solitary automata of grief, tending the corpse of the world, ensuring that the museum of kinetic potential remains forever frozen, forever warm, and forever dead.
