This is a world where geology has finished its work, and biology has taken over the monstrous task of filling the silence. The inhabitants of this flattened earth are products of an environment with zero verticality, infinite moisture, and relentless heat. They are creatures built for endurance, not speed; for wallowing, not climbing.

The fundamental basis of life here is the slime. It is not merely vegetation; it is a living geological layer in its own right. The polished bedrock is treacherous with it. The algal mats are thick, gelatinous carpets that tremble slightly with their own metabolic activity in the hot sun. The neon greens and bloody reds are not static; they are churning, microscopically violent battlegrounds of bacteria and protozoa, generating the faintly metallic, rotting smell that pervades the humid air. This living mucus is the pasture upon which almost everything else grazes.

Teeming within the ankle-deep, tepid soup are myriads of invertebrates that have remained unchanged for countless millennia. The silt pockets are alive with segmented, eyeless worms the thickness of a thumb, processing the sludge endlessly. Clouds of oversized, bivalved crustaceans, like giant seeds, drift through the sluggish channels, filtering the opaque water with feathery appendages. On the rock surfaces, armored slugs the size of dinner plates scrape away the colorful bacterial mats with rasping mouths, leaving temporary trails of clean grey stone that are quickly reconsumed by the growth.

The dominant vertebrates are the amphibian giants, the lords of the horizontal. They are the creatures that haul themselves onto the wet slabs, their slate-grey, pebbled hides indistinguishable from the iron-grey bedrock. These are not sleek amphibians; they are squat, wide bodies of dense muscle and heavy bone, built low to the ground to conserve energy in the crushing humidity. Their limbs are thick and stubby, ending in broad, splayed webbed feet designed to adhere to slick rock, not to run. When they move, it is a slow, laborious drag, belly harsh against the stone, a wet scraping sound that carries far over the water. They sit for days in a semi-conscious stupor, milky eyes lidded against the glare, their throat sacs slowly pulsing as they respire the saturated air.

In the deeper channels, the things that create the oily V-wakes remain mostly legendary, known only by their sonic output and their displacement. These are huge, flattened fish-creatures, evolutionary dead-ends that found paradise in this stagnant endlessness. They are sluggish, armored cylinders of flesh with crushing jaw plates designed to crack the giant crustaceans or take chunks out of a careless amphibian's tail. Their bellows—deep, resonant, vibrating tones that you feel in your chest more than hear—are territorial declarations that ripple the surface of the still pools for hundreds of yards. When they feed near the surface, it is an explosive, muddy violence that subsides as quickly as it began.

The only things that occupy the space above the water are the architects of the deafening noise. The air is thick enough to support insects of colossal proportions. The dragonflies are terrifying, shimmering engines of predation with two-foot wingspans of rigid, veined membrane. They do not flit; they patrol with the mechanical hum of low-flying aircraft, their multifaceted eyes scanning the water for anything that breaks the surface. They are hunted in turn by clumsy, fist-sized robber flies that tackle them mid-air with an audible *thwack*.

And beneath the high-pitched whine of wings is the droning bass of the beetles. They are chitinous dreadnoughts, heavily armored and iridescent purple and green in the damp light. They fly with little grace, crashing through the heat haze like airborne stones, seeking out the rare, lonely reeds to lay their eggs, their passage marking the humid atmosphere with trails of low-frequency sound.

In this flat, wet eternity, life is heavy, slow, and unbelievably loud, a screaming, croaking, buzzing testament to survival at the monotonous end of time.
