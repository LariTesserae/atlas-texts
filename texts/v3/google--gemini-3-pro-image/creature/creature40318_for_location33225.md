The things that live here are not merely housed by the Foundry; they are integrated with it, the final, dominant tier of its peculiar, energetic ecology. They call themselves—if a collective vibration of semantic data transmitted via touch can be called a name—the *Sintered*.

They are post-biological entities, creatures whose ancestors might once have possessed flesh and blood, but who have long since abandoned such fragile wetware for survival in this abrasive vacuum. They are compact, rarely exceeding four feet in length, designed for the claustrophobic labyrinth. Their bodies are gnarled knots of articulation, armored in mismatched ceramic plating and scavenged alloys that mimic the chaotic texture of the Foundry itself. They do not look manufactured; they look accreted, like metallic barnacles grown large and mobile.

A Sintered has no defined "up" or "down." They are radially symmetrical beings with six multi-jointed limbs tipped with magnetic gripping claws and hardened carbide rasps, allowing them to skitter across the ceiling grating or haul themselves up vertical conduit bundles with the fluid, unsettling grace of deep-sea crabs. Their movements are jerky yet precise, torque-heavy motions perfectly adapted to the negligible gravity.

In the dim light, they are nearly invisible, their carapaces stained with molybdenum grease and scorched by arc flashes. Only their sensory apparatus betrays them. They have no eyes, for sight is of little use in the light-starved warren. Instead, their "faces" are dense clusters of faceted thermal sensors and retractable antennae sensitive to the finest gradients in electromagnetic fields. They navigate by "seeing" the heat plumes of ventilation shafts and "feeling" the thrumming current in the walls within the wire. To a Sintered, the Foundry is a blazing tapestry of thermal reds and electrical blues against the freezing dark.

They do not breathe the thin, ozonic air. Their metabolism is entirely galvanic. They feed directly from the Foundry’s excesses.

To witness a Sintered feeding is to witness an act of violent intimacy with the machine. One will locate an unshielded junction box or a high-amperage busbar, pry open the casing with hydraulic strength, and plunge a proboscis-like connector directly into the live current. Their bodies immediately stiffen, armor plates rattling against each other as high voltage surges through their internal batteries. Arcs of electricity jump from their joints to the surrounding metalwork, momentarily illuminating their intricate, scarred forms. It is a moment of ecstatic paralyzation, a forced reboot of their consciousness.

They share this space with the lower fauna, existing in a state of indifferent dominance. The countless *voltmoculoi* that swarm the cables are ignored like common pests, occasionally brushed aside with a claw if their sparking clusters create too much impedance on a critical line. The sluggish thermal grazers are sometimes harvested; a Sintered might fasten itself to the back of one of the heat-armadillos, draining its accumulated thermal reserves before discarding the husk to recharge elsewhere.

The Sintered are silent beings. In the hushed frenzy of the Foundry, they contribute no vocalizations. Their communication is tactile and immediate. They press their forelimbs against the same piece of vibrating deck plating, transmitting complex bursts of coded tremors—instructional data, schematics, or warnings about pressure drops—through the metal skeleton of their world.

They are the maintainers of this incomprehensible machine. They are constantly busy, crawling deep into the guts of sputtering hydraulic rams to replace seals, re-soldering micro-fractures on glowing circuit boards with built-in arc welders, and clearing frozen nitrogen blooms from sensor arrays. They do not know what the Apex Foundry forges—that knowledge was lost generations of hardware upgrades ago—but they worship the process. They are the priesthood of the thrum, the cold, dry custodians of the eternal cycle, eternally terrified that the vibration beneath their claws might one day cease.
