This is a realm where life has abandoned biology as we know it and embraced geology, engineering itself not for growth, but for sheer structural integrity against the crushing weight and the violent, endless thrashing of the ocean’s internal tides.

The base of this ecosystem, the "grass" of the Ossuary Plateau, are the Osteo-Scrapers. These are the flattened creatures clinging to the bulbous towers. Up close, they resemble colossal, segmented chitons or trilobites stripped of all delicate appendages. Ranging from dinner-plate size to behemoths thirty feet long, their dorsal side is a seamless shield of mineralized hide, nearly identical in texture and color to the ancient bone they adhere to. They are camouflage brought to the level of architecture; when stationary, they are indistinguishable from the landscape.

They move with agonizing slowness, inching forward on thousands of microscopic, hydraulic tube-feet that generate vacuums strong enough to resist the shearing currents. Beneath their armored skirt lies a mouth that is nothing but a grinding apparatus. Utilizing radulas studded with crystalline teeth—harder than diamond—they rasp away at the calcified substrate. The sound of a billion scrapers feeding is a constant, high-pitched shriek that underpins the low thrum of the water, a noise like steel wool scouring iron. They digest the ancient minerals, excreting a fine, silty paste that is immediately snatched by the currents—the essential fertilizer for the open-water life.

In the spaces between the arches, where the water churns in chaotic vortices, live the Current-Riders. These are the translucent, eel-like schools. They are ghosts in the gloom, bodies made of dense, clear muscle and connective tissue, lacking eyes or internal organs that would crush under pressure. They are living rheostats. When a massive internal wave slams through a canyon, they do not fight it. Instantaneously, they snap their ribbon-like bodies into rigid, geometric shapes—v-forms, boxes, stiff coils—turning themselves into organic hydrofoils. They ride the violence, hurtling hundreds of feet in seconds, their gaping, circular mouths held open. Inside these maws are intricate lattice-gills, filtering the bone-silt created by the scrapers and kicked up by the turbulence. They are a synchronized chaos of glass shards, forever falling through rushing water.

In the highest-velocity zones, where the shearing force of the water whirling around the smooth towers is enough to flay softer flesh, dwell the Lattice-Spiders. These are not arachnids, but ten-legged crustaceans that have evolved into living anchors. Their legs are impossibly long, thin, and jointed with locking mechanisms. They wedge themselves into the crevices between the fossilized exoskeletons, spanning gaps ten feet across. Once locked in, they are immovable. They spread a "web" between their forelegs—not of silk, but of excreted, rapid-hardening osseous mucus that forms a rigid, glassy grate. They wait for the current to hurl a hapless Current-Rider into their grate, the force of the water doing the work of killing the prey before the spider slowly dismantles it.

Cruising the dark periphery is the apex predator, the Resonance-Maw. It is the "larger shadow" detached from the cliff. It is a creature of horrifying bulk, perhaps sixty feet long, shaped like a flattened torpedo, clad in thick, pitted plates of bone-armor that make it look like animated geology. It has no eyes. Its entire massive, blunt head is covered in sensory pits sensitive to pressure differentials. It can "hear" the heartbeat of a scraper through ten feet of solid bone; it can feel the displacement of a Current-Rider school a mile away.

The Resonance-Maw does not hunt with speed. It hunts with inevitability. It drifts on the deep thrum of the currents, navigating the invisible roadways of pressure. Upon locating a large Osteo-Scraper clinging to a wall, the Maw approaches slowly, aligning its body with the current to minimize drag. It glides over the prey, sealing its cavernous, circular mouth over the entire armored shell of the victim. Then, with massive internal musculature, it creates a violent, localized hydraulic vacuum. The sound is deafening—a thunderous *CRACK* as the scraper’s suction is broken and it is ripped from the wall. The prey is sucked into a gullet lined with crushing plates, ground back into the bone dust from which it was ultimately made, the cycle of the Ossuary completing itself in cold, grinding darkness.
