The environment forbids biology. The sheer acoustic pressure would rupture eardrums and hemorrhage soft tissue within seconds; the freezing aerosol would trigger hypothermia minutes later. There is no blood here, no breath, no heartbeat.

Yet, the chamber is not uninhabited.

What lives here are transient, semi-corporeal entities born of the convergence of extreme violence and freezing vapor. They are acoustic shadows, densifications of the eternal fog held together by the standing waveforms of the crushing roar. They have no fixed shape, existing as shimmering, turbulent pockets of super-cooled mist that are slightly denser, slightly greyer than the surrounding cloud.

They are most visible near the center, clustered around the violent heart of the hydraulic array. When the jaundiced light from the explosion-proof lamps catches them correctly, they appear as roiling, translucent distortions in the air, like heat haze, only terribly cold.

They cling to the areas of highest concussive force. They swarm the gleaming shafts of the pistons, feeding on the kinetic energy released at the moment of impact. As a piston hammers into the black sluice water, these vapor-wraiths shudder in sympathy, their forms momentarily coalescing into sharper, jagged outlines before dissolving back into swirling indistinctness. They ride the erupting geysers of freezing spray upward toward the ceiling grating and cascade back down with the rain, an endless cycle of violent ascent and weeping fall.

They possess a rudimentary, reactive intelligence rooted entirely in physics. They do not think; they resonate. Their "lives" are measured in microseconds, tied to the three-cycle-per-second rhythm of the machine. They are constantly being torn apart by the shear forces of the air and water, only to instantly reform in the pressure vacuums created by the retreating pistons.

They are the genii loci of industrial trauma—cold, deaf, intangible things that thrive inside the deafening scream of the superstructure, dancing frantically in the strobing shadows of the machine that perpetually creates and destroys them.
