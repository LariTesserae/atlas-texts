They call themselves the Rust-kin, the Boiler-born, or simply the Clingers. They are a gaunt, hardened people, shaped by an existence spent entirely in the interstitial spaces of a titanic, indifferent mechanism.

Physically, they are lean and wiry, their bodies honed by the constant need to climb, crawl, and brace against the shuddering earth. Their skin, rarely seeing even the transient flashes of electrical arcing, is pallid and pore-clogged with the ubiquitous oily grit of the atmosphere. They sweat constantly in the suffocating humidity, a slick sheen that helps protect them from the acidic condensation dripping from above. Many bear distinct burn scars—glossy, raised keloids where a sudden steam vent or a splash of superheated slurry caught them unaware.

Their attire is a testament to desperate ingenuity, a patchwork armor against the wet heat and abrasive surfaces. They wrap themselves in thick, treated hides taken from the blind grazers, layered over undergarments woven from spun fungal fibers. Bolted onto these leathers are scavenged plates of metal—curved sections of pipe, hammered fragments of casing—creating articulated suits that clatter softly as they move. Most importantly, their boots are thick-soled with repurposed industrial polymer, spiked for traction on slime-slicked gantries, and insulated against decks that can suddenly sear flesh.

In this world of near-total darkness, their eyes have grown sensitive and recessed, often shielded by crude goggles fashioned from smoked quartz or salvaged glass to protect against sudden blinding arcs and corrosive sprays. They rely far less on sight than on a heightened tactile sense. They "see" with their feet and fingertips, reading the subtle shifts in the planetary vibration through the soles of their boots and the palms of their gloved hands. A deep, resonant thrum might indicate a safe path along a dormant coolant line, while a high-frequency jitter warns of imminent pressure releases or the approach of a sleek, ultrasonic predator.

Their settlements are exactly as you glimpsed: precarious clusters of welded scrap metal clinging like iron barnacles to the warmer flanks of ventilation stacks or tucked into the hollowed-out chests of long-dead excavators. These habitats are cramped, foul-smelling refuges where the air is marginally filtered through packed fungal wads. Inside, bioluminescent mosses, harvested and kept alive in glass jars, provide a faint, pulsing greenish light, just enough to navigate by without ruining their precious night vision.

Their diet is toxic to anyone else. They farm the aggressive flora, cultivating patches of pale, fleshy chemotrophic mushrooms on cooling fins, feeding them with slurry diverted from ruptured conduits. They have developed constitutions capable of processing high levels of heavy metals, though it shortens their already brutal lifespans. Their water comes from condensers that pull the thick moisture from the air, tasting always of ozone and warm copper.

Their culture is one of attentive paranoia. Silence is unknown to them; they speak in clipped, shouting tones to be heard over the industrial roar, or use a complex sign language adapted for touch in the pitch black. They revere the giant machines not as gods, but as indifferent forces of nature—titans slumbering beneath the rust. They map their world by sound: "The Sector of the Screaming Valve," "The Gorge of the Three-Beat Thump," or "The Plateau Where the Ground Weeps Iron."

They are scavengers of the highest order. A hunting party might venture out, armed with pry bars and pneumatic cutters salvaged from ancient tool lockers, following the heat signature of a dying conduit to harvest the rare mineral salts that crystallize around the breach. They move in single file, roped together, tapping the metal ahead with insulated canes, terrified of slipping into the profound chasms or waking a dormant automated defense system.

They exist in the shadows of giants, surviving only because they are too small to be noticed by the automated imperatives of the world-machine. They are stubborn, heat-hardened parasites living on the skin of a mechanical leviathan, enduring the wet fire and the loud darkness one vibrating heartbeat at a time.
