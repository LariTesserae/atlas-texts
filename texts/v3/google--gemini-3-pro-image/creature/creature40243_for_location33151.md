This sunken amphitheater is not merely inhabited; it is composed of its inhabitants. The boundary between flora, fauna, and geography has dissolved in the eternal wetness.

The primary citizenry is a vast, gelatinous collective of the blind. In the deep twilight, evolution has deemed sight an unnecessary extravagance. The salamanders that slick the walls are colored only by the blood flowing beneath their translucent, permeable skins, appearing as shifting veins of pale rose against the dark green moss. They have no eyes, only shallow depressions in their flattened skulls, guiding themselves through the crush by the scent of rot and the endless vibrations of dripping water. They do not hunt in a conventional sense; they merely open their mouths and let the saturation of smaller life—the larvae, the minuscule worms—wash in.

Sharing the moss-scape are the slugs, creatures of monstrous proportion here. Turgid with water, they are thick as a man's thigh and longer than his arm, moving with agonizing slowness. They are the color of drowned flesh, coated in a mucilaginous slime so thick it pools around them as they graze eternally on the decaying underside of the fern canopy. They are living processors, churning the endless growth into the trembling peat below.

Moving between and over these soft bodies are the armored ones. The woodlice here have grown gigantic in the oxygen-rich, predator-free damp. They are the size of dinner plates, their segmented carapaces softened by the constant moisture into the consistency of wet leather. They are the only source of sharp sound, a soft, multitudinous clicking that underpins the sound of dripping water as thousands of them mill tirelessly over the living slopes.

But beneath this seething, visible surface, down deep in the trembling sponge of the central peat bed, lives the anchor of the hollow's stasis.

It has been there perhaps as long as the cliffs themselves. It is a singular entity, a massive, sedentary amphibian that has grown to fill the lowest depression of the bowl. It is vast, bloated, and utterly immobile, a immense heap of wrinkled, fungal-white flesh that is almost indistinguishable from the waterlogged peat surrounding it. It has no need to move; the bowl feeds it. The nutrient-rich runoff, thick with the waste of the millions above and the bodies of the dead, filters down into its waiting, cavernous maw, which rests permanently agape just beneath the surface of the mire.

It is the apex of this closed loop, a blind, sleeping god of digestion. Its slow, deep metabolism sets the tempo for the entire hollow. It breathes perhaps once an hour, a shuddering heave that sends a slow ripple through the peat bog, momentarily disturbing the millions of smaller creatures crawling above it. It is the embodiment of the equilibrium, the heavy, beating heart of the cold, wet, everlasting darkness.
