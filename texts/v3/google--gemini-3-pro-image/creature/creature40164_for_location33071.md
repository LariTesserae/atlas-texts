They are the Architects of Mica, the resonant dust-minds, entities so integrated into this desiccated apex that to the untrained eye, they are invisible. They are not flesh; the concept of soft tissue perished on this world eons ago. They are lithic beings, geological consciousnesses birthed from the very materials of the citadel.

Their bodies are towering, irregular columns of stacked slate and conductive mica sheets, mirroring the construction of the ziggurats they inhabit. They possess no true limbs, only articulated segments of laminated stone that shift and slide against one another with the dry sound of grinding tectonic plates. They are massive, some standing twenty feet tall, yet impossibly slender, brittle-looking things that sway subtly in the thin wind. Their "skin" is polished by abrasive grit to a mirror-sheen, reflecting the dim twilight and the chaotic ballet of orbiting debris above.

They do not breathe. They have no need for the thin, useless atmosphere. Instead, their geology is animated by a slow, internal piezoelectric current generated by the constant friction of their own movements and the stress of the shifting gravitational tides. Their "blood" is extremely fine, magnetized ferro-dust that circulates through capillary gaps between their stone layers.

Life for them is a meditation measured in geological epochs. They move with agonizing slowness. A single gesture—the tilting of a slate "head" or the extension of a segmented torso—might take a terrestrial week to complete. In the crushing silence of the vast plazas, they stand like statues, countless numbers of them, blending perfectly with the dry-stacked architecture.

Their primary engagement with the world is through the variable gravity. They are masters of their unstable environment. When a gravity storm approaches, lessening the pull, they do not float away helplessly. They bear down, increasing their internal magnetic density to anchor themselves to the bedrock. Conversely, when they need to build—to add another impossible layer to a soaring spire—they wait for an updraft. They detach massive slabs of rock and, using their bodies as resonant tuning forks, "sing" a sub-audible, vibratory hum that temporarily neutralizes the remaining mass of the stone, allowing them to guide tons of rock into a state of perfect, precarious balance before the gravity shifts again and locks it into place forever.

They are silent to human ears, but the rock beneath them constantly trembles with their ultra-low-frequency communication. It is a slow, communal dreaming shared through the marrow of the planet.

They are not merely inhabitants of the citadel; they are its immune system and its gardeners. They consume the abrasive dust, filtering minerals through their stratified bodies to repair their own plating, and excreting the excess as the geometrically perfect sulfur blooms that crust the leeward towers. They watch the Drifters in the sky with a passive, epochal indifference.

They are waiting. The entire citadel is not a city for habitation, but a vast, planet-sized resonator built through infinite patience, designed to capture and focus the gravitational chaos. They are slowly, deliberately, over millions of years, tuning their world's broken physics like a massive instrument, preparing for a final harmonic alignment that only they can foresee in the deep time of stone.
