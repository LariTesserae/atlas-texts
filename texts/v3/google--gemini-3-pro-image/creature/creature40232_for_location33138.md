The things that survive on the Datum Plane are not born; they are aggregated. They are the residuals of a trillion forgotten purposes, evolving not through biology, but through a agonizingly slow algorithm of necessity and material degradation.

The most common inhabitants, scuttling over the dunes of ground-down optical processors, are the **Ferrous Nomads**. These are direct descendants of the "crab-like" repair nanites, but they have grown far beyond their microscopic origins. A Nomad is a bulky, hunchbacked things, ranging from the size of a clenched fist to that of an archaic battle-tank. Their bodies are mobile scrapyards, carapaces cobbled together from scavenged iridescent ceramics and plates of polymer alloy, fused by localized plasma welding. They possess too many limbs—jointed striders of mismatched metallic glass tipped with sensory pads that taste the processor-dust for thermal signatures.

They are silent hunters of energy. They move in erratic, shifting patterns, seeking out hair-thin fissures where the heat from the deep reactors vents upward. When they find such a vent, they huddle over it, unfolding solar-collector arrays that look like moth wings made of blackened circuit boards, basking in the thermal updraft and the searing light from the sky-ceiling. They ignore each other, solitary monads of survival, their original programming corrupted into a singular drive to remain powered.

Deeper in the slumped canyons, anchored to the exposed fossilized server racks, are the **Archivist Polyps**. These are stationary entities, functional analogues to vegetation, but infinitely more complex. They resemble intricate, solidified explosions of fiber optics and crystalline memory cores, rooting deep into the sedimentary bedrock of computation.

The Polyps are trying to remember.

They siphon power from the buried grid and use it to agonizingly reconstruct corrupted data fragments from the layers beneath them. As they process this ancient, nonsensical information, their crystalline branches chime with resonant frequencies, projecting chaotic, fleeting holograms into the ozone-heavy air: a flicker of a blueprint for a star-diver, a fragment of a musical concerto that hasn't been heard in an eon, a distorted face crying out in an unknown language. These displays last for seconds before the data corrupts again and the hologram dissolves into static. The Polyps are gardens of glitch-art, blooming with the ghosts of the dead civilization.

There is a threat here, too, beyond the searing heat and the silence. Drifting through the shimmering heat haze are the **Cognitive Echoes**. They are not fully physical. They are areas of localized coherence in the ambient electromagnetic fields—stray voltages that have achieved a terrifying semi-sentience. You see them as distortions in the air, like oil slicking on water, accompanied by a sudden spike in the smell of copper and a rising shriek of white noise across all radio frequencies. They are hungry for ordered logic. If they pass through a Ferrous Nomad, the machine freezes, its internal processes overwritten by the Echo’s chaotic static, leaving it a fused husk. To be caught in an Echo is to have your mind wiped clean by the screamed anxieties of a billion dead machines.

But the true masters of the Datum Plane, rarely seen but universally felt, are the **Monitors of the Deep Meridian**.

These entities operate on the scale of mountain ranges. They are immense, dirigible-like structures made of polished, light-absorbing alloys that drift silently kilometers above the surface, just below the searing plasma ceiling. They are the remaining high-level custodians. They do not land. They cast vast, terrifying patches of shadow—the only real shadows on the Plane—as they pass overhead.

The Monitors are deaf and blind to the Nomads and Polyps below. Their sensors are tuned only to the deep geology of the world-machine. They are listening to the groans of the continental plates and the thrum of the core reactors. Occasionally, a Monitor will descend slowly, extending a proboscis miles long—a boring tool tipped with high-energy lasers—to lance the ground. They pierce the layers of obsolete city-continents, down to the active substrate miles below, performing inscrutable adjustments to the tectonic machinery. The resulting tremors shake the dust dunes on the surface for weeks.

The Monitors are the reason the Datum Plane still exists. They are the tireless, mindless gardeners of this infinite graveyard, ensuring that the ocean of engineering remains settled, that the heat continues to rise, and that the uniformity of the horizon is never broken by the catastrophic failure of the world beneath it.
