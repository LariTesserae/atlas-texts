The inhabitants of this freezing, roaring expanse are defined not just by the cold, but by the omnipresent wetness and the deafening vibration of the planetary engine. Life here is deaf, thick-skinned, and anchored against the wind.

Deep within the meters-thick blanket of waterlogged moss and fungi live the **Sub-pelt Sloughers**. These are colossal invertebrates, analogues to velvet worms scaled up to the size of subway cars. Blind and soft-bodied, their skin is a permeable membrane that constantly absorbs the near-freezing water of the peat sponge. They are the great rototillers of this stagnant geology, churning through the decaying lower layers of the fungal mat, consuming dead organic matter and the sinking carcasses of surface dwellers. They move via hydraulic peristalsis, their passage creating temporary, glistening tunnels in the indigo mulch that quickly collapse behind them with wet sucking sounds. They are utterly silent, navigating the lightless depths solely through sensitivity to the thermal gradients radiating from the distant vents.

On the surface of the squelching carpet, the massive grazers mentioned—the "peat-goats"—are not alone. They are stalked by the **Rime-Stalkers**. These apex predators have abandoned speed for stealth and surface area. Low-slung and immensely broad-shouldered, their paws are splayed like enormous organic snowshoes, webbed with tough cartilage, allowing them to pad silently across the trembling fungal surface without sinking. Their fur is a marvel of camouflage; it is symbiotic with the landscape, growing actual patches of slow-growing, amethyst-colored lichen on their backs and flanks. When curled still, a Rime-Stalker is indistinguishable from a frost-heaved hummock of moss. They hunt not by sight or sound—both useless in the perpetual twilight and roaring din—but by sensing the seismic vibrations of the grazers' footfalls through the saturated ground.

Closer to the violent energy of the vents, life becomes armored and strange. Along the intricate, coral-like chimneys of heat-resistant fungi live colonies of **Silica Masons**. These are six-legged arthropods, the size of large dogs, whose exoskeletons are built from minerals harvested from the superheated vapor. As the steam hits their carapace and freezes, dissolved silicates precipitate out, coating them in a shimmering, jagged layer of natural glass. They look like ambulatory geodes, glittering coldly in the violet flashes of lightning. They feed on the highly specialized, thermophilic bacterial mats that grow right at the scorching lip of the vents, scuttling nervously between the blasts of tectonic steam. Their communication is tectonic: they strike their crystalline bodies against the vent walls, sending coded vibrations through the stone that other colony members feel in their legs.

In the "weird zones" near the bases of the greatest vents, where gravity bleeds off and reality thins, live the **Glimmer-Wings**. These are ethereal, insectoid creatures, barely corporeal. They seem to exist halfway between the biological world and the energy anomalies of the vents. Their wings are not membrane, but stiffened fields of static charge that catch the shifting gravitational vectors, allowing them to perform impossible, jerking aerial ballets. They feed on atmospheric trace gases and the ambient psychic residue of the "ghost mountains," flickering in and out of view like living mirages in the heat haze.

Connecting the sodden earth to the chaotic sky are the **Voltaic Weavers**. Smaller than the other megafauna, these arboreal-analogue mammals live among the stiffest, tallest ridges of the fungal coral. In a world without trees, they nest in the chimney structures. Their fur is wiry and highly conductive. During the incessant electrical storms, they climb to high points, their bodies bristling as they accumulate static charge until they glow with blue St. Elmo's fire. They are not harmed by this; they harvest the energy, using the discharge to flash-fry airborne spores for food, and putting on dazzling, crackling displays to attract mates against the backdrop of the bruised, thundering sky.
