They are the architects of this frantic kiln, and they are indistinguishable from it.

At first glance, amidst the steam and the throbbing green fungal light, you see only shifting walls. Massive, uneven bulges of red mud seem to heave and reposition themselves in the claustrophobic gloom. It is only when a section of wall detaches itself with a wet, sucking sound and begins to crawl against the localized gravity that you realize you are looking at an inhabitant.

They are colossal, lumpen things, built to survive the crushing pressure and scalding temperatures of their own making. Their bodies are fundamentally crude, lacking symmetry or defined features. They resemble half-formed sculptures abandoned by a frustrated potter—great, monolithic torsos of calloused, heat-cracked hide that matches the baked-mud bastions perfectly. They steam constantly, their mountainous backs slick with condensation that runs in gray rivulets down their flanks.

They have no faces, for they have no need of senses that operate at a distance. There are no eyes to see through the blinding steam, no ears to parse the deafening grind. Instead, their "heads" are blunt, heavily armored battering rams, pitted with sensory pores that taste the metallic heat and feel the vibrational weakness in the structure around them.

Their limbs are where the "clumsy, urgent thumbs" are manifest. They possess four to six massive, hydraulic appendages that are less like arms and more like excavation equipment. These terminate not in hands, but in fused, shovel-like mitts of bone and ceramic-hard callus. They are clumsy tools, incapable of fine manipulation, designed only for slapping, crushing, and dragging. When they move through the lattice, they hook massive, curved claws—like rusted anchors—into the fibrous hemp-cables, hauling their bulk through the shifting gravitational fields with jerky, desperate strength.

The most terrifying aspect of these creatures is that they are living factories for the environment itself. They are the engine of this genesis.

They feed on the raw, chaotic matter erupting into the pocket reality. You might see one heave its bulk toward a fissure where "nothingness" is leaking in, pressing its faceless front against the void. Inside their massive, barrel-chested bodies—which glow with internal furnace-heat—they process this raw potential.

The building materials come from *them*.

The red mud is vomited up in boiling, viscous boluses. They heave forward, their entire bodies convulsing, and expel gallons of the superheated clay directly onto a framework. Then, using their spade-hands, they furiously pat and scrape it into place with manic, repetitive motions, creating the uneven, thumb-marked walls. The *shluck-shluck* sound is the rhythm of their digestion and excretion made manifest.

The fibrous cables are spun from spinnerets located beneath their heavy jowls. They extrude thick, sticky ropes of grayish protein that rapidly harden in the heat. They attach an end to a ceramic rib and then lurch backward into the void, groaning with exertion, stretching the fiber until it snaps tight, creating a new structural support.

They are utterly subsumed by their task. They do not communicate, they do not rest. They climb over one another with the indifference of stones in a landslide. The lush, parasitic vegetation that chokes the station grows on them as well; their backs are Often covered in mats of dark, succulent vines that drink the steam rising from their skin, and bioluminescent fungi sprout from the harder ridges of their armored shoulders. They are walking ecosystems, ignored hosts to the life their heat supports.

They are creatures of pure, mindless industry, driven by a biological imperative to fill the emptiness with heat, mud, and noise, forever building a cage that is growing faster than they can understand.
