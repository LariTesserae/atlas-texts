The entities that inhabit this drowned cathedral are not visitors; they are concretions arising from its pressurized, mineral-saturated womb. They are the **Ferro-Kelp Graziers** and the **Vent Wardens**, slow-moving sentient accretions that have evolved not just to survive the crushing depth, but to become indistinguishable from it.

They are enormous, sluggish beings, their physiology dictated by the immense weight of the warm ocean. They possess no gas-filled cavities to be crushed; instead, their bodies are dense, cartilaginous masses armored with thick, segmented plating. Over centuries of life, these plates absorb the iron and sulfur from the water, mineralizing into heavy, corroded carapaces that look exactly like the rusting bulkheads they cling to. Barnacles the size of shields grow upon their backs, and bioluminescent algae streak their flanks in stripes of electric teal, making them look like mobile sections of the industrial architecture.

Sight is an irrelevance here. In place of eyes, their armored heads are bristling arrays of sensory barbels and feathered antennae, similar in structure to the red-tipped tube worms. These organs act as sophisticated chemical spectroscopes, tasting the metallic water to navigate the intricate currents of heat and sulfur plumes. They can sense the electromagnetic fields radiating from the colossal cables, using the industrial grid as a roadmap through the murky twilight. They perceive their world not as shapes, but as a complex topography of temperature gradients, chemical trails, and the deep, vibrating thrum of the geothermal engines, which they feel reverberating through their dense cores.

They do not swim in the open water; the viscosity is too high, the energy expenditure too great. Instead, they are permanent climbers on the coral reef of machinery. They move with glacial deliberation along the corroded girders and thick pipes, gripping with multiple clawed limbs designed to puncture layers of bacterial matting and find purchase on the metal beneath. To watch one move is like watching a tectonic plate shift; it is a slow, grinding relocation accompanied by the groan of settling metal.

Their ecology is entirely chemosynthetic. The **Graziers** are the farmers of this dark world. They use rasping, mineralized mouthparts to scrape the thick, gelatinous purple algae from heat exchangers and consume the bone-white kelp that tangles in the power lines. Their digestive tracks are chemical furnaces, breaking down sulfur compounds and heavy metals to fuel their slow metabolisms.

The larger, older individuals become **Vent Wardens**. These immense creatures, sometimes the size of small habitats themselves, locate the hottest hydrothermal vents or unshielded geothermal conduits. They settle directly over the heat source, their thickest armor facing the superheated plume. They enter a state of near-suspended animation, becoming living caps on the vents, absorbing the direct thermal energy and rich mineral outflow. Over millennia, they may stop moving entirely, their bodies fusing with the surrounding rock and pipes through calcification, becoming living, breathing components of the city’s plumbing.

They are a silent species. The only sounds they make are the grinding of their shells against the infrastructure and a low-frequency drumming produced by vibrating internal membranes—a sound that blends seamlessly with the planetary heartbeat of the engines below. They are the blood cells of this sleeping industrial giant, keeping the pipes clean of excessive growth, capping dangerous pressure leaks with their own bodies, and existing in a timeless, heavy stupor, perfectly adapted to a world of warm darkness and crushing weight.
