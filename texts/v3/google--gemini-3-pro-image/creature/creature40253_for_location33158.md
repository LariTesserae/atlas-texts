This warm, oppressive dark is dominated by the **Mycelial-Crustaceans**, the silent, lumbering citizens of the bone metropolis.

They are creatures sculpted by crushing weight and absolute absence of sight, a hybrid biological solution to an impossible environment. They are gargantuan, flattened arthropods, their bodies shaped like irregular, rough-hewn pancakes of calcified armor, broad enough to span the narrower "streets" of the city. An adult might measure four meters across but only a few feet thick, designed to press low against the substrate, letting the heavy currents flow over them rather than against them.

Their exoskeletons are not smooth chitin, but rugged, porous landscapes that mimic the city itself. Their backs are studded with nodules, mineral accretions, and barnacle-like growths, making them look like detached pieces of the architecture that have begun to move. This rough hide is often fouled with the grey bacterial mats that grow near the vents, giving them a shaggy, decaying appearance, cloaking them in the scent of sulfur and silt.

They have no heads in the traditional sense, and certainly no eyes. The front edge of their massive, flattened bodies is fringed with a dense, trembling corona of sensory tendrils. These organs, pale and fleshy like drowned roots, act as their eyes, ears, and noses combined. They constantly "taste" the viscous water, reading complex chemical trails—the rot of a dead builder-organism, the metallic tang of a nearby vent, the pheromonal highway of their own kind.

Beneath this armored hood, a multitude of articulated legs, thick and stump-like, carry their immense bulk with deliberate slowness. They do not swim; the energy cost would be prohibitive. Instead, they crawl, their hooked claws finding purchase in the sediment and the pitted surfaces of the osseous towers. Their movement is a slow-motion heave, a grinding progress that adds its own low vibration to the city's soundscape.

They are grazers and scavengers, the sluggish cattle of the abyss. Their undersides are equipped with powerful, grinding mouthparts designed to crush the hard-shelled bioluminescent crustaceans that scuttle too slow and to scrape the thickest bacterial mats from the vent rims. They also consume the city itself; they are known to anchor themselves to an older, decaying structure and slowly gnaw away at the calcified secretions, recycling the ancient minerals to build their own ever-thickening armor.

They are communal but not social. They congregate in the wider plazas and cluster around the warmest vents in torpid heaps, their armored bodies stacked like discarded shields, enjoying the thermal energy. When they rest, they wedge themselves into the larger cavities of the cathedral-mounds, their rough backs sealing the opening perfectly, becoming indistinguishable from the wall itself.

They communicate through the medium that defines them: pressure. By drumming their heavy abdomens against the seafloor, they send low-frequency thrums traveling for miles through the silt and the interconnected city structures—slow, booming signals of location, distress, or the discovery of a large food fall, felt by others as a shift in the crushing weight of the world.
