The things that call the Gesticulating Hollow home are not masters of their domain; they are its most stubborn parasites. They are creatures forged in a crucible of impact trauma and scalding steam, an intelligent echo of the chitinous nightmare fauna that surrounds them.

To see them is difficult. You might be staring at a jagged outcropping of basalt, slick with the constant slime-rain, only to have a section of the rock suddenly detach and skitter away. They are hunched, bipedal humanoids, but their silhouette is broken and thorny. Their "skin" is a haphazard armor of calcified plates and thick, leathery hide, gray and black like cooling magma. Over their lifetimes, mineral-rich water dripping onto their bodies deposits layers of quartz and iron, fusing with their natural carapace until they become walking geological samples. Older individuals are heavier, slower, encrusted with glittering, crystalline scabs.

Under the harsh, cold burn of the bioluminescent sky, their eyes are enormous, glassy, and entirely black. They have no pupils or whites, just deep obsidian pools shielded by triple eyelids—transparent membranes that snap shut instantly against steam blasts or flying shale. They are pale where the armor doesn't cover, a sickly, grub-white that speaks of a deeply subterranean evolution lacking ultraviolet light.

They do not walk with a smooth gait; the shifting ground forbids it. They move in bursts of frantic energy, low to the ground, their limbs splayed like lizards. Their hands and feet terminate in hooked, dense claws designed to grip slick rock and tear through fungal mats. When a tremor hits—which is constantly—they drop flat, their armored backs blending perfectly with the churning debris field, waiting for the earth to stop groaning.

The deafening cacophony of the Hollow means spoken language is useless. Instead, they communicate through a complex, rapid-fire series of clicks produced in their throats, pitched to cut through the background roar, supplemented by seismic communication. They slap the stone with calloused palms, sending vibrational patterns through the rock to coordinate hunts or warn of impending geyser eruptions. They feel the world faster than they hear it.

Their existence is a perpetual ambush. They are the apex scavengers. Armed with spears tipped with flakes of obsidian sharper than any surgical steel, they hunt the giant, ox-sized arthropods. It is not a graceful hunt. It is a brutal, close-quarters mobbing, hacking at joints and eye-stalks before retreat is cut off by a sudden river of mud.

They live in a state of hyper-vigilance. Their settlements of lashed exoskeletons are temporary camps, meant to be abandoned in minutes when the ridge they sit upon decides to grind itself into dust. Their culture is one of placation and fear. The totems they carve are not meant to be beautiful; they are grotesque, asymmetrical things of iridescent rock, mimicking the violent, unpredictable shapes of rising magma and collapsing ceilings. They worship the instability. They pray to the quake, offering the polished skulls of their prey to the open fissures, hoping the Gesticulating Hollow will accept the bone and spare the flesh, if only for one more rotation of the dripping sky.
