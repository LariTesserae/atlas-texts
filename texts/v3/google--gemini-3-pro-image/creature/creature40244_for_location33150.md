The dominant life forms here are not merely residents; they are the creators of their own world. They are the **Calcifiers**.

These are the pale, soft-bodied arthropods mentioned, but to observe them closely is to understand the terrifying dedication of their existence. They range in size, but their morphology is consistent. Imagine a creature partway between a termite queen and a naked mole-rat, stripped of all pigment and fur, leaving behind a skin that resembles wrinkled, water-logged chamois leather. They are a translucent, milky white, sometimes showing a faint, pulsing grey where internal fluids move lazily beneath the surface.

They have no eyes; in this bruise-colored twilight, sight is redundant. Instead, their oversized heads are equipped with massive, feathery antennae that constantly sweep the air and surfaces, registering humidity gradients and the exact tackiness of the walls with atomic precision. Their primary features are their complex mouthparts. Below a smooth, bulbous "forehead," a set of multi-jointed palps and mandibles work ceaselessly. These are not for biting, but for extrusion and sculpting.

From an aperture beneath their "chin," they excrete the thick, cream-colored biological cement. Their forelimbs, shaped like wide, flattened spatulas, scoop this material up. With agonizing patience, they spread it along the growing ridges of the spires, smoothing it, patting it into bulbous shapes, and blending it seamlessly with the previous layer. The sound of their work is a relentless, wet *schluck-schluck-schluck*, muffled by the moss.

They move with a hydraulic slowness, their numerous stubby legs dragging their heavy abdomens over the slick floors. They are almost indistinguishable from the architecture they build. When one pauses to rest, curled against a fresh wall, it looks like nothing more than a larger, wetter drip of the ceramic paste.

Sharing this humid silence are the **Vitrine Snails**. These grazers grow to immense sizes, some with shells as wide as a wagon wheel. Because calcium is excessively hoarded by the Calcifiers for construction, the snails have evolved shells made mostly of hardened protein and chitin. They are almost perfectly clear, like blown glass or brittle cellophane.

Through these spiraled windows, one can watch the slow-motion churning of their dark, simple digestive tracts as they process the giant ferns. Their bodies are a deep, glistening slate-grey, immense muscular feet that glide on thick ribbons of highly adhesive mucus. They are the gardeners of the city, consuming the colossal droppings from the ferns and keeping the main avenues clear of encroaching moss. When they retract into their shells, they look like giant, discarded blisters glistening on the cave floor.

Hiding in plain sight are the **Calcium Mimics**. These are the "stick insects" of this alien geology. They are long, segmented creatures, but their exoskeletons are not smooth. They are covered in irregular, warty nodes and protrusions that precisely match the texture and cream color of the cured building material. They are ambush predators, though "predator" implies too much speed. They position themselves on the lower arches of the bone-bridges and wait for days, perfectly still.

When a smaller Calcifier or a juvenile snail wanders too close, the Mimic does not strike quickly. Instead, it slowly unfolds, extending grasping forelimbs coated in a paralytic slime. It embraces its prey in a lethargic, deadly hug, absorbing nutrients through its porous underside over the course of weeks, leaving only a dried husk that eventually gets plastered into a new wall.

Finally, there is the **Luminous Moss itself**. It is not merely passive flora; it forms a symbiotic lattice across the city floor. It feeds on the excess moisture and the nitrogenous waste trails of the snails. In return, it provides the only internal light source—a faint, pulsing bioluminescence in shades of sickly lime and electric indigo. This light is just enough for the creatures to register shadows and proximity. The moss beds are deep and impossibly soft, acting as the city's acoustic dampeners, absorbing every sound and ensuring the metropolis remains untroubled by noise.

The entire ecosystem is a slow-motion ballet of secretion, consumption, and construction, playing out in a hot, wet silence, forever building a city that no one from the surface will ever see.
