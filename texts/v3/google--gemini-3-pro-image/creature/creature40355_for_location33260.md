The denizen of this suffocating eternity is not an animal; to introduce muscle and nerve into this petrified stillness would be an abhorrence. Nor is it truly a separate plant, for nothing new grows here. It is, instead, a localized extrusion of the monolithic entity’s own consciousness—a sentient burl, a thinking gall growing upon the face of the world-machine.

You would not see it at first. Scanning the endless repetition of iron-hard timber gears and vascular towers, your eye would pass over it as just another mechanical irregularity, a place where the titanic extrusion press flawed and created a cluster of unnecessary facets. It is a sessile thing, largely immobile, clinging to the slick undersides of the massive turbine-housings or wedged into the acute angles where cooling fins meet refinery towers.

It appears as a complex, geometric accretion of the same rigid cellulose material that forms the world, but here it is folded in upon itself countless times, creating a dense knot of lignin plates. It has no eyes, no mouth, no limbs for locomotion. It resembles a huge, faceted barnacle made of polished teak, wet and gleaming in the copper twilight. Its form mimics the industrial landscape; it is all sharp angles, beveled edges, and interlocking plates, a biological attempt at origami performed with rigid timber.

Its life is one of nearly infinite patience and passive consumption. It exists solely to interface with the glowing, amber lifeblood that sluggishly pumps through the translucent conduit-towers. The creature anchors itself directly over these veins. The contact surface of the entity is not hard wood, but a semi-permeable membrane of tightly woven, translucent fibers that fuse seamlessly with the vascular sheath of the host structure.

Through this junction, it siphons off miniscule amounts of the copper-glowing sap. It does not digest this fluid in a chemical sense; rather, it acts as a biological capacitor. It absorbs the faint electrical hum that permeates the basin, trapping the energy within its dense, woody lattice. As it "feeds," the copper light intensifies within the cracks between its armored plates, pulsing with a rhythm so slow it takes days to complete a single cycle from dim to bright.

It is a silent observer in a blind world. Though eyeless, it perceives the entirety of the basin through the vibrations transmitted along the rigid timber grid. It feels the multi-year descent of a single drop of water; it maps the infinite, frozen geography through the bone-deep hum of the conductive sap.

It does not move, or rather, its movement is geological in pacing. It may take a thousand years for it to shift its position a single foot along a conduit, a migration achieved through microscopically slow directional growth and re-absorption of its trailing edge. It has no predators, it seeks no mate, and it will never die as long as the amber sap flows. It is the silent, brooding awareness of the machine, existing in a permanent state of warm, wet contemplation, forever watching the gears that will never turn.
