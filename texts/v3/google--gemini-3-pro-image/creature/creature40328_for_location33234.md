The denizens of this vertical labyrinth are creatures of the mist and stone, their biology and culture shaped by the immense pressures, the perpetual chill, and the omnipresent moisture of their caldera home. They are a people built for slow endurance rather than speed, possessing a physiology that mirrors the geological stability of the pillars they inhabit.

Physically, they are squat and broad, possessing barrel-shaped chests adapted for extracting oxygen from the thin, high-altitude air. Their movements are deliberate, heavy, and incredibly assured; they navigate the slick, precipitous gangways and funicular cages with a grounded certainty, never rushing, their centers of gravity low. Their skin is thick, cool to the touch, and seemingly porous, colored in shades of slate grey, granite hues, and deep, oxidized rust tones, allowing them to blend seamlessly against the architecture. This skin doesn't repel the constant moisture but rather seems to thrive in it, possessing a slightly slick, salamander-like quality that prevents chapping in the biting cold.

In the perpetual twilight, their eyes are striking—large, dark, and encompassing, lacking visible sclera. They possess a reflective quality similar to nocturnal animals, catching the faint glow of gas lamps or the pulsing bioluminescent fungi in alleyways, flashing with a somber, pale luminescence.

Their clothing is purely utilitarian, a heavy armor against the elements built from varied layers reflecting the city's architectural history. Close to the skin, they wear thick, felted wools derived from unseen subterranean sources, topped with garments of waxed, oiled canvas and heavy leather that sheds the incessant dripping from above. Over time, their outer layers become ecosystems unto themselves; it is common to see patches of the city’s ubiquitous moss growing on the shoulders of their heavy coats or sprouting from the seams of their thick, cleated boots, which are shod in bronze to grip the wet stone.

They are the silent custodians of the city's intricate, slow machinery. Their hands are broad and strong, permanently stained with mineral deposits and grease, adept at tightening the massive bronze bolts of the water-harvesting nets or lubricating the colossal gears that turn deep within the rock. They do not seem to build *new* things so much as infinitely maintain the old, tending to the geothermal vents and hydraulic systems with a reverence akin to religious duty. They are stewards of the city’s "heartbeat," ensuring the subterranean thrum continues uninterrupted.

Like their environment, they are profoundly quiet. They speak rarely, and when they do, their voices are pitched incredibly low, a resonant, vibrating bass rumble designed to cut through the thick, damp air and the low-frequency sounds of the machinery. A conversation between two of them sounds less like human speech and more like tectonic plates shifting deep underground—a slow, rhythmic exchange of pressure and release that is felt in the chest as much as heard. In the dense fog, they often communicate through a complex system of slow hand gestures and deliberate taps on the metal pipes that snake along the buildings, sending vibrations along the city's metallic nervous system.

They are a somber, sturdy people, their temperaments damped down by the cold and the scale of their existence. Yet, there is a deep, prevailing calm about them. They hold no fear of the dizzying drops beneath their balconies, only a bone-deep understanding of gravity and stone. Like the city itself, they seem ancient, weathered by aeons of slow friction, living in a state of permanent, industrious hibernation above the clouds.
