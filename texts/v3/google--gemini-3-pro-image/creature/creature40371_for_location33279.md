This is a realm of silence, slick surfaces, and deep time, and its inhabitants are the terrible, patient masters of all three.

They are barely distinguishable from the architecture they inhabit. In the chilly, clinical glare of the ceiling-stains, their skin appears as a translucent, greyish membrane, constantly moist, sharing the exact texture and sheen of the damp basalt walls. They have no hair, no scales, no dry chitin; they are entities of muscle and cold mucosa. To touch one would be like touching the underside of a river stone—slick, yielding, yet impossibly dense beneath the surface.

Over the eons, they have evolved—or perhaps eroded—into a form perfectly suited to their churning, silent world. They are broadly humanoid in skeletal structure but smoothed at the edges. Their joints do not possess the sharp articulation of surface-dwellers; instead, their limbs bend in fluid, curving arcs, mirroring the worn oval doorways and ramped staircases of their city. They do not walk so much as glide, their wide, splayed feet gripping the slick rock with sucker-like pads that allow them to move silently up vertical shafts or across inverted overhangs.

They have no eyes. In a world of blinding overhead glare and treacherous shifting shadows, vision is a liability. Instead, the entire front of their elongated, smooth skulls is a complex sensory surface, a ripple of sensitive ridges and pores. They "see" the world through an acute acoustic echolocation that utilizes the ambient dripping of water, and a hyper-developed sensitivity to vibration. They feel the architecture resonating; they sense the minute shudder in the floor that precedes a cliff-slide days before it happens.

Their connection to the stone is intimate, almost parasitic. They are the "sentient wind" that carved the city. Their hands are large, powerful tools, the fingers tipped not with nails, but with dense, calloused ridges of natural abrasive material that they constantly regenerate. With infinite patience, they rub and grind away at the bedrock. They do not construct; they subtract. A dwelling is not built in a year, but abraded into existence over three generations. They are perpetual sculptors, forever smoothing the rough edges of a geology that refuses to sit still.

They are a silent people. Vocalization is useless in a world that swallows sound. Communication is tactile and vibratory. They gather in the flowing plazas, pressing their hands against the damp walls or linking arms, transmitting complex information through subsonic thrums sent through their bodies and the surrounding rock. A debate looks like a huddled mass of swaying, slick grey forms; a greeting is a slow, sliding touch of palms.

They are few, a sparse population haunted by the scale of their ancestry's achievements. They move with agonizing deliberation across the swaying tendon-bridges, unbothered by the silent chasms below. They exist in a state of permanent, calm acceptance of the chaotic churn around them. When a section of the city silently slides into oblivion, they do not mourn; they simply migrate to a new face of raw basalt, press their abrasive palms against the stone, and begin the long, slow work of eroding a new home.
