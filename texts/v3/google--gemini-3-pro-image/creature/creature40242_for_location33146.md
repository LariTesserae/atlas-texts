The primary denizens of this frozen, grown metropolis are elusive, solitary beings that seem less like animals living *in* a forest and more like mobile extensions of the architecture itself. They are the reason the city exists in this form, the "ancestral ecological pressure" made manifest.

Call them the Rime-Wardens.

They are large, quadrupedal creatures, though they often rear up on hind legs to navigate the verticality of their world. Their physiology is completely defined by the wet, biting cold. They are covered in exceedingly dense, shaggy pelage that ranges in color from dirty glacial white to the mottled grey-brown of frozen bark. This fur is not merely insulating; it is incredibly absorbent. The outer layers are perpetually sodden with the weeping moisture of the city, freezing into stiff, jagged plates of ice that clatter softly when they move. They are walking glaciers, armored in the very climate that seeks to freeze them. Underneath this icy exterior layer, their true fur is thick and oily, trapping body heat with intense efficiency.

Their faces are mostly hidden beneath this frosted mane, revealing only disproportionately large, dark eyes adapted for the sub-aquatic gloom, reflecting light like polished obsidian pebbles. They have no visible ears, but their sense of vibration is acute; they feel the groaning of the wood and the cracking of ice through the sensitive pads of their six-fingered, clawed paws. These claws are immensely strong, curved like ice axes, allowing them to cling effortlessly to the underside of root-bridges or scale the sheer, striated walls of the vine-towers.

The Rime-Wardens are silent tenders of the city. They do not build with tools; they guide with biology. Their saliva possesses a unique chemical property that accelerates the calcification of the fungal growths and toughens the woody vines. Where they grip frequently, the city hardens into durable pathways; where they gnaw at overgrown spurs, the plant matter recedes and heals into bulbous, hollow chambers. They are gardeners whose slow, multi-generational migrations shape the urban topography. They live within the hollows they create, curling up in the insulated, sap-warmed cores of the largest towers, their slow metabolisms requiring little food.

They share this space with smaller, equally specialized life.

In the deepest emerald gloom, where water pools in the frozen root basins, blind, salamander-like creatures the length of a man's arm sluggishly swim. Their skin is translucent and slimy, producing a natural antifreeze that keeps the pools liquid even when the air temperature drops well below zero. They feed on the detritus that falls from the heights—frozen moss clumps, dead insects, and the nutrient-rich droppings of the Wardens above.

Scuttling along the spiraling bark ramps are colonies of what look like frosted stones brought to life. These are oversized, heavily armored arthropods, their chitinous shells mottled with white and blue lichen that mimics the rime ice. They are the city's pruning shears. They swarm over dead or dying vine-sections, their mandibles making a soft, continuous clicking sound—like sleet hitting a windowpane—as they devour decaying matter before it can rot and threaten the structural integrity of the towers.

Nothing here moves quickly. The thin air and intense cold demand energy conservation. The ecology pulses slowly, a deep, frozen heartbeat. The inhabitants are rarely seen together; a meeting between two Rime-Wardens is a solemn, silent affair of posture and scent-marking before they part ways into the mist. They are ghosts in their own machine, an integral, silent part of the shivering, weeping intelligence of the wood-city.
