The entities that dwell here are not discovered by sight, but by an unsettling realization of scale. You might spend a week swimming alongside a particularly dense, light-absorbing wall of oily-black fronds, only to realize the "wall" is curved, and that the underlying structure is not granite, but something else entirely.

They are the Litho-Sentinels, the makers of the spirals.

They are colossal beings, immense, flattened domes of bio-silicate that can reach the size of small town squares. They are functionally immobile for centuries at a time. Their dorsal surfaces are rugged, calcified carapaces that precisely mimic the texture and "wet iron" color of the surrounding peaks. Over millennia, they become heavily encrusted with the thick mucilage of the Sky-Mire flora. Massive crimson mainsail-leaves anchor into their shells, and forests of fleshy bamboo grow from their flanks. A Sentinel is perfectly camouflaged, appearing only as a slightly more symmetrical, densely forested hummock on the side of a submerged mountain.

Their connection to the hemispherical depressions is one of patient thermal exchange. The underside of a Sentinel is not armored, but is a softer, highly vascularized membrane that radiates intense heat, glowing with a dull, volcanic red bioluminescence that is rarely seen.

They locate the hydrothermal seeps on the granite shelves—the hottest points on the peaks—and settle over them like colossal limpets. Over deep time, the immense heat generated by their own metabolism, combined with the geothermal venting they trap beneath them, softens the primordial granite. They do not dig; they sink. They literally melt their way into the mountain substrate, "convincing" the rock to conform to their shape through persistent, localized hyperthermia. The spiral patterns arise from eons of slow migration as vents shift or die out, forcing the community to slide inches at a time to follow the warmth.

They are creatures of the deep hush. They have no eyes or ears. They perceive the world entirely through thermal gradients, hydraulic pressure changes, and the subtle gravitational warping of the peaks they cling to. Their consciousness is vast, slow, and geological. A single "thought" might take a season to fully articulate across their massive neural nets.

They do not eat in any traditional sense. They are geothermal autotrophs, feeding directly on the heat of the planetary fragments and the nutrient-soup of the water around them, absorbed through their porous upper shells.

Their only significant movement occurs during major environmental shifts. If a geothermal vent goes completely cold, or if gravity’s infidelity causes their specific ocean globule to begin tearing away from the peak, a Sentinel must move. This is a catastrophic event in the quiet world. The creature creates a massive surge of internal heat, super-buoying itself. With a subsonic groan that is felt in the bone rather than heard, it detaches from its perfectly fitted granite bed.

It then rides the powerful thermal convection currents, a flying mountain covered in jungle, drifting silently through the twilight water looking for a new heat source. The rarest and most terrifying sight in the Sky-Mires is a Sentinel forced to cross an air gap—a leviathan of stone and flesh bursting through the meniscus, falling silently through the thin, cold air for miles, before crashing with world-shaking force into a lower suspended sea to begin its slow search for warmth anew.
