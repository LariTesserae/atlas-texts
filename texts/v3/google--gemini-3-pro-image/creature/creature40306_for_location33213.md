The primary inhabitants of this weeping basin are not merely dwellers in the dark; they are extensions of its suffocating industry. They are the **Curators of the Rust**, creatures whose biology has totally surrendered to the geological churning of the machines.

They are rarely seen whole, usually manifesting as a localized thickening of the mist, a hunching shadow against the greater darkness of a dormant refinery structure. When revealed by a fleeting pale light, they are monstrosities of texture. Their skin is not hide, but a thick, pebbled tegument that perpetually sweats a greasy, conductive fluid, mirroring the condensation on the iron giants they tend. They are the colour of wet slate and oxidized copper, blending perfectly with the oily soil and corroded metal.

They have no eyes; the concept of sight died in this basin eons ago. Instead, their elongated, asymmetrical heads are dominated by complex arrangements of damp, trembling bristles and sensitive palps that taste the chemical composition of the air and feel the minutest vibrations in the mud. They navigate the "loose space" not by vision, but by sensing the psychic weight of the surrounding matter, feeling the density of an iron bulkhead before their clawed hands brush against it.

Their forms are vaguely humanoid but ruined by adaptation. They are hunched, heavy things, built low to the ground for stability on the slick girders and sucking mudflats. Their limbs are too long, jointed in ways that allow them to fold into impossible crawlspaces within the labyrinthine piping.

But the most striking aspect of the Curators is their integration with the environment. They do not just live among the machines; they are slowly becoming them. It is impossible to tell where their flesh ends and the industrial detritus begins. Plates of flaking iron are fused to their shoulders like carapace; bundles of copper wiring seem woven into their exposed muscle fibers. A forearm might terminate not in a hand, but in a fused, rusted wrench-head, permanently grafted to the bone. They are hybrids of necessity, scavenging the corpses of the engines to patch their own bodies, until they are as much artifact as organism.

Their purpose is as slow and obscure as the machines they serve. They are the tenders of the glacial dynamism. A Curator might spend a human lifetime painstakingly clearing a single sluice gate of accumulated slag, bucket by tiny bucket, in anticipation of a discharge that will not happen for another century. Another might spend days motionless, its oily body pressed against a massive, frozen piston, applying an enzyme-rich secretion from its own pores to slowly, microscopically eat away the rust fused in the joint.

They operate in total silence, save for the wet slurping of their footfalls and the soft scraping of metal-on-metal as their integrated tools brush against the structures. They ignore the smaller, skittering scavengers. They ignore intruders from the outside world, viewing them perhaps merely as another type of transient, soft debris.

The "watching intentions" felt in the psychic static emanate from them. Their minds are slow, heavy pools of thought, entirely dedicated to the infinite maintenance of a broken reality. To be near them is to feel the crushing weight of unending, pointless labor, a psychic pressure that makes your own thoughts thick and sluggish, urging you to lie down in the warm mud and let the rust take hold.
