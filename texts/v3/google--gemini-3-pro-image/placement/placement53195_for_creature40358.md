The air is not air; it is a heated velvet abrasive, thick with invisible, microscopic grit that scours the back of the throat with every shallow inhalation.

I am standing, or perhaps hanging—the distinction is meaningless here—on a ledge of sintered burnt umber. My biology is a screaming siren of alarms in this silent, hot jewel box. I am a creature of membrane and moisture, a soft, pressurized sack of saline fluids that this environment is actively trying to sublime into vapor.

My skin feels too tight, coated in a layer of tacky, drying sweat that refuses to evaporate into the saturation-thick nitrogen. Every movement I make is explosively fast compared to the geological slumber of this place; my heart rate is a frantic hummingbird flutter against the slow, deep *thrum* that resonates in the very architecture.

I am an anomaly. I am the only soft thing in a universe of razor edges.

Around me, the "garden" glows. I flinch away from a patch of gypsum needles, their points so perfect they seem to vanish into the air. I make the mistake of reaching out to steady myself against a wall as the gravity slews sideways, and slice my palm open on a flange of terracotta that is sharper than surgical steel. There is very little blood; the intense heat cauterizes the wound almost instantly, sealing the precious moisture back inside.

I stumbled through the nave, chasing a stability that doesn't exist. The perspective plays tricks on my aqueous eyes. I see a corridor of glowing fluorite blooms ahead, but as I hurry towards it, the space warps, folds like hot wax, and I find myself looking at the soles of my own boots retreating around a corner I haven’t reached yet. The nausea is constant, a churning in the gut tied to the shifting gravitational anchor point.

I freeze. The hush of the chamber deepens, the thick air swallowing even the sound of my own panicked breathing.

Something is moving.

It is not the small, ceramic clicking of the arthropods. This is a vibration that travels through the soles of my feet, up my spine, and settles in my inner ear. The gravity shifts violently, pulling me down onto my hands and knees. "Down" is no longer the floor; "down" is *It*.

The mountain at the center of the cathedral is waking up.

It doesn't stand; it unfolds. The sound is colossal—the shriek of tectonic plates grinding together, the deep, resonant boom of unglazed pottery the size of hills rubbing against itself. I squeeze my eyes shut against the noise, covering my ears, but the sound is physical, vibrating my very bones.

When I open my eyes, the landscape has rearranged itself. The massive agglomeration of terracotta armor plates has shifted, revealing a vast flank studded with quartz antlers that pulse with terrifying geothermal heat.

I am paralyzed, a speck of soft, wet matter before a mountain of sentient kiln-fire. I feel its attention swing toward me. It is not a gaze; it is a sudden, intense pressure, like descending deep underwater, accompanied by a wave of focused heat. The glittering quartz sensors on its hide flare brighter, tasting the chaotic chemistry of my fear, mapping the unbearable wetness of my being.

It knows I am here. The realization is terrifying, not because it is hostile, but because it is so utterly indifferent.

I feel the immense weight of its assessment. To it, I am a hyper-accelerated blur, a momentary spasm of chaotic biology that will burn itself out in microseconds of its geological lifespan. I am not a guest; I am dust-in-waiting.

A massive pseudopod of shifting ceramic plates reaches out, passing over me, ignoring me completely. It looms over a patch of damaged mica ferns near my feet. A cloud of fine mineral powder exhales from the seams of its armor, dusting the crystalline plants, mending a fracture I can’t even see. The heat radiating from the limb is enough to blister my skin, yet it tends the brittle garden with impossible delicacy.

The gravitational tide recedes as the Entity settles back into its epochal stillness. The grinding ceases, replaced once more by the suffocating hush and the tiny *tink-tink* of cooling crystal.

I remain on my knees, gasping in the thick, hot air. My lungs burn. My eyes sting, unable to produce tears fast enough to combat the evaporation. I understand my place here now. I will sit in the warm, silent luminescence of the fluorite blooms. I will wait as the moisture is slowly, inexorably pulled from my cells. And eventually, I will become part of the architecture, a microscopic layer of trace minerals added to the perfection of this endless, dry tomb.
