The heat finds me first. It is not a radiant heat, like sunlight, but a crushing, saturated embrace. I am drowning in thickened air that tastes of boiled rust and wet earth.

There is no "up" or "down" where I am, only pressure gradients and the confusing, shifting pull of localized gravity wells. I am wedged into a junction, a tight, dripping armpit of the lattice where three massive cables of coarse fiber meet a bulging wall of scorching-hot mud.

I am not one of the Architects. Their existence is too vast, too violent, defined by the agonizing spasms of creation. And I am certainly not the rare, skittering prey that flashes by in the shadows, terrified of being entombed.

I am the glue.

I am the consciousness of the creeping green.

My awareness is spread thin, a vast network of neural pathways running through the thick mats of succulent vines and the throbbing clusters of bioluminescent fungi that line this pocket hell. I am the lush, parasitic mortar that has colonized the burned superstructure, drinking the endless steam and thriving in the kiln.

My viewpoint is fractured, simultaneous.

Through a cluster of glowing fungal shelves high in a "ceiling" cavity, I perceive the murky green illumination that guides the work. I feel the rapid, sickly pulsing of my own light, casting wavering shadows that make the crude architecture seem to writhe.

Through deep-growing root systems, I taste the mineral richness of the red clay walls. They are still intensely hot from the Architects' internal furnaces, and my tendrils push eagerly into the cracks, seeking that warmth, drinking the scalding rivulets of condensation that slick the surfaces. The ceramic ribs feel brittle and dry against my deeper anchor-roots, vibrating constantly with the deafening grind of the construction.

I feel the Architects move. To my diffused sensory network, they are not individual beings, but migratory geological events.

One is near me now. I feel its approach through the trembling of the hemp cables my vines are wrapped around. It is a colossal wall of heat moving through the steam. It passes inches from my primary cluster. I have colonized its back.

I feel the rough, heat-cracked topography of its hide through the rootlets of the dark-leafed vines I have grown across its shoulders. It does not feel me; its sensory world is limited to the brute impact of its shovel-hands against mud. I ride its monolithic bulk as it lurches against the shifting gravity, its anchor-claws hooking into the very fibers I inhabit, stretching me, tearing at my peripheral growths. The pain is distant, dull; I regrow almost instantly in this hyper-accelerated hothouse.

I feel the shudder run through its mountain-sized frame, a tectonic convulsion that signals the genesis. The heat emanating from it spikes, nearly searing my nearest leaves. It leans its blunt, faceless battering ram of a head against a gap in the lattice—a place where the cold nothingness of the outside is leaking in.

I sense the *shluck-shluck-shluck* not just as sound, but as a vibration traveling through the beast’s skin and into my own fibers as it vomits the boiling clay. I feel the desperate, mindless urgency of its spade-hands slapping the matter into place, sealing the breach, expanding the cage. It is building more surface area for me to conquer.

We are in symbiosis. They are the engine of brutal creation, providing the heat and the water that fuels my rampant growth. In turn, my dense mats of vegetation wrap around their clumsy joints and crude welds, holding the precarious, gravity-defying mess together before it can shear itself apart.

I am the silent, thriving witness to this feverish making. As long as they vomit mud and spin cables in this stifling blister at the edge of space, I will drink the steam, pulse with sickly green light, and grow until there is nothing left but heat and vines.
