The air is thick enough to taste. It is a warm, cloying soup of atomized water, fungal spores, and the faintly sweet, metallic tang of raw sap bleeding from a billion unseen wounds in the wood.

I am hanging inverted.

The concept of "inverted" is a intellectual pleasantry here, a vestigial idea from a time before the Lattice. "Down" is simply the direction the water drips, the direction the heavy, algal-furred things fall when they lose their grip. "Down" is the endless, misty gradient stretching toward a core I will never see.

I am clinging to the underside of a tertiary vascular branch, a sprawling limb roughly the width of a terrestrial six-lane highway, woven from countless fused trunks of iron-hard wood. My anchor is secure. The wide, spatulate pads of my four-toed feet are ridged like a gecko’s, finding molecular purchase on the sleek, slime-coated bark. For added security while I work, the curved keratin hooks tipped on my fingers are sunk deep into the fibrous meat of the bough.

My skin feels like the world around me: cool, damp, and resilient. It is the texture of waterlogged driftwood, mottled with patches of slate-grey and lichen-green. A thin sheen of mucus covers me entirely, repelling the ceaseless inundation of the mists while my skin drinks in the oxygen-rich humidity.

I do not use my eyes. The light is a useless, static grey diffusion that reveals no depth. Instead, I perceive the abyss through the sprawling cluster of fleshy, coral-like tendrils that bloom from my brow and cheekbones. They are my true eyes. They taste the subtle shifts in air pressure caused by a membrane-winged mammal gliding silently fifty yards away. They map the void by sensing the minute differences in humidity plumes rising from structural platforms hidden in the fog below. Most importantly, they feel the vibration of the City itself—the incredibly low-frequency hum of mega-tons of timber under impossible tension, a song of strain and growth that resonates through my bones.

In my right hand, I hold an abrasive tool made from the cured heartwood of an ancestor-branch that died four thousand years ago.

My current task is a small, vital violence. A young, vigorous shoot—thick as my thigh and pulsing with aggressive green energy—has erupted from the parent bough. It is growing wildly, threatening to push against a delicate water-harvesting array nearby.

I am not here to cut it. I am here to teach it.

With agonizing slowness, I begin to abrade the bark on the side of the shoot facing the main trunk. I scrape until the raw, wet cambium layer is exposed, weeping amber fluid. Then, using a pre-woven rope of tensile fibers, I begin to bend the shoot backward, forcing the open wound against the parent branch.

It resists. I feel the living wood shudder under my hands. I pause, breathing slowly, letting my own pheromones shift from concentration to soothing. I stroke the bark, clicking softly in my throat, a sound the wood seems to absorb. *Patience. Unity. Return to the whole.*

I increase the tension minutely.

This graft will take perhaps two hundred years to Fully fuse, to become a solid, structural flying buttress that will support weight instead of threatening it. I will be dead long before it is load-bearing. My task is merely to create the wound and apply the initial guidance. A tender two generations from now will remove the binding ropes.

A tremor runs through the branch I am standing on, distinct from the background hum. Someone is moving along the top side of the thoroughfare.

I do not look up. My facial tendrils twitch, tasting the air. A faint, complex scent reaches me. *Curiosity. Transit to upper canopy. Greeting.*

I release a response scent without breaking the rhythm of my work. *Acknowledgment. Grafting in progress. Peace.*

The vibrations pass. I am alone again in the warm, infinite grey. I pull the fiber rope tight, locking the rebellious shoot against the breast of the City. I lean back into the harness of my own limbs, hanging over the misty nothingness, and listen to the world drip.
