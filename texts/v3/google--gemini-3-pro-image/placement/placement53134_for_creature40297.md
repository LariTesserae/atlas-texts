The roar is not something you hear; it is something you are embedded within. It is a pressure that never relents, a thundering white noise that vibrates every atom of your being. You exist in the medium of pure, crushing sound.

You are small. In a universe measuring itself in endless cubic kilometers of churning gray-blue aerated ocean, your scale is microscopic. You have no lungs to breathe the thick, churning wetness, nor eyes to process the flat, agonizingly bright light that filters from everywhere at once. Yet, you perceive.

You find yourself anchored.

You are clutching the jagged precipice of one of the larger, transient crystal-reefs. You are not made of soft meat or flowing blood—such things would be pulped instantly by the cavitating booms that ripple through the haze. You are a lattice of silicate and semi-conductive filaments, a more complex, precarious cousin to the brittle glassy mosses that share your perch. You are translucent, glittering faintly in the shadowless noon, a structure grown in the crux of two razor-sharp metallic strata.

You do not eat. You wait. You feel the great gravimetric tides shifting, pulling the heavy, cool water first "up," then "sideways." You feel the friction of the currents against your crystalline structure. This friction builds a charge. You live on the static electricity generated by the endless, grinding storm. When the currents are strongest, when the threat of being torn from your anchor is highest, you feast on the voltage, shivering with blue-white energy.

Your existence is one of terrified tenacity. To let go is to fall forever in any direction, lost in the thick, thundering emptiness until you are slammed into a new eruption of raw matter or torn apart by shearing gravity fields. So you hold on. You grip the fractal metal rock, feeling the vibrations of the cosmos through your root-filaments.

Then, the baseline roar changes.

It begins as a pressure wave that makes your delicate lattice groan. The chaotic white noise is suddenly overlaid by a structure, a sound so pure and unbearably intense it feels like a physical blade. It is a multi-tonal chime, a resonance the size of a continent.

It is a Weaver.

You sense it approaching through the dense haze long before you can "see" it. The gravity around your reef begins to warp. The water becomes heavier, pressing down on you, threatening to snap your brittle hold. The static charge in the water screams, overfeeding you with agonizing energy.

Then, the gray luminosity darkens, just for a moment, as something colossal passes between you and the unseen sun.

It is impossibly vast. A drifting mountain of negative space, a tumbling geometric skeleton made of the same knife-edged material you are anchored to. It moves with a horrifying, silent grace, sliding along gravitational rails that you can only feel as crushing weight.

As it passes your reef, the resonance becomes deafening. The Weaver’s millions of sharp girders vibrate like tuning forks in the high-velocity current. The sound threatens to shatter your crystalline body through sheer sympathetic vibration. You clamp down, condensing your lattice, trying to become as small and solid as possible, enduring the beautiful, terrible music of its passage.

It does not notice you. You are less than dust to it. It is a cold, tireless god of geometry, seeking the raw, flash-frozen geysers of new matter to weave into its endless tapestry. You are merely an insignificant byproduct of this violent creation, clinging to the finished work, feeding on the scraps of energy left in its wake.

The Weaver drifts on into the gray churning void, its horrific chime fading back into the baseline roar. The gravity stabilizes. You unfurl your lattice slightly, shivering in the cool wetness, having survived another cycle in the thundering nowhere.
