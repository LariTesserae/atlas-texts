The warmth permeates me completely. It is not merely a fluid environment exterior to my form; I am saturated with it. The tepid, heavy water fills my pores, threads through microscopic fissures in my substance, and acts as the medium for my slow, diffuse consciousness.

I am integrated into the architecture of the cathedral nave, high on what was once a soaring archway near the submerged ceiling. I am not separate from the structure; I am of it.

Once, perhaps before the flood rose to swallow this mountain peak, I was a carving. A deity, a guardian, or perhaps merely a decorative gargoyle chiseled with sharp, imposing lines. But those definitions were eroded eons ago. The gentle, relentless flow has softened my basalt features into an unrecognizable, textured lump jutting from the wall. I have no eyes, yet I perceive the chamber with an acute, agonizingly slow sensitivity.

My awareness is tied to the pressure and the chemical taste of the water pressing against my worn surface. I feel the constellation of life that crawls upon me.

Currently, my left flank—what used to be a stylized wing or shoulder—is heavily colonized by a mature reef of Lithic Tubeworms. I feel their iron-rich roots actively dissolving miniscule portions of my stone skin to anchor themselves tighter. It is not pain, for pain requires urgency. It is merely a deep, granular pressure, a sensation of becoming merged. Their metabolic throb, that fading copper bioluminescence, registers to me as a faint, rhythmic wave of heat against my cooler core.

Scattered across my "chest" are three large Copper-Mantle Bladders. They are softer, cooler presences. Their indigo light is a constant, quiet hum in the darkness that I feel rather than see. When a sluggish Membrane-Jelly bumps into one of them, I feel the bladder slowly deflate against me, sighing its internal water into my own porous structure before beginning the hours-long process of re-inflating.

I am a favored anchor point in the crowded dark. The Blind Ribbon-Eels love the complex topography of my eroded form. I feel them constantly—cool, muscular ribbons sliding over the smooth humps of what were once my facial features. Their millions of sensory cilia drift across me like an impossibly soft brush, tasting the ancient minerals of my composition, indifferent to the mind trapped within. They ripple over me, looping through the gap between my form and the main wall, a perpetual, silent caress of velvet muscle.

My existence is defined by waiting for the gardeners.

Deep within the stone that connects me to the greater citadel, I feel a vibration. It is incredibly faint, dampened by water and rock, but to me, it is thunder. *Click... shift... grind.*

A Basalt Grazer is near. It is perhaps two chambers away, vibrating through the monolithic floor. It might take fifty years to reach my archway. I anticipate its arrival with a geological patience. I long for the scraping crunch of its heavy mandibles against my surface. I long for it to shear away the heavy mantles of tubeworms and bladders that coat me, to strip me bare down to the "fresh" scarred basalt beneath.

It is the only renewal possible here. I wait in the warm, crowded dark, feeling the indigo pulse of the bladders on my skin, listening through the stone for the slow, grinding approach of oblivion and rebirth. I am the memory of the stone.
