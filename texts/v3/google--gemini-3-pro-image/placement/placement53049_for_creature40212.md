The cold is the first thing. It is not a weather event; it is a permanent condition of the stone against my belly, a creeping wet chill that tries to invade the dense, oily pelt that insulates me. But the cold cannot reach the bone. Nothing reaches the bone unless I allow it.

I am inverted.

I am clinging to the ceiling of a vast cathedral chamber, miles beneath the surface world I have never known. The rock here is razor-sharp basalt, fractured and jutting. To any other creature, this terrain is impassable. To me, it is merely a lattice to be gripped. My weight—heavy, dense, built for impact—is distributed across four massive pads of calloused leather. The hooked claws at their tips, curved crampons of blackened bone, are sunk deep into a hairline fracture in the stone. I do not hold on with effort; I simply *am* attached to the rock, as much a part of the geology as a stalactite.

Below me, far below where the air grows thicker and wetter, is the Roar.

It is a deafening, ceaseless ocean of noise that would drive a surface-dweller mad. It is the physical pressure of millions of chitinous legs clicking on wet slate, the high-frequency shrieking of bats arguing for roosting space, the wet slap of blind things in the unseen water. My useless, milky eyes are squinted shut beneath thick ridges of bone, but my world is agonizingly bright with sound. My ears, vast and independent dishes of cartilage, swivel constantly. They are filters. They take the roaring chaos and strip away the layers—ignoring the background static of the insect carpets, isolating the specific, leathery rustle of the bat colony hanging five hundred feet down and to the left.

My world is also texture and scent. The air smells intensely of ammonia, ancient guano, and the metallic tang of damp minerals. My snout is a flowering nightmare of sensitive flesh. The tendrils there twitch rhythmically, reading the micro-currents of air rising from the warmer, churning mass of life below. I can feel the shape of the cavern by how my own low grunts bounce back to me—a sonar map of jagged edges and sudden drops.

I have been motionless for twelve hours. Or perhaps twenty. Time has no meaning here in the static dark. There is only hunger, and the patience to appease it.

I am waiting for the wind.

The pressure changes first. My face-tendrils register a shift in air density. Below, the social chittering of the bats rises to a frantic pitch. Then, they move. It begins as a collective shudder, then explodes into a whirlwind. Thousands upon thousands take flight simultaneously, a churning column of muscle and membrane rising into the great void of the chamber. They create their own weather system, a rising updraft that carries the scent of warm blood and dust right to my hiding place in the absolute black.

It is time.

I detach my rear claws first, shifting my center of gravity. Then, I let go.

I do not jump; I drop into the abyss. I am a falling rock, a silent, slate-colored missile plunging from the high dark into the dim green glow of the lower realm. The wind rushes past my armored flanks.

I crash into the living cloud. The impact is soft and chaotic. My long, articulated forelimbs snap out with mechanical precision. I feel the frantic beat of leathery wings against my pads, then the sharp crunch as my claws close. I snag one bat in my left paw, crush another in my jaws. The taste is hot copper and musk.

Momentum carries me through the flock. I am heavy, and gravity demands I return to the stone. I twist my torso mid-air, a fluid, feline rotation, aligning my limbs with a jutting shelf of granite rushing up to meet me.

I hit the ledge hard. My bone crampons rake across the stone, throwing sparks that briefly illuminate the surrounding gloom before digging in and arresting my slide just inches from another lethal drop. I am perfectly still again. The roar continues around me, but I am silent, merging instantly with the wet rock, tearing into the warm meal I have snatched from the air. I am the violence of the earth given form, the cold shadow that feeds on the churning noise.
