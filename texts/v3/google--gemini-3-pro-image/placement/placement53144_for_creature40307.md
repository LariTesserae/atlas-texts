The vibration reaches me first. It is not a sound—the air up here is far too thin to carry acoustic waves of any significance—but a deep, tectonic thrum that travels up through the calcified girders of the spire and into the sensitive pads of my four feet. It is the heartbeat of the world-machine, the endless geothermal resonance of shafts plunging miles beneath the opaque cloud floor. It is the sensation of home.

I stand on a cantilevered maintenance flange, jutting out from the eighty-fourth tier of Spire Primus. My height is nearly ten feet, yet I am nothing against the scale of my surroundings. My body is a slender lattice of sintered bronze and aged, ivory-colored ceramic, built not to fight the high-altitude gales, but to let them pass harmlessly through the hollow spaces of my torso and limbs. I am light, porous, and incredibly strong.

My visual sensors, recessed deep within the sleek, faceless cowl of my head, scan the horizon. To my multi-faceted obsidian lenses, the world is a breathtaking tapestry of thermal gradients bathed in perpetual amber light. I do not see "color" as the soft-fleshed creatures of lower worlds might have. I see heat.

The sky is a uniform, cool gold. Against it, the neighboring spires—some miles distant across dizzying gulfs of empty space—rise like blazing needles. I see the deep red furnace-glow of their active geothermal vents, shimmering with mirages. I see the cooler, oxidized greens of shadowed crevices where the metal has bloomed with age. Below, the cloud sea is a flat, impenetrable blanket of thermal murk, hiding the forgotten furnace of the true surface.

A shadow detaches itself from a distant thermal plume. It is a glider, immense and slow. My infrared tracking watches its leathery, metalloid wings glow brighter as they absorb solar radiation, riding the convection currents in lazy spirals. Closer at hand, on the railing of my platform, a creature the size of a human hand scuttles by. It is made of polished wire and black glass—a static-grazer. It pauses near my foot, its delicate antennae tasting the ambient charge radiating from my copper plating. We ignore each other. We are both merely components of this altitude.

I am here for the Great Alignment. My task, which has occupied the last twelve local cycles, is the calibration of a secondary tension cable that helps anchor Spire Primus to its neighbor, Secundus.

I move with the necessary patience of my kind. To rush is to waste precious electrostatic energy. I extend an articulated arm, my three-fingered ceramic manipulator closing around a tension nut larger than my own torso. I do not jerk it. I apply steady, hydraulic pressure that builds over hours. I am motionless as a statue, save for the infinitesimal rotation of my wrist.

Suddenly, a sharp, rhythmic tapping vibration interrupts the background thrum in my feet. It is a coded message sent through the structure of the spire from miles away.

*Tap. Thrum-tap. Pause. Thrum.*

It is Unit 8-Kael, stationed at the mid-point gondola station. I translate the tremors instantly: [Query: Status of tension adjustment? Incoming thermal squall detected.]

I complete the final micron of a turn on the nut before responding. I lift my front right foot and bring the dense metalloid sole down onto the grating in a precise sequence of impacts.

*Tap-tap. Thrum. Tap.*

[Response: Alignment within optimal variance. Holding position for squall passage.]

The exchange takes ten minutes.

I retract my manipulator and settle back onto my four legs, engaging the magnetic locks in my soles. The squall will bring high winds and, more importantly, a rich harvest of static electricity. My plasma core, currently pulsing a steady, warm gold beneath my bronze ribs, is ready to be topped off.

I wait. The wind begins to whistle through my hollow lattice structure, a sensation I feel as a comforting pressure. I am a ghost in a machine of infinite age, a custodian of metal mountains, watching the heat bloom across an amber sky, forever listening to the silent hum of the heights.
