The dampness is the first thing; it is not merely on my skin, but part of it.

I stand near the edge of the spire, where the smooth, rounded rock curves abruptly downward into the crushing nothingness. My feet are broad, splayed, and slightly webbed, gripping the slick, algae-coated stone with practiced ease. My skin is the color of the wet rock itself—a deep, polished slate-grey that glistens in the ambient light. It is thick, hide-like, yet incredibly sensitive to the shifts in humidity and temperature.

I am humanoid in rough shape only: taller and leaner, with elongated limbs jointed to allow for slow, fluid movements through the dense tangle of bioluminescence. I have no need for rapid motion here; haste is a relic of worlds with predators faster than the slow creep of decay. My head is smooth and elongated, lacking external ears, for the sounds here—the booming gongs, the deep thrum of the machines—are felt in the marrow of the bone as much as they are heard. My eyes are large, dark pools with double lids to squeegee away the constant condensation, sensitive enough to perceive the ultraviolet spectrum of the fungal glow, seeing the world not just in light, but in heat and chemical trails.

I am a **Hydric Tender**.

My existence is tied to the intricate balance of water and warmth on this precarious island. I am not a beast of the undergrowth like the ruby-eyed Lumen-Stalker, nor an ethereal parasite like the glass-moth Attendants. I am a gardener of the flow.

Currently, I am kneeling beside one of the shallow pools, where the warm condensation has gathered too deeply. A thick mat of viridian moss threatens to choke off a vital runnel that guides excess water over the spire's edge. If the water pools too long, it cools, and the delicate thermal balance required by the cathedral fungi is disrupted.

I reach out with a long, six-fingered hand. My fingertips are hardened, almost metallic, allowing me to work with the stone without scraping my flesh. With slow, deliberate movements, I tear away the excess sponge-moss, reshaping the channel. The warm water rushes free, tumbling over the lip of the abyss in a steaming cascade that will vaporize long before it hits whatever lies below.

As I work, a faint vibration travels up my arm through the wet rock. It is the nearby obsidian pylon. The Architects' Attendants flutter around it in a glittering cloud, drunk on its waste energy. I, too, need the machines, but differently.

I finish clearing the channel and walk toward the pylon. The heat it radiates is intense, a localized pocket of fever in the warm night. I place my palm flat against its flawless, shifting surface. The deep, powerful hum resonates through my chest cavity. This is how I feed. I do not eat the flora or hunt the fauna; I absorb the excess thermal radiation and the low-frequency vibratory energy emitted by the transcendent technology. I am a biological heat-sink, ensuring the area around the pylons doesn't become too hot for life, while drawing my sustenance directly from the ancient source.

A shadow passes overhead, momentarily dimming the indigo glow of the forest. A Void-Sifter is making its slow turn, its massive, camouflaged underbelly passing just yards above the canopy. I pause, looking up, feeling the displacement of the thick, ozone-heavy air against my face. It is heading toward the distant spire from which the latest resonant boom echoed—a multi-year journey across the cold gulf.

I turn back to my task. The water is flowing correctly now. The hum is steady. The eternal night continues its slow, dripping rhythm. I move toward a cluster of drooping ferns, sensing a blockage in the soil beneath them. My work is never finished, but there is no hurry. I have all the time in the world.
