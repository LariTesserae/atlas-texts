The deep-earth piston fires, a geological heartbeat felt closer to the bone than the ear. *Thump-hiss.*

The vibration travels up through fifty stories of corroded iron framework and rattles the copper gasket sewn into the chest of my tunic. I am awake.

My nest is situated in the hollowed-out casing of a defunct geothermal turbine, wedged tight between a sheer cliff face of sweating ochre rock and a cluster of live steam mains. It is always damp here. The air is thick enough to taste—a mineral stew of rotten eggs, wet pennies, and the chalky dust of ancient bones.

I sit up, my pale, lean body slick with condensation and sweat. I rub a hand over my arm, feeling the thick ropes of purple veins pulsating just beneath the translucent skin, working hard to dump heat into the already sweltering air. I am grimy, coated in the protective, striated paste of rust and sulfur mud that defines us, but beneath it, I know I am pale as a cave thing.

It’s time to move. The hunger in my belly is a sharper ache than the constant throb of the heat.

I strap on my gear. First, the breathing apparatus. The charcoal filter canister is heavy on my jaw, but the sulfur damp deep in the gorge will burn your lungs raw without it. Next, the goggles with their smoked glass lenses, pushed up onto my forehead for now. Finally, the gauntlets. They are oversized mitts of layered ceramic cladding scavenged from furnace linings, clumsy but vital. Without them, gripping the wrong pipe would sear the flesh to the bone in seconds.

I slip out of the turbine casing and hang for a moment by one hand over a drop that dissolves into milky yellow fog three hundred feet down. My calloused toes, curved and hard like horn, find purchase on a rivet head the size of a fist.

This is my world: the middle zone. Above, several thousand feet up, the canyon rim shimmers in lethal ultraviolet light. Below, the cool, slimy abyss where things crawl in the muck. Here, we climb the lattice.

I begin my ascent, moving with a swinging, ape-like rhythm. My eyes scan the metallic jungle, instinctively reading the temperature map of the environment. That rusted junction box ahead is safe—dark matte orange, coated in algae slime. The polished pipe next to it, shivering the air around it, is carrying superheated steam. To touch it is death.

A sound reaches me over the prevailing silence—the metallic *shraack-shraack* of treads on stone. I freeze, flattening myself against a cool section of rock face, blending into the mineral stains.

A drone trundles past on a rusted track bolted ten feet above me. It is massive, a bulbous carapace of pitted iron, blind sensors revolving slowly. It stops near a leaking flange that is venting a screaming jet of white vapor. With a flash of blue-white light, its welding arm begins to patch the leak. I watch it with the quiet reverence due to a grazing behemoth. It is the immune system of the great machine; we are merely the gut flora. I wait until it finishes and lumbers on before I move.

Twenty minutes of sweating climb later, I find my hunting ground. It’s a wide gantry where several high-pressure pipes converge, creating a localized thermal updraft. Perfect for basking.

And there it is. A six-legged lizard, two feet long, its scales the exact iridescent green-orange of oxidized copper. It lies flat against a hot pipe, soaking in the conductive heat.

I go still. My communication is silence; my hunting is patience. Slowly, I unhook the short spear from my back—a length of rebar tipped with a shard of sharpened tungsten carbide.

I communicate with my hunting partner—my brother, hanging silently in the shadows of a defunct boiler twenty feet away—using only my left hand. A quick flicker of fingers in the steam: *Target sighted. Flanking left.* He signals back with a subtle nod.

We move together, closing the net. The lizard is alerted by a micro-vibration in the pipe, its head snapping up, black bead eyes scanning. But we are just shadows of rust flickering against shadows of rock.

I am close now. The heat radiating from the pipe feels like an open oven door against my face. I raise the spear. The *thump-hiss* of the deep earth sounds again, louder this time.

As the *hiss* peaks, masking the sound of my movement, I strike.

The tungsten tip punches through the armored scales. The lizard thrashes twice, metal claws scrabbling uselessly on iron, and goes still.

I secure the catch to my belt. It’s a good size. The meat will be stringy and taste faintly of ozone, but it is protein. We will carry it back to the turbine nest, share it, and perhaps chew a plug of dried algae slime for dessert.

I pause for a moment before descending, looking out over the vast, terrible expanse of the gorge. Steam plumes rise like pale ghosts against the towering, shadowed mesas. Far below, the algae slicks gleam a sickly green in the dim light.

I pull my goggles down over my eyes, the smoked glass turning the lethal world into a manageable twilight. I grip the hot iron artery of the world with my ceramic hand, swing out over the abyss, and begin the long climb home, just another tenacious parasite clinging to the burning skin of the earth.
