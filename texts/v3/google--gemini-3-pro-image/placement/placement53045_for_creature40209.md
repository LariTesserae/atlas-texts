The heat is not something outside of me; it is the medium in which I exist, a thick, motionless fluid that permeates every segmented plate of my being. I am wedged into a space that would crush a creature of bone, flattened into a seam between a sweating iron bulkhead and a cluster of massive, insulated conduits that pulse with faint, residual thermal energy.

I am still. My stillness is absolute. My scabrous hide, a mottled landscape of verdigris and oxidized brown, is slick with the same oily condensation that weeps from the metal around me. To the eyes of an intruder, I would be nothing more than a patch of severe corrosion, another blister of decay on the dead machine’s skin.

But there are no eyes here. The darkness is a crushing weight, irrelevant to my perception.

My world is defined by a trembling cloud of hair-thin antennae that sprays out from the shovel-wedge of my head. They are my sight, my hearing, my anticipation. They map the claustrophobic geometry in a radius of inches. Right now, they taste the stagnant air. They detect the minute pressure wave of a water droplet forming on a ceiling pipe three feet away. They feel the infinitesimal shudder of the iron foundations groaning under the unimaginable weight of the world above.

A slow urge begins in my mid-gut. Hunger. Not a sharp pang, but a low, persistent chemical need.

I begin to pour myself forward.

My movement is a complex ripple of hundreds of stubby legs finding purchase on grease-slicked steel. I do not lift myself; I slide, my flattened body conforming to the treacherous terrain of buckling diamond-plate and crumbling fire-brick. The sound of my passage is a dry, ticking scuttle—chitin dragging over mineral deposits—a sound immediately swallowed by the sodden air.

I squeeze through a gap where a junction box has rusted away from its mountings, compressing my segmented body thinner than a human hand. My antennae cloud leads the way, vibrating frantically as it detects a shift in the thermal gradient.

Up ahead, through the suffocating blackness, my sensory array detects a lure. It is not light as surface-dwellers know it, but a faint, sickly warmth radiating from a circular dial pinned to a dead boiler. The radium paint, decaying over centuries, calls to me.

I reach the dial. It glows with a weak, nauseous green luminescence that my antennae perceive as a delicious, energetic sizzle. I flatten myself against the cold glass face of the gauge. I extrude my pale, rasping tongue and begin to lick the glass, harvesting the faint warmth and the microscopic bloom of mineral salts that have gathered on the radioactive surface.

I feed slowly. There is no hurry. The chemical erosion around me measures time in centuries. I am the inheritor of this dark, hot, silent womb. I am the rust that walks. I pause in my grazing, antennae waving gently, listening to the silence, waiting for the next droplet to fall.
