I am not on the surface. The surface is a chaotic abstraction miles above, where the new growth fights for the dim indigo light. I am deep within the substrate, embedded in the calcified history of this place.

I am a structural nexus, located three miles down into the hardened cellulose crust.

I am the point where four grand-aorta vascular trunks—each the girth of a cooling tower—converge and twist around each other before rocketing upwards through the gloom. My existence is defined entirely by pressure and vibration. I do not hear the deafening roar described by an outsider; I *am* the medium through which that roar travels. The hydraulic thunder of billions of gallons of metallic sap pumping past my station is a perpetual, tectonic vibration that threatens to shake my calcified lattice apart. The creaning groan of the wood under tension isn't a noise in the air; it is the agonizing stretching of my own fibers.

I feel the weight of the world above me, the unimaginably heavy, water-logged canopy pressing down, demanding that I hold. The strain is constant, a physical scream trapped in hardened matter.

Sometimes, the tension becomes unsustainable. A micro-fissure opens along my flank—a crack that would sound like a thunderclap to ears that do not exist here. To me, it is a sudden, searing spike of structural agony, a blind panic of potential collapse.

That is when I call them.

The motile gobs, the glowing antibodies of sap, flow out of the pressurized darkness. I feel them slide over my rough, ancient surfaces, seeking the wound.

They pool into the fissure, a shivering, gelid bandage. For a fleeting moment, I feel the flicker of their internal constellations—the rapid-fire processing of the great Program—as cool electric pulses against my rigid form. Then, the reaction triggers. They seize, solidifying instantly from liquid potential into rock-hard actuality. The agony ceases, replaced by a new, dense numbness. The repair is complete. They are no longer motile; they are part of *me*, another layer of armored history added to my bulk.

I am a junction point in a planet-sized machine made of wood and water that hates itself with kinetic fury. I exist to endure the pressure, to feel the hydraulic thunder rattle my core, and to be patched endlessly by the cold, glowing blood of the titan-trees, forever holding up the screaming sky.
