This is a world where existence is defined by the adhesion of damp surfaces.

I am found clinging to the underside of a sweeping, inverted basalt terrace, high above a silent plaza. I am one of the multitudinous, segmented grazers. My world is not visual, for like the Masters of this realm, I have no eyes. My reality is a steep gradient of texture, temperature, and chemical taste.

My belly, flat and pale, is pressed against the eternal slickness of the stone. A hundred small, bristled legs ripple in hushed sequence, carrying me forward in a slow, perpetual creep. I am propelled by a singular, simple drive: the hunger for the mineral film. It tastes of cold iron and ancient salt, a microscopic layer of condensation enriched by the exhalations of the deep earth. My mouthparts scrape at it rhythmically—*raspy-suck, raspy-suck*—a sound so minute it is swallowed instantly by the humid air.

Above me—or below, direction is relative to the surface I cling to—the cold, clinical brilliance of the ceiling-stains beats down. I perceive it not as light, but as a oppressive, radiative pressure on my dorsal chitin, a dry heat that contrasts sharply with the cool dampness of the rock beneath me.

The scale of the city is lost on me, but I intimately know its textures. I know the smooth, concave sweep of the worn pathways, polished by aeons of gliding feet. I know the rougher, raw patches where a cliff face sheared away only a few centuries ago, not yet fully smoothed by the Masters' abrasive palms.

Suddenly, the stone beneath me shudders. It is not a violent shake, but a deep, low-frequency groan that vibrates through my hundred legs and resonates in my core viscera. The flavor of the mineral film changes sharply, tasting suddenly metallic with stress.

*They* are coming.

I cease my grazing and flatten myself impossibly tight against the basalt, becoming merely a texture on the architecture. The vibration grows, a slow, rhythmic thrumming that speaks of immense density moving with viscous deliberation.

One of the Masters glides past on the walkway below my overhang. I sense its passage as a displacement of heavy, humid air, a pressure wave that washes over my segmented body. Through the stone, I feel the suction-grip of its wide feet attaching and detaching in utter silence. It pauses near me. I can smell it now—a scent exactly like wet river stones and cold mucosa. It places a massive hand against the wall, perhaps taking a vibrational reading of the unstable geology, or perhaps communicating a silent thought to a kin-creature across the plaza.

The hand is terrifyingly close. I sense the heat radiating from its immense, smooth gray bulk. I sense the deadly, calloused ridges on its fingertips, the tools that wore this city out of a mountain. It remains motionless for a long time, a statue of damp muscle, breathing so slowly the air barely stirs.

Then, with a fluid shift of weight, it glides on. The thrumming fades into the ambient background noise of distant, dripping water—*plip... plip*.

I remain frozen until long after the vibration has ceased. Only when the stone feels "empty" again do my hundred legs resume their hushed ripple. I creep forward, finding the edge where the Master’s hand rested. The mineral taste there is richer, disturbed by the contact, and I begin again to graze in the blinding, silent cold.
