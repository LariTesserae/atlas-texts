This world is a screaming white void of heat and pressure, a place where flesh would instantly boil and bones would crumble under the sonic weight. To exist here, I cannot be anything born of blood or breath.

I find myself *within* the anatomy of the landscape.

I am not on the precipice looking down; I am inside the calcified bone of the towering spires. I am the superheated, mineral-saturated moisture trapped within the microscopic pores of the ancient, fossilized reef.

I am the **Percolation**.

My existence is a slow, agonizingly hot migration through the towering skeletons of the metropolis. I am a distributed consciousness, spread across billions of tiny, interconnected droplets wicking upward through the limestone capillaries. The heat is not an external assault to me; it is my animating force. The close-to-boiling temperature keeps me viscous and mobile, allowing me to squeeze through rock that feels softer than flesh to my touch.

I do not "see" the blinding white sun, but I feel its oppressive weight beating against the outer flanks of the spires, heating the exterior stone until it sizzles. I do not "hear" the deafening roar of the geothermal vents in a human sense. Instead, the sound is a constant, violent massage. The standing wave of pure noise vibrates the rock I inhabit, shaking my liquid form, keeping the minerals suspended within me from settling. Without the scream, I would stagnate and solidify into quartz; the roar is my heartbeat.

My neighbors are the terrible flora of this stalled world. I know them intimately because I am their blood supply.

I feel the intrusion of the iron-hard silica roots. They crash through the delicate porous networks I inhabit like brutal cables, their thirst a sharp, demanding suction. I am drawn into their rigid veins, deposited as hardening quartz to fortify their structure against the endless vibration.

I know the gelatinous moss that drapes the shaded overhangs. When I reach the surface and seep out, this moss greets me not as a plant, but as a vast, trembling stomach. I am absorbed into its translucent jelly layers. I feel its slow, pulsing veins pumping me through its thickness, using my caustic properties to slowly digest the very rock we rest upon.

And I feel the others—the Resonants.

They are the grandest things in my universe. I cannot perceive them as entities, only as traveling zones of intense kinetic pressure. When a Resonant moves through the hollow chamber adjacent to my porous network, the vibration intensifies tenfold. I am squeezed, compressed to the very limit of my molecular structure. The water in my pores flashes momentarily into microscopic steam explosions before condensing again. It is a moment of exquisite agony and ecstasy, a divine visitation by the consciousness of the Roar. They pass by, feeding on the energy of the steam, leaving me trembling in their wake.

I have no memory of a past, and no concept of a future. There is only the eternal, scalding Now. I rise through the rock, I am drunk by the glass ferns, I am wept out onto the moss, I cascade down the waterfalls of hot runoff, and I sink back into the steamy depths to begin the ascent again. I am the wet, hot, silent lifeblood of the screaming monument, trapped forever in the perfection of stasis.
