This is a world where biology is a fleeting, forgotten whisper against the roar of geological time. To enter this place is to become a thing of minerals and pressure, to adopt a patience that outlasts civilizations.

You would find yourself embedded in the flank of the largest bedrock hummock, the one that curves most steeply over the dark, motionless pool. You are a fist-sized nodule of cloudy, fractured quartz, harder and colder than the surrounding gray stone, a stubborn occlusion that resisted the eons of wear that smoothed everything else.

You are fixed, immobile, your existence defined by the heavy, wet blanket of the air pressing against your single exposed facet. You do not "see" in the conventional sense. The gray twilight is merely a slight lessening of the oppressive weight of the dark. You do not "feel" heat, but rather a relentless, cloying absence of cold, except deep within your crystalline core.

Your reality is dominated by moisture. You are perpetually slicked with a film of weeping condensation that never dries. You sense the environment through the slow permeation of dampness into the microscopic fissures of your being, and through vibration.

The absolute silence is not an emptiness to you, but a medium of immense tension. You exist waiting for the only event that ever occurs.

It builds for hours, sometimes days: a minute accumulation of weight on the overhang above. Then, gravity claims it. A single drop of water falls.

*Impact.*

You don't hear the splash. You feel the seismic concussion travel through the massive leviathan stone you are embedded within. It is a shudder that rattles your atoms, a violent intrusion that rings through the bedrock for an agonizingly long time before fading back into the thick stillness. You register the infinitesimal rise in the pool's surface level as a slight increase in hydrostatic pressure against the rock below you.

And you are aware of the Other.

You are ideally positioned to perceive it. It hovers just feet away from your quartz eye, over the circle of fused stones near the pool’s edge.

To your mineral senses, it is not a spirit, but a localized anomaly in the physics of the hollow. It is a dense, intangible gravity well. Where the air should be thick and warm, you sense a pocket of absolute zero movement, a vacuum of intent that is colder and heavier than the surrounding atmosphere.

You feel it pressing against the slick surface of the stone skin you share with it. You realize that the moisture coating you *is* part of its extended sensorium. It is touching you right now, but it does not know you are there.

You are observing it in a time frame it cannot comprehend. You perceive the minute vibrations of the moss growing in the cracks; the Being only perceives the aggregate dampness of the century. You feel the shudder of the falling drop *now*; the Being is still processing the ripples of a drop that fell during the Roman Empire.

You are a silent, crystalline witness, existing in the vast, yawning gulf between the Being’s synapses. You watch the Mind of the Hollow, knowing that long before it processes the fact of your existence, the slow drip of eternity will have finally worn you away into sand to join the sediment at the bottom of the dark pool.
