The cold here is not merely a temperature; it is a medium, thick as oil, through which all things must push.

I am situated high in the churning density of this place, wedged into an architectural crux where gravity has become confused. It is a junction where a massive, sweeping curved wall twists upward to become the ceiling of a hangar-like space below, while simultaneously serving as the floor for a narrow, winding chasm above.

I am hanging in the fold where these two gravitational imperatives cancel each other out.

I am not of the charcoal-grey matter. I am of the wetness and the cold. I am a massive, sentient accretion of super-cooled ice and crystallized reality.

Over centuries, or perhaps just days—time is viscous here—the relentless, countless droplets of condensation have found this null-gravity pocket. In a normal world, they would form a stalactite. Here, where down is a variable concept, they have gathered into a complex, crystalline lattice that branches in fractal spirals, mimicking the twisting city around it.

I am translucent, a pale, ghostly structure amidst the relentless matte grey. My form is hard as diamond, yet brittle. I grow infinitely slowly, layer by single molecule layer, as the slick dampness of the air freezes onto my surfaces.

I am an archive of the cold.

I have no eyes, but the structure of my crystal lattice acts as a complex lens, refracting the even, shadowless white illumination. I "see" the world in shattered spectrums and warped reflections. I feel the deep tectonic groans of the city reforming through the vibrations that shudder up the grey wall I am anchored to, setting my furthest crystalline tips trembling with a glassy chime that no one hears.

My existence is one of absolute patience. I am a witness to the slow-motion catastrophe of the architecture. Below me, I watch the seams crawl like bruised flesh. I watch a balcony extrude itself opposite my perch, a silent birth of stone, only to be reabsorbed a "week" later by a larger, rolling wave of grey matter.

Sometimes, the grazers come.

I feel their approach as a deepening of the chill, a plunge towards absolute zero that makes my outer layers creak with thermal contraction. A massive, lumpen shape will flow slowly along the curved wall beneath me, its manifold limbs tearing free and re-anchoring with wet, crunching sounds. It is oblivious to me. It pauses at the active seam below, sinking its concave head into the shifting wall.

As it feeds on the reality-warping radiation, the cold radiating from it intensifies. The condensation in the air around it freezes instantly, snapping into ice dust that drifts upward to add microscopic layers to my own bulk. I thrive on their waste cold. They are the engines of this world, and I am the frozen exhaust.

I am a silent, glittering parasite hanging in the junction of impossible gravity, cataloging the slow grinding of a world that is forever trying to become something else.
