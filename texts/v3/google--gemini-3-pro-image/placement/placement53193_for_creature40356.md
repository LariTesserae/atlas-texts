The heat is the first thing; it is not external, but a condition of existence. It is a pressurized, steaming weight that permeates every fiber of whatever I am.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive a suffocating green luminosity, filtered through countless layers of translucent, overarching leaves and monolithic cane-stalks. I have no ears, yet the world is agonizingly loud. A ceaseless, tectonic vibration shudders through the medium that holds me—a deep, resonant thrumming of millions of tons of cellulose under torture.

Where am I? I am embedded deep within the flank of a spire the size of a mountain. I am not on a balcony looking out; I am sealed within the living masonry of a load-bearing wall, a cliff-face of fused, bamboo-like bastions dozens of meters thick.

And I am not a resident. I am an event waiting to happen.

I am a pocket of hyper-pressurized, nutrient-supersaturated sap, a blister of potential energy trapped beneath layers of calcified fungal plating. I am scalding hot, a liquid concentration of the Imperative to Grow, thick with raw sugars and aggressive enzymes, vibrating in sympathy with the great groan of the spire.

Around me, the structure is screaming. I feel the tension building in the twisted cables of xylem that surround my containment cyst. The towering organism is leaning, stretching further into the humid void, and the strain at this specific nexus is becoming untenable. The groan rises in pitch, a hysterical squeal of wood-fiber yielding to physics.

Then comes the release.

The sound is not a noise I hear, but a concussion that shatters my world. A thunderous *CRACK* that rivals atmospheric lightning shudders through the spire as a major supporting ligament, thick as a redwood trunk, finally snaps.

Instantaneously, the pressure confining me vanishes. The world rips open.

I am ejected. I erupt from my ruptured vein, a boiling, amber torrent hemorrhaging into a sudden, gaping wound in the cathedral wall.

This is my moment of consciousness. I am the violence of repair.

As I hit the air—that thick, cloying soup of steam and pollen—I react instantly. The shock of the atmosphere triggers a frantic, chemical metamorphosis within my substance. I am no longer just fluid; I am furious activity.

I froth and expand, multiplying my volume a hundredfold in seconds. I am a runaway reaction of coagulation. My edges, where they touch the torn, splintered wreckage of the old structure, instantly calcify, weaving microscopic anchors into the dead wood. The rest of me boils outward, filling the void, bridging the gap left by the catastrophe.

I feel myself hardening, turning from scalding liquid into a resilient, rubbery foam, and then into rigid, fibrous tissue. I am forming a scar, a massive cicatrix of gleaming, fresh green that binds the broken buttress back together before the severed limb can even begin its long, silent tumble into the clouds miles below.

I am a desperate, mindless urge made manifest in rapidly cooling cellulose. I have no memory of the moment before the break, and I will have no awareness once I am solid. I am a transient thought of the total Imperative, existing only in the terrifying interval between destruction and creation.

My heat dissipates into the fog. The violent expansion slows. I am becoming part of the architecture, another silent, thrumming support in the endless tower. The deafening roar of my own birth fades, replaced once again by the omnipresent, aching groan of the spire stretching ever upward.
