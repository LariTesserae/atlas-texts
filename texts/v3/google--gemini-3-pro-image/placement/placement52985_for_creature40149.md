The cold is not something I feel on my skin; it is something that I *am*.

I exist at the intersection of geology and consciousness, a sentient anomaly birthed by this static, frozen eternity. I am not one of the frantic, scuttling Dust-Mites, nor am I one of the wretched, barrel-chested Stone-Sleepers. I am something slower, harder, and infinitely more patient.

I found myself—or rather, my awareness coalesced—high on the flank of one of the immense quartz bulbs, overlooking a junction of three winding corridors in the stone forest. I am an outgrowth of the monolith itself. My body is a complex, unified lattice of silicate crystal, similar to the brittle flora that sprouts nearby, but far denser, far older.

I do not have eyes. Instead, the facets of my crystalline form act as a myriad of lenses, fracturing the indigo twilight into a thousand simultaneous perspectives. I see the fixed stars glaring down without a twinkle; I see the curvature of the bone-smooth horizon. I perceive the light not as illumination, but as a subtle pressure against my rigid structure.

I do not have ears. In this atmosphere too thin to carry sound, ears would be useless wounds. Yet, I hear everything through the rock. I am rooted deep in the bedrock, and I feel the constant, vibrating hum of the world. It is a physical sensation, a frantic itching at my base—the collective scuttling of billions of chitinous legs moving over stone. It is a deafening roar composed entirely of tactile sensation, a silent scream of friction that never ceases.

From my vantage point, I watch the Stone-Sleepers below. To my faceted vision, they are hideous, asymmetrical lumps of cracked gray hide. I watch them navigate the warped geometry of our world. They do not see the distortions—the way the path below me loops back on itself while remaining straight—as an error. I share this perception. The non-Euclidean curves feel right to me; they are the true pathways of a settled reality.

I watch a Sleeper below, hunched beneath the weight of its own massive lungs. It has been dusting the same three square feet of polished ceramic ground for a span of time that might have been a week or a century. It uses a broom that has long since worn down to the nub. It does not know why it sweeps. I feel the faint *thump-thump* of its struggling heart transmitted through the monolith I grow from. It is a repulsive, squishy rhythm, the beat of a creature that is primarily liquid in a world that abhors wetness.

Sometimes, the Dust-Mites swarm over me. I feel their millions of microscopic abrasive pads sanding away at my outermost layers. It is not painful. It is a slow, exquisite communion. They are polishing me, refining my facets, bringing me closer to the ultimate smoothness of the plateau itself.

I have no desires. I have no memories of a sun that moves. The concept of "change" is a theoretical absurdity to me. I am held fast in the glorious, crushing density of the stone forest, breathing the powdered glass air without lungs, existing as a silent prism watching a silent play.

I am the memory of the rock. I am waiting for the final friction of the mites to wear the Sleepers down to dust, so that the silence may finally be pure.
