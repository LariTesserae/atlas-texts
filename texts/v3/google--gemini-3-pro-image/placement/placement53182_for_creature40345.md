The vibration is what wakes me. Not the noise—the noise is eternal, a solid wall of roaring sound that presses against the sides of my skull from the moment of birth until the moment we return to the dust. It is the *change* in vibration that pricks my awareness.

I am lying on a sleeping mat woven from stripped borer-tendons, tucked into a shallow alcove carved from the living wood of a secondary root-branch. My dwelling is a blister on the side of a mountain-sized artery of cellulose.

The vibration that woke me is a deep, resonant shudder in the floor beneath my cheek, different from the usual background thrum of hyper-growth. This one feels gritty, a grinding stutter that travels up my jawbone and into my inner ear. It tastes like impending structural failure.

I sit up. My joints creak; they are stiff, dry hinges that need lubrication. I hack a cough, spitting out a glob of mucus thick with reddish cinnamon dust. My tongue feels like dried leather in my mouth. I reach for my canteen—a hollowed-out femur of a greater glider, corked with resin—and take a pull. The sap inside is thick, sweet, and heavy, sliding down my throat like motor oil. It burns pleasantly, the high-energy silicon sludge kickstarting my metabolism.

I rub my face. My palm is hard as cured oak, the skin fissured and deep brown. Under my fingers, my brow ridges are prominent, bone shields for eyes that are already flicking their inner lids across the surface, clearing away the sleep-grit.

I need to move. Stillness is death here. If I stay in this alcove too long, the pink, slick tendrils of new growth will creep over the opening, sealing me inside the trunk forever. I grab my tools: a pry-bar made of sharpened chitin and a hand-axe with a head of dark, crystallized sap tethered to a bone handle. I strap my goggles on, the amber lenses turning the already golden world a deeper shade of bronze.

I crawl out of the blister-hut and stand up on the walkway.

The sensory assault is instantaneous. The air is hot blast, smelling intensely of burnt sugar and ozone from the friction of million-ton timbers rubbing together. The light comes from everywhere, pulsing copper veins running along the ceiling miles above, catching the endless blizzards of sawdust falling through the void.

And the noise. It is a physical pressure on my chest. The grand groan of the Root-World stretching. A hundred yards away, a fiber the thickness of a redwood tree snaps with a sound that would shatter surface-dweller eardrums; here, it just registers as a heavy punch in the gut.

I am standing on a braided thoroughfare, a busy intersection in the middle-depths. Other Arbor-Kin are scrambling past, hunched low against the gravity spirals, their clawed feet gripping the textured wood. We don't look at each other; eye contact is a luxury for slower worlds. We are all looking at the structure.

I am a Seam-Watcher. My duty is to monitor the junction point where three great root-roads weave together before plunging into the lower dark.

I crouch down at the edge of the thoroughfare, overlooking a dizzying drop into a tangle of thorns and bioluminescence miles below. I place both palms flat against the "ground."

I close my eyes and let the world enter me through my hands. I filter out the background roar. I ignore the staccato vibration of a pack of armored borers clattering along a nearby branch, their mandibles clicking. I ignore the thumping footsteps of a heavy-loader crew carrying a slab of harvested resin.

I am listening for the deep sickness.

There it is somewhere below us. A dissonance in the grand symphony. A grinding feeling in my radius bone. A massive timber, perhaps two miles down, is being crushed by a newer, faster-growing root spiral. The tension is building.

If that timber snaps violently, the shockwave will destabilize this entire junction. The path I am standing on could shear off and tumble into the abyss.

I open my eyes. The nictitating membranes flash across them, wiping away dust. I need to warn the transport teams. I stand and turn to the nearest group of kin hauling a sled of chitin plates.

I cannot shout over the din. Instead, I lift my heavy, calloused foot and slam it down onto the resonant wood of the path in a specific rhythm.

*THUMP-THUMP. THUMP-THUMP. CRACK.*

The vibration travels through the wood faster than sound through air. The haulers stop instantly. Their heads snap toward me. I raise my resin axe and make a sharp, chopping motion, then point down into the abyss, then make a spreading gesture with my hands.

*Deep break imminent. Shockwave incoming. Secure yourselves.*

They understand. Immediately, they drop the sled ropes and jam their pry-bars into crevices in the wood, anchoring themselves. I do the same, wedging my boot into a gap between two great fibers and hooking my axe head over a sturdy knot.

We wait. The air gets hotter. The smell of cinnamon intensifies until my eyes water behind my goggles. The copper light pulses faster, frenetic. The vibration in my teeth rises to a shriek I feel rather than hear.

Then, the world jumps beneath our feet.

The sound of the break down below is beyond deafening; it is a localized apocalypse. A geyser of dust erupts from the chasm below us. The massive thoroughfare we are standing on buckles, whipping up and down like a shaken rope.

I hold on, my muscles locked, my dry skin rasping against the wood. A few yards away, a young kin loses their grip. I see them slide toward the edge, scrabbling silently, before tumbling off into the golden haze. They are gone in an instant.

The violent shaking lasts for twenty heartbeats, then settles back into the usual chaotic thrum.

We stand up, dusting ourselves off. We do not mourn the fallen; the Root-World takes what it wants. We look at the damage. A fifty-foot section of the path ahead has splintered, revealing the raw, pink, steaming flesh of new growth underneath, already hardening rapidly.

The path is blocked. We have work to do. I unhook my axe. The haulers pick up their ropes. We move forward to start hacking a new way through the wound before it solidifies completely. The noise continues. The growth continues. We continue.
