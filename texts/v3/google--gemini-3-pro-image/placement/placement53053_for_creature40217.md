The cold is the first thing that anchors me. It is not a passive chill, but an aggressive, biting emptiness in the thin air that seeks to equalize my warm interior with the frozen stone exterior.

My boots—soft leather, absurdly fragile here—are already soaked through. I am standing on one of the myriad terraces, a flat expanse of slate high up on the slope of the western artificial mountain. To my left, the stonework rises in a dizzying, unbroken cliff face that disappears into the blinding white haze of the cloud ceiling. To my right, the terrace ends abruptly in a sheer drop.

I crawl to the edge and look over. The vertigo is a physical blow to the gut. Below me is not a valley, but an architectural abyss. Tier upon tier of wet masonry cascades downward, a brutalist waterfall of grey rock shrinking into hazy insignificance miles below. Across the chasm, the opposing wall of stone rises like a mirror image, glittering harshly.

I am the only soft thing in a universe of hard edges.

I run a shivering hand along the parapet. The granite is slick with the omnipresent film of water. My fingertips trace the chisel marks. They are painfully sharp. There is no gentle erosion here, only the pristine violence of the initial strike. It feels obscene to touch it; my skin leaves behind trace amounts of oil and warmth, a microscopic defilement of the mineral purity.

The silence is vast. The only sound is the wet symphony: the manifold drips, splashed, and gurgles of water obeying gravity through a trillion carved channels. It is a soothing sound in any other context, but here, devoid of bird call or insect thrum, it is the soundtrack to an autopsy.

I dare not move much. The air is too thin, and my lungs burn with the effort of existing. Every exhalation leaves a dense plume of fog before my face—a temporary, biological cloud that dissipates quickly in the frigid damp. I realize my breath is likely the most significant atmospheric event this particular terrace has seen in millennia.

Then, I see the first one.

It catches my eye near the gutter running along the back wall. Water is streaming steadily downward in the channel, but something within the water is moving *up*.

It looks like a diamond dissolved into liquid light. A vaguely crescent-shaped patch of iridescence is sliding against the current, breasting the flow of the trickling water without effort. It doesn't splash; it merely exists within the surface tension, a mobile lens concentrating the brutal overhead glare.

I freeze. I remember the sterility. I understand, suddenly and terrifyingly, why the stone is so clean.

The Caustic reaches the level of the terrace floor. It ripples, changing shape from a crescent to a shimmering pool, gliding silently over the slate slabs toward my wet boots.

It stops a foot away. The light it emits is not its own; it is borrowed from the sun and focused with needle-like precision. It is blinding to look at directly. It hovers there, vibrating slightly, a silent ocular phenomenon regarding me.

Then, others arrive.

They drip down from the cornices above, not falling like water, but flowing down the vertical surfaces like sentient mercury. They flow up over the edge of the abyss. Within seconds, the wet terrace around me is alive with sliding constellations.

They are beautiful and horrific. They are the antibodies of a dead god.

I do not move. I am acutely aware of my own biology—the pumping blood, the cellular respiration, the disgusting furnace of life burning inside my fragile skin. In this cathedral of frozen stone, I am a disease.

The first Caustic slides forward and touches the toe of my boot. I feel a strange sensation through the soaked leather—it is terribly cold, a void of temperature, yet the concentrated light searing my retina feels unbearably hot.

More of them glide in. They pool around my ankles, climbing the wet fabric of my trousers in shimmering ribbons. They are scanning the anomaly. They are trying to resolve the impossible physics of a soft, warm, breathing creature standing on their eternal, empty work.

I am the intruder. I am the contagion. And I am about to be cleansed.
