I exist at a junction of screaming metal, wedged deep in a jagged fissure between two city-block-sized slabs of weeping iron.

I am not flesh. To be biological here would be to boil instantly into a smear of carbon. I am a dense coagulation of cooled slag, pressurized steam pockets locked within obsidian-like glass, and fractured basalt shards fused together by impossible pressure. I am roughly the size of a small boulder, a heavy, abrasive knot of matter that is sentient only by the standards of this gargantuan foundry.

I do not have eyes. I perceive this world through thermal gradients and gravitational waves. The air—that scalding blanket of moisture—is not something I breathe, but something that feeds me thermal energy, keeping my internal slag-core molten and aware. The deep, sub-bass groaning of the foundation isn't a sound I hear, but a constant, terrifying shudder that threatens to rattle my loose cohesion apart.

I am located near the edge of one of the simmering, oily lakes. Through the vibrations in the iron floor, I feel the liquid slowly sloshing. The heat rising from it is intense even for me, a thick column of thermal distortion ascending into the bruised twilight.

I am a "Friction-Pearl." I am a byproduct of the reality breakdown.

I was formed cycles ago during a catastrophic failure of physics nearby. A black pyramid, towering miles into the haze, suddenly succumbed to the fluctuating reality. It didn't fall; it *melted* sideways, stone turning to viscous sludge in defiance of its own nature. The sheer psychic shock of physical laws failing—the grinding friction of "what is" rubbing against "what shouldn't be"—compressed raw matter and localized gravity into *me*.

I stay wedged in my crevice because it is safe. Out on the open expanse of the crude iron floor, the "blind architects" roam.

Just now, the floor shudders violently. Thermal sensors spike. One of the tectonic giants is passing. It is a tumbling mountain of semi-molten rock, a moving zip-code of destruction. The heat radiating from its flanks turns the edges of my iron cradle cherry-red. I feel the immense gravitational drag of its passing, a tide of force pulling at my constituent parts. If I were less dense, if I were not anchored by my own concentrated gravity, I would be dragged out of my hiding spot and absorbed into its mindless, tumbling mass, lost forever in the churn. It passes, blind and deaf to my existence, leaving behind a trail of cooling sparks that rain down like meteors.

As the giant rumbles away into the haze, the environment shifts again. The oily lake near me begins to bubble furiously. I sense a change in local density—a thinning of reality.

From the surface of the hot oil, a Steam-Draft peels upward. To my thermal sense, it is a patch of sudden, shocking cold against the ambient heat. It is a two-dimensional phantom, a flat sheet of complex vapor. It hovers over the crevice where I hide. If I could see, it might look like a blueprint of gears, but I perceive it as a complex pattern of thermal absences. It hangs there, silent, observing the cooling trail of the giant. It knows I am here, but I am too small, too static to be cataloged. I am merely debris.

My purpose is cryptic, even to myself. I do not build. I do not observe with the detachment of the Drafts. I am a repository. I hold within my dense core the vibratory memory of when stone forgot how to be stone. When the iron floor buckles and new structures vomit upward from the magma below, I feel the "wrongness" of it resonate in my basalt bones. I am an anchor point of remembered physics in a world where physics is a decaying dream. I wait in the sweltering dark, absorbing the heat, enduring the tectonic grinding, watching the eternal, mindless cycle of the foundry from my crack in the iron ocean.
