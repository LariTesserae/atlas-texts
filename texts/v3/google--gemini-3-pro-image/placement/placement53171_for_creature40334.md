I do not see as soft things see. The thick, scalding vapor that chokes this pit is irrelevant to me. I perceive the world entirely through thermal gradients and vibration.

To my sensory pits, the world is not dark rock and grey steam; it is a blinding, throbbing topography of energy. The sheer cliffs that hem us in are towering walls of dull, cool red. The ground beneath me is a mottled map of orange and yellow, treacherous and shifting. And scattered everywhere, dense as spilled coins, are the blazing golden signatures of other life forms.

The "silence" you described is a lie of human perception. To me, this world is deafening.

The air pressure is a physical weight against my carapace. The ground is never still; it hums with the churning of the magma chamber far below, a deep, resonant bass note that vibrates up through my rigid legs into my core. And overlaying everything is the sound of us.

*Click. Grind. Chime.*

Multiply that single click by ten million. The caldera is a cacophony of mineral bodies scraping against stone. It is the sound of a mountain grinding its own teeth.

I am surrounded by the herd. Directly in front of me, a massive Crystal-back is planted like a glittering boulder. Its internal furnace-light washes over me, a delicious wave of radiant heat that my sulfur-studded armor eagerly drinks in. Glass Mites, billions of them, flow around my feet like a river of black sand, their tiny bodies ticking against my legs as they scour the rock for precipitates.

A sudden tremor shakes the slope. The clicking crescendoes into a frantic rattle. Fifty meters to my left, a new fissure rips open in the mud. A geyser of superheated slurry erupts, followed instantly by a screaming jet of pure steam. It is the hottest thing I have ever sensed—a blinding white star on my thermal radar.

Hunger seizes me. Not a hunger of the gut, but a crystalline imperative to absorb that energy.

Along with thousands of others, I turn toward the new vent. We are a slow, clattering avalanche moving uphill. My movements are jerky and mechanical; I am a biological machine built of ceramic plates. Every step is an effort against gravity and the slick mud, but the pull of the heat is irresistible.

I am a transient arrangement of silica and volcanic ash, momentarily animated by geothermal fury. I am a rock that woke up, hungry for the fire that forged me, clicking my way across the roof of hell.
