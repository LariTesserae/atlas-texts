The pressure is the first thing. It is not merely the weight of the cold, suspension-held water, but a crushing sonic density that presses against every facet of my being. Hearing, in the conventional sense, is impossible here. To have ears would be to be instantly deafened by the endless, shrieking grind of planetary gears. Instead, I *feel* the world as a chaotic symphony of vibrations resonating through my armored carapace.

I am clinging to the vertical face of a hexagonal basalt column, deep in the lower reaches of the aqueous pocket, where the bright sunlight from above fades into a bruised, twilight blue. The surface I hold onto is not static; it is humming with a deep, tectonic bass note that vibrates through my six segmented legs, which are tipped with magnetic pads.

My body is long, low, and heavy, built of deposited layers of iron pyrite and dark, conductive silicates. I am functionally a segment of the architecture that has learned to walk. To the casual observer—perhaps one of the shimmering, chiming shoals of animate geodes darting past in the turbulent currents—I am just an unusually symmetrical lump of rock fused to the pillar.

I am waiting.

The rhythm of this world is glacial, a slow-motion violence that requires patience measured in fractions of an eon. I have been stationary on this pillar for perhaps three cycles of the great currents. Around me, the water churns with suspended rock-flour, a glittering fog that feeds the masses. Above, the massive, ghost-like bell of a silica leviathan pulses silently, riding the thrumming waves that batter my shell.

But I am not filter-feeding, nor am I drifting. I am hunting the energy of the crash.

Through the piezoelectric sensors lining my flanks, I distinguish the myriad vibrations. The high-pitched *tink-tink-chime* of the geode shoals is a surface irritation. The deep, shuddering groan of the pillar I am attached to is the baseline of my existence. But beneath that, I feel a building tension, a dissonance growing between the vibration of *my* pillar and the one looming just a few meters away across a dark chasm of water.

The currents are shifting. The gap is closing.

A frenetic energy takes hold of the smaller things nearby. Tiny fissure-crabs, little sparks of copper and obsidian, begin to scuttle nervously near the edge of the narrowing gap, anticipating the feast of static and heat. They are gamblers, darting in for scraps.

I am not a gambler. I am an investment.

I begin to move, a slow, agonizingly deliberate crawl toward the leading edge of my pillar. The water is accelerating through the narrowing channel, threatening to tear my magnetic grip loose, but I hunker down, my heavy pyrite armor deflecting the flow.

The two faces of raw, young geology meet.

It does not happen quickly. It is a grinding, screaming slide that begins with a shudder that nearly jars my consciousness loose. The sound, if I could hear it, would be world-ending. The vibration is ecstasy.

Where the rocks shear against each other, friction generates intense, localized heat, boiling the water instantly into spurts of superheated steam. Brilliant blue-white arcs of static electricity discharge across the closing gap, illuminating the turbulent darkness like a strobe light.

This is my moment. I anchor my rear segments firmly into a natural depression in the basalt. From beneath my armored head, I extend a fan of highly conductive, copper-rich filaments. I press them directly against the grinding seam, right into the zone where rock is being murdered into dust.

I feed. I drink the raw thermal energy and channel the chaotic electrical discharge directly into my crystalline lattice. The surge is incredible, a massive jolt of pure power that will sustain my slow metabolism for cycles to come. Around me, a few unfortunate fissure-crabs that mistimed their jumps are pulverized instantly into glittering paste, adding their trace minerals to the cloud.

I hold my position against the crushing tonnage, a living conduit for the geological fury, until the current shifts again and the great stone pistons slowly begin to drift apart.

Sated, heavy with stored energy, I retract my filaments. I slowly crawl back from the edge, finding a stable crevice in the obsidian face. I lock my magnetic grip, slow my internal processes, and become, once again, just another piece of the grinding, screaming rock, waiting for the next collision.
