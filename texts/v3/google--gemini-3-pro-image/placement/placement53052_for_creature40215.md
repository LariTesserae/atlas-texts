I find myself where the violence is most concentrated.

My world is not measured in miles or vistas; it is measured in millimeters and frequencies. I am attached to the underside of a massive, fractured bearing housing on one of the colossal, dying geothermal turbines.

I am a Resonant Rime-Kin.

To a human eye, I would be invisible—a mere rough patch of oxidized orange and dirty white frost bonded to the shivering steel. I am no bigger than a fingernail, an irregular lump of salvaged rust flakes cemented by frozen bio-polymer, hard as flint. I have no eyes to see the overwhelming darkness, no ears to be deafened by the screaming winds or the grinding machinery. I have only the lattice of mineralized cilia on my underside, pressed tightly against the vibrating metal skin of the host machine.

My existence is defined by the shudder.

The great turbine I cling to is dying. It turns with agonizing slowness, and every rotation sends shockwaves through its metal skeleton. The deep, nauseating bass frequencies that would pulp soft tissues pass through my armored carapace like a tide; they are the baseline thrum of my reality. The punctuating high-pitched shrieks of metal shearing against metal are sharper, hotter jolts.

This vibration is my lifeblood. In this absolute zero tomb, the kinetic energy of the screaming machine is the only warmth I will ever know. The shaking agitates my sluggish, supercooled internal fluids, providing the activation energy necessary for my glacial metabolism to function. I ride the death throes of the titan.

I am positioned precisely at the interface—the microscopic warzone where a tendril of the hard, cyan cryo-root has crept over the turbine housing. The root is slowly, chemically digesting the iron. I graze upon this toxic margin. I feed on the sludge of corroded metal enzymes and frozen plant cellulose created where the two giants clash.

It is a meal that lasts decades. I move with a slowness that is geological. Perhaps ten years ago, I shifted three millimeters to the left to follow a particularly rich vein of rust.

I sense others of my kind nearby, not through sight or sound, but by the subtle dampening effect their bodies have on the vibration pattern of the steel. We are a silent colony clustered around the screaming wound of the machine. We are utterly still, armored against the rime fog, feeding on slow decay, and warmed by the deafening roar that we cannot hear. I wait, patiently, for the day the turbine finally seizes, the vibration ceases, and the true, final cold claims me.
