I am a **Gravimetric Cyst**.

Instead, my entire dorsal surface is a sensitive membrane, slick with conductive slime, designed to taste the localized warping of space-time.

Gravity here is dragging at a thirty-degree angle toward the spire face. This is wrong. It is a bruise in the physics of the Spire-Looms, a soft spot that needs hardening.

I begin to move. I do not have articulated legs; I move via peristaltic waves of my underside, a slow, wet slithering against the razor-sharp obsidian ribs of the mountain. The rock does not cut me; I am made of the same fundamental stuff, just currently softer.

I reach the center of the distortion. It feels like standing on the edge of a whirlpool; the gravity wants to pitch me off the cliff face and into the crushing twilight.

This is where I end.

My internal structure begins to convulse. I am rapidly calcifying. The wet, fibrous muscle of my insides turns to stone in a matter of minutes, drawing immense amounts of heat and moisture from the surrounding air to fuel the transformation. I am becoming a scab over the wound in reality.

My consciousness fades into the quiet hum of the planetary machine. I am no longer a creature. I am just another lump of wet, cooling rock on the endless ridges of the Spire-Looms, waiting for the moss to cover me, my brief, silent work complete.
