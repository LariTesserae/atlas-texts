The roar is the first thing that defines me. It is not a sound I hear; it is a permanent vibration rattling my ribcage, a relentless pressure against my eardrums that has long since deafened me to anything softer than a scream. I don't speak; I tap.

I am clinging to the underside of Sector 7G—or what my clan calls "The Dripping Ribs." My fingers, calloused into rigid, permanent hooks, are wrapped around a bundle of conduit thick as a torso. The casing is slick with warm slime and condensation, but my grip doesn't falter. It can’t. My skin is pale and puckered like an old prune from lifelong immersion in this steam bath, coated in a thin layer of harvested machine grease that repels the worst of the constant deluge.

Below me, past my curled toes, is the milky void. A kilometre of humid nothingness before the unseen grinders down in the dark.

I am a Pipe-Walker. I am the softest thing in this world of iron and rust, yet I abide.

I shift my weight, swinging lankily to the next handhold—a rusted girder jutting out from the main circumference wall. My enormous eyes, sensitive to the perpetual twilight, scan the immediate area. The air is thick enough to taste—metallic, algal, and warm like breath.

A hundred meters below, a patch of the wall seems to detach itself and slide slowly upwards. It’s a Ferro-Kril, massive as a hab-unit door. It’s grazing, its grinding-wheel mouthparts scraping a slurry of velvet moss and corroded iron from the bulkhead. The vibration of its chewing adds a deeper thrum to the universal roar. I ignore it. Kril are landscape, not prey. Their shells would break my best debris-hammer.

I am hunting something softer.

I creep along a precarious catwalk, stepping lightly over gaps where the corrugated flooring has rusted away completely. My armor, a patchwork of beaten zinc plates tied together with braided condenser-vine fibers, clinks dully. I stop.

I feel it before I see it. Not a sound, but a specific dissonance in the vibration of the metal beneath my feet. A sharp, stalking tremor that doesn't match the rhythmic groan of the shaft.

I freeze against a cluster of dripping pipes, pulling my vine cloak tight, becoming just another lump of biomass.

Twenty feet above me, inverted on the ceiling of the overhang, a shadow detaches from the gloom. It’s long, six-legged, and coated in oily bristles that shine like wet tar. A Corrosion Cat. It is hunting blind in the fog, its paws pressing against the metal, feeling for the same vibrations I just felt. It pauses, its reptilian head twitching. A bead of warm water lands on my nose. I do not flinch. To flinch is to die.

The Cat waits, motionless, a gargoyle of slick muscle. Then, deciding the tremor was a ghost in the machine, it flows silently around a corner, its adhesive pads making no sound on the wet steel.

I exhale slowly, the damp air rushing back into my lungs. The tension leaves my wiry frame, and I continue my patrol.

I reach my destination: a dead-end alcove behind a massive, dormant ventilation fan, choked with aggressive ferns. There, my trap has sprung. A snare woven from high-tensile copper wire scavenged from a busted junction box holds a struggling gecko-mammal. It’s about the size of my forearm, multi-limbed and frantic.

I dispatch it quickly with a sharp blow from my debris-hammer. Dinner. Its skin will patch my cloak; its fat will grease my own skin against the water.

Securing the catch to my belt, I look out into the churning, milky center of the shaft. A Draft-Diver plummets past, a sleek black missile cutting through the fog, riding the chaotic currents down into the deep.

It is time to return to the clan hideout in the hollowed-out turbine casing up in Sector 4. I take my hammer—a heavy chunk of hardened steel bolted to a rebar handle—and swing it against the hollow pipe next to me.

*CLANG-CLANG... CLANG-CLANG-CLANG.*

The sound is sharp, cutting through the background roar. I wait. A few seconds later, faintly, vibrating up through kilometers of connected metal, the response comes.

*CLANG... CLANG-CLANG.*

Safe. I hook my fingers onto a thick hanging vine and push off into the humid air, swinging across the abyss toward home. This wet, screaming wound in the world is terrible, but it is ours.
