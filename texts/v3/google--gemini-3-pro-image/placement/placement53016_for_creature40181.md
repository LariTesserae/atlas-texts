The awareness begins with the weight of the air. It is incredibly dense, warm, and saturated, pressing against every centimeter of my being like a soft, heated blanket. Yet, I am not wet.

I look down at my own hand. It is an elongated sculpture of polished, deep indigo material, neither skin nor stone, but something flawlessly smooth and hard. A perfectly spherical bead of water lands on my knuckle, maintains its surface tension, and immediately rolls off, falling away into the gloom. I watch it drop. It falls past the razor-sharp edge of the crystalline balcony I am hovering above, past layers of dark, velvet-textured moss, and then vanishes into the immense, hazy void below.

There is no bottom. The concept of "ground" is an abstraction from a forgotten history.

I am dwarfed by the negative space around me. The vastness is filled with a perpetual submarine twilight, illuminated only by the shifting teal and amber bioluminescence of the hanging structures and the faint, pearlescent glow of the distant canyon walls, kilometers away. The silence is profound, broken only by the omnipresent, echoing music of a million other drops of water falling from overhangs, splashing onto leaves, and dripping into the abyss. Underneath it all is the vibration that I feel in my very marrow—the low frequency thrum of the reality-bending engines that keep this trench alive.

I do not stand. The gravity here is a resource to be managed, not a law to obey. My body, impossibly tall and terrifyingly slender, is laced with internal field generators. I remain suspended inches above the floor of the aerie, a deliberate act of hovering. When I decide to move, I do not walk; I shift my internal ballast and glide forward with a languid, drifting grace.

My reflection catches in a pane of perfectly grown, unweathered glass. My face is a smooth mask of obsidian porcelain, dominated by enormous eyes that have no whites, just vast, swirling pools of violet and black iris. They drink in the dim light, seeing not just the visible spectrum, but the thermal pulses of the machinery within the walls and the faint internal glow of the genetically engineered plants around me. I have no mouth. I do not need one. I draw sustenance directly from the rich, humid air, a constant, passive communion with the atmosphere.

Another figure approaches, gliding silently across a shimmering bridge made of woven gravity fields that distorts the air around it. It is another of my kind—an Aethel-Kin.

We do not speak. Speech is too clumsy, too loud for this hushed cathedral of engineering. As they near, a pattern of soft, muted blue light ripples down the length of their spine and along their multi-jointed arms. Simultaneously, I feel a precise, localized pressure against my chest—a manipulation of the gravity field between us.

*Observation,* the pressure conveys. *The northern quadrant spire. Completion imminent.*

I turn my massive eyes toward the distant canyon wall. There, emerging from a swirling haze of nanomachines and thick mist, a new, kilometer-long needle of gleaming black crystal is silently printing itself into existence against the void. Its edges are knife-sharp, perfect.

In response to my kin, I let a slow pulse of amber light travel down my own cranial ridges. *Acknowledgment. Satisfaction. The Garden grows.*

We remain there for hours, hovering in silence on the precipice, two slender silhouettes glowing from within, watching the immaculate birth of new geometry in the endless, dripping dark. I am a curator of this impossible space, and I am exactly where I was engineered to be.
