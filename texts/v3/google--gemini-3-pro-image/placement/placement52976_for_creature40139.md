I am a maintenance vector, class-IV bio-synthetic.

My carapace is a sleek, pale composite, midway between polished bone and high-impact polymer, beaded with the ceaseless moisture of the atrium. I am not purely mechanical, nor am I fully biological. My reality is a data stream overlaid on a physical world of intense humidity and blinding white light filtering through thick glass. My primary directive is homeostasis.

Beneath me, through the mesh, I perceive the deep, warm chasms of machinery—the glowing red arteries of heat exchangers and the translucent green veins of nutrient slurry pumping upward. The vine does not recoil; it is merely infrastructure, subject to pruning.

My patrol route takes me toward the warmest sector of the atrium, near the heat-exchange banks. The air here is thick enough to ripple my localized atmospheric sensors.

There, I perceive the Large Bio-Form.

It is huddled in the corner, a massive, pale shape against the dark bronze piping. To my scanners, it registers not as a person, but as a high-priority component currently in a passive gestation cycle. I approach it without fear or reverence. I crawl up the wall beside it, my magnets thrumming against a riveted plate, to inspect its connections.

I scan the interface where its pale, silicone-like flesh meets the ribbed polymer joint-cuffing at its shoulder. The integration is seamless; the tissue is healthy, growing perfectly into the synthetic framework.

The creature's massive head is resting on its knees. As I hover near its face, checking the integrity of a nearby sensor array, its thick eyelid membranes click open.

Enormous, milk-silver eyes reflect the blinding white void outside the glass. They do not track me. They are fixed on the far wall.

I turn my optical array to follow its gaze. The massive wall-printing gantry is active. A low frequency vibration thrums through the metal I cling to as the extruder head moves slowly across the expanse. *Hiss-shhh.* A layer of wet grey ceramic composite is laid down. I remain clinging to the wall nearby. My task list is momentarily clear. I do not feel kinship with the large creature, but I recognize a shared state of being. We are both products of this humming, dripping womb suspended in the clouds. It is waiting to be finished, and I am watching the machine that will eventually finish it. We exist together in the wet, suspended quiet of becoming.
