This is not a place for soft skin or lungs that require temperate oxygen. To enter this pocket-world is to be assaulted by a reality hostile to human life. Therefore, I cannot be human.

I find myself in the upper reaches of the chamber, where the heat is most intense—a steady, searing fifty degrees Celsius near the ceiling. I am anchored into the corner where two massive, sweating conduits intersect, directly adjacent to a cluster of those corroded terminals that spit blue lightning.

I am not mobile like the frenetic Insulation Hounds below. I have no need to scuttle or hunt for scraps of current. I am sessile, ancient, and patient.

My body is a massive, rugged accretion of materials, blurring the line between biology and geology. I resemble a vast, flattened geode cracked open, grown directly onto the vibrating substrate of the machine walls. My outer shell is a thickened crust of precipitated metals and heat-hardened ceramics, scavenged molecule by molecule from the metallic vapor in the air over decades. I am dark, matte-black and iridescent oil-slick gray, armored against the steam blasts and the corrosive drips.

Where a face might be, I have a concave array of crystalline receptors. I do not "see" in a conventional sense, as the darkness is near absolute. Instead, I perceive the chamber as a complex, three-dimensional map of vibrations and thermal gradients. The Great Tone—the colossal groan of planetary energy generation—is not something I hear; it is the medium in which I exist, a constant, comforting pressure that resonates through my crystalline core. I feel the frantic tapping footsteps of the Hounds on the grating below as mere surface noise on a deep ocean of sound. I feel the slow, hydraulic shift of the Weaver-Stalker hanging near me, its movements registering as a subtle displacement of air currents.

My sustenance is the purest source available in this cavity. I do not chew wires or filter steam. I wait for the arcs.

When the corroded terminals near my anchor-point reach critical potential, the air tastes sharply of ozone—a flavor I anticipate with a slow, internal thrilling. Then, the snap occurs. A blinding, chaotic stroke of electric blue jumps the gap.

In that split second, my crystalline array flares open. I absorb the raw, unshielded voltage directly. It is a massive injection of pure energy that makes my armored form shudder against the wall. The flash illuminates the chaotic zoo below for a frozen microsecond—the scattering Hounds, the pulsing Polyps, the churning Eels—before plunging everything back into the red-lit dark.

I hold that energy. I metabolize lightning.

I am the **Thunder-Bight**. I am the apex of this dark, hot ecosystem, the oldest living thing in the cavity. The others—the Hounds that live for days, the Weavers that live for weeks—are fleeting sparks. I have watched generations of them flash-fry and dissolve into the sump. I am the silent, armored witness, growing incrementally thicker with every discharge, feeding on the raw fury of the machine that houses us all.
