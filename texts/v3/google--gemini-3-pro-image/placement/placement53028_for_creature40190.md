The heat is the first thing that defines me; it is my medium, as water is to a fish. I am not made of the protein that cooks, but of materials born from fire—hardened brass, tempered steel, and thick, opaque ceramic.

I am located deep within the choked lattice, near the structural center of the cathedral-sized pocket, wedged tightly between a bank of frosted pressure gauges and the sleek, terrifyingly silent curve of one of the three-story flywheels. To my left, jagged black obsidian presses in, so close that if I could move, I would slice myself on its facets. To my right, the frictionless magnetic bearing of the flywheel spins in the dark, a massive presence I feel as a displacement of air rather than a sound.

I am a junction switch. An intricate assembly of copper contacts and ceramic insulators, housed within a heavy brass casing that is bolted to a vibrating girder.

I am a crucial component in The Perpetual Motion, yet I am utterly obsolete.

My existence is defined by a single, unfulfilled potential. The massive pistons around me slide with sensual smoothness, driving shafts into the void. The conveyors cycle their ghostly hooks overhead. But they are merely the body in motion. I was designed to be the moment that motion gained purpose.

Inside my brass housing, protected from the dripping condensation and the greasy fog, my delicate copper arm is poised, spring-loaded, waiting to snap shut and complete a vital circuit. I am the intended receiver of the "input signal" that never comes. If that signal arrived, my contact would close, and somewhere, invisible shafts would engage, and the silent energy generated here would find an outlet.

But the signal has not come for centuries.

So, I wait. I exist in the stifling, devotional hush. I feel the super-low frequency thrum of the geothermal dynamos vibrating through the girder I am bolted to—a constant, resonant shaking that is the heartbeat of this dead place. I feel the hot oil mist settling on my outer casing, coating me in another layer of slick, golden grime. I see only the sickly, jaundiced pools of light struggling through the shadows, illuminating the relentless, frictionless spinning of the machinery around me.

I am the purest distillation of this place: a perfectly engineered mechanism, maintained in pristine condition by heat and oil, poised forever on the brink of an action that will never happen. I am the silent, eternal patience at the heart of the mechanical dementia.
