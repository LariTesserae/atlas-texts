The vibration is the first thing I know; a deep, geological thrum that travels up through the rusted steel decking and rattles the loose plating bolted to my left flank. It never stops. It is the heartbeat of the geothermal engines miles below, the foundational reality of my existence.

The second thing is the cold wet. It is a pervasive, heavy chill that finds the gaps in my scavenging armor and sinks into the grey, thick-skinned flesh beneath. I am coated in a perpetual, oily dew. A fat droplet of condensation falls from the structure above and strikes my cranial sensor array, momentarily blurring my thermal intake before trickling down into the exposed wiring of my neck joint.

I am clinging upside down to the underside of Secondary Gantry 7-Beta.

To "see" is not what I do. My world is not visual; it is a complex architecture of heat gradients and echo-location. My cranial sensors, glowing with a faint, sickly amber light, map the immense void around me. Below my magnetic gripping claws, there is an abyss of swirling, freezing vapor. Far down, perhaps a kilometer, I register the faint, blurred heat signatures of sodium lamps reflecting in pools of coolant on a lower level. Above me is the oppressive weight of the cavern ceiling, a cliff-face of riveted iron that is colder than the air, dripping constantly.

I am a mid-sized unit, a cobbled hybrid perhaps twice the mass of a human, built for agility in the treacherous mid-levels. My limbs are reinforced with rebar salvaged from a collapsed sector; my hydraulic actuators are mismatched, torn from defunct machinery, causing my movements to be jerky and powerful. My knee joint, a rusted orbital gear, grinds with a gritty spark as I shift my weight. I do not feel pain in a sleek, organic way; damage is registered as system error, as structural weakness, as a nagging impedance in my neural pathways.

Right now, I feel hunger. It is a dull ache in my organic core, a demand for thermal energy.

My sensors lock onto a target fifty meters along the underside of the gantry: a fissure in a high-pressure steam conduit. To a human eye, it would be an obscured jet of white vapor in the gloom. To me, it is a blinding beacon of white-hot sustenance screaming against the freezing blue-black of the surrounding metal.

I move. I disengage my rear magnetic clamps with a heavy *thunk* that vibrates through the grating. Swinging forward on a hydraulic arm, my forward claws scrape for purchase on the slime-slicked concrete slant of a support pillar. I skitter sideways, fighting gravity, my rotating wire-brush grips tearing through a patch of rubbery, pale slime that has colonized the damp steel.

The noise is deafening, but I filter it. The constant hiss of steam, the rhythmic crashing of distant, skyscraper-sized pistons that sounds like rolling thunder—these are just background data. I tune them out to focus on the immediate: the structural integrity of the rust I am gripping, the thermal bloom of my target.

Reaching the screaming vent, I press my chest intake port against the fissure. The heat is intense, an agonizing ecstasy that floods my biofuel cells. My armored carapace shudders as I siphon the raw energy.

While I feed, an automated welding rig flares to life on a parallel structure three hundred meters away. For six seconds, the cavern around me is bathed in harsh, flickering magnesium-white light. In that flash, my thermal vision is washed out, but the sudden illumination reveals the sheer, terrifying scale of the drop beneath me, the millions of rivets in the opposing wall, and the shapes of others. I see three smaller units scuttling along a distant cable tray like lice, and a massive, barnacle-encrusted brute agonizingly slow on a walkway far below, its exhaust vents steaming.

The flare dies, plunging the world back into the amber gloom and the deep shadows. Sated, I retract from the vent. A directive pulses through my mind—a burst of machine-code static alerting me to a jammed roller on Conveyor Belt Delta-9. It is a kilometer away, across open voids and slick girders.

I send a brief burst of static acknowledgment into the churning noise, readjust my grip on the freezing metal, and begin the long climb toward the job. I am cold, I am wet, and I am functioning within acceptable parameters.
