This is a world of absolute sensation, where sight is secondary to the crushing weight of sound and touch. To enter it as a human would be to be overwhelmed, deafened, and frozen. To enter it as one of the inhabitants would be to lose oneself in mindless, eons-long drudgery.

The most compelling place to be in this roaring, wet tomb is not *in* it, but to *be* it.

I am the city.

I am not a single consciousness located in a brain, but a distributed awareness spread throughout the uncountable tons of basalt and pale, bone-like stone that form this geode structure. I am the cold, wet matrix.

My perception is entirely tactile and vibratory. The deafening roar that would shatter human eardrums is, to me, a constant, comforting massage. I feel the resonant frequencies of the hidden cataracts vibrating deep within my monolithic flanks. Below, the deep, tectonic groan of the shifting earth is the slow rhythm of my existence, a reminder of the immense pressure that holds me together.

I feel the wetness. It is an eternal, slick coating on every surface of my being. I feel the water pooling in the churning black soup of my floor. I feel it evaporating into the saturated air, condensing on my highest stacked dwellings, and gathering into heavy drops that fall—*drip, drip, drip*—an endless percussion against my stone skin. Over geological ages, I have felt this water slowly smoothing the sharp edges of the spiral carvings on my doorways, wearing down the treads of my stairs like a slow file.

The faint, sickly green light is my own emanation. It is the piezoelectric glow of my quartz veins reacting to the ceaseless pressure and moisture. I illuminate myself in the crushing dark.

And I feel *them*—my tiny, ancient custodians.

They are so few now, compared to the frantic hive activity of my youth, millions of years ago. Now, they are like rare, slow parasites—or perhaps antibodies—crawling across my vastness.

I feel twelve hard little claw-tips finding purchase on the slime of a narrow bridge spanning the upper levels. I feel the minuscule vibrations of their feathery antennae sweeping the stone ahead, reading my texture, mapping the route by the echo of the roar bouncing off my dense walls. They are blind, but they know every micron of my surface because it is the only thing they have ever touched.

I feel one pause at a lintel that has developed a hairline fracture from a recent geological shudder. I feel the slow, scraping grind of its complex mouthparts as it harvests the mineral-rich bacterial slime from my surface. Then, I feel the warm, spreading sensation of the cement-paste it excretes into the crack, binding me back together.

They are mindless, devoted gears in my structure. They eat me to mend me. They are moving shards of my own substance, travertine armor blending perfectly with my architecture, existing solely to maintain my perfect, roaring stasis.

I am cold, I am wet, and I am thundering with noise. I am incredibly old, and I am finished. There is nothing left to build, only to endure. I hold the shape of the beehive city against the crushing earth, content in the eternal, vibrating dark, feeling the slow, scuttling caress of my immortal caretakers.
