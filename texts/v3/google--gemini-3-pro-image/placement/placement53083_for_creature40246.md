The vibration starts in your teeth. It’s a low, grinding hum that travels down your jaw, through your spine, and settles into the dense plates of bone and mineral that encase your shins. You are crouched—always crouched—on a jagged cornice of cooling basalt, your center of gravity kept low against the shuddering earth.

The air is a suffocating blanket of wet heat, thick with the copper tang of fresh magma and the cloying reek of fungal spores. Above, the ceiling of the cavern is a vast, dripping firmament of cold white fire. The bioluminescence is so bright it hurts, casting shadows sharper than obsidian shards. You blink, and a nictitating membrane snaps horizontally across your immense, pitch-black eyes, clearing away the constant film of warm slime that rains down from the living sky.

You are perfectly still, a statue carved from the hostile terrain.

Your skin is pale and clammy where it is exposed at the joints, but mostly, you are armored. You carry the geology of the Hollow on your back. Years of mineral-rich drips have fused quartz and iron to your natural leathery hide creating heavy, glittering scabs that break up your silhouette. To anything watching from below, you are just another ugly protrusion of rock waiting to fall.

Around you, the noise is a physical assault. The shriek of million-legged things, the crunch of mandibles on iron-hard fungi, and the deeper, terrifying groan of tectonic plates grinding together miles away. Your ears are useless for nuance here; you listen with your palms. Your clawed hands are pressed flat against the slick stone ledge, reading the seismic tremors like a language.

*Tap-tap-thud.*

The vibration comes through the rock from the left. It’s distinct from the background geological roar. It’s a signal from another of your kind, perched on an adjacent spire, invisible against the gray stone.

*Movement below. Big.*

You shift your weight slowly. The quartz nodes on your shoulders chime quietly. Below your precarious perch, a canyon floor that didn't exist yesterday is churning with mud and steam. Through the haze, a massive shape lumbers—an arthropod the size of a small hill, its carapace studded with crystalline spikes.

You feel the hunger pang in your gut, sharp and sudden. Your settlement—a flimsy collection of hollowed-out carapaces lashed to a semi-stable ridge half a kilometer away—needs meat. But the ground beneath your claws gives a sickening lurch. A geyser erupts nearby, blasting scalding vapor past your ledge, and your triple eyelids snap shut again.

You are a scavenger, a lurker, a stubborn parasite on a world trying to shake you off. You tighten your grip on a spear tipped with glass-sharp obsidian. You wait for the earth to stop heaving, or for the beast below to get stuck in the cooling mud. You will wait as long as the Hollow allows, praying to the terrifying, shifting shapes of the rock gods that the ledge you stand on holds for just one more hour.
