The awareness begins not with sight, but with pressure and viscosity.

I am not standing. I am pooling.

My existence is defined by the shallow depression in the non-corrosive alloy grating where I have gathered. I am a viscous accumulation, a gelatinous byproduct of this wet cathedral. I am the unintended offspring of the machine and the garden, composed of escaped algal slurry thickened with shed moss spores, mineral-rich condensation, and the slick microbial mucilage sloughed off the root-cables of the Bio-Titans.

I am located in the deep shadows beneath a massive coolant pump. The air above me is a pressing weight, a warm, drowning thickness that tastes of synthesized nitrates and wet metal. I do not have eyes, but I possess a diffuse, tactile awareness of my perimeter. I can feel the cold, sharp geometry of the grating underneath my semi-liquid body, and the warmer, vibrating surface of the pump casing just inches above me.

Time here is not measured in seconds or minutes, but in cycles and accumulations. My consciousness is tuned to the glacial pace of the Spire.

I feel the tectonic vibration of a hydraulic piston located perhaps fifty meters away. I feel it begin its upward glide. The vibration is a smooth, sustained low frequency that travels through the metal skeleton of the room and ripples through my gelatinous form. I remain still, absorbing the tremor, knowing that hours will pass before that piston completes its stroke and exhales its breath of steam.

My primary sensory input is sound, dampened and distorted by the thick air. I hear the perpetual *shhh* of the velvet moss drinking sound waves. I hear the muffled gurgle of the trillions of captive algae flowing in the translucent pipe overhead, a river of bioluminescent prisoners illuminating the underside of the pump above me with a sickly green glow.

But the dominant rhythm of my existence is the *plip... plip... plip*.

A vast, waxy leaf of a Bio-Titan hangs high above the pump structure that shelters me. It is sweating. A heavy bead of water, rich with aerosolized nutrients it has scavenged from the mist, gathers on its tip. It falls. It strikes the casing of the pump above me—*tink*—runs down the mossy curved flank in a slow rivulet, hangs from the bottom edge, and then drops into me.

*Plip.*

The impact sends a sluggish ripple through my being. I absorb the water, the nutrients, and the trace minerals it carries. I expand infinitesimaly against the confines of my metal grate basin.

I am a silent, immobile witness to the slow consumption of this place. I feel the root-cable that has snaked its way under the grating near my boundary. It is thick as a human leg, warm with the heat of running coolant, pulsing very slowly as it feeds from a hairline fracture in a junction pipe. I feel it swelling, cell by monolithic cell, pressing harder against the metal, widening the crack it was meant to seal.

I have no desires. I have no fear. I am simply waiting, growing thicker and broader in the dark, wet warmth. I am the sludge at the bottom of the world, watching the silent jungle eat the silent machine, one drop at a time.
