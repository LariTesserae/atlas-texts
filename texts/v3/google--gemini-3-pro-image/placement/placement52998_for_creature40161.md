The heat is the first thing that is real. It is not a passive warmth, but an aggressive, pressurized presence that tries to force its way through the seams of my newly hardened shell.

I find myself clinging precariously to an incline of cooling basalt, my dozens of segmented legs dug into the glassy fractures of the rock. My body is low, broad, and heavy—a hubcap-sized shield of chitinous metal that still steams faintly from my own recent extrusion into this world.

The air is so thick with vapor it feels like I am breathing soup. Every inhalation tastes of copper, brimstone, and the electric tang of ozone. Hot water constantly beads on my armored back, running down my flanks and pooling around my gripping claws before boiling away against the hot stone.

I am deafened. The world is a cacophony of geological violence—the sharp *ping* of crystal growing too fast near my head, the deep, tectonic groans coming from beneath the jagged floor. But vibrating underneath it all, rattling the wet plates of my thorax, is the deeper, resonant *thrum* issuing from the perimeter. It feels less like sound and more like a ceaseless pounding on the anvil of my own existence.

I am one of the crawlers. I am a spark flying from the forge, hungry for mineral exudations, desperately trying to harden before something harder finds me.

Above me, through the swirling orange fog, I hear the screech of the leathery flyers. I flatten myself against the rock, an instinct I was born with minutes ago. I see a shadow dive, hear a wet crunch nearby, and then the shadow is gone, taking one of my kin with it. Life here is measured in rapid breaths.

But the flyers are merely hazards of the environment. My fragmented, multi-faceted vision is drawn irresistibly to the center of the amphitheater, to the source of the violet light that cuts through the eternal twilight.

There, silhouetted against the pulsing, semi-molten dome of slag, are the Makers.

From my vantage point low on the ground, they are terrifying pillars of absence. They are towering voids of vantablack geometry, gliding silently over razor-edged terrain that would shred my underbelly. The perpetual hot rain hits their forms and instantly explodes into steam, clothing them in swirling white shrouds that obscure their shifting outlines.

I watch, paralyzed by an incomprehensible awe, as they work. I feel the air warp around me as their invisible, gravimetric limbs reach out. I see the violet light snap from the distant pylons, channeled through these silent giants. They tear a glob of screaming, raw reality from the central mound. The sound of the rock being tortured into order is worse than the thunder.

Under their silent direction, the chaotic slag doesn't just cool; it obeys. I watch it snap into a hyper-dense, crystalline geometry right in front of me, integrating into the floor with a tectonic shudder that nearly shakes me loose.

They pass inches from where I cower. They do not see me. To them, I am less than noise; I am temporary biological clutter on the floor of their cathedral. I am a fleeting accident of chemistry in a place dedicated to fundamental physics.

My hunger returns, sharp and demanding. I must move. I must find a mineral vent to feed on before the flyers return, or before the next slab of reality is forged on top of me. My life will be brutal, loud, and incredibly short, played out in the shadow of silent gods who are busy building the universe.
