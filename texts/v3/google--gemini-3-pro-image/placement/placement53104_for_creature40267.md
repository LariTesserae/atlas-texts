Above you, the wound you just burst from is a dripping gash in a leaf face that curves away into hazy kilometers. Below you, the woven root-floor looks like a microscopic texture on a distant fabric.

The vibration in the floor spikes, becoming a violent shuddering. You sense it before you see it—the massive blister forming on the wall high above where you fell. The superstructure knows you are here. It needs what you carry.

The smell is overwhelming—coppery ozone and raw, sticky growth. Obsidian thorns, slick with fluid, jut from its hide, clicking against each other like daggers.

It is time.
