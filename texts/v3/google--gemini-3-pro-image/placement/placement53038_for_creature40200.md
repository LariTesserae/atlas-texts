The thrum is the first thing you know. Before thought, before identity, there is the vibration that rattles your molars and resonates in the marrow of your shinbones. It is the deep, tectonic bass note of the world-machine operating miles beneath your feet.

You become aware of the air—thick enough to taste, a hot, copper-penny tang on the back of the throat. It’s incredibly humid, a suffocating wet blanket that makes the sweat slick beneath your heavy layers instantly.

You are crouching. Your knees are bent, braced against a sloping surface of corrugated metal that is slick with oily condensation and patches of velvet-textured, indigo moss. Your hands are gloved in thick, treated leather reinforced with strips of hammered pipe casing. Through the gloves, you feel the heat of the metal beneath you—warm, like a living beast with a fever.

You are one of the Clingers.

You take a breath, filtering the heavy air through a wad of fibrous fungal matter stuffed into a perforated metal cup strapped over your nose and mouth. It smells of damp earth and ozone. You reach up and adjust your goggles. The smoked quartz lenses are fogged, smeared with the ubiquitous grit of the atmosphere. You wipe them with the back of a gauntlet, though it does little good in the profound darkness.

You don't rely on sight anyway.

You press your free hand flat against the rusted plating of the ventilation stack you are currently adhered to. You close your eyes behind the goggles and *listen* with your palm. Beneath the primary planetary rumble, you feel the specific rhythm of this stack. Thump-hiss. Thump-hiss. It’s a "breather," cycling heavy air from the lower depths. The rhythm is steady. Safe. If it were stuttering, or vibrating at a high, whining frequency, you would already be scrambling back toward the safety of the settlement.

You are not alone. A few yards ahead, a darker smudge against the dark metal, is Kael. You know it's him by the specific clatter of the scrap-metal greaves strapped to his legs as he shifts his weight. He taps his pry-bar against the metal three times, sharply.

*Move up. Clear.*

You push off, your boots heavy and cumbersome. The soles are thick industrial polymer, fitted with jagged metal spikes that bite through the slime and find purchase on the corroded iron grate of the catwalk. Every step is a deliberate act of friction against the slickness of the world.

You are high up. How high is meaningless, but the drop below the catwalk is an abyss of churning steam and shadow. You are traversing the "Canyon of Broken Teeth," a gorge formed where two massive, ancient radiator arrays drifted apart centuries ago.

Suddenly, the world goes white.

Below you, perhaps a kilometer down in the churning fog, an electrical fault arcs between two corroded capacitor banks. For two seconds, the canyon is illuminated by blinding, searing blue-white lightning.

The scene is burned into your retinas: towering cliffs of weeping rust, festooned with draping curtains of pale, ghostly chemotrophic vines that dangle hundreds of feet into the void. You see the massive, crystallized mineral deposits clogging the cracked seams of the machines, glowing fiercely in the arc light. You see the silhouette of a massive, armored grazer on a distant flange, frozen by the flash, its eyeless head turned toward the noise.

The heat wave hits you a second later, a physical blow of superheated steam rising from the chasm.

You don't flinch. You and Kael instinctively flatten yourselves against the warm metal skin of the ventilation stack, letting the scavenged boiler-plate armor on your backs take the brunt of the thermal wave. The steam shrieks past you, rattling the loose grating of the catwalk.

Then, the darkness slams back down, heavier than before. The thunderclap catches up, a sound so loud it bypasses your ears and punches you in the chest, momentarily stopping your breath.

As the echo dies away, replaced by the returning baseline roar of grinding gears and dripping water, you peel yourself off the metal. You check your seals. You taste fresh blood in your mouth—you bit your tongue during the thunderclap.

Kael taps his bar again. Two taps. *Still alive.*

You tap back.

You continue along the catwalk, moving toward a patch of bioluminescent fungi glowing faintly green on a rusted valve housing ahead—a good spot to harvest tonight's dinner. You are a flea on the hide of a mechanical leviathan, enduring the wet fire and loud darkness, one vibrating heartbeat at a time.
