The darkness is not empty; it is a heavy substance that fills your lungs. You are crouched low, your center of gravity shifted close to the slick, oily mud that serves as the ground. You are not cold—the air is thick with the humid warmth of a fresh kill, a cloying heat that keeps the ubiquitous moisture perpetually shuddering between mist and rain.

You do not rely on eyes. Sight is a forgotten sense, a phantom limb that stopped aching ages ago. Instead, your world is constructed of texture, taste, and vibrating pressure.

Your face—if it can still be called that—is angled toward the unseen ceiling of the sky. A dense cluster of damp bristles along your jawline and brow twitches, tasting the metallic tang of the drifting fog. You smell ancient rust, the ozone sizzle of a dying reality, and the rich, rotting sweetness of the fungal lattices decaying in the sump nearby.

You are partway through a transition you do not fully understand, a slow surrender to the environment. Your skin feels tight and pebbled, slick with a greasy discharge that helps insulate you against the erratic currents leaking from the earth. You raise a forearm. It is heavy. Scabbing over the flesh from wrist to elbow is a curved fragment of corroded brass piping, fused tightly to your ulna. It is not yet a tool, merely a heavy excrescence of the landscape that your body has accepted, growing around it, making it bone.

Small, many-legged things skitter across your hunched back. You feel the sharp pricks of their chitinous feet, but you do not brush them away. They are grazing on the slime of your skin, scavengers feeding on a larger scavenger. They are irrelevant dust in the grand, slow churning of this place.

You are waiting. You are not sure what for, only that the oppressive quiet demands stillness.

Suddenly, the silence is dented by a sound. *Krrrk-shhh-unk.*

It is colossal, yet quiet. A vibration that rattles your brass-plated arm. Somewhere in the crushing blackness, perhaps a mile away, perhaps ten feet, a massive hydraulic joint has shifted half an inch.

The vibration triggers an instinct, a resonance within the sluggish, heavy pool of your own thoughts. The psychic static in the air thickens, a command pressing down on your skull. You feel the presence of the greater ones nearby—the fully realized Curators. You cannot hear them move, but you feel their immense, sorrowful density pushing against the loose fabric of space. Their minds are projecting the crushing necessity of labor.

Driven by an urge that feels like gravity, you begin to crawl.

You move on all fours over sucking mud and jagged ridges of glassy rock. You navigate by the sense of mass; you feel the looming, terrifying weight of a dormant refinery tower before your outstretched hand brushes its cold, weeping flank. It is a mountain of iron, silent and sweating.

You find what you were drawn to. Near the base of the tower, there is a thick cluster of copper cabling, frayed and sparking weakly into a puddle of iridescent sludge. The insulation has rotted away.

This is your task. It is small, meaningless in the face of the tower’s scale, but the psychic weight demands it be tended to. You sit in the warm mud, the dampness soaking into your armored legs. You reach out with your brass-heavy arm. You begin the slow, infinitesimal work of scraping away the necrotic rot from the wires with a jagged fingernail.

You will sit here for a very long time. Perhaps days. Perhaps until the brass plating on your arm grows down to fuse your fingers into a permanent claw. The darkness presses in, warm and comforting in its absolute weight. The rust is calling, and you are answering, one scraped millimeter at a time.
