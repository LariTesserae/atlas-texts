The vibration rattles my teeth long before I register it in my bones. It is a constant, deep-frequency shudder traveling up through the leaden soles of my magnetic boots, shaking the marrow of my shins. The world is silence, but it is a violent silence.

I am clamped onto the third tier of catwalks surrounding Engine 4, a shivering expanse of slick metal grate polished to a lethal sheen by decades of condensation and oily runoff. The air is not something I breathe; it is something I push against. It is a hot, wet solid that forces its way past the filtration rags wrapped across my lower face. Every inhalation is a deliberate, muscular act, pulling the saturated heat into lungs that always feel half-full of fluid.

A spasm hits my chest. I stop—a slow, rolling halt—and bend forward over the railing. A wet, tearing cough rips its way out of my atrophied throat, expelling a glob of yellowish mucus and condensed steam that splatters onto the grating below. It takes a moment to recover my breath, to steady the horizon of piping and riveted steel that swims behind fogged, scratched goggles.

A weight presses on my right boot. I don't look down immediately. It’s just a pressure, moving casually across the armored plating. When I finally shift my gaze, I see the slick, grey-brown flank of a rat the size of a small dog sliding over my instep. Its tail, thick as a power cable, trails behind it. It ignores me. I ignore it. We are both just biomass clinging to the hot metal.

My attention returns to the task. The vibration under my feet has changed rhythm; a syncopated stutter in the floor grating tells me a mineral occlusion is building in the secondary venting stack ahead.

I begin the slow shamble forward. My movements are hydraulic, measured. To rush is to overheat, to slip, to fall into the boiling slurry far below. My left arm is heavy, weighed down by the three-foot pipe wrench that feels less like a tool and more like a fused bone extension of my forearm. My rubberized apron, cut from a dead conveyor belt, slaps wetly against my shins with every step.

I reach the venting stack. It’s a towering column of rusted iron, sweating profusely. A chaotic crust of yellow and white minerals—sulfur and calcium—has bloomed around a leaking flange, sealing the pressure release valve.

I brace my feet, feeling the magnets lock onto the swaying catwalk. I raise the massive wrench. The effort makes the veins in my neck bulge against the thick, fissured leather of my skin. I bring the tool down in a slow, heavy arc, striking the mineral crust.

*Thud.*

I feel the impact shudder up my shoulder. Shards of hot crystal explode outward, peppering my rubber apron and clattering off my goggles. I strike again. And again. It is a mindless rhythm of weight and impact.

Finally, the crust fractures. A geyser of superheated steam blasts horizontally from the cleared valve. It hits my chest plate with enough force to rock me backward, momentarily blinding me with a whiteout of heat and moisture. My suit holds. The heat washes over my calloused skin, a familiar, almost comforting scorch.

I lower the wrench. The vibration in the floor smooths back out into the standard, monolithic thrum of the complex. The loop continues.

Across the gap, on the parallel catwalk of Engine 5, I see another shape through the murky twilight. It is another custodian, hunched and bulky, silhouetted against the sickly orange flare of a distant stack. They are motionless, perhaps sleeping standing up, or perhaps just watching the steam rise.

I raise a heavy, grease-caked glove and strike the hollow railing beside me three times with a closed fist. The vibration travels down the metal skeleton of the complex.

A moment later, three faint pulses travel back through the soles of my boots. Acknowledged.

I turn my back to the other and begin the slow, rolling walk toward the next vibration, the next tightening flange, the next eternity in the drowning bowl.
