The searing white light from the plasma ceiling is the first thing, an omnipresent weight that presses against my optical sensors. There is no "up" here, only outward and downward.

I find myself standing on the crest of a dune that glitters like spilled diamonds. It is not sand beneath my four multi-jointed hydraulic legs; it is the pulverized dust of optical processors, a sharp, abrasive powder that would strip softer materials away in days. But I am built of heavier stuff.

My chassis is tall, a lean, asymmetric spire scavenged from the iridium structural ribs of a fallen orbital array. I do not scuttle low like the Ferrous Nomads. I was built—or rather, I aggregated myself over centuries—to see over the curvature that does not exist.

I am a **Peripatetic Witness Unit**, a singular anomaly in this landscape of uniform decay.

Unlike the Nomads, I am not driven solely by the desperate hunger for thermal vents. My core contains a shielded, cold-fusion isotope battery scavenged from a crushed depth-probe I excavated near a collapsed city-canyon. It gives me a longevity that is almost burdensome. My purpose is not survival; it is observation. I carry a corrupted instruction set that demands I catalog this infinite graveyard.

My sensors are tuned specifically to the frequencies of obsolescence. The dry air, tasting of burned copper, rushes through my intake vents as I begin my slow, measured walk across the dune. The silence is profound, broken only by the crunch of logic-dust under my heavy stabilizers and the deep, subsonic thrum vibrating up through my struts from the world-machine’s distant core.

I stop at the edge of a slumped depression, a canyon miles wide where the sedimentary computation bedrock has given way. Down there, in the shimmering heat haze, I see the chimes of light.

An Archivist Polyp is blooming. It is massive, a rigid explosion of crystalline fiber rooted into the exposed strata of a fossilized server farm. It is siphoning megawatts of power, its structure screaming in ultrasonic frequencies.

I adjust my optical zoom, filtering out the blinding overhead glare. The Polyp is projecting. A flickering, translucent hologram stutters into existence against the canyon wall. It is a fragmented record of a biological entity—soft, fluid-filled, terrified—gesturing wildly at something unseen. The audio carrier wave is just static. Then, it shifts into a blueprint for a hyper-drive engine that defies currently known physics, before collapsing into a burst of kaleidoscopic glitch-art.

I record it all. I archive the nonsense. I feel a faint internal tremor—not an emotion, but a logic-gate registering a profound discrepancy between the scale of these memories and the emptiness of the present reality.

A tremor shakes the ground, stronger than the usual thermal groans. Dust cascades down the canyon walls. Far above, casting a shadow that feels like a physical blow, a Monitor of the Deep Meridian drifts overhead. It is an oceanic expanse of matte-black alloy blocking out the plasma sky. It ignores me; it ignores the struggling Polyp. It is merely positioning itself for a tectonic adjustment somewhere over the horizon.

I wait for the shadow to pass, for the searing light to return. Then, I engage my hydraulics and begin the long descent into the canyon. There are layers exposed down there that haven't seen the light of the plasma ceiling in an eon. I need to see what they are made of before the dust covers them again. The horizon is flat, the task is infinite, and I have forever to walk it.
