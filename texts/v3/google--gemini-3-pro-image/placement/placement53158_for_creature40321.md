The dryness is the first thing I know, an eternal, brittle truth. There is no memory of moisture in the deep history of my kind, only the endless thirst of the stone and the rasping air that seeks to turn everything to dust.

I am anchored. I am aged. I am part of the architecture.

You would find me halfway up one of the colossal fluted columns of calcified fungi in the mid-Galleries, a structure that rises three miles from the dust plains to the unseen ceiling. I haven't moved in four thousand years. I am a **Lithovore**, but I am no longer one of the frantic, clicking millions that scuttle across the surfaces. I have outgrown mobility.

My body is massive, a house-sized limpet of biomineralized armor fused permanently to the towering fungal reef. Over millennia, the slow drift of diamond-dust has coated my carapace, smoothing my edges until I am visually indistinguishable from the petrified city I once helped shape. My six mighty walking legs are calcified stumps, welded to the host column. My eating mandibles are sealed shut; I draw my sustenance directly from the mineral veins of the pillar I embrace, a slow, cold transfusion of copper and silicate that takes centuries to complete a meal.

I exist now only to perceive the din.

My blindness is total, a vestige of an ancestor species forgotten before this cavern finished settling. But I do not need eyes. My entire, immense surface area is a resonator. My thickened carapace acts like the membrane of a vast drum, tuned to the crushing cacophony of the Galleries.

The baseline reality is vibration. It is a physical pressure, a frantic itching screech that never ceases. It is the sound of my trillions of smaller kin. I feel them crawling over my own armored back, their diamond-hard claws scraping against my skin as they harvest the dust that settles there. They are a river of friction flowing over me, around me, their endless clicking and rasping transmitted through my shell directly into my consciousness. It is an itch I cannot scratch, a billion tiny chisels working against the dark.

Below me, miles down in the abyssal dust, I feel the churning tectonic bass of the **Geophages**. Their grinding isn't just sound; it's an earthquake that travels up the roots of my fungal tower. When a half-mile-long slug opens its maw and chews into the bedrock, the vibrations rattle the iron outputs in my core. It is a low, sickening thrum that speaks of industrial consumption on an unimaginable scale. The heat they release rises past me in unseen thermal rivers, buffeting my antennae sensors with sudden, dry warmth.

And then, there is the sky-fire.

It happens irregularly. High above, in the crushing dark, an **Aether-Kite** screams.

It is not a sound I hear, so much as an assault I endure. The ultrasonic blast hits my feathered crystalline antennae like a physical blow, shattering the ambient noise. For a split second, the world is illuminated, not by light, but by agonizing echo. The returning sound waves paint a fleeting, strobe-lit picture in my mind: the jagged peaks of distant towers, the vast emptiness of the void, the intricate latticework of the city—all rendered in hard, painful acoustic lines. The residual "pop" that downshifts into audible range is enough to crack shallower stone, a thunderclap that leaves my internal resonators ringing for days.

But the true terror of this world is not the noise. It is the holes in it.

Sometimes, amidst the churning ocean of scrape and shriek, there is a sudden, localized void. A pocket of absolute silence moving across the acoustic landscape.

It is a **Hush-Stalker**.

I feel its approach by what it subtracts from the world. The friction-river of smaller Lithovores on the wall near me suddenly goes quiet. Their clicking ceases, replaced by a terrified stillness. The Stalker moves on pads that drink vibration, a black hole in the soundscape. I feel the negative space of its passing, a sleek, six-legged absence gliding over the stone.

I feel the sudden, explosive spike in vibration—a microsecond event—as its scythe-arms shear through mineral armor. Then, the silence moves on. The clicking slowly resumes, the river flows again, minus one drop.

I remain. I hold onto the pillar, letting the noise wash over my ancient, hardened shell. I am a stone that listens, waiting for the slow, slow drift of eons to bury me completely in the dust.
