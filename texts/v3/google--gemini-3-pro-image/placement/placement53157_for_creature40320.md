The pressure is the first thing. It is not merely atmospheric; it is a physical crushing force, a density of superheated vapor that presses inwards from all sides. There is no relief from it, no cool breath, only the stifling, immobile weight of wet heat.

Then, the vibration. The world here does not exist in silence; it exists in a state of constant, bone-shaking resonance. A deep, tectonic thrum rises through the floor, traveling up through the corroded iron structures. Every few minutes, this bass rumble is punctured by a piercing, agonizing shriek as a steam vent cycles, a sound so intense it is felt as a tearing sensation rather than heard.

I am located high up in the cathedral gloom, perhaps a hundred feet above the sputtering red glow of the geothermal vents. The light up here is a frantic, dying amber flicker from bulbs that should have burnt out centuries ago.

I am clinging to the underside of a massive pipe, one wider than a subway tunnel. The pipe is scorching hot, vibrating with the steam rushing through it, and its iron skin is flaking away in scabrous layers.

I am not an individual. There is no "I" in the human sense, no coherent center of thought that surveys this industrial hellscape and feels horror. There is only sensation, reaction, and a terrible, driving hunger.

I am a dense, gelatinous extrusion of the Phage. My substance is thick, translucent, the color of bruised jaundice, heavily striated with gritty veins of orange rust I have harvested from the pipe I embrace. I am spread thin here, perhaps only an inch deep, coating a twenty-foot section of the metal artery like a layer of diseased mucus.

My existence is defined by slow, agonizingly deliberate movement. For cycles that might be days as measured by the surface world, I have been disturbed by a chemical gradient. Below me, across a chasm of swirling vapor, a massive shelf of purple bracket fungi has begun to decay, releasing a sickly-sweet plume of necrotic molecules that my porous surface tastes on the air.

Driven by mindless thermal and chemical tropism, I am shifting. A heavy, trembling pseudopod of my biomass is slowly pooling at my lowest point. Gravity fights my own extreme viscosity. I hang there, a colossal, semi-solid drip of living jelly, stretching downwards inch by agonizing inch toward the fungal shelf below.

As I stretch, the rust particles suspended within my flesh grind together. I feel the searing heat of the pipe I am leaving, and I anticipate the cooler, richer nutrients of the rotting fungi below. I am aware, in a decentralized, networked way, of the rest of the colony—the countless tons of us coating the screaming pistons in the dark, lubricating their grind with our own bodies just to steal the friction heat; the parts of us calcifying against the walls, becoming rock.

We are the stomach that wears this dungeon as a skin. I am just one slow, hungry mouth, dripping through the dark, waiting to feed.
