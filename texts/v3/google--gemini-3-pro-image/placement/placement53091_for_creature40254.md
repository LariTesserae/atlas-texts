The silence of the plateau is not an absence; it is a heavy, pressurized presence. It is the held breath of a capacitor waiting to discharge.

In this world of arrested violence and crystalline stillness, I am the smallest possible unit of change.

I am not one of the massive, tectonic Lithovores, though I am destined to become part of one. I am not a rooted copper tree, though I share its ancestry.

I am a mote of native silver dust, spun into being from the hot, metal-rich atmosphere by a freak discharge of static electricity near a volcanic vent. I am microscopic, a gleaming, conductive speck suspended in the thin, violet air.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive the world with agonizing clarity. I "see" in magnetic fields and thermal gradients. The sky above is not a color, but a terrifying vacuum of cold, a void pulling at my tiny mass. Below, the jagged basalt ground is a roaring furnace of infrared radiation, a wrinkled blanket of heat that keeps me buoyant.

The landscape is a chaotic map of energy. The dendritic forests of copper and hematite glow to my electromagnetic sense like fiery nervous systems, channeling the immense power from the subterranean source. I drift on unseen currents of voltage, blown not by wind, but by the pushes and pulls of electrostatic charges.

I tumble past razor-edge obsidian shelves that would slice soft flesh into ribbons; to me, they are just cool, insulative shadows. I drift through a grove of sulfur crystals, feeling their delicate, feathery structures as dead zones of resistance against the prevailing current.

A greater attractive force seizes me. It is a deep, resonant magnetic pull, far stronger than the ambient background hum of the plateau. I am accelerated downward, falling toward a faceted mountain of dull iron and gleaming silicate hexagons.

It is an Elder Lithovore, ancient beyond reckoning, anchored near a massive verdigris outcrop. To my sensory input, it is a blazing sun of geothermal warmth on the dark ground, its armored hide shimmering with the flow of tapped electricity. It is so still it appears dead, but the magnetic aura radiating from it is deafeningly alive.

I am pulled relentlessly toward its flank. I strike a hexagonal plate of basalt near the creature’s "shoulder"—a junction where a thick vein of conductive silver breaks the surface.

There is no sound of impact in the thin air. There is only connection.

Instantly, my drift ends. The moment I touch the conductive vein, I am welded to the carapace by the intense localized electrolysis. I cease to be a free-floating mote. I become an extension of the Lithovore’s sensor grid.

The planetary hum, which before was an external buffeting force, now rushes *through* me. I feel the immense, slow voltage that sustains the behemoth. And beneath that current, I feel something else.

A vibration. Tectonic in its slowness, vast in its wavelength. It is a thought, forming in the silicon-and-metal core of the creature. It is a thought that began forming fifty years ago and is only now reaching the surface.

I am now a tiny, shining facet on an immortal body. I am a new nerve ending on an ancient skin. My existence as an individual speck is over; my existence as a watcher on the plateau has just begun. I settle in to wait for the next thousand years, feeling the sun as a rhythmic pressure of heat, waiting for the next atom of silver to land beside me and continue the slow work of growing a mountain.
