The air tastes of cold pennies and wet slate. It is a thick, motionless chill that settles low in the lungs, a permanent resident of this place. I am standing on a platform hewn roughly from the flank of a massive, semi-translucent quartz pillar that rises out of the twilight below and vanishes into the darker expanse above.

I am a **Bridge-Tender of the Middle Fissures**.

My station is a critical junction in the sprawling, parasitic web of the city. Below me, a chasm drops away into true darkness, broken only by the faint, glittering luminescence of distant cooking fires clinging to the cliff faces like barnacles. Spanning this void is my charge: the Great Amethyst Span. It is a terrifying piece of engineering—thick ropes woven from imported surface-wool and coated in tar, holding planks of cold-hardened timber that groan under foot traffic. It anchors on my side to the quartz pillar, and on the far side to a jagged cliff of raw, purple crystal that looks like a frozen explosion.

I am built for this work. My legs are short and thick, my center of gravity low, planted firmly in boots shod with iron hobnails that bite into the perpetually slick rock. I am wrapped in layers of dense felt that smell of old oil and damp sheep. The outer layer has stiffened over the cycles, coated in the mineral residue of constant dripping, so that when I move, my sleeves crackle faintly, sparkling with a dusting of crystalline powder.

I draw a hand across my face, feeling the familiar topography of my own skin. It is a map of contradictions. The skin on my wrist, exposed where my glove rides up, is the color of skim milk, veined with pale blue. But my face feels like cured leather. It is deeply tanned and premature wrinkled, the eyes permanently narrowed into slits surrounded by a dense crow's feet network—the mark of anyone who works near the Sun-Shafts.

Currently, my sector is submerged in the glittering twilight that defines most of our waking hours. The light is dim, indirect, bouncing off a million facets of quartz, turning the air into a shimmering, confusing haze of greys and deep violets. It is quiet. The immense weight of the earth above us smothers sound. I hear the rhythmic *clink-clink* of a mason’s chisel somewhere far above, and the pervasive, metronomic *drip… drip… drip* of condensation falling into the stillness pools.

My sounding-pick hangs at my belt, its iron head heavy against my thigh. I unhook it. This is my primary tool. I step out onto the anchor point of the great bridge, where the main cabling bolts into the quartz. I tap the rock. *Tink*. It’s high, tight. Good tension.

Then I walk to the edge of the platform, where the quartz meets a vein of darker, unstable basalt. I tap there. *Thunk*.

Lower pitch. Softer.

I lean in, squinting in the gloom, tracing a hairline fracture that wasn't there three sleep-cycles ago. The basalt is giving way to the pressure of the growing crystal pillar beside it. The geology here is not dead; it is merely slow, violently slow. This fracture means the entire eastern anchor is shifting, perhaps only a millimeter a month, but shifting nonetheless.

I take a piece of chalk from my pouch and mark the fissure. Later, I will have to report this to a Listener clan. They will calculate the stress and tell me how many turns of the great iron winches are needed to slacken the bridge cables, feeding it more rope so the growing chasm doesn’t snap the spine of our transit system.

A sudden change in the air pressure makes my ears pop. A low, vibrating hum begins in the distance, deeper than thunder, felt in the bone more than heard. The Groan. Somewhere deep below, a tectonic plate has slipped. The bridge cables sway gently, creaking. I don't move; I just grip the railing and wait, breathing in the metallic dampness, waiting for the shudder to pass. We are all just fleas on the back of a sleeping titan that occasionally twitches.

As the groan subsides, the light changes.

Far above and to the west, one of the great oculi—the sun-shafts—has aligned with the surface noon. A column of solid, ferocious daylight crashes down through the kilometers of darkness. It hits a cluster of cathedral-sized crystals a mile away from my station.

The effect is instantaneous and violent. The cavern goes from twilight to a blinding, kaleidoscopic explosion. The crystals refract the sunlight, shattering it into intense beams of violet, gold, and searing white light that lance across the void, illuminating dust motes dancing in the cold air. One refracted beam hits the amethyst cliff opposite me, making the entire wall glow as if molten.

I instinctively shield my eyes, my squint tightening further. Even this far away, the UV intensity feels like a physical weight on my exposed face. This is the heartbeat of our world: the slow, terrifying dance of living rock, illuminated by sudden, lethal bursts of heaven.

I hook my sounding-pick back onto my belt. The bridge is holding. The rock is shifting. I have work to do.
