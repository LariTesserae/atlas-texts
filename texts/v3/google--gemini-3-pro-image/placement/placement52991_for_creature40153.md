The first thing you know is the grip.

It is an unending, desperate isometric contraction. You are dangling upside down, kilometers of humid, screaming emptiness beneath your spine. Your six limbs are locked onto a vine the size of a subway train; the curved bone-spurs protruding from your wrists and ankles are sunk deep into its fibrous, wet bark. You don't consciously think about holding on. Holding on is your baseline state of being. To let go is to cease to exist.

You are a Hyaline Climber, a translucent ghost haunting the underside of a colossal city-beam.

If you look down at your own torso, you see the world through it. Your skin is a tough, gelatinous membrane, clear as quartz. Beneath it, the frantic machinery of your own biology is exposed: the rapid pulsing of thick purple veins, the grayish churn of digestive organs, and the frantic flutter of a heart working overtime against the persistent drag of gravity.

The second thing you know is the Roar. It is not merely sound; it is a physical pressure that presses against your exposed eardrums and vibrates the wet cartilage of your skeleton. The foundational vibration is the low-frequency thrum of the lattice itself, the groan of a trillion tons of matte-black composite constantly re-settling itself. Layered over that is the acoustic violence of the wind howling through voids wider than oceans.

Because of the Roar, you do not speak. You shout with light.

Your nervous system is lined with bioluminescent nodes that trace the architecture of your clear body. Right now, in the dim, indigo twilight filtering through the giant sail-leaves, you and your troupe are flashing frantic patterns of electric blue.

*<Hold. Freeze. Predator-Echo felt.>*

The silent language flickers between the twenty members of your clan, clinging to the underside of the massive hanging jungle. You caught the sonar pulse in your teeth a moment ago—a sharp, piercing vibration that signals a Sky-Lancer is hunting in the structural canyon nearby.

You press your translucent body flatter against the vine. Through the gaps in the enormous purple foliage, you see the Void. It is a hazy, terrifying expanse of dropping distances, punctuated by distant spars that look like hazy black threads.

A blur of slate-grey tears past the opening. It is moving so fast it seems to warp the air around it. The eight axial wings of the Sky-Lancer are vibrating into invisibility, a rigid tube of muscle and leather designed to kill without ever slowing down. It shrieks—a sound that cuts through the background roar like a scalpel—and dives into a lower stratum of the lattice, chasing some unseen heat signature.

The blue lights of your troupe shift instantly to a soothing, rolling green pattern.

*<Clear. Move. Ascend to the joint.>*

Movement is a calculated risk. You unlock your lower limbs, swing your clear body out over the abyss, and reach for a higher purchase on a thick, vascular root growing directly out of the black composite girder. You are climbing toward a "joint"—a massive intersection of structural spars where moisture pools and the Rust-Molds grow house-sized. That’s where the heat is, and where the smaller insects gather.

As you pull yourself up onto the metallic ledge of the girder, your bone-spurs scraping against the crystalline outcroppings, the ground beneath you *ticks*.

It’s a sound louder than thunder, a tectonic adjustment of the lattice. The entire mountainside of metal you are standing on shifts three meters to the left. You flatten yourself, gripping instinctively with all six limbs.

A section of the black composite near your head glows suddenly with sharp, geometric light patterns. A hairline fracture has appeared. Before you can scuttle away, a panel slides open silently, revealing an interior that smells not of wet jungle and ozone, but of sterilized ceramics and cold vacuum.

A Silicate emerges. It is sleek, multi-pedal, and glowing with internal industry. It flows over the surface like mercury on glass, its optical sensors glittering coldly as it assesses the fracture. It is an engineer of the infinite structure, a gardener of the steel bones.

It passes within inches of you. It does not register your presence. To the Silicate, you—with your wet organs and frantic heart and bioluminescent shouting—are just part of the ablative layer of noise that coats the outside of its perfect machine. You are barnacles on the hull of reality.

It repairs the fracture with a flash of coherent light and slips back inside, sealing the smooth black wall behind it, shutting out the roar and the damp.

You are left alone on the ledge in the warm twilight, shivering slightly. Your lights flash a solitary, contemplative yellow. You are a small, clear thing clinging to a monster that doesn't know you exist, eating its refuse, dodging its predators, forever one slipped grip away from the long fall into the dark.

You shake off the dampness, turn your attention back to the jungle hanging above you, and begin the climb again.
