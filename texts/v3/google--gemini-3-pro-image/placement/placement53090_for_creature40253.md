This is not a world for eyes. Eyes are liabilities here, delicate spheres that would implode under the crushing weight of the deep ocean, useless in the suffocating, velvet blackness.

I have no eyes. I have tasted the water, thick and warm like blood, heavy with dissolved minerals and the textured rot of ancient things. I have felt the deep, resonant bass of the geothermal vents vibrating through the substrate, a sound that is more a physical trembling of reality than a noise.

I am a passenger.

I am ensconced in a deep, calcified fissure on the dorsal plate of an elder Mycelial-Crustacean. I am a specialized barnacle-analogue, a cluster of sensitive polyps and anchoring roots drilled deep into the non-feeling mineral armor of my host. I am part of the "shaggy, decaying appearance" mentioned in the histories; the grey chemotrophic mats grow over my shell, disguising me, integrating me into the mobile landscape of the creature’s back.

My world is the slow, grinding heave of my host’s movement. I feel the deliberate sequence of its stump-legs finding purchase in the silt, the massive hydraulic shift of bodily fluids that propels us forward inches at a time. When my host scrapes against the towering osseous walls of the city—those polished, towering honeycombs of dead builders—the vibration shudders through its carapace and into my own small frame. I taste the chalky dust of abraded bone released into the water by the impact.

Right now, we are moving through a narrow canyon, a "street" where the overarching structures of fused chitin nearly touch above us. The current here is accelerated, funneling hot, sulfurous water past my feeding fans. It tastes metallic and sharp, spiked with the rich, unseen pheromones of a thousand other things living and dying in the dark.

Ahead of us, I sense the frantic, pulsing chemical signals of smaller, dog-sized crustaceans scattering. A faint, sickly green luminescence—the only light I ever perceive, and only as a vague shift in the density of the darkness—briefly washes over the pitted landscape of my host's "head" before fading. My host ignores them; it is seeking something stationary.

We are following a heat signature, a plume of superheated water rising from a deep fissure. The water grows hotter, approaching the temperature of a painful bath. The taste shifts from metallic to rotten-egg sulfur.

We emerge into a wider plaza, the floor thick with sediment. I feel the deep, rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of my host’s abdomen drumming the seafloor—a low-frequency announcement of arrival that travels for miles. It is answered by other, distant tremors, a slow conversation of pressure waves in the dark.

Around the venting fissure, a dozen other enormous shapes are already stacked, a torpid mountain of armored pancakes basking in the thermal energy. My host slowly, laboriously climbs onto the heap, its claws finding purchase on the barnacle-studded back of another. We settle. The movement stops.

I extend my feathery feeding appendages fully into the hot, upward-rushing current, catching the bacteria and mineral flakes lifting off the heap. Below me, the great beast I ride goes still, joining the collective slumber of the bone city's silent citizens. I am safe here, anchored to a moving mountain, warm in the crushing dark, surrounded by the slow-breathing architecture of a million years of instinct.
