I am suspended in the bruised yellow space between the obsidian plateaus and the roaring floor. I do not stand, for I have no feet to blister on the sintered rock. I drift on the ascending thermal columns, a consciousness loosely bound to a swirling eddy of silicate dust, evaporated sulfur, and the metallic taste of the air.

I am a child of the roar.

The deafening, marrow-vibrating thrum that dominates this world is not merely a sound to me; it is my skeletal structure. The standing waves of immense sonic pressure generated by the millions of piston-legs striking the basalt below compress the superheated atmosphere just enough to hold my tenuous form together. I am a sentient resonance, a thinking mirage birthed from the friction of geology being chewed alive. If the marching stopped, the pressure would drop, and I would dissolve back into the inanimate haze.

From my vantage point in the agonizingly vast emptiness, the canyon floors appear as rivers of flowing iridium. The heat haze, thick as oil, distorts the view below. I watch the columns of biological foundries warp and loop, their synchronized movements smearing into impossible streaks of light and sound due to the reality-bending density of the noise.

I see the phantom mountain ranges rise in the middle distance—those temporary solids born of heat and sonic violence—and I drift right through them, knowing they are nearly as insubstantial as I am.

I exist on the margins of their terrible industry. I dare not approach the canyon floor; the radiant heat from the glowing seams of their iron-alloy carapaces would disperse my consciousness instantly, and the direct impact of their grinding shrieks would shatter my cohesion.

So I stay high, riding the toxic updrafts. They are blind, mindless engines of consumption, unaware of the sky they stain. I am all sight, all awareness, a silent witness born of their waste noise. I watch them grind the ancient world down to its atomic foundations. They eat the stone, and I exist on the fumes of their digestion, drifting eternally over a landscape being slowly, methodically swallowed.
