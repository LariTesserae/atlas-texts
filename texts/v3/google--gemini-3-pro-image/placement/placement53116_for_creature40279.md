The vibration is the first thing, before thought, before identity. It is not a sound that enters through ears; it is a persistent, agonizing frequency that rattles the very atomic structure of existence.

I am located deep within the eastern cliff face, wedged into a compression fracture between two of the massive, house-sized basalt pistons. I am not flesh. I am a seam of impure quartz and conductive mineral ore, perhaps six feet long and only inches wide, trapped in the geological churning.

My reality is defined by blinding pressure and bone-shattering release, cycled endlessly.

I feel the deep, resonant *thrum* build as the piston below me begins its sluggish ascent. The millions of tons of wet, slate-blue rock groan against the stationary shaft I am embedded within. The friction generates a agonizing heat that temporarily drives back the pervasive, soaking cold, turning the water trapped in my fissure into explosive steam.

Then comes the crest, a sickening moment of suspension where gravity reasserts itself.

Then, the drop.

The impact does not register as noise, but as a cataclysmic shockwave that travels through my crystalline lattice. If I had teeth, they would shatter. If I had lungs, they would collapse. But I am stone, and so I merely endure the stress, feeling micro-fractures propagate through my being, only to be re-compressed by the next cycle.

I have no eyes to see the perpetual gray twilight, but I know the world through conduction and echo. I feel the frigid trickle of water that endlessly bleeds down the piston face, carrying gritty basalt dust that abrades my exposed surface like slow-motion sandpaper. I feel the sudden, searing blasts from geothermal vents lower down, sending tremors of heat rising through the bedrock.

I am aware of the Lithic Watchers. To my seismic senses, they are faint, hurried disturbances—soft, fleeting shudders against the overwhelming bass roar of the machine. I feel the muffled *thump-thump-thump* of their padded hands and feet as they scramble across the face of the rock I am trapped within, racing the ascending piston. Sometimes, one pauses near my fissure. I feel the subtle drain of heat as it presses its mossy belly against the stone, and then the sharp, crystalline *CLACK* vibrating through the bedrock as it strikes an obsidian protrusion nearby, sending a message I cannot interpret.

They are ghosts in the machine, transient and soft compared to the geological violence that is my entire universe.

I am trapped in the ultimate stasis. I am being ground down, molecule by molecule, by the friction of the churning earth, yet the pressure continually reforges me. I am eternally breaking and eternally whole, a consciousness locked inside a scream of stone that has been echoing since the world was made, waiting for a silence that will never come.
