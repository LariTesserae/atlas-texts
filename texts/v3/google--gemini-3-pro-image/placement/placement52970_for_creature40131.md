The dampness finds you first. It is not a passive wetness, but an aggressive, intrusive saturation that works its way through the oiled wool and cured leather of your outer casings, settling into the marrow of your bones like a persistent ache. You are heavy. Your movements are hydrologic, slow and pressurized, fighting not just the erratic gravity but the sheer accumulated weight of your own protection.

You are standing on a grate of rusted iron, vibrating so intensely that your teeth chatter in a perpetual, subconscious rhythm. You know this vibration; it is the combined song of a thousand tectonic pile-drivers hammering into the birthing rock of the peaks. It travels up through the magnetic soles of your boots, past the scarred flesh of your shins, and registers in your skull as the only trustworthy map of reality.

You cannot see the sky, nor the abyss below. Your world is restricted by the brass frames of your goggles, fitted with lenses so thick and smoked they reduce the terrifying sprawl of the city to a murky, fractured mosaic. You see only what you need to operate: the three feet of walkway ahead, the bleeding orange rivet that needs tightening, the handle of the winch. Peripheral vision is a luxury you surrendered long ago; to see the periphery is to risk seeing the horizon tilt ninety degrees, and that way lies madness.

You are a Bridge Tender. You belong to the caste of Anchorites charged with maintaining the tenuous tensile connections between the shuddering basalt crags.

Your left arm is your own, thick with muscle developed solely for pulling levers against stubborn resistance. Your right arm ended at the elbow years ago during a rock-shear incident. Now, it is capped with a crude, steam-assisted hydraulic pincer, jury-rigged from salvaged excavator parts. It leaks warm, viscous fluid that stains your sleeve black, smelling of burned sugar and ozone. You prefer the mechanical arm; it does not tremble when the void calls.

Currently, you are kneeling—a slow, multi-stage process of lowering your center of gravity—at a junction point where a suspension bridge meets the raw rock of "Sector 7G-Emergent." The rock here is hot to the touch, steaming as the ambient moisture hits it. It is newborn stone, thrust up from below only yesterday.

You raise a massive wrench with your hydraulic pincer. The task is simple: snug down a bolt the size of a human head that anchors a stabilizing cable.

You swing the wrench.

There is no sound of impact. The heavy iron tool passes effortlessly, silently, through the bolt and sinks into the steel girder behind it, as if plunging into gray water. Your mechanical arm follows, passing through the solid structure up to the hydraulic cuff.

You do not recoil. You do not curse. You have trained yourself out of the human reflex of surprise. Inside your sensory deprivation hood, your breath catches in the respirator filters with a wet rasp, but you hold your position. You remain perfectly still, your prosthetic limb buried inside the ghost-architecture of the bridge.

You wait. You listen to the conduction of sound through your bones, feeling for the rhythm of the world to reset. You count the tectonic thuds.

*Thump.* (The metal around your arm remains intangible mist.)

*Thump.* (A flicker in your fractured vision; a building across the chasm momentarily appears to be made of glass before solidifying into slate.)

*Thump.* (Reality snaps back with an almost audible crack.)

Your wrench clangs violently against solid metal, sending a jolt up your hydraulic piston. The girder is real again. You exhale slowly, a plume of oily vapor escaping your mask. You apply pressure. The hydraulics whine, the metal shrieks, and the great bolt turns half a degree.

Job done. You begin the slow, arduous process of standing up, your knees popping in sync with the gears of your environment. A patch of iridescent copper moss brushes against your helmet as you rise; it has already begun to colonize your shoulder plate. You are heavy, you are numb, and you are enduring. You trudge toward the next rivet, a small, moving piece of rust on the face of a screaming machine.
