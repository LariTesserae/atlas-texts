The heat finds you first. It is not a passive element of the environment; it is an aggressive, physical weight. It presses against the slick rind of your shoulders, a humid blanket heavy with the scent of accelerated decay—a cloying, rich perfume of fermenting sugar and mushroom spore.

You are standing on a surface that gives slightly, a dense sponge of woven roots that breathes warm vapor against the soles of your feet. The air you draw in is so thick it feels nutritional, coating your throat with moisture.

The sound is deafening, a physical vibration that travels up through the spongy floor and into your marrow. It is a unending groan, the sound of a thousand ship hulls splintering simultaneously, overlaid with the high-pitched shriek of something amphibian being hunted in the canopy above. Yet, you do not flinch. To you, this isn't noise; it is the hum of the city's metabolism operating at peak efficiency. Silence would mean death.

You are tall, heavy, yet you feel light, constantly shedding weight. You look down at your forearm, thick and curved like a polished segment of dark violet shelf-fungus. A translucent layer of epidermis is peeling away from the wrist, sloughing off like wet tissue paper. It dissolves into the slick moss at your feet almost before it touches the ground. You feel a cool tingle of renewal beneath it. You are perpetually molting, a river of flesh flowing into the compost beneath you.

You have no urge to speak. Your throat lacks the architecture for it, and the roar of the growing city would swallow any sound you could make. Instead, you communicate with the air itself.

You flare the complex, fleshy lattices clustered where a human nose and mouth would be. The membranes ripple, tasting the dense currents of the atmosphere. It is a chaotic stew of information. You taste the copper tang of a structural stress pheromone drifting from a burl-hive three kilometers downwind; you taste the dusty, musky spore-packet that carries architectural blueprints for a new spire extension. You filter through the noise, the biological static, hunting for your task.

There. A distinct sharp flavor, acidic and urgent: a major arterial vine-bridge nearby has completed its lifecycle and collapsed into nutrient-sludge.

You move with a slow, swinging gait, your large, multifaceted eyes cutting through the aqueous yellow-green haze. You see the world in thermal blooms and chemical trails. The bioluminescent fungi lining the path pulse with a cool indigo rhythm that matches the faint glow along your own spine.

You arrive at the chasm. The previous bridge is already gone, just a smear of brown slime on the opposite bank. The gap is fifty feet across, a drop into a churning lagoon of warm mud below.

You do not panic. You are a gardener of velocity.

You kneel at the lip of the chasm, placing your four-fingered palm flat against the weeping, raw edge of the root-substrate. You focus. The porous pads at your fingertips tingle as you excrete a precise cocktail of hyper-auxins and viral catalysts.

The response is instantaneous. The ground beneath your hand convulses. The roaring sound intensifies, shifting pitch into a scream of rapid cellular mitosis. Thick, pale green tendrils erupt from the bank, thicker than your thigh, lashing out across the void like striking cobras. They meet in the middle, braiding, fusing, thickening, their bark hardening from pale green to woody brown in seconds. Steam pours off the new structure as it cooks itself into existence.

You stand, the vibration of the new growth rattling your chest. Before the steam has even cleared, before the new bridge has finished groaning into its final shape, you step onto it. It is firm, warm, and alive beneath your feet. It will last perhaps an hour before it needs to die and return to the compost.

You walk across, already tasting the air for the next urgent flavor, a calm center of patience inside the roaring, rotting miracle you help sustain. You are a Rot-Stoker, and the compost heap is hungry.
