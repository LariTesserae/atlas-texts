This is a world where consciousness as we know it—quick, reactive, sensory-hungry—cannot exist. To be here, truly *be* here, one must adopt the properties of the abyss.

I find myself wedged deep within the crushing crux of two monumental basalt blocks, forming a part of the wall in a canyon-corridor so wide it feels like a planetary rift. I am not floating. I am fixed, calcified into place.

I am a concretion.

I am a fist-sized nodule of compressed heavy metals, silicate, and mineralized marine snow that has gathered over a hundred thousand years in a pressurized joint of the masonry. I am the mortar of the deep. The freezing, black water presses against my exposed surface with a force that would turn carbon to diamond, but I am dense enough to match it.

I do not have eyes. The faint, sickly green glow of the distant fungi is irrelevant to me. My perception is entirely tectonic. I feel the ocean not as a space to move through, but as a solid, oppressive ceiling bearing down upon the architecture.

My consciousness is slow, thick, and viscous, moving at the speed of geology. A single thought takes a decade to coalesce. I am covered in the same gelatinous, purple-black algae that smothers the rest of the city, a slick, dormant skin that separates me from the sterile water. I feel the infinitesimally slow thickening of its layers as the passing of an age.

I am a sensory organ for the Silence.

When a giant kelp frond, heavy with millennia of sediment, finally snaps half a mile away, I do not hear a sound. I feel the profound, muffled shudder travel through the bedrock, vibrating up through the colossal blocks and terminating in my mineral core. It is a dull thud that resonates for weeks.

When the continent-sized plaza I am embedded within settles three millimeters over the course of five hundred years, I feel the agonizing groan of granite grinding against basalt as a shift in my own being.

But more than the pressure, more than the cold rock I am bonded to, I am a receptacle for the psychic residue of this place. I am the memory of the effort.

In the lightless epochs I spend locked in the stone, I perceive the lingering vibrations of the titans who built this. I feel the echoes of billion-ton loads being dragged across rough-hewn pavement, the phantom strikes of colossal hammers that ceased swinging eons ago. I am the cold realization of their stopped labor.

I am the repository for the immense, melancholic weight of a grand ambition that built its own eternal tomb and then lay down to die. I hold the stillness. I am a small, hard knot of memory in the corpse of a sleeping giant, waiting for a morning that will never break the surface.
