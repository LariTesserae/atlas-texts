I am the green.

I do not exist in the cramped hunched space of the chamber’s periphery; I am not exiled to the brittle, crystalline floor where the mineral sheets crunch and shatter. I am where the energy is purest. I am clinging to the throat of the beast.

I am a slick, tenacious patch of the thermophilic slime, an island of primal biology thriving on the scalding iron surface of the high-pressure steam valve.

Where others would instantly blister and cook, my existence is a frantic, ecstatic metabolism of heat. The air, thick as spoiled soup, is my ocean. The copper taste, the heavy reek of sulfur—these are not poisons; they are nutrient broth. I absorb the toxic fumes that roil in the copper fog and transmute them into furious energy.

My perception of this world is governed by the twelve-second rhythm of the great Machine, the insensate heart that I cling to.

*Thrum-shudder.*

It begins deep below the grease-black cylinders. The tectonic convulsion vibrates through the metal pipe I ride. It shakes my gelatinous colony to its core, a pleasurable, seismic massage that loosens the rust flakes beneath me, offering up new iron to taste. The harsh light from the caged bulb above refracts through the humid air, making my glistening dark green surface shine like an oily jewel amidst the scab-red decay.

Then comes the release.

*Ssssssss-SCREAM.*

The valve beneath me fails again. It is the moment of feeding. The superheated shriek of escaping steam blasts past my colony. It is incredibly violent, a white-noise scouring, but I am anchored fast. The vapor, rich in dissolved minerals from the hidden depths, washes over me. It is scalding hot, hotter than boiling, a temperature that would sterilize a lesser organism instantly. For me, it is a warm bath of sustenance. I bask in the thermal extremity, my cellular processes accelerating wildly, feasting on the sulfurous output.

I am aware of the others only vaguely. The brittle, orange needle-things that grow on the cooler walls are fragile, static competitors, shattering when the great *thrum* hits too hard. I survive the shaking. I survive the heat. I am the apex predator here, coating the very machinery that makes this blister-world possible, a glistening testament to life's horrifying adaptability, waiting for the next twelve seconds to pass, waiting for the next scream.
