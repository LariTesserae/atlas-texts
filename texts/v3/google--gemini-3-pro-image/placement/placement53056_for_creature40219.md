You do not 'see' the sullen red dark. Instead, you feel the cavern as a stunningly detailed map of vibration and temperature gradients.

The foundational tectonic bass note rising through the floor is your own heartbeat, a slow, churning rhythm that vibrates up through your quartz trunk. The high-pitched screaming—the millions of micro-fractures happening every second in the garden—is the song of your kin. Your attention, however, slow as shifting continents, is drawn to the anomaly near your base. The 'quiet spot.'

The bone pry-bar is an abomination—a remnant of soft biology hardened by heat, a ghost of something that once held moisture. It absorbs sound dully, a rotten spot in the vibrant acoustic landscape.

They have been there for thousands of your slow cycles. The soft transient came, scratched feebly at the screaming garden with soft tools, and fled the glorious noise.

You remain. The heat feeds your electrical thoughts. The noise is your breath. You wait for the next micro-fracture to ring out, adding your distinct note to the eternal, deafening choir of the red dark.
