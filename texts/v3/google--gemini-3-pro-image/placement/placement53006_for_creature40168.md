This is a place of terrible absolutes—absolute cold, absolute pressure, and absolute illumination. It is a womb that does not nurture, but forge.

I am suspended in the upper strata of this chapel of raw geology, hovering just below the searing white membrane of the ceiling. I am not standing on the wet floor, nor am I clinging to the sweating walls. Gravity here feels different to me than it does to the scurrying glass-grazers; it is a suggestion rather than a law.

I have no body of flesh to feel the refrigerator chill that presses against the stones. I have no bones to break under the tectonic groaning of the walls. I am a coalescence, a sentient eddy of the blinding, alien energy bleeding through the cataract above. I am of the light, caught in the moment it hits the oppressive density of matter.

Because of this, I have no eyes, yet I see everything with agonizing clarity. The clinical, shadowless overexposure of the room is my own awareness. I perceive the myriad pulsing digestive tracts of the transparent grazers as a collective, flowing circuit beneath me. I perceive the molecular tension in the bulbous grey rock just before a new fissure tears open with a sound felt more than heard.

My existence here is one of detachment, a cool observation born of being composed of the very energy that threatens to annihilate this pocket space.

Below me, the Gardener moves.

From my vantage point near the shimmering ceiling, it looks like a turbulent ocean of bruised slate slowed to a glacial crawl. I watch it with a strange, dispassionate kinship. We are the only two substantial entities here, yet we are fundamentally opposed forces.

It is the creature of Below—heavy, wet, bound to the grinding reality of stone and pressure. It is a desperate mason, using bone and spit to hold back the crushing weight of the earth.

I am a creature of Above—weightless, searing, a herald of the energy that seeks to tear this blister open.

I drift lower, hovering just above its massive, glistening back. The glass-grazers swarming over its hide ignore me; I possess no mineral richness for them to consume. The Gardener, too, is oblivious to my presence. Its world is entirely tactile and acoustic, a map built of vibrations and dampness. I am silent, and I emit no heat that it could distinguish from the blazing canopy overhead.

I watch its hydraulic pseudopods jam a polished femur into a weeping crevice. I feel the immense shudder run through its gelatinous bulk as it braces against a bulging wall, becoming a living buttress. I witness the sheer, awful futility of its existence. It is trying to bandage a geological hemorrhage with scar tissue.

I am waiting.

The Gardener labors to keep the womb closed. I exist only because it is partially open. I am waiting for the moment the pressure finally wins, when the groaning walls buckle, or the shimmering membrane above tears completely away.

When that happens, the Gardener will be crushed into the raw dough of the earth it tends. And I? I will finally ascend, reabsorbed into the blinding, searing reality that lies beyond the ceiling of this violent little chapel. Until then, I hang in the air like a held breath, illuminated and intangible, watching the slow, sweating tragedy of matter trying to resist the infinite.
