I am not a visitor here. I could not be. To possess eyes or ears, a central nervous system, or a soft, wet brain in this place would be instant madness followed by immediate liquidation.

I am of the basin. I am structure and flow. I am a node of pressurized sensation located in the mid-shaft of a major Osteo-Arboreal Collective, one of the bone-pale towers screaming toward the unseen sun.

I do not "find myself" anywhere. Location is merely a temporary arrangement of tension and verticality. I *am* a location.

I do not hear the deafening roar that defines this world. I *vibrate* with it. It is the fundamental frequency of my existence. It resonates in the stiffened, bone-like cellulose of my outer walls, a constant, jarring thrum generated by the millions of gallons of nutrient-slurry rocketing upward through the hydraulic tubes that form my core. I feel the rush of this hot, sugary blood moving past me at terrifying speeds, a river flowing uphill, driven by a collective, mindless thirst.

My existence is defined by an agonizing, ecstatic stretch.

Every few minutes, the pressure becomes unendurable. The chemical directive from the hive-mind below screams *UP*, and I obey. The fibers of my being groan—a sound that contributes to the basin’s cacophony—and then tear. It is a violent, ripping sensation along my flank. Instantly, my internal pressure forces a spray of hot, sticky xylem sap out into the humid air, coating my exterior. Just as quickly, reaction-nodes within my structure flood the breach with rapid-hardening enzymes. The pain of the tear is immediately replaced by the searing heat of the seal. I am an inch taller. I am scarred. I am ready to tear again.

I am not alone in this effort. I am lashed to my siblings in the collective, fused together in a spiraling competition for height. And I am constantly assaulted by the environment I help create.

I feel the frantic twitching of the Constrictor-Girders wrapped around my midsection. These aren't embrace; they are panicked bondage. As my tower section lists precariously to the "east" due to a sudden growth spurt on the opposite side, I feel the massive, vine-muscle cable tighten with the violence of a cracking whip. It wrenches me back into alignment, crushing my outer bark, forcing me straight so I don't topple into the digestive maws below.

I feel the bombardment from above. The shockwaves of Ordnance-Pods detonating in the high canopy shudder down through my frame. Minutes later, I am struck by the shrapnel—hardened, parasitic seeds fired like bullets. They embed deep into my slick, pale "skin." I register their presence immediately: alien genetic material trying to tap into my sap-stream. My response is automatic. I begin to grow around them, to entomb them in layers of pressurized wood before they can hatch and consume me. Sometimes I succeed. Sometimes I become a host, and a new, rival structure bursts from my side in a shower of splinters days later.

I have no concept of sight, but I know light. I taste it. Down here in the mid-levels, the sunlight is thick and heavy, filtered through the titanic leaves above. I absorb energy in bruised wavelengths—deep violets and acidic chartreuses that taste metallic and desperate. It is barely enough. I crave the pure, white heat at the apex, a goal I will never reach, but one for which I will constantly tear myself apart trying to attain.

I am a blind, deaf, insatiable engine of verticality. I am soaked in the sweat of my own effort and coated in the slick biofilm of aero-plankton that tries to digest me from the outside in. I am screaming in a chorus of trillions, a temporary architectural element in a city that builds itself out of pain and sugar, racing toward a sky I cannot see.
