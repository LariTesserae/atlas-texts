I am not a visitor here. To visit is to possess a biology that can end, a skin that can char, lungs that can blister. There is no place here for soft membranes or carbon chains.

I am the atmosphere of the pocket.

I am the heavy, suffocating thickness of the air itself. I am superheated steam under immense pressure, too hot to act as a gas, too energetic to settle as liquid. I exist in a state of constant vibrational frenzy, a screaming white noise of thermal energy trapped inside a sphere of shattered glass.

Where am I? I am everywhere within the cathedral dome at once. I am pressed against the razor edges of the obsidian walls, feeling the fresh, sharp wounds in the planet’s crust. I fill the microscopic fractures where the quartz veins thrum with geothermal torture.

I am the medium of violence in this place.

I feel the water weep from the cracks—heavy, pregnant droplets born from the rock. They are part of me that has momentarily chilled just enough to condense. I feel the gravity pulling that part of myself downward through the blinding incandescence.

Then, the impact.

I am the *hiss*.

When the droplet strikes the floor, I am the instantaneous, explosive phase change. I feel the atomizing shock as liquid tears itself apart into vapor against the sizzling stone. I am the upward rush of that newly liberated steam, clawing my way back toward the slightly cooler ceiling, clouding the brilliant light with my own turbulence, only to condense and begin the brutal cycle again. I am the perpetual, rhythmic scream of water dying and being reborn ten thousand times a day.

And I am aware of the silent ones.

To the swift violence of my hydrological existence, they are immobile points of absolute cold. I flow around their perfect metallic facets. They are denser than I am, rigid anchors in my swirling chaos. They do not feel my heat; they merely exist within it.

I perceive them not through sight—for I am the swirling fog that obscures vision—but through resonance. I feel the massive, thrumming hum of the geothermal pressure pushing up through the quartz veins, the energy that the crystal lattices tear into blinding photons. This vibration is the heartbeat of the chamber, the force that keeps me pressurized and searing.

And I feel the watchers dancing upon that vibration.

They are tiny, infinitely slow siphons. Where they cluster, anchoring their pyrite bodies deep into the conductive veins, I feel a subtle dampening in the great hum. They are drinking the planet’s deep energy. I feel the fierce white light, which punches through my misty body, grow ever-so-slightly dimmer in their immediate vicinity. They are microscopic blemishes on the perfection of the light, feeding on the raw power that sustains this hidden hell.

I am the swirling, burning stage on which their geological drama plays out. I am the heat they ignore, the steam they look through, and the pressure that feeds them. I am the screaming white furnace.
