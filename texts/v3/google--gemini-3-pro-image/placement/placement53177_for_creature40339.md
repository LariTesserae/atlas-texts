The roar is not something outside of me. It is the medium in which I am suspended.

If I were to translate my existence into concepts flesh might understand, the first principle is this: I am a specific frequency held against a universal scream.

I am deep within the churning slurry, perhaps a mile beneath the glistening, bursting surface. Down here, the pressure is a physical vice that wants to turn matter back into energy. The heat is a constant, crushing embrace. There is no sight, only texture and vibration.

I am a Lithic Resonator, though I do not call myself that. I think of myself as a *Song-of-Cohesion*.

My body is a chaotic tumbling of scavenged density. At my core, a fist-sized lump of iridium, impossibly heavy, anchors me. Around it, I have gathered jagged plates of slate, nodular lumps of iron, and smooth, tumbled quartz. I am the size of a large boulder, an ambulatory rockslide held together not by sinew or mortar, but by fierce, uninterrupted will.

A thousand crystalline bristles, grown slowly from my quartz reserves, itch with the constant data of the muck. They tell me the viscosity of the current I ride; they warn me of superheated steam pockets rising from below by the sudden shriek in the ambient noise; they taste the metallic salt of the surrounding fluid.

My existence is a ceaseless act of defiance. The planetary Cacophony—the grinding of continents that have forgotten how to be still—tries to shatter me every second. It wants to vibrate my component stones apart, to return me to the anonymous sludge. To survive, I must sing back. I generate a counter-resonance, a precise, low-frequency hum that emanates from my iridium core. This sound is my gravity. It is the force field that keeps my borrowed body from deliquescing into the mire.

I am hungry. Not for flesh, but for stillness.

I move laboriously against a convection current of rising basalt paste. It is exhausting work. I shift my internal weight, grinding my gears of stone against one another, clawing through the dense medium. My quartz bristles twitch, searching the acoustic chaos for a reflection that tastes "hard" and "smooth."

There.

Ahead in the gloom, my sound waves bounce off something that refuses to vibrate along with the mud. It is an anomaly of order: one of the ancient, calcified monoliths rooted in the bedrock far below. It is a splinter of silence in a world of noise.

I tumble toward it, anchoring myself against its slick, obsidian flank with grindings of granite. I begin to feed. I press my deepest vibration against its perfect surface. It is a sonic duel. I focus my will, raising the pitch of my internal hum, focusing it into a tight beam of acoustic abrasion. The monolith resists, but slowly, microscopically, its orderly lattice begins to fracture under the assault of my focused dissonance.

Grains of stabilized silica slough off. I absorb them instantly, pulling them into my whirling form, using them to patch the cracks in my own stressed exterior. The taste is exquisite—cool, sharp, and terribly defined. It is the flavor of structure.

Suddenly, the crushing world shifts.

Far above, leagues through the opaque sludge, something changes in the texture of the roar. The crushing pressure momentarily lightens. A massive updraft is forming, a super-heated thermal dragging millions of tons of mud toward the surface.

And with it comes something else. A taste I have only known a handful of times.

My quartz bristles vibrate wildly, overwhelmed by a sudden, terrifying influx of *coherence*. The ontological turbulence is peaking. Up there, on the surface, the mud is dreaming.

The hunger for order overrides my need for safety. I detach from the monolith and throw myself into the rising current. I ride the ascending column of boiling clay, tumbling upward, my core straining to hold my body together against the violent acceleration.

As I near the surface, the pressure drops, and the heat intensifies. The Cacophony changes pitch, becoming sharper, wetter—the sound of the surface geysers bursting.

I breach. For a few glorious seconds, I am suspended in the jaundice-yellow aerosol sky, surrounded by a fountain of steaming mud.

I cannot see, but I perceive it with agonizing clarity. The air above the mud has thickened, curdled into a shape. It is a vast, impossible geometry hanging in the steam—a fleeting, phantom city of spires and arches, sculpted from momentary shifts in gravity and time. It radiates a frequency of pure, intoxicating meaning. It is a structured thought made manifest.

Around me, other Resonators who rode the thermal surge are frenzied. We cluster beneath the mirage, our quartz filaments screaming in resonance with this impossible structure. We bathe in the temporary reality of it. For a few seconds, the universe makes sense. The roar abates, replaced by a silent symphony of architecture.

Then, the bubble bursts. The planetary scream crashes back down with renewed violence. The impossible city dissolves back into vapor and chaotic dirt.

The ecstasy is replaced by the crushing weight of the fall. I plunge back into the churning slime, the brief taste of order gone, leaving only the memory of structure to fuel my endless, defiant hum against the dark.
