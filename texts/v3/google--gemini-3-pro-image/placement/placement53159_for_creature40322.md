The descent was a shedding of the self, a scraping away of the soft world above until only essential awareness remained to squeeze through that final granite throat. But arrival here is not a release; it is an embedding.

The light finds me first. It is a physical pressure, that frigid, blue-white interrogation from the central pillar. It passes through the outer layers of my being, scattering coldly. I am located about four feet off the floor, on a precarious, razor-edged shelf of calcite that juts from the northern wall.

I am part of a structure.

I am trapped in the exquisite agony of the "almost." I am tucked into the microscopic gap between a harvested shard of amethyst quartz—sharp enough to split an atom, it feels—and the main body of one of those intricate, foot-high towers.

I am the resin.

I am that dollop of opaque, deeply sticky paste mentioned in the description of the hives. My existence is defined by a desperate, unfulfilled chemical urge: the urge to cure. To harden. To become structural.

But in this screaming photograph, chemistry has failed. I am perpetually tacky, caught in the exact microsecond of application. I feel the precise, serrated texture of the crustacean's mandibles that placed me here. The creature is frozen, its face inches from mine, its multi-faceted eyes reflecting the central pillar like dozens of tiny, cold suns. I feel the downward pressure of the amethyst shard it just pressed into my embrace. I am meant to bond this shard to the tower, to complete this segment of the hive.

I grip the quartz with viscous desperation. I adhere to the calcite shelf beneath me. I am the bridge between the raw geology and the biological architecture, and I am failing at both.

Because I am still wet, the deafening, static white noise of the chamber—that resonating kinetic potential—vibrates through me with terrible clarity. The sound travels through the floor, up the calcite shelf, and into my gelatinous body, shaking my suspended molecules. I am a conduit for the scream of the room.

The light blasts through the translucent quartz shard I hold, turning it into a violent violet lens that heats my upper surface just enough to keep me pliable, denying me the peace of solidification. I am eternally sticky, eternally compressed, holding a breath that I have been forbidden to exhale. I am the mortar in a monument that will never be finished, vibrating in sympathetic resonance with a million other trapped souls.
