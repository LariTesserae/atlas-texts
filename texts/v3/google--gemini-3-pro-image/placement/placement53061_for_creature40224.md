I am not the weaver, but the woven. I am the plush, saturated weight upon the spine of an elder custodian.

I am the moss.

My consciousness is diffuse, a slow, creeping awareness spread across thousands of tiny, purple-black tendrils rooted deep into the pitted obsidian-chitin armor of my host. I am ancient, perhaps as old as the machine city itself, a thick pelt of velvet vegetation that has colonized the walker just as my kin have colonized the static catwalks.

My world is warm moisture and vibration. I exist in a state of perpetual, delicious drowning. The air here is thick enough to taste—a mineral-heavy steam that settles upon my myriad fibers, swelling me until I press tight against the sleek, hard carapace of the creature beneath me.

I feel the world through its six multi-jointed legs. The constant, deafening thunder of the unseen waterfalls below vibrates up through the spun-glass architecture, through the atomic grip of my host’s pads, and resonates into my damp core. The rhythmic thrum of the geothermal pumps is my heartbeat, a slow, tectonic lullaby that never ceases.

Right now, we are inverted. Gravity tugs at my water-heavy bulk, pulling me away from the chitin, but I hold fast. We are hanging beneath a skeletal bridge that spans a void so vast it feels like the throat of a god. The spray here is intense, rising in cool clouds from the churning darkness miles below. I drink greedily, my colors deepening from violent violet to near-black as I saturate.

My host is working. I feel the intricate, fluid twitch of pewter-colored polymer muscle beneath the armor plates as its upper limbs perform delicate surgeries on a fraying energy conduit. I am a passive partner in this maintenance. I provide camouflage, blurring the hard lines of the walker against the organic architecture. Perhaps I even help regulate its temperature in this oppressive heat, a wet blanket against the geothermal burn. In return, it carries me to the richest seams of condensation, to the places where the energy leaks taste sharp and metallic.

I do not "see" the flashes of bioluminescent language my host ripples across its chest plates to others in the dark; those are beneath me, hidden from my view. But I feel the quickening pulse of its metabolism when another custodian draws near.

I am waiting for the discharge. I feel the pressure building in the air, an electrostatic prickling that makes my finest hairs stand on end. It comes suddenly—a silent, blinding explosion of violet light as the conduction towers arc across the chasm.

My host freezes mid-motion, clinging to the underside of the bridge like a burr. The bioluminescent fungi sharing space on its shoulder joints flare wildly in response to the energy deluge. I, too, feast. The light is not just illumination; it is food. The sudden blast of raw plasma energy sears the moisture from my outer layers, a momentary, thrilling dryness before the humid dark rushes back in to soothe me.

The arc fades. The thunder reasserts itself. My host resumes its slow, meticulous spider-walk along the glass threads. I am heavy, damp, and content, riding the ghost in the gears of this beautiful, terrible machine garden.
