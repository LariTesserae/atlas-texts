The sensation comes first, long before any thought forms. It is pressure—a crushing, omnipresent weight that defines up from down not by sight, but by the agonizing heaviness in my own fluids.

I am clinging to the vertical face of eternity.

My four primary limbs are splayed flat against the basalt. The calcified spurs at their tips are driven deep into a diagonal fissure, locked with a tension that has become subconscious. I do not feel the cold in the way soft things might; my carapace is thick, mineral-hard, and armored in layers of accumulated rime and frozen moss spores. I feel the cold as a physical lever, a brittle force constantly trying to pry my grip loose from the wall.

My head is lowered against the stone, tucked into the armored cowl of my shoulders. I have no eyes to see the oppressive indigo twilight or the terrifying drop into the unseen abyss below. Instead, my world is composed of texture and vibration.

The resonance plates tight against my skull are my true eyes. Through the unforgiving stone, I feel the planet’s heartbeat. *Thrum-mm-mm...* It is a low, grinding sub-bass that shudders up from the unimaginable depths, vibrating through my marrow. The geological engine is cycling. It is this rhythm that dictates my life.

My feathery antennae unfurl slightly, tasting the thin, vicious air. It is brittle, devoid of moisture, yet carries the distinct metallic tang of deep-earth chemistry. Sulfur. Iron vapor. And beneath it, the faint, musky scent of a wool-moss patch.

I need to move. The *Thrum* tells me the heat is coming.

Moving is an act of high-stakes engineering. I shift my weight to my lower limbs, feeling the spurs grind deeper into their anchorage. Slowly, deliberately, I disengage the upper-right limb. A shower of ice crystals detaches from my armored hide and falls silently into the void. There is no sound of them hitting bottom. There is never a sound of hitting bottom.

I reach upward. My sensory spurs map the microcosm of the cliff face—the slick smoothness of frozen fog, the brittle crunch of rime, the welcome, fibrous friction of wool-moss beginning to take hold. I find a new purchase, lock it in, and test it with a pull that would rip a lesser creature apart. It holds.

I drag my heavy, segmented body upward inches at a time. I am an elder Lithak; my back carries a miniature ecosystem of grey-blue moss and ice, weighing me down, camouflage for a war against gravity.

I am climbing toward warmth. The taste of sulfur grows stronger, coating my antennae. The rock beneath my ventral plates begins to change. The biting cold recedes, replaced by a subtle, creeping warmth that makes the rime ice slick with momentary slush.

I crest a slight overhang and enter the oasis.

It is a fissure in the titan's skin, perhaps three meters wide, choked with huge, spongy mats of grey-blue wool-moss. The air here is thick, humid, and rank with chemical heat. I press myself flat into the damp moss, letting the geothermal steam thaw the deeper layers of my chitin.

Above me, sensing the heat plume, hang the inverted chandeliers of the fungi. I cannot see their sickly glow, but I can feel the slight electrical hum of their bioluminescence, and I can taste the rich, sugary spores they drop into the rising vapor.

I am not alone, though the silence is absolute. Through the rock, I feel a sharp, rhythmic tapping near the fissure's edge. *Tap-tap-scrape. Tap-tap-scrape.* It is the vibration signature of another Lithak, one named Kruk-ta. The pattern tells me he is chipping away an ice dam threatening to choke the vent. It is dangerous work; a sudden pressure release could blow him off the wall.

I remain still, pressed into the warm moss, an armored barnacle on a skyscraper built above hell. The great subterranean engine reaches the peak of its cycle. The rock groans beneath me, a vibration so intense it makes my teeth ache. A blast of scorching, mineral-laden steam erupts from the fissure, washing over me. It is the breath of life in a frozen universe.

I hold on. I wait for the warmth to soak in, gathering the energy to move another few feet, to tend my patch of moss, to exist for another slow cycle in the crushing dark.
