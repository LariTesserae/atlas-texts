The light tries to kill you first. It is not an illumination, but a pressure, a searing, achromatic weight that seeks to drive itself through any opacity. But I am dense. I am opaque.

I exist in a state of permanent, profound vibration.

My world is defined by impact. I am suspended in a gripping, chaotic stillness that suddenly shatters into violence, over and over again. I am held fast by something warm and yielding that reeks of rancid grease and old sweat—a clumsy, mittened grip coated in sand.

I am a chisel fashioned from the thigh bone of a long-dead lowland behemoth.

I am the hardest thing in this immediate world, harder than the mittened hand that drives me, and just barely harder than the blue-white enemy I am forced against.

I am currently jammed into a hairline fracture in the living wall of the glacier. The air around my handle is incredibly thin; I feel the skittering drafts caused by the frantic passage of the bristled white many-legged things that swarm over the surfaces, their movements a silent blur. But where my beveled edge bites into the ice, there is no silence.

Every time the heavy stone hammer strikes my flattened poll, a shriek travels through my marrow structure—a sound heard only by bone and ice. It is the sound of crystalline structures shearing on a microscopic level. I am driven millimeters deep. The ice resists with a glassy, brittle fury. It wants to snap me. It sends bitter, numbing cold shooting up my shaft into the warm hand that holds me.

Through the narrow, smoked-quartz slit of the creature holding me—a bulky, shuffling mound of matted furs—I catch slivers of the outside world. It is a blinding, overexposed nightmare where the only colors are painfully bright white, electric blue, and the dark, scab-like brown of the human hive clinging to the cliff.

I feel the tension in the arm holding me. We are working frantically. A deep, subsonic moan is rising through the mountain, vibrating up through the slushy ledge we stand upon, traveling through the human boot, up the leg, down the arm, and into me. The glacier is waking up. It is preparing to heave.

We are trying to widen a pressure relief channel before the wall behind us buckles. I am struck again. Harder. Chips of ice fly, instantly turning into glittering diamond dust in the sub-zero air. The human holding me is breathing in short, desperate puffs that freeze into ice fog before they clear their mask.

I am a tool of desperation. I am the point of contact between a terrified parasite and a blind, frozen titan. I am about to be struck again, destined to chip away at an infinite, moving mountain until I either shatter against its indifference or am dropped into the bottomless slush below.
