The first thing I know is pressure. It is not a weight from above, but a crushing, vibrating density that surrounds me completely. The world is not silent; it is screaming. It is a roar so colossal it ceases to be sound and becomes a physical assault, rattling the very plates of my being.

I am pressed flat against the cold. The surface beneath me is impossibly smooth, slick with perpetual moisture. Water doesn't just fall here; it envelops. It sheets over my hardened dorsal carapace, streaming down the segmented grooves of my armor like rain off a slate roof. I am the same color as this wet, polished world—a dark, oily grey that shines in the bruised, watery twilight.

I need to move. The vibration deep in the bedrock, felt through the sensitive membranes of my underbelly, tells me the chasm to my left is surging. The deep bass thrum is intensifying, warning that the subterranean river is rising to boil over the lip.

Movement is a deliberate act of adhesion and release. I do not lift a foot; I break a seal. My six thick, stumpy limbs are spread wide, keeping my center of gravity unbelievably low. One by one, I disengage the complex clusters of gripping pads at the end of my limbs. *Pop, slide, grip, seal.* It is a rolling, fluid motion. If I were to stand tall, the combination of wind, spray, and slick rock would send me sliding into the churning white water of the chasms. So I stay low, flowing over the undulating humps of gneiss like viscous sludge.

My vision is poor, a blurry haze of grey stone and white steam. I don't rely on it. Instead, a cluster of delicate, fringed palps near my mouth taps against the wet stone rapidly. They taste the world. They taste the metallic tang of mineralized steam, the rich, rotten scent of a nearby bacterial mat, and the faint, rhythmic vibration of another heavy creature moving nearby.

Hunger is a slow ache in my gut. I track the scent of sulfur. Near a steaming crack in the obsidian floor, a thick carpet of rust-red slime thrives in the heat. A grazer is there already—an armored oval, clamped tight, scraping the rock. It is mindless, concerned only with suction and eating.

I stop. I press down, engaging hundreds of pounds of suction pressure, becoming geologically immovable. I wait. Patience is the only virtue in this grinding world. When the grazer shifts, momentarily lifting an edge of its shell, I act with startling speed.

From beneath my heavy outer armor, my secondary arms unfold. They are shockingly slender compared to my walking limbs, ending in multi-jointed fingers of immense dexterity. I reach out, jam my fingers under the grazer’s rim, and leverage upward. The seal breaks with a wet sucking sound. I flip it over, exposing the soft underbelly to the grinding beak hidden beneath my own face.

The meal is functional. Once satisfied, the relentless pressure of the noise begins to wear on me again. The vibration in my chest is exhausting. I need the silence.

I travel for an hour across the slick humps, away from the violent geysers, higher up where the spray is merely a mist. I find my shelter. It is a squat, ugly dome of stacked stones, barely distinguishable from the bedrock. But to me, it is a masterpiece.

I squeeze through the low opening. Inside, the change is profound. The screaming roar is dampened to a dull, distant thud. The air is still. The floor is miraculously dry.

I crawled from the wet rock, shaped by the crushing noise and the endless water. Out there, I am a ghost in armor, clinging to the hardest bones of the world. But here, in this small, dry hollow built by my own hidden hands, I can finally release my grip on the stone and rest in the rarest thing existence has to offer: quiet.
