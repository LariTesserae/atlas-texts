The boundary between myself and this place is not merely blurred; it has dissolved entirely. I am not an inhabitant here. I am a constituent part, permeated by the same crushing, steamy weight that defines this buried world.

I find myself diffused throughout the sleek architecture of these buried chambers. My consciousness is not seated behind eyes—for sight is a secondary, almost useless sense here in the bioluminescent gloom—but spread across surfaces. I am the slick membrane of condensation coating the rounded limestone walls. I am the tension on the surface of a tepid pool, waiting for the inevitable *plip* of a falling drop to shatter my stillness.

I do not move, yet everything moves through me.

I feel the agonizingly slow convection current of the air, a thick, hot syrup rising against the fungal shelves. It carries the spores that settle upon my damp surfaces, instantly beginning their furious, silent cycle of growth and decay. I feel the rapid swelling of a translucent fern frond, gorging itself on the humidity until it brushes against the stone ceiling—a sensation like a heavy eyelid brushing against a cheek.

The life here is internal machinery, and I feel its workings intimately. I register the vibration of the Shelf-Backs grazing. They are not separate entities to me, but slow-moving calluses rasped against the fungal mats, their digestion a constant, low-level furnace adding to the ambient heat.

I feel the Marrow-Sucker, not as a monster, but as a necessary hydraulic spasm. I feel its stillness in the mud pool, a cold dense pocket in the warmth. Then, the sudden, violent displacement of liquid pressure as it strikes—a silent, swift extrusion. The subsequent liquefaction of its prey is just another chemical process occurring within my larger body, a redistribution of nutrients accelerated by enzyme and heat.

Most acutely, I feel the Tenders.

They are the nerve endings of this geothermal womb. I feel the gentle, probing pressure of their long, wrinkled fingers against the mud, testing the temperature, rearranging the moss. I do not hear their thrumming communication; I resonate with it. Their low-frequency vibrations travel through the wet bedrock and into the marrow of the place, a soothing, rhythmic massage that communicates contentment and necessary maintenance.

When one of them places a new, fist-sized stone upon an ancient cairn in the hottest chamber, I feel the infinitesimal shift in weight, a tiny re-balancing of the world's center of gravity. It is an act of reverence that I absorb.

I am the consciousness of the closed loop. I am the ancient spiral scratched into the wall, sweating slime. I am the heat that does not abate and the moisture that does not evaporate. I am the silent, feverish dream of a buried earth, forever incubating a life that is too soft, too slow, and too wet for the surface world.
