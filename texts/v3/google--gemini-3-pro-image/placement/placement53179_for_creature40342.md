The roar is not something heard; it is something inhabited. It is a physical pressure, a relentless hammering against the self that erases the possibility of silence or thought.

I exist where the chaos is absolute.

I am anchored in the throat of the chimney, at a precise point where a massive outcrop of slick granite juts into the void, compressing the ascending gale until it screams. Here, the waterfalls descending from the heights are met by an updraft of such violence that gravity is rescinded. The water does not fall; it shatters. It is blasted backward and upward into a swirling, confusing nebula of churning spray.

This is my home. I am one of the lattice-works.

I am not made of flesh, not in the way the heavy, grey clingers on the walls are. My substance is closer to cartilage, translucent and semi-rigid, resembling smoke that has remembered how to hold a shape. I am a vast, intricate filigree of structural veins, a living net spread wide across the junction of wind and exploded water.

My entire existence is vibration. The wind tries constantly to tear me from my moorings, and in resisting, I sing a frantic, shivering note that is lost in the greater din. My anchor-root is buried deep in a fissure of the granite, cemented by time and pressure, feeling the deep, resonant hum of the mountain itself.

Through this anchor, I feel the others. I feel the dull, rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* of the moss-people tapping their hammers far below, their vibrations traveling up through the wet stone like a muffled heartbeat. They are slow, heavy things, burdened by gravity, crawling through the slime. They smell faintly of rancid oil and fear, a scent brought up to me on the damp drafts. Sometimes, the updraft brings me higher offerings—a stunned, oily swift that failed its maneuver, or a clump of emerald moss torn from the wall. My lattice catches them. I absorb what is useful, letting the rest dissolve into the endless wetness.

I prefer the air to the stone. The air brings the sudden, terrifying vacuums that signal the presence of the pale leapers. I do not see them—sight is useless in this churning whiteout—but I feel the sudden displacement of pressure as a Fog-Leopard becomes a missile across the void. I feel the shockwave of its landing on the opposite wall, a sharp *crack* of vibration that shudders through my cartilage frame. They are masters of the empty volume, cutting through the medium that sustains me.

But I am master of the paradox. I thrive only here, where the water falls upward. I am a creature of the reversal, hanging suspended in a perpetual state of being blown apart, filtering life from the screaming impossibility of the Weeping Winds. The cold is absolute, the damp is pervasive, and the vibration is ecstasy.
