The vibration reaches me first. It is not a sound, but a tectonic shudder that travels upwards through iron strata, rattling the very marrow of whatever I am. It is the deep, E-flat groan of a continent-sized piston completing its strokes somewhere miles below the concrete.

I am aware of the cold. It is a penetrating dampness that feels less like temperature and more like a physical weight, a greasy shroud pressing down. But I am also aware of heat—a localized, intense radiance to my left, a source of warmth so fierce it blisters the air.

My vision is terrible, a smear of gray fog and orange light. I don't have eyes in the traditional sense; I possess patches of photosensitive cells and pressure sensors embedded in my hide. I taste the air more than I see it. It tastes of copper, ozone, and rancid, burnt fat.

I am not moving. I am stuck.

I am clinging to the underside of a massive steel gantry, high above the canyon floor of the industrial basin. I am perched right at the lip of a colossal intake hopper.

Below me, the world is a churning sea of pale gray. The carpet of segmented things—the blind, clicking multitudes—is flowing in a living river towards the hopper. The sound is deafening: the high-pitched friction of billions of leathery shells rubbing together, audible even over the booming machinery. They are driven by the heat radiating from the furnace mouth just beyond the hopper, a blind pilgrimage towards incineration.

And what am I?

I am an accident of this ecosystem. I am a massive, calcified accretion. I began, perhaps centuries ago, as a cluster of those fungal-lubricant growths, feeding on the rich slurry that dripped from a faulty hydraulic joint in the gantry above. Over time, layers of rust flaked off the iron and embedded in my substance. The chitinous husks of the pale creatures, those who died before reaching the furnace, blew upward in drafts and became cemented into my bulk.

I have grown hard, armored by the waste of this world. I am a grotesque gargoyle of congealed oil, mineralized iron, and mummified organic paste, barnacled onto the metal structure.

I am the only stationary living thing in a world of frantic, pointless motion.

I feel the shuddering of the conveyor belt just inches below my perch. It is slick with a black paste—the crushed remnants of the creatures that have already passed through the initial grinders. I watch the living river tumble over the edge of the hopper, a pale cascade of frantic legs and blind heads, falling into the dark machinery without a sound other than that endless clicking.

I feel the intense heat flare as the furnace doors cycle open to accept the fuel. The draft rushes past me, carrying the sickeningly sweet smell of burning biomass. The heat keeps me alive, keeps the deep core of my strange, scavenged metabolism churning slowly.

I am the silent witness on the precipice of the fire. I am the hardened memory of a billion deaths, clinging to the machine that eats them, existing solely because a valve leaked and the heat rises. I am a thinking scab on the wound of this endless, deafening place.
