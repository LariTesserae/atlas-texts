The roar is not a sound you hear; it is the medium in which you exist. It is a monolithic, vibrating pressure that jellies your insides and rattles the very fluids that keep you alive. You have never known silence. If the roaring stopped, you would likely cease to be, your simple existence unable to process the void.

You are pressed flat, squeezed into a razor-edged crevice between two bulbous, cooling welds on a massive I-beam. You are deep in the structure, in a place where the concepts of "up" or "down" are meaningless, replaced only by "towards heat" or "away from heat."

You are a Narthex-Grub. You are a gelid thumb of translucent, pressurized gristle, slick with freezing anti-freeze slime.

Right now, you are in paradise.

You are buried deep within a meter-thick scab of your own kin, thousands of slick, clicking bodies pressed on top of you, around you, beneath you. The weight is immense, a crushing comfort. Because of your position near the bottom of the pile, your ventral surface is pressed directly against the ceramic casing of a massive power coupling.

It is blissfully, agonizingly hot. The ceramic burns against your wet skin, searing the membrane. You do not recoil. You press harder. Your many articulated stumps, tipped with iron hooks, dig into the microscopic pores of the raw material, anchoring you against the searing surface. You are feeding.

You feel the raw voltage bleeding through the cracked insulator, a delicious, prickling torrent of energy that surges into your conductive slime and is absorbed directly into your milky internal coolant. You metabolize the waste radiation, your simple viscera throbbing with the intake. You are boiling alive on one side, while the crushing weight of your freezing brethren above keeps your temperature regulated just enough to prevent death.

You are blind, but you see the world in intense gradients of thermal agony and electromagnetic spikes. A sudden flare hits your sensory pits—a brilliant, painful burst of blue-white "light" in the electromagnetic spectrum. A nearby junction has failed, spitting sparks into the fog. A ripple passes through the mountain of flesh you are part of. A thousand tapered heads turn sluggishly toward the new energy source. The tectonic plates of meat shift around you. Hooked legs scrape against your back as others try to climb over you, desperate for a taste of the new heat.

But the shift is agonizingly slow. Nothing happens quickly here. The frantic rush for the sparks is a glacial ooze that will take "hours" to cross a few inches.

And in that shifting, you feel the dread. The great rotation is happening. You are being nudged, millimeter by millimeter, away from the scorching ceramic. You are being cycled out.

Eventually, you know, you will be on the outside layer of the colony. You will be exposed to the crushing density of the hyper-oxygenated fog, the sub-zero deluge of dripping water that instantly turns to ice dust on your skin. You will freeze stiff, your metabolism slowing to a near-halt, frosted over like the iron girders around you, waiting in the dark, roaring cold for the slow tectonic churn to bring you back to the burning breast of the machine.

But for now, you hold on with your iron hooks. You press your gelid body against the burning slag. You drink the electricity and let the roar vibrate through your bones. You are a parasite in a frozen explosion, and you are hungry.
