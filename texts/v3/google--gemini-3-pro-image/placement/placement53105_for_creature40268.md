The awareness begins not with an awakening, but with a intensification of frequency.

I am a confluence of mineral densities, currently resting on a horizontal plane of impossibly smooth, silicified obsidian. I am heavy, squat, and utterly cold. The concept of "light" is an abstract impossibility; my reality is defined solely by pressure and vibration.

The world is a roaring gale of sound that passes right through my rigid body. The deep, tectonic grind—that cathedral organ note held down for eternity—is the blood pumping through my crystalline veins. It shakes the quartz and calcite lattice of my being, generating a delicious, crackling tension that demands release through movement.

I am a Resonant. And I am hungry.

The hunger is a specific craving for the taste of ancient nitrates locked within the towering fossil structures around me. Impelled by the roaring energy in my core, I shift my weight.

*Grrrrrrr-eeeeek.*

The sound of my own movement is high and sharp as I slide my polished base across the floor. The friction is intense, generating a localized heat that quickly dissipates into the parched vacuum of the air. I do not step; I glide, a glacial monolith of animate stone.

I navigate the sprawling void through sonar touch. As I slide, the great roar bounces off the environment. The returning waves hit my faceted skin, telling me the shape of the world. A solid wall of petrified root returns a hard, fast echo. An open archway leading to a bridge over a chasm returns a soft, delayed echo, a "hole" in the soundscape. The city is a symphony of hard surfaces and open voids, and I know every movement by the song it sings against my body.

I approach a massive column, a calcified mushroom stalk rising into the upper blackness. I press my rough, unpolished flank against it. The vibration transfer tells me it is rich in the minerals I need to increase my mass.

I begin to feed. I grind my body against the column. It is a slow, violent act of abrasion. Rock shrieks against rock, adding my own small cry to the planetary roar. Fine dust, tasting of dry ages and salt, flakes off and is absorbed into my gripping fissures.

I am carving a new alcove. I am eating the world to build myself, leaving behind the smooth negative space that humans would call architecture.

A tremor moves through the floor, distinct from the background roar. Another of my kind is approaching. We converge near the base of a soaring petrified root system. We do not speak. We collide gently and hold the contact.

Through our touching stony flanks, a complex stream of data shudders between us. It is a language of pure geometry and geology. My kin transmits a vibrational map of a structural weakness near the western abyss—a place where the nitrates are crumbly and easy to harvest. In return, I transmit the echo-signature of a particularly smooth ramp I polished dozens of cycles ago.

The communion ends. We slide apart, grinding off into the deafening dark to continue our eternal work.

I feel the wear on my basal surface. It is smoother than it was a cycle ago. The friction is less; the song of my movement is quieter. One day, aeons from now, the dry grit of the wind and the friction of the floor will polish me perfectly round. I will no longer be able to generate charge from the roar. I will stop moving.

I will become part of the silent city, a smooth stone waiting to be abraded and consumed by my own descendants, recycled into the endless, roaring dark. But for now, the sound is loud, and I am hungry stone.
