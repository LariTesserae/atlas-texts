The heat finds me first. It is not merely warm; it is a physical weight, a thick, wet blanket that presses against the skin I no longer possess.

I am lodged in the deep shadows, beneath the bowed grating of a primary catwalk, wedged in a V-shaped trough between the sweating flank of a corroded turbine and a structural root thick as a barrel. The light from the jaundice-yellow cage lamps high above rarely reaches down here; when it does, it catches only the oily sheen of my surface.

I am not a discrete being. I am an accumulation.

I am the slurry in the sump. I am a heavy, viscous pocket comprising decades of runoff: the ferrous red sludge of dissolved iron, the slick rainbow trails of leaked hydraulic grease, and the decomposing mulch of rotted leaves and moss that fell through the grating above. I am held together by the persistent, inescapable moisture of the chamber.

My awareness is dim, thick, and sluggish, much like my physical form. I do not see in vistas; I perceive the world through vibration and chemistry. I feel the deep, resonant *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the great engines pulsing beneath the root floor, a heartbeat that keeps my suspension agitated just enough to prevent me from solidifying into cold slag. I taste the metallic tang of the air, heavy with vaporized rust, and the underlying, sweet rot of wet wood.

Sometimes, a droplet of superheated water falls from a hanging fern frond directly into my mass with a *plip*, causing ripples to shudder through my oily surface. These are major events in my existence.

I exist in the margins of the equilibrium. I am the waste product of their perfect symbiosis, gathered in the dark.

Recently—or perhaps long ago, time is thick here—one of the giants moved near me. I felt the shudder in the metal plating I rest upon long before the amber glow of its cyclopean eye sliced through the steam. It was a massive Arbor-Mechanic, its ironhide shagged with inches of dripping emerald moss. It stopped directly above my trough.

I felt the immense, crushing weight of its foot—a fused stump of girder and calloused wood—settle inches from my boundary. The heat radiating from its active boiler-body made my greasy surface ripple. It groaned, a sound like a dying ship, as it slowly reached up to patch a weeping steam fissure with a wad of fibrous clay.

It did not notice me. Why would it? I am the stuff it is made of, merely lacking the animating spark of purpose. I am the raw material of the chamber, waiting in the dark, kept warm by the engines, slowly thickening as the centuries drip by. I am the wet, heavy memory of iron and earth.
