The moisture is the first thing known, before self, before place. It is a pressure that presses inward against the porous boundary of my being, a warm, saturated embrace that never relents. There is no visual horizon, only a graduating density of white luminescence that my facial membrane interprets as "up."

I am standing on the Third Terrace of the basalt amphitheater. I know this not by sight, but by the specific resonant frequency of the water rushing in the slate channel beneath my feet, and the precise mineral flavor of the mist that clings to my upturned crest. The air tastes of ozone and pulverized young rock.

I am heavy. My mass is considerable, anchored by broad, rooted feet that grip the slick stone with clustered fibers. My outer integument, rough as ancient bark and slick with a thriving colony of emerald moss, is fully permeable. I am drinking the air. Every inch of my surface is a mouth, accepting the perpetual weep of the clouds.

A vibration travels through the basalt beneath me—a low frequency shudder that might be a distant rockfall or merely the settling of the violent earth. It registers without alarm. Urgency is a concept that does not exist here.

I feel the urge to commune.

My movements are hydraulic, pressurized and immensely slow. To turn my torso toward the central street channel requires a deliberate shifting of internal fluids, a gradual creaking of stiff, fibrous joints that takes many long minutes. There is no sound to this movement, only the internal sensation of swelling wood and stretching fibers.

I reach the edge of the open aqueduct. The water racing within it is colder than the air, a vein of glacial runoff pulsed from the jagged peaks above. I lower a thick, pad-ended limb into the flow.

The shock of cold is exquisite. Instantaneously, my sensors are flooded with data. I taste the raw geology of the summit—the sharp tang of iron, the chalky grit of feldspar. This is sustenance, the mineral slurry that hardens my carapace and fuels my slow existence.

But beneath the minerals, there is the Dialogue.

As the water washes over my submerged sensors, I taste the chemical signatures of my kin upstream. It is not language as sound, but a complex melody of flavors and electrical impulses.

*A metallic bitterness:* The stabilizers on the high suspension bridge near the second spire are fraying under the weight of water-logged reed mats.

*A sweet, earthy rush:* A large tree fern on the upper tier has finally succumbed to gravity, its decomposition already beginning to enrich the soil runoff.

*A neutral saline pulse:* Acknowledgment of the tremor felt earlier. All sectors report stasis.

I absorb these flavors, integrating them into my awareness of the city's living lattice. I am a node in this silent, flowing consciousness. In turn, I contribute. I trigger a gland near the base of my submerged wrist cluster, releasing a precise sequence of enzymes into the current. My message is simple, a flavor-thought that will wash down to the lower terraces: *Third Terrace cisterns at capacity. Flow diversion recommended.*

I withdraw my limb. The cold flavor recedes, replaced again by the warm blanket of atmospheric steam.

The task regarding the frayed bridge stabilizers requires attention. It is perhaps four hundred yards away and two levels up, across a span of woven vines hanging over the silent, misty abyss. It will take me perhaps three cycles of light and dark to reach it.

I begin the slow rotation of my body toward the precarious stairs carved into the cliff face. I move with the patience of a glacier. I am a gardener in a world of stone and water, a silent watcher in the perpetual fog. I have existed for centuries, and I will exist until my fluids cease to move, and I finally become the rock I tend.
