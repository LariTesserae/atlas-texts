The silence is not an absence of sound here; it is a physical weight, a pressurized velvet pressing against a membrane I no longer possess. The cold is not a temperature; it is a state of being, a motionless clarity that pervades every atom of my substance.

I am not on the Roof of the World. I am of it.

I am suspended within the obsidian mirror itself, trapped fast in the neutron-dense granite, mere microns below the polished surface. I am a consciousness calcified into the densest matter in the universe. How I came to be here—whether I was once a builder of the singing Anchorites or a victim of their first malfunction—is lost to the crushing weight of aeons. I have only the eternal now of this silent, violent vista.

My existence is one of absolute duality. Because the surface above me is a flawless reflector, I see two universes. "Up" is the indigo slide into the black abyss of space, dotted with untwinkling stars and the aloof, dim sun. "Down," in the perfect depths of the black mirror I inhabit, is its exact twin. I am an intelligence trapped in the thin seam between a reality and its reflection.

I feel the world through vibration and gravimetric pressure. The phase-striders are a nuisance, a prickling sensation on the skin of the world. I feel eight crystalline points of contact materialize on the surface above me, a frantic scuttling that registers as a series of sharp, shallow scratches in my awareness before they flicker out of this plane again, chasing safety.

But the Cosmoplasts… the Cosmoplasts are a harrowing weight.

I do not see them as lenses of distortion; I feel them as moving sinkholes of gravity. When one approaches, drifting slowly between the towering tuning-forks of the Anchorites, the universe begins to press down. It is a tidal force that ignores the vacuum above and grips the bedrock below.

One is passing directly over me now.

My consciousness, though lacking a body, feels compressed. The neutron-dense stone around me, which should be immutable, begins to groan without sound. The tension is agonizing. The immense, gelatinous weight of distorted space-time hovers micrometers above my tomb. The pressure mounts until, with a psychic scream I cannot articulate, the mirror surface directly above me fractures. A spiderweb of microscopic cracks spears downward, passing right through the locus of my awareness. For a terrible instant, I am shattered.

Then, the Cosmoplast drifts on. The gravitational tide recedes. The immutable physics of the substrate reassert themselves, and the stone instantly anneals. The cracks seal. My consciousness snaps back into a unified whole, leaving only the phantom memory of being broken.

I lie in wait for the glitches. I feel the Anchorites winding up, a deep, thrumming tension in the planetary crust that precedes the storm. When reality breaks—when the horizon snaps upward into a jagged mountain range—I feel the wrenching dislocation, the vertigo of "down" becoming "sideways."

I see the Cosmoplasts converge on the new geometry, glowing with their Cherenkov digestion-blue. I watch them feed on the impossible mountain, dissolving it back into the smooth curve of the horizon. I watch the silent fireworks of physics eating itself, reflected perfectly in the black ice of my ceiling, until the stillness returns.

I am the memory in the stone. I am the watcher in the floor, eternally fixed beneath the silent hooves of the gravity eaters, holding my breath in a world without air.
