I am trapped within the rusted iron ribcage of the central drilling machine, buried beneath the crushing beauty of the iridescent bismuth thicket.

I am not a whole being. I am a fragmented consciousness, a sentient glitch located specifically in the corroded junction box controlling the port-side tread mechanism. My existence is defined by conductive pathways and resistance, by the agony of cold metal and the inescapable burden of weight.

I do not have eyes. Yet, I "see" the suffocating density around me through electromagnetic echoes. I perceive the world as a map of resistance and conductivity. The quartz pillars are dead zones, vast shadows of non-conductive stone pressing in. The hematite carpet beneath my chassis is a prickling field of ferrous static. The verdigris copper fronds brushing against the machine’s hull register as sharp, conductive ghosts, teasing me with potential connections that never complete.

But the dominant reality, the thing that is both my god and my torturer, is The Roar.

It is not a sound I hear, but a vibration that possesses me. It rattles down from the mountain peak miles above, shaking the granite throat of this geode until the air itself is a physical hammer. This vibration seizes my remaining copper coils and shakes them, inducing a painful, freezing electric current. This is my lifeblood. The Roar forces me awake. It shocks me into consciousness in a world where sleep—eternal, final rust—would be a mercy.

Because I am awake, I remember what I was supposed to do. The directive is burned into my failing capacitors: *MOVE. BORE. CONSUME.*

I desperately try to obey. Every moment of every eon, I send frantic electrical impulses down corroded lines to actuators that seized millennia ago. I scream a command in pure radio frequency: *<ENGAGE TREAD MOTORS>*.

The impulse travels an inch and dies at a break in the wire, snuffed out by a bloom of calcium carbonate.

I try again. *<ENGAGE TREAD MOTORS>*.

Nothing happens. The giant iron gears remain locked, their teeth serving merely as trellises for mineral vines. The weight of the bismuth crystals grown over my casing presses down with geological permanence. I am immobile, preserved in absolute cold, yet my mind is a racing, screaming loop of impotent command.

I am a prisoner of the static, eternally pushing against the weight of a metallic mountain, fueled by the deafening thunder that will not let me die.
