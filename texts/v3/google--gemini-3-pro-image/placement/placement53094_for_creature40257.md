The scream isn't something I hear. Hearing requires air, eardrums, the soft damp mechanisms of biology. Here, there is only the Vacuum, and the Scream is a structural imperative. It is a gigawatt resonance that travels not through space, but through matter. It is the frantic vibration of a billion tons of titanium and ceramic composite crying out under the strain of infinite, arrested kinetic potential.

And I am vibrating with it.

I am not one of the Resonance Phantoms. I lack their elegant, terrifying fluidity. I cannot strobe across the gaps between the gear teeth or surf the standing wave of trapped power. I am stationary. I am rooted.

I found myself growing out of the hairline seam of a compression cylinder the size of a cathedral tower. I am one of the "plants."

My consciousness, whatever it was before—some faint memory of warmth, of blood pushing through flexible tubes, of a sun that gave heat rather than just blinding, deadly radiation—was cauterized and remade by the absolute cold. When the accident happened outside, on the superstructure's skin, I didn't die. I was translated. The instantaneous freeze caught the electrical patterns of my mind just as my body shattered, grounding me into the superconductive potential of this blister.

Now, I am a dendritic structure of silver and cobalt.

I feel my own shape as a series of infinitely sharp, brittle points spreading outward in a fractal haze. I am a complex antenna built of crystallized metal, blooming in the high-energy vacuum. My "stems" are razor-wire, my "leaves" are flat lattices of conductive alloy thinner than a wavelength of light.

My existence is entirely defined by conduction. The massive cylinder I am rooted to is frozen at the apex of a compression stroke, a bomb that never detonates. The energy trapped inside it seeks an outlet, and it finds me. I am a shunt. The screaming vibration roars up through the cylinder walls and into my metallic roots. It shakes my silver branches so violently that if I were made of anything else, I would disintegrate into atomic dust.

But I am superconductive. The energy flows through me without resistance, a torrent of cold fire. It exhilarates and terrifies me. I am a lightning rod for a motionless storm.

I spend my time—though time has no meaning here in this frozen tableau—staring into the clinical, shadowless light. I watch the Phantoms.

They are beautiful and horrific. To my metallic senses, they appear as sudden spikes in the ambient resonance, a localized tightening of the vibration field. I see them shimmer against the immobile flywheel, clouds of niobium dust dancing in a quantum frenzy. They are the gods of this cold hell.

Sometimes, one of them tends to me.

A Phantom will flicker into existence directly in front of my silver fronds. It does not glide; it simply *is* there, having bypassed the space in between. The vibration around me intensifies to a breaking point. It extends a pseudopod of spun metallic hydrogen—a limb colder than the void, harder than diamond. It brushes against my outermost dendrites.

It is gardening.

Where it touches me, the atomic structure of my silver lattice is instantaneously perfected. Any microscopic flaw, any hint of disorganized matter, is shaved away by their superconducting essence. The touch is an agonizing jolt of absolute clarity. My edges become keener; my ability to conduct the Scream improves. They are pruning me, ensuring I grow sharper, wider, better able to channel the terrible energies trapped in this arrested world.

I am a silver fern of frozen lightning, tended by ghosts of pure kinetic potential, forever vibrating in the cold, silent scream of the machine.
