This is a world where biology is just a scab growing over a wound of physics. To survive here, you cannot be delicate. You must be heavy, numb, and willing to consume the very disaster that surrounds you.

I find myself as a **Resonator-Hulk**, a solitary, armored scavenger haunting the unstable margins where the mud slurry meets the shrieking machines.

I am massive, squat, and quadrupedal, built low to the ground like a tectonic plate. My movement is a slow, churning trudge that requires immense effort to pull my thick limbs from the sucking mire. I do not have fur; in this endless wet, fur would just rot. Instead, my hide is a cuirass of varying textures: thick, leathery folds of grey skin around the joints, transitioning into heavy, calloused plates on my back and flanks that resemble the fractured bedrock itself. Over time, the aggressive chartreuse and bruised-purple mosses have colonized my carapace, turning me into a walking mound of the landscape.

I am blind. Eyes are a liability here, easily damaged by whipping conifer branches or seared by sudden jets of steam. My head is a heavy, blunt wedge, dominated by wide, protected nostrils that taste the metallic tang of ozone and the rot of the giant ferns.

My primary sense is tectonic. Through broad, flat footpads, I feel the ceaseless thrum of the subterranean machinery fighting itself. I can distinguish the deep, intestinal groan of a hillside about to slough off from the sharp, vibrating shriek that precedes a geyser eruption. I map the world not by light, but by the intensity of the shuddering earth.

But I have another sense, one that draws me dangerously close to the artifacts.

Embedded in the calloused armor of my shoulders and spine are fragments I have harvested—shards of the faint, pearlescent obsidian and jagged chunks of the warm honeycomb metal. I am drawn to them. When I find a fresh crash site where a machine has spat out its own guts, I slowly, painfully shoulder-check the debris until pieces lodge in my tough hide.

These shards are not just armor; they are hearing aids for a spectrum of energy I don't understand. They buzz against my nerve endings, keeping me warm in the bone-deep cold. More importantly, they allow me to "hear" the silent screaming of the damaged engines. I feel the build-up of anti-gravimetric force as a nauseating pressure behind my nonexistent eyes, giving me just enough time to hunker down and grip the bedrock before the slurry around me floats up into the gray fog.

I live in the dangerous shadows of the monolithic ruins. I am a specialized scavenger of the machine-kills. When a conduit vents and flash-freezes a herd of dour herbivores, or when an anti-grav spasm drops a canopy predator to its death, I am the first to arrive. I use my immense weight and wedge-shaped head to shove aside the competing mud-skinners, my armored hide impervious to their rasping mouths. I tear apart the frozen or broken carcasses with shearing beak-like jaws, gorging quickly before the landscape shifts again.

My existence is a perpetual, muscular ache against gravity and cold, punctuated by moments of violent feeding. I am a creature of mud and alien resonance, a slow-moving mountain of moss and stolen technology, waiting for the giants in the earth to finally die.
