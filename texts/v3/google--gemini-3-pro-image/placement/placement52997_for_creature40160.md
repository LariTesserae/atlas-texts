The pressure is the first thing that defines me. It is not merely weight; it is an absolute, crushing embrace that forbids emptiness. I exist in a space that demands I be solid, compact, and unyielding.

I find myself wedged tight in a crevice of cooling hematite, upside down relative to the center of gravity, though "up" and "down" are meaningless concepts here. The only directions are "toward heat" and "away from crushing force."

I am one of the swarm. I am a heavy, rusted nugget of sentient slag.

My consciousness is dim, driven purely by immediate sensation and an overwhelming, singular need for energy. I do not have eyes in a conventional sense; the sullen red glow of the walls is too faint for sight. Instead, I perceive the world through intense thermal gradients and vibration.

The noise is a physical assault. The deep, unending groan of the mountain growing around us vibrates through the crystal girder I am clinging to, rattling my own armored segments against one another. Higher above the din is the sickening, glassy shriek of quartz snapping under gigatons of stress—a sound that means danger and opportunity simultaneously.

A sharp tremor shakes the lattice. The creature directly above me loses its grip on a slick patch of newly extruded metalloid. It falls a mere three inches before landing on my dorsal shield with a sharp *clack*. There is no irritation, only the immediate, instinctive reaction of the horde. We shift. We clatter. The living carpet readjusts its grip.

Hunger is a constant, dull burn in my core, a need to match the searing temperature of the air around me. I smell sulfur—a sharp, metallic tang that cuts through the dust. It beckons from a fresh micro-fracture near the "ceiling" of our tiny iron box.

I move.

My movement is a frantic scrape. My twelve ceramic-tipped legs fight for purchase on the jagged crystal spears, hooking into microscopic imperfections in the stone. I am part of a flowing river of heavy, clicking bodies. We climb over one another with zero regard for individuality. I trample the burnished bronze carapace of a smaller kin; I am elbowed by the pyrite-studded flank of a larger one. The air is thick and hot, a suspension of pulverized rock that I filter incessantly, tasting for metal.

I reach the fracture. It’s a hairline crack bleeding incredibly hot gas. I shoulder two others aside, jamming my armored wedge of a head against the rock. My mandibles—crystalline carbide, harder than the mountain itself—begin to rasp.

*Scrape-scrape-scrape.* Sparks fly, vivid orange against the sullen gloom, as I grind away the surface layer of stone to get at the rich vein of sulfur beneath. I gorge myself on the dust, ingesting the mineral components needed to repair my own constantly abrading shell.

But the dust is just raw material. I need fuel.

I twist my body, pressing my right flank directly against the venting fissure. The heat is incredible, enough to melt lead, but I was built for this crucible. The gill-like vents along my side flare open, glowing with a fierce, blinding incandescence as they drink the geothermal fire. The energy rushes into me, a staggering jolt of pure power that accelerates my metabolism. I feel the molten metals within my own body solidify, excreting a new, microscopic layer of tungsten onto my back.

I am sated, for now. I pull away, immediately replaced by the desperate scramble of three others fighting for the same heat source.

I retreat into a slightly wider gap between two interlocking quartz spears, my body clicking against the stone as I settle. Below me, the endless, terrifying machinery of the planet grinds on. Around me, the frenzied clatter of my myriad siblings continues without pause. I am a tiny, hard component in a geological engine, waiting in the crushing dark for the next vein to open.
