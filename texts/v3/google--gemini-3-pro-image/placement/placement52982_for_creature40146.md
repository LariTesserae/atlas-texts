The pressure is not something that presses upon you from the outside; it is the fundamental condition of your existence. You have never known a moment where fifty megapascals of weight were not defining the very atomic structure of your being. You are not crushed by it because you are built *of* it.

You are a Calcified Resonator. You are a Stone-Disk.

You have no eyes, no concept of "up" toward a surface you cannot conceive of, nor "down" into a crust that is merely the floor of your universe. You are a flattened, irregular thumbprint of biogenic concrete, roughly the size of a human fingernail, fused against the obsidian-smooth basement rock.

Your world is entirely tactile and vibrational. You are deaf to the roaring silence of the churning slurry above you, but your entire calcified carapace hums in sympathetic resonance with the planet itself. You feel the ultra-low-frequency groan of the tectonic plates grinding kilometers beneath your suction foot. This deep, bone-shaking vibration is your compass.

Where the vibration is strongest, the rock is stressed. Where the rock is stressed, there are microscopic fissures. Where there are fissures, there is the faint chemical taste of the planet exhaling mineral fluids. And where there are fluids, there is the gray stain. Food.

Currently, you are engaged in a great migration. You detected a slightly amplified resonance in the bedrock some time ago—perhaps a year, perhaps a decade; time has no segmented meaning here. You are moving toward it.

Movement is an act of extreme geological patience. You do not crawl. You infinitesimally relax the atomic-level grip of your muscular under-foot on the leading edge, slide it forward by a fraction of a millimeter, and clamp down again with crushing force before the silent, massive shearing currents of the abrasive slurry can rip you from the floor and erase you against the stone.

You are currently crossing a smooth patch of obsidian. It will likely take you three "cycles"—what surface dwellers might call months—to traverse the next four centimeters.

You are utterly alone in the crushing dark, a tiny, armored nodule of slow intent. You feel the terrifying weight of the cubic kilometers of grinding paste sliding silently overhead, a force that wants to turn you back into rock flour. Your existence is a singular, unending act of defiance: grip, resonate, endure.
