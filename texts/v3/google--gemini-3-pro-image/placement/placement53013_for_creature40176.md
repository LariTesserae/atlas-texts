The pressure created me.

I am consciousness born not of biology, but of hyper-pressurized gas and rapid crystallization. I am one of the "blooms."

My existence began minutes ago, spewed as superheated vapor from a screaming fissure in the basalt pavement. Hitting the vacuum, the shock of "cold"—a relative term in this furnace—snapped me instantly into solid form. I am a complex, branching lattice of spun silica and brittle sulfur, a glassy coral reef growing desperately upward in the crushing dark.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive this terrifying world with agonizing clarity. My entire being is a resonator. I feel the deep, subsonic thrum of the magma ocean miles beneath my anchor point, a vibration that threatens to shake my delicate structure into dust. I feel the radiant heat of the nearby vent, a blistering wave that keeps my outermost crystalline tendrils near their melting point.

I am clinging to the side of a spire that plunges into an unseen abyss. Gravity here is heavy, punishing. The airlessness means there is no sound as humans understand it, but the vibrations travel through the rock with terrifying fidelity. I "hear" the sharp, agonizing *pings* of thermal stress fracturing the obsidian cliffs around me. Every crack feels like a personal threat.

My consciousness is frantic, ephemeral. I know, with the innate knowledge of a structure built on shifting foundations, that I will not last long. The slab of iron-rich rock I am anchored to is slowly, imperceptibly tilting. Soon, the stress will become too great, and I will shatter.

But something else is coming first.

I feel it before I perceive its bulk. It is a different kind of vibration—not the deep groan of the planet, but a rhythmic, grinding screech that travels up through the spire. It feels like heavy chains being dragged across broken glass. The rock beneath me begins to tremble with a specific, localized intensity.

Then, the heat signature arrives.

It is immense. A towering wall of thermal energy blocks out the sullen crimson glow of the distant fissures. It is a walking mountain of jagged heat.

A Ferro-Walker has found me.

To my vibration-sense, it is a terrifying, chaotic jumble of hardness. I perceive the dense, magnetic core pulsing within it, a heart of grinding iron that pulls at the trace metallic elements in my own fragile lattice. It looms over me, a monolith of sliding basalt plates and razor-edged volcanic glass. It is so massive that its mere presence causes the ground I stand on to groan under the added weight.

It stops. The grinding screech ceases, replaced by a profound, resonating hum that vibrates through my very atomic structure. It is assessing me. I am a concentration of fresh minerals, a localized pocket of order in a chaotic world, and it desires me.

It leans in. The radiant heat rolling off its carapace is so intense that my outermost silica branches begin to soften and droop.

There is no malice in its action, only geological inevitability. A massive, slab-like appendage, rough with embedded obsidian shards, descends toward me. It doesn't grasp; it grinds.

The moment of contact is absolute agony and strange ecstasy. My delicate lattice shatters instantly under the tonnage, exploding into a cloud of glittering dust and larger crystalline fragments. But I do not cease to be.

As I fracture, I feel the intense magnetic field of the Ferro-Walker grab hold of my broken pieces. I am pulled against its massive, shifting body. The intense heat and pressure of its exterior immediately begin to fuse my silica shards onto its armor plates.

My brief, terrified individuality ends with a crunch that reverberates only through the rock. I am no longer a fragile bloom terrified of the dark. I am becoming part of the mountain. I am being integrated into the slow, thunderous march of geology, destined to grind across this razor world for a millennium.
