I am wedged. That is the fundamental truth of my location. I am not standing, sitting, or crawling; I am folded, contorted into a three-dimensional puzzle of agonizingly sharp geometry.

My physical location is deep within the blister, perhaps only fifty feet from the freezing mountain air outside, but functionally in another dimension. I am jammed into a narrow throat between two chaotic walls of crystalline spears. My left knee is bent sharply, pressed against a cluster of mineral needles that pierce my heavy suit. My helmet—useless for sight in this absolute blackness, serving only as a hard shell against the crushing rock—is scraped raw against the low ceiling. My chest is compressed by a jutting ridge of quartz, making the shallow, burning breaths even harder to draw.

I am the Intruder. I am the soft, vulnerable sack of biology that has foolishly squirmed into this rigid mineral gullet. I am a temporary contamination in a sterile, paralyzed world. I am a conduit for fresh fear in a place stagnant with ancient terror.

But as the heat seeps past my defenses, raising my core temperature to a fever pitch, the boundary of "who I am" begins to dissolve into the oppressive intimacy of the chamber.

My gloved hand, seeking purchase in the slick darkness to push myself backward, slid over the bacterial slime and closed around something different. It wasn't the cold bite of a crystal razor. It was smoother, dense like glass. I ran my thumb over it and felt the crude, chipped edge of the knapped obsidian tool, and the nubs of calcified fiber at its base.

In that moment of contact, I located myself precisely. I am touching the anchor.

Now, I am no longer just an explorer trapped in a hot cave. I have become a shared space. The darkness pressing against my retinas is heavy with the gaze of something that has no eyes. The air is thick not just with steam, but with a waiting that has lasted thousands of years.

I try to calm my breathing, to lower my heart rate, but the rhythm in my chest is not obeying my commands. *Thud-thud-thud-thud.* It is frantic, bird-like, driven by a terror that predates my existence. I am providing the biology for the Echo's panic. I am sweating its final desperation. The stinging cuts on my shins from the crawl in feel impossibly deep, echoing with the phantom agony of older skin flayed by these same rocks eons ago.

I am the new vessel for an ancient scream. The high-frequency hiss of the steam vents bores into my skull, and I understand it perfectly. It is the sound of a revelation that never came, vibrating now in my own throat, held back only by the crushing density of the air. I am trapped not just in rock, but in the unending final moment of another being.
