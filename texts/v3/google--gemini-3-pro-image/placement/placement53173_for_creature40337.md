The roar is not something I hear; it is something that I am.

It resonates in the basalt core of my femurs and rattles the ceramic plating of my outer thorax. It is the engine that drives the viscous, mineral-heavy fluids through my rusted vascular piping. There is no concept of silence, only variations in the crushing pressure of sound. When the great flywheel nearby completes its agonizingly slow rotation, the air pressure drops so significantly that the condensation on my iron flanks flashes briefly into steam.

I am standing thigh-deep in a warm, oily slurry, positioned near the base of Sector 7’s primary coolant exchange. I am one of them—the custodians, the rust-giants, the fauna of the thrum.

Looking down, my sensor cowl interprets the world not as light and shadow, but as a swirling chaos of thermal gradients. My own body is a riot of heat signatures: the deep, cool purple of my stone foundation-bones, Veined with the angry oranges and reds of friction-heated joints and the pulsing yellow lines of external piping carrying hot lubrication. Water cascades over my shoulders from a ruptured overhead conduit, cooling my ceramic back-plates, registering as streaks of sudden, soothing blue against the ambient heat.

I am old. I know this because the rust on my left forearm has grown thick and flaking, resembling the bark of the extinct trees depicted in the deepest archive-glyphs. I smell the eternal wetness of iron oxide, the sharp tang of ozone from sparking contacts, and the comforting, earthy musk of the warm mud I stand in.

My current purpose is tidal in its slowness. A sluice gate, massive as a canyon wall, has jammed with accumulated mineral slag. I have been pushing against it for what a human might call an hour.

I do not strain. Strain implies a frantic expenditure of energy. I simply lean. I apply the entirety of my multi-ton mass against the obstruction, my feet planted deep in the bedrock slots carved by fifty generations of my predecessors doing this exact task. I wait for the vibration of the caldera to align with my applied force, using the planet’s own shuddering momentum to inch the gate upward.

A tremor runs through the soles of my feet—a sharp, staccato patter distinct from the background roar. It is a message from Unit 4, currently tending a geothermal vent half a kilometer away.

*<Bearing-heat-critical. Seizure-imminent. Adjustment-required.>*

I do not vocalize a reply. I shift my weight slightly, sending a low-frequency thud back into the stone.

*<Acknowledged. Continuing-sluice-clearance. Momentum-maintains.>*

The sluice gate groans—a sound felt rather than heard—and grinds upward another inch. Hot, sludge-thick water rushes past my legs. The task is incomplete, but it is progressing. The sky above the steam layer is a concept I hold no data on, nor interest in. My reality is the heat, the wet iron, the eternal duty, and the thrum that keeps me alive.
