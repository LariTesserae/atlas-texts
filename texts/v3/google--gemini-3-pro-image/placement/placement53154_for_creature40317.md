The air here does not move; it sits. It is a physical weight, a hot, wet woolen blanket pressed against every inch of my segmented body. To breathe is to drink a soup of suspended spores, vaporized resins, and the cloying, coppery tang of eternal decay.

I am blind. We are all blind here in the underbelly. Sight is a useless extravagance in a world smothered in bruised twilight and absolute night. Yet, I see with excruciating clarity. I see with the sensitive bristles that line my legs, detecting the sub-sonic thrum of a billion other things crawling through the muck. I see with the feathery antennae that taste the complex chemical architecture of the air, reading pheromone trails like illuminated manuscripts.

I am small. Not microscopic, but insignificant against the scale of the architecture here. I am a cleaner, a scavenger, a mottled creature of chitin and velvet fuzz, no bigger than a human hand.

And I am riding a god.

My world is the third segment behind the head-plate of a Myco-Titan. This great beast, thirty feet of armored muscle and grinding jaws, is my host and my universe. I live in the deep, warm crevice between its massive dorsal plating, where the symbiotic fungi grow thick like shag carpet. This is my grazing ground. I keep the Titan clean of parasitic molds that might rot its armor, and in return, I eat the rich, velvet moss that thrives on its back, and I am carried through the dark.

Right now, the world is vibrating. A low, rhythmic shudder travels up through the Titan’s thousand legs as it churns forward. It sounds like a muffled avalanche of wet gravel. We are plowing through a drift of fallen mushroom stalks, each one thicker than an ancient oak. I feel the Titan’s massive mandibles working ahead, a slow, hydraulic crunching that vibrates through its entire carapace.

The chemical landscape shimmers around us. To my left, the air tastes sudden and sharp—alarm pheromones. An Armored Hulk is nearby, signaling its territory with deep, resonant drumming in the soil. I feel the thuds in my own joints. My host ignores it; it is too big to care about the territorial disputes of lesser giants. It continues its slow, inexorable harvest.

Above us, far above in the canopy of circus-tent caps, I sense the gentle, sticky rain of spores falling from the silent Spore-Gliders drifting on thermal drafts. The spores coat my carapace, adding another layer to the film of moisture that perpetually slicks my body.

We are approaching a heat source. The temperature rises from oppressively hot to nearly blistering. The scent changes, becoming acrid, ammoniac, and intense. We are nearing a Cathedral of the Mound-Builders. It looms in my chemical senses like a roaring furnace. I can hear the millions of tiny clicks of the workers inside, a unending static of industry as they tend their subterranean fires. The Titan gives the mound a wide berth. Even a god respects the billion-fold power of the colony.

I burrow deeper into the fungal shag on the Titan's back, finding a pocket of slightly cooler, damper air. I am safe here. I am part of the great, slow churning. I am a single enzyme in the belly of the world, content to ride the beast, taste the rot, and wait in the dark for centuries to pass.
