The heat finds me first. It is not an external thing that warms a separate body; it is the medium in which I am suspended, a thick, amniotic pressure of two hundred degrees, saturated with vaporized oil and the heavy scent of wet metal.

I am not distinct from the gloom. I have no eyes to pierce the shadows between the distant furnace flares, no ears to process the monolithic, grinding roar that makes the very air shudder. I do not hear the sound; I vibrate with it. It is the constant, geological basso profundo of a subterranean turbine, a tremor that passes through the iron foundations and into the marrow of what I am.

I exist at a point of intense friction, a forgotten crevice located midway up the weeping flank of one of the lesser Leviathans.

I am a concretion.

I am built of layers, a sedimentary record of the endless war in the dark. My foundational layer is scale—flakes of oxidized iron and mineral salt shed by the great machine beast I cling to, cemented together by polymerized grease that leaked from a weeping joint centuries ago. Over this lies a stratum of scorched organic matter: the carbonized remains of milky vines that tried to choke the heat source beneath me, only to be flash-cooked by a sudden venting of superheated steam.

Above that, the new growth constantly tries to claim me. I am slick with the insulating mucilage of the Pale Sprawl. Thick, translucent root-systems, blind and desperate for thermal energy, mat over my surface. I feel their hurried, jerky growth—a spasmodic twitching against my hardened exterior as they elongate inches by the hour, probing for a fissure in the grease-scale to drink the metallic warmth.

I am the scab forming over a wound on the machine.

I have no agency, only existence and reaction. When the Leviathan "breathes," exhaling a screaming jet of vapor nearby, my temperature spikes. The outermost layer of mucilage sizzles and hardens into a protective crust, adding to my bulk. When the condensation rains down hard, washing industrial runoff from the heights above, I become slick and receptive, my pores opening to absorb the toxic sludge, fusing it to the iron beneath.

I am waiting for the inevitable swing of the pendulum.

Currently, the Sprawl is winning in my small corner. The etiolated vines are thick around me, insulating the machine's heat, causing the iron skin beneath me to sweat oil more profusely. The pressure of the growth is immense, a slow, hydraulic squeezing.

Eventually, the balance will tip. The Leviathan will shudder, perhaps registering an infinitesimally small internal pressure change caused by the vegetable blockage. A massive, rounded piston nearby, currently dormant, will fire with the inexorable force of a shifting tectonic plate.

When it moves, I will be sheared away. The friction will be absolute. I will be ground between tons of brass and mats of fibrous jade, reduced back to my constituent parts—grease, rust, and mulch—to fall into the oily mire far below.

But the piston will retract. The heat will return. The grease will drip. The vines will twitch forward again. And in this hot, wet, deafening silence, I will begin to accumulate once more.
