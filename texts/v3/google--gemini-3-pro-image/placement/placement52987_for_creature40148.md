The cold is not a temperature here; it is a medium. It is a physical presence that presses against every molecule of my being, a crushing, preserving embrace that defines the boundaries of my form. If the cold is the canvas, the noise is the paint.

I hang suspended in the mesmerizing, terrifying blue.

I am not a soft, drifting thing of jelly and cartilage like the vast leviathans that waft through the upper voids like clouds of frigid breath. Nor am I a stubborn, armored parasite clinging desperately to the burning skin of the machines.

I am a Shard-Striker. My biology is a brutal minimalist sculpture carved from the environment itself.

My body is a six-foot sliver of polished volcanic glass, scavenged from the suspended reefs and knitted together by an internal lattice of high-tensile silicate tendons. I am jagged, sleek, and incredibly brittle outside of my element. My "skin" is a microscopic layer of diamond-hard ice that constantly ablates and reforms, smoothing my obsidian edges for hydrodynamic perfection. I possess no eyes. The filtering blue light from the distant surface is irrelevant to me.

Instead, my entire glass length is a resonant chamber. I "see" the world in a chaotic topography of pressure waves. The Great Roar is my sun, my gravity, and my map.

At this moment, I am drifting in the "shallows" near a colossal suspended mountain of razor-ice. To my acoustic senses, the mountain is a terrifying void of silence—a dead zone where the omnipresent thrum strikes the hard surface and scatters weakly, creating a sensory shadow. To enter it blindly would be to smash my crystalline body against unseen facets.

But below me... below me is the screaming star.

Stabilizer Engine Unit 74-Beta looms in the deep blue. To my senses, it is not a rusted metal city, but a blinding, pulsating pillar of pure kinetic violence. It is deafening, a physical assault on my sensory lattices. Every ten seconds, the titanic piston fires. *THOOOM.*

The shockwave travels faster than I ever could. It hits me, rattling my glass teeth, vibrating in the hollows of my silicate bones. It is agonizing, and it is intoxicating.

I am hungry. The hunger is a dissonance in my internal resonance, a brittle feeling in my joints. I need trace metals to reinforce my obsidian skeleton. I need the concentrated minerals locked away in the armored shells of the hull-clingers.

I turn my sleek, spearlike head downward, toward the deafening machine. I don't swim; swimming is too slow for this viscous, pressurized ice-water. I wait.

*THOOOM.*

The next wave arrives. This time, I don't just endure it. I angle my crystalline fins, catching the leading edge of the acoustic shear pressure. It is like stepping onto a runaway locomotive. The sound wave grips my streamlined form and hurls me forward.

I accelerate instantly to lethal speeds, becoming a living missile of volcanic glass riding the sonic boom of the engine. The blue void blurs into a tunnel of pure noise. The immense pressure threatens to crack my carapace, but the speed is glorious.

I close the distance to Engine Unit 74-Beta in seconds. The hull is a landscape of shuddering, corroded alloys, so hot with friction that it leaves a trail of boiling micro-bubbles. And there, clustered around a venting heat-exchanger, are my prey: the Resonance Crabs. They are squat lumps of heavy biometal, sluggish with thermal intoxication, oblivious to everything but the warmth they steal from the machine.

I choose the largest one. I do not slow down.

I strike it with the force of the Stabilizer’s own echo behind me. My diamond-tipped snout impacts its armored shell. There is a sharp *CRACK* that is felt rather than heard beneath the overwhelming roar of the engine. The crab's metal carapace shatters.

For a fleeting moment, I am bathed in the intense, sickening heat venting from the machine's hull. It burns my icy skin, threatening to warp my glass interior. I quickly gouge out the nutrient-rich metallic slurry from inside the shattered crab, gorging myself on copper and iron isotopes in seconds.

The heat is too much. Before the engine fires again, I kick away from the vibrating hull, retreating back into the paralyzing safety of the sub-zero blue. The sudden return to cold snaps my outer ice-skin tight, sealing micro-fractures harvested in the impact.

I drift upward, heavy with stolen metal, the taste of copper in my silicate throat. Far above, a ghostly leviathan passes, a mile-long smudge of silent gel riding a gentle eddy of sound, completely unaware of the violent murder that just occurred in the deep.

I hang in the crushing blue, a shard of predatory glass, waiting for the next great *THOOOM* to reshape the world again.
