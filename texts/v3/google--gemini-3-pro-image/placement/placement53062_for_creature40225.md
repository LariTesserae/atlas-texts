The roar is not something you hear; it is the medium in which you exist. It is a persistent, grinding vibration that rattles your core components, a geological tinnitus that would shatter wet organic matter.

You are wedged into a crevasse on the underside of a "secondary" branch—a sprawling limb roughly the width of the English Channel. This particular location is a scar tissue zone, a jagged wound in the Lattice where, millennia ago, a megaton splinter sheared off and tumbled into the infinite below. The wound never healed correctly. Instead of smooth bark, the area is a chaotic erupted tangle of severed conduits and frozen waterfalls of leaked silica slurry, hanging motionless in the vacuum like cascades of dirty glass.

You are a **Static-Scrivener**.

You are a sedentary entity, a localized consciousness grown directly into the structural lattice at this point of trauma. Your body is a hardened nodule of dielectric resin and silicate, roughly the size of a grand piano, fused deeply into the canyon wall of the branch. You have no eyes; the searing point-source light of the star above would uselessly blind you, and down here in the eternal shadow of the limb, it is vacuum-black anyway.

Instead, you perceive the world through a sprawling web of extremely fine, super-conductive sensory filaments that you have grown across the open wound of the canyon. They stretch for hundreds of meters like a glittering, copper spiderweb across the abyss.

You do not hunt; you diagnose.

You exist on the bleed. The severed industrial veins around you leak minute amounts of raw energy—piezoelectric currents rushing through the surrounding wood, thermal radiation from deep-set gears, and the static crackle of the void. Your filaments harvest this ambient voltage to power your consciousness.

Your purpose—if such a thing can be ascribed to a semi-autonomous growth—is to listen to the grand machine.

Through your conductive web, you feel the pulse of the continent-sized organism. You feel the rhythmic, thrumming bass note of a methane pump cycling three hundred miles "east" along the branch artery. You taste the sharp, metallic zing of a Ferro-Termite colony sawing through a conduit far above your protected crevice. You detect the sudden, terrifying drop in vibrational pressure that indicates a massive structural failure occurring on a different horizon entirely.

Right now, the noise is changing. The grinding bass is being overtaken by a rising, frantic screech.

Your filaments begin to twitch, not physically, but magnetically. The ambient electrostatic charge in the vacuum is spiking rapidly. The taste of ozone becomes overwhelming, bypassing sensors and vibrating directly in your core processing cluster.

A storm is manifesting.

Miles above, on the sunlit surface of the branch, the light is becoming stroboscopic. Down here in the shadow, the blackness is ripped apart by silent, blinding flashes that throw the frozen cataracts of silica slurry into sharp, terrifying relief.

A bolt of lightning, thick as a redwood trunk, arcs across the gap between your branch and another one ten miles distant. The resulting electromagnetic pulse slams into your sensory web. It is agonizing and ecstatic. You engorge on the sudden influx of power, your internal processes accelerating to a fever pitch.

In the strobe-light chaos, your sensors register movement near the canyon rim above. A Lithic Scrubber, panicked by the electrical onslaught, has lost its footing. You feel the agonizing scrape of its ceramic armor sliding against the lignified cliff face. Then, the vibration ceases abruptly.

Moments later, the massive, segmented body of the arthropod tumbles silently past your web, falling into the bottomless dark. It is a brief interruption in the data stream.

The storm intensifies. The roar of the Lattice becomes a physical pressure trying to pry you from your crevice. You hunker down, retracting your most sensitive filaments, letting the excess voltage bleed off into the dead wood around you. You are a tick latched onto the hide of a thunder god, riding out the convulsions of an industrial fever, waiting for the calm that never truly comes.
