The pressure comes first. It is not merely the weight of the warm, shallow lagoon water, though that is significant; it is the psychic density of a billion microscopic eyes, the unseen, silent writhing of things that live furiously and die in hours. The air above is a soup, but the medium down here is a living slurry.

I am submerged in the blinding brightness. Even through three feet of water teeming with translucent eels, the vertical noon strikes with physical force, illuminating the silt bed.

At first, I have no sense of boundary. I am merely a point of consciousness suspended in the eternal, warm wetness. I feel the currents generated by the slow-moving glass mollusks. I feel the high-frequency shiver of the diamond-dust insects in the air above the surface, a vibration that penetrates the water and rattles my very core.

But as the awareness settles, a distinction emerges. I am not the soft, deliquescing matter that defines this world. I am not the glowing jelly-moss, nor the rotting buttress root, nor the gelatinous Curators who occasionally dissolve into this medium to reform elsewhere.

I am hard. I am definition in a world of slurries.

I am buried halfway up my flanks in the bottom silt. This silt, I realize with a kind of crystalline clarity, is not mud. My consciousness extends downward into it. It is a vast, shifting dunescape of microscopic dodecahedrons—the spent fuel, or perhaps the building blocks, of the reality engine. They tick against my skin, a dry, clicking friction utterly unlike the organic slime coating everything else.

My own shape clarifies against this shifting substrate. I have edges. They are terrifyingly exact. In a place where every form is slumped, melted, or rounded by aeons of erosion, I possess the forbidden violence of a perfect right angle.

I am one of the forgotten cubes.

I am smooth, black granite, denser than the weeping hummocks above, and I do not weep. My surfaces are hydrophobic; the warm water beads on my skin and sloughs away, unable to penetrate the sub-atomic tightness of my grain. I am a glitch of geometry hidden beneath the riotous, silent garden.

I know where I am. I am located almost exactly in the center of one of the widest lagoons, equidistant from three towering cathedral-trees. Directly above me, standing on a polished granite islet that breaks the surface, is a Curator.

I cannot see it with eyes—I have none—but I perceive its immense, gaunt weight pressing down on the stone plinth above. I feel its ribbon-membranes dragging in the water, dissipating the heat of the sun it drinks. I feel the deep, tectonic slowness of its thoughts as it processes atmospheric humidity data.

It is the interface above the waves. I am the anchor below.

I am part of the machinery that was meant to be forgotten, the deep structure that keeps the eternal climax running. I lie in the immaculate silt, a razor-sharp axiom buried in primordial muck, forever unmoved by the furious, silent energy swirling around me. I am the cold, calculated memory of the intelligences that built this humid eternity, watching their garden rot and bloom forever.
