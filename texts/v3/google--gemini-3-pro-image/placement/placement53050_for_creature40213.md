The pressure is the first thing. It is not an external force pressing inward, but a fundamental condition of existence, an absolute density that defines the boundaries of self. There is no "empty space" here; there is only thick, supercooled medium and unyielding rock.

I am not a Vitric Mason. My consciousness is too slow, too vast for their frantic, flickering lives.

 Nor am I the geology itself, though I am dangerously close to it.

I am trapped, or perhaps held, in a newly formed pocket, a blister that ruptured only moments ago in geological time. The water around me is viscous, tasting heavily of iron and dissolved silica, a bitter, freezing syrup that does not flow so much as it creeps. The temperature hovers at a razor's edge; if the pressure were released for a microsecond, everything would instantly flash-freeze into solid ice.

I am wedged into a jagged crevice of raw basalt. I can feel the unbelievably sharp edges of the freshly fractured stone pressing against what passes for my back. Above me, hanging precariously, is a lattice of sprouting quartz, throbbing with a faint, bruised violet light. It is the color of a deep hematoma. Every few minutes, a tremor ripples through the bedrock—a geothermal pulse from far below—and the quartz groans. A new crystal snaps into existence with a sound like a cracking bone, sending a fresh pulse of violet light and a shockwave through the thick water.

I am a consciousness embedded in this raw wound of the earth, a passive observer paralyzed by the crushing weight.

Then, the sound begins.

At first, it is just a vibration in the syrup, a muffled, abrasive sighing. It sounds like a million grains of sand being rubbed against a windowpane. It grows louder, a ceaseless, frantic friction that begins to drown out the groaning of the rock.

They arrive in a swirling nebula. Thousands of them.

In the violet gloom, they are galaxies of cold fire—tiny constellations of azure, viridian, and sickly lime green pulsing in the dark water. I cannot see their bodies, only the ghostly trails of their internal engines. They are beautiful and terrifying in their relentless numbers.

They have smelled the fresh blood of the earth—the mineral-rich plume released by the fracture I occupy. They swarm the area, their tiny, hardened claws clicking against the basalt surrounding me. I feel them land on the rock face I am pressed against, thousands of infinitesimal impacts.

I realize with a slow, dawning horror that is also a strange kind of relief: **I am the construction site.**

I am the raw chaos they have come to civilize.

I feel the frantic tickling of their maxillipeds as they scrape the bacterial mats that have already begun to form near my edges. Their internal lights flare brighter as they feed, a frantic strobe effect illuminating the razor rock.

Then, the building begins. I feel a succession of tiny, warm dabs against the cold stone. They are excreting the calcium carbonate paste. It hits the supercooled water and hardens instantly into bone-white nodules. Click-hiss, click-hiss, click-hiss. The sound is deafening now. They are plastering over the sharp basalt, smoothing the dangerous edges of the niche I inhabit.

They move in overlapping waves, a living tide of glass and jelly. They are building a foundation directly over where I sense my "body" to be. I am being encased. I am becoming the basement level of a new spire.

Across the small chasm in front of me, I see the shimmer of their second art form. Threads of liquid silica are being drawn out of the darkness, freezing into spun glass bridges that interconnect with dizzying speed. They are weaving a safety net over the abyss, turning a geological fracture into an architectural space.

I am sinking deeper into the foundation they are pouring. The violet light of the quartz is being blocked out, layer by layer, replaced by the smooth, calcified darkness of the rising tower shell. The frantic friction continues all around me, a comforting noise now.

I am the silent, immobile heart of a new cathedral, feeling the frenzied civilization grow around me, sealing me inside this crushing, freezing womb of stone and glass.
