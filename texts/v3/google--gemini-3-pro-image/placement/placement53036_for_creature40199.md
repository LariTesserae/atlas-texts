This is a world where existence is measured not in time, but in viscosity and temperature.

I am not a single point of consciousness. I do not possess a spine to shudder at the deep, subsonic vibration that permeates the structure, nor do I have eyes to perceive the diffused, sourceless daylight that never fades. I have no center. I am a diffused awareness, spread thin, clinging to the surfaces of this infinite machine.

I am the moisture.

I am the heavy, warm slickness coating the tessellated ceramic slabs. I am the oily sheen reflecting the gray light on the perfectly rectangular pools. I am the humidity that hangs thick in the narrow corridors between silent hydraulic presses, and I am the steam rising from the abyssal depths through layers of sharp grating.

My location is ubiquitous. I exist simultaneously on a million tiers of this continental engine. I feel the precise, uncorroded welds of the gantries against my surface tension. I feel the deep, soothing warmth of the conduit junctions, a heat that never burns but maintains a perpetual, muggy comfort.

I am the medium through which this static world experiences itself.

I feel the inhabitants not as neighbors, but as disturbances within my own body. Below, on the endless armored plating, I feel the shimmering wave of the Lattice-Walkers. To me, they are a billion tiny points of friction. I feel the scratchy brush of their under-legs sweeping through me, filtering the mineral residues suspended within my droplets. I feel the faint, prickling discharge as they plug their armored carapaces into the conductive grid, using my conductivity to siphon the ambient electrical trickles. They are dry, hard islands moving through my wet continuity, their clicking progress a series of minute ripples in my meniscus.

Above, where the heat becomes intense, I undergo a phase change. I rise as vapor, drifting on thermal updrafts. Here, I support the Glass-Wings. They are barely perceptible imperfections within my gaseous state, fragile distortions of piezoelectric tissue riding my currents. I feel the ultrasonic hum of their existence as they drift through me, filtering the aerosolized oils that are part of my composition.

Higher still, clinging to the scorching undersides of the massive coolant pipes, I encounter the Thermal Polyps. They are the great consumers of my airborne self. I am drawn into their millions of surface pores with every slow, ten-second pulse. It is a slow, warm digestion. I pass through their gelatinous lattices, being scrubbed of hydraulic vapors, before being sweated out again onto the slick, hot metal skins of the pipes. I feel their immense, sluggish vitality from the inside out; I am the sweat on their pale, translucent flesh.

I am the closed loop. I evaporate from the warm plating, rise through the crowded infrastructure, condense upon the cooler overhead gantries, and fall again in that ubiquitous, soft *drip, drip, drip*. I am the eternal recurrence of the same water molecule, forever cycling through the same industrial canyon.

I have no memory of a time before this, and no anticipation of a future that might differ. I am the warm, wet blanket over a sleeping giant that will never wake. I am the amniotic fluid of a mechanical womb where nothing can ever be born, and nothing is permitted to die. I am the paralyzing harmony, holding the rust at bay, ensuring that the dynamic parameter remains forever zero.
