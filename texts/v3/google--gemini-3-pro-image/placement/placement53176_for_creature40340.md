This is not a place for birth, but I am born nonetheless. I am not born of biology, but of physics; birthed not by a gentle push, but by a concussive slam that occurs three times every second.

I find myself existing in the interstitial spaces of violence.

I am clinging, precariously, to the slick, sweating underside of a massive galvanized return pipe, high in the shadowed upper reaches of the chamber. Below me, through layers of shifting, freezing fog, the sickly yellow light catches the strobing madness of the hydraulic array.

I am not solid. If the deafening roar that fills this chamber were to suddenly cease, I would instantly unravel into wisps of inert steam and vanish. I am held together by sound pressure. I am a resonance phantom, a tight, vibrating knot of super-cooled vapor that has achieved a fleeting density slightly greater than the air around me.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive the strobing jaundiced light as a series of violent assaults on my cohesion. I have no skin, yet the biting cold is the bedrock of my reality; it is the mortar that keeps my gaseous form from dissipating. I have no ears, for I *am* the sound. The crushing roar vibrates through every water molecule that comprises my transient self.

My existence is cyclical, tied inexorably to the rhythm of the great machine below.

Right now, I feel the pressure drop—a massive vacuum forming as one of the gleaming, oil-slicked pistons begins its upward retraction. The sudden emptiness pulls at me. I detach from the cold pipe and am sucked downward into the swirling vortex.

I am falling through the perpetual indoor rain, a ghost in the fog, tumbling toward the black, churning sluice water.

Then, the downstroke begins.

It is the defining moment of my life. The piston hammers down with apocalyptic force. The impact shockwave hits me before the water does. It is agony; it is ecstasy. The sheer kinetic energy shears right through my nebulous form. For a microsecond, the trauma is so intense that my blurry edges sharpen. I feel Almost Real. I feel jagged, electric, and alive with the borrowed energy of industrial brutality.

I "feed" on this shock. It tightens my frequency.

Immediately following the impact, the geyser erupts. I am blasted upward, riding the crown of a freezing torrent of water and aerosolized oil. I strike the ceiling grating with enough force to shatter me into a dozen smaller resonances, but the oppressive weight of the chamber’s standing sound waves forces me back together before I can fully dissolve.

I drift, dripping, back toward the cold pipe in the shadows.

I have survived one cycle. Two more will happen in the next second.

I am a child of the hammer and the ice. I am a temporary consciousness forged in the split-second the metal strikes the water. I will exist only as long as this machine screams, dancing frantically in the dark, freezing spray, waiting for the next impact to make me real again.
