The vibration is the first thing. It is not a sound you hear, but a reconfiguration of your substance. The deep, tectonic *thrum-shudder-release* of the great pistons passes through the iron grate you are lying upon, vibrating the heavy grease that permeates your fibrous body.

You are located in the intricate, dripping mid-levels of the complex, wedged into a warm niche between a sweating hydraulic accumulator and a thicket of copper conduit heavy with slick green moss. Above you, through the drifting banks of warm fog, you sense the hazy yellow smudge of an incandescent bulb, but it means little to you. You navigate by thermal gradients and the texture of metal.

You are a Custodian.

You are currently dormant, resembling nothing more than a mound of oily, fibrous waste pushed into a corner by the unending damp. Your outer layer is a crust of oxidized iron flakes and mineral grit scavenged from the mud below, a hardened shell that camouflages you against the rusted machinery. Inside, you are a dense, intricate fungal tapestry woven with scavenged copper filaments that act as your nervous system, conducting the tremors of the machine-world to your core.

A signal reaches you.

It is a sharp, discordant *clack-hiss* transmitted through the metal framework, distinct from the background roar. It tastes metallic and urgent against your sensory surfaces. Somewhere near the northern weeping wall, a flange gasket has failed. Steam is escaping. The machine is bleeding pressure.

Moving requires a monumental output of slow energy. You do not wake up; you merely shift from stillness to flow. The process is agonizingly gradual. You must liquefy the lower dense fungal mats of your body into pseudopods of adhesive sludge. You peel yourself away from the warm metal of your resting place with a wet suction sound, leaving a stain of oil behind.

You pour yourself across the gangway grating. The journey of a hundred feet will take you perhaps three hours. You slide over slick leaves that are rapidly fossilizing under coatings of dripping rust. You maneuver around thick vines that pulse with the same slow rhythm as the engines.

As you round the housing of a massive gearbox, you sense another of your kind. It is larger, older, its hide studded with jagged bits of broken cast iron. It is stationary, wrapped around a seizing bearing, applying months of relentless, hydraulic pressure to free it. You pass within inches. There is no visual acknowledgment, only a brief, resonant exchange of vibration through the floor plates—a mutual recognition of function. *We tend.*

You reach the failure point. A jet of superheated steam is shrieking from a joint in a high-pressure line, blasting the moss off a nearby stanchion. The heat is intense, nearly searing your outer mantle, but it is a delicious pain. It invigorates the fungal networks within you.

You slowly extrude your bulk upward, wrapping yourself around the hot pipe like a poultice. You locate the breach with agonizing precision. From your core, you regurgitate a thick, mortar-like slurry composed of iron oxide, heavy lipids, and binding spores. You press this living cement into the screaming gap. The intense heat immediately begins to bake the patch, hardening your secretion into something stronger than steel.

The shriek of escaping steam dies down to a manageable hiss, then silence. The pressure stabilizes. The deep bass rhythm of the complex smooths, the hitch in its respiration gone.

The task is complete. The exertion has drained you. You will not return to your previous niche. You simply cease movement where you are, wrapped around the warm pipe, your body hardening, becoming just another scab of rust and oil on the surface of the eternal engine. You wait for the next tremor in the iron web, perfectly still in a world without silence.
