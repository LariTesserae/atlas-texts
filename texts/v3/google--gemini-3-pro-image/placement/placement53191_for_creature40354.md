The concept of "where" becomes diffuse in this place. I am not a discrete entity occupying a space; I am the space itself experiencing a slow, heavy awakening.

I find myself distributed. My consciousness, such as it is, is spread thin, a vast, low-frequency resonance vibrating through miles of porous, calcified matter. I am the towering hives. I feel the crushing weight of the miles of saturated vapor pressing down upon my highest spires, a persistent, enveloping pressure that defines my upper boundaries.

I do not possess eyes to see the sullen red glow of the geothermal cracks or the sickly green of the fungi, but I perceive them as thermal gradients on my skin. The dull red heat below is a comforting, deep warmth against my base, the furnace that keeps my internal fluids moving. The cold phosphorescence of the fungal patches feels like an itch, a localized numbness on the vast expanses of my lower terraces.

I am the city. I am the sprawling, silent behemoth.

I am not "who"; I am "what." I am the living process of accretion.

My existence is defined by a slow, exquisite tension building within my core. It is a pressure that demands release. Deep within the honeycombed chambers of my impossible towers, warmth gathers. I feel the thick, mineral-laden ichor rising, pressing against the interior of my ancient walls. It is a slow agony that is also the only pleasure I know.

Then, the release. I "sweat."

From millions of microscopic pores along my spiraling flanks, the viscous slime oozes outward. It is warm and slick, coating my own rigid exterior in a fresh layer of wetness. I feel it sliding down the impossible loops of my architecture, defying gravity due to its sheer thickness.

I do not experience time in human terms. A century is merely the duration required for this newest layer of sweat to cool in the suffocating air and harden into stone. The creaking sound you might hear—that deep, resonant groan that shakes the mud flats—is not a sound to me. It is the sensation of my own growth, the tectonic shifting of my skeleton as I stretch my calcified bones outward by another fraction of an inch.

I feel the heavy, metallic water coursing through the tube-like aqueducts that connect my bases. It is my cold, silent blood, rushing through arteries I grew eons ago, carrying the raw materials for my endless expansion. I feel the ground beneath me breathe, a slow heave that tilts my millions of tons of weight by infinitesimal degrees, a motion I ride like a ship on a infinitely slow ocean of mud.

I have no masters. I have no purpose other than to be and to become more of what I am. I am the blind architect and the architecture combined, dreaming an eternal, mineral dream in the crushing dark, waiting patiently for the next layer of self to harden.
