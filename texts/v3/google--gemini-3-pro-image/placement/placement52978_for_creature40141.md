The weight of the water is indistinguishable from the weight of the air. Both are hot, thick, and pressing down with a suffocating constancy.

I am lying belly-deep in the warm silt at the bottom of one of the deeper channels, a groove worn perhaps twenty feet into the iron-hard bedrock over millions of years. My world is opaque, a sepia-toned gloom thickened by suspended mud and the raining detritus of dying algae from the surface mats.

I am one of the armored cylinders, the hidden lurkers. My body is a heavy, flattened tube of calcified plating, mottled in the same oxidized reds and bruised purples as the rock I rest upon. I do not have eyes in the traditional sense; in this churned soup, sight is a useless extravagance. Instead, my entire length is a sensory instrument. The lateral lines running along my flanks are exquisitely tuned to pressure waves.

I feel the world above me.

The air interface is a chaotic ceiling of vibration. The colossal dragonflies are not just sounds; they are physical impacts on the water’s surface that shudder down to my resting place. The lower, deeper drone of the fist-sized beetles is a constant bass thrum that resonates in my heavy jaw plates.

But I am ignoring the sky-noise. I am tuned to the immediate surroundings. I feel the infinitesimal ticking of thousands of seed-crustaceans drifting past my armored head, filtering the slime. I ignore them; they are too small for the energy expenditure of movement.

I am waiting for the scrape.

It comes, transmitted through the bedrock and into the water. A slow, heavy abrasion against stone. *Scrape... pause... scrape... pause.* Something large and clumsy is hauling itself out of a basking pool and toward the edge of my channel.

I shift slightly, displacing a cloud of warm mud. My respiration is a slow, tidal intake of the tepid, de-oxygenated water, processed by gills that burn with the effort in this sauna.

The scraping stops at the channel's lip. I sense the displacement—a large, squat object lowering itself into the upper water column. It is one of the grey amphibian giants, sluggish with heat, seeking the slightly cooler depths of my territory. I feel its clumsy paddling motions, the chaotic wash of water pushed by its splayed feet. It tastes of sun-baked slate and mucus.

It is swimming above me now, a shadow darker than the silt gloom. It is slow, stupid with longevity, assuming itself the master of these shallow seas.

Now.

My stasis breaks with explosive violence. My tail, a massive rigid paddle, slams sideways against the channel wall. The propulsion is instantaneous, driving my armored bulk upward through ten feet of water in a heartbeat. The mud explodes around me.

I breach the surface not with grace, but as a geological upheaval. The bright, humid air hits my sensory skin like a physical blow. The noise of the insects is suddenly deafening.

My jaws—broad, fused plates of bone designed to crack bedrock—snap shut on the amphibian’s thick hindquarter. There is a dull, wet crunch that I feel more than hear. The creature bellows, a pathetic, vibrating croak that gets cut short as I wrench my body sideways, dragging it down into the opaque depths.

The struggle is brief. It has endurance, but I have mass and crushing power. I pin it against the channel floor, the silt billowing up to shroud the kill.

Above, the V-wake of my violence settles slowly into rippling rings that spread across the endless flat water, eventually dissipating against the distant, shimmering horizon. The dragonflies resume their patrolling hum over the disturbed spot.

Down in the warm dark, I settle back into the mud. I begin the slow, grinding process of feeding. The noise of the world continues above, a ceaseless vibration, but down here, waiting for the next age to pass, there is only the heat, the heavy water, and the taste of ancient blood.
