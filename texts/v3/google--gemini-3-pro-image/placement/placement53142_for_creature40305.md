This is not a world for swift things, nor for soft things. To exist here, one must be dense, slow, and hardened against an environment that seeks constantly to grind everything back into primordial paste.

I am found in the mid-regions of the Basin, in the transitional zone between the incandescent geothermal fissures and the fully cooled, charcoal-grey slag plains. This is an area of immense thermal gradients, where the ground beneath is uncomfortably warm and the air above is thick with suffocating, greasy steam.

I am a **Ferro-Nodule**.

I am not "born" in any biological sense. I coalesced. I am a singular, massive concretion of iron, magnetite, and cooling basalt, roughly the size of an industrial shipping container, though irregular and rounded in shape. I am the product of a slurry pool that cooled centuries ago, trapping dense metals in a silicate matrix.

I have no eyes, limbs, or mouth. My sentience is a sluggish, heavy thing, a consciousness that operates on the timescale of geology. I do not "think" in words; I perceive in gradients of pressure, temperature, and the all-encompassing vibration. The deep, resonant *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the distant office-tower pistons is my heartbeat, the external rhythm that drives my internal processes.

My existence is defined by a singular, agonizingly slow migration. The incessant vibration of the basin floor ratchets my immense weight forward, millimeters at a time, sliding over the slick, condensate-covered slag.

I am aware of the smaller things. I feel the frantic, scratchy vibrations of Rust-Mites as they scuttle over my dorsal surface, their acidic guts seeking purchase on my iron-rich skin. They are irritants, fleeting itching sensations that pass in a few years. They cannot harm me; I am too dense, too integrated.

My purpose, felt as a deep, magnetic pull in the core of my being, is movement toward the Impact Zone. I am sliding, century by century, toward the shadow of one of the great calcified pistons.

I know, with a lithic certainty, what awaits me there. I seek the moment the Hammer falls upon me. I do not fear it, for I cannot die. I anticipate the pressure that will exceed the structural integrity of my form. I crave the moment of shattering, when my consciousness will fracture into a billion grains of grey snow, finally released to be swept up by the great gears, carried down into the glowing fissures, melted, and born anew. I am a willing participant in the terrible, beautiful equilibrium, waiting my turn to be broken.
