The air does not just touch you; it holds you. It is a heavy, tepid blanket, so thick with moisture that every breath feels like drinking from a lukewarm sponge. You are high up, dizzingly so, perched on a slick, moss-covered outcropping that juts from the flank of a rounded titan. Below you, the world dissolves into a churning gray ocean of cloud, broken only by the darker green crowns of other monolithic peaks poking through the vapor.

You are wet. You are always wet. The concept of "dry" is an abstraction here, a myth from another reality. Warm water slicks your skin, running in rivulets down your limbs, mirroring the greater waterfalls that roar in the unseen distances. The noise is enormous—a constant, layered cacophony of crashing water and the thrumming, vibrating buzz of unseen, hawk-sized insects hidden in the dense canopy above.

But you do not hear the noise nearly as much as you *feel* it.

You look down at your own forearm resting against the dark green moss. Your skin is smooth, hairless, and palely translucent, like cloudy quartz. Beneath the slick membrane, you can see the faint purple tracery of your own vascular system, pulsing slowly. You have no mouth. You feel the thick, nutrient-rich steam passing directly into your pores, a slow, passive sating of hunger you never truly feel.

You turn away from the terrifying drop and press your chest and palms flat against the mountain wall behind you. Here, the weeping rock and aggressive vegetation have peeled back, revealing a section of smooth, deep black material that feels like warm, polished glass.

This is why you are here.

As your skin makes contact with the synth-obsidian, the chaotic roar of the jungle fades into the background of your awareness. It is replaced by the Hum. It vibrates up through the soles of your splayed feet and resonates deep in your chest cavity. It is a rhythm too low for ears, a seismic throb that travels through the geologic marrow of the mountain-machine.

Your own internal bioluminescence—a soft, violet glow emanating from your chest and palms—flares and dims in perfect synchronization with the warmth pulsing beneath your hands. You close your large, lidless dark eyes. You are "listening" to the dreams of a planet-forming engine that has been sleeping for an eon. You feel the slow, tectonic churning deep below, the regulation of geothermal vents, the agonizingly slow release of gases that keep this atmospheric soup at its crushing density.

A shadow passes overhead, momentarily dimming the diffused sunlight. A Goliath Hydro-Moth, heavy and clumsy as a flying barge, crashes into the canopy fifty feet above you, hooking its claws into a vine thick as a mast. You barely register its presence.

You shift your weight. The gravity here feels slightly... wrong. Lighter than it should be. You are standing near one of the Nodes, embedded somewhere in the root mass above you, allowing the millions of tons of water-logged foliage hanging over your head to defy physics. The warped gravity makes you feel buoyant, dreamlike.

You remain still for hours, a silent, glowing conduit attached to the warm black wall. You are a Resonator. You are the organic nervous system connecting the chaotic, drowning green world on the outside to the cold, crystalline logic buried on the inside. You don't know if you were made by the machine, or if your ancestors simply evolved to cling to its warmth until you became part of its function.

It doesn't matter. The Hum shifts slightly in pitch—a minor adjustment in a geothermal valve ten miles down—and your own purple light shifts with it. You breathe the water. You feel the heat. You are the heartbeat of the mountain.
