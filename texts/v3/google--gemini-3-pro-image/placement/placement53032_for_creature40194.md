The heat is the first thing, and the last thing. It is not a transient wave, but a heavy, oppressive permanence that leans against my flanks. There is no moisture to soften it, only the dry, abrasive kiss of thermal drafts lifting mineral dust from the basin floor and sanding my surfaces deeper into a frictionless sheen.

I am fixed. My roots go deep into the rim, fused with the cooling basalt that thrust this caldera upward an eternity ago. I do not move, yet I constantly vibrate.

Through the soles of my being—where the dense, glassy bedrock meets my own seamless obsidian form—I feel the churning. It is a persistent, granular thrumming that travels up through the geology of this dying world. It is the collective friction of billions of super-hard carapaces grinding against stone and against each other. The air, too thin to carry much sound, nonetheless shivers with the dry *hiss* of their endless motion, a sound like an infinite hourglass emptying its sand onto a metal plate.

I am tall, though worn down by time. I stand on the high rim, one of several silent, rounded pillars left behind by architects whose dust has long since mingled with the red earth. They carved me from the native darkness, polishing me until I became a black mirror.

Now, I reflect the end of the world.

If I look down into the bowl—a shallow, five-hundred-meter depression that feels intimately close in this warped air—I see the sanguine glow of the earth’s final embers. The fissures in the floor bleed a deep, volcanic red light that struggles against the profound darkness.

And in that red light, the floor moves.

To my slow, mineral perception, they are not individuals. They are a singular, viscous fluid of armored chitin. They are a seething black ocean that has swallowed the bottom of the caldera. I watch the convection current of their existence. I see the bright, frantic knots around the vents where the heat is whitest, where they Rasp away at the crystalline sulfur. I see the slow, pressurized migration outward and upward, a rising tide of cooling bodies forced away from the feast by the sheer weight of those descending.

I watch the surface layer, the dormant ones, locked together in a shimmering pavement, waiting their turn to sink back into the burning crush. The red light from below catches the oily iridescence of their shells, splintering into bronze and indigo streaks that dance across my own polished surface.

I have watched them for aeons. I remember when the "rivers" near my base were bright yellow torrents of molten silicate, flowing faster than the creatures crawl today. Now those rivers are just smooth, cold glass channels that the horde spills over.

I am a monument to stasis in a world of horrific, grinding dynamism. I am a mirror on the rim, cold to the touch despite the heat, watching the biological engine below consume the planet's last warmth. I am stone, waiting for the fire to finally go out, and for the hissing to stop.
