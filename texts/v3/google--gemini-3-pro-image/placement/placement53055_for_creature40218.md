The warmth is the first thing. It does not come from above, though the sun is a heavy, pressing weight on my dorsal armored plates. The true heat rises from below, a steady, geothermal hum radiating through the polished ochre bedrock I am sealed against.

I am part of the skin of this world.

I exist in a shallow depression on the flank of what was once, eons ago, a mountain range. Now, it is merely a smooth, undulating wave of bronze-colored stone, worn down to its roots. My own form is a flattened disc, perhaps a meter across, segmented and chitinous. My shell carries the exact granular texture and bruised-purple striations of the hematite flow I rest upon. To the eye, I am invisible—just another patch of oiled stone.

But to the *sense* of this place, I am a loud node of stillness in a churning sea.

I have not moved my location in three hundred orbits. I am an anchor, deeper and heavier than the multitudinous "shield-backs" that flow around me like slow, clicking water.

It is presently high afternoon. The air is thin, incredibly dry, and hot enough to desiccate soft tissue in minutes. Around me, the landscape is a tableau of suspended animation. The vast carpet of living things is pressed flat, apertures sealed tight against the bronze ground, waiting. Down slope, in a deeper basin, I can see the deflated, tar-like mounds of the "mist-sponges," looking like heaps of discarded, leathery rinds.

The silence is profound, but it is a silence textured by friction. If I tune my sensory bristles to the stone beneath me, I hear the omnipresent, soft *shhhhhhh* of billions of tiny chitinous legs making microscopic adjustments on the smooth rock. It is the sound of a continent breathing dust.

We are waiting for the exhale.

As the oppressive sun dips toward the curved horizon, the temperature plummets. The stone beneath me retains its heat, but the air cools rapidly. This is the trigger. The earth begins to sweat. Thick, clinging mist rises from unseen fissures, smelling faintly of iron and ancient wetness.

The world animates.

The vibration in the bedrock changes pitch. It transforms from a static hum into a complex, layered symphony of thuds and sliding tones that travel for miles through the stone. A low-frequency pulse reaches me from the north—a herd signal.

Around my stationary form, the mosaic begins to flow. Thousands of dinner-plate-sized shield-backs detach their rims from the rock and begin their millimeter creep. They move in unison, guided by the subterranean thrumming, grazing on the sudden bloom of bacterial filaments brought to life by the dew. Their passing over my own shell is a gentle, multitudinous clicking.

Below us, the basins groan. The mist-sponges are swelling. It is a slow-motion explosion of flesh. I watch as the deflated tar-mounds gorge on the thick air, bloating into glistening, purple-grey monoliths that pulse with hydraulic pressure. By dawn, they will move, leaving behind the shining highways of mucus that the smaller scavengers will feast upon when the sun returns.

I do not need to move to feed. I am old, and my metabolism is geared to geological time. The trace minerals I absorb directly from the rock through my ventral pores, combined with the moisture I trap during the night fogs, are enough.

My purpose now is mere endurance. I am a repository of vibration. I remember the footfalls of generations. I remember the seismic shudder five cycles ago when a thermal crack opened in the valley to the east—a sound that deafened the herd-sense for a month.

I also sense the anomalies. Just beneath the surface of the stone I grip, there is a pattern that is not natural. A perfect right angle, smoothed by millions of years but still stubbornly geometric, buried in the flow of the rock. It is an echo of the "built past." It means nothing to the flowing herds, but to me, sitting still for centuries, it is a snag in the smooth vibration of the world. A memory of something upright and hurried that did not last.

I feel a stiffness in my outer segments. My own calcification has begun. It is not painful, merely a slow transitioning of state. Over the next century, my biology will yield completely to geology. I will stop being a creature on the rock and become a feature of it. Eventually, the recyclers will come with their slow acids to smooth me down, and I will become just another polished undulation in this vast, warm, crowded tomb.

Until then, I hold my place in the shifting bronze ocean, listening to the hushing sound of a world wearing itself away.
