The heat is the first thing that defines me. It is not an external force, but a medium I swim through, a heavy, wet fluid that pushes against every inch of my being.

I am low to the ground, crouched in the perpetual twilight created by the towering, steam-wreathed walls of the amphitheater. The air here is so saturated that breathing is less an intake of gas and more a filtration of hot vapor. I am hidden in the deep, slick shadow beneath the curled frond of a giant door-sized fern, its black-green surface sweating onto my carapace.

My form is necessitated by the landscape. I am not soft like the rapid-cycle vegetation; the razor-floor of shattered basalt would flay soft flesh in seconds. I am six-legged and low-slung, built for stability on the chaotic jumble of knife-edges. My outer shell is a fused mosaic of scavenged obsidian shards and excreted mineral silicate, a jagged mirror reflecting the fractured sky, allowing me to blend perfectly with the deadly terrain. I am small—perhaps the size of a human forearm—insignificant against the scale of the cathedral.

I possess sight, but it is secondary. In this swirling fog, eyes are easily fooled. Instead, I perceive the world through an agonizingly acute thermal awareness. The steam vents are blinding pillars of white-hot agony to avoid; the cooling pools are soothing patches of blue and purple. The ferns are lukewarm islands.

My purpose here is lowly, a bottom-feeder in this high-energy womb. I am a scavenger of the alchemical runoff.

A sudden, violent hiss cuts through the rhythmic *plip-plop* of the drizzle. A geyser erupts nearby, spewing superheated water and mud. I hunker down, my clawed feet gripping the glass rock, letting the scalding spray wash over my heat-resistant shell. As the eruption subsides, I scuttle forward. The hot water has washed over a patch of the strange, metallic flora, bursting their cells. I reach the resulting puddle of sludge. I lower my segmented head and extend a proboscis. I drink the slurry, filtering out the distinct, thrilling tang of copper and the heavy, earthy taste of dissolved minerals. This is my sustenance: the metallic dregs of the mountain's hidden machinery.

Then, the vibration changes.

Beneath the omnipresent deep thrum of the nearby black pylon—a sensation I feel in the very core of my armored joints—comes a heavier, rhythmic impact. The ground shudders.

The Gardener is coming.

I immediately freeze, flattening my jagged body against a slab of wet basalt, becoming just another piece of the shattered floor.

To my thermal sense, the entity is overwhelmingly massive, a towering eclipse cutting through the steam. It is a paradox of temperature. Its armored plates are a void, absorbing all thermal radiation, registering as impossibly cold black holes against the ambient heat. Yet, the slivers between those plates radiate with the ferocious, blinding intensity of an exposed magma vein. It is like watching a walking volcano contained within a suit of absolute darkness.

It moves with terrifying, silent slowness, sliding past my hiding spot. The sheer displacement of air by its bulk nearly tips me over. It does not perceive me; I am too small, too cold, part of the inanimate background noise of its garden.

I watch, paralyzed by an instinctual awe, as it approaches a cluster of the geometric crystalline flowers that have sprouted near a pylon. The creature towers over them, its cowl tilted. I see the delicate, four-fingered hand reach out—obsidian claws that could rend solid rock.

It strikes a crystal bloom. *Ting.*

The sound is pure, sharp, and somehow painful. It cuts right through my carapace, vibrating against my own mineral structure. It is slightly off-key with the deep, subterranean hum of the pylon, creating a beat frequency that makes my insides quiver.

The Gardener waits, its magma-glow pulsing slowly. It seems dissatisfied with the resonance. It strikes the crystal again, harder this time. A hairline fracture appears in the quartz.

*Tiiiiiiing.*

The pitch shifts lower. The dissonance resolves into a strange, heavy harmony with the pylon's vibration. The air feels suddenly denser, more stable.

The great cowl nods once. The tuning is complete. The Gardener turns, its massive, shovel-like foot landing inches from my face, crushing the very rock I grip before it slides silently away into the steam, continuing its eternal, incomprehensible rounds.

I remain frozen for a long time after the magma glow has faded into the fog. Only when the ambient heat returns to its normal, suffocating level do I unlatch my claws from the rock and creep back towards the copper-rich sludge, feeding quietly in the shadow of giants and machines.
