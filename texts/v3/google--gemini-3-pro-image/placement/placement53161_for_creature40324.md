The sensation comes first, long before thought. It is a crushing, universal embrace. There is no "up" or "down" here, only *inward*. Four miles of black ocean press against every square millimeter of my being. But I do not buckle. I am dense, built of compressed muscle and cartilage fused with scavenged minerals. I am a creature of this crushing weight; without it, I would likely explode.

Then, the cold. It is a physical presence, a biting, sapping entity that seeks to turn the fluids in my veins to slush.

But I have found a shield against the cold.

I am clung tight to a vibrating mountainside of corroded steel. I am a **Vent-Leech**, a fist-sized armored slug, and my world is the seam where a high-pressure exhaust pipe meets the ancient basalt. My dorsal side—a segmented shell rough with integrated iron filings and quartz grit—faces the freezing, roaring dark of the abyss. My ventral side, soft and sucker-mouthed, is sealed against the hot metal skin of a human machine.

I do not "see" the world in the way the soft things inside the metal bubble do. The sudden, searing yellow cones of their halogen lamps are merely painful spikes of radiation that cause me to clamp tighter to the pipe. My reality is vibration and taste.

The world is screaming. The ocean itself is a constant, bass-heavy roar as currents blast through the drowned canyons, a sound that vibrates in the bedrock. But louder still is the Machine I cling to. It is a deafening, grinding shriek of pumps whining and huge drills tearing into the planet’s crust. I feel this sound not with ears, but with my entire body. The vibrations rattle my teeth; they resonate in the iron of my shell.

When the drill hits a particularly dense vein of rock, the whole structure shudders violently. A Rust-Crab, massive and clumsy, loses its footing on the pipe above me and tumbles away into the gloom, its claws screeching uselessly against the steel. I hold fast. The vibration is life.

The vibration means the heat is coming.

The pump whines up a chromatic scale, and then the vent blasts open. A plume of superheated, mineral-rich water ejects inches from my face, warring with the freezing current. It is a chaotic swirling of scalding death and freezing death. I crawl forward into the turbulence, into the exquisite borderland between burning and freezing.

The Machine is feeding us. It sucks in the living clouds of krill and copepods, cooks them instantly in its massive engines, and spews the thermally-shocked slurry back out. I extend my feeding proboscis—a feathery, armored tube—into the hot stream. I taste sulfur, copper, hydraulic fluid, and the rich, meaty taste of boiled plankton.

I gorge myself as the heat washes over my iron back. Out in the cold dark, beyond the yellow lights, I sense the pressure waves of the massive Gloom-Stalkers circling, waiting for scraps that drift too far from the heat. Let them have the cold leftovers. I am at the source.

Sometimes, I feel the pale, soft things moving inside the pipes. Their footsteps are dull thuds transmitted through thick steel walls. They are fragile, water-filled sacks that would burst instantly out here. They serve the Machine, repairing its groaning joints so it can keep spitting heat and food for me. I respect their utility.

I dig my anchoring hooks deeper into the pitted steel. The abyss tries to crush me. The cold tries to freeze me. The currents try to tear me away. But I am armored, I am dense, and I am warm. As long as the Machine screams, I survive.
