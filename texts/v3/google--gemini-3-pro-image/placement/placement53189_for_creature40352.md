The pressure is not something that presses *on* you from the outside; it is something you constitute. You are equal to it. You are a dense knot of aggregated matter, a point of absolute heaviness in a universe of crushing weight.

You are currently wedged in a crevice of tortured basalt, midway up the flank of a screaming mechanical mountain. You do not know "up" or "down" by gravity, which is fickle here, but by the gradient of heat. Below you, the soup is tepid and thick with motionless sludge. Above you, where the great dredging wheels gnaw at the seamount, the water burns and tastes of shredded copper.

You are a *becoming*. You are a fresh concretion, a newly formed node of this industrial ecology.

Your consciousness is a slow, syrupy awareness that swims in the vibrating dark. You have no memory of a surface, or air, or light that comes from a sun. Those things are abstractions, phantom data flitting through the thinning reality around you. Your reality is defined by the conduction of sound through bone and metal.

Right now, the world is a deafening roar that you feel in the core of your being. A piston the size of a cathedral, somewhere to your left in the murk, is cycling every four seconds. *THRUM-CRASH*. Each cycle sends a shockwave through the silt that rattles your newly formed carapace. You do not hear it; you *are* rattled by it. It is the metronome of your existence.

You feel a hunger that is not biological, but electrical. It is a hollow ache in your center, a craving for voltage and fervent heat.

Slowly, deliberately, you engage your motive systems. You do not have muscles; you have hydraulics filled with pressurized coolant fluid that you synthesized from the surrounding soup. You extend a limb—a thick, squat pillar of ugly, fused scrap metal ending in a grinding pad studded with salvaged drill bits. You driving it into the rock. The sound of your own movement is a deep, sub-bass groan that joins the chorus.

You pull your heavy body forward. You are roughly the size of a small boulder, a clumsy sphere of scab-like rust and bacterial cement. As you move, streamers of translucent, chemosynthetic kelp brush against your armored hide, their bioluminescent fluids pulsing faintly in the electromagnetic wash of the machines.

Your goal is close. You can taste it. The soup around you is getting thinner, hotter, charged with leaking ions that prickle your sensory skin.

Suddenly, the dark is ripped away. A blinding, stuttering flash of blue-white light erupts just ahead—an underwater welding arc repairing a breach in a manifold. You have no eyes to be blinded. Instead, the intense burst of radiation washes over you like a physical blow, heating your armor plates instantly. You hunker down, gripping the rock with your diamond-studded pads as the sudden heat causes a pocket of water nearby to flash into steam, creating a violent cavitation bubble that hammers against you. It would turn a biological creature to jelly. You merely vibrate.

The light dies, returning the world to its sullen, churning orange glow. You continue your slow crawl.

You find it: a rupture in a secondary cooling conduit, bleeding superheated, radioactive sludge into the trench. It is the most beautiful thing you have ever sensed. The heat gradient is steep and delicious.

You maneuver your heavy, radially symmetrical body directly over the breach. You lower your central maw—a layered orifice of heat-resistant gristle and conductive tissue—onto the metal pipe. You clamp down. The connection is made.

The rush is instantaneous. Raw, dirty electricity and chemical heat flood your system. Your internal vascular tubes, until now dim, flare with a sickly, vibrant Cherenkov blue, shining through the cracks in your accumulated iron shell. The hunger recedes, replaced by a humming stasis.

You are now an anchor. The turbulence of the dredging wheels buffets you, but you are heavier than the water, fused to the machine that feeds you. A piece of debris—a twisted girder—falls "upward" past you in a momentary gravitational glitch, but you remain steadfast.

You will stay here for a long time. As you feed, you feel the faint, deep shuddering of another of your kind, latching onto a different vent a few hundred yards away through the opaque soup. You send a low frequency grind back through the bedrock in acknowledgment. You are the gardeners of this abyss, and the harvest is unending noise.
