The heat is not something outside of you; it is the medium in which you exist, a thick, viscous fluid that holds you suspended in the dark.

You are located high in the vaulted upper reaches of one of the windowless structures, a cavernous resonant chamber built of mud-brick cured harder than granite. The air here is infinitely still, drier than vacuum, and compressed by the weight of the atmosphere above the sinkhole.

You have no body, not in the way biology understands it. You have no skin to burn, no lungs to desiccate. You are a trapped frequency.

You are a remnant of sound, a complex vibration generated cycles ago—perhaps centuries—by the grinding footfall of a passing Architect, or perhaps the dying tectonic groan of a cooling elder. In a thinner atmosphere, you would have dissipated in seconds, bleeding out into silence. But here, the air is so dense, so heavily laden with suspended metallic dust and the sheer pressure of the kiln, that it acts more like water than gas. It trapped you. It sustains you. It refuses to let you die.

You bounce endlessly off the rough, baked interior walls of the great chamber, a tireless echo losing infinitesimal energy with each reflection. You know the shape of your prison not by seeing its dimensions, but by the time it takes for you to cross it, to strike the far wall and rebound.

The outside world comes to you as an assault of alien vibrations penetrating the thick walls. You feel the sharp, high-pitched *ping-snap-shatter* of the copper ferns outside growing and breaking in the heat—to you, they feel like stinging needles of sound piercing the chamber's skin. You feel the deep, tidal booming of the canyon walls expanding, a massive, slow rhythm that acts as the heartbeat of your universe, pumping fresh energy into your decaying waveform, keeping you extant.

You are waiting. You don’t know what waiting is, but it is your entire state of being.

Then, the floor of the chamber begins to tilt. Not physically, but vibrationally.

A deeper, heavier resonance approaches. It is so profoundly low that it threatens to unravel the coherence of your own frequency. The entrance to the chamber far below, a gaping maw in the stone, is obscured. Something colossal is entering.

It takes hours for the entity to fully get inside. You feel the agonizingly slow scrape of ceramic plating against the stone doorframe—a screech that would shatter human eardrums but to you feels like a rough, tectonic embrace. The air pressure in the chamber rises sharply as the massive volume of the Architect displaces it.

The giant settles. The chamber becomes truly dark as its bulk blocks the ambient twilight from outside. You feel the immense heat radiating from its carapace, a localized furnace entering the kiln.

It has come to speak.

The great geo-mechanical being presses its flank against the very wall you are currently bouncing off of. You feel the wall stiffen, tensing for the transmission.

Suddenly, the Architect releases its message. A seismic wave of unimaginable power slams into the stone structure. The building is no longer just a shell; it is a megaphone, and the Architect is shouting to its kin across the obsidian plazas miles away.

The sound is absolute. It is a physical blow.

Your tiny, ancient frequency is instantly overwhelmed. You are not destroyed; you are absorbed. You are swept up into the massive, rushing torrent of the new vibration. You lose your individual shape, your history as a forgotten echo.

You become part of the broadcast. You are fired out through the walls, traveling through the bedrock of the basin floor at terrifying speed, becoming a syllable in a slow-motion geological sentence that says: *The vents run hot in Sector Seven. The copper harvest is brittle.*
