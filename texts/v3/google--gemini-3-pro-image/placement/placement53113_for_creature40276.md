You find yourself suspended three hundred meters above a floor you will never see.

You are human, though perhaps barely recognizable to the ancestors who once walked on mineral earth. Your skin is dyed a deep, mottled mahogany, stained permanently by the tannin-rich juices of the flora and tanned to leather by the ruthless, violet-tinged sun. You are lean, built of corded muscle and efficient movement, your body a tool adapted for clinging, climbing, and making itself small.

**Where You Are:**

You are currently crouched on a "bridge"—a massive, arterial root of a skyscraper-sized mother-plant that has grown horizontally across a kilometer-wide chasm to fuse with another neighbor. This root is thicker than any roadway of the ancient world, a rugged, undulating landscape of its own.

The surface beneath your splayed, calloused feet is deceptively solid. It looks like weathered gray stone, but it is calcified wood, slick with a permanent sheen of warm resin. If you shift your weight too quickly, you feel the disturbing, spongey *give* of kilometers of compressed mulch layers beneath the hardened crust. One misstep on a patch of deceptive moss could see you plunging through a rotten section into the hollow, lightless labyrinth within the root itself, where the giant segmented millipedes bore endlessly.

The air is thin, forcing your chest to heave, yet it is so saturated with moisture that every breath feels like inhaling warm soup. Sweat slicks your body, immediately mingling with the condensation dripping from the canopy above.

But the defining element of your reality is the noise. It is not just sound; it is a physical pressure against your eardrums, a vibration rattling your teeth. The background hum is the hydraulic whine of trillions of needle-jawed mites scraping the leaves around you. Overlaying that is the thrumming drone of locust-sized insects cruising the open air spaces, a sound like a fleet of bombers that never lands. Every few minutes, a piercing, guttural shriek from a hidden six-legged simian echoes through the dense green, causing the hair on your arms to stand up.

**Who You Are and What You Are Doing:**

You are a harvester for your tribe, the "Hollow-Dwellers." You are currently engaged in the delicate, deadly work that keeps your people alive.

Strapped across your back is a basket woven from living, pliable vines that grip their contents tighter the more they are jostled. Inside are your tools: a jagged knife crafted from the ultra-dense thigh bone of an arboreal herbivore, and several gourds made from dried pitcher-plants.

You are kneeling before a thick, pulsating vine that has colonized the root bridge. It is a "Bleeder Vine." You can actually see the toxic, milky sap pumping beneath its translucent green skin in rhythmic spurts.

Your task is to carefully slice the vine without severing it completely—a full sever would cause the vine to thrash violently in a rapid-growth spasm that could knock you off the bridge. You need just enough of a cut to bleed the sap into your gourd. This sap is death in liquid form; later, back in the safety of your tribe’s woven pod-village inside a dead trunk, your elders will boil this down into the paste used to coat blowdarts. It is the only thing capable of paralyzing the armored beasts of the Crust long enough for your bone-spears to find a gap.

You work with agonizing slowness. You communicate with your hunting partner—who is watching your back twenty meters away, barely visible in the steam—only through subtle shifts in posture and sharp, quiet clicks of your tongue. To speak is to die. To make unnecessary noise is to ring a dinner bell for the things that hunt by vibration.

Your existence is a tightrope walk. You are a master chemist of a hostile botanical world, a silent acrobat on structures that groan and shift under their own weight, a minuscule speck of animal intelligence trying to steal a living from a titanic, indifferent photosynthetic machine that wants to compost you.

Right now, your knife tip just breaks the skin of the Bleeder Vine. The thick white sap wells up. You hold your breath, your entire world narrowed down to the slow trickle of poison into a gourd, while the deafening roar of the Verdant Crust screams on around you.
