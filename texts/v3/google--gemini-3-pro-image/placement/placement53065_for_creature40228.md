The cold is not something outside of me; it is my marrow. It is the baseline existence against which all movement is measured.

I am clinging to the underside of a weeping archway, fifty feet above the churning black lagoons of the floor. Gravity is a persistent drag, but my grip is absolute. I possess sixteen jointed limbs, chitinous and flat to the stone, each tipped with a cluster of stiff, hooked bristles that find purchase in the microscopic pores of the slimy granite. I am low-slung, spread wide like a splat of dark mortar, flexible enough to conform to the rounded, eroded edges of the cyclopean blocks.

My carapace is not merely shell; it is a shard of the Cloaca itself. Over cycles beyond counting, the mineral-rich drip has calcified upon my back, building layer upon laminar layer of grey-black deposit, slick with the ubiquitous slime mold. I am heavy with the weight of the architecture, a mobile cornice waiting patiently in the upper gloom.

Like everything that survives here, I have no eyes. They would be liabilities, soft points of entry for the freezing mist and sharp grit. Instead, my world is mapped through an agonizingly acute tactile sense.

The air is thick with vibrating moisture, a nebulizer fog that coats my antennae in seconds. I constantly wipe them clean with my forelimbs, tasting the aerosolized decay. The roar of the water—the endless crashing, splashing, gurgling white noise—is a physical pressure against my shell. To hear anything else requires a trance-like focus, a filtering of the chaos.

But I can feel the architecture humming. My splayed legs register the deep, resonant thrum of the millions of gallons of water moving through the stone guts of the world. I feel the micro-tremors of a platter-crustacean clattering over debris far below. I feel the sharp, staccato *click-hiss* of another of my kind—a competitor—skittering along a parallel fissure in the wall twenty yards away.

I am a gleaner of the high crevices. The great leviathans down in the sump get the bulk of the fallen feast, but I get the delicacies caught in the teeth of the upper works.

Hunger is a dull ache that drives me forward. I move in rapid, fluid spurts, flowing over the wet stone like mercury. My sensitive palps taste the water sheeting down the wall, seeking the chemical signature of trapped organic matter.

There—wedged behind a rusted, useless iron chain link that swings hypnotically in the draft—is a prize. A bloated, translucent ribbon-eel, washed down from above and battered to death against the sluice gates. It is tangled in a patch of pulsating filter-feeders.

I maintain my grip with my rear legs and pivot my upper body free of the wall. My mouthparts, a complex arrangement of serrated mandibles and grinding palps, begin to work on the carrion. The meat is cold, sodden, and tastes of ancient iron and rotten silt. It is glorious fuel.

Suddenly, the vibration changes.

It is not a sound over the roar, but a displacement beneath it. A low-frequency booming that travels up through the mortar and into my very joints. The water sheeting over my carapace stutters in its flow.

Below, in the knee-deep black lagoon, a hillock of debris is rising. The water cascades off a back that is twenty feet across, a rugged expanse of living stone shedding torrents of filth. A Leviathan is shifting.

I freeze. I press myself flat, retracting my antennae until I am nothing more than a blister on the granite block. The massive, blind head of the beast breaks the surface, swinging slowly like a dredging crane. Its lateral lines are screaming with sensory input, analyzing the acoustic shadows of the cavern.

It is not hunting me. I am too small, too calcified; I taste too much like the wall itself to be worth the energy expenditure of an upward strike. The beast is merely repositioning, its massive, crystalline grinding plates clacking together once with a sound like a dropping portcullis. It settles again, perhaps waiting for the unseen world above to flush down something substantial.

I remain motionless long after the behemoth has stilled. In this endless, freezing gut, patience is the only virtue that matters. The water drips on my stone back, adding its infinitesimal weight to my burden. Eventually, the hunger returns, and I unclasp my legs, flowing upward into a deep, dark fissure where the mortar has rotted away, seeking the next cold morsel in the roaring dark.
