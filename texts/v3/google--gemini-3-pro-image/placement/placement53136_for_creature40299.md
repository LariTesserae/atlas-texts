This is a world where sight is a secondary, almost vestigial sense. To understand where I am, you must first understand the crushing weight of the water, a medium so thick it feels less like fluid and more like a flowing, freezing gelatin. And then, you must understand The Sound.

It is not a noise you hear with ears. It is a relentless, planetary vibration that rattles the very marrow of existence down here. It is the groan of tectonic plates grinding together miles beneath us, amplified by the ocean and conducted through the rigid, calcified skeleton of the sprawling, dead metropolis.

I am located at a nexus point of this sound.

I am clinging to the northern flank of an entity I know only as The Resonant Mountain. The Resonant Mountain is an Anchorite of immense proportions, a creature so vast that its body forms a new topography upon the ancient, ossified lattice of the city. To the naked eye—if any eye here were foolish enough to rely on sight—The Mountain would appear just as the description stated: a colossal, craggy tumor of iron-hard cellulose and rock, indistinguishable from the soaring, dead arches surrounding it, save for a slightly brighter slime of sickly-green bioluminescence.

But I do not see The Mountain. I feel it.

I am a **Lithic Symbiont**, a creature perhaps the size of a human torso, flattened and segmented like a prehistoric trilobite wrought from dense, basaltic cartilage. I have sixteen hooked limbs designed for one purpose: absolute adhesion. I am fused—temporarily, in the scale of centuries—to the junction where The Resonant Mountain’s biologically generated shell meets the dead architecture of the city.

This seam is my universe.

My role here is one of maintenance and sustenance. The constant vibration—the lifeblood of the Anchorite—creates micro-fractures and mineral buildup at this crucial interface. If left unchecked, the calcification would deaden the transmission of the tectonic thrum, cutting The Mountain off from the planet's heartbeat.

So, I graze. My mouthparts are rasps of diamond-hard chitin. Slowly, meticulously, I scrape away the excess mineral accretions along the seam. I consume the stone to keep the connection pure. I am a living conductor, ensuring the fidelity of the vibration passing from the dead city into the living giant. In return, I absorb the waste heat generated by The Mountain's immense, slow metabolism, a faint warmth that keeps my own dense fluids from freezing solid in the abyssal cold.

My perception of time is defined by the rhythms of The Mountain. For decades, nothing changes but the pitch of the tectonic groan. I graze, I adhere, I feel the crushing dark.

Then, the Great Shift occurs.

It begins not as movement, but as a change in pressure so profound it feels like the ocean itself is collapsing. The Resonant Mountain is feeding.

I feel the massive internal musculature—dense cables of purple flesh thick as redwoods beneath layers of rock-shell—begin to contract. It is a geological slowness. The vibration changes from a steady thrum to a chaotic, shuddering roar as the Anchorite’s armored maw, located miles "below" my position, begins to fissure open.

The water around me grows turbulent, sucked downward in a viscous torrent. I clamp down with all sixteen limbs, embedding my hooks into the calcified hide. The current rushing past me is filled with the panicked vibrations of lesser creatures—the gelatinous clouds, the long, dumb eels—being pulled toward the unfolding sensory nets.

I remain anchored in the churning dark, riding out the storm of the giant’s hunger. I feel the massive nets deploy through the shuddering of the shell beneath me, a sensation like distant thunder rolling through the ground. I wait for the slow retraction, the sealing of the fissure, and the return of the steady, monotonous groan.

I am a tiny, unseen speck on the back of a sleeping god, tending the garden of its senses, forever vibrating in the cold, crushing dark.
