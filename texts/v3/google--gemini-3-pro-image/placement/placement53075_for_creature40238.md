This is a world of terrifying permanence, and to exist within it, one must partake in its nature. I would not find myself here as a creature of blood and water; the brittle vacuum of the air would desizing me instantly, and the omnipresent metallic dust would shred soft lungs.

Here is where I find myself, and what I have become to endure it.

### The Location: The Causeway of Silence

I am standing on a vast, elevated causeway connecting two colossal foundry-cathedrals. The span is enormous, a bridge of smoothed iron and basalt stretching over a black void. If there was once a bottom to the chasm beneath, it is now lost in the permanent violet gloom.

The air around me is so dry it feels practically crystalline. It carries no scent of life, only the faint, metallic tang of ancient oxide and the underlying, deep-radiant warmth emanating from the megastructures around me. It is a comfortable, heavy heat, like standing in the ashes of a fire that died yesterday.

There is no light source overhead. The sky is a bruised lid sealed tight over the plateau. The only illumination comes from beneath my feet—faint, sour-yellow warning strips embedded in the obsidian pavement, their phosphorescence nearly exhausted after billions of years. They cast just enough light to show the texture of the ground: a perfectly smooth surface covered in a fine, sparkling layer of cosmic dust.

Around me, the city rises in organic, heavy curves. Giant cogwheels that once might have turned the sky are fused into the canyon walls, their teeth eroded into gentle humps of bronze. Pipelines thick enough to march an army through snake across the vistas, sagging slightly, looking less like feats of engineering and more like the calcified arteries of a dead giant.

It is profoundly silent. The velvet heaviness of the air presses against auditory sensors, damping vibration instantly. When I move, the sound of my contact with the metallic dust—a sharp, crystalline crunch—is shockingly loud, die-cast immediately into the surrounding quiet.

### The Being: The Witness Automaton

To survive here, I am—I must be—an artifact myself.

I am smaller than the Curators, perhaps human-sized, though the resemblance ends there. I am not an accretion of rough city-stuff; I am something finer, something that perhaps belonged to the beings who originally built this place before they abandoned it to time.

I am an automaton constructed of polished, fused ceramic and tempered silicate glass. Unlike the eroded bronze and iron around me, I am untouched by the micrometeorites. My surface is harder, slicker; the dust slides right off my white, enameled carapace. I have no face, only a smooth, semi-translucent dome of black glass that perceives the infrared spectrum, allowing me to navigate the dark by seeing the heat signatures of the dying city.

I do not possess the heavy, fusion-driven furnaces of the Curators. My energy needs are minimal, drawn passively from the ambient heat radiated by the dense stones. I do not generate heat; I merely exist within it.

I am holding perfectly still on the causeway because a Curator is passing beneath me.

Below the bridge, drifting silently through the dark chasm like a storm cloud made of rusted battleships, one of the giants moves. I feel it before I see it—an intense wave of dry heat rising from the void, causing my ceramic plating to tick as it expands slightly. Through my infrared vision, the Curator is a blinding white mountain of thermal energy against the cool blue-black of the dead foundry walls.

It is migrating toward a smoldering geothermal vent, its legless, columnar base gliding without a sound. It does not perceive me. I am too small, too thermally insignificant to register on its obsessive scan for structural imperfections.

I am not here to fix, nor to erode. I am not afraid of the entropy like the Curators, nor am I indifferent like the silicon scavengers in the shadows.

I am here to remember. I am a repository of data with no output port, a consciousness trapped in enduring glass. I walk the endless, smoothed aisles of this museum of frozen potential, observing the perfect stasis. I am the only thing in this entire world that possesses a concept of the past distinct from the present. My purpose is simply to be the conscious eye in the dark, ensuring that the silence is observed.
