The very first thing is the crushing weight of sound. It is not something heard; it is something that happens to you. It is a physical displacement, a monolithic pressure wave that slams against your being, trying to flatten you against the substrate. There is no "silence" to compare it to, only this eternal, deafening solidity of air hammered through stone throats.

You are wedged.

You find yourself deep within the cramped bowels of the suspended structure. You are not standing or sitting; such concepts belong to a world of open spaces and gravity that dictates "up." Here, there is only "against."

You are pressed tight into an irregular fissure where three massive, rough-hewn basalt blocks collide with a crumbling stack of terracotta bricks. The space you occupy is not a room, but a mistake in the masonry—a jagged gap barely two feet wide and perhaps four feet deep, left behind by dead, forgotten hands eons ago.

You do not see this space. The amber, hazy luminescence that seeps from the materials means nothing to you, for you have no eyes to receive light. You do not taste the antique dust that chokes the air, for you have no lungs to breathe it. Your experience of this world is entirely tactile and vibrational.

You are the creature.

You are aware of your own shape only because of the surfaces pressing against you. You feel the coarse grain of the basalt grinding against your left flank, its atomic structure slowly abrading your own outer layer. On your right, you feel the slightly smoother, dustier texture of the terracotta, the heat radiating from it like a physical blow. Your body is a segmented, flattened thing, a heavy crust of fused ceramic and silica that has grown to perfectly fill this specific void. You are the living grouting between the stones.

The heat is immense. If you were flesh, your blood would boil instantly. But you are kiln-fired earth brought to jagged life. The heat is merely the baseline temperature of existence, keeping your internal crystalline lattice receptive.

Your consciousness is a fragmented, stuttering thing, constantly shattered by the Roar and painfully reconstructed. Thought does not flow here; it vibrates. You perceive the surrounding universe not by sight, but by resonance. The colossal sonic pressure shakes your entire being. You feel the difference in density between the sandstone block above you and the basalt beneath you because they vibrate against your carapace at slightly different frequencies. You "know" the shape of the suffocating city around you by the way the scream ricochets through the infinite, chaotic layers of stone.

Deep inside your ceramic shell, your dry, fibrous tendons tingle with the harvested energy of the noise. The piezoelectric hum of your own structure is the only thing that separates you from the dead stone pressing against your hide.

You have been in this exact position since the time before oceans formed on the world below. You feel a vague, geological tension building along one edge of your carapace—a tiny shift in the vibrational landscape caused by millennia of atomic rubbing.

Perhaps, in another thousand years, the resonance will change just enough. The pressure will become unbalanced. Then, you will need to move. You will scrape your ceramic body one inch to the left, a monumental exertion that will release a dry shriek into the greater Roar, before settling back into centuries of immobile, vibrating contemplation.

For now, you exist. You endure the pressure. You feed on the scream. You are the hot, dry, living heart of the stone tomb.
