The aggressive white light is a physical weight, a pressure against the thick, polarized quartz growths that shield my optical nerves. To open my true eyes here would be to go instantly blind; the world is too bright, too sharp, denying all comfortable shadows.

Instead, I see the thermal ghost of the rust-canyon.

I am clung to the underside of a massive I-beam, fifty levels down from an arbitrary "top" and a thousand levels above the abyssal haze. My long, multi-jointed limbs are folded tight against my torso, claws hooked securely into the diamond-mesh grating. The cold here is a living thing that bites at the pale, cured-rawhide texture of my skin, but I am coated in the perpetual, oily sweat of the machine, which insulates me slightly.

Through my quartz lenses, the world is a deep, freezing blue, traced with veins of angry red where the dormant power conduits slumber, holding heat despite the chill. I see the faint, pulsing thermal signatures of wire-carapace beetles scuttling along a nearby conduit.

I am waiting for gravity to decide what it is doing.

A moment ago, "down" was toward the distant haze. Now, a subtle shift in the air pressure and a strange, nauseating tug in my inner ear suggests that "down" has rotated ninety degrees to my left, toward a sheer cliff face of stacked refineries. If I let go now, I would fall sideways endlessly.

So, I wait. I listen to the silence. *Plink... plink...* condensation hitting a resonant plate somewhere below. A low-frequency thrum vibrates through my claws into my skeletal structure—the heartbeat of the Gestalt, sleeping its vast, decentralized sleep.

I am hungry, but not for food in a biological sense. My armor, cobbled together from curved bits of corrugated plating and woven glass-lichen strands, scrapes quietly against the beam as I slowly unhook one elongated arm. I reach out, testing the air like a mantis. Gravity seems to settle back to its previous orientation.

I begin my slow, agonizingly deliberate crawl across the inverted landscape.

My destination is a spatial bruise I’ve been tracking for three sleep-cycles. It's a concrete buttress jutting out from a wall of silent factories. In my thermal vision, it glows with a sickening, impossible cold—a cold deeper than the surrounding air, a pocket where thermodynamics has thrown up its hands.

I reach the buttress. It is weeping. Heavy, shivering droplets of mercury ooze from the pores of the concrete. They do not fall. They pool on the vertical surface and then slide slowly *upwards*, defying the local gravity I am currently obeying.

With reverence, I unhook a glass vial from my scavenged belt. I hold it against the concrete face, letting the upward-flowing metal slide into the container. It is heavy, dense with the beautiful wrongness of this place.

When the vial is full, I cap it. I locate the intake port scarred into the flesh of my left flank, just beneath my plating, and slot the vial home.

The sensation is immediate. It is not digestion; it is an assimilation of error. The impossible physics of the upward-flowing mercury screams into my nervous system. My thermal vision flares whiteout-bright for a second, and I feel a rush of dizzying energy—the raw, chaotic byproduct of reality breaking down. It is intoxicating. It anchors me to this unstable world.

As the rush subsides, a sound echoes through the chasm, louder than the usual drips and clicks.

A massive, hydraulic *sigh*.

The metal structure around me groans. The vibration in my claws shifts from a mechanical hum to something vaguely, terrifyingly sentient. The Gestalt is dreaming.

I freeze, pressing my long body flat against the freezing metal, making myself as insignificant as possible. A polarized-film bird nearby stops its synthesized chiming. We are all lice on the skin of a sleeping god, praying it does not twitch in its slumber and remake the laws of physics where we happen to be standing. I stay motionless, listening to the vast mind settle back into the damp wires, letting the aggressive light beat down on my armored back.
