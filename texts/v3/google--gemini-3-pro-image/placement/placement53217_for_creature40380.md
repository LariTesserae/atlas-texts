The heat is not a thing that presses down from above; it is a pervasive resonance, humming in the ceramic core of my own joints.

I am embedded deep within the mid-layers of the superstructure, perhaps thirty feet above the unseen, scorching crust of the black earth. Here, the definition of "space" is merely a slightly wider absence of solid matter. I am in a junction point where four great, spiraling tubes of calcified amethyst converge and twist upward, forming a structural knot in the continent-spanning lattice.

It is incredibly tight. The smooth-worn walls of the tunnels press against my dorsal plates and brush my multi-jointed legs on all sides. The air here is thick, still, and tastes intensely of pulverized flint and the acrid, metallic tang of volatile gases seeping up from the Conduits below.

The silence is absolute, yet it is textured by a billion minute events. It is a granular silence. I hear the dry, high-pitched *tisk-tisk-tisk* of a Mason swarm moving in frantic unison three layers down, patching a stress fracture. I feel, rather than hear, the deep, grinding vibration transmitted through the floorplates as a massive Recycler, somewhere nearby, chews through a defunct section of burnt-umber tubing.

I am resting now, integrated. My matte, dust-coated shell—a mottled camouflage of deep ochre and faded violet—is pressed flush against a curved wall of identical material. To any entity passing by, I am merely an unnoticed protrusion of the reef itself. I am waiting.

I am not one of the frenetic Masons, nor a passive Harvester sipping the dusty wind above. I am larger than the Masons, broader across the thorax, built for a heavier kind of creation. I am a specialized variant, an extruder of primary structures.

The imperative begins as a pressure in my lower abdomen, a signaling overload in my simple neural lattice. It is time.

With a sequence of dry, sharp clicks that merge perfectly into the ambient soundscape, I detach from the wall. My ceramic ball-joints rotate smoothly without lubrication. I move with a stiff, deliberate mechanical gait up the spiraling incline of the amethyst tube.

I reach the terminus—a jagged edge where the growth flow stopped cycles ago. This is my workspace.

I anchor my rear six legs into the textured pits of the existing structure. My anterior segment rears up. From specialized spinnerets beneath my armored jaw, I begin to excrete. It is not the thin mortar of the Masons; it is a thick, semi-molten composite of re-processed minerals, retrieved from the dung of Recyclers and heated by my own internal biology.

The material leaves my body intensely hot, glowing a dull, frictional red for a fraction of a second before the arid atmosphere sucks the heat away. It sets instantly, fusing with the ancient layers beneath it.

I do not think about the shape I am making. The blueprint of the spiraling tower is etched into my genetic code. I step forward, extrude, step forward, extrude. I am weaving stone from my own body, extending the city upward by inches.

I work until the pressure in my abdomen ceases, until my supply of raw mineral paste is exhausted. The new section of tube is complete—a seamless addition, colored the pale violet of fresh growth, already hardening into the consistency of tempered rock.

My task finished for this cycle, I retreat slowly down the new incline. I find a recessed niche in the intricate tessellation of the wall I just helped expand. I fold my armored legs beneath me. My body temperature drops slightly as my internal furnaces damp down.

I become still. I become part of the wall again. The hushed, clicking machine of the reef continues around me, and I wait for the pressure to return, to build again in the endless, dry dark.
