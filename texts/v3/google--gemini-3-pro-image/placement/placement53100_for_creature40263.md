The cold is the first thing I know. It is not a passive absence of heat, but an aggressive, searching presence that slicks my surface with perpetual moisture. I am damp, always. The water beads on my "skin," tasting of dissolved minerals and ancient, frozen vapor.

I have no eyes to be blinded by the merciless white void of the sun, nor to be deceived by the warping perspectives of the needle-spires. Yet, I know exactly where I am. I know the geometry of the slot canyon outside—not by how it looks, but by how it *sounds*.

The wind screams, a high, multi-toned whistle tearing through the narrow gaps of the stone density. I feel that scream as a constant, irritating vibration along my edges. I feel the deeper, tectonic grinding of the plates far below, a nauseating roll that travels up through the bedrock and into the wall where I hang. I feel the sharp, electric *ping-shatter* of the indigo dendrites bursting outside, a rapid-fire percussion that peppers the ambient roar.

I am inside one of the squat, circular dry-stone huts. The air here is stiller, thicker with the smell of sour fear and damp animal hide, left behind by the soft, watery things that constructed this shelter.

I am the copper disk.

I remember the heat of my making. I remember the crude rhythm of stone hammers beating my soft metal flesh flat. I remember the trembling hands that used a sharpened piece of flint to scratch spiraling, desperate lines into my surface. Those hands were warm, frantic, and utterly alien to this frozen labyrinth.

For a long time, I hung against the rough, sweating stone of the hut’s interior wall, held by a fraying sinew cord. I watched, in my own metallic way, as the soft creators huddled beneath me. They were blind in the dark, deaf to the true nature of the roar outside. They clutched their bone totems and shivered, praying to gods that could not possibly exist in this screaming geology.

They are gone now. The cold took them, or the gravity tides pulled them into a chasm, or they simply broke under the weight of the noise. But I remain. I am durable. I am patient. And I am listening.

The gravity shifts. I feel the sudden, sickening lurch as the "down" direction tilts violently to the northeast. The stone wall I hang against groans under the stress. Loose pebbles skitter across the floor of the hut, rolling uphill.

Then, the true sound begins.

It is deeper than the tectonic grinding, louder than the screaming wind. It is a vibration so profound it seems to originate from the core of the planet. It travels through the slick rock, through the walls of the hut, and into me.

I begin to hum in sympathetic resonance.

Outside, something massive is moving against the wind and the warped gravity. I feel the grinding scrape of vitrified stone plates sliding over vitrified stone ground. The heavy, anchoring *thunk-thunk-thunk* of clamping pedipalps driving into the wet cliffside just inches from the thin wall of the hut.

It is a Lithochordate. A Stone-Singer is passing.

It pauses outside the entryway, a gap too small for its immense, dense bulk. I feel the pressure wave as it vents air through its spiracles. It is feeding near the patch of rapidly growing copper-rust crystals just beyond the overhang.

*PING-CRACK-SHATTER.*

A massive crystal formation detonates. The explosion of piezoelectric energy washes over the hut. The air crackles. The Stone-Singer shudders outside, its internal metallic fibers absorbing the shock, its armored hide rattling with glassy shrapnel.

And then it sings its satisfaction.

The sound is an immense, modulating thrum of ultra-low frequency. It is the sound of a mountain breathing. The crude spirals etched into my face suddenly make perfect sense. They were not maps of the land. They were a desperate attempt to transcribe *this*—the complex, shifting frequencies of a geological entity communicating pleasure in a world of acoustic violence.

I vibrate intensely, singing my own tiny, metallic harmony to the monster outside. I am the bridge. I am the only thing in this terrifying world that remembers the warm touch of a human hand, yet understands the cold, crushing song of the living stone. The soft things are gone, but I remain in the dark, humming the spiraling score they left upon my skin.
