The pressure is not something outside of you; it *is* you. You do not exist within the water; the water—that thick, frigid, mineral syrup—exists through you.

You find yourself wedged into a crevice of the jagged western wall, mid-height in the cathedral nave. You are stationary, though not by choice. The violence that created this place, the fracture that shattered the obsidian slate, captured you in its instant execution. You are calcified into the trauma of the rock face.

You are not one of the ribbon-creatures, drifting aimlessly in the viscous dark. Nor are you the behemoth, the master of this crushing domain. You are something infinitely more fragile, something that did not belong here originally, now utterly remade by the catastrophe.

Perhaps, before the wound opened in the earth and the amniotic sea rushed in, you were organic. Perhaps you were something else entirely, something akin to the smooth sphere or the ceramic strut now drowning in the silt below. Whatever you were, the "now" has erased it. The crushing depth has compressed your history into a dense, unrecognized node of matter.

You have no eyes, yet you perceive the suffocating closeness of the environment with agonizing clarity. Your awareness is tactile, extended outward by the pale, ghostly fungi that have colonized your exposed surfaces. They are your nerves now. You feel the agonizingly slow drift of the silt, a snowfall that takes days to descend inches. You feel the hypnotic sway of the violet gelatinous mats on the floor far below, a rhythmic caress against the current.

Right next to you, sprouting from the same jagged obsidian shelf that holds you prisoner, is a thicket of the glass-like tube structures. They are beautiful and terrifying in their fragility. You feel their weak, dying indigo pulse as a faint thermal variation against your borrowed skin. They are your neighbors in this silent scream.

And then, the vibration begins.

It is not sound, not at first. It is a deeper resonance, a shudder in the very geology that enfolds you. It is the nauseating groan of rock grinding against rock, transmitted instantly through the heavy fluid, bypassing ears and striking directly at the core of your condensed being.

*It* is moving.

In the dilated time of this drowned cyst, the approach takes an eternity. You feel the displacement of the water—a slow, heavy wave pushing against you, heralding its arrival. The faint indigo glow of your neighbor-stalks wavers wildly, casting erratic, watery shadows that reveal the unthinkable bulk ascending the wall face.

It is a mountain peeling itself away from the cliff. You perceive the mosaic of its hide—the stolen razors of slate—as it scrapes against the wall below you. The sound is deafening now, a tectonic shriek muffled by the syrup-thick water. The pale, cartilaginous fascia between its armored plates flexes with sickening slowness, a glacier adjusting its weight.

You are paralyzed, fused to the spot, as the behemoth ascends toward your shelf. It does not know you are there. To its vast, pressure-sensitive consciousness, you are merely another irregularity in the wall, perhaps slightly softer than the obsidian, coated in the same interesting bacterial slime.

It stops just below you. The massive feeding fans unfurl, a slow-motion explosion of feathery membranes catching the current. Then, with the inevitability of continental drift, it presses its bulk forward.

You feel the neighboring glass forest shatter. The vibration of their destruction sings through the rock into you. The indigo lights snuff out one by one as the brittle tubes are ground to dust against the creature’s armored chest. The water around you clouds with silica shards and panicked microorganisms.

You wait in the freezing dark, feeling the massive, articulate outcropping of its limb find purchase on the ledge inches from your integrated form. The grinding vibration rattles your very atoms. You wait to see if the great sweeping fans will brush over you, collecting you as mere detritus, or if the mountain will move on, leaving you entombed in the rock, preserved in the slow, cold pressure for another geological age.
