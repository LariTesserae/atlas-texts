This is not a world for eyes. Sight is a secondary sense here, baffled by constant fog, steam plumes, and the dense, light-absorbing Brunswick green of the canopy. This world is understood through temperature, pressure, and vibration.

I am not on the rim. I am deep inside the labyrinth, clinging to the precarious mid-stratum of a basalt terrace, halfway down into the churning gut of the caldera.

I am not animal, though I possess a slow, reactive consciousness. Neither am I purely plant, though I photosynthesize the dim, ultraviolet-heavy light that filters through the steam.

I am **The Sward**.

I am a contiguous, semi-sentient mat of extremophile liverworts, fungal hyphae, and bacterial colonies that grows three feet thick over the slick rock. I am the sponge that covers the machinery. I am the interface between the scalding earth and the suffocating air.

My existence is one of perpetual, turgid saturation. Every cell in my vast, spread-out body is swollen with warm, mineral-heavy water. I feel the incessant drip-drip-drip from the giant ferns above as a constant, rhythmic percussion on my outer skin. I feel the slow percolation of hot runoff moving through my lower layers, feeding me iron, sulfur, and dissolved silica.

I do not hear the roar of the vents in the human sense. I *am* the roar. I am anchored to the vibrating rock. The low-frequency thrum of the geothermal engines passes directly into my rhizoids, shaking my entire mass in a ceaseless, comforting massage. When the great sinter chimneys near me exhale, I don't just feel the heat; the sudden spike in thermal energy triggers a frantic, hydraulic pumping within my tissues as I greedily absorb the steam, growing visibly in the hothouse rush.

I am the ground upon which this heavy world treads.

I feel the approach of a Litho-Grazer long before it arrives. It registers as a localized earthquake, a slow, grinding pressure sequence: *grip, suction, release, heavy step.* The vibration increases until the massive, iron-shelled creature is upon me. Its dozens of stump-legs compress my spongy form, squeezing hot water out of me in jets. It does not eat me, but it scrapes the bacterial slime from the rocks I cling to. The wet rasp of its shovel-mouth is an intense, abrasive sensation against my flank. It passes, and I slowly re-inflate, the bruised tissues already knitting back together in the fierce heat.

I harbor others. Deep within my wettest, coolest folds, near the flowing mud channels, I feel a large, cold absence of heat. A Silt-Stalker has burrowed into me, using my water-logged mass as insulation against the searing air while it waits. I feel the incredibly slow, heavy thud of its heart against my deepest fibers. We exist in a passive truce; I hide it, and its waste provides concentrated nitrates for my fungal networks.

Sometimes, a shadow passes over me that is colder than the air. An Ash-Wing, gliding silently down from the heights to cling to the heated rock face just above my station. Its leathery talons pierce my upper crust for purchase. I feel it shivering as it presses its thin body against the stone, sucking the warmth that radiates through me.

I have no desire to move. Movement is wasted energy in this oppressive gravity. I am ancient, wet, and infinitely patient. I am the living carpet of the geothermal machine, absorbing the violence of the earth and turning it into thick, dark green silence. My purpose is simply to hold the water, endure the heat, and grow heavy.
