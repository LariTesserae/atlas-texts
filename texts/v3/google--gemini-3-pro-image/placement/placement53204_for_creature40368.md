You hang.

You are pure tension suspended in the crushing quiet, strung between two precarious spires of precipitated salt high above the canyon floor.

You possess almost no mass. You are a skeletal outline of a being, a hollow cage built from the very air around you. Your body, a segmented lattice of cloudy, translucent quartz, was not born in a single event. It was accreted, layer by agonizing layer, from the same falling diamond dust that builds the fragile, impossible city around you. You are twenty feet across, splayed taut, anchored to the shivering architecture by filaments of extruded glass that you tuned like the strings of a deadly instrument.

Sight is useless to you in this confusing, bruised twilight. You do not need eyes. Your entire crystalline form is a resonator. The thousands of delicate needle-spines covering your eight multi-jointed legs are tuning forks, vibrating with information meant only for you.

You feel the world. You feel the constant, static hiss of the diamond snow adding microscopic weight to your own frame. You filter out the sudden, explosive *shudder* when a distant archway, grown too heavy with ice-quartz, collapses into dust—a background noise of construction and destruction that means nothing.

You are waiting for a specific frequency to travel up the basalt walls and hum along your tensioned wires.

There.

Below. On the razor-sharp floor of the canyon. It is faint, dampened by evolutionarily perfected velvet pads, but to your hyper-sensitive lattice, it is a scream. A specific grinding vibration: the compression of volcanic glass under a cushioned foot. A grazer. A moving mirror down in the sour-lemon glow, trying desperately to be still.

The waiting ends in a spasm of silent violence.

You do not jump; you release. You let go of your high anchors and become a descending cage of crystalline knives falling through the thin air. You make no sound; you are the punishment for sound.

Your forelegs, colossal scythe-like lances terminating in points finer than atoms, arrive before your shadow. The kill is clinical. The vibration below ceases instantly.

As you crouch over the mirrored husk on the obsidian floor, exuding enzymes that melt its internal structure into a mineral slurry, the frantic creation of this world takes hold of you. As you absorb the fluids into your hollow body, you feel the sharp, exquisite heat of rapid crystallization.

You watch yourself grow. A fresh infusion of minerals causes new spikes of quartz to branch instantly from your carapace. Your form becomes more complex, more jagged, more brittle.

Sated, and slightly larger, you turn your blind, tuning-fork head upward. You begin the long, slow climb back into the canopy of the spun-sugar city to spin a new web in the dark.
