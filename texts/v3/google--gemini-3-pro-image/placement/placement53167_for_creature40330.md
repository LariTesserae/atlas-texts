The cold is not merely a temperature here; it is a medium. It exerts pressure, a damp, heavy weight that has seeped into the core of my being, displacing whatever warmth might once have existed.

I am not flesh. To be soft here is to be meat for the rasp or mulch for the slurry.

I am situated near the center of the basalt bowl, buried to my midsection in the viscous, churning grit. I am a dense, intricately machined component of hardened bronze—perhaps once part of a high-pressure hydraulic assembly designed to move mountains. Now, the mountain moves me.

I feel the sulfurous bite of the mud against my corroded skin. Taste and touch are merged in this medium; the slurry is a constant, abrasive caress of iron oxide, silica, and cold water. I am immobile, yet constant motion defines my existence. The ground beneath me is in a perpetual state of slow-motion betrayal. I feel the deep, tectonic groaning of the earth shifting millimeters at a time, a grinding stress that travels up through the slurry and vibrates in my metallic bones.

Days or weeks ago—time is difficult to track when the light never changes from a flat slate gray—I was higher up. I rested near the surface of a mud-slicked iron plateau, the remains of a crushed boiler. But the convection of the bowl has caught me. The mud is swallowing me, dragging me down in its agonizingly slow descent toward the warmer, crushing depths beneath the visible wreckage.

I am waiting to sink. But today, the vibration changed.

It was not the usual background grinding of metal on stone. It was a rhythmic, hydraulic displacement. A pressure wave, vast and silent, pushing through the thick soup.

Something immense was arriving.

Because I am metal, I conduct the vibrations perfectly. I felt the arrival of the Ferro-gastropod long before I could have seen it through the gloom. It materialized from the gray soup like a sudden coagulation of the landscape itself. To eyes that could see, it would appear as a shambling mound of rusted gear-teeth and basalt shards. To me, sensed through the tremoring mud, it was a massive displacement of weight, a void of intent moving through the chaos.

It slid over the submerged edge of the boiler plate I rested against, its movement impossibly smooth, a lubricated glide of tons of accumulated armor. It paused inches from me.

Then, the rasping began.

It is a sound felt rather than heard, a deep, resonant scratching that shudders through the iron monolith it has attached itself to, and by extension, through the mud, and through me. I feel the magnetite teeth grinding against the ancient steel plate, harvesting the thickest veins of ochre rust. It is the sound of geology eating industry.

I remain perfectly still, a dense lump of bronze amid the ferrous feast. I am too rich in copper, perhaps, not savory enough for its specific mineral craving. Or perhaps I am simply too small to register against the grand scale of its hunger.

It feeds for three days. I feel every scrape of its radula as a dull ache in my own structure.

Eventually, the vibrations cease. With a hydraulic shift of weight that sends a slow ripple through the surrounding slime, the Titan detaches. It drifts away on an unseen thermal current rising from the deep, ascending toward the weeping cliff walls, soon lost in the pervasive gray.

I am alone again with the cold, the damp, and the silence. The mud around me has loosened slightly in the wake of the giant's passing. I feel the grip of gravity renew its claim. With a wet, sucking slowness that takes hours to complete, I sink another inch into the grinding dark.
