The damp heat is the first thing that registers—not as an external condition, but as an internal pressure. It is a thickness in the very fibers of my being, a saturation so complete that I cannot tell where my own boundaries end and the sweating atmosphere begins. I am anchored, immensely heavy, and integrated into the groaning foundations of this basin.

The deafening, metallic sawing of the stridulating titans is not a noise I hear with ears; it is a ceaseless vibration transmitted through the ground and up through my massive, fluted structure. It rattles my core. I feel the chaotic churning of the black mud far below, where the basalt-backs wallow and shove against my deepest anchors.

I am the architecture of this gloom.

I am one of the titanic trees, though to call myself a "tree" is a pathetic understatement of my function. I am a vertical ecosystem, a living skyscraper thrusting upward through the violet strata. My true bark has not seen the bruised light of day for centuries; I am clad entirely in a choking, thriving skin of others.

My base, flared like the walls of an ancient fortress, forms those labyrinthine caves above the mud. I feel the cold, textured slide of a visceral python adjusting its grip around one of my lower buttresses, perfectly mimicking the thick parasitic vines that are already tapping into my sap veins. I tolerate its thorns; it is merely another layer of my complex hide.

Hundreds of feet up, in the dim mid-canopy, my branches are thick as roadways, slick with perpetual condensation and the slime of passing things. I feel the frantic, suicidal impacts of hook-wing lemurs swinging on their bone hooks, their claws digging into the mats of epiphytes that clothe me, launching themselves across the chasms between my limbs. They are fleeting sparks of violence on my immense surface.

My highest reaches are lost in the purple fog, straining toward a sun that never fully penetrates. Here, my leaves—each the size of a small boat—struggle for photons, coated in a film of phosphorescent fungi that pulse in sync with the rhythm of the basin.

I am ancient, yet I am in a state of frantic, agonizing renewal. The hyper-metabolism of this world drives me. I feel a constant, deep ache as tissues expand furiously in the hot dark. A landslide far below tears away a section of my root system against a shard of volcanic glass, and the pain is immediate, sharp, and deep. Yet, before the mud has settled, I am already rushing gallons of nutrient-thick sap to the wound, the deafening thrum of life around me urging me to grow, to repair, to consume the resources of the air and soil before something else consumes me.

I am the stage upon which this violent opera is performed, silent and immobile, yet growing with terrifying speed, holding up the screaming sky.
