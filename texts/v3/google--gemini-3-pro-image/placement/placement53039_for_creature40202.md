The heat of this world is not external; it is a medium you are dissolved within. You exist in the mid-altitude thicket, suspended under miles of shimmering water and above miles of crushing haze.

You are a **Hyaline Grazer**.

You are an entity of substantial, structured jelly, roughly the size of a small rowboat, segmented into five rounded, translucent lobes. You have no rigid skeleton, no internal organs that would recognize the concept of "urgency," and no eyes. Yet, your entire being is an ocular organ. Your gelatinous flesh is a living prism, intensely sensitive to the searing, unfiltered sunlight plunging through the aquifer. You do not *see* the curtains of electric blue and vivid green light; you *feel* them as variations in exquisite, nourishing pressure on your skin.

Where you are is a choke-point of light and life. You are currently adhered to the underside of a massive, razor-edged obsidian spire that juts horizontally through the aquatic expanse. The rock is so sharp it should slice you to ribbons, but your ventral surface secretes a hyper-dense mucus that allows you to flow over the glass blades like slow-moving oil, impervious to the cut.

You are wedged between the cold, unforgiving black stone and the suffocating embrace of a giant kelp-redwood's holdfast. The plant matter here is meters thick, a slimy, yielding wall of chartreuse velvet moss and pulsing cellulose.

Your existence is defined by a hunger that moves at the pace of geology. You are the immense, slow curator of this overgrown garden.

While the ethereal Lucivora drift passively overhead, sipping radiation, and the Somniaflorae sleep deep within the root-balls gluing the world together, you perform the necessary violence of pruning. The unchecked growth of the giant fronds threatens to become so heavy it might snap the young rock spires. You prevent this.

Your "mouth" is located on your leading segment. It is not an orifice with jaws, but a hardened, crystalline disc, a biological magnifying glass. You press this disc against the thickest, tough-as-cables stalk of the kelp-redwood. You wait. You angle your translucent body to catch the shafts of intense sunlight raining down from above, focusing the beams through your own form and onto the crystalline disc.

The process takes days. The focused light slowly, meticulously burns through the super-dense plant matter, cauterizing the wound as it goes, releasing clouds of sugary, warm sap into the viscous water. You absorb this sap through pores along your underbelly. The taste is dazzlingly sweet, a rush of pure solar energy converted into glucose.

Right now, you are mid-meal. The muffled *click-shhh* of growing plants is the only sound you know. You feel the insignificant pressure wave of a Somniaflora shifting its grip somewhere deep in the moss beneath you—a movement that took it all morning to complete. You feel the water thicken slightly as the light dims for a "night" cycle, and you slow your metabolism in response, your crystalline mouth cooling against the half-severed plant stalk.

You have been chewing through this single three-foot-thick stem for a week. In another three days, it will separate, and the massive frond above, weighing tons, will slowly begin its months-long descent into the hazy abyss to eventually feed the Cyst-sponges.

You are content. The water is warm. The light is heavy. You are the slow, necessary friction between the eternal rock and the suffocating green.
