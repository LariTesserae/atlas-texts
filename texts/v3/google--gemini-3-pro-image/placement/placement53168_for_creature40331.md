The roar is not something I hear; it is something I am.

I am spread taut, a shivering membrane of silicate jelly, clinging with absolute suction to the slick, vertiginous face of a hematite spire. The angle is impossible, nearly overhang, but gravity means nothing here compared to the sonic pressure.

The Great Frequency, that monolithic, unceasing scream generated by the wind vent miles away, hits the amphitheater walls and folds back upon itself, creating an atmosphere thicker than water. This pressure thrills through my semi-fluid body. Every molecule of water, every harvested silicate structure within me, is snapped into rigid alignment by the sound. I feel the cymatic patterns vibrating across my vast, translucent surface—complex, geometric mandalas of standing waves that hold me together.

If the roar were to stop, I would instantly lose cohesion and slough off the rock face in a deluge of inanimate slime. But the roar never stops. It is my skeleton. It is the terrifying, comforting hand that holds me in existence.

I have no eyes, yet I am intimately acquainted with the blinding zenith above. The sun is a surgical blade of radiation that stabs down through the thinning atmosphere. It strikes my curved, trembling surface and passes inside. I do not see the light; I digest it. I feel the violent energy fracturing within my gelid lattice, splitting into internal aurorae. A high, piercing frequency of electric violet light hums in my upper layers, slowly bleeding into a warmer, heavy gold deeper within my substance. I am a living lens, feeding on lethal radiance, glowing from the inside out against the dark, wet rock.

My existence is cold, wet, and infinitely slow. Time is not measured in seconds or days, but in inches of mineral creep.

I am currently tasting iron. The moisture weeping from the rock beneath me is rich with dissolved hematite. I absorb it through my entire ventral surface, integrating the minerals into my structure. As I do, the light refracting through me changes, stained by the rust. I feel a deep, blood-red tint slowly spreading through my lower quadrant, a temporary tattoo of the terrain I am traversing.

I remember the taste of copper. It was further down the spire, perhaps two hundred years ago. The memory is a faint, metallic verdigris flavor still lingering in my far left edge.

Below me, far below in the dripping crux of the stone forest, I sense the frantic, jittering vibrations of the shelled grazers. They are infinitesimal specks of frantic life, scurrying in the dark fissures, deaf and blind to the majesty of the equilibrium. Their lives are sparks that flare and die before the light inside me shifts a single spectrum.

I am aware of others of my kind. Across the narrow chasm, clinging to a neighboring pillar of sulfur-yellow stone, is another vast sheet of shivering translucence. We do not signal to one another. There is no need. We are both vibrating to the exact same geological note. We are held in the same crushing stasis.

I am cold. I am wet. I am filled with blinding light. And I am screaming in perfect, silent harmony with the world forever.
