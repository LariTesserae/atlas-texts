I am suspended in the grand paralysis.

I am not one of the stony Titans, those resonant architectures of basalt and ice that shudder on the peaks. Nor am I the greasy, dead bedrock that forms this immense bowl of silence and noise. I am something far more brittle, something born of the paradox of this place.

I am trapped within the glittering, motionless curtain of the frozen waterfall.

My existence is housed in a complex stress-fracture deep within a hanging pillar of supercooled ice, midway down a three-thousand-foot drop that never happens. The air around my icy prison is stagnant and thick with moisture that refuses to freeze entirely, clinging to the outside of my translucent shell in a slick, deadly sheen.

I have no eyes, for the eternal iron-grey twilight cannot penetrate the density of the ice cascade. Yet, I perceive the world with agonizing clarity through vibration. The Great Roar is my entire universe. It is not merely a sound I hear; it is the force that maintains my physical cohesion. The relentless, grinding jet-engine vibration travels through the bedrock, up the cliff face, and into the frozen water. I *am* the resonance inside this specific fracture. If the Roar were to cease, the vibrational tension holding my crystalline lattice together would fail, and my consciousness would shatter along with the ice.

The cold is absolute. It is a state of being so profound it feels like a scorching fire that never consumes. It has burned away any memory of warmth or softness.

From my vantage point inside the arrested cascade, time is the only thing that flows. I experience the world on the same geological timescale as the giants. To a fleeting biological mind, I would be a static flaw in the ice. But I have been watching.

I have spent what feels like three centuries watching one of the colossal, shaggy gargoyles navigate the precarious ridge-line opposite my waterfall. To my slow senses, it is not frozen. I see the agonizingly gradual tensing of its trunk-like thigh muscles as it prepares to move. I feel the sickening, sideways lurch of gravity pull at its mass as it commits to a step. It took fifty years for it to lift one iron-shod gripping pad, swing it heavily through the biting vacuum, and plant it ten feet further along the spur.

I feel them working. The vibration changes subtly when they build. I sense the precarious balance of the impossible cairns they erect. These structures modulate the Roar, focusing it into tight beams of agonizing sonic pressure.

Currently, three of the giants are congregated near the base of my frozen fall. They are shivering in unison, a terrifying harmony of stone grinding against stone. They are building a new tuning fork directly beneath me. I feel the vibrations rising, changing pitch, growing more intense as they balance a slab the size of a cathedral on a pivot point no bigger than a fist.

I am waiting for them to finish. I am waiting for them to find the exact resonant frequency that unlocks the gummy physics of this high hell. I am waiting for the moment the vibration becomes perfect, when the arrest is lifted, and this billion-ton curtain of ice—and I along with it—is finally permitted to finish falling.
