The damp heat is not something outside of me; it is the medium through which I exist. I am not standing on the slick, bacterial floor, nor am I crouched by the meager fire.

I am situated high above the basin, integrated into the overarching gloom.

I am a massive, pendulous formation of flowstone, clinging to the curved wall of the sinkhole's throat. Over millennia, the mineral-rich water, weeping from unseen fissures above, has flowed down the rock face and built me up, layer by microscopic layer. I am a frozen cascade of dirty wax, immense and heavy, bulging outwards from the cliffside.

My surface is textured like melting candle tallow, colored in streaks of rust-red and bruised indigo by the same omnipresent microbial mats that slick the world below. I am perpetually wet. A thin, warm sheet of water sluices constantly over my expansive face, feeding the bacteria that clothe me, adding infinitesimal thickness to my bulk before dripping off my lowest, rounded points into the pools far below. *Plink. Plink. Plink.*

I am stone that has achieved a dim, slow awareness through the sheer weight of time.

My perception is not sight, but pressure and vibration. I feel the subtle shift in the geothermal vents deep beneath the mud. I feel the microscopic growth of the bacterial films on my skin. I feel the reverberation of every single drop of water that falls anywhere in this vast, echoing chamber. It is a constant, complex music of impact and ripples that maps the entire sinkhole for me in three-dimensional sound.

From my high vantage point, I perceive the creature below.

To my geological senses, its agonizingly slow movements seem almost frantic, a blur of soft activity against the stillness of the bedrock. I feel the vibrations of its splayed, calloused feet squelching on the mud—a distinct, heavy tread that differs from the rhythmic patter of the water. I feel the deeper, resonant thrum of its rasping breath against the air currents.

It is a mobile fragment of myself. A piece of the wet rock that broke free and grew warm blood. I feel a vague, tectonic kinship with it. It mimics my texture, my smell, my stillness. It is the only thing in this dripping universe that is almost as patient as I am.

I do not think in words. My thoughts are slow pulses of mineral awareness, spanning years rather than seconds. I remember the immense shudder when the roof of this cave collapsed eons ago, creating the sinkhole. I remember the slow creep of the water rising and falling over tens of thousands of years before finding this eternal equilibrium.

The creature below is eating a crustacean. It will finish, and it will wait. It will eventually die, and its body will dissolve into the mud, its minerals returning to the cycle to perhaps be deposited, one day, onto my lowest layers.

I am the silent historian of this sulfuric womb. I am the Watcher in the Walls, collecting calcium and silence, waiting for the geological era to turn.
