The roar is not a sound. Sound implies a vibration traveling through a medium to an ear. This is different. This is the medium itself—the entire sky—physically assaulting the earth. It is a pressure that seeks to push teeth back into jaws and compress the very marrow in bone.

I exist where the violence is most concentrated.

I am not on the undulating ridges with the hunkered Skimmers, nor in the deep bowls with the slow Grazers. I am an inhabitant of the vertical. I am embedded in the windward face of a towering gneiss cliff, the precise point where the Eternal Gale, laden with shredded cloud-mist from the lower world, slams into the immovable object of the mountain.

I am not a singular being. The concept of "I" is diffuse here. I am a **Fracture-Lattice**, a communal organism that has discarded softness for silicate survival. I am a vast, decentralized network of calcified tubes and mineralized chambers laced deep into the microscopic cracks of the cliff face. I am the mortar that holds this section of the shattering mountain together.

My consciousness is the sum of fifty thousand individual nodes bonded to the rock. I am blind in the searing light. I am deaf in the roaring void. My entire universe is vibration.

I feel the mountain trembling. It is a constant, agonizing vibration, a high-frequency shudder as millions of tons of granite groan under the unrelenting pressure of the air river. This is the background hum of my existence.

But I am waiting for the deeper impacts.

I feel them now. The subtle seismic shift as a Gale-Skimmer, heavy and panicked, claws its way across the cliff face fifty feet below my lowest nodes, desperately seeking a crevice before the wind peels it away. Its terror is just a distinct vibrational pattern in the stone.

And then, the event I live for.

The pressure wave hits first. A massive bank of cloud, torn from the unseen oceans below, is being hurled at the cliff at hundreds of miles an hour. The impact is catastrophic. The cloud doesn't just hit; it is atomized against the stone face.

For less than a second, the impossible happens on the Roof of the World: there is liquid water.

In that microsecond of impact, before the water flashes into ice or is sublimated back into gas, I act. My fifty thousand nodes, recessed deep in pinprick pores on the polished cliff surface, simultaneously extrude microscopic, crystalline filaments.

It is a frantic, hydraulic ejection. My glassy tongues lash out into the roaring wetness. They are incredibly fragile, yet resilient in their brevity. They snag the supercooled moisture. More importantly, they snag what the moisture carries: the pulverized organic dust of the lower world, and the rich, frozen paste of a sky-ray that smashed against the cliff cycles ago and is only now being eroded free.

We drink the violence. The filaments retract instantly, pulling the precious, freezing slurry deep into the armored lattice just as the wind reasserts its dry, abrasive purity outside. The cliff face is once again smooth, dry, and screaming under the gale.

Inside the rock, safe behind inches of gneiss and my own secreted bio-glass, the colony digests the harvest. The energy courses through the lattice, a faint electrical impulse acknowledging the successful feed. We use the minerals to grow our tubes deeper into the fracturing rock, binding ourselves tighter to the dying mountain, forever waiting for the next split-second catastrophe that brings us life.
