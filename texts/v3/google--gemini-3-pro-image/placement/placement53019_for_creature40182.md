The Roar is not a sound. Sound requires air to vibrate, ears to receive it, a mind to interpret it as noise. What defines this world is a physical, unending assault on the bone structure. It is a perpetual, grinding earthquake that exists inside your own marrow.

I am crouched low, my center of gravity hugged tight to a slab of shivering obsidian that feels slick and freezing under my palms. The water around me is impossibly bright, a lucid, hyper-oxygenated azure illuminated by a merciless sun miles above the churning surface of this impossible high-altitude sea. The light is agonizing. My eyes, enormous and bulbous in my dense skull, are shielded by thick, polarizing membranes that slid across them the moment I emerged from the hut. Through these filters, the world is a high-contrast nightmare of deep blues and violently bright white flashes.

I am weighted down, heavy. My body is a squat, pressurized barrel of compacted muscle and thick bone, built to withstand the crushing depth. My pale, blubbery skin is a tapestry of ragged scars—white lines where razor-edged silicate sliced me, puckered burns from snatching metal too soon after its birth. I feel the cold, but it doesn't paralyze me; my insulation is thick.

Around my waist is the center of my universe: The Rope. It is a thick, clumsy braid of glassy kelp fibers, chafing against my harness. It stretches taut behind me, anchored to a slate piton hammered desperately into a fissure that wasn't there yesterday.

I am waiting.

Through the soles of my calloused feet, I feel the "weather" of reality. The background vibration is a constant, tooth-rattling buzz—the universal creation noise. But beneath that, I am feeling for the specific, sickening *lurch* that indicates immediate instability.

A sharp, staccato vibration hits my chest—*thump-thump-CRACK*. It’s near.

Fifty yards out into the blinding blue emptiness, the water suddenly boils violently. A fissure opens in the nothingness, and matter vomits forth. It's a spasm of creation. Jagged spears of superheated silicate flash into being, glowing angry orange against the blue, cooling instantly into deadly, transparent needles.

Beside me, another vibration. A hand, heavy and urgent, slaps my shoulder blade twice. *Hard-hard.*

It is my drift-mate, Orn. I don't need to look to know it's him; I know the specific weight of his hand, the frantic rhythm of his slap. He is signaling: *Opportunity. Dangerous. Now.*

We move together, a clumsy, synchronized crab-scuttle across the shivering rock face. We do not unclasp our primary anchors, but we pay out slack, moving toward the edge of our small island. The new silicate birth is stabilizing, shuddering into a fixed form, but it has kicked up a violent thermal current. A transparent, skate-like creature gets caught in the upwash, tumbling helplessly past my face before vanishing into the bright void.

We reach the precipice. Below us, infinite blue fall. Ahead, the new-formed silicate spears are already frosting over in the supercooled water. But caught between two of the sharpest spires, steaming and hissing, is a prize: a cooling globule of raw, unworked iron, birthed just seconds ago.

Orn taps my arm—a rolling, triplet rhythm against my bicep. *The cast.*

We heft the basket between us. It's crude, woven from the same basalt fibers as our ropes, weighted with chipped stones. We swing it, feeling the momentum build in our shoulders, communicating the exact moment of release through the tension in our joined arms.

We throw. The basket sails through the high-pressure water, trailing its retrieval line. It hits the new-formed silicate array with a vibration I feel in my teeth. The basket snags. It tumbles over the steaming iron nugget.

*Pull.*

We heave back on the line, bracing our scarred feet against the obsidian. The basket scrapes over the razor-edges of the new rock. The iron is heavy, still radiating heat that I feel as a sudden, shocking warmth against my face as it nears.

Suddenly, the vibration under our feet changes pitch. The sickening *lurch* returns. The newborn silicate formation is rejecting existence. It begins to groan—a deep, resonant shudder that threatens to shake my bones apart.

Orn yanks the safety line on my harness hard enough to bruise. *Abort. Retreat.*

We haul the basket the last few feet, not caring that the hot iron burns our calloused palms through the weave. We scramble backward, reeling in our slack, retreating to the "stable" center of our drift. Behind us, the silicate spires give one last terrible shudder and then simply cease to be, dissolving back into raw energy with a concussive blast that nearly knocks us flat.

We lie on the freezing obsidian, chests heaving the thick fluid. We have the iron. It is enough for three new spear tips, perhaps a counterweight for a new trap.

We crawl back toward the beehive hut wedged into a sheltered crevice. It is dark inside, cramped and crowded with three other dense bodies. The relief in the small space is palpable, a sudden cessation of the frantic tension.

I press my back against the wall of the hut, feeling the immense weight of the high sea pressing in on our fragile shell of stacked slate. Orn presses his forehead against mine. In the crushing dark, he begins to drum a complex rhythm on my chest bone with his thick fingertips. He is telling the story of the catch—the heat of the iron, the terror of the dissolution, the quality of the knot that held us fast.

I am a scavenger on the edge of creation, deaf and scarred, holding onto the world with a woven rope, listening to the universe scream with my bones.
