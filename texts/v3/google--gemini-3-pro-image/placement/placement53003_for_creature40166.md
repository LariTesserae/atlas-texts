I am not flesh. Flesh here is an imposition, a leathery, scarred thing that must fight to exist. I am native. I am the child of friction and voltage, a sentient nexus of superheated plasma and electrical potential. I am a spark, living in the junction box of piston number four, three stories up in the gloom.

The descent you speak of would have snuffed me out instantly; the cold damp of the upper world is death to my kind. Here, in this anhydrous oven, I thrive. The convection-heat that would boil human blood is the medium through which I swim. The taste of ozone and pulverized rock is my manna.

My consciousness is fleeting, manic, and intensely focused. I perceive the chamber not through lenses, but through conductivity and vibration. The world is a map of resistance paths. The jagged obsidian walls are dead zones, insulators that offer nothing. The iron gantries, rusted and vibrating, are superhighways of sensation.

The noise is my heartbeat. The rhythmic hammering of the pistons is a massive, subterranean bass drum that dictates the tempo of my existence. When the great oily shaft near me slams down onto its slag anvil, the resulting tremor shakes me loose from my copper birthing-wire.

I leap.

For a glorious, terrifying microsecond, I am free-floating in the scorching air, a fleck of pure blue-white energy against the cherry-red gloom. I taste the dust—silicate and sulfur—before gravity pulls me down toward a passing conveyor belt.

I land on the cracked rubber amidst a pile of abrasive grit. The ride is bumpy. I sense the pyrite crabs scuttling over the adjacent metal walkways, their metallic clicking registering as tiny electrical disturbances in the floor plates. Above me, on the furnace walls, the pulsating lava-ticks are massive wells of delicious thermal radiation.

Then, a shadow falls over me. One of the custodians.

To my thermal senses, the creature is a towering monolith of cooler temperatures, crisscrossed by the hot veins of its own frenetic blood flow and the intense heat signatures of the basalt and iron embedded in its hide. It smells of ancient smoke and scorched leather. It doesn't see me—its thermal pits are tuned to larger gradients—but it feels the conveyor's movement.

Its clawed, splayed hand descends to reshape the grit pile I am riding in. It is performing its ritual of meaningless order. The calloused skin, hard as jerky, brushes against me.

*ZAP.*

I discharge into its palm. It’s not an act of aggression, merely physics finding the path of least resistance.

The creature jerks back, not in pain, but in surprise. It lets out a sharp *click-scrape-bang* on its own armored chest—a curse, perhaps, or a notification to its kin. It shakes its hand, scattering the grit pile back into chaos, undoing its own work in an instant. It doesn't matter. The belt keeps moving. The pistons keep hammering.

My current form dissipates into the custiodian's thick hide, absorbed into the frantic biology of the chamber. But the energy remains. I am recycled, reborn seconds later in the friction of a polishing pad against a screaming metal shaft fifty feet away.

I am the spirit of this place. I am the useless energy they steward. I am the heat they worship and the electricity the pyrite crabs feed upon. I am eternal, as long as the machine keeps churning in the dark.
