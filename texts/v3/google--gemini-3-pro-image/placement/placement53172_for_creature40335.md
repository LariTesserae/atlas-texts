The heat is the first thing, a thick, wet blanket that presses against my polished, mahogany-colored skin. My own body responds in kind, sweating a slick coat of sweet-smelling resin that beads and runs, mingling with the condensation dripping from the vaulted root-ceiling far above.

I am clinging to the underside of a major vascular conduit, a vine-artery thick as a tunnel, suspended over a yawning chasm of swirling warm mist. My four-digited hands and feet, tipped with dense, curved keratin claws, are hooked deep into the sweating, fibrous outer layer of the pipe. Beneath my grip, I feel the immense, rhythmic thrum of the titan’s lifeblood—hot, pressurized sap—surging toward the refineries.

I am a Vascular Mender, an Arboreal deep within the industrial gut of the root-system.

The noise is extraordinarily loud, a physical pressure against my skull. The deep, cello-groan of the nearby redwood pistons provides a constant bass vibration that rattles my ribcage. Over that lies the high-pitched, manic shrieking of steam venting from overworked valves, and the ubiquitous, deafening buzz of millions of fist-sized insects swarming the fungal lamps. My intricate bark-ears twist constantly, filtering this wall of sound, tuning out the chaos to focus on the specific pitch of fluid dynamics within the conduit I am straddling.

There is a rupture near my left knee. A hairline fracture in the vine-wall is spraying a fine, scalding mist of amber fuel. It hisses against the iridescent chitin pauldron strapped to my shoulder—salvaged armor from a giant scavenger-insect.

I need to seal it before the pressure tears the vein open.

I shift my weight, my long, vine-like muscles bunching. The jade orbs of my eyes, unblinking, cut through the gloom. The world is rendered in shades of sickly bioluminescent green, shadowed by the hulking shapes of botanical machinery, though sudden, harsh flashes of orange light erupt from below where others of my kind stoke the tuber furnaces.

I reach into the stalk-leather pouch at my waist and withdraw a tool grown for this exact purpose: a textured patch of raw, sticky cambium harvested from a rapid-growth node.

I cannot speak above the roar. To coordinate the repair, I lean back, holding onto the conduit with my legs, and strike my hardened chest plate with a clenched fist—*THWACK-THWACK-THWACK*.

The sound is percussive, resonant wood striking wood.

A moment later, an answering rhythm hammers back from across the misty void. Another Mender, clinging to a valve junction fifty feet away, has heard me. I see their pale jade eyes flash in the darkness as they hand-sign a confirmation.

Almost instantly, the thunderous vibration beneath me lessens slightly as they divert flow. The spray of sap slows to a trickle. Working with practiced, fluid speed, I slam the cambium patch over the fracture. The living tissue bonds instantly to the vine-artery, sealing the wound.

I signal again—a single, sharp *CLACK* against a hollow railing pipe. The pressure returns with a mighty shudder that nearly shakes me loose, but my claws hold fast. The patch holds.

I do not pause to rest. My ears have already detected another dissonance—the grinding screech of a botanical gear running dry on a walkway below. I detach one claw, then another, beginning the slow, inverted climb down the sweating curve of the artery, moving toward the next task in the endless cycle of keeping the titan alive.
