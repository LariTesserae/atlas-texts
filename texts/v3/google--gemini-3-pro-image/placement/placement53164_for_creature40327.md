The roar is not something outside of me. It is the medium in which I exist. It vibrates through the calcified strata I press my belly against; it hums in the stiff bristles of my sensory palps; it rattles the very plates of my own segmented armor. To be alive here is to be vibrated ceaselessly by the desperate, screaming hunger of a billion other things.

I am tucked deep within a jagged fissure, a stress fracture in one of the immense, sweeping flying buttresses that holds up a terrace higher than I have ever climbed. The material surrounding me is smooth, cold, and slick with an eternal slime. It tastes faintly metallic, like old blood turned to stone. My own carapace is rough, colored the mottled grey-green of the parasitic liverworts that choke the outer surfaces, a necessary camouflage in a world of chitin grinding against chitin.

I am not one of the deafening multitude. I do not stridulate or shriek. I am a listener in the hive of noise.

My form is flattened, wider than I am tall, allowing me to wedge myself into the tightest gaps of this immense biological masonry. I have eight jointed limbs tipped with micro-barbs that grip the wet, osseous slopes with unbreakable tenacity. My eyes are vestigial pits, useless in the perpetual bruise-colored twilight; instead, I see with the feathery antennae that telescope from beneath my armored hood. They taste the thick air, heavy with the musk of insect reproduction, the rot of sodden moss, and the metallic tang of the "stone" itself.

But mostly, I hunt through my feet.

Pressed tight against the ancient structure, my legs act as seismographs. I can distinguish the baseline maddening thrum of the Resonators carpeting the outside walls—a high, scratching vibration that makes my own chitin itch. Beneath that, I feel the heavy, churning rumble of a Segmented Titan grazing three levels down, a low frequency that shakes the condensation from the ceiling of my crevice.

I am waiting for the anomaly.

The noise outside is a chaotic tapestry, but it has rhythms. I wait for a break in the pattern. A stumble. A frantic, irregular tapping that signifies something softer, something lost and panicked in the dark.

There.

It registers in my forward two limbs. A skittering vibration, light and irregular. Not a Resonator fixed in place; something mobile, confused by the overwhelming acoustic pressure. It is moving across the lip of my fissure. The taste of it reaches my antennae—a faintly sweet, dusty scent. Perhaps a Gloom-Drifter that battered itself against a spire and fell, its wing broken, now crawling blindly on the slick surface.

I do not move with the hydraulic smoothness of the Serrated Architects high above. My movement is a sudden, violent release of tension. I erupt from the crevice. My barbed forelimbs snap outward, impacting something soft and fuzzy. There is no sound to the struggle—or rather, whatever sound it makes is instantly swallowed by the universal roar.

I feel the frantic thrashing against my palps, the soft scales of moth wings dusting my armor. I drag the prize backward, retreating deep into the narrow safety of the fissure where the larger predators cannot follow. The vibration of its struggle dies out against the cold stone.

I settle in the dark, the great weight of the humid air pressing down on the hive. Outside, the screaming continues unabated. I am a small, silent gear in this colossal machine of flesh and calcified bone, fed by the rain of failures from the canopy above, secure in the cracks of a dead civilization that hums with endless, violent life.
