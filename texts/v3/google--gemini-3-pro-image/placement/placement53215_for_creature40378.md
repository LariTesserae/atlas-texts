The air is not something you breathe; it is something you push against. It feels like warm, wet wool packed tight against your skin.

You are located high up, perhaps on the third or fourth tier of one of the unfinished ziggurats that rise from the endless expanse. The perspective here is dizzying. Looking down, the sweeping terraces of sweating red and ochre stone stretch out until they blur into the watery haze, looking deceptively like a curved seabed. The blinding, shadowless white-gold light presses down from everywhere, an interrogating lamp that never clicks off.

You are not one of the colossal Anchors, nor are you the mute, sliding fauna. You are something smaller, something rooted, yet possessing a slow, deep awareness.

You are a patch of the velvet moss, thick as a mattress, growing in the deep, permanently shadowed crevice between the neck and the massive shoulder ridge of a Weigher.

Your world is the texture of its hide—a landscape of mineral-hard skin, fissured like a dried riverbed but eternally weeping warm fluid. You drink this sweat. It tastes of ancient salt and dissolved iron. Your tendrils have worked their way deep into the cracks of the Anchor's armor, anchoring you so securely that you have become indistinguishable from its flesh. You are the green fur on a living mountain.

For an eternity, your host has not moved. It sits hunched on the ziggurat edge, its tonnage pressing water from the stones beneath it. You feel the immense, slow heat radiating from its core, battling the oppressive heat of the air. Above you, the overhang of its heavy brow cartilage shields you from the worst of the searing light, creating a humid, twilight microclimate where you thrive aggressively.

You exist in a state of pure sensation. The slickness of an oily amphibian crawling across your surface. The stifling weight of the atmospheric jelly. The blinding static visual of the horizon.

And then, there is the communion.

It arrives not as sound, but as a shifting of the foundations of your reality. A vibration, incredibly deep, travels miles across the interlocking stone floor, up the massive structure of the ziggurat, through the calloused feet of your host, and into the flesh where you are rooted. It is a geological groan felt only in the marrow.

*Thrum.*

Your host does not stir visibly. Its milky, membrane-shielded eyes continue to stare at nothing. But beneath you, you feel the answering resonance build. It is a slow gathering of pressure inside a body built to withstand non-existent gravity. It takes aeons of this frozen time for the response to generate.

Finally, the answering vibration rolls out of the massive body you cling to, shaking the water droplets from your velvet leaves.

*Thrum.*

You are the recipient of this silent conversation. You feel the simple, profound reassurance passing between the lonely islands of verticality: *I am heavy. I remain. The floor is held.*
