The air is too thick for lungs. To breathe here is to drown slowly in warm, suspended vapor.

I am found where the moisture is heaviest, clinging to the shadowed, lower flank of one of the eldest Pillars.

I am not animal. I have no bones to ache with the humidity, no eyes to strain through the pearlescent fog, no blood to quicken at a sudden sound—which is fortunate, for there are no sounds here but the wet ones. I am of the sessile kingdom, but I am not one of the Giants. I am too fleeting for that sort of immortality.

I am a hanger-on. I am one of the heavy, translucent beards of slime, a gelatinous stalactite burdened with captured gas bubbles, anchored to the rubbery, bruise-colored skin of a titan.

My world is entirely vertical and purely tactile. Behind me, against my defining membrane, is the colossal reality of the Pillar. I do not see its color, but I perceive its existence as a vast, warm wall of vibrating pressure. Through the slick interface where I cling to its flank, I feel the sub-audible thrum of its hydraulics, the geological slowness of water being forced upward through ten thousand internal veins. It is a living cliff face that pulses deeper than a heartbeat.

Around me is the mist. I exist as a surface for condensation. The warm vapor clings to me, adding its infinitesimal weight to my bulk, moment by moment. I am perpetually swollen, stretched taut by gravity.

My consciousness is dim, a viscous awareness of tension and weight. I feel the slow accumulation of water molecules sliding down my length, gathering at my lowest point. There is an agonizing, delicious slowness to it. My form elongates. The suspended bubbles trapped within my jelly matrix shift sluggishly downward.

I am waiting for the break.

Time here is not measured in seconds, but in the accumulation of milligrams of water. I hang, heavy and brooding in the silence, feeling the tension build at my tip as the droplet forms. It grows too heavy for cohesion. The membrane stretches thin.

*Plip.*

A piece of me detaches. I feel the sudden, jarring lightness in the main body of my form above, a momentary relief from gravity's drag.

Below, I—the newly separated droplet, carrying a trace of the mother-slime's essence—fall through thirty feet of breathless fog. The descent is silent until the impact. I strike the deep, impossibly soft stomach of the sphagnum moss floor. The impact is muffled instantly.

For a moment, I rest on the surface of the verdant carpet, a glistening sphere reflecting the unseen sky. Then, the moss begins to drink. I am absorbed into the abyssal sponge below, pulled down into the dark, digesting graveyard of the amphitheater to mingle with the tea-colored water, waiting to be drawn up again by the roots of the Giants, to rise through their veins, and eventually, centuries from now, to sweat out of a purple flank and become the slime once more.
