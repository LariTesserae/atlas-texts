The heat is not merely around me; it is within me. It is the fuel that allows my segmented body to function. I am clinging tenaciously to the underside of a massive, corroded iron flange, high above the basin floor. This is where two conduits, wide enough for a human to crawl through, join together. The seal failed long ago, and now it weeps a constant, pressurized curtain of superheated vapor.

This is my niche. I am a creature built for the scald. My carapace is flat, segmented like slate, and the exact burnt-orange color of wet rust. I am smaller than the giants that pave the walls—perhaps the length of a finger—but I am denser, built to withstand the pressure blasts. My many legs are tipped with hardened hooks that find purchase in the micropores of the pitted metal. I am pressed tight against the iron, letting the deep, subterranean thrum vibrate through my ventral plates. It is a comforting, endless shudder that tells me the world is alive.

Below my perch, the air is a thick soup of grey fog and oil slick. Through the murk, I perceive the world not just with my multifaceted eyes, but with the intense chemical sensors along my antennae. The air tastes of ferrous oxide, sulfur, and the rich, rotting musk of a billion other exoskeletons.

I am waiting for the eruption. It comes rhythmically. The pressure builds inside the pipe I cling to—a rising groan of metal under stress. Then, the release. A deafening *HISS* blasts outwards from the flange. Scalding white steam envelops me. Any softer creature would be boiled instantly. I merely press closer to the metal. The steam carries mineral deposits from deep within the earth, and microscopic bits of cooked organic sludge. As the blast subsides, coating the iron in fresh, hot moisture, I scuttle forward. My mouthparts, specialized scrapers, harvest the nutrient-rich scale that forms around the leak. It is a hard, hot meal, flavored with iron and salt.

From my high vantage point, I watch the ecosystem churn. The walls opposite me are shimmering. It is the *Blattodea gargantua*. They are drawn to the heat of my pipe, but they cannot endure the direct blast like I can. They form a living cornice just outside the splash zone, a shivering mat of iridescent brown bodies. Their antennae wave frantically towards the moisture, a thicket of sensory threads tasting the steam I bathe in. They are frantic, numerous, and endlessly hungry.

Far below, on the grid of a rusted catwalk, I hear the heavy, deliberate *clack-clack-clack* of an armored hunter. The sound is distinct against the background sibilance of the roach-tide. The black beetle is patrolling, its heavy carapace slick with condensation. It pauses beneath a dripping girder. A piece of the living wall detaches—a roach that lost its footing on the slime. It falls silently, landing near the hunter. The *clack-clack* speeds up. There is a sharp, wet crunch that echoes faintly up to my level. The hunter feeds.

I do not fear the hunter; I am too high, too wedged into the burning crevice. I do not fear the roaches; I am armored against their jostling, and I live where they cannot remain. I am a barnacle on the hull of a dead industrial god. I listen to the deep thrum of the pumps far below, taste the sulfur on the hot wind, and wait for the next nourishing blast of steam. The world is wet, hot, hard, and perfect.
