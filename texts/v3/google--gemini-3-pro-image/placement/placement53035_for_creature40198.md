My gauntlet, a fused mass of pitted iron plate and mineral-hardened leather, grips the safety rail. The metal is slick, weeping cold sweat that immediately soaks into the thick pad of electric-green moss growing in my palm.

I am standing on Catwalk Sector 4, suspended over nothing.

Below me, through the rusted iron grate that serves as a floor, is a swirling gray void of steam and shadow that drops away for hundreds of feet. Above, framed by the parallel, glittering black walls of the canyon, is a thin ribbon of pale, distant sky that I have never touched.

I am one of the custodians.

The noise here is not sound; it is atmospheric pressure. It is a physical assault that calcified my eardrums cycles ago. I perceive the world through conduction. The constant, churning *thrum* of the deep subsurface pumps vibrates up through the iron grating, rattling the bones of my feet inside my layered boots beneath their coating of rich, damp slime. A sudden, violent *shudder* in the handrail tells me that the number three hydraulic press has just cycled, half a mile down-canyon.

My sensory helmet is brass and heavy glass, forever fogged with condensation on the outside. I see the world in blurred shapes of wet gray, rust orange, and vibrant jade. I do not need clear vision to do my work; I know this sector by the texture of the rust under my gloves and the specific frequency of the vibrations in the floor plates.

A heavy droplet falls from a towering intake pipe overhead, striking my shoulder pauldron with a dull *thunk* that vibrates into my collarbone. It joins the ecosystem thriving in the seams of my armor. A fern, rooted in the accumulated grit in the joint of my elbow, brushes against my side as I shift my weight. A small, six-legged thing with fur like oily wire scuttles across my forearm, using the warmth of my hydraulic bracing as a bridge to the next steam line. I do not acknowledge it. It is part of the machine, just as I am.

A sharp, percussive tremor runs through the railing under my hand—a specific, syncopated rhythm distinct from the background roar of industry.

*Tap-tap-shudder-tap.*

It is another of my kind, somewhere below in the mist, perhaps on the intake platform. They are signaling a pressure variance in the geothermal bypass.

I detach from the railing to respond. My movement is ponderous, a controlled collision of heavy gears and stiffened hide. *Clank. Pause. Clank. Pause.* I raise a heavy wrench, its handle wrapped in spongy jade moss, and strike the railing in return. *Tap. Tap.* Acknowledged.

I turn slowly, a process that takes several deep breaths, and begin the long lumber toward the bypass valve. The suspension bridge ahead groans under my tonnage, the rusted cables singing a low, straining note that vibrates up my spine. I walk with geological patience, a moving speck of rust and green against the sheer, glittering black face of the broken earth, tending the steam that bleeds from its wound.
