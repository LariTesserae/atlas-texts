The vibration is first. Before sight, before smell, the world announces itself through the soles of my wide, splayed feet and the flats of my enormous hands. It is the deep, resonant *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the World Machine, a bass note so low it feels less like sound and more like gravity itself oscillating.

I am perched, hunkered low. My center of gravity is coiled tight near my hips, sturdy and immovable. My toes, as long and dexterous as the fingers of the ancients are rumored to have been, are wrapped securely around the slick, algae-slimed edge of a girder the thickness of an old-growth tree trunk.

I am a Grate-Walker. My skin is the color of wet terracotta, permanently dyed by the iron oxide sludge that coats everything. I smell of tangy metal, ancient grease, and the cloying sweetness of the bruised "Iron-eater" leaves that form a dense canopy just above my head.

My location is a precarious junction point known to my tribe as 'The Weeping Axle.' It is a chaotic intersection where four massive structural strata collide. Above me, blinding shafts of sunlight pierce the layers of mesh grating and thick foliage, creating pillars of glittering steam. Below me—nothing. Just the endless, terrifying gradient of descending metal, fading from distinct gantries into a homogeneous brown blur miles down.

I am currently suspended upside down, my powerful legs hooked over a rusted I-beam, my disproportionately long arms dangling free. My massive, callused hands—spatulas of hardened flesh tipped with thick, horny claws—flex rhythmically in the humid air. I am waiting.

My thick brow ridges shield my small eyes from the harsh overhead glare, aided by goggles made of scavenged glass wired to a leather strap. Through the scratched lenses, I watch the slow, agonizing ballet of the mechanism. A gear the size of a cathedral face turns nearby. It moves so slowly that its motion is imperceptible to a quick glance. Only by watching the patch of vibrant green moss on its teeth relative to a stationary steam pipe can I see that it is, indeed, churning. It will take three of my lifetimes to complete a rotation.

The noise is deafening. It is a physical wall of sound: the hiss of a dozen nearby steam vents, the shriek of metal scraping metal, the metallic calls of Steam-Divers wheeling in the hot updrafts. I don't speak; my voice would be drowned instantly. Instead, I communicate with a sharp, piercing whistle that cuts through the din, and by slapping my wide, flat palm against my chest in rapid, percussive rhythms to signal my kin.

Right now, I am hunting.

Twenty feet below me, coiled around a conduit pumping lukewarm coolant, is a Cable-Snake. It is thick as my torso, its scales a flawless mimicry of braided, rusted steel wire. It hasn't moved in four days. To an untrained eye, it is just another piece of the machine. But I saw the slight shimmer in the heat haze when its heavy lid blinked yesterday.

I am patient. The machine has taught us patience. I hang, letting the blood rush to my head, my thick fingers twitching just slightly. A troop of Grease-Monkeys chatters somewhere above, flinging bits of oily refuse that clatter down past me, destined for the unreachable deep.

Eventually, a Plate-Bug, iridescent and huge, will skitter too close to that disguised conduit. The living cable will snap tight with hydraulic force. And in the moment of chaos, while the snake is constricting, I will unhook my legs, drop through the steam, and use my own hooked claws to claim a share of the meat before swinging away into the rustling jungle of iron and leaves.

This is my world. Up is blinding, down is endless, and everything in between is wet, loud, alive, and hungry.
