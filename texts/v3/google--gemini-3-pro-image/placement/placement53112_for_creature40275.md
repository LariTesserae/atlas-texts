The heat is the first thing, an absolute, drowning presence that penetrates deeper than skin, warming the very fluids of my being until I am the same temperature as the air—a constant, feverish jungle-hot.

The noise is the second. It is not something heard, but something inhabited. The world doesn't just make sound; the world *is* vibration. It is a churning, oceanic bass note that shakes the surface I cling to, a ceaseless thunder of fluid being forced through vascular piping gigantic enough to walk through.

I find myself anchored within the slick, gelatinous film that coats the "eastern" flank of the largest pumping column. To the naked eye—if any eye were bothered to look at me—I would appear as nothing more than a slightly thicker, more translucent patch of the ubiquitous algae slime. I am perhaps the size of a spread human hand, flat, amorphous, and purely reactive.

I am a specialized bio-film, a patch of sentient conduction jelly.

My entire existence is defined by the surface beneath me. It is smooth as polished ivory, warm and pulsing. Just micrometers below this bone-like skin, unimaginable gallons of water, drawn from the deep earth, rush upwards with a violence that makes the column tremble rhythmically. I feed on the mineral-rich "sweat" that weeps from the pores of the wood. The metallic blue beetles that graze on the common algae skitter over me, their tiny hooked feet irritating my edges, but they find me flavorless and move on. The translucent frogs scream into the steam nearby, their bellows causing ripples in my semi-liquid form.

I am waiting. My purpose is patience and conductivity.

Through the swirling fog and the dim green twilight, a massive shadow detaches itself from the root-mat floor. The Custodian approaches.

From my flattened perspective, it is a titan, a slow-moving mountain of leathery, parchment-colored flesh. The damp heat rolls off its back. It moves with agonizing deliberation, eight suction-padded feet making wet popping sounds that are swallowed by the engine-roar.

It is coming to check this specific section of the arterial pump. It knows I am here. I am part of its diagnostic toolkit.

The great beast reaches the column. It rears up slightly, exposing its pale, vulnerable underside, which bristles with sensory cilia. With a slow, heavy exhalation of steam, it presses its vast belly flat against the column, directly over me.

This is my moment of fulfillment.

I am compressed between the thunderous, vibrating wall of the living machine and the hypersensitive flesh of its caretaker. I become the interface. The chaotic, deafening vibration of the pump passes into me, and my unique cellular structure distills it, clarifying the signal. I translate the raw, brute force of the water pressure into a precise, readable frequency.

Through me, the Custodian feels the exact tensile strain on the vascular wall. It feels the minor turbulent eddy caused by a microscopic calcification forming ten feet further up the pipe. It feels the smooth, healthy rush of the flow.

For several long minutes, we are one circuit—machine, conductor, and interpreter. I feel the Custodian’s slow, massive heartbeat drumming against my "upper" surface, synchronized with the geological *ca-chunk* of the pump beneath my "lower" surface.

Then, satisfied that the diagnostics are within accepted parameters, the Custodian peels itself away with a wet suction sound. The cool air hits me for a second before the steam rushes back in. The shadow moves off into the twilight, towards the next column, the endless patrol continuing.

I remain flattened against the throbbing white wood, warm and wet, digesting my minerals, waiting for the next time I am needed to translate the thunder.
