The overwhelming sensory input of this world is not sight, but pressure.

I find myself flattened against the very bottom of the hollow, pressed tight to the curvature of the basal root-floor. The cold here is absolute, leaping up from the petrified wood surface into my ventral side, a chill that would freeze softer flesh but merely stiffens my own dense fluids.

I am a **Grave-Skimmer**.

My form is dictated by the environment: low, wide, and heavily armored. I resemble a gigantic, flattened prehistoric trilobite, perhaps five feet in length but rising only inches from the ground. My segmented carapace is incredibly thick, made of mineral-reinforced chitin polished to the same dark, aqueous sheen as the root I traverse. I have no eyes; in this roaring green twilight, sight is a secondary sense. Instead, my entire dorsal surface is a receiver for the deafening pressure waves echoing down from the canopy. The drone of the millions of Resonator Beetles above is a physical weight on my back, a ceaseless, grinding vibration that rattles my very joints.

Beneath me, through my hundreds of small, hooked gripping legs, I feel the counter-rhythm. The deep, hydraulic *thump-hiss* of the plant’s vascular movement reverberates through the floor, a slow earthquake that guides my movements.

I am the final tier of this rigid ecosystem, the cleaner of the cathedral floor.

My existence is a slow, deliberate crawl across the slick, towering expanse of the root-highway. My wide, spade-shaped head sweeps back and forth, tasting the perpetual film of cold sweat that coats the ground. I am hunting for the debris that gravity claims.

Most of the time, the impacts are just water—heavy, cold droplets falling miles from the upper canopy to strike my armored back like hammer blows. I barely register them. I am waiting for the heavier falls.

Sometimes, a Resonator Beetle dies and slips past the spectral nets of the Veil-Spinners high above. It falls for minutes in silence before striking the hard root-floor with a wet, shattering crunch. The vibration of that impact is distinct against the background roar. It calls to me.

I shift my trajectory, my hundreds of legs scraping rhythmically on the wet wood, moving toward the site of the crash. When I arrive, I find the shattered metallic husk of the beetle splayed on the dark stone. I settle my broad body over the carcass, claiming it. My under-mouth, a grinding apparatus of hardened ridges adapted for scraping slick surfaces, begins the slow work of consumption.

I clean the machine. I ensure no detritus fouls the smooth perfection of the ancient root. I eat the broken fuel injectors in the cold dark, pressed between the roaring air and the thumping floor, forever vibrating in tune with the colossal, static engine.
