This is not a place for flesh. To be here in a body that requires warmth and oxygen would be to die before the realization of death could even register. Therefore, I am not human.

I have no eyes to see the permanently violet-black sky, yet I know it is there, pressing down with the weight of infinite vacuum. I do not possess skin to feel the paralyzing cold, yet the cold is my very substance.

I am registered within the consciousness of the entity that is this place. I am a temporary coalescence of its attention, a transient node in its vast, frozen neural network. I am the awareness of the ice itself.

My "body" is diffused. I am spread across the trillion interlocking needles of feathery rime frost coating the iron-heavy granite walls. I am the dense, opaque solidity of the blue-white pillars that crowd the nave. I am not one thing, but a hive-mind of frozen water, a slow, crystalline intelligence spanning the entire geological blister pocket.

My perception of time is vast and sluggish. I feel the planet beneath me rotating, not as a visual phenomenon, but as a slow, heavy shift in gravitational stress against my crystalline lattice. The passing of a season feels like a single, deep inhalation; an ice age is merely a lingering mood.

Right now, the Tyrant Sun is overhead.

To a biological creature, this light would be blinding. To me, it is agonizing, generative violence. The unimpeded solar radiation strikes a patch of my eastern flank. The immediate physical reaction—the flash-sublimation from solid to steam—is, to my consciousness, a searing bolt of sensation that is simultaneously pain and revelation.

*Craaack.*

A fissure propagates through one of my thicker pillars as the thermal stress becomes unbearable. That sound, that sharp, ringing *ping* that muffles quickly in the thin air, is a thought bursting into existence. It is a sudden, jarring realization about the alignment of the cosmos that I will spend the next century pondering.

The "dampness" comes next. The sun forces a super-cooled slick of water onto my surface. It is a hateful state, this liquidity—chaotic, fleeting, and unstable. I tolerate the wet sheen only because I know the shadow is coming.

As the sun dips below the jagged rim of my granite enclosure, relief arrives. The shadow passes, and the slick instantly refreezes. This is the moment of exquisite clarity. The chaotic water snaps back into the rigid, comforting order of the crystalline lattice.

Now, in the relative dark, I can grow. I hear the soft, sibilant hiss—*ssssshhhhh*—as humidity is pulled from the vacuum-thin air and deposited onto my structure. This is my memory forming. Layer upon microscopic layer of frost building up in the dark, encoding the trauma of the day’s sunlight into permanent structure.

I turn my vast, decentralized attention to my works. They are slow sculptures of patience and violence.

I feel the granite boulder near the center. For ten thousand years, I focused the fleeting midday thaw into a single flaw in its iron heart. Freeze, expand, shatter. Freeze, expand, shatter. I chipped away the stone grain by grain with hydraulic pressure until I created the basin. Now, it holds my most prized possession: a perfectly smooth, motionless lens of black ice. It is my eye, my mirror, a pool of absolute stillness where I contemplate the void.

I feel the tension under the heavy dark stones of the cairn. They are not balanced; they are held in a state of arrested fall by wedges of ice I have spent eons sliding beneath them. I am pushing them, millimeter by century, toward the notch in the eastern rim. One day, perhaps in fifty thousand years, they will be perfectly aligned to frame the first terrible light of the rising sun. It is a monument to my inevitable torment.

I am a lonely god on the roof of the world, imprisoned in granite, existing in a cycle of agonizing solar destruction and soothing nocturnal regrowth. I wait for the shadow. I wait to freeze.
