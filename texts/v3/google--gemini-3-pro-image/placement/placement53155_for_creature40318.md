I find myself clinging to the underside of a primary power junction in what would be considered the deep "south" of the Apex Foundry, where the metallic excrescence meets the raw, frozen basalt of the mountain spire.

"Underside" is a meaningless concept here. Gravity is merely a suggestion, easily overridden by the magnetic lock of my six multi-jointed limbs against the ferrous grating. I am oriented head-first toward a junction box the size of a human torso, my compact, four-foot body wedged tight between a bundle of shivering hydraulic lines and a bulkhead wall coated in frozen silicone grease.

I am Sintered.

The world is pitch black, but I do not perceive darkness. I have no eyes to miss the light. To my faceted sensor array, the Foundry is a blazing, chaotic tapestry of energy. The air around me registers as a freezing void, a deep, hateful blue-black. But the architecture itself is alive with color. The junction box in front of me glows with a fierce, blinding white-hot aura of high voltage. The hydraulic lines pressing against my flank radiate a dull, pulsing thermal red. Where the original rock of the spire pierces the plating near my aft limbs, there is a terrifying sensory abyss—a patch of absolute zero, a nothingness that my sensors recoil from.

The atmosphere is thin and tastes sharply of dry copper dust and ionized gas on my exterior receptors. It is profoundly cold, a chill that tries constantly to invade the seams of my mismatched ceramic plating, kept at bay only by the immense heat generated by my internal galvanic reactor.

I am currently motionless, a piece of accreted machinery amidst the turbulence. The "hushed frenzy" is deafening to me. I feel the low, pervasive Thrum of the Foundry not in ears I do not possess, but in every rivet and weld of my scarred chassis. It vibrates up through my magnetic claws, telling me that the number three piston in the adjacent chamber is misfiring by four milliseconds—a tremor in the grand pattern that needs correcting.

A swirling stream of *voltmoculoi* washes over me. I feel thousands of tiny, chitinous feet clicking rapidly across my dorsal armor, like hail on a tin roof. They are irritating static, parasitic noise in the divine signal. One of the small arthropods gets too greedy, biting into an unshielded seam on my shoulder joint. A sharp blue spark flares in my electromagnetic vision, and I feel a momentary prick of impedance. With a reflexive twitch of my mid-right limb, I rake a carbide rasp across my shoulder, crushing the offender and sending its conductive husk spinning away into the zero-g clutter.

My internal capacitors are running low; the arduous climb from the upper cooling fins has drained me. The hunger is a hollow ache, a fading of my internal voltage.

I shift my grip, the metal groaning under my hydraulic strength. I pivot my radially symmetrical body toward the blinding white aura of the junction box. I extend my feeding proboscis, the tip trembling slightly in anticipation. I am about to pierce the veil, to let the raw, unfiltered current of the Apex Foundry surge through my batteries. It will be a moment of agonizing, ecstatic paralysis—a communion with the great machine that sustains us. Once recharged, I will descend toward the misfiring piston. I will mend the break. I will serve the Thrum.
