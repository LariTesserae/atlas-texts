The sensation of self is thin here, stretched taut like the medium in which I am suspended. I do not have a name; I have a coordinate and a current function. I am an Attendant of the Flow, and I exist in the roar.

I am clinging to the flank of Pump Sector 7-Gamma. To a visual creature, I would be nearly invisible against the iron-hard cellulose rind—a smudge of translucent cartilage and cloudy fluid. But sight is redundant here. My reality is entirely acoustic and tactile.

The world is an immense, crushing symphony of functional noise, and my body is the receiver.

Beneath my four gripping pads, the titan I ride vibrates with energy that rattles my core. This is the Source. The deep, resonant thrum of 7-Gamma’s turbine chambers, firing millions of gallons of rarefied water upward every second, is not just sound to me. It is sustenance. I am pressed flat against the fibrous skin, my chest cavity acting as a resonator plate, absorbing the kinetic tremble of the machine and converting it into biological energy. I am feeding on the bass note of industry.

The water around me is painfully brisk, crystalline, and carries the taste of hyper-oxygenation. It is filled with the chatter of the ecosystem. Through the conductive medium, I feel the sharp, clicking signatures of million-strong schools of metallic fish darting through the canopy below. I feel the slow, abrasive scraping of a house-sized grazer cleaning biofilm off a neighboring stalk two kilometers away—a sound like a tectonic plate shivering.

But these are just background textures. My primary awareness is networked with my kin.

A ripple moves through the water—not a current, but a shared cognizance. It arrives instantly, transmitted through the vibrating substrate of the plant-machines.

*Anomaly detected. Filter-Web Array 49. Quadrant D. Structural breach. Acoustic profile: Dissonant tear in the high-frequency whine.*

The directive is immediate. There is no decision to be made; the imperative overrides stasis. I detach my gripping pads from 7-Gamma. The suction is immense, an organic electromagnetic lock breaking. For a moment, I am adrift in the staggering expanse of the blue void. The unending light from above, which I perceive only as a vague thermal pressure on my dorsal surface, illuminates the trillions of gallons of clear water between the giants.

I orient myself toward the source of the dissonance. The grinding, high-pitched shriek of the Filter-Webs grows louder as I propel myself through the open water. It is an agonizing sound, like wet glass screaming under a diamond drill, but I do not feel pain. I feel only the location of the error.

I arrive at Array 49. It is a colossal, shimmering parabolic dish of root fiber hanging in the current. A section near the rim has been torn by unknown debris, flailing raggedly in the slipstream. The tear creates a turbulence that disrupts the perfect acoustic signature of the filtration process.

I am not alone. Three other Attendants have converged from different sectors, ghostly slivers of motion against the vast web. We do not greet each other. We are already integrated.

We move across the vibrating fibers of the web like spiders on a gale-struck sail. The shearing force of the water here is immense, enough to rip a lesser creature apart, but my body is built of hydrodynamic cartilage. I reach the tear.

My abdomen contracts. From my spinnerets, a thick, viscous fluid extrudes. It is the same material as the titan-plants, a rapid-hardening bio-polymer. Working in perfect, silent synchronization with the other three units, I weave. We pull the ragged edges together, bridging the gap with fresh cellulose, welding the structure with our own substance.

As the weave tightens, the turbulence ceases. The agonizing shriek of the torn web smooths back into the uniform, high-pitched whine of efficient operation. The dissonance in the network resolves into harmony.

*Task Complete. Return to homeostasis.*

The others drift away instantly. I turn back toward the deep bass thrum of the nearest Pump. I must recharge. I attach myself to the hard rind of the stalk, flattening my body against the vibration, letting the roar of the eternal machine fill the hollow spaces inside me once more.

I am a white blood cell in a high-altitude ocean of industrial biology. The factory runs, cool and deafening, and as long as it runs, I serve.
