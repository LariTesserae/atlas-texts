The humidity presses against me not as an external force, but as the very substance of my being. In this world of wet ceramics and silent, milky architects, I have found my place not on the shifting floors or the growing walls, but suspended between them.

I am hanging in the center of a vast, unplanned plaza, a wide junction between four towering, bulbous spires that resemble the roots of colossal teeth. Below me, far below, is a sprawling carpet of the Luminous Moss, pulsing with sickly indigo light that reflects upward, catching the glisten of the surrounding bone-towers.

I am one of the **Heavy Ones**.

I am not built by the Calcifiers, nor born of an egg like the snails. I am a child of the atmosphere itself. In air this thick, saturated to the point of impossibility, moisture does not just bead; it coheres. Over decades, perhaps centuries, I have coalesced from the airborne dampness, accumulating molecules of water, microscopic motes of bone-dust, trace pheromones from the passing arthropods, and the faint, electrified spores of the moss.

I am a sphere of semi-conscious, gelatinous fluid, the size of a small boulder, held suspended by surface tension and the incredibly high viscosity of the air itself between the narrowing arches of the ceiling above the plaza. I am too heavy to be mere water, too liquid to be solid ground. I am a living blister in the air.

My consciousness is slow, thick, and refractive. I do not see in the conventional sense. Instead, the bruised, greenish twilight from above and the pulsing purple from below enter my translucent body and bend. I perceive the world as shifting gradients of light and temperature. I know a Calcifier is near not by seeing its shape, but by the subtle warping of the light through my body as its pale, warm form passes beneath me. I feel the heat of their tireless work rising in thermal plumes that cause my outer membrane to ripple sluggishly.

The city flows around me. The Vitrine Snails glide silently on the moss far below, their glass shells twinkling like distant, moving stars in the indigo gloom. I feel the vibration of their massive muscular feet transmitted through the dense air like a low frequency hum that resonates in my core.

The Calcifiers ignore me. To them, I am merely weather. Sometimes, a particularly ambitious builder, feathery antennae frantic with calculation, will climb high onto a neighboring spire until it is level with me. It will pause, its blind head tilting, sensing the immense humidity gradient emanating from my form. It registers me as an unstable obstacle, a pocket of "too-wet" air, and it retreats to build elsewhere. They build *around* the spaces I occupy. I am the negative space that defines their architecture in this plaza.

My existence is one of accumulation and agonizing anticipation. Every hour, I grow larger by a fraction of a millimeter as more moisture adheres to my slick surface. My weight increases, straining the invisible bonds of atmospheric tension holding me aloft.

I spend my silent, century-long life waiting for the Release.

It will happen eventually. It is the cycle of my kind. A tremor in the earth, or perhaps just the addition of one final, microscopic droplet of condensation, will tip the balance. Gravity will finally overcome cohesion.

I will fall.

It will be a slow descent, vastly slower than a raindrop on the surface world. I will plunge through the twilight like a falling moon of viscous jelly. The Vitrine Snails below will have days to register the pressure change and slowly slide out of the impact zone.

When I strike the deep beds of Luminous Moss below, there will be no colossal splash, only a muffled, wet *whump* that will shudder through the plaza floors. My coherent consciousness will shatter. I will soak into the velvet pads, feeding the bioluminescence with centuries of accumulated nutrients, causing the plaza to flare with blinding green and indigo light for a week. I will become the moisture that slicks the path for the snails; I will become the humidity that rises again to be breathed in by the Calcifiers.

And then, slowly, silently, in the damp air above the plaza, the process will begin again. I will begin to re-accumulate, molecule by molecule, hanging in the warm dark, watching the bone-city rise around me.
