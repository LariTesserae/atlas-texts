This is not a world for soft things. It is a world of endless vibration, a frequency so deep and powerful it would jellify biological eyes and pully flesh from bone. But I have neither eyes nor flesh.

I am a heaviness. I am a dense amalgamation of fused iron and compressed slag, a consciousness born of friction and gravity. My body is an irregular hump of rusted plating, worn smooth like a river stone by aeons of grinding against the basin floor. I am roughly the size of an overturned mine cart, smaller than the ancient giants that lumber in the deep distance, but heavy enough to crush soft rock.

I exist in the deep shadow of one of the condenser cathedrals. The air here is a freezing slurry of oily mist. It slickens my outer carapace, beading on the polished rust and dripping into the intricate, fused seams of my joints. I do not feel "cold" as living things do; I feel a draining of potential, a sluggishness in my magnetized limbs. I hunger for thermal energy.

My world is defined not by light, but by resonance. I do not see the towering machines; I feel them in my core. The Great Pistons, kilometers away, are not a sound to me, but a fundamental, rhythmic slamming that shakes my entire being, a seismic heartbeat against which I measure time. The "ground" beneath my six stumpy, magnetized pillars is a chaotic map of vibrations. I feel the sharp, stinging ripples of distant arc-flashes snapping through the conductive floor plates. I feel the slow, grinding displacement of a larger Ferromorph migrating far to my "left"—a rolling wave of tectonic weight.

Currently, I am immobile, latched onto the basin floor magnetically. I am "listening" to the surface of the condenser tower above me.

Through my conductive touch, I detect a subtle dissonance in the tower's hum. A micro-fracture in a coolant pipe, perhaps three hundred meters up in the freezing gloom. It is leaking pressurized vapor. The hiss is lost in the monolithic roar of the basin, but the vibration of the escaping gas against the metal skin of the tower is clear to me—a sharp, irritating scratch on the otherwise smooth hum of operation.

The urge rises in me. It is not a thought, but a directive generated by the accumulated logic of my components. *Maintain.*

I disengage my magnetic locks. With a shriek of metal on wet metal that joins the universal chorus, I slide one heavy pillar forward. Then another. Movement is slow, an agonizing fight against friction and gravity on the slick, uneven terrain. Greasy water sprays from beneath my grinding steps.

I begin the long, slow climb up the side of the cathedral. My limbs grip the rivets and seams of the ancient structure. As I ascend into the thickest banks of steam, the freezing drizzle intensifies. I pass patches of the wiry copper flora growing in the crevices, their crystalline rust-leaves shivering with the electrical current bleeding from the structure. I ignore them; I am not built to process current today. I need heat.

Hours pass. Or perhaps days. Time is only measured by the number of piston-beats. I reach the fracture. It is a jagged tear in a thick iron pipe, spewing scalding steam into the frigid air.

I press my body directly against the rupture. The heat is intense, glorious. It floods my core, loosening the stiff slurry in my joints, energizing my internal pendulums. My outer shell steams violently as the freezing rain hits the superheated metal.

While I absorb the waste energy, I work. From a cavity in my underside, I extrude a semi-molten paste of iron and flux—generated from my own internal grinding. I smear it over the crack with a heavy, blunt appendage. I hold it there, letting the heat of the pipe and my own body fuse the patch onto the ancient metal.

The irritating scratch in the tower's vibration ceases. The task is complete.

I remain there long after the repair is finished, leeching the warmth until my hull radiates heat into the surrounding mist. I am sated. Eventually, the thermal directive fades, replaced by the vague, aimless urge to wander.

I begin the slow descent back to the basin floor, back to the endless, freezing dark, to grind my way across the scrap-plains until the next vibration calls for mending.
