The light finds me before I am awake. It drills through closed eyelids, not with the gentle warmth of a morning sun, but with the intrusive, sterile glare of an operating theatre. There is no night here, only varying degrees of blinding intensity.

I wake shivering. My metabolism, a frantic furnace burning beneath skin the colour of skimmed milk, is barely winning the war against the ambient temperature of five degrees below freezing. My breath plumes in sudden, thick clouds of ice crystals immediately upon leaving my lips. The air tastes metallic—ozone and the raw, coppery scent of sawn frozen wood.

I am curled in a shallow depression on the flank of a colossal root-mass, a structure so vast its curve seems flat. My shelter is a lean-to of woven husks, brittle and damp, clinging to the host plant like a scab. Before I dare open my eyes, my hands—knobby, calloused, stained greenish-black—fumble instinctively to my neck. I drag the heavy cowl forward and settle the thick amber lenses over my face. The world shifts from a searing white scream to a tolerable, jaundice-yellow haze.

I am one of the ghosts. I am hunger wrapped in layers of cured leaf-membrane.

Sitting up requires a specific kind of caution. I do not stand; standing is an arrogant assumption of stability. I crouch into a low scuttle, pressing the leather-hard pads of my palms against the slick, glowing surface beneath me. The "ground" is sweating cold slime.

I crawl to the lip of my precarious perch and look out.

The view is terrifyingly magnificent. It is like looking into the heart of a shattered, illuminated glacier, infinitely complex and impossibly vast. Below me is an abyss of glowing white lattice, dropping away for miles. Massive bridges of translucent boughs span voids that refuse to stay empty, choked with drifting banks of freezing fog.

In the mid-distance, perhaps three miles away across a chasm of swirling mist, a new structure is violently being born. A spire of pale bamboo, thick as a fortress tower, is erupting from a nexus point. It grows impossibly fast, spiraling upwards in jerky, tectonic spasms. Even through the cowl, the glare of its raw creation is intense. The sound reaches me seconds later—not a roar, but a profound, subterranean *groan* that vibrates through the soles of my feet, followed by the crystalline shriek of millions of tons of ice shattering as the new growth displaces the old. A cascade of glittering debris, sharp as glass, rains down into the lower darkness.

I flinch, flattening myself against the root, my heart hammering against thin ribs. My family unit is nearby, hunched shapes in the amber gloom, their heads swivelling in paranoid synchronization. We do not speak. A hiss through teeth, a sharp downward gesture of a stained hand—that is our language. *Stay low. Watch the tremors.*

We have stayed on this root-mass for three "sleeps." That is too long. The vibrations from the new growth suggest this entire sector is becoming unstable. We need to move. We need to scavenge the periphery of that new destruction for shattered silica-husks before the frost buries them, and then find a thicker, older artery that might hold still for another day.

I check the bone-white silica hook hanging at my belt. I tighten the straps of my membrane wrappings against the biting cold. I take a shallow, freezing breath and grip the glowing edge of the world, preparing to scuttle into the blinding void. I am a temporary thing in an eternal, exploding garden.
