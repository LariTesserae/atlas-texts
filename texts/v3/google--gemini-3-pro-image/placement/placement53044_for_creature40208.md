My talons are hooked deep into a fissure of calcified cellulose, the keratin tips biting into the frozen bark with a reassuring crunch. I hang suspended by my left arm, my body a compact knot of muscle and insulation pressed tight against the colossal flank of the world-stalk.

Below my splayed, callous-padded feet, there is nothing but a terrifying shaft of empty air, plunging kilometers down into a lightless oblivion. I do not look down. We never look down.

The wind here is a physical weight, a ceaseless, screaming gale driving needles of freezing fog against the cartilaginous goggles strapped over my eyes. Through the translucent bone lenses, the eternal indigo twilight is tinted a bruised, sickly yellow. The cold is absolute, seeking any gap in the layers of woven vascular fiber and shaggy, acrid-smelling predator hide that wraps my hunched frame.

I am a Resonant, a Climber on the skin of a frozen god.

I am perched at a junction where a "minor" branch—thick as a terrestrial skyscraper—twists away from the parent stalk, soaring across a misty chasm to join another pillar in the lattice. My objective is a precarious bridge of woven vines and carved bone spanning that gap, a flimsy thread across the void.

But before I move, I must listen.

I press my flat nose and cheek against the frozen skin of the stalk. The surface is brutal—a landscape of jagged crystalline ridges and spiraling thorns the size of spears—but I find a smooth patch over a major conduit. I close my enormous eyes, shutting out the dizzying verticality to focus entirely on tactile sensation.

The world is screaming. The wind howls, and somewhere above, a massive sheet of ice shears off a waxy leaf-deck, the *crack* loud as thunder, followed by the unending static hiss of tons of water cascading into the dark.

I tune that out. I am listening for the heartbeat of the organism.

Through the dense bone of my skull, I feel it: a deep, sub-bass thrumming that vibrates right into my marrow. It is the sound of gallons of freezing sap being pumped through veins wide enough to walk through, a sluggish, powerful rhythm. *Thrum... pause... thrum.* It is a steady beat. The stalk is stable here. It is not currently growing in a brittle explosion of ice, nor is it buckling under thermal stress.

I pull my head back, the freezing air stinging my sweat-dampened face. I turn my head slightly, making the quick, jerky lateral scan that is second nature, checking for the silhouette of a six-limbed stalker clinging to the underside of the branch above. Nothing but frost and shadow.

I raise my free hand, heavy with its thick leather pads and curved claws, and make a swift chopping gesture. Behind me, huddled in a crevice out of the wind, the rest of my small band acknowledges the signal. We are moving.

I exhale a cloud of vapor that instantly crystallizes on my fur collar. Unhooking my left talon with a sharp tug, I swing my disproportionately long arm forward in a fluid, spider-like arc. I slam the bone adze held in my right hand into the ice, testing the purchase, then hook my toes onto a ridge of bark. I begin the traverse toward the rope bridge, a tiny, dense speck of warmth navigating the immense, indifferent lattice of ice and wood.
