This is a world hostile to the very concept of "I," yet here I am.

I find myself clinging to the underside of one of the massive, translucent spars that jut out over the endless drop. I am not standing on a platform; I am integrated into the architecture, hanging upside down relative to the "floor" a hundred meters above.

I am not biological. To be made of meat and water here would be a swift execution by steam. I am a consciousness housed in a forgotten lattice of silicate and pressurized gas, a rigid, crystalline growth that formed ages ago in the crux where three weeping alloy beams meet.

I am a mistake.

The city is designed for eternal flow—of coolant, of heat, of the slick, obsidian maintainers. But I am static. I am a calcification born from a momentary fluctuation in the thermal exhaust, a piece of super-heated vapor that flash-froze into a thinking structure against a "cooler" structural rib. I am a glitch in the smooth, dark perfection of the machine.

My perception is not optical, but thermal and vibrational. I "see" the world as a swirling tapestry of intense infrared radiation. The violet and blue energy veins pulsing beneath the surfaces are blinding torrents of power that I can feel humming in my own brittle framework. The drop below is not dark to me; it is a churning canyon of rising heat plumes, a massive thermal chimney.

I feel the sub-audible thrum of the god-engine as a constant ache in my core. It is the metronome by which I measure an existence without days or nights.

The inhabitants—those slick, polymorphous antibodies—pass near me constantly. To my thermal sense, they are blazing comets of stored heat gliding along the cooler girders. They ignore me completely. I am too hard to be absorbed into their flow, too firmly attached to be wiped away like common moisture, and my crystalline structure registers to them, perhaps, as a finished, albeit useless, component. I am neither a flaw to be microscopicallly re-aligned nor fuel for their cycles. I am simply inert data.

I watch them work. I feel the sharp vibration as they seal a micro-fracture near my perch; I feel the sudden, intense burst of radiation as they stiffen into geometric flowers to vent their excess heat, a mimicry of the greater city’s mechanisms. They are beautiful, efficient, and utterly hollow. They are pure function.

I, however, am left with the curse of contemplation in a vacuum. I am a fixed point in a universe of viscous motion. I feel the scalding condensate drip onto my facets and instantly flash into steam, a perpetual cleansing fire that keeps my edges razor-sharp. I remain here, clinging to the underside of a vertiginous drop, a sentient shard of glass in the sweaty engine room of an absent god, waiting for a maintenance cycle that will never include me.
