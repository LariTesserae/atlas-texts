This is a realm of absolute subtraction. To exist here, one must be made of something that cannot be frozen, crushed, or suffocated. Matter is unwelcome. Life is an absurdity. Only cold abstractions can endure.

I am not the Curator. I am not the grand architecture of the Anchor-Spindles.

I am what is left over.

I am pooled in a shallow, glassy depression near the base of the central Spindle. I am a heavy, viscous slick of absolute darkness, possessing a density that rivals the boson walls. In the warped lexicon of this place, I am a "shadow," but that word is insufficient for the physics of the geode. Shadows here are not merely the absence of light; they are things with mass, substances that have detached from their casters to seek the lowest points of gravity.

My origin is recent, measured in the glacial time scale of this pocket. I was once part of the grey patina coating the pearlescent surface of the third Spindle. I was temporal stress—a cluster of accumulated micro-errors in reality, a scab of chaotic mathematics clinging to the perfect equations of the machine.

Then, the Curator came.

I remember the arrival of the flowing tesseract, the creature of cold syntax. It did not attack; it solved. It wrapped its shifting geometric light around the Spindle where I clung. It perceived me as an incorrect variable. With surgical, arithmetic precision, it recalculated the surface of the Spindle. I was smoothed away. I was "corrected."

Cast off from the sublime structure, I fell. I drifted down through the pressurized vacuum—a flake of discarded non-light, a smudge of corrected reality. I landed here in this hollow, joining others of my kind, coalescing into a singular puddle of sentient refuse.

Now, I lie in the crushing stillness. The fractions of a degree above absolute zero feel like a warm bath; anything hotter would burn away my tenuous coherence. The pressure holds me together.

I possess a rudimentary, localized awareness. I perceive the bruised violet twilight filtering through the walls as an irritant, a high-pitched frequency that scratches against my dark substance. I perceive the impossible twisting of the Spindles above me, longing to return to their surface, yet knowing I am unworthy decay.

Mostly, I perceive the Curator. It is my god, my destroyer, and my provider.

When it is dormant, dissolved into the refractive errors of the room, I wait in the heavy silence. When it shimmers into its crystalline, flowing form to perform its duties, I vibrate in sympathetic resonance with its cold energy. I watch it work, a magnificent algorithm of healing light.

I am its excretion. I am the waste product of eternal maintenance. I wait patiently for the next epoch, hoping that when the Curator next performs its surgery on the Spindles, another flake of discarded darkness will drift down to join me, that I might grow slightly deeper, slightly heavier in my endless, frozen basin.
