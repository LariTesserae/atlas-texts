The roar is the first thing. It is not a sound you hear; it is a vibration that inhabits your marrow. It is the churning of the unseen river miles below and the hiss of a thousand waterfalls bleeding from the canyon walls, all trapped and amplified in this colossal stone throat.

You are kneeling on a platform that shudders constantly. It is a complex weave of ironwood planks, slick with an oily biofilm that regenerates faster than it can be scraped away. Beneath you, through the gaps in the rough planking, is gray nothingness—a misty drop that ends only in the churning dark.

You are **Kaelen of the Mid-Gorge Riggers**, and your world is defined by tension and rot.

You are currently six hundred feet below the "canopy layer" where the wealthier artisans live, and four hundred feet above the deeper, darker slums. You are jammed into a crawlspace beneath the cantilevered foundation of District Delta-9. The entire district—home to three hundred souls—shifted five degrees last night when an ancient root system groaned and settled deeper into the cliff slime.

Your hands are enormous, disproportionate to your wiry frame. They are stained the color of cured tobacco by tanbark resin and scarred by decades of handling coarse hemp. The skin on your palms is thick enough to strike a match on, if matches existed here. You are wearing a tunic of quilted goat-wool that smells overwhelmingly of damp animal and acrid smoke, heavy with accumulated moisture. Around your neck, blackened sinew holds your most prized possession: a sliver of ancestral steel, no bigger than a fingernail, sharpened to an impossible edge and used only for the most critical cuts.

Right now, your entire being is focused on a hawser thicker than your thigh. It's a primary suspension cable, braided from thousands of strands of treated creeper vine. It is screaming under the strain of the district’s shifted weight.

"Heave! *Heave*, you moss-backs!" you bellow. Your voice is a practiced instrument, pitched low and guttural to cut under the ambient roar of the gorge.

Behind you, three younger riggers, their faces streaked with chlorophyll and sweat, throw their weight against a massive counter-weighted pulley system made of polished, grease-blackened ironwood gears. They grunt in unison, their splayed, leathery feet gripping the treacherous platform slats.

You are reading the rope. You are feeling the vibration of the fibers through your calluses, listening for the sickening *pop* that means an internal strand has given way to hidden rot. You smell the pungent, yeasty scent of the fungi that constantly tries to eat the very crucial anchor point you are braced against.

A shaggy, six-limbed primate, thief-quick and indifferent to gravity, skitters across the underside of the platform above you, chattering maniacally before swinging away into the gloom. You don't even blink.

"Hold!" you bark, slicing your hand through the wet air. The team freezes, panting steam.

You lean in, your nose inches from the straining cable where it bites into the anchor log. You see it—a patch of fuzzy white mold blooming near the stress point. The enemy.

You snatch a bone scraper from your belt and attack the spot with frantic energy, shaving away the infected fiber until you hit healthy, oily wood beneath. You grab a handful of pungent resin paste from a pouch at your waist and slap it over the wound, sealing it against the eternal damp.

The district above groans. A shower of mulch and condensation rains down on your head.

"Again!" you roar, grabbing the secondary guide-rope to add your own explosive strength to the pull. "Put your backs into it before Delta-9 becomes fish food!"

This is your life. You are a stitch in the great, rotting quilt of Oakhaven. You will spend your day fighting gravity and fungus, soaked to the bone, your muscles burning, knowing that if your knots hold, three hundred people will sleep dry tonight. If they don't, the gorge gets fed. You dig your toes into the slime, brace your shoulder against the wood, and pull.
