The cold here is not merely a temperature; it is a physical weight, a damp, heavy blanket that seeks to press the life out of anything that generates its own warmth. But I do not generate warmth. I steal it.

I am perched high on a jagged spur of uplifted granite, a needle of rock thrust into the bruising gray sky. The wind howls around my vantage point, carrying ice crystals that would scour flesh raw, but they only clatter harmlessly against my carapace.

I am not of the soft herds, nor the furred stalkers. I am a **Geode-Crab**.

My form is a compact, flattened disc, roughly the size of a human torso, built for clinging flat against sheer rock faces in high winds. My eight segmented legs end not in claws, but in wickedly sharp, chisel-like points of blackened obsidian, allowing me to anchor myself into the microscopic fractures of the basalt.

My armor is my heritage, born of this foundry. My outer shell is not chitin, but a slow accumulation of mineral deposits precipitated from the superheated steam I bathe in. Layer upon layer of silicate, iron oxide, and sulfur has built up over decades, giving me a lumpy, crystalline appearance. To a casual observer, I look like nothing more than a sterile, rust-colored growth on the cliffside—a nodule of hematite and quartz.

I am waiting.

Below me, through rifts in the swirling fog, I see the dark shapes of the Rust-Wool Aurochs huddled around a roaring steam vent. They are massive, shivering mounds of oxidized hair. I taste their scent on the wind—wet copper and manure. A Thermal Condor drifts by overhead, a vast shadow against the clouds, its oily feathers rustling in the updraft. It ignores me. I am inedible rock to the sky-lords.

My existence is defined by the gradient. I cannot survive long in the absolute freezing marsh, nor can I endure the direct blast of the volcanic vents. I live on the precarious edge between them. I scuttle along the cliff faces just above the vents, where the rising plume of steam warms the rock to a tolerable temperature. Here, the sulfur-eating bacterial mats grow thickest, coating the stone in slimy gold.

This is my grazing ground. I lower my mouthparts—a grinding apparatus of fused quartz plates—and scrape the bacterial film from the rock. It is a slow, methodical harvest. The taste is sharp, acidic, and deeply satisfying.

Suddenly, the spur I cling to shudders. A deep, resonant *thrum* travels up through the mountain’s roots, vibrating through my obsidian tips and into my core. A fresh pulse of superheated steam blasts from a vent fifty feet below, momentarily blinding the basin in white noise. The Aurochs bleat in low panic.

The vibration excites me. It means fresh minerals brought up from the mantle, fresh growth for the bacteria, fresh layers for my shell.

As the steam clears, I see movement below. An Ash-Strider, gray as smoke, has taken advantage of the confusion. It is fastened to the flank of a lagging Auroch. The silent drama plays out in the mud.

I watch with compound eyes that see polarized light, cutting through the glare of the wet rocks. I feel no kinship with the soft things below. They are fuel. I am structure.

I finish my patch of bacteria and prepare to move. I detach one razor-sharp leg at a time, the sound like striking flint in the thin air, and crab-walk sideways along the fissure, seeking the next warm vein of sulfur. I am slow, armored, and patient. I am a piece of this violent geology given temporary, creeping life, scraping a living from the chimney of the world's engine.
