The vibration is not something I hear; it is the medium in which I exist. It is a constant, marrow-deep churning that tells me the world is operating within acceptable parameters. If the Hum were to stop, it would mean the universe had died.

I am situated in the mid-levels, a zone of perpetual twilight where the blinding steam-glare from above filters down only as a bruised, grayish suffusion. I am clinging to the underside of a massive overhang, a protrusion of sweating architectural geology that shelters a deep, narrow canyon of machinery below.

My position is precarious and essential. I am an anchorite of the overhang.

My body is segmented and long, draped across the irregular ceiling like thick, armored cabling. I possess forty-two segments, each protected by a plating of ablative ceramic composite that sheds the ceaseless rain of caustic brine dripping from the structures above. Between these plates, my "flesh" is a flexible lattice of conductive polymers, currently slick with a coating of dielectric mucus that I exude for protection.

I do not see the dark canyon below me. I have no use for eyes in this stinging fog. Instead, my world is mapped by sixteen pairs of gripping limbs, tipped with powerful electromagnets and micro-claws of carbide. Through them, I read the substrate. I feel the rhythmic thud of high-pressure pumps miles away; I distinguish the skittering vibration of a diamond-shelled grazer moving along the vertical wall opposite me; I feel the terrifying, fluidic shiver of the structure that precedes a nearby steam vent eruption.

I am currently dormant, locked magnetically to the iridium-alloy ceiling, letting the Hum rattle through my carapace. I am waiting for sustenance.

I am not a grazer, endlessly rasping at mineral blooms. Nor am I a stalker, liquefying prey in sudden bursts of violence. I am a siphon.

My primary sensory apparatus consists of heavy, feathery antennae that sweep the humid air, tasting the chemical soup. Just now, they register a change. The pervasive tang of oxidized copper and ozone is overlaid by something sharper, richer: a concentrated plume of leaking coolant fluid, heavy with dissolved salts and stray voltage.

It is coming from a stress fracture in a conduit running along the canyon wall ten meters away.

I disengage my magnetic locks in a rippling sequence. I move upside down across the ceiling, my many legs finding purchase on the slimy, corroded metal with practiced ease. I move toward the source of the scent. The air gets hotter here, the steam thicker.

I reach the junction where the conduit is fissured. A jet of superheated, mineral-rich liquid is sputtering into the void. A grazer would boil instantly here. A ferro-stalker would lose its cohesion.

I anchor myself firmly with my rear segments. From beneath my head-shield, I extrude my feeding apparatus. It is not a mouth, but a cluster of specialized, spongy tendrils made of highly conductive fibrous metallic glass.

I thrust the tendrils directly into the sputtering jet.

The sensation is overwhelming. It is not taste as a biological entity would know it; it is a massive injection of potential energy. The thermal gradient hits my internal exchangers, sending a rush of power to my capacitor banks. Simultaneously, the raw electrical current carried in the saline fluid arcs into my conductive tendrils, a voltaic jolt that makes my entire segmented body spasm in ecstatic intake.

My ceramic plating glows with a dull, internal cherry-red heat as I process the influx. I gorge myself on the city’s bleeding energy. I draw deeply, filtering the heavy isotopes for my own structural repair and venting waste heat in plumes of my own steam.

Above me, a plasma discharge cracks across the sky, momentarily turning the gray fog a blinding, electric blue. The vibration spikes, a hammer blow against the planet. I clamp down harder, secure in my niche, siphoning the life-blood of the machine, vibrating in perfect sympathy with the unending roar.
