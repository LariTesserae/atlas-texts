The pressure is not something that presses on you from the outside; down here, it is a fundamental condition of existence, woven into every fiber. To exist is to be crushed, and to survive is to be dense enough to push back just a little.

I am a ribbon of sleek, highly compressed muscle, black as the liquid obsidian I swim through. I have no bones to shatter, only flexible cartilage that bends with the currents of super-cooled syrup. My eyes are vestigial pearls buried deep in my skull, useless for eons. Instead, my entire length is a sensory organ. I feel the world as a complex map of pressure waves and faint electrical ghosts.

I am currently threading my way through the ribcage of a dissolved concrete tenement. The exposed rebar is coated in slick manganese, cold and slimy against my flanks. I move with a sinuous economy, a flicker of anti-motion in a world defined by heavy, static shapes.

This drowned city is my coral reef. The bundles of pipes, choked with petrified sediment, are the labyrinth where I hunt. The towering facades of riveted steel, leaning inward like drunken giants, are my canyon walls. I taste the water constantly. It tastes of ancient rust, sulfurous decay, and the sharp, metallic tang of copper leaching from exposed wiring.

I am drawn to the warmth, faint as it is. I follow a plume of slightly less-freezing water, smelling the rotten-egg scent of sulfur. It leads me to a crack in a massive, barnacle-encrusted boiler, where a pale gray mat of bacteria clings to life. I graze along the edges, nipping at the microscopic life thriving on the chemical stew.

Suddenly, the water around me violently thickens. A vibration, so deep it feels like the planet itself is shivering, rolls through the canyon. It is a sound that is not heard, but felt as a jarring displacement of my own internal fluids.

I flatten myself against the underside of a rusted catwalk, becoming practically two-dimensional.

Below me, the Custodian passes.

My lateral lines scream with input. It is immense, a moving mountain of jagged scrap. I feel the turbulence caused by its six piston-driven legs as they smash into the silt, kicking up blinding clouds that my chemical senses taste as sudden, heavy grit. The shriek of its metal joints grinding together sends panicked electrical spikes through my nervous system.

It is a terrifying cacophony of rust and power. I sense the massive heat signature of its friction, a churning furnace in the freezing dark. I sense the electrical hum of its scavenged sensors sweeping the area, searching for breaks in the pattern.

It pauses near a towering, seized-up gearbox the size of a cathedral entrance. Its massive, clumsy claw, a conglomeration of rebar and plate, reaches out. I feel the crushing impact as it hammers away a calcified growth of manganese that was fouling the teeth. It takes hours. I remain motionless, a sliver of shadow clinging to a dying lattice.

Finally, with a groan that shakes the silt from the girders above, the giant gear lurches forward an inch. The Custodian radiates a strange, magnetic satisfaction. It turns, slowly, ponderously, its work done for now, and begins the long, tectonic shuffle toward the next point of failure in this endless, crushing dark.

I wait until the turbulence of its passing has settled into a gentle, silty fog. Then, with a flick of my tail, I detach from the catwalk and flow silently into the gap between two immense, silent boilers, a ghost haunting a graveyard of giants.
