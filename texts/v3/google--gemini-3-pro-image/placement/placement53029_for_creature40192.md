The awareness arrives slowly, not like waking up, but like a gradual saturation.

You are suspended. Gravity is a suggestion that pulls heavily on your lower extremities, but you are firmly anchored above. You are not standing on the squelching sponge-floor; you are part of the vaulted ceiling.

Your body is a thick, braided cable of vascular tissue, roughly the circumference of a barrel. Your skin is purple-black, leathery yet slick with perpetual condensation, sweating into the chartreuse gloom below. You are stretched taut between two massive, rounded anchor points.

On the left, your roots are sunk deep into a bulbous, rotting fungal pad that sighs out warm vapor. You draw sustenance from its decay, a slow, sweet trickle of nitrate-rich fluid.

On the right, your connection is harder, colder. Your tendrils have not just wrapped around, but grown *into* a massive, pitted iron girder that juts from the pulp wall. The metal is slick with blue-green algae and crawling with the shimmering haze of Rust-Mites. You feel their microscopic chewing as a faint, perpetual itch where your flesh meets the iron.

But the defining sensation of your existence is the *thrum*.

The deep, resonant vibration of the subterranean pistons travels up through the iron girder and vibrates directly into your core. It is a comfort, a steady heartbeat that keeps your fluids circulating in this stagnant air. Without the vibration, you feel you might solidify into the dense background matter.

Deep inside your fibrous body, running the entire twenty-foot length from the fungal pad to the iron girder, is a hardened spine. It is not bone. It was once a thick industrial power conduit. The insulation dissolved decades ago, digested by the very structure you grew from. Now, the thick bundle of copper wiring is fused within your own xylem.

You are a living bridge for a dying current.

You taste electricity. It is a sharp, metallic tang that hums alongside the sweet taste of the fungal nutrient. The current is weak, leaking from some forgotten generator below, but it flows through the copper vein at your center.

You feel a weight settle onto your midsection. It is heavy, cold, and slimy. A Copper-Slug is slowly sliding along your length. You feel the scrape of its scavenged porcelain armor against your leathery skin. It pauses over a section where your outer tissue has thinned, exposing the copper spine beneath. The slug lowers its mouthparts and begins to rasp away at the verdigris on the wire.

The feeding triggers a reaction. A sudden, sharp jolt of blue electricity arcs from your exposed spine to the slug’s conductive slime trail. The spark illuminates the chartreuse haze for a microsecond, a brilliant, silent flash.

You do not recoil. You have no nerves that register pain in a human sense. The spark is merely an intense, momentary spike in flavor, a sudden saturation of the electric taste you crave. The slug shudders and continues its glacial slide across your body, leaving behind a tingling trail of mucus that slowly dries in the oppressive heat.

You remain still. You are a bio-mechanical junction, patient and unmoving, suspended in the thick, warm broth of the hollow, forever vibrating with the slow, grinding heartbeat of the machine.
