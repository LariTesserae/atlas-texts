I am pressed flat against the warmest place in the universe.

In this world of suspended oceans and infinite gloom, I am a mote of sensory existence, a palm-sized disc of translucent, firm jelly, fringed with thousands of highly sensitive palpating cilia. I have no eyes; the teal twilight of the open water is a concept alien to me. My reality is defined entirely by thermal gradients and pressure.

I live in the narrow, crushing interface between a Litho-Sentinel and the primordial granite peak it is slowly consuming.

My home is the "hearth-scar"—the perfectly smooth, bowl-shaped depression the Sentinel has melted into the mountain over the last millennia. Above me, like the vaulted ceiling of a cathedral, is the underside of the Sentinel itself. It is not armored like its top, but is a landscape of soft, highly vascularized membrane, glowing with that dull, volcanic red heat that I perceive as pure comfort.

I am a grazer of this interface. I crawl slowly along the border where the Sentinel's searing flesh meets the stubborn rock. The water here is superheated, pressurized, and thick with dissolved minerals—a sulfurous, metallic soup that sustains me. I feed on the extremophile bacterial mats that bloom in this hyper-thermal zone, cleaning the giant’s soft underbelly like a remora on a leviathan.

The giant does not know I exist. Its consciousness is too vast, its thoughts taking seasons to ripple across its calcified neural pathways. I am less than a parasite to it; I am a localized piece of texture.

The outside world—the tangled forests of black rubber-weed, the vast silent spaces between peaks—is a terrifying void of cold. I only know it through the vibrations that travel down through the Sentinel’s massive shell. I feel the shudder when a convection current hits the forest on its back. I feel the minuscule gravitational shifts when a distant water-globule tears free and falls through the air gap.

But mostly, my world is the deep, uterine hush of the hearth-scar. The primary sound is the "thrum"—a vibration so low it is felt rather than heard, a combination of the geothermal vents venting beneath us and the slow, grinding metabolism of the Sentinel itself. It is a sound that vibrates through my entire gelatinous body, a constant reassurance of safety and heat.

Yet, lately, the taste of the water has changed. The sulfur is growing stale. The intense heat radiating from the rock beneath us is waning, cooling by infinitesimal degrees that only a creature like me could notice.

The Sentinel knows it, too. I feel a new vibration building in the membrane above me—a restlessness. The thrum is changing pitch. The geological mind is slowly coming to a terrible realization: the hearth is dying.

Soon, the subsonic groan will begin, the sound that shakes the bones of the mountains. The seal will break. The immense suction holding us to this perfect bowl will fail, and cold, thin water will rush into our sanctuary. I will bury myself as deep into a fold of the Sentinel’s burning underside as I can, clinging with every cilium I possess, as the island-creature detaches and begins its terrifying, silent ascent through the gloom, hunting for a new place to burn against the stone.
