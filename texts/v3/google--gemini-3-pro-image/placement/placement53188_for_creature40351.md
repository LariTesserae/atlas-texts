The cold is not something you feel as an intrusion; it is the medium in which you exist, a heavy, pressurized blanket that presses against every inch of your slick, nacreous skin. You are high above the flooded floor, clinging effortlessly to the underside of a spiraling ramp that bridges two massive fungal towers.

The position would be precarious for another creature, but your wide, splayed feet, padded with spongy, porous flesh, grip the slimy calcification with absolute certainty. You are hanging upside down, gazing into the abyss below.

Your massive, lidless eyes, huge orbs that take up nearly a third of your angular face, are wide open. They do not see darkness. To your multifaceted vision, the cavern is a vibrant, shifting tapestry of low-light hues. You see the distinct cerulean radiation of the deep bacterial pools far below, cutting through the mist like beacons. You see the soft violet aura of the sound-dampening moss that coats the structure you cling to. You see the faint, ghostly cascade of glowing spores falling from the distant ceiling miles above, a slow-motion blizzard of light.

You are still, utterly motionless save for the rhythmic fluttering of the slit-vents beneath your jawline, filtering oxygen from the saturated air. Your breath releases as a faint, rapidly dissipated fog. You are waiting.

A minute passes, perhaps an hour. Time is measured differently here in the eternal cool.

Finally, you spot it. A minute stress fracture is forming in the crystalline lattice of the ramp just ahead of you, caused by the unrelenting weight of waterlogged moss. Water is dripping through the crack at an accelerated rate—a sharp, staccato tap that cuts through the surrounding hush.

You move. Your motion is a slow, deliberate flow. You do not jerk or scramble; you pour your elongated body across the stone. The bone-white coral plates that armor your shoulders and spine scrape softly against the architecture you mimic, a sound immediately swallowed by the damp air. Wisps of fine green moss growing from the seams of your own biological armor brush against the city's identical covering.

Reaching the fracture, you unhook a curved tool fashioned from the hardened core of an ancient fungus from your belt of woven root-fiber. With delicate, practiced movements, you scrape away the excess moss around the wound in the stone. The crustacean grazing nearby—a creature the size of a large hound, mottled white and grey—pauses its slow chewing to watch you with stalked eyes, then dismisses you as part of the scenery and resumes its meal.

Once the area is clear, you lean close. You open your small, lipless mouth and allow thick, viscous saliva to pool on your tongue. You carefully drip this enzyme into the fracture. It hisses faintly as it hits the mineral-rich water, reacting instantly to bind the silica and calcium, sealing the crack with a fresh layer of glittering, instant rock.

The immediate danger averted, you linger. You run a webbed hand over the repair, feeling the smoothness of the new growth against your sensitive palm. It is good work. The city grows, it breaks, and you guide it back to wholeness.

A tremor runs through your lean frame—not fear, just the perpetual shiver necessary to keep circulation moving in this near-freezing world. The cold is biting deeper now. It is time to return to the communal hive, to find the warmth of others pressed together in the dark.

You detach yourself from the overhang, flipping upright in mid-air with fluid grace before landing silently on the ramp's surface. You begin the long, slow climb down toward the cluster of bulbous dwellings glowing softly in the distance. As you walk, you pass another of your kind ascending. You do not speak. Instead, the bioluminescent patch on your chest pulses a slow, deep blue three times. *The path is mended. The water flows correctly.*

The other pauses, their massive eyes reflecting the ambient glow, and pulses back two quick flashes on their palm. *Acknowledgement. Return to warmth.*

You continue your descent into the mist, a silent gardener tending a world of freezing stone and light, carrying the weight of the cavern on your armored back.
