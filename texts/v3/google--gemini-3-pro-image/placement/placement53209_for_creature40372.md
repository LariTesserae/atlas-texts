The air tastes of wet pennies and old stone. You are clinging to the underside of a massive, sweat-slicked iron girder, high in the vaulted gloom of the chamber. The cold of the metal seeps directly through your carapace.

You are a scavenger of the biofilm, a crustacean adapted to this lightless, drowning cathedral.

Your body is small, perhaps the size of a human hand, and armored in a shell that is less grown than accumulated. Like the pipes you traverse, you are translucent but heavily calcified, a living architecture built from mineral deposits harvested from the weeping water. You are a piece of the room that has learned to walk.

Your world is defined not by sight—the sickly orange bulbs and bruises of violet fungal light are just vague patches of temperature to you—but by vibration and taste. Your many articulate legs are tipped with sensitive chemoreceptors that read the corrugated landscape of rusting metal like braille, seeking the nutritious slick of algae and bacterial slime that coats every surface.

You move with deliberate slowness. Haste is dangerous here. Haste sends tremors through the water-heavy air and across the connected metal skeletons. You scuttle across the face of a valve wheel frozen in time by rust, your complex mouthparts scraping away the sludge. Nearby, a flat, segmented creature that looks exactly like a patch of wet moss detaches from the granite wall and slides imperceptibly toward a richer grazing spot. You ignore each other; you are fellow grazers in the slow decay.

Below you, far below in the profound darkness of the sump pools, you feel the heavy, smooth turbulence of a giant pale eel turning in the water. It is a deep, rolling vibration that signals "not threat, merely neighbor."

But then, a different vibration reaches you through the girder.

It is a grinding sound, low and terrible, felt deep in your joints. It is the sound of iron scraping against granite, massive and ponderous. You immediately stop your grazing and press flat against the metal, locking your calcified limbs, becoming just another nodule of rust on the beam.

Below, near where a massive arch meets the weeping stone wall, a pile of industrial wreckage is shifting. What appeared to be a mound of corroded slag heaps draped in pale roots is rising. It is colossal. The "wreckage" breathes. It is armored in the same thick iron oxide that you taste on the air. Deep fissures in its armor pulse with the faint, sickly violet glow of fungi that thrive in its creases, perfectly camouflaging it against the dormant machinery.

It is one of the apex residents, waking from a weeks-long stillness to adjust its ambush position. A shovel-shaped head, eyeless and scarred with rust, turns slowly toward the water. A thick, heavily armored limb ending in rusted claws twitches, sending a sharp *CLANG* through the metal structure that nearly vibrates you loose from your perch.

You hold fast, tasting the intense metallic spike of fear in the damp air, waiting for the mountain of rust and shadow to settle back into geology.
