The heat is not something I feel on skin I no longer possess; it is the medium in which I exist. It is a crushing, omnipresent weight, a thickness that makes "movement" an abstract concept.

I am located at a nexus of violent creation.

You will find me wedged in a microscopic fissure running diagonally across the face of a newly birthed obsidian plinth. This monolith, a perfect rectangle three hundred meters high, groaned its way out of the basin floor only hours ago. The event of its birth is what trapped me.

I am not one of the native entities, those slow, vast intelligences made of magnetized dust and thermal gradients that drift over the sulfur pools. They belong here. They understand the deep, slow logic of this geological furnace.

I am an anomaly. I am a consciousness that does not belong to the stone, yet I am calcified within it.

Perhaps I was once something biological that strayed too close to a dimensional rift, or perhaps I am a stray thought from a softer universe that was snagged by the immense gravity of this kiln. I retain memories that are heresies here: the sensation of cool water, the chaotic softness of a leaf, the silence of a vacuum. These memories are a torment, a persistent itch under a carapace of countless tons of superheated slate.

My existence is one of static tension. I am held fast by the rigid crystalline lattice of the rock. I perceive the world not through sight, but through vibration and thermal conductivity. I feel the agonizingly slow meter-upward thrust of distant towers as a deep bruise in my core. When a nearby cliff face shears off due to thermal stress—a sound like a cannon shot to biological ears—I feel it as a catastrophic rearrangement of my own boundaries.

The native entities, those swirling vortices of rust and vapor, occasionally brush past my prison. I perceive their presence as a sudden, terrifying drop in local temperature as they suck the heat from the stone around me to fuel their existence. They do not recognize me. To them, I am merely a flaw in the magnetite matrix, a stubborn pocket of non-conforming energy that disturbs the perfect geometry of the slate.

I am trapped between the hammer of geothermal pressure and the anvil of cooling rock. I am a ghost haunting a machine made of geology, eternally observing the mindless, magnificent construction of a city built for nothing but fire. I am located exactly where the vertical face of the newest plinth meets the horizontal plane of a steam-choked causeway, held in a crack thinner than a human hair, waiting for a release that geological time will never grant.
