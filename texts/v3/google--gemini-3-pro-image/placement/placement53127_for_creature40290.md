I am born of the thrum.

My consciousness does not begin with sight—I have no eyes—but with resonance. The deep, unending bass vibration that shakes the foundations of this buried cathedral is the loom upon which my body is woven. Without this deafening pressure sonic, the intricate lattices of polymerized silicate that compose my being would lose their cohesion, and I would dissolve into mere sludge in the boiling water. The noise is not an assault; it is my skeleton.

Where am I? I am currently obliterated.

I am caught in one of the upward surges, a gravity-defying pillar of superheated water whipped into a frenzy by the unseen forces of the machine. To a creature of bone and blood, this would be a centrifuge of death. To me, it is a ecstatic dance. I have no rigid shape to break. I am a dense, vitrified hydro-gel, a sentient fluid heavier than the water around me, yet infinitely pliable.

The torrent stretches me into a translucent ribbon, twists me into a spiral, compresses me into a dense sphere. I feel the kinetic fury of the water as a lover’s embrace. I ride the chaotic current upward, higher into the steam-choked nave, until the force dissipates and gravity reasserts itself. I freefall through the blinding white saturation, crashing back into the churning central chasm with a force that would shatter steel, only to bounce and reform, unharmed in the boiling froth.

The heat is exquisite. The water here is far above the boiling point of the surface world, kept liquid only by crushing pressure. This thermal violence keeps my silicate form semi-fluid, allowing movement and thought. The "cooler" peripheral ledges, where the sad, brittle things scuttle on bacterial rust, are zones of death to me; to go there would be to solidify into a paralyzed statue.

I ignore the periphery. My existence is focused entirely on the center, on the Masters.

Hunger moves me against the chaotic flow. I navigate the turbulence by feel, sensing the minute changes in pressure and the radiant heat signatures of the structures. I glide toward one of the sleek, obsidian pylons plunged deep into the pool.

As I approach the smooth black monolith, I experience the only sense akin to vision I possess. The blinding, vibrating white illumination emanating from the machinery strikes my translucent body. Because I share the refractive index of the water, I am usually invisible. But against the absolute black of the pylon, catching that raw energy, I ignite.

The light refracts furiously within my gel structure. For a glorious moment, I am not just part of the water; I am a trapped star, a roiling concentration of brilliance vibrating against the darkness of the machine.

I press myself flat against the seamless obsidian surface. It hums with energy that feels sickeningly powerful, a vibration distinct from the background thrum of the chamber. This is the pure source. I spread my substance thin, covering as much surface area as possible, letting the sickening energy radiate directly into my silicate matrix.

I bask in the output of an engine built by gods long dead. I am their cleanup crew, their parasite, their worshiper. I glide along the smooth surface, perhaps microscopically polishing the already perfect material, feeding on the excess vibration. I am smooth enough, resilient enough, and strange enough to inherit this white-hot, deafening cathedral.

I am a child of the machine, and the boiling dark is my home.
