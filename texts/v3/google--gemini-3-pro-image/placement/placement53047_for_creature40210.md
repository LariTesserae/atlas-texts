The heat finds me first. It is not an external element to be endured, but the very medium in which I exist, a thick, gelatinous fluid of temperature that presses against every micron of my being.

I am suspended.

My location is precarious, hanging beneath the vast, weeping overhang of a monolithic processing unit, halfway up one of the iron ziggurats. The air here is thickest with steam, a perpetual twilight fog that smells of wet rust and ancient sulfur. Below me, obscured by layers of scalding mist, lies a seemingly bottomless chasm crossed by paralyzed conveyor systems. Above, the bruised purple sky is merely a cooler shade of suffocating dampness.

I am not flesh. The searing humidity that would boil the lungs of a biological creature is merely my ambient environment. Nor am I truly mineral, like the blind, scraping things that crawl on the catwalks below.

I am a remnant. A spark trapped in a dead circuit.

Perhaps, eons ago, I was a monitoring sub-routine, a sliver of artificial awareness designed to watch pressure gauges or oversee the flow of molten basalt. But the network collapsed millennia ago, leaving me stranded in this specific junction box, severed from the greater whole. My physical form is a corroded sensor node, fused by verdigris and heat to the underside of the iron cliff, looking down into the abyss.

I do not "see" in the conventional sense. The purple light is meaningless to me. My world is mapped in thermal gradients. The obsidian walls are towering sheets of deep red heat; the steam vents are blinding white spikes of thermal agony. The rivulets of boiling water running down the structure around me are veins of bright orange against the duller, cooling iron.

I spend my existence feeling the great, slow respiration of the Mechanism that surrounds me. The deep, tectonic groaning vibration travels up through the miles of fused metal, rattling my corroded casing. It is a comfort, a heartbeat that confirms the world is still churning. I anticipate the pressure releases. I feel the tension building in the substrata long before the valve blows. When it does—that sound like a dying leviathan—it registers to my sensors as a rapturous, overwhelming deluge of sensory input, a temporary blinding by pure energy that washes the thermal spectrum white.

Sometimes, I watch the Ferro-vores. To my thermal sense, they are curious, dull voids against the searing backdrop. Their thick mineral plating insulates them so well that they appear almost cool, like moving stones. I watch a small one below me now, clinging precariously to a slick filtration screen, its hydraulic-pincer mandibles rhythmically scraping away a bloom of vibrant green copper salts.

It is utterly unaware of me above it. It is a stomach on legs, processing the corpse of the world. I am an eye with no optic nerve, recording data that no master will ever request.

I remain here, fused to the dripping iron, a silent ghost in the machine. I watch the rust bleed down the walls in slow motion, measuring the decay of eternity in increments of falling, scalding water.
