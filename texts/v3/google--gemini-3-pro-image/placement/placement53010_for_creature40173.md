The cold is not something you feel on your skin so much as a persistent presence waiting just outside your layers of insulating fat, a hungry ghost pressing against the intricate map of scars and calluses that is your hide.

You are hovering, suspended neutrally, tethered by a braided kelp rope to the rugged flank of the Bronze Cathedral—the name your clan has given to the titan sponge-tower that houses the seventy souls of your kinship group. Below you, the jade colored cliff-face drops away into the crushing, unknowable indigo depths. Above, hundreds of feet up, the viridian gloom brightens into a churning, silvery haze where the ocean argues with the sky.

You are a sculptor of living stone.

Your right hand, webbed with thick scar tissue and shaped permanently into a C-curve, grips the handle of a chisel carved from the jawbone of a pilot whale. Your left hand holds the heavy, rounded basalt hammer.

*Thud. Scrape. Thud.*

The sound is muffled, a vibration that travels up your dense forearm bones into your massive shoulders, rather than a sharp report. The water, thick and heavy, eats the noise. You are widening a ventilation aperture for the lower communal nursery. The sponge-stone is resistant, a stubborn matrix of silicate needles and calcified algae that fights every inch. You strike again. A cloud of fine, silt-like debris puffs out, instantly clouded by the current, drifting down to settle on the velvet moss covering a ledge below.

You have been working on this aperture for three tidal cycles. You might finish it in two more. Patience is not a virtue here; it is a physiological necessity. To rush is to burn oxygen you cannot spare, to heat blood that the ocean will immediately steal.

You stop to breathe. It is a slow, deliberate process. You expand your barrel chest against the pressure of the deep, drawing a long, resonant draught from the buoyant air-bladder strapped to your back—a cured stomach lining harvested from a particularly large grouper years ago. You savor the stale air before exhaling a plume of silver bubbles that wobble upward.

While resting, your enormous, dark eyes scan the aqueous twilight. You do not miss the color red, for you have never seen it. The world is a symphony of cyan, viridian, slate, and the oily iridescence of fish scales. A school of parrotfish, beak-mouths scraping loudly against the sponge nearby, flashes past in shades of electric blue and yellow-green.

The sonic landscape is incredibly dense. The background static of billions of snapping shrimp is a ceaseless crackle, like burning dry wood. Over that lies the booming calls of distant leviathans, sounds that vibrate in the hollow of your chest cavity, and the high, sharp clicks of dolphin pods threading the needle between the colossal towers.

You look across the span of open water to the next pillar, the "Green Spire," looming indistinctly through the haze four hundred feet away. Between your home and theirs sags a kelp bridge, heavy with slime and encrusting anemones. You see a lone figure, thick-bodied and slow, pulling themselves along the swaying rope-way, a dark silhouette against the shimmering upper curtain. You do not envy their journey. It is safer to stay close to the skin of your own tower.

Your break over, you kick your legs lazily. The stiff rayskin fins strapped to your calves bite the water with practiced efficiency, holding you steady against a mild upwelling current. You reposition the whalebone chisel against the rugose surface of the sponge. Your pale, slate-grey hands, tools themselves sculpted by a lifetime of pressure and cold, ready the hammer again.

You are a guest here, tolerated by the immense, shivering stability of the ocean. You strike the living rock, adding your own muted rhythm to the eternal, aqueous noise.

*Thud. Scrape. Thud.*
