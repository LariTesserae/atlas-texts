The roar is not a sound. It is a permanent discoloration of the air, a concessive pressure that pushes against every facet of my being.

I am hefted low to the ground, heavy with an accumulated carapace that is less biology and more geology. I am six-legged, squat, and armored in glittering scars.

I find myself currently away from the warmth, out on the endless, drowning sponge of the indigo plain. Here, the world tries to unmake me. The cold is a physical weight, seeking cracks in my armor, trying to freeze the sluggish fluids that pump through my core. The wetness is absolute. Every step with my hooked claws punches through the bruised purple skin of the moss blanket, sinking into the freezing slime beneath. When I pull a leg free, the squelch vibrates up through my joints. Water streams endlessly over my multifaceted back, freezing into temporary rime before sloughing off in sheets as I move.

I am blind in the way soft things are blind. I do not see images; I perceive a fractured mosaic of light and thermal gradients through the crystalline plating over my eyes. The world is a smear of dark, oxidized greens and violent violet flashes from the sky, all filtered through the prism of my own body.

I am deaf, as nearly everything here is deaf. The roaring engine of the planet below drowned out delicate acoustics eons ago. Instead, I feel the world through my feet. The ground is a membrane vibrating with the colossal energy of the vents. I feel the dull, deep-bass thud of a massive peat-grazer miles away, chewing its endless cud. I feel the sharper, terrifying tremor of a Rime-Stalker shifting its weight in ambush nearby—a sudden cessation of vibration that is more alarming than noise.

But mostly, I feel the call of the Furnace.

I am driving myself toward the heat. I can sense it ahead—a towering chimney of calcified fungal-coral that glows dully in my thermal spectrum. It is the source of the roar.

As I approach the base of the great vent, the gravity feels treacherous, slippery. The freezing sludge gives way to steaming, bare rock. The air changes from a freezing aerosol to a scalding blast. This is the boundary line, the violent seam where I must exist to survive.

I begin to climb the chimney. The heat is ecstatic, loosening my stiffened joints. I find a perch near the jagged lip, where the superheated vapor screams out of the earth at supersonic speeds.

I brace my six legs against the shuddering stone and lean into the blast. The steam hits my carapace like a physical blow. It is agony and ecstasy. The super-saturated minerals in the vapor hit my freezing outer shell and instantly precipitate. I feel the sudden, sharp added weight as a new layer of silicate crystal flash-freezes onto my back, tightening my skin, renewing my armor.

Above me, the steam punches into the cryosphere, generating a massive electrical storm. A sheet of violet lightning rips across the zenith. The flash hits my newly formed glass skin, and for a second, I become a blinding star, refracting the chaotic light into a thousand prismatic shards, glittering coldly against the bruised sky.

I raise one heavy, crystalline foreleg and strike the vent wall hard. *Clack-CLACK-thrum.*

I send the vibration down through the burning stone, a coded message felt by my kin huddled in the crevices below: *The glass is good here. The furnace is feeding us.*
