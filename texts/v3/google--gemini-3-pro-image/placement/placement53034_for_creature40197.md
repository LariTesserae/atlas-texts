The vibration reaches you first. Before sight, before smell, before the oppressive weight of the air, there is the bone-deep thrum of the tectonic machinery. It travels up through the soles of your heavy, iron-cleated boots, shivering the tibia, rattling the ribcage, settling into a permanent, grinding resonance at the base of your skull. You do not hear the city so much as you embody its frequency.

You are three levels down from the "surface," though "surface" here only means the layer exposed to the hazy, indifferent sky. You are deep within the sedimented chaos of the conurbation, in a high-pressure zone sandwiched between two colossal, slow-turning flywheels.

The heat here is absolute. It is not merely warm; the air is a physical substance, thick as broth, supersaturated with moisture evaporating from cooling tanks and instantly re-condensing on the cold, weeping iron of the catwalks. You breathe through a brass-fitted filtration mask. Each inhalation is a labor, pulling the dense, metallic atmosphere through layers of oiled canvas. The exhaust valve clicks rhythmically with your breath—a small, personal metronome against the oceanic roar of the pistons. Inside the mask, the smell of your own recycled sweat mixes with the pervasive stench of hot grease, wet rust, and ancient, mineralized sludge.

You are bulky, encumbered by necessity. Your outer skin is a stiff, vulcanized rubber apron over armor-thick waxed leather, stained a uniform slick black by decades of exposure to oily runoff. Your gloves are enormous, mitt-like things designed for gripping slime-coated wrenches, not for fine manipulation.

You are a Maintainer of the Deep Grates.

Currently, your world is narrowed to a three-foot section of iron sluice gate that has become fouled. A mixture of mineral slag and unidentifiable organic slurry has hardened into a cement-like plug, threatening to back up a cooling line that serves a subterranean impeller.

You work with a slow, agonizingly deliberate rhythm. Speed is impossible here; the heat saps explosive energy, and the perpetually slick footing means a sudden movement could send you sliding into the churning darkness of an open gear-pit. You lift a heavy, chisel-tipped pry bar made of pitted steel. You bring it down. *Clang*. You lift it again. *Clang*.

You are not alone in this work.

Around your cleated boots, a living carpet of pale isopods ripples. They are frantic with the bounty you are liberating from the grate. You do not look down. You feel them crunch beneath your iron treads—a wet, brittle popping sensation—but there are too many to avoid, and their lives are too inconsequential to matter. They are merely a textural variation on the slippery floor.

A larger shape bullies its way through the chitinous tide. An armored crab, its carapace scarred and stained oily brown, scuttles towards the chunks of foulness you are chipping away. It is the size of a terrier, brute and ugly. It snaps a heavy claw nervously in your direction. You pause in your hammering, resting the heavy bar on the grating and turning your masked head slowly toward it. The glass eyepieces of your mask are hopelessly fogged, rendering the world in smeary gray shapes, but the creature senses your attention. It recognizes the silhouette of a larger predator in this ecosystem. It clatters backward a few steps, retreating into a shadow dripping with lukewarm condensation, waiting for you to finish your harvest before it begins its own.

You return to your labor. Lift. Strike. Lift. Strike.

You have no memory of green. You have no concept of dry. You are a specialized biological component in a geological-scale engine. Your purpose is to fight entropy in this specific, dripping corridor, to ensure the sludge keeps flowing, to ensure the great grinding heart of the plateau does not seize. The sweat running down your back inside the leather suit, the vibration in your teeth, the squelch of bugs under your boots, and the eternal hiss of steam—this is the sum total of existence. And you have much work left to do.
