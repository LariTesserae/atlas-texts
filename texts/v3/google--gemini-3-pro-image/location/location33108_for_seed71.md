The air here is thin, sharp, and carries the persistent metallic tang of ancient cold. You stand on a high plateau, but "plateau" is a misnomer. It is the flattened, eroded roof of a structure so vast it defies immediate comprehension.

This is a landscape of stone worn smooth by eons of wind and ice. The terrain is defined by colossal, rounded humps of gray gneiss and granite that stretch to a curved horizon—a continental scale of geology that is, upon closer inspection, entirely artificial. These are not mountains; they are the ossified remains of a city built on a planetary scale, now ground down by deep time into something resembling a natural formation. The original architecture is lost, the hard edges bevelled into soft curves, the canyons between towers widened into glacial valleys. It is a world settled into its final form, an urban skeleton polished by eternity.

It is intensely cold. The temperature rarely rises above freezing, yet the environment is pervasive with a strange dampness. It does not rain; instead, the air itself seems to sweat. Rime ice coats every windward surface in intricate, glittering carapaces. In the deep, shadowed gorges between the monolithic structures, where the sun never reaches, banks of freezing fog throb like a slow pulse.

Despite the cold and the stone, there are pockets of startling, almost aggressive vegetation. In the leeward shelters of the great stone humps, where moisture collects and is protected from the biting wind, life explodes in dense, compact riots. Walls of furred moss, easily ten feet thick, cling to vertical cliff faces, ranging in color from deep emerald to bruised purple. Hardy, stunted trees with trunks like twisted iron cables root themselves in unseen fissures, their leaves waxy and thick to retain heat. These pockets of lushness are sparse, separated by miles of barren, ice-glazed rock, appearing like vibrant green cicatrices on a dead gray body.

There is no animal life. The silence is profound. There are no bird calls, no buzzing insects, no scuttling feet. The only sounds are the inanimate groans of the world itself: the sharp *crack-boom* of frost shattering stone, the deep subterranean grinding of the ancient foundations shifting, and the endless, hollow whistle of the wind sluicing through canyons ten miles deep.

The human presence here is tiny, huddled, and primitive. Small communities cling to the sheltered crevices like barnacles on a dead whale. They build lean-tos of scavenged stone shards and weave thick textiles from the moss fibers. Their technology is stone-age—hand-chipped blades of flint, hammers of basalt, fire struck from pyrite. They huddle around dim, smoky fires in the guts of structures built by gods they cannot conceive of.

And then there is the wrongness.

The "weather" here is not just meteorological; it is ontological. The ancient mechanisms buried deep within the world-city are failing, causing reality to break down in active, dynamic cycles.

As you watch, a shimmer passes over a distant mountain-building. For a moment, the massive stone structure seems to fold in on itself, geometry twisting into impossible, non-Euclidean angles before snapping back with a soundless concussion that vibrates in your teeth.

In some valleys, gravity is merely a suggestion. Enormous boulders of ice and rock float lazily mid-air, suspended in invisible, fluctuating fields, occasionally drifting together to fuse with a wet crunch. The damp air sometimes solidifies into transient, shimmering curtains that show views of places that do not exist—a sun-drenched jungle, a burning star—before dissolving back into freezing mist.

The landscape is actively churning under the influence of this breakdown. You see a glacier of blue ice flowing *uphill*, pushed by a reversed gravitational current, grinding away a cliff face that has stood for a million years. A patch of lush moss accelerates its growth cycle ten-thousandfold in a localized time-warp, blooming, fruiting, dying, and turning to dust in the span of a minute.

It is a high, cold, quiet tomb of a world, vast and dead, yet twitching with the terrifying, active dreams of its long-gone architects.
