This is the **Desiccated Lattice**.

It is a world suspended in a high, cold void, a place where "ground" is a relative term. The landscape is not geological; it is biological. The entire visible reality is composed of a colossal, sprawling network of petrified plant matter, a tangle of roots and branches grown to a continental scale. There is no bedrock, only an endless, woven architecture of lignified cellulose and silicate structures hanging in a thin, frigid atmosphere.

The scale is vertiginous. The main "arteries" of this structure are dozens of miles in diameter, curved horizons of pale, bone-dry wood that look more like ancient canyon floors than branches. From these, secondary limbs the size of mountain ranges branch off, creating a chaotic, three-dimensional web that stretches to the vanishing point in all directions.

Yet, despite the fact that vegetation constitutes the entire environment, the impression is one of sparseness. The gaps between these immense branches are vast abysses of empty air. You could fall for hours before striking a lower limb. The density of matter is low; it is a world composed mostly of open space, framed by gigantic, arid scaffolding.

The light is punishingly bright. A fierce, unblinkered star dominates the upper sky, casting razored shadows. There is no moisture to soften the light, no atmospheric haze to diffuse it. The colors are bleached and mineral: ochres, burnt umbers, chalky whites, and the oxidized copper-green of ancient, hardened sap. It is a hyper-desiccated environment. There is zero free water. The very concept of "wetness" does not exist here. The air is so dry it crackles with static electricity, snapping against any movement.

The dominant feature of this world, however, is the noise. It is deafening, a physical assault on the senses. The Desiccated Lattice is not dead wood; it is a living, bio-industrial engine running at maximum capacity.

The vegetation has evolved to function as industrial technology. The great limbs are not just structural; they are conduits and factories. Beneath the bark-like exterior, which is scarred and scoured by aeons of abrasive wind erosion, lies a frenetic internal mechanism. You can feel the vibration through the soles of your feet—a deep, geological thrumming.

Great crystalline nodes jut from the branches, acting as solar collectors, channeling the intense sunlight into internal processes. The sounds are mechanical yet organic: the grinding roar of gigantic gears made of hardened resin, the high-pitched whine of conductive filaments straining under electrical loads, and the rhythmic, thunderous pumping of non-aqueous fluids—perhaps liquid methane or pressurized silica slurry—rushing through veins the size of subway tunnels.

The environment is violently dynamic. The energy coursing through the Lattice creates its own weather. Sudden, intense electrical storms explode between the branches, arcs of lightning kilometers long jumping across the voids, accompanied by thunder that shakes the entire structure. Parts of the lattice are in a constant state of turbulent growth and self-destruction; massive splinters of wood, large as skyscrapers, shear off under internal pressure and tumble silently into the depths, while elsewhere, rapid, crystalline growth spurs erupt from the surface with the sound of shrieking metal.

Despite the chaos and the lack of water, fauna is abundant, though adapted to this deafening, electrified ecosystem.

The creatures here are armored and deeply strange. There are "resistor-mites"—crustacean-like beings the size of trucks with shells of conductive ceramic, grazing on the piezoelectric moss that grows around energy leaks. Swarms of "spark-wing" gliders, possessing translucent, foil-like wings, ride the thermal updrafts generated by the industrial heat of the branches, their bodies flashing as they catch the static discharges in the air. Deep within the crevasses of the larger limbs, parasitic organisms tap directly into the flow of industrial saps, their metabolisms powered by the chemical slurry meant for the Lattice's own growth.

It is a world of blinding light, freezing shadows, and an unending, roaring turbulence, where life is built not on water, but on electricity, friction, and dust.
