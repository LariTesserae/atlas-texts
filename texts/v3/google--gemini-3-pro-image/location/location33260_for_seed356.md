This world is a limitless, suffocating basin, wrapped in eternal night. The air is thick, warmer than blood, and saturated with moisture that refuses to fall as rain. Instead, it hangs as a heavy, invisible suspension, coating every surface in a perpetual, slick sheen. There is no sky, only an upward continuation of the density that fills the below.

There is no earth here, no geology in the traditional sense. The entire landscape, stretching beyond the horizon in all directions and plunging deep into uncharted depths, is composed of a single, monolithic biological entity. It is a total vegetation event where the plants have become the substrate, the architecture, and the atmosphere.

But these are not soft leaves and yielding stems. This is a petrified engine room grown from cellulose and lignin. The terrain is a dense, tangled junction of rigid, industrial forms that mimic machinery on a continental scale. Massive, sharp-edged formations of iron-hard timber rise like turbine housings and cooling fins out of the dark. They look freshly formed, their edges razor-keen, as if extruded from a titanic press only moments ago, yet they are utterly motionless.

Great fluted columns of vascular tissue, thick as refinery towers, act as silent conduits. They are densely packed, layering over one another in a chaotic yet functional lattice. Through their translucent, woody outer sheaths, a viscous, amber sap sluggishly circulates. This fluid glows with a faint, sickly copper light—the only illumination in the endless dark—revealing the outlines of gears that will never turn and pistons fused to their cylinders by their own growth. The faint electrical hum of this conductive sap is so low it is felt in the bone rather than heard.

The "industry" here is biological metabolism scaled to a planetary level, then frozen in perfect equilibrium. It is a vast, silent manufactory of stasis. No leaves rustle, for there is no wind. No animals crawl across the slick surfaces; the ecology is pure, uncontested flora, devoid of predators or prey.

The only sound breaking the heavy silence is liquid. The condensation on the "machinery" pools and eventually becomes too heavy, sliding down the polished wood with a viscous slur, landing years later on a lower platform with a muffled, wet thud. Nothing grows, nothing rots. The system has achieved a terrifying stability, a warm, wet, dark eternity of bio-mechanical forms held in everlasting suspension.
