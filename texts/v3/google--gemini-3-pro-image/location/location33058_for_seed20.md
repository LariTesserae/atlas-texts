The primary sensation is sound. It is a physical pressure, a roar so absolute it ceases to be noise and becomes a medium you exist within, denser than the liquid itself. It is the sound of a trillion tons of water grinding against itself in a space too small to contain it.

You are submerged in a sphere of violent, tepid ocean suspended in a high-altitude void. This is not a sea on a planet's surface; it is a cathedral-sized globule of roiling water hanging in a dark, atmospheric nether-region, held together by forces that defy conventional physics. There is no "up" to break the surface of; the boundary of this aquatic pocket is a chaotic membrane where churning water meets vacuum, constantly flashing into explosive steam and instantaneously collapsing back into liquid. This perpetual cavitation is the source of the deafening, bone-shaking roar.

The water itself is warm, the temperature of blood, and murky with silt and pulverized minerals. Visibility is low, a perpetual twilight illuminated only by sudden, short-lived flashes of sonoluminescence—cavity bubbles imploding with such force they generate light—and a faint, bruised purple glow emanating from the center of the sphere.

At the heart of this turbulent centrifuge is a jagged spire of rock. It is young geology, raw and razor-sharp, a sliver of obsidian that looks like it cooled only yesterday, thrusting upwards (though "up" is relative here) for perhaps three hundred feet. It is the only stable point in the churning chaos. The density of things is low; apart from the water and this single rock needle, there is mostly terrifying emptiness.

Clinging precariously to the sheltered leeside of the obsidian spire are the only signs of "vegetation." They are not plants, but brittle, crystalline growths, chemosynthetic lattices that mimic the form of scorched coral, feeding on the mineral-rich turbulence. They shatter easily in the currents, their shards adding to the abrasive slurry.

Scattered along the sharper ridges of the central rock are markings. They are not structures, but deep, precise gouges that form spiraling patterns, too regular to be natural fractures. They glow with that faint, violet bioluminescence. They feel pre-tool, as if clawed by something harder than diamond, ancient sigils left by an intelligence that predates the current instability, now abraded by the endless churning.

Life is exceedingly rare. The violence of the environment forbids complexity. Yet, occasionally, a shape moves through the murk that is not rock or current. It is a colossal, slow-moving entity, perhaps the only one of its kind trapped in this sphere. It is a smooth, eel-like thing the size of a subway train, eyeless and gray. It does not swim against the current; it rides the chaotic knots of pressure, its body undulate and durable as cured rubber. It feeds on kinetic energy, pressing its massive flank against the areas of highest turbulence, absorbing the brutal force that would pulp anything else.

The rules here are fluid. Gravity is a suggestion, fluctuating wildly as the sphere rotates and pulses. Sometimes the rock spire is "down," and sometimes the center of the sphere pulls you in, and sometimes the violent boundary layer tries to eject you into the void. It is a place of perpetual creation and destruction, a womb of violent water that will never birth anything but more noise.
