This is the Roof of the World, a place of paralyzing altitude and blinding clarity. It is a landscape defined not by what is there, but by the sheer, crushing volume of the empty space surrounding it, and the single, overwhelming force that occupies that void.

The terrain is vast, a regional expanse of incredibly ancient mountains that have been ground down from jagged peaks into smooth, undulating ridges and immense, rounded plateaus. The scale is deceptive; what looks like a nearby hill is a day’s hard travel away across a valley of polished stone. The rock itself is granite and gneiss, stripped bare of soil, weathered not by tumbling water but by the aeons-long abrasion of air. Every surface is slick, rounded, and cool to the touch.

It is incredibly bright. The sun, unimpeded by thick atmosphere, strikes the stone with a savage, ultraviolet intensity. The light is hard, white, and merciless, casting shadows blacker than ink. Yet, beneath this searing light, the air remains persistently cool, thin, and incapable of holding warmth.

The defining character of this place, the thing that vibrates in your teeth and bone, is the sound. It is deafening. A ceaseless, monolithic roar that obliterates all other senses.

This is the home of the Eternal Gale. The atmosphere here does not gust or swirl; it flows in a constant, high-velocity torrent, a solid river of air hammering the mountains without pause, forever. This wind is the architect of the smooth terrain, sandblasting the mountains into submission over geological timescales. It is a dynamic system locked in a terrified equilibrium—the force never changes, the direction never shifts, the volume never drops. It is a frozen violence.

Because of the wind, the "damp" parameter manifests strangely. There are no standing pools or running streams—the wind would atomize them instantly. Instead, moisture arrives when clouds from the unseen lower world smash against the windward cliffs. The water is instantly shredded into a superfine, high-speed mist that slicks the rock faces, creating treacherous, microscopically thin layers of ice and water in sheltered overhangs before being sublimated back into the gale.

Vegetation is almost nonexistent. In deep, mesmerizingly smooth bowls carved out of the leeward stone, where the wind howls over the top like a flute, tiny, incredibly tough cushions of silvery moss and crustose lichens cling to life, feeding on the scant moisture that drips from the condensation above. They are rock-hard, slow-growing, and look more mineral than vegetable.

Yet, paradoxically, the air is alive. The fauna is abundant, but it is entirely aerial. The sky is filled with creatures evolved to exist solely within the roaring current. Great, translucent membrane-wings, some the size of small gliders, hang motionless in the airstream, anchored by the perfect consistency of the wind. They are filter-feeders of the sky. They do not flap; they ride the infinite gale, mouths agape, harvesting unseen aerial plankton, dust, and organic detritus blown up from the forgotten lands below. They are silent creatures, their own calls lost in the roaring void, living their entire lives on the wing, mating and giving birth in the crushing slipstream. Their shadows race across the bright, empty rock below, the only things moving in a landscape paralyzed by its own noise.
