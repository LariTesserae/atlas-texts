This is a high place, thrust up into a thinning atmosphere where the light of the sun is not merely bright, but surgically piercing. It is an immense, elevated basin, a vast amphitheater of stone that stretches for miles, cupped toward a zenith of deep, violet-blue sky.

There is no green here. Not a spore of moss, not a blade of high-altitude grass, not even the stain of lichen. The world is entirely mineral, a geology of slick, polished surfaces. The bedrock, a dense, metamorphic slate ranging from charcoal gray to bruised plum, is universally wet. It does not seem to rain, yet everything is saturated. Moisture weeps continuously from microscopic fissures in the stone, coating every surface in a shimmering, viscous sheen. Shallow pools of perfectly clear, motionless water reflect the blinding sky like scattered mirrors, never draining, never overflowing.

The basin is not empty. It is densely, claustrophobicly packed with formations. Rising from the wet bedrock are countless thousands of pillars, spires, and bulbous mounds of mineral accretion. They are crowded together like a petrified forest, leaving only narrow, winding corridors barely wide enough for a person to squeeze through. These structures are built from the mineral-rich weep of the ground, layer upon microscopic layer deposited over eons—calcium carbonates, silicates, and iron oxides that have dried and re-wetted into a landscape of bleeding rusts, sulfur yellows, and coppery greens. They are smooth to the touch, slick as wet glass, and cold.

The air temperature is brisk, eternally hovering just above freezing. Your breath condenses instantly in the thick, humid air, adding to the perpetual, translucent mist that hangs between the stone pillars, refracting the intense sunlight into sudden, blinding rainbows that stand still in the air.

But the defining characteristic of this place, the thing that vibrates in your chest and jawbone, is the sound.

It is deafening. A monolithic, ceaseless roar that saturates the air, leaving no acoustic space unfilled. It is not the chaotic crashing of waves or the varied howl of a storm. It is a single, sustained, unending note of immense power—a geological frequency generated by high-altitude winds being forced through a massive, unseen natural vent somewhere in the surrounding peaks, magnified by the basin's perfect acoustic geometry. The sound pressure is immense; it feels thick, almost gelatinous, contributing to the sensation of extreme density. You cannot speak here. You cannot think clearly over the drone.

And nothing changes. The shadows do not seem to lengthen, for the sun, high overhead, appears fixed in its intensity. The water does not flow; it exists in perpetual seepage. The sound never varies in pitch or volume. The landscape is locked in a terrifyingly stable equilibrium of blinding light, pervasive wetness, and crushing noise.

Only rarely does life register against this static backdrop. Perhaps, in the deepest crux between two dripping iron-spires, you might see a slow movement. A creature, no larger than a thumb, encased in a segmented shell that mimics the mineral colors perfectly, slowly grazing on the chemical film of the wet rocks. It has no eyes, and certainly no ears, existing solely to consume the weeping stone in this bright, roaring, endless stasis.
