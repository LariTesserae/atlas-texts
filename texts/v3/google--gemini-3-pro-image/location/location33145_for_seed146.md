This is a realm of interiority, an immense hollow jewel buried unimaginable distances beneath a planetary crust. There is no sky, only a ceiling lost in the profound, velvety gloom, kilometers overhead.

The dominant sensation is a dry, pervasive warmth, like the breath inside a kiln that has slowly cooled for a millennium. The air is thick, heavy, and utterly devoid of moisture; it tastes of hot iron, sulfur, and pulverized rock. Your skin immediately tightens, the dry heat pulling at the moisture in your eyes and throat.

You stand upon a surface that defies the softness of earth. The terrain is a chaotic, glittering expanse of razor-sharp crystalline formations. This is a world geologically "young," or perhaps suspended in the moment of its violent creation. It looks as if a titanic explosion shattered the bedrock, and the resulting fractures were instantly frozen in time.

Every surface is facets. Massive spires of translucent mineral, ranging from pale amber to deep violet, thrust upwards like a petrified forest of spears. They are so densely packed that navigating between them requires squeezing through narrow, glittering chasms, constantly risking laceration on edges sharp enough to split a hair. The density of matter here is overwhelming; there is no open space, only layers upon layers of crystalline growth crowding the vision.

It is dark. The primary light source is the faint, sickeningly green phosphorescence that emanates slowly from certain veins of rock, casting long, unsettling shadows that never move. This weak glow reveals the sheer scale of the place; looking out, the field of jagged crystal stretches into the blackness like an infinite, inverted mountain range. Far in the distance, hazy clouds of ultra-fine mineral dust hang motionless in the static air, evidence of a weather system that has ceased to function.

Despite the aridity and the lack of any recognizable vegetation, life is abundant here, though it is life adapted to a mineral existence. The "fauna" are strange, armored things. Great, segmented creatures, like sightless, chitinous millipedes the size of railway cars, move with agonizing slowness across the crystal fields. They do not eat flesh or plants; they graze on the rock itself, their diamond-hard mandibles scraping away mineral rime with a sound like grinding glass, which is often the only noise breaking the immense, stifled silence. Other, flatter creatures, resembling translucent manta rays made of mica, cling to the warmer vents in the floor, absorbing geothermal heat directly.

There are signs of intelligence here, though it is scattered and primitive. Wedged into the sheltered overhangs of larger crystal formations are crude lean-tos. They are constructed not of wood, but of large, flat slabs of cooler, opaque minerals that have been laboriously pried loose and stacked together. You might find a tool left behind: a heavy chisel made from a dark, volcanic glass, strapped to a handle of braided mineral fiber. These structures are few and far between, swallowed by the glittering vastness of the landscape. The inhabitants, whoever they are, must possess tough hides to survive the ambient sharpness of their world.

The overwhelming impression is one of stasis. There is no day or night cycle to mark the passage of time. The temperature never fluctuates. The dust clouds do not drift. The air does not move. It is a magnificent, terrifying, glittering tomb, preserved perfectly in dry heat and eternal dark.
