This is a place defined by an almost unbearable antiquity. It is a high landscape, a vast plateau raised significantly above the surrounding world, but it possesses no peaks or jagged edges. Eons of existence have ground everything down to the lowest energy state. The terrain is a seemingly endless expanse of polished bedrock, undulating in gentle, flattened waves like a sea frozen in stone. These are not boulders; they are the rounded skulls of ancient mountains, worn smooth by wind and time until they resemble enormous, fused cobblestones, some the size of houses, others the size of cities.

The rock itself is warm to the touch, radiating heat absorbed from a sun that feels closer here than in the lowlands. The colors are muted and earthy: deep ochres, burnt umbers, and bruised purples, all covered in a slight, oily sheen.

Despite the pervasive warmth and the dominance of bare stone, the air is heavy and strangely damp. There is rarely rain. Instead, the moisture exhales from the earth itself during the cooler nights, rising as thick, clinging mists that coat every surface in heavy dew. By midday, the sun burns this away, leaving behind isolated pockets of lukewarm water trapped in the smooth hollows of the rock—ephemeral silver mirrors dotted across the bronze landscape. In the deepest shadows of crevasses, a perpetual, slick condensation remains.

Vegetation is almost nonexistent, unable to find purchase on the impenetrable stone. There is no soil, only dust. Where the damp lingers long enough, crusty patches of highly specialized lichen cling like rust stains—mustard yellows and oxidized greens that feed directly on the minerals of the rock and the trace moisture in the air.

The density of this world does not come from plants, but from life. The fauna is overwhelming, yet uncanny in its silence.

The stone surface is alive. It is a shifting mosaic of countless creatures, a living carpet that covers nearly every square meter of the polished ground. They are slow, armored things, built for patience and conservation. There are chitinous entities the size of dinner plates, flat and segmented, that inch across the rock face, their shells perfectly mimicking the mineral patterns beneath them. There are soft-bodied, slug-like behemoths that leave glistening trails of rapidly drying mucus, grazing on the microscopic bacterial films brought by the morning dew.

There is no buzzing, no calling, no roar of predation. The interactions are chemical and tactile. Creatures communicate via pheromone trails and subtle vibrations transmitted through the stone. A herd of thousands of many-legged, crystalline-shelled grazers might shift direction in perfect unison, a silent wave undulating across a valley floor, responding to a signal undetectable to human senses. The primary sound is a low, pervasive friction—the soft hushing sound of billions of tiny legs and bodies moving against smooth rock, like sand pouring through an hourglass.

Scattered across this living geology are the faintest echoes of a built past. They are so integrated into the erosion that they are easy to miss. A pillar of rock might be just slightly too symmetrical to be natural, its edges rounded into a perfect cylinder by millions of years of weathering. A line of shallow depressions on a ridge might suggest a foundation of a wall laid down before the current species had evolved. These remnants are not ruins in the traditional sense; they are fossils of architecture, dissolved almost entirely back into the geology they came from. They suggest a time before the silence, before the flattening, but they offer no answers.

The passage of time here is agonizingly slow. A major event is the cracking of a rock surface due to thermal stress, a sound like a gunshot that might occur once a decade. The life cycles of the creatures are immense; they move slowly, grow slowly, and die slowly, their bodies calcifying and becoming part of the dust that polishes the next generation of stone. It is a high, warm, crowded, and silent tomb, teeming with a life that has forgotten how to hurry.
