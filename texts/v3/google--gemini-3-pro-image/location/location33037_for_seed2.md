This place is less an ocean and more a container of incomprehensible magnitude. It is a submerged void, a hollowed-out space within a crust of solid matter, filled entirely with cool, pressurized water.

There is no natural substrate here. If one were to swim down for days, they would never reach a seabed of sand or basalt. The "bottom," "top," and "sides" of this realm are colossal feats of industrial architecture that recede into the aquatic distance. The scale is continental; it is a tank the size of a small sea, completely artificial.

The density of matter is terrifyingly low. A traveler here spends most of their time suspended in vast, terrifyingly clear blue-green water, miles away from the nearest solid object. The water itself is not murky deep-sea darkness; it is strangely, brightly illuminated. Powerful, functional industrial floodlights are bolted onto distant structures, casting immense beams that crisscross the void like searchlights in fog. The light is uniform, functional, and harsh, revealing everything with surgical clarity.

When a structure finally looms out of the blue, its artificial nature is undeniable. These are not ruins. The materials look new, sharp-edged, and free of significant corrosion. There is very little erosion; every bolt head, every girder flange, every weld seam is distinct and crisp.

Enormous pipes, wide enough to drive a train through, run in bundles across distances so vast they look like threads. Massive intakes suck water with the force of a waterfall, feeding unseen subterranean processes, while equally large vents expel heated, turbulent plumes that create artificial currents strong enough to tumble whales. The architecture is brutalist and functional: vast lattices of girders supporting smooth, featureless walls of composite metal that stretch upwards into the lit haze. There are gear systems the size of city blocks that rotate with agonizing slowness, their purpose obscured by their sheer size.

The environment is deafening. Water conducts sound perfectly, and this world is filled with the cacophony of heavy industry. The thrum of massive pumps is a constant pressure on the eardrums. Metal clanks against metal with the resonance of church bells, carrying for dozens of miles. The rushing sound of water flowing through constrictions creates a white noise that underpins everything.

Despite the artificiality, life is abundant here. The ecosystem has adapted to the metal reef. Vegetation is sparse—limited mostly to slick, brownish films of chemosynthetic algae that coat areas near heat vents, or stubborn, metallic-shelled barnacles that cluster around light sources. But the fauna thrives. Immense schools of silvery, tuna-like fish flash in the artificial light, migrating along the pipelines. Crustaceans the size of cars, with heavy, calcified armor, cling to vertical girders, grazing on the algal films. Predatory cephalopods hide in the perfectly square shadows cast by I-beams, waiting for prey to drift past in the man-made currents.

It is a world of cold water, bright light, loud metal, and vast, terrifying emptiness, where a thriving ecosystem clings to the machinery of an unknown purpose.
