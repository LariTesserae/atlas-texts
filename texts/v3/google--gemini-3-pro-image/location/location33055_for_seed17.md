This is the Cryo-Acoustic Suspension Zone.

You are submerged, but not in a seated ocean. You are thousands of miles above any planetary surface, encased in a gargantuan, globe-spanning shell of supercooled liquid water suspended in a vacuum. The pressure is immense, but equilibrium is maintained by artificial means.

The temperature is permanently fixed just fractions of a degree above absolute freezing. It is a piercing, preserving cold that permeates everything. There is no organic plant life; the ecology has never known photosynthesis or greenery. The "terrain" consists of suspended mountain ranges of razor-sharp volcanic glass and newly crystallized, diamond-hard ice that have coalesced out of the medium. These formations are geologically young, jagged and brutal, untouched by currents or weathering because the medium does not naturally move.

The scale is stupefying. You can gaze for hundreds of miles through the strangely clear, blue-lit liquid—illumination filters down from an unseen, bright "surface" far above—and see nothing but the terrifying emptiness of the suspension. The density of matter is incredibly low; vast voids separate the rare clusters of jagged ice-islands.

The defining characteristic of this realm, however, is the noise. It is deafening. It is not a chaotic noise, but a singular, titanic, rhythmic thrum that vibrates through the liquid with enough force to rattle bone. It is an omnipresent, ultra-low-frequency roar that never ceases, never shifts in pitch, and never falters.

This sound is generated by the "Stabilizers." Scattered across the immense emptiness, often anchored to the largest of the floating glass-ice mountains, are colossal industrial installations. They are brutalist monuments of corroded alloys and ceramic, the size of cities, yet dwarfed by the space around them. They are automated, unmanned engines running on loops established eons ago. Massive pistons the size of skyscrapers fire into the surrounding medium with clockwork precision, generating the acoustic pressure waves necessary to keep the supercooled ocean from collapsing into ice or evaporating into the void.

Despite the cold, the noise, and the lack of vegetation, life is abundant here. It has adapted to the industrial resonance. Great translucent leviathans, kilometers long, drift through the silent voids between the screaming machines. They are mostly cartilage and fluid, sightless creatures that navigate solely by feeling the acoustic topography created by the Stabilizers. They feed on the chemical plumes vented by the engines or filter the microscopic mineral shards suspended in the water. Swarms of smaller, armored creatures cling like barnacles to the vibrating hulls of the machines, siphoning off waste heat.

It is a world of paradox: deafeningly loud yet perfectly static. The engines fire, the noise roars, the leviathans drift, but nothing ever truly changes. It is a frozen, thundering equilibrium, held in place by ancient machines screaming into the cold water.
