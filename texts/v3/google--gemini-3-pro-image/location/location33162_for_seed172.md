The location is a contained, pressurized blister clinging to the exterior of a superstructure that ascends far above any breathable atmosphere. It is a pocket-universe of heavy industry, entirely constructed, with no view of the outside world save for thick, multi-layered viewports that look out onto pure black vacuum or a blinding, cloudless sun.

The environment is lethally cold, hovering near absolute zero. There is no moisture here, no water ice. The air inside the blister is nonexistent; it is a hard vacuum. The floor, walls, and ceiling are not ground or sky, but a complex lattice of milled titanium, ceramic composites, and conductive alloys.

The space is dominated by enormous machinery—building-sized pistons, gear trains with teeth the size of cars, and conduits thick as redwood trees—but everything is arrested. It is a tableau composed at the precise moment of maximum output, then instantaneously frozen. A massive flywheel is halted mid-spin, its spokes a blur solidified into sharp metal. Pistons are caught at the apex of their strokes. The "erosion" here is not wear, but the pristine sharpness of freshly milled metal, edges keen enough to split an atom, untouched by time or friction.

Despite the total lack of atmosphere and movement, the noise is deafening. It is not a sound carried on waves of air, but a structural resonance of terrifying intensity. The entire built environment is vibrating at a frequency that screams through the bones of anyone present. It is the sound of immense, gigawatt energy trapped in a closed loop within the conductive materials, a standing wave of industrial power that has nowhere to go. Touching a wall feels like grasping a live high-tension wire; the vibration rattles teeth and blurs vision. The light is clinical, shadowless, emanating from luminescent strips embedded in the machinery, illuminating every sharp edge of the frozen tableau.

Sparse "vegetation" exists here, though it is not biological. Growing slowly from the seams of the frozen engines are dendritic fractal structures of pure superconductive silver and cobalt. They resemble jagged, metallic ferns or frost patterns magnified thousands of times, blooming in the high-energy, ultra-cold vacuum. They are brittle, incredibly sharp, and represent the only semblance of chaotic growth in the rigid, manufactured world. They, too, are perfectly still, vibrating in time with the screaming metal screaming around them.
