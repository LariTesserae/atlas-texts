### Physical Preparation
1. **Breathing and Pressure Adaptation**  
   Since Thalassic Hollow is entirely submerged (rated 2.8/3 for water), you cannot rely on natural breathing. Standard diving equipment like scuba gear might suffice for short visits, but the depth (a few hundred meters below the surface) and prolonged exposure require advanced solutions. Consider the following:
   - **Rebreather Technology:** Invest in a closed-circuit rebreather system modified for extended underwater use. These recycle exhaled air, reducing the need for bulky tanks, and can sustain you for hours or days. Ensure it’s rated for the pressure at these depths (consult a multiverse-tech expert for gear capable of handling mineral-heavy water).
   - **Pressure Resistance Training:** Train your body to adapt to the pressure through gradual acclimation in a hyperbaric chamber before departure. While the pressure isn’t abyssal, it’s significant enough to cause discomfort or injury if you’re unprepared. Focus on equalizing techniques to protect your ears and sinuses.
   - **Liquid Breathing Alternative (Optional):** If accessible through multiverse technology, consider experimental liquid breathing systems (using oxygenated perfluorocarbons). This would allow you to “breathe” the water-like medium directly, aligning more closely with the Hollow’s environment, though it requires extensive medical preparation and carries risks.

2. **Thermal Protection**  
   The water temperature (1.7/3) is warm, akin to a stagnant bath, but prolonged exposure can still sap body heat or cause skin irritation due to mineral content. Wear a full-body wetsuit or drysuit with thermal insulation, designed for long-term underwater exploration. Opt for materials resistant to corrosion from metallic residues in the water.

3. **Mobility in a Viscous Medium**  
   Movement in Thalassic Hollow is sluggish due to the water’s density and viscosity. Swimming will be slow and energy-intensive, and the silty, debris-laden “ground” poses risks of entanglement or injury. Prepare with:
   - **Underwater Propulsion Device (UPD):** A personal underwater scooter or thruster will help you navigate without exhausting yourself. Choose a compact, durable model that can handle sediment and kelp entanglements.
   - **Protective Footwear:** Wear reinforced dive boots to protect against sharp debris like shattered glass and rusted metal on the uneven terrain.
   - **Training in Low-Gravity Movement:** Since water alters your perception of weight and balance, practice moving in simulated underwater environments to mimic the Hollow’s slow, deliberate pace (dynamic at 0.9/3).

4. **Health and Nutrition**  
   You won’t find edible resources in Thalassic Hollow—fauna is sparse and likely toxic due to industrial pollutants, and vegetation is alien and untested. Pack nutrient-dense, waterproof food packs (energy gels, compressed bars) and a portable desalination/filter system for hydration if your breathing gear doesn’t recycle moisture. Carry anti-contaminant medication to counteract potential exposure to polluted water or mutated microbes.
