### Physical Preparation: Equipping for the Terrain and Climate

   - **Footwear**: Sturdy hiking boots with good grip are a must. The terrain is uneven, with slick, damp stone, loose scree, and compacted earth.    - **Accessories**: Pack gloves (both lightweight for dexterity and insulated for warmth), a scarf or buff for your neck, and a hat to shield against wind and retain heat. Sunglasses might be useful for the filtered sunlight (1.9/3), which can still glare off the quartz-veined granite.

   The Hollow is small (a few hundred paces across), but its craggy cliffs, alcoves, and winding paths can disorient an unprepared traveler. The surrounding mountain range likely offers little in the way of landmarks at this altitude.  
   - **Navigation Tools**: Carry a compass and a topographic map of the area if available in your multiverse travel kit.    - **Safety Equipment**: Pack a multi-tool for minor repairs or cutting, a first aid kit for scrapes or twists on the rough terrain, and a whistle for emergencies.    - **Climbing Aids**: The tiered platforms and narrow stairways carved into the cliffs suggest some vertical exploration. A lightweight rope and carabiners could be useful for safety, though the structures are not excessively high. Avoid overloading yourself, as the thin air will make exertion more taxing.

3. **Supplies for Sustenance and Hydration**  
   With water present but not dominant (0.8/3), you cannot rely on finding potable sources in the Hollow. Vegetation and fauna are sparse (0.9/3 and 1.1/3), so foraging is not viable.

   - **Shelter**: While the trip might be a day journey, unexpected weather or a desire to linger could necessitate an overnight stay. Carry a lightweight emergency bivy or tent, rated for wind and damp conditions, and a sleeping bag suitable for near-freezing temperatures. Set up in a sheltered alcove or near the primitive stone walls for wind protection.

4. **Physical Conditioning**  
   The high elevation (1.6/3) means thinner air, which can lead to altitude sickness—headaches, nausea, or shortness of breath—for those unaccustomed to it. Prepare by engaging in cardiovascular exercise and, if possible, acclimatizing to higher altitudes in your home world or during prior travels.
