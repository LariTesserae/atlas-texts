### Physical Preparation
1. **Protective Gear for High Altitude and Cold**:
   - The Shattered Zenith sits at an extreme elevation with a thin, biting atmosphere (temperature 0.4/3). You’ll need a high-altitude insulated suit, preferably with integrated heating elements to combat the frigid conditions. Look for materials that are lightweight but thermally efficient, as movement across the vast terrain will be taxing.
   - Oxygen supplementation is critical. Carry a portable oxygen system with a mask or rebreather, designed for prolonged use in thin atmospheres. Ensure you have backup canisters, as the scale of the plateau (2.7/3) means help or resupply points will be far apart.
   - Waterproof and moisture-wicking layers are essential due to the pervasive dampness (water 1.5/3). The constant mist and seeping water will saturate unprotected gear, and frost can form quickly on damp surfaces. Boots with excellent grip and water resistance are a must for navigating the slick, jagged basalt and icy patches.

2. **Physical Conditioning**:
   - Train for endurance and altitude acclimatization before departure. The slightly lighter gravity may ease some strain, but the vast distances and uneven terrain (low erosion 0.2/3) will challenge your stamina. Practice hiking with a weighted pack on rocky, uneven surfaces to simulate the plateau’s harsh landscape.
   - Strengthen your core and legs for stability—balance will be key on the slick stone and along the translucent, rippling bridges that connect some of the alien structures.

3. **Nutrition and Hydration**:
   - Pack high-calorie, lightweight rations that can withstand freezing temperatures. Dehydrated meals or energy gels are ideal, as cooking may be difficult in the damp, windy conditions.
   - Carry a filtration system for water, as the seeping springs and troughs of icy slurry could be a source if properly treated. Be cautious of mineral content—test small amounts first, as the metallic tang of the air suggests potential contaminants.
   - Insulated water containers will prevent freezing, and a small portable heater could help if you need to melt frost or ice for hydration.
