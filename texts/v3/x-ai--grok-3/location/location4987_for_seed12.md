In the embryonic cradle of a world still folding into form, there lies a place I will call **The Mist-shrouded Heights of Veylraan**, a sprawling, half-born region suspended in a paradoxical state of liquid abundance and raw, unfinished geology. Imagine an immense plateau, elevated far above an unseen base, its edges lost in a haze of endless vapor. This high place, perched at a towering elevation, is not solid in the way we understand mountains or cliffs; rather, it is a shifting, porous expanse of barely-coalesced stone and silt, as if the earth itself were still deciding its shape. The erosion here is nearly absent—not because the land resists wear, but because it has not yet had time to be worn. It is emerging, embryonic, a landscape in the first throes of becoming, with jagged protrusions of unpolished rock jutting out like the bones of a thing not yet fleshed.

Water dominates this realm with an almost suffocating presence, scored at a near-maximum of 2.9. It is not an ocean in the traditional sense, nor a lake or river, but a pervasive, atmospheric saturation that blurs the line between liquid and air. A ceaseless, heavy mist clings to every surface, so dense that it feels like walking through a wall of rain that never quite falls. Droplets hang suspended in the air, glinting faintly in the dim light (1.1), creating a world where visibility is limited to a few paces ahead before all dissolves into a silvery-gray veil. Beneath the surface of the plateau—if one can call it a surface—pools of warm, shallow water (temperature at 2.5) accumulate in the hollows of the unformed rock, their edges seeping into one another as if the boundaries between solid and liquid are still being negotiated. These pools ripple subtly, not from wind, but from the slow, patient tremors of the earth itself (dynamic at 1.1). The air, thick with moisture, leaves every breath tasting of mineral and salt, a reminder of the deep, unseen reservoirs that may lie beneath this high place, feeding the mist with their unseen exhalations.

Vegetation (1.8) thrives in this wet, warm environment, though it, too, bears the mark of a world not yet settled into permanence. Lush, tangled growths of fern-like plants and mosses carpet the uneven terrain, their fronds and tendrils reaching out through the mist as if seeking something unseen. These plants are vivid in their greens and muted golds, but strangely ephemeral—some seem to wither and regrow in mere hours, as if experimenting with their own forms. Root systems are shallow, clinging desperately to the raw, unweathered stone, or floating in the warm pools, their tips swaying like underwater hair. There are also peculiar, bulbous growths, almost fungal, dotting the landscape, their translucent skins filled with liquid that glows faintly, contributing to the dim, otherworldly illumination of Veylraan. This lushness, while abundant, feels tentative, as if it, too, is waiting for the world to decide its final shape.

The atmosphere of Veylraan is one of quiet strangeness (weirdness at 1.8, sound at 1.3). The dense air muffles most noise, creating a pervasive hush broken only by the occasional drip of water from a jagged overhang, or the soft, hollow echo of a pebble dislodged by an unseen movement rolling into a pool. There is a sense of pressure in the silence, as if the world is holding its breath, waiting to exhale into something more defined. Yet, there are sounds beneath the quiet—subtle, almost imperceptible hums that seem to emanate from the earth itself, as if the plateau were singing to itself in a register too low for most ears to catch. The weirdness permeates deeper: the mist sometimes seems to form shapes, vague and fleeting—figures or faces that vanish when looked at directly. The warm pools occasionally bubble without apparent cause, and their surfaces reflect not the dim gray sky above, but fractured, impossible glimpses of other places, as if the water were a window to something not yet here.

Fauna (1.2) is rare in Veylraan, but not absent. Small, translucent creatures—something between fish and insect—drift through the mist, their bodies catching the faint light in prismatic flashes before they disappear. They leave no sound, no trace, as if they are as much a part of the air as the water droplets. Occasionally, a larger shape—a sinuous, eel-like thing—can be seen gliding through one of the deeper pools, its body half-visible through the murk before it sinks out of sight. These creatures seem hesitant, as if they, like the land, are unsure of their place in this emerging world, testing their forms with slow, cautious movements.

The scale (1.7) of Veylraan is open and regional, extending beyond the immediate into a vast, mist-obscured expanse. From any vantage, the plateau seems to stretch endlessly, its far edges swallowed by the silvery haze. Yet, within this expanse, the density (1.6) is tangible—every space feels crowded, layered with mist, vegetation, and the overlapping edges of unformed rock and water. There is no emptiness here; even the air presses close, heavy with moisture and presence, as if the world is too full of itself to allow for voids.

Amidst this raw, natural chaos, there is evidence of a built environment (1.6), though it is as primitive as the technology (0.6) that shaped it. Scattered across the plateau are structures of roughly hewn stone and woven plant fibers, assembled with simple tools of wood and chipped flint. These are not the towering edifices of a modern city, but the low, sprawling constructs of an early urban settlement—huts and communal shelters clustered in small enclaves, their forms half-submerged in the mist and water. Narrow paths of tamped earth and stone connect these clusters, winding through the uneven terrain and often disappearing into shallow pools only to reemerge on the other side. The builders, whoever they are, seem to have adapted to the liquid nature of their world, constructing platforms of layered rock to lift their homes above the wetter hollows. These structures are dense, packed tightly together in defiance of the endless openness of the plateau, as if the inhabitants seek safety in proximity. There are signs of craftsmanship—crudely carved symbols on stones near the shelters, depicting spirals and waves, perhaps an attempt to understand or appease the strangeness of their environment. Yet, the builders themselves are absent, or at least unseen, leaving their works to stand silent in the mist, as if they, too, are waiting for the world to solidify.

The rules of this world are as embryonic as its terrain. Gravity seems softer here, the mist supporting weight in subtle, unnatural ways—droplets linger too long in the air, and small objects sometimes float for moments before sinking. Time, too, feels unreliable; hours stretch or contract without reason, and the dim light never shifts into true day or night, remaining locked in an eternal twilight. The warmth of the air and water is constant, almost oppressive, as if the world is cradling itself in a fevered embrace. There is a sense that Veylraan operates on a principle of patience (dynamic at 1.1)—change happens, but slowly, across cycles too long for a mortal observer to fully grasp. The rock may shift, the pools may deepen, the mist may thicken or thin, but these transformations are measured in centuries, not days.

As I linger in the imagining of Veylraan, the place grows stranger still. I see now that the mist is not just water vapor, but something alive in its own right—a medium that carries whispers of thought or memory, brushing against the mind with half-formed ideas that vanish as quickly as they come. The pools, too, reveal more of their nature; their surfaces sometimes harden into a glass-like sheen, only to crack and liquefy again without warning, as if testing the boundary between states of matter. The vegetation begins to hum softly in unison, a sound so faint it might be imagined, resonating with the earth’s deeper tones. And those rare creatures—those translucent drifters and hidden swimmers—seem to watch from the edges of perception, their presence felt more than seen, as if they know something of this world that even the land itself does not yet understand.

Veylraan is a place of becoming, a landscape caught in the act of creation, where water, warmth, and strangeness weave together a tapestry that is both urban and wild, dense and open, silent and subtly alive. It is a world that does not yet know its own name, but waits, with infinite patience, for the forces of time and will to sculpt it into whatever it is destined to be.
