In the shadow of the earth’s surface, far below where the sun reigns, there lies a vast subterranean expanse known as the Murken Hollows. This is a world carved into the underbelly of a primordial continent, a sprawling network of caverns and tunnels that stretch for miles in every direction, a regional under-realm untouched by the hands of builders or the scars of civilization. Here, elevation dips low, the air hangs heavy with a quiet, ancient weight, and the landscape feels as though it has only recently been born, its jagged edges and sharp contours untouched by the softening hand of time.

### Terrain and Materials
The terrain of the Murken Hollows is a labyrinth of young, unweathered rock, a testament to its recent emergence from tectonic upheaval or some silent, subterranean cataclysm. Walls of dark basalt and slate rise in sheer, angular cliffs, their surfaces rough and unpolished, glinting faintly with veins of quartz under the dim, scant light. The ground underfoot is uneven, a mosaic of cracked stone slabs and gravelly scree, interrupted by sudden drops into deeper fissures or shallow, bowl-like depressions. These natural basins hold the only water in this otherwise arid underworld—small, stagnant pools fed by intermittent drips from the ceiling, where condensation gathers on stalactites and falls in slow, deliberate rhythms. The dampness is subtle but pervasive, a faint sheen of moisture slicking the stones, making every step a careful one. The air smells of wet mineral and the faint, earthy musk of unseen decay, a reminder that even in this barren place, life finds a way to rot and renew.

### Atmosphere and Light
The atmosphere of the Murken Hollows is cool, a constant, unyielding chill that seeps into the bones. It is not frigid enough to freeze, but it carries a sharpness that makes breath visible in faint, fleeting clouds. The dimness here is near-constant, broken only by the bioluminescent glow of certain fauna and the occasional shimmer of reflective minerals embedded in the rock. There are no natural light sources from above; instead, the Hollows are lit by a strange, ambient murk, as if the darkness itself holds a muted memory of light. Shadows pool in every corner, and the vast openness of the caverns feels both exposed and claustrophobic, as if the weight of the world above presses down invisibly.

### Sounds
Despite its isolation and stillness, the Murken Hollows are far from silent. The soundscape is loud, a chaotic symphony of life reverberating off the stone walls. The drip of water is a persistent heartbeat, irregular but unending, echoing in the vast chambers. But it is the fauna that dominate the auditory realm—shrieks, chitters, and low, resonant hums fill the air, bouncing off the hard surfaces to create a disorienting cacophony. There are moments when the noise seems to swell into a unified roar, as if some unseen migration or ritual is unfolding just beyond sight, before it fractures again into individual cries. This relentless soundscape is a stark contrast to the otherwise static, unchanging nature of the Hollows; it is the only evidence of a world in motion.

### Ecology and Fauna
Vegetation is sparse, clinging to life in the form of pale, wiry mosses and lichen that spread in thin, ghostly patches across damp rocks. These plants are adapted to the dimness, their muted greens and grays barely visible unless one stoops close. They are survivors, drawing what little sustenance they can from the mineral-rich moisture and the decay of fallen fauna. But where flora is scarce, fauna is overwhelming, a teeming mass of life that seems to defy the harshness of this underworld. Insect-like creatures skitter in vast swarms, their iridescent carapaces flashing dimly as they clamber over rocks and one another. Larger, predatory forms—hulking, multi-limbed beasts with segmented bodies and clicking mandibles—prowl the shadows, their low growls a warning to any interloper. There are smaller, darting things too, bat-like in their erratic flight, emitting high-pitched screeches as they navigate the caverns. Bioluminescent worms and grubs writhe in the gravel, casting eerie glows that serve as both lure and warning. This ecosystem is a brutal one, a constant cycle of hunt and escape, yet it exists in a strange, frozen equilibrium—life thrives, but the balance never shifts, as if time itself has paused in this endless dance of predator and prey.

### Scale and Density
The Murken Hollows are open and vast, a regional expanse that seems to stretch indefinitely beneath the surface. Caverns open into one another, connected by narrow tunnels that widen suddenly into echoing halls the size of small valleys. Yet, despite this scale, the density of presence is sparse. The rock dominates, empty and imposing, with life and moisture confined to scattered pockets. One can walk for hours through barren stretches of jagged stone, only to stumble upon a sudden burst of activity—a pool surrounded by darting creatures, or a cliff face crawling with glowing larvae. This balance of emptiness and sudden saturation gives the Hollows a haunting, desolate beauty, as if it is a world still waiting to be fully claimed by life.

### Rules of the World
The Murken Hollows operate under a set of unspoken, immutable rules, as if the very rock enforces them. First, there is no change—time feels locked in place, the landscape and its balance of life and death unchanging, as if caught in a singular, eternal moment. The young, sharp features of the rock show no sign of erosion beyond their initial formation, and the cycles of life play out in endless repetition. Second, survival is relentless—every creature, no matter how small, is adapted to fight or flee, and there is no room for weakness. Even the mosses seem to cling with a quiet ferocity. Third, there is no interference from above or beyond; this is a world untouched by external forces, a self-contained realm where the only history is written in claw marks and mineral veins. Tools and technology, if they exist at all, are primitive—perhaps a sharpened stone or a bone used by some forgotten visitor long since consumed by the fauna. But such things are rare, as if the Hollows reject anything not born of their own brutal essence.

### Emerging Details and Atmosphere
As one lingers in the Murken Hollows, the place begins to reveal subtler layers. There are faint, almost imperceptible vibrations in the rock, a deep thrumming that might be the heartbeat of the earth itself or the distant movement of tectonic plates. The pools of water, though small, sometimes ripple without cause, as if stirred by an unseen presence or a memory of flow. The fauna, for all their ferocity, seem bound by strange patterns—swarms move in spirals, predators stalk in precise, ritualistic loops, as if following an ancient script. There is no weirdness in the conventional sense, no overt strangeness, but the sheer persistence of this unchanging, loud, life-drenched underworld begins to feel uncanny, as if it defies the very concept of progress or decay.

This is a place of raw, unformed potential, a world still in its infancy yet locked in stasis. To stand in the Murken Hollows is to feel both lost and acutely present, surrounded by the clamor of life in a landscape that refuses to evolve. It is a testament to endurance—a cold, dim, damp expanse where the only certainty is the endless chorus of survival, echoing through caverns that have never known the sun.
