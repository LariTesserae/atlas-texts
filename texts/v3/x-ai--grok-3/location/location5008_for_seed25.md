### The Terrain and Scale: A Low, Sprawling Maze of Eroded Forms
This landscape sprawls across a vast, low-lying expanse, a regional depression where the ground seems to have settled over eons into a stable, weathered state. The elevation is modest, perhaps a wide basin or sunken plain, ringed by distant, barely visible rises that trap moisture and sound within the bowl of the land. The scale feels open yet intimate—stretching far enough to suggest a world beyond the immediate, but dense enough to crowd the senses. The terrain is not smooth; it is a labyrinth of eroded forms, jagged yet softened by time. Sharp ridges of dark, glassy stone rise like fractured ribs from the ground, some no taller than a person, others towering ten or twenty meters, their edges dulled by the patient work of erosion. Between these ridges are channels and hollows, some shallow, others deep enough to hide a figure entirely, forming a natural warren that twists and turns without pattern. These forms have stood for millennia, their shapes established and in balance, as if the world has long since decided what it will be.

### Materials and Water: A Damp, Mineral World
The ground and ridges are composed of a material that defies easy categorization—a dense, black, semi-translucent stone that resembles volcanic glass but feels denser, colder, almost metallic underfoot. It doesn't reflect light so much as absorb it, giving the landscape an oppressive dimness even when some faint illumination trickles through. There is no vegetation, not a trace of plant life, not even lichen or moss. This is a mineral world, a place where biology as we know it has either never taken hold or long since perished, leaving only the raw stuff of the earth behind. Yet there is moisture here, a persistent dampness that clings to everything. Condensation beads on the glassy surfaces, trickling down in rivulets that pool in the hollows between ridges. Occasional, light rains—more like a heavy mist than a downpour—drift through the basin, leaving the air thick and cool. In the deepest channels, small pockets of inky water collect, still and mirror-like, their surfaces trembling with unseen vibrations. The water level is modest, never overwhelming, but its presence is constant, a quiet reminder of cycles beyond the control of this barren place.

### Atmosphere and Temperature: Cool, Heavy Darkness
The air hangs heavy, cool to the touch, with a temperature that brushes the edge of discomfort without biting into chill. It is the kind of cold that seeps into the bones over time, a damp coolness that lingers in the lungs with every breath. No sun pierces the atmosphere directly—perhaps a thick, low cloud cover obscures it, or perhaps this world orbits too far from its star for warmth and brightness to dominate. What little light exists comes from faint, bioluminescent pulses in the distance, erratic and sourceless, casting fleeting shadows across the jagged terrain. The darkness is not absolute, but it is oppressive, forcing the eyes to strain for detail, turning every shape into a half-seen suggestion of something more.

Their behavior hints at a strange, alien ecology. They are life, but life at its most alien, shaped by rules we cannot fully grasp.

### Rules of the World: A Dynamic, Alien Balance
