### Ecology and Rules of the World

The ecology of Karrveth Spine is a complex, self-sustaining loop of mechanical and microbial life. The fauna feed on the minerals of the rock and the faint energy discharges from decaying industrial systems, while the microbial films, in turn, break down the waste of the fauna, recycling it into nutrients that seep back into the stone. Water is the lifeblood here, carrying trace elements and sustaining the delicate balance; without the constant condensation and seepage, the system would collapse. The larger creatures act as predators, scavenging broken-down kin for parts, while the smaller swarms maintain the crumbling infrastructure, patching cables and clearing debris in an endless, instinctive repair cycle. It’s as if the Spine’s original builders programmed life to outlast them, creating a biosphere of industry that persists long after their disappearance.

The rules of this world are governed by endurance and integration. Nothing survives here without adapting to the wet, high, eroded conditions, and everything—fauna, microbes, even the ruins themselves—exists in a symbiotic state of decay and renewal. Gravity feels heavier on the Spine, not just from elevation but from the weight of history; movement is slower, deliberate, as if the world resists haste. Technology is not a tool but a living force, woven into the fabric of life; to interfere with one is to disrupt the other. And though the light is normal, it carries a peculiar clarity on the Spine, revealing every detail of rust and sheen, as if the sun itself is complicit in exposing the world’s ancient truths.
