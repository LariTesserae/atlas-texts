If they have a society, it is not structured in ways we’d recognize. There are no apparent hierarchies or conflicts among them; each Keiyar operates with a shared purpose, their actions coordinated without visible communication. Their “language” might be the hum of the conduits, the flicker of light across the plains, or even shifts in the land’s temperature—a dialogue carried through the Hollows itself. When two Keiyar converge, their forms blend momentarily, light merging into light, as if exchanging fragments of essence before separating to resume their tasks.

Their interaction with outsiders is enigmatic at best. They are not hostile, but their indifference mirrors the Hollows’ own. These moments suggest they are aware of outsiders, perhaps even intrigued, but their priorities lie with the Hollows, not with explaining themselves.
