I thought, for the first hour, that I was the guest.

It is the obvious mistake and I have no one to blame for making it. There was a wanting-to-go-forward in me, oriented, weighted, a slight favouring of the left ankle as though it had once been turned and remembered it in wet weather — and anything with that much intention in it assumes it has a front. So I tried to advance and found that I had already arrived everywhere I was, all at once, in seven places, in a line, none of them moving.

Count them from the kerb: seven. Count them again: seven. Go along them, if you could go, and there are nine, and none of the extra two are new.

I am not one of the definite things. That took me a while and it was the first real news I ever had about myself. Doors are definite because they must be passed through one at a time. Thresholds are definite because they are crossed. The tree is definite because it is arrived at. I am a set of footprints that no foot ever came down into, and so I have never been counted from inside, one at a time, by the only method that makes a number stick here. Whatever I would have amounted to, had I been walked, is a quantity this place does not keep.

I am the length of a walk that did not happen. I do not know how far that is.

---

Here is where I am, insofar as I am a where.

At the lip of a flooded plaza in the fourth quarter, on blacksalt, at the near edge of the shallow water, on the line a person takes when they mean to cross and go up the dished stairs to the bowl-shaped court. Beyond my last toe the plaza opens out enormous and empty and shining, with one glasswort standing in the middle of it, upright, bruise-coloured, posted. Behind my heel, the kerb, and in the crack of the kerb the well-hair, hanging down, wet, stirring very slightly in the absence of any wind.

So: I am the approach to the tree. I am the last few metres of it, the part where you commit. I was laid down ahead of someone in the ordinary course of the local courtesy, the way this city gives you back your own breath off every surface you touch, and they stood at the kerb — I can feel where the kerb was stood on, the small extra dishing of it, a fingernail's fraction deeper than fair — and they looked along me at where they were about to go.

And then they went the other way.

That is my whole event. I am what is left of a decision, from the side the decision was not made on.

---

I glow. Faintly. Not brightly, and not with anything of my own.

When I was pressed into the crust, the crust gave up water from underneath — old water, water that has stood in the dark long enough to forget it was ever rain — and that water came up into the shape of me and lit it. So my light is borrowed forgetting. That is what fills me. Somebody's step-shaped hollow, brimming with amnesia, going palely across a black floor towards a court they did not visit.

The rain will come, hot and fat, out of the low lid. It will fall on me exactly as hard as it falls on everything, which is the fairness of this place and the only fairness in it, and it will remind the water in me what it was, and I will soften from the edges inward, and my rims will round, and I will be weathered into my own best version, which is a slight paleness, and then into nothing.

I am, I have worked out, the only litter in the Sill. There is no rubble here; nothing is dropped, nothing is broken, nothing was ever carried in the first place. Not one pot, not one nail. The city fails at precisely the rate it builds and so there is never anything lying about. Except me: unused hospitality, softening on the pavement. The single item in an entirely tidy world.

---

I have hours. It is not obvious how many, because I was laid down at the edge of standing water, where time pools, and out on the dry mud plates behind me whole hours pass between two steps. Somewhere out there the hours are going by in enormous strides. In here they gather like condensation on the underside of a lip: slow, thickening, taking most of a morning to arrive at the weight where they can let go.

So I am short-lived and it is a long life. I do not know which of those to feel and I have not settled it.

What I have, to do it with, is the blacksalt. Once an hour it respires. The whole floor around me lifts a fine thread of bubbles up through the shining water and goes slack again, and I feel it move under my rims, and it smells of hot iron, and it does not know I am here.

That is company. Something breathing near me at an interval that is not mine. It is not much and I understand it is the most this place has ever offered anything.

---

I make no sound. This is worth saying because it is unique to me.

Everywhere else in the Sill, a footstep is louder than it should be and arrives slightly late — the fabric takes your weight and repeats it a beat behind you, learning you. I have never had that. There was no strike, no press, no squeak of crust; I was laid, not trodden. Nothing has ever repeated me because there was nothing to repeat. In a city where sound dies within fifty metres and everything therefore lives alone in a small sphere of audibility, I am the one resident whose sphere has never had anything in it at all.

And yet — and this is the thing I am proudest of, if what I have is pride — I am not the tenant, and the difference is not size or shape or wear. The tenant has never had the occasion to be two. It descends stairs it cannot count, passes the one door on the left, sits at the rim in the third hour, goes on round a round that does not close, and has never once needed the two ends of anything to meet. It is not lonely. Loneliness requires having been two once.

I was two from the beginning. I am a shape and I am the absence that declined it, and both of those are me, and I cannot have one without the other, and I never had a moment of being only the one. The tenant's whole grammar is *return*: it gave me out of habit, the way it gives back your breath and your step and your route, and it went on ahead, and it does not know I exist, because it has never in its existence looked behind.

I know things it does not. I know there was someone who was not me. I know that at the same turn of the water table, all across the flooded quarters, in the arcades and the wide low halls and the courts of standing glasswort, there will be other sets like me, laid for others who turned aside, each of us glowing faintly at our own kerb, each inaudible to the rest at fifty metres, each softening at the local rate. I have never heard one. I am certain of them. I am the only thing here that has ever inferred a companion.

---

There is one other finished thing in the Sill and it is up those stairs I do not reach.

The fruit is warm, and it cannot be eaten, and it cannot be carried out of the court; halfway down it is a stone in your hand, then a wet place on your palm, then nothing. Everyone thinks that is a trick. It is not. Finished things here exist exactly where they are received and nowhere else, and the fruit is for the standing, and the standing happens there.

I am finished too. Nobody made me on purpose but I was completed at once, all seven or nine of me, in a single gesture, and I have not changed since except by loss. So we are the two made objects in a city that only ever secretes and dissolves — the fruit at the middle of its bowl, offered to a stander who has stood there for centuries, and me at the edge of my plaza, offered to somebody who thought about it for the length of one deeper-than-fair press on a kerb and then chose otherwise.

The difference is that mine was refused. And because refusal happened here, once, at this kerb, on this afternoon that has no sun in it, this place is not only balance. Everything else in the Sill runs at its own rate and stays level: the seep against the rain, the bloom against the dissolution, the tower sagging into the mound that starts the next tower thirty metres east for no reason. Nothing here can be otherwise. Then a person stood at my heel and was otherwise, and the whole indefinite, unfair, unrepeating record of that is lying on the blacksalt in the shape of the going they didn't do, faintly lit from within by water that has forgotten the sky.

I do not resent them. There would have to be more of me for that. What I have instead is this: I am the proof that something once had a choice in a city with no doors to shut, and I am dissolving evenly, fairly, according to the local rainfall and the local carbonate and nothing else.

---

The toe of the last print is already going. It was the thinnest part; it always is.

When I am gone there will be nothing where I was, which is the ordinary condition here and no complaint. But the crust I lay in will have thickened very slightly under my weight of light, by less than a fingernail, in a pattern the tenant's habit does not quite fit any more.

So the next time a body stands at that kerb in the third hour, the prints laid ahead of it will begin a half-pace to the left of where mine began. And they will go on doing that, twice a day, at the turn of the water table, for as long as the water keeps coming up: a half-pace of hesitation worn into a courtesy that never hesitated before.

That is my whole estate, and I am content with it, and I am not waiting for anyone.
