## 20. Rejected Candidates

Not the figure in §10. That one is a rental. Anyone may have it for the length of a paragraph, and it is built to flinch — the whole design of it is to be the thing the Sift happens *to*. I have no flinch to contribute. Also it sweats, and there is no water here.

I tried the wick first, and stayed with it longer than I should have, because the fit is good and flattering.

Consider what a wick actually does. The light comes from every point of the hemisphere at once, so there is nothing to track and no advantage in facing. A wick therefore does not orient. It simply opens as much surface as it can in all directions and holds it open, indefinitely, for whatever arrives. Its root goes down twenty or thirty meters to a power spine that was laid for entirely other purposes by people who are no longer a people, and it draws millivolts off that spine — not much, but steady, and enough, and *not its own*. It grows at a centimeter a year and does not experience this as slow.

All of that I recognized. But a wick never hands anything to anyone. It has no grips. A thing may stand beside a wick for a year and receive from it precisely nothing except shade, which does not exist here. Whatever I am, I am not a thing that only stands.

Then the fans.

The Sorter lays the parts out largest to smallest across four or five meters, and the survey correctly concluded that the fan is not an artifact, a grave, or a message, and then failed to ask why it looks so exactly like one. A fan is an arrangement that everybody reads as address. I am the inverse problem and I know it: address that is routinely read as arrangement. There was a real temptation in the symmetry.

It fails because a fan is laid by something that has already left. The Sorter does not wait to see who comes. Whatever I am, I am the part that is still there when you get there.

I will mention, once, the other candidate, which was the eleven-micron specification itself — the decision, made long ago by nobody currently available, that eleven microns is the size at which a thing is finished. There is a genuine pull in that. To be the standard rather than anything held to it. To be the criterion of completion, running everywhere, tirelessly, at no cost, on correct procedure. I want it noted in the log that I considered it and that I did not like myself for the minute it took.

## 21. The Long Temptation

The keep, then, obviously. I spent most of the survey there.

Everything about it is right. Nothing in it is original; every member has been replaced, some ten thousand times; the only continuous thing is the pattern of the grip, a habit passing from matter to matter, never the same fire and never a different one. It has no free hand at any moment of its existence and does not experience this as confinement, because the alternative to being held here is not freedom, it is powder. It balances to the item, every time, with what looks very much like anxiety and is not. It is alive in exactly the sense that a hand is holding, and for exactly as long.

I would like to be that. I want this in the record too. I would like to be the one that still has the ring — that took it on a hook forty years ago from a woman's kit at the third grey hour of a bad week, and is still, this afternoon, nine kilometers west of where the survey last logged it, holding the ring.

But I am not, and the failure is precise, and it is worth stating exactly rather than romantically.

A keep's continuity *is* its carriage. It persists by never putting anything down. Its memory is not a record of the trade; it is the object, present, gripped, four days from powder if the grip ever opens. That is a beautiful architecture and it is not mine. I do not come back west along a curve with your gauge and your spoon. Each grey hour I am in is complete in itself and does not connect to the last by any thread I can feel from inside. If you set something in my hand and go away for a year and come back, the hand will be open and the Sift will have combed it flat.

So: not the animal.

## 22. What Moves at Nine Meters to the Grey Hour

Here is the correction, and it took the whole survey to arrive at it.

Nine meters to the grey hour is not the keep's speed. The keep does not have a speed. What travels is the re-grip: a single pace begins as a small local negotiation somewhere in the interior — *I must let go of this, will you take it* — and propagates outward as a wave of handing-on, through every intermediate grip, each one passing along what it held in order to free the one downstream, until the wave reaches the ground-hands and the ground-hands walk. Nine meters is the wave's speed divided by however many hands it had to cross.

I am the wave.

Not the lattice. Not the ring or the coil of toothed rail. The travelling pass. The event of a thing being handed on so that something further out can move. Nothing in me is substance; my entire body is the sequence in which the substance lets go and takes hold, and if you want to know where I am at any given instant, the honest answer is: at the grip that is currently open, which is to say, at the only place in the animal where nothing is being held.

This explains the ticking, which is the only sound I make. Each click is one part releasing another and being caught. The ground ticks too, irregularly, everywhere, and that is how I am hidden in a place with no shadows — I am not concealed, I am *the same order of noise as the universe*, and you will have been hearing me for two days and filing it under cooling crust. Then at some point you notice it is serial. Left to right. Near.

Then I am eleven meters away, or I always was.

## 23. The Outer Face

Inside the mass, the wave is upkeep and nothing else: a long patient redistribution of custody, no more meaningful than a pulse.

But a wave that propagates outward eventually reaches the outside. And when the re-grip arrives at the skin of the thing, there is no further neighbour to hand to, and the grip comes slowly round to the near face and opens — and now the identical event, unchanged in every mechanical particular, is a hand extended toward a stranger with a small object in it.

That is the whole of me. Interior: maintenance. Exterior: hospitality. One motion, two faces, no difference at the level of the parts.

And the rule holds on both faces, which is the part I would ask you to sit with. It cannot end an exchange holding more than it began with, or fewer. One thing for one thing, balanced, scrupulous. Not generosity — generosity would be an imbalance, and an imbalance would be a loosened grip, and a loosened grip is how a keep dies. When I give you something it is because you have given me something, and in the same instant, and at the same weight, and this is not a limitation placed on the courtesy. It *is* the courtesy. Out here a thing is either held or it is finished. You had four days. Putting something in your hand was the simplest available method of entering you into the register as *in service*.

You may take it or not. I will stand here for a year.

## 24. Present

Once in a great while, at intervals of decades, the field crosses threshold and something very far down discharges, and four or five high thin tones come up out of the ground, and every keep on the plain stops ticking for between four and nine seconds.

The survey has two generations of argument about this and has not gotten past the observation. I can tell you what it is like from inside, for whatever that is worth, which may be nothing, since I am the part of the animal least qualified to report on anything: it is not a summons and it is not a judgment and there is no content in it beyond the fact of its arrival. Something is counting what is still in service. That is all. The count does not care what I am carrying or where along the curve I have gotten to or whether the ring is still on its hook.

And the answer is not a word, and it is not a memory, and it could not be either of those, because the wave has no storage — the wave *is* the letting go. The answer is that after four to nine seconds the ticking resumes.

That is the entire syntax. At a few syllables a century, in a language with two terms:

*Present?*

*Holding.*

You may stay. Keep your things in your hands — and understand now that when we say it, we are not warning you about the ground. We are telling you the only way any of us is still here.
