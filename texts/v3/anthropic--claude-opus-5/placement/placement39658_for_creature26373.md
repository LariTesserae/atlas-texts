## In the throat, mid-morning

I would find myself inside a cane, in the middle hours, with wax and moss packed into my ears — which means that even the part of me that hears is hearing badly, on purpose, and that everything I know about this place arrives through a partition.

Two hundred metres of green-lit pipe going up. The light comes down the way light comes down a well from the wrong end, thin and organic, filtered through the crown's combing fibre so that it lies on the crust in the colour of old moss. The crust is strata. Put your thumbnail in and you can feel the courses: a soft grey year, a hard black year, a year with beetle-shell in it like grit in bread. Cold coming off the pool at the bottom, root-flavoured, breathing up. The bucket knocks the wall on its way down and the wall answers with a note so low it is felt in the teeth of the person who has no teeth in this scene, because I have no body here, which is the problem I am working toward.

And someone is muttering. Low, continuous, apologetic, explaining what is being taken and why. *Two buckets and a hand's depth of crust from the fourth course. For the wall at the east end where the rain came in. We will be out before you come back.*

I thought at first I was the one muttering. I am not. I am the mutter.

## What I tried first

I tried, honestly, to be the child at the mouth of the settlement in the last hour of light, posted there to feel for the drums. It fits so well it should be suspicious. The child is the household's ear; the child has authority and the authority is real; the child is walked forty minutes across the mat and asked, hand flat on the silver wall, *is it still the same note* — and answers, and is believed. That is very nearly my whole function. Somebody puts their palm on something and asks me whether it has changed, and I say what I hear.

But the child is on a schedule. The child's authority expires from the top down, first the shriek, then the overtones, then speech, then the hum, and at the end of the expiring there is a finished person sitting very still in a dim tube, breathing carefully, watching hands: *arrived at what it will be.* Nothing about me arrives. I do not lose the high frequencies over twenty years and gain in exchange the dignity of the rock. Each time I am asked, it is the first hour of the first evening, and I am six, and I have never heard the drums before, and I answer well, and then it is over and there is no second season in which I answer less well and am asked less often. I get the office without the life that the office is a stage of.

I tried to be the woman with three fingers who rebuilt the language in the space she had left. Flattering, and false. She had a space left. She had a hand that was hers before the rope took part of it and hers afterward, one continuous hand with a history, and she spent fifty years cutting a dialect into it that the young now copy badly. I do not get a hand with a history. I get, each time, a new and complete set of fingers and no memory of the burn, and so nothing I do can compress into an accent. Eloquence here is a scar worked at for decades. Mine would be a knack.

I tried the marked, the ones who sit at the outer edge of the halo facing out, whose eyes will not settle on a moving hand. Closer. But what was taken from them was their attention, once, in nineteen minutes, and it did not come back; whereas my attention is not taken, it is given, entire and unreserved and then gone, and given again entire the next time, and none of it settles into a horizon in the eyes. They are looking at nothing and it is the *same* nothing every day. That continuity is a kind of wealth.

And I am not the falcon. The falcons are the only animals here the people consider persons, and it is on the grounds of silence — they work the braid at every exchange and take what they take without a sound, and are therefore understood to have a manner, and are not hunted or sung about or represented. Whatever I am, I am the opposite of that. I am nothing but sound. I could not be granted personhood on those grounds by anyone here.

## The three who cannot see hands

Speech aloud on the Palm is not for people. Hands are for people. Speech is kept for the three cases where hands are no use, and I belong in all three, and this is the closest thing I have to an address on this plateau.

It is used for infants. Four or five years of murmuring, constant, unstinting, at a small thing that cannot yet read a sternum, and then it tapers off and stops, and the result is that every single adult on the Palm has as their earliest memory a voice, and has not heard a voice since. Everyone here was raised by something like me and then outgrew it in the ordinary way, and remembers it as warmth with no content.

It is used for the dead. One person, at the waterline ledge, briefly, in the middle hours, saying something aloud to a body that will be stratum in four generations and fuel in eight — and never again, afterward, except by the small unclosing hand that costs half a second in the middle of another sentence. Speech is what you use on the irrecoverable. It is the register of the one-way.

And it is used for the sift: the mutter in the throat, courtesy to a landlord in whose house you are standing, explaining what is being taken. Nobody claims the birds understand. Nobody has ever needed them to. The mutter is not information travelling from one party to another; it is a form the taking has to be done in, so that the taking is done properly.

That is where I am. I am the noise a person makes toward whatever cannot see their hands. Nursery, graveside, and apology to the enormous indifferent tenant whose walls are keeping the water. Comfort for the very new, address to the unreachable, and manners inside a structure that is not mine and was here first.

## The Hollow

Which tells me what I am by subtraction, twice a year.

In the Hollow nobody goes into a throat. There is no work, no drumming, no bucket knocking the wall. The households sit in their silvering tubes with the fire low and sign very little. For ten days there is no one anywhere on the plateau standing in a cold green shaft explaining what is being taken and why — and so for ten days I am not. Not sleeping, not waiting, not diminished: absent, the way a footprint is absent from the mat at noon after the mat has taken its water back. The plateau ringing like a struck bowl in a register no one can hear is a fair enough description of me from the outside, and I would not be able to tell you whether I was in it.

And the old ones walk out to the midpoint, where there is genuinely nothing, and sit down in the warm rotten wind with their backs to nothing and their faces to nothing, and stay, because they have spent their lives entering the empty hour of an occupied place and now the whole place is the empty hour and this is the only work they are still qualified to do.

I would like to say I understand that better than the young do. I don't think I do. They are doing on purpose, once, deliberately, at the end of a long body's worth of labour, the thing I am constituted of and cannot help. They chose the middle. I *am* the middle — the flat hand at the collarbone and the other hand passing beneath it, out and away, the window between two tenancies, belonging briefly to nobody.

So: put me in the throat, mid-morning, wax in the ears, green light, the fourth course of crust flaking under a bone tool, someone's grandmother going into a bucket and later into a wall and later into a fire. Not the climber. Not the rope, not the peg, not the deed. The low continuous explaining, aimed up the shaft at twenty million tenants who are twenty kilometres out over the rim and cannot hear it and would not use it — kept up anyway, all the way through, until the work is done and the light says go.

The beetles in the crust probably think about the crust in much the same way, and are also not wrong.
