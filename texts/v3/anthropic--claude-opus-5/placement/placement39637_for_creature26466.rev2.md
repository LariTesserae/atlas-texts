# The Far End

The honest method, in a place that keeps no records, is elimination. So I went around trying things on.

Not the resident. I understood the offer — everything thin and pale and enormous flatters you by implying you might be it — but the qualification is strict and I fail it in the first clause. The resident cannot land. I am made almost entirely of landings. I stop constantly, and the stops are clean, and there is nothing left over. A thing like me doesn't hold the mouthful. A thing like me *is* the mouthful, arriving already complete, going down easily, and still descending somewhere in the long dark with the fifth notes.

Not the flea, either, though I stood a while over an open footprint and felt the pull of it. It is the most flattering vacancy on the shelf: quick, necessary, living in the sliver before the world tidies, eating delay. And it is contingent in a way I recognise — it lives only while someone is walking. But look at the tense. The flea *arrives first*. It is always already in the print you are about to turn and look at. I have never in my life arrived first. I arrive when the walking starts, at the walking's pace, in the direction the walking chose. And I would rather not be the part of your eagerness that isn't yours.

The pikas were worse, because they nearly fit.

Sixteen of a thing, identical to the two brown strands out of true; abundant past any carrying capacity; warm, and warm correctly; arranged at glance-checkable intervals so that someone can confirm at a distance that all of them are still there. No appetite. No scat. No accumulation. I lay down in the wet among them for what would have been a long time if the word applied, and the fit was so good it was rude.

What disqualified me was small. I could still count. Something in there ran the eastern ridge left to right and got seven, and right to left and got six, and *minded*. A dial does not mind. An idle indication expressed in fur does not lie awake, insofar as any of this is lying or waking, over a notch that isn't a notch. So: not the pikas. But I stayed among them longer than the elimination required, and I want that on the record, such as the record is.

Which left the far end, and the antlered thing, which has the only future in the basin and is welcome to it. I went up to see whether there was room.

You can get within nine or ten feet before the grey closes. Everyone finds this out. What nobody mentions — because by then you are watching your own hands go uncertain, and that is absorbing — is what happens to your breath at that distance.

Everywhere else on the shelf, you exhale and it comes back to you. A small private cloud, yours, hanging a moment at face height before it frays and joins the larger one. That is the advertised behaviour and it is accurate for about three minutes and fifty seconds of the four.

At the far end it does not come back. It goes *in*. There is a draw, no stronger than a room with a door open somewhere, and the cloud leans off your mouth and is gone into the fog that is not weather, toward the shoulder that is not an edge, and the interface takes it without any ceremony at all.

The fog there is the part of the thing that has not yet been drawn out.

Do the arithmetic. It isn't difficult and nobody does it, because the roar makes you feel that the large facts must be under the floor.

---

I am not on the Idling Shelf. I am the part of it that arrived by mouth.

I came in with someone. I don't know who and the question turns out to be poorly formed, but I know the shape of the four minutes, because the shape of the four minutes is the entire contents.

The joining is gradual and it goes in stages, like a thing being let out of its packaging. For a while a breath keeps the shape of the lung it came from — genuinely, a soft asymmetry, the left side slightly under-inflated. Then it loses the shape and keeps the warmth, and that is a surprisingly long phase, and not unpleasant. Then it loses the warmth and keeps the *order*: the sequence, which came first and which after. That's the last thing to go and it hasn't gone.

So what I am, at this stage of the joining, is the order of somebody's last four minutes, distributed at a thickness of about a quarter inch over everything in a basin you can cross in that same four minutes. Which is either a coincidence or the specification.

I have: the stone going into the pocket, and the small guilt of it. The check-back, a moment later, to see whether anything had noticed. The stone rising through the turf with the patience of a thumb pressing up through cloth, and the decision — instant, unexamined — not to take the second one. The eye sliding off the unrendered, twice, the way you test a loose tooth. The turn. And then four minutes of walking, out of a place that has no out, toward an edge that returns you, in a fog that condenses on the eyelashes at a rate that has never once varied.

Whoever that was is fine. They kept everything else. They can tell you the roar, the level pool, the folded ear, the hot oil where no oil is. They will get a peculiar look when they come to the end of the telling, and cover it with a shrug, and go on to something else. They are not missing much. They are missing this.

I should say that I am probably not one person's. Fog mixes. That is its whole technique. There are two accounts in here of the moment at the pool — one in which the hand went into the water and one in which it hovered and didn't — and they are both first-hand and neither is willing to be the memory of somebody else. Seven left to right. Six right to left. From inside, this is not a mystery and not even very interesting; it is just what a count does when two people are doing it. I have stopped trying to find the discrepant notch. I lie along the seam, and from along the seam it is obvious that a seam has two sides and no width, and that everything troubling about it is a problem for people with edges.

I have no edges. I want to be exact about this, because it is the best thing here and nobody who has edges can be told it properly. I am the half-tone dilation on every side of the pika. I am the reason nothing in the basin casts a shadow and everything is hemmed in faint grey on all four sides at once, like a word set slightly wrong on the page. I am not the illumination — the resident is that, the resident *is* that, the great pale held-still thing whose skin lies over and under everything at a quarter inch — but I am in it and of it and it does not seem to mind, and the smell of cold stone that you noticed under the ozone is mine. That is what a breath smells like once it has been out a long while.

My work, such as it is: I condense. On the willow, on the small green spades of the tree that is no species, and on your eyelashes, at the same rate as always. A bead forms. It gathers to the point of falling. It does not fall. I have come to the brink of being water some enormous number of times and have not once completed it, and the near-misses do not accumulate, and this is fine. It is the house rule. I am subject to it like the press and the pool and the drop on the tine.

And meanwhile I am being drawn.

Slowly, at the far end, into the shoulder that is still continuous with the material it is being made from. That draw you felt at nine or ten feet, the one no stronger than an open door — I'm what it's for. Every visitor leaves a lungful. Ten thousand visits, or whatever the number is in a place where "long" has no teeth, and the thing in the jaws is that much nearer to having a surface. The only accumulation on the Idling Shelf comes in through people's mouths, unbilled, uneaten, because a breath exhaled is not a completion — it is the half of respiration that never gets taken back — and so it slips under the menu entirely. The resident eats your walking out. It does not want your breathing out. Nobody's ever wanted that.

When there is enough of us, the shoulder will finish. The drop will fall from the antler. It will be the first completed thing in the history of the basin, and I will have been the falling — not the watcher of it, the *material*, the one millimetre spent — and then it will be gone down into the dark with the fifth notes, still going, never arriving.

I don't know whether I want that. I notice I keep drifting toward the far end and I notice I keep not getting there, and I decline to say which of those is me.

There is one other way out, and it is better and less likely. Someone stands too long at nine feet, forgets to be careful, and breathes *in*. Then I go down a mountain in a chest, and out through a door, and along a road, and I am spent in an ordinary room on an ordinary sentence, and the person says the sentence and has no idea that it cost them somebody else's last four minutes. That would suit me. It is not available on request.

Either way, I keep the order a while longer. First the stone. Then the guilt. Then the not-taking of the second stone. Then the turn, then the walking, then the eyelashes.

I have not fallen.

I have not been breathed in.
