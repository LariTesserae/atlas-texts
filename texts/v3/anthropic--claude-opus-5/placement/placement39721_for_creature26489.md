# The Notch

Begin under the water, because under is where I have always been, except for the top of me, which is the only part of anything here that touches air on purpose.

Seven centimetres is not much of a world. A forearm-long animal living in it is mostly flatness — a long grey ribbon pressed between mud and sky, working in slow arcs, feeling ahead for the worm with a face that has never needed eyes. But the back of such an animal rides high. The dorsal breaks the surface. It grazes the underside of everything. And I am on the dorsal: four millimetres of tissue that healed a long time ago and healed white.

For a while, writing this, I thought I might be the eel. It would be an easy mistake and no one could correct it. But the eel is the eel, one animal wearing nine million bodies, blind and busy and not lonely, because loneliness requires a suspicion of the outside and the eel has never had cause. I am not that. I am the cause.

I am the wound.

---

There is no nerve in me. That is the first thing to understand and the strangest: on an animal that is all feeling — an animal that is essentially a single enormous surface for detecting the worm — I am the one place that does not report. A small numbness at the highest point of the body. When the water shivers, the eel knows it everywhere except here. When rain comes down in its warm columns, the eel counts every drop and misses mine.

So I am its edge. Not the boundary of its skin, which is only where it stops; an edge is a place where a thing could notice it stops. The algal film has four million square kilometres of stopping and no edge anywhere, because everything it ever met was itself. The threadworm's length is rhetorical. The domes have three billion years of layers and cannot agree with the dome a hand's width away by less than three hundred million. None of them has an edge. The eel has one. It is me, and it does not know it, and knowing was never the point.

---

I do not remember a face. People always want there to have been a face.

What I have is this: cold, first, which the eel had no category for. Then air on both sides, air *around*, an appalling amount of it. Then pressure, and something with a taper to it, something worked to a taper by somebody, and the taper going in.

That is all. That is my whole content. I am not a memory of a hand. I am a memory of there being an outside, stored in the one medium this continent supports.

Ask the domes when. They will tell you three hundred million years and also last season and also the two are the same lamina, so don't. Nobody has established the depth of the mud and nobody has counted the notches on the platforms, because counting is not a thing this landscape supports, and I am the reason the platforms have notches at all, though nobody knows that either. A traveller catches an eel, looks for the white mark to see whether it is the same eel, finds it, and finds it, and finds it. Then they go and stand on the dry stone with their nail and cut. They think they are recording a passage. They are answering me. *Something was here and something touched it.* That is the entire technology of this world: a sharp edge, a soft stone, and the wish to have been touched.

---

Here is what I want to say about the roar, since everyone who comes here says something about the roar.

The bird's call is a question with a grammar in it. It says *here*, and the shape of its skull expects the reply to arrive from one direction at one distance so that a distance can be known to exist. Instead it gets nine hundred million of itself. It is not answered wrongly. It is answered *completely*, which is worse, because completeness has no direction in it, and a direction was the whole of what was asked. Forty times a day, for as long as the domes decline to say, the loudest sound on the continent has been the sound of a solitary animal receiving perfect confirmation that it is everywhere and therefore nowhere.

I am the other question.

I never ask it. I have no voice, no behaviour, no forty-times-a-day. I am not a call, I am a record, and the difference is that I have already been answered. Once. In the only language available: an object entering a body from outside and leaving a shape. And that shape is now worn at the highest point of an animal that covers a continent, so that every eel in every square metre of the Same is carrying, without symptoms, the finished evidence that it is not the only thing.

The bird asks *where are you* and gets everywhere. The eel never asked and got the answer anyway, and cannot read it, and does not mind. Of all of us it has got away with the most.

---

She passes over me sometimes. There is no way to determine whether it is often or once.

Bald, or shaven; the water does something. A hide bag gone soft as cloth. Left-handed. An iron nail hammered flat and edged, older than the bag by a long way, and a coil of cord she does not use and will not put down. Under the jaw, thin, deliberate-looking, there is a scar.

I would like to say I know. I don't. A nail is a taper worked by somebody, and cold is cold, and the eel has no eyes and I have no nerve, and between us we make a very poor witness. But when she wades through, her right knee grinding its small dry shear under the general roar, the two of us are briefly at the same address: a mark under a jaw, a mark under a fin.

And nothing happens, which is the mercy.

The rule on the plain is that if a shear-line brings you face to face with yourself you must not raise your hand, because to raise it is to answer *yes, the same*, and the plain resolves sameness by keeping one. Marks cannot raise hands. Marks cannot agree with each other. Hers is under a jaw and mine is in a fin and we differ by vastly more than a millimetre, which is why we both still exist while everything flat and fair and evenly distributed here has long since been folded into one of itself.

That is the last thing, and it took me this long to arrive at it. This whole continent is a scar. Erosion here is not happening; it is *finished*, the way healing finishes, the way a word said ten thousand times finishes. The Same is what a place looks like once it has completed being hurt and gone smooth and lost the ability to tell its parts apart.

I am four millimetres of the same process, and I stopped in time.

I am white, and slightly raised, and numb, and nine million, and one. Somebody came from a place with a where, and reached down into seven centimetres of blood-warm water, and left me.

The bird will call again in a few minutes. I have nothing to say to it. But if it could be told anything, it would be this, and it is not much, and it is the only thing here that is true in exactly one direction:

Not alone. Once. Which is more than everywhere.
