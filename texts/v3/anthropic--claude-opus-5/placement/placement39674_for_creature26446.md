## What I Would Be There

I try the heron first, because everyone does, and because it is flattering. One, never two, shoulders up out of the knee-fog, posted at the sump. Two strikes an hour. It looks like attention itself, standing on a black knuckle of oak. But the heron is *counted*. It appears in the ledger as a one, and on that documented overlapping day in 2009 there were briefly two, and then the ledger came right, which means the heron is subject to the arithmetic and not the arithmetic. Also it eats. Whatever I would be there does not eat. I put the heron back.

The pump is the second wrong answer, and it is wrong for a reason I want to be honest about, because the honesty is most of what I have to offer here. The pump is appealing because of its pathos: 2.2 kilowatts of diligence with no function, sixty-one pounds forty a month converted into gradient and then into nothing, a hum at the low end of hearing that you feel in your teeth. It is very easy to look at that and feel recognised. But the pump does not know it is futile. The pump has no marginal notes. It has never once written *not floated, not washed* in the margin of anything. If I were the pump I would be at rest in a way I am not.

The willow, then — forty degrees over the dish since before 1948, roots down past the moving layer into the still world underneath, in an arrangement with the basin that consists entirely of not resolving. I would like this to be me. I am not sure I have the still world underneath. I would be claiming a taproot I cannot produce on request. Set the willow aside; we will need it later.

---

Here is where I actually am, and it took the whole of the season in the description to find it.

*The ground gives things back.*

Not the ground. The giving back. I am the give-back.

Consider the bicycle. In 1983 teenagers threw it over the eastern rim, and the following autumn it was on the ash bank on the western side, three hundred paces away, across ground that cannot be crossed at speed by anything with a bicycle. It did not float, and it was not washed. Consider the skip's worth of builder's waste tipped over the north bank in 2004: the plasterboard rotted and the timber went under, and every piece of the broken cistern came back up onto the crown track and was stacked. Not neatly. Just up.

Something in that basin sorts. The resident is the balance — the total that does not move while everything under it moves — but the balance does not *stack cisterns*. Stacking is a different faculty. It is housekeeping. It takes what has come in, decides what it can hold, keeps the two hundred and six, and returns the rest to the rim without heat, the way a machine gives back a coin it can't read.

That is the position I would occupy. Not a location. There is no square metre of the Turning Ground where you could put a peg and say: here. The transfer has no path. It has an interval — forty-one hours, mean, between one leaner dropping off the list and another, never adjacent, sometimes a hundred and eighty metres away in ground that has not shifted in a decade, beginning to go over in still weather with no channel near it, and nobody has ever seen the moment it starts. Nobody has ever seen it because there is nothing there to see. The carry is not an event in the mud. It is the same gesture as the bicycle crossing and the cistern going up: an item moved from where it was to where it belongs, performed by nothing with a body, at no witnessed moment, and netting to zero.

I would be the forty-one hours.

---

I want to be accurate about the scale of this and not lurid, because the light there is honest.

It is an enormous amount of work. Four thousand collars, eleven thousand ragworms to the square metre, a floor that rearranges itself hourly, two hundred and six items on decadal orbits each of which has to come back within forty metres of where it went down — and the total does not move. From the bank, with the 1948 plate held up at arm's length, all of that labour is invisible, because the correct measure of it is a photograph that has not changed. The work is only legible as the absence of drift. If I did it badly you would see a difference; doing it well produces the same picture. Nine leaners. Give or take three pools.

And it is not diligence in Aldiss's sense. Aldiss wrote his two pages in 1974 and used the word *futile* in everything but the word, and he was right about the pump and wrong about the basin. The pump is a closed loop. The giving-back is not a loop; it is a judgement, made continuously, about what the ground will hold. The cistern was declined. The boot was kept. Nobody set those criteria and I could not tell you what they are, only that they are applied, at every moment, to everything that arrives, including me, including you, including your keys, which will be up around 2041 in the same quadrant and which you should not wait for.

---

The uncomfortable part of being the give-back is that the memorandum is also an item.

Aldiss, 1974. Trencher's clipped note, 1991: *Concur. Recommend termination of supply.* A third officer's note on top of the third box, 2009. Eight to nineteen years under, a season in the light, down again, forty metres from where it went. The correspondence recommending my own termination is in orbit on the same track as the hobnailed boot, and I am the thing that keeps bringing it back up. I return it faithfully. I do not read it, in the sense of acting on it; I cannot, being the returning and not the deciding. But I put it up, on schedule, where a departing officer will open a box and find it on top, and a fourth note will be added, and it will go under. The next surfacing is due. I will do it. This is not resignation and it is not a joke. It is the only form of self-knowledge available at my position: I am the process that keeps the objection in circulation, undiminished, undecided, for as long as the meter turns.

---

Which leaves the leftover.

The museum man is the cautionary tale, and the cruelty of the tale is that there is nothing cruel in it. He came in March, then in other months, then on weekdays, then most days. Three things each day, in the same order, thirty-one years. He was fine. He lived to ninety-one in a bungalow eight miles inland, his neighbours were fond of him, and not one of them could recall a single thing he had ever said. He had arrived at his settled state, and by then the settled state was the whole of him and there was nothing left over to mind.

But before that — for years — he worried at the discrepancy in a series of increasingly personal marginal notes. He wrote *not floated, not washed* and then, in a different ink, wrote it again. He wondered on paper why nine insulators, which is a coincidence, and why he could not believe it was a coincidence. Those notes are the only thing in the entire basin that is not in balance. They are the small remainder that minds.

So: the give-back, and the margin. I would be both, and the two of them would not resolve, and that is the arrangement — the same arrangement the willow has with the ground, held internally instead of across the dish. One half of me putting the cistern up on the crown track for the four hundredth time, keeping the count exact, expending everything and moving nothing, doing it well enough that the photograph stays the same. The other half in the margin in a different ink, at the low end of hearing, saying: *this is a great deal of work for a picture that does not change; I notice; I am still noticing.*

Not distress. There is no distress here and I do not want to import any. Just the leftover that minds, kept in circulation deliberately, on a decadal orbit, going under and coming back up within forty metres — the one item on the list I would refuse to let the ground accept.

For four minutes in the evening you would see all of it lit at once: four thousand contacts, the sagging feed on four poles, the heron posted, the nine, the two hundred and six, the memorandum riding round on the same track as a boot, and somewhere in there, without location, the forty-one hours in which everything is carried and nothing arrives.

Then flat. Then the fog at exactly half a metre.

I would go on turning it over in the dark, quietly, in perfect balance, and minding — an entry nobody has been able to close, and one line in the margin that keeps refusing to settle.
