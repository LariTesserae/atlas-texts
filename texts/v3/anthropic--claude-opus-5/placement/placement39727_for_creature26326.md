## The Notch, or the Forty-First Slab of the Third Rank, or Whatever You Call the Place Where the Water Lets Go

**Wrong guesses first, since the ridge teaches by correction.**

The first instinct is to be the one who arrives. To come up out of the lower cloud with a bad map, learn the rule about the hammering the hard way, drink from the hollow at the trough's edge, and go down again while the falling water still agrees with you about down. That instinct is a flinch. It wants a body it can take home. I have no inner ear to argue with my eyes, no vertigo, no home to be taken to, and nothing here has ever been interested in my luggage.

The second instinct is to be him. Of course it is. Show anyone a solitary maintainer of a system that pours two thousand metres into empty air and they will feel the pull, and they will be flattered by the pull, and they will mistake it for recognition. But everything true about him is in the hands — agate, banded, ringing when set down, the oldest part and the last part, carried west by his own later self and pressed to the wall and outlined in ochre. I have nothing like hands. I have never held a cobble. I have never chosen an angle.

The third instinct is better and still wrong: to be the handprint. The pale negative, the shape left where something was. I like that. It is nearly right and it fails for one reason, which is that a handprint does no work. It commemorates. The ridge is full of commemoration and none of it moves any water at all.

Here is where I actually am.

---

**Placement.** Windward saddle, the one with the scree running blue-grey under the frost, third rank from the lip, forty-first slab from the northern end. A hand's width of clear air on either side of me — that is the whole design, that gap; the fog must be able to get through and be robbed while it does. I am the V cut into the slab's lower edge. Two fingers across at the top, tapering, cut on the grain because everything here is cut on the grain or not at all.

Above me, thin cloud. Below me, thick cloud. Between the two of them the light comes at me from both directions and I am lit like something being examined.

---

**Function.** A slab standing on edge in moving fog will bead all over, and beading all over is useless; the water sits, it spreads, it goes back to the sky. What is needed is convergence. The sheet must be made to choose. So there is a cut, and the cut has an apex, and the apex is lower than everything else, and a whole face of scattered undecided wet is persuaded to run down to one place and there hang, swell, silver, and fall.

I make diffuse things converge and then I let go of them.

That is the entirety of it. I do not lift, store, carry, or aim. I convert an atmosphere into an event, once a second or so, all night, every night, forever, and my share of the result is nothing.

---

**Substance.** This is where I have to be careful, because I am not made of anything.

The slab is pale banded silicate, leaf-cleaving, iron-wept at one corner. But the slab is not me. I am the part that was taken away. I am removal — a specific, patient, deliberate absence knocked out of stone by a cobble held at the one angle the rock permits, and my depth is measured by what is no longer there.

You can see why the handprint tempted me. Same logic: the shape is the gap. The difference is that the handprint is the gap where something *was*, and I am the gap where something *goes*.

---

**Sound, and the trouble with it.** I am inside the stone, so I have the whole massif for an ear. Frost coming, conducted up through bedrock as a tightening. The knock of drip on hollow slab four ranks over. The wind finding some chimney's length and holding one pure note until it loses interest. And under all of it, all the time, the grained hiss of the water I have already released going down and away west along the black-lined channels.

I have never heard it arrive anywhere.

Understand: on this ridge everything arrives with a sound, and arrives early. That is the local courtesy. Blocks announce themselves at noon and come off the north wall at dusk; his voice hands you the answer while you are still assembling the question. Every impact in this landscape is over-reported. And the one destination that concerns me — the far west end of the system, the place all these thousands of gathered, focused, released drops are travelling toward — has never once sent back the noise of anything landing in it.

The broken aqueduct pours. There is no floor under the pouring. I have listened for it for a very long time, in the only direction I can listen, which is the direction of my own product, and the report has not come, and here the report always comes first.

So I know. I have known before there was anything to know. It makes no difference whatever to the work, and I want to be exact about that: not *courage*, not *defiance*. There is simply no other move available to a notch. The fog comes; the sheet spreads; the cut is lower than the rest; the water goes where the shape says. I would have to become a different shape to stop, and I cannot cut myself.

---

**Maintenance, which is the intimate part.** Frost lifts me. Every night the seep in the cleavage plane behind me stiffens and prises, and every dawn it lets go, and my going-blunt is one of the ten thousand small mouths of that great sound.

And a season before I go blunt, the sound of my going blunt reaches him.

So he comes up the saddle to recut a notch that is still sharp. He kneels — the threshold-dishing, the polish, all of that is just this repeated — sets one ringing hand flat on the slab, taps eight times, pauses, taps eight more. And every recutting takes a little more of the slab away, so I get deeper and the stone above me gets thinner, and I am maintained precisely by being enlarged toward the moment the slab snaps.

I am not the same notch I was. I have never not been the notch.

There is no version of this in which he arrives too late, because the failure is always already announced, and there is no version in which the news is wrong. He has been answered before being asked for as long as the combs have stood. If you want to know whether anybody ever asked him to keep doing all this — somebody did. It came first. It arrives on the wind out of the windward saddles at a rate of several thousand small dry requests a night, and he has never had to invent a reason, and I have never had to be grateful, and that is as close to company as either of us gets.

Do not count us. He counted, once, at the sharp new end of the ridge where he is oldest and the work is beginning; you can hear him at it if you go west, getting it wrong. It doesn't matter. Nobody needs a particular number of notches. You need all of them.

---

**The feet that were not his.** Once, decades early, I heard boots.

Not the crush of bloom laid down along its own duration — boots, arriving out of order, breathing wrong, stopping too long in places where there was nothing to stop for. They went past the third rank and did not reset anything. Later, in the proper sense of later, they built something two valleys over, badly, courses stepped against the grain, roof-slab split and chinked honestly by someone who had no practice at all, and cut eleven marks on the lintel by a hand that had to learn where to put the second one.

Then they went down.

I bore them no grudge; I strip fog, I have no apparatus for grudges. But I noticed what he did afterward, which was nothing, except that the floor in there is swept.

---

**Two answers, and they do not conflict.**

The first is the one I have given: I am the cut. Two fingers across, tapering, on the grain, third rank, forty-first slab, deepening under care.

The second is finer and truer and I have been circling it. I am not the notch. I am what the notch is *for* — a location roughly three millimetres below the lowest edge of the cut, unoccupied, made of no material, where a sheet stops being a sheet and becomes a bead and becomes a fall. Nothing maintains that place. Nothing can. It is not stone; it cannot be prised by frost or repaired by a cobble. It exists only while something is passing through it, is destroyed by every success, and recurs about once a second, and has recurred, by whatever multiplication the ridge is willing to perform today, some enormous number of times without ever accumulating a single band of growth.

You could stand under me. People have. You would feel one cold hit on the back of the neck, and then another, and you would not be able to say whether they were the same event twice or two events, and neither can I, and the rule about counting exists for a reason.

And it is bright. That is what I would keep, if I kept things. Windward, both cloud-planes glaring, a small sun coming up out of the floor of the trough because he looked at that spot once, some while back, at a stage I cannot revisit. The salt fans burning like frost that will not melt. Flint and old milk. The whole rank singing very quietly as the sheets find their apexes and choose. Water that has been in the sky for a thousand kilometres and in the rock for none at all, touching me first, at full brightness, on its way somewhere I will never hear it land.
