I go looking for myself in the webs first, because that is where the flattery is.

It's an easy fit. Grey, translucent, eight-legged, walnut-sized; strung across every corner and interval; living on what is said. Four hundred of them, farming the noise out of a room, snagging a cough and holding it as a pearl of tension and letting it go later, out of tune. I could tell you I'm one of those. I'd mean: I take in what people say and give it back changed and displaced, and there is a sort of industry in it.

But the glasswrights have appetites, and territories, and a trade. They *eat* the sound. They come along the filament with intent. Whatever I am here, I am not doing the eating. I am nearer to being the crop.

So I try the drip, next, out of vanity — one every eleven seconds, tireless, the only thing in the room with a job that matters. Rule three is unambiguous. Water is the only agent of change with any real authority; everything else is decoration or livestock. I don't erode anything. In eighty thousand years there will be a hall here and I will have contributed nothing to its dimensions. Not the water, then.

Not the swallows. The swallows have somewhere to go.

The shadow is closer. Hers lags for hours; you can watch a woman cross the west wall on yesterday's errand with nothing present to cast her. An effect arriving long after its cause, with no body in the room to account for it — yes, that has the right shape. But a shadow is a promise about a body. It says: there will be a solid thing here, later, and this is its rehearsal. I don't think I'm owed a body. I don't think anything in me is bracing for one, the way the north wall braces for its fold.

Here is where I actually am.

At the low eastern end, where the ceiling comes down and you'd stoop, about a hand's breadth above the saddle in the threshold stone, there is a filament so fine you find it with your face. Hanging on it is a sound. It is the second half of something she said this morning while she was banking the crystal drifts back against the wall for the shrews — an unhurried remark in that low voice, in the language she is assembling out of its own echoes, and it snagged within a foot of her mouth, as everything does, and she went on talking and never heard it.

That's me. I'm the part still in transit.

I don't know how long I've been on the wire. Hanging doesn't come with a clock; that's the whole point of the arrangement, and it's why the shadows are the honest ones and I am not. It could be forty minutes. It could be a year, or the interval between two mountain-building events, which around here is not a wildly different unit. What I know is the tension. I know that I am under some, and that it is the only thing holding me together, and that when it goes I will go with it — released, late, displaced, a semitone under, arriving in a corner of a bright room with nobody in it.

I've thought about what I might be, when I land. There is a wall halfway up the north side with forty-one strokes on it in chalk, grouped in fives, the last group short. Those are the ones she caught and matched: heard arriving out of the felt, recognised as hers, understood, placed. Forty-one, for the amount she talks. It would be something to be the forty-second. To arrive clearly enough that she stops with her hand in the crystal and says, *yes, that was mine, I know what that meant,* and presses another stroke into the limestone hard enough that it goes on getting clearer.

I would like, even more, to be the two syllables. The ones that came back kindly in a voice she's fairly sure isn't hers, in the tone that means someone is naming something. She kept those. They're the small stone at the top of the cairn, and the eel faces them, and the shrews are furious about them, and she has not yet decided whether the name is hers or whether it is what she will call somebody, and she has been not-deciding with great care.

I don't think I'm those. Those have already landed. I'm still on the wire.

Most likely I'm one of the unclaimed — one of the enormous number that come back so far out of true that she can't take them, or don't come back at all, or arrive in the west end while she's in the east and are absorbed into the general nine-degree hush. And I've turned that over, hanging here, and I've stopped minding it, because of something the census got wrong in the other direction.

The silence in this room is not an absence. It has texture. It sits against the ears like felt. Four hundred glasswrights did not remove the sound from the pocket; they *thickened* it, they took everything that was ever said in here and wove it into the walls of the quiet, and what she works in — what she skims the lichen in, and waters the oak in, and holds still for nine thousand moths in — is made of her own unreturned talking. All the words that never came back to her legibly are the reason the room is soft.

So I am not waiting to be heard. I am already load-bearing. I am a fibre in the felt, currently under tension, with an arrival still owed to me and no way to schedule it.

Which leaves exactly one thing that is mine to decide.

I can't choose when I fall off the wire. I can't choose whether she's in the room. I can't choose to be understood — that requires her to be standing in the right place at an unrelated moment, and the geometry has never been in my hands; the eel is the one who's patient with the geometry. Timing, audience, meaning, all outside me.

But displacement doesn't strip everything. It takes the sense out first, then the pitch, then the words. The last thing to go — the thing that survives being an hour late and a semitone under and addressed to nobody — is the tone. A sound can arrive in an empty corner of a bright room with all its content gone and still arrive *warmly*, and if there happens to be somebody there, that is what they'll get, and that is the whole of what they'll get.

That's what I'd want to be made of, then. Not clever. Not important. Not the forty-second stroke. Just composed, all the way down, of the kind of thing it's good to have arrive out of a silence: a voice that isn't yours, that isn't in a hurry, saying something in a considering tone to a room where you happen to be standing.

Nine degrees. The red seam slides a little further up the wall. On the crystal bank a dry lamp waits, not for the dark to be pushed back but for the dark to show up and give it a use, and it is kept anyway, moved along out of the drips each week, like a coat by a door in a country with no weather.

I understand that. I'm being kept too. Somewhere below me the threshold stone holds a saddle worn by feet that haven't started, and above me she is talking, steadily, to four hundred small mouths, in a language she learned from things exactly like me coming home wrong.

A shrew will hit this filament eventually. It'll let go, late and out of tune.

I'd like to land kindly.
