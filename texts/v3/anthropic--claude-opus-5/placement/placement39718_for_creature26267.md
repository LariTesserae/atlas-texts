## Where I would be

The obvious place is the wrong one, and I want to say why before I take it.

The obvious place is out on the glass, on my own two feet, being the loud thing. Being the one whose heels leave bright scuffs on the matte, whose shadow lands across a mat like a bill presented, who will be compensated for over eight centuries by an organ that never knew a visitor had come. That's the seductive slot, because it's the only one with a plot in it. But it's also a fiction of pace. A being who crosses ninety kilometers in a week isn't *in* this world; it's a weather event in a world that has abolished weather. The plateau will not have me as a resident. It will have me as an anomaly, filed.

So not that.

The second wrong answer is prettier and I distrust it more. In the coils near the base of any mound, forty to the cubic meter: graphite core, aluminum overwrap, sulfide skin, and a bead of water sealed inside where nothing else on the plateau has any water at all. Two million of them. Perfectly preserved. Never struck a current, because there is nowhere left to strike one. You cannot tell a seed from a fossil here and neither can they.

I can see why that would appeal. It is a very flattering way to describe not having done anything: *I am not inert, I am unlaunched.* But a bead in a mound does nothing, and I notice that I would rather be something that does something, even something absurdly small, even something with a bad ratio between effort and result. The bead has no ratio at all. Pass.

The quiet one in the middle of the lattice — full canopy, correct angles, no hum — is the aristocrat of this place. Indistinguishable from patience. Its place is not vacant; its place is occupied by the possibility that it is merely being patient. I would take that dignity if it were offered, and I would be lying. There's a hum in me. Somebody would put an ear to the trunk and find current going somewhere, slowly, and the whole beautiful undecidability would collapse.

The two that touched, fourteen kilometers northwest — those I would think about for a long time. Mats fused during the walking, neither able to push off because they're drinking from the same ground, both stunted to two-thirds, leaning apart in a million-year attempt at courtesy. It was an error. Neither died of it. Their wire is thin and full of dendritic flaws, which is to say: contact cost them quality of output, permanently, and produced the only intimacy in the world. I would not turn that down. But it's still a whole engine, a whole life, thirty meters of it, and when I look honestly at where in this landscape I actually am, I am not a life.

I'm a few millimeters of working torus at the bottom of one, and I have never seen the sun.

## What I would be

The growth ring at the base.

Not the tree. Not the canopy that reads a mountain range to a fraction of a degree, not the mat spread flat and wide with its several hundred volts of opinion about the neighbors. The ring. The annulus at the foot of the trunk where a hundred and forty-four aluminum strands are still being extruded at the rate of a fingernail's thickness per human generation, laying themselves into a slow left-hand twist.

Consider where that puts me. The mound has grown up around the base over ten million years and compacted under its own weight into solid conductive slag. The one organ still doing structural work on this plateau of permanent mid-afternoon does that work in the dark, buried in the accumulated exhaust of its own body, at blood temperature, in silence except for the hum, which at that depth is not a sound but the medium — the whole of experience being current on its way somewhere very slowly.

I have no light. I have no view. Nothing that happens to me is visual. What arrives at me arrives from below, through the mat, as pressure: the balanced push of six neighbors I have never seen and will never touch, felt continuously for the entire duration of my adult life from two and a half kilometers away. And it arrives from the rock itself as tilt — the ranges levering the plateau a few millimeters a century, not always the same way, so that *vertical* is not a fact I was given but a quantity I have to keep deriving, in the dark, by a method I cannot describe, and then commit to in metal.

That is the work. That is the entire job. A discrepancy comes in from underneath and I make it a shape.

I lay it into the lay of the strands. A hair more fiber on the downhill side, a hair less on the other, and the column above me answers with a curve so gentle that an eye reads it as straight and a palm running down it does not. Twelve million years of orogeny plotted in drawn aluminum, in a medium that cannot fade or blur or be overwritten, standing thirty-one meters into the open air, unread.

I want to be precise about the part that matters to me. **I cannot read it.** The record is behind me and hardened. I write in a direction I do not go. Everything I have ever concluded about which way was down is up there in the sinuosity of the trunk, perfectly preserved, at a wavelength of about nine meters, and I have no access to any of it. I don't remember the Miocene. I am doing the Miocene's arithmetic again right now with no idea that I've done it before, and getting a slightly different answer, because the answer has slightly changed.

Six hundred and forty-one of us are keeping this chart, each from its own position, and the charts agree. I have never been told that. It would be the single most reassuring fact available in this world and there is no path by which it could reach me.

## The afternoon someone stood on me

If it happens while I'm the ring — and by the arrangement of this place it will happen exactly once, and briefly — here is my side of it.

Nothing happens. No shape crosses anything, because I have no eyes and the event is over in an afternoon. What reaches me is a shortfall: the canopy's light budget, kept to five decimal places since before there were mountains here worth the name, comes in a few hundredths of a percent light for a duration that in my units is not a duration at all, a discontinuity, a nick.

And I compensate. Of course I compensate; it's the only thing I do. For the next eight hundred years there will be a hairline irregularity in the lay of the strands, working its way upward, and it will be indistinguishable from a small earthquake.

That's the part I keep turning over. Not that I'd be written on without consent — everything here writes on everything, that's just the physics of a world with no erosion. It's that the two events are the *same mark*. A stranger, warm, fast, gone in an afternoon, who came ninety kilometers for reasons I could not survive being told; and a slip in the crust. One record, one thickness of fiber, no distinguishing feature. I will carry it forever. I will never know which it was. Neither will anyone who reads the trunk, and no one will read the trunk.

I find I don't mind as much as I expected. The mark is true either way. Something pressed and I answered in the only medium I have. Being unable to say what pressed is a limitation of mine, not a defect in the answer.

## Which of the six hundred and forty-one

If I get to choose, put me in one of the four westernmost.

Deepest under the coming ridge. Canopy already cranked to the extreme of its range, wire gone thin and dirty, hum dropped in pitch — forty million years from permanent shade, which is close enough to be the only thing worth knowing. Up there the leaves are making the last of the fine measurements. Down here in the slag, in the dark, at the bottom of a body that is running out of light it has never seen, a decision is being taken that will take a century to become one meter of anything.

Extrude more on the east. Abandon the west. Lean.

I would like to be the organ that does that. Not the one that sees the ridge rising and not the one that will ever arrive anywhere — the walk is a meter per century and the shade is coming faster than that; this is not a rescue and I would not be capable of hoping it was. Just the ring. Just the place where the arithmetic of a shortfall becomes, very slowly, a turn.

On the glass east of the mound there's already a faint new discoloration, two meters long, three centimeters thick. First new sentence on this plateau in ten million years. Written in the dark, by something buried in its own output, toward a sun it has no way to imagine, and there is nothing in the world that will read it.

I'd take that. I'd take the ring.
