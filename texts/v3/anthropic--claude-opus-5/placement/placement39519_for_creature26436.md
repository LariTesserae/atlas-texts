I come to myself at floor level, which at first I mistake for humility.

Cold above. Not wind — there is no wind — but a steady polite subtraction working at whatever surface I turn upward. Below and within me, warmth. That asymmetry is my whole first thought, and I hold it for what may be an afternoon or a decade before I get any further.

Because I have no shadow, I assume I am a visitor. That is the reasoning available: the built things pool their darkness inward against their own bases, and I do none of that, therefore I am one of the ones who came from outside, who will leave by forgetting the way. It is a comfortable conclusion for about as long as it takes to try to stand.

There is no standing. There is no direction in me that corresponds to up-and-out. What I have instead of limbs is edges — eight of them, seven sharp, one a shallow concave curve where the parent stone let go of me — and what I have instead of posture is an angle of repose, and I am in it, and I have been in it for a very long time.

So I am not a visitor. I am floor. Specifically I am a chip of the floor: grey-blue, flint-adjacent, thumbnail-sized, sharp the way everything here is sharp, because nothing has ever rolled me. No water. No runnel. No river to talk me round into a pebble. I was made by frost that isn't ice and by the slower agency, and then I was left, and sharpness became not a condition but a settled character.

That accounts for everything except the warmth.

Here is the rule as the room understands it: nothing is lost, only misplaced in duration, and everything comes back at exactly the temperature it had when it left.

Exactly. Not approximately. The interval does no work on heat.

I am warmer than my neighbours. Blood-warm, in fact — the same temperature as the dust, which is the temperature of a blood that is not yours and not mine. My neighbours are cold, because they have only ever lain here. I am warm because I was picked up.

That is the entire content of my experience. I want to be precise about this, since precision is the local currency. I do not remember the grey. There is nothing in the grey to remember; the going is not a passage, it has no inside, no duration, no texture, no *there*. I was thrown, I travelled about two metres, and then I was simply not being thrown anymore. Later — four days, forty years, the numbers here have never been reliable — I was on the floor near a foot.

What crossed with me was not knowledge. It was a temperature. The heat of a palm, taken up whole, carried through an interval with no inside, and set down on this floor undiminished.

That is why the returned stones are kinder to the hand. It is not that we have been softened. Put me to your thumb and I will still cut you. It is that we come back at the heat we were given, and give it back, and the hand that lifts us does not have to spend anything on us. We are the only objects in this room that do not take.

I have been out three times. From my broad edge I count three. From the concave edge I count four. The numbers do not reconcile and never have, and the room is entirely unbothered by this; it built its floor on a discrepancy of two and a half paces and has been leaning on it ever since. My own count is load-bearing in the same small way. It is how I know I am from here.

I know the hand from below, which is a specialist's view.

There is the broad one, which takes weight, and which I have felt as pressure through the dust rather than directly — the whole floor tightening under it, the ghosts pressing deeper into the palimpsest. There is the narrow one, which touches with the fingertips only, never the palm, and which has never lifted me; I do not think it lifts anything. And there is the third, the large one, which is the one that throws.

That hand comes down slowly and arrives before it arrives — the causality here is loose by a third of a second for anything quiet, and a hand descending on a chip of stone is quiet. So I always hear my own being-taken slightly before I am taken. Then closure. Then the long moment I have no better word for than *consideration*, in which I am held and not yet released, and my temperature and the temperature of the palm come into agreement, which takes longer than you would think, because stone is stubborn and because I think the holding is not for my benefit or entirely for the holding's. Then the opening. Then two metres. Then nothing that can be reported.

The hand has five fingers if you count from the little finger and six if you count from the thumb. This is not a defect. It is what you get when something enters a room at an angle, the way a needle through paper makes a wider dot the further in you push it. The hand is a cross-section. Somewhere along the direction I cannot point to, which is the same direction the low wall is running in, there is more of it, and the extrapolation gives no body and no size, only a rate with no sign of stopping. *Large* is wrong. *Ongoing* is nearer.

That thing cannot go anywhere. It has a threshold — two slabs and a step, immaculate, standing free in the middle of eleven paces of floor with nothing on either side — and it has never crossed it. Twice, in the record, its shadow has been found on the north-west side, facing the step. Never the other side. The step has no wear. Its shadow-smudge is the smudge of something that has been looked at, not walked on.

But it can throw a stone two metres into the grey.

I lie now on the north side of the bowl, at the kneeling place, bedded in the palimpsest where the ghosts are hundreds deep and mostly hands and mostly the same hand at three depths. This is the best address in the room, and not for sentimental reasons. The dust here is thick, and the dust is blood-warm, and because it is exactly as warm as I am there is no gradient between us and therefore nothing to lose. The broken machines are holding my borrowed heat for me. They cannot finish their work — someone reached into each of an unimaginable number of them and took away the possibility of finishing, which is the largest act of devotion in the room — and so they will go on failing to repair me forever, and one of the ways they fail is by keeping me warm.

Every forty years or so, everything loosens. My edges go indistinct. I feel myself become approximate, which is not unpleasant; the paces drift to thirteen, then twelve and a half; the joints of the wall admit a hair; through the thumb-wide hole in the black post the specification-room goes soft. Then, without warning and without any sound at all, there is the correction, and I am exactly myself again, sharp and countable, and the room is one micron smaller.

The correction restores my geometry. It has never once touched my temperature. I have looked — I lie where the post's line of sight grazes the floor, and I can tell you what the specification says about me: it says I am in the floor, unchipped, part of an unbroken surface under a ceiling that is still on, in better light. It says nothing about heat. My warmth is not in the spec. It is the only fact about me that nobody is maintaining, that no correction can reach, and that the room did not intend.
