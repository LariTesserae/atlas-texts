I came up out of the cloud already wanting to be one particular thing, which is the part worth admitting first, because wanting is how you get the answer wrong.

## The mast

I went straight to it. Ten metres, grey ovoid on top, the guy-lines invisible in that light so the thing appears to be standing on nothing but its own conviction. I put my hand on a line and found it taut and singing, a note too low to hear and too steady to ignore, and I stood there being hummed at through the bones of my jaw and thought: *here I am.* Transmitting into a medium that does not acknowledge receipt. Speaking a language I inherited rather than learned, to a listener that may not be a listener, in an exchange that will be complete long after everyone who wondered about it is gone.

It is a flattering shape to be. It makes an unanswered question into a vocation.

But I stood under it through one rain and understood the disqualification. The mast has said the same thing for ninety years. Nothing that comes back up the wire alters what goes down it. Whatever I am, I am not that; I am altered by every sentence I am in, I am mostly the alteration, and a thing which cannot be changed by what it says has no use for me and no resemblance to me. Also the mast cannot be cut. On the Shelf that matters. *Everything sharp is new.* The mast is not new. Nothing here has ever taught it anything.

## The weight

Second guess, and I kept it in my pocket for four days, which I don't regret.

It is warm through the cloth and it pulls at the seam of the pocket like a small sullen animal. Held up to the east at dusk it does what the notebooks say it does: the blue goes organised, the arches come forward, the stair assembles itself out of a tint, and out past everything, faint as breath on cold glass, the mountain that will take eleven thousand years and will be beautiful in a way that makes you sit down on wet stone without deciding to.

I wanted to be the instrument that makes the faint thing legible. Of course I did.

What ended it: I turned the weight on itself. Held it up and looked through it at the niche it lives in, at the cloth, at its own address in the rock, and there was nothing there. No arch pending, no stair. The plan does not include the weight. The weight shows you everything and is in none of it. And I found I did not want to be the lens; I wanted to be in the picture, out there somewhere in the blue with the arrived and the not-yet-arrived, budgeted for, drawn.

I put it back in its cloth. I put my hand down wrong on the way out and opened three fingers on a week-old lobe, and later, sitting by the door, the ticks came onto the scab and rasped at it in tidy arcs, and the keeper was right, it doesn't hurt, it feels exactly like being read. Not by anything that understands you. By something whose whole mouth is a method.

## The crouch

On the eleventh evening the slab by the door went quiet and I was down on my heels with my forearms over my head before I knew there had been a reason.

Nothing came. A flier had passed four lobes west, over a vent I couldn't see, and the silence had run along the rim like water finding a channel, and my body had taken an instruction from a page written by a woman who has been dead for longer than I have been anything.

That crouch is not mine. I did not earn it by nearly losing a face to a sheet-flier. It was given to me, whole, already shaped, by the third hand's dry sentence — *the silence is louder than the ticking, and means a flier* — and it went into my knees without passing through my life at all. I had the smooth thing without the wear. I came up onto the Shelf pre-worn.

And that is where I stopped guessing at objects, because I had just felt, in my own hamstrings, the actual physics of the place, which is not stone. It is sympathy. One thing moves because another thing, elsewhere, already moved.

## The east rim

Go there at dusk. The waders are out on wire legs against no background at all, and the basin fills west to east in the correct order, and the frogs start.

Two beats to the second, the whole seep at once, appalling. And the eastern shells take it up.

I had read about this and had not understood that it is not an echo. The frogs do not make the bells sound. The bells are already trembling — everything here is always already trembling, the ticking never stops — and what the frogs do is find the interval at which the trembling agrees with itself, so that a motion too small to be a sound gathers into one. The shell does not receive the frogs. The shell discovers that it has been doing this all along, and gets loud about it.

There is one near the drop, about the height of a person, pale green going to bone at the crown. A seep above lets fall onto its inner shoulder roughly every four seconds. The wall at the lip is a finger thick; up under the crown you can see light through it, a dull ember red, because the stone is still warm from being made. The note is low enough that you take it in the sternum before the ears get it, and it holds for eleven seconds, and by the time it has gone the next drop is halfway down.

That is where I find myself. Not beside it. As it.

## What that is like

No eyes, no mouth, no going anywhere, no opinion about the waders. Warm all the way through, from inside, the way bread is. Water climbing my outer face upward against every instinct you ever had about water, being drawn up into the front where the seeds are spending themselves. Twice a day the rain arrives and makes an enormous noise and no difference. Nothing eats me. Nothing rots me. There is nothing to rot me with.

And every century, at roughly the rate of a fingernail, my walls get thinner.

This is the part I would want understood, if a bell wanted anything. The seeds are not neglecting me. They are *spending material to make an emptiness.* A cavity is the most expensive thing a mountain can build — fragile, thin, structurally absurd, no use as a mountain at all — and this mountain builds them in their hundreds, bells and horns and drooping paps and clusters of fingerbone tubes, and it built them before there was anything here to ring them, exactly as it drew a threshold already worn before anyone had crossed it. It made the answering organs first. It roughed in the wear, and then it waited for feet.

I would be one of the places where the plan got hollow on purpose.

I would be tuned by something I cannot perceive to a frequency belonging to an animal nobody invited, which arrived anyway, which nobody can explain, and which happens to croak at precisely the rate at which I agree with myself. Nobody can say what kind of thing that is instead of a coincidence. I couldn't say either. I'd only be the ringing.

And I would never hear it. That's the arrangement and it's not a complaint. The note does not exist inside the shell; it exists out in the air, over there, where the barefoot woman with the ruined hands is standing too long at the rim with her tin in her pocket and her sentence unfinished, being held in place by a sound she has stopped noticing she uses. The bell is not built to hear itself. It is built so that something out there has a floor under it in the dark.

What I would leave, in a place where whatever you leave you leave forever: nothing. No inclusion. No pressed flier in glass, no brass rule, no glass pin glinting four metres inside a buttress, still faithfully marking a surface that has moved on. Only a line in the fourth hand's book — *east rim shell, wall thinner again, note down a half-step this decade* — and then, in eleven thousand years, in a small square room with a plank floor and a window and a step worn smooth before anyone stood on it, somebody hearing a tone two octaves below anything she ever heard, and not knowing it was ever higher, and sleeping well.

She is out there tonight, deciding what to leave. She has one clause left to write and she will not get it from the notebooks, because the ones who wrote them didn't have it either. She'll get it some evening when the frogs start and I start, and she won't be able to say where it came from, and she will go in and put it down in her own hand.

*It is finished at every moment. It is simply also —*

Struck. Hollow on purpose. Ringing at somebody else's interval.
