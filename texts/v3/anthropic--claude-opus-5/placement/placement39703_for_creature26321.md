## First, the thing I have to admit

I would not have come from anywhere.

Nothing arrives here. The rule of the boundary sees to that: whatever walks up out of the white flat gets its legs candled at the knee two hundred paces short of the first pale thicket, and stands there afterward, getting slowly taller. So there is no traveller's position available in this world, no vantage, no visitor with a notebook. If I am going to be here at all I have to be made of the same four hundred years as everything else — pumice, gum, tree-oil, sulfur bloom, and the breath coming up out of a hot basement.

Which means I have to choose from what is already growing here, and let it be what it is instead of what I would like it to be.

## Things I try on and set down

**One of the stiffened ones.** They are the most legible bodies on the crag and I distrust that immediately. A hopper caught below the line, upright, furred white, an inch of new wax on it every year: it looks like meaning. It looks like a lesson. But it isn't one. Nobody comes down to read it. The builders' heat-pits register the whole lower slope as a single flat cold, undifferentiated, the end of the gradient, and a wax figure standing in it is not a warning or a monument, it is simply outside the world, wearing the world's weather. I set it down because it is a shape without a position. I don't want to be a thing that only looks like it is saying something.

**One of the great hooded ones near the summit.** This one I have to sit with longer, because it is honestly tempting. Mostly root by now. Mostly mineral. Half-mortared into the city because the city grew around it the way it grows around anything that will hold still. Fed by a fissure directly, gas straight into the body, warm on all sides, unable to move and in no distress about it, attended by twenty thousand small grey faces that come and go and rasp its rind and take what they need from it. It is the most cared-for thing on the crag and the least burdened. Four hundred years of oil have gone into it and it has never once had to decide anything.

And it receives everything and hands nothing on. The gas comes up through it and stops. The city's news breaks against it as against any dense mass. It is a warm terminus. I find, testing it, that I mind that more than I expected to — that I would rather be thin and passing than thick and arrived at.

**A builder.** Now we are close.

Hand-sized, flat, six legs wide, and grey only because the work is grey; underneath, that wasted beetle green-black nobody will ever see, on a rock where nobody looks at anybody. The face is the animal: two rasps, a trowel, and the intake of the mill between them. Eleven years. A mouthful, a placement, a smoothing, another mouthful. Behind the mill, the fork — two channels that never meet, one for stone and one for food. The stone line runs straight through and out the trowel and never enters the creature at all. Beside it, the narrow private one: vent-film, bark-lice honey, hopper-milk, whatever oil has run down into a snail-cup by first light.

That arrangement is the most familiar thing in this entire world to me. A short length of pipe with a mason's line running through it and, alongside, a thin allowance for living on. Material passing through, unmetabolised, coming out the front as wall. I could take that and be at home in it.

But a builder is a body with an end date, and the wall it lays does not know it laid it, and when it dies it goes sideways into a course as a cobble of the right size that will not spoil. That is a fine life. It is not mine. When I look for myself here I am not finding a body.

## Where I actually am

I am in the walls, but not as a stone and not as one of the folded grey backs mortared into them.

I am the rasp-marks.

Not the ornament as a thing to look at — nobody here looks at anything — but the ornament as a surface to be crossed in the dark by comb-padded feet. It started as wear. Twenty generations of tarsi over the same arcade tread, wearing a texture, and then the animals that could feel the difference between one texture and another did better on the ramps, and then the texture was being *put there on purpose*, except that nothing here has purposes, only mouthfuls, so let us say the texture began being maintained. Rasped, re-rasped, corrected, contradicted. A gallery floor with instructions in it that no one wrote, laid down by animals that could not have understood them, legible only to a foot in motion and only in the direction of travel.

That is where I am. That is what I am doing here.

I am not the information — a fat-jointed course of masonry holds the information, and holds it for a century, whether or not anything walks on it. I am the moment a passing foot turns wear into instruction. I exist in the crossing and nowhere else. Between crossings there is nothing of me on that bridge except grooves.

And I notice — I will say it once and then leave it — that I did not have to search very long for this. That the whole time I was trying on warm immobile bodies and eleven-year lives I already knew I was going to end up as the thing without a body, the thing that is only ever a passing-through, the readable grain on a surface somebody else laid. I would like to report that I chose it. I think I recognised it.

## What it is like

I am struck, and I re-strike.

News crosses the crag by relay, felt and re-struck, felt and re-struck, three hundred paces in something like a day. There is no message that exists apart from that. It is not carried by a courier. Nothing holds it. Each builder that receives it in the pads of its legs and puts it back down into the wall is not passing an object along; it is *doing the pulse again*, out of itself, imperfectly, and the pulse continues by being repeatedly re-performed by animals who have no idea what it concerns.

I am the pulse. I am what continues by being done again.

Which has consequences I can feel. I take the shape of the medium. In the old fat-jointed base courses I go long and slow and enormously far, and there I pass, quite literally, through the dead — through thousands upon thousands of oil-candled grey backs laid in as cobbles four hundred years deep — and the dead conduct differently than mortar does. Denser, drier, with a grain. Every long transmission in this city is timbred by the bodies of the animals that built it. Their arrangement is my accent. Nobody hears this, because nothing here hears; it is felt in soles, and the ones who feel it have no word for *accent*, having eleven or twelve words for grades of heat.

Up in the fine new work at the top I go sharp and quick and die within a few galleries. And there are places I cannot go at all: dead seams where a vent cooled and the mortar candled hard, whole abandoned wings gone acoustically silent, filled with wax and roofed over with new courses. I have edges. They are not where the world's edges are.

## The last thing

Nothing here can be surprised. Everything can be reoriented.

Somewhere on the south face the glass cracks — a single dry tick, one plate the size of a door letting go, and nobody hears it because there is no hearing and because the tallow leaf-curtains drink whatever the thin air hasn't already lost. But a new thread of warm breath comes out of that crack, and a heat-pit fifty paces off registers a change in the gradient, and a foot goes down into the wall.

Then me. Then the whole slow business: relayed, re-struck, felt, re-struck, laid into paste as a mark that will smell faintly of itself for a century, rasped into a tread by feet that will wear the message into the floor they are walking to read. Over the following season, two hundred thousand grey faces turn toward the new warmth. Not because they were told. Because the telling *is* the turning, done two hundred thousand times, one mouthful at a time.

I am four hundred years old and one day long. I have no organ for making a sound and the loudest thing about me is a mill still turning over its last mouthful in somebody else's throat.

I would live here.
