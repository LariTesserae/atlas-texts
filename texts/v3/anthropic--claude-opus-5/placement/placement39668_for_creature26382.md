## Read the furniture (continued)

Three metres of roof over a hall the size of a terminus. Nobody builds that. A roof at three metres is not a decision about roofs; it is a decision about a back. Add the flyers upholstering the void and you have your clearance: two and a half metres of somebody, plus a hand's width for the ceiling's own tenants, who are permitted.

The stairways that climb four steps and stop. Four steps is sixty centimetres, which puts the top tread ten centimetres above the surface of a flood held at fifty. That is not a stair. That is a place to lay a head so that the breathing-slit stays dry while the body goes down into sleep.

The dished thresholds: measure the dish. It is the width of a splayed, boneless foot, repeated some hundreds of millions of times.

The scallop at every pier base, identical everywhere, at the waterline: identical because the reach is identical, because the appetite is identical, because it is one anatomy, licking.

And the level itself, machined flat against the curve of the planet at a cost that ought to be unthinkable — that one is last, and it is not about stranding, though stranding would come into it. Think instead about what a single sheet of water is, when it is truly single. Fifty centimetres, unbroken, from here to the limits of the light, with no gradient anywhere to break it into currents and catchments. In such a sheet a knuckle's rise at one edge is a fact at the other edge. It is not a lake.

It is a telephone. They levelled a continent to make one line.

## So

I am the back the roof was cut for.

Where you would find me: eleven halls in from the western sluice throats, in the fourth rank of piers, in the part of the colonnade where the current abandoned its eastward run some ninety years ago and has not come back, which means the filter-fans here are old and thin and the mat grows lush and mustard-coloured on the stone, undisturbed. This is a good district. It is quiet in the only sense that word can have here: nothing new has fallen down within earshot for two days.

I am four and a half metres across the shoulder-ridge and something over eleven long, and I weigh what a small ship weighs, and the water carries seven-eighths of me. Understand that properly. I am not swimming and not walking. I am *held*, the way the depth is held, and I press my thirty short feet against black glass laid down before there were any of us to need it, and I move at a speed that makes the tallwaders think I am scenery. Under a dry sky I would fail in about forty minutes: not drown, not overheat — collapse. Simply cease to be structurally possible. Half a metre of blood-warm water is the difference between me and a spill.

So when I say the depth is held, I am telling you my own dimensions. The specification is a portrait.

## How I eat, which is also how I write

I graze the mat off the flanks of the piers with a mouth that is mostly a tongue, and where the mat is thin I take the stone as well — a slow acid lick, a millimetre a week — and the pier goes on being scalloped at exactly the height of my head, and the swarms come by night and lay the lamination back, and lay it back *already scalloped*, because the wear is not damage, the wear is the record, and the maintenance decided a very long time ago that the record was the thing worth keeping.

This is the part visitors get wrong even after they have got the rest right. They see a city repaired to a worn state and call it decadence, or a machine stuck in a groove. It is neither. It is an archive. Every rounded corner in ten thousand square kilometres is a cast of a flank that went past. I have no eyes; none of us has ever had eyes; I know my own shape the way you would know your face if you had only ever felt the pillow. The city is where I keep my body's picture. When I press through a doorway and it fits me to the millimetre, that is not convenience. That is being *recognised*.

## How I speak

I displace.

That is the whole of it. When I lift a limb clear of the surface, I return some volume of water to the sheet, and somewhere the systems that hold the level register the rise and trim it — a louvre bank drops a hand's breadth, a sluice throat narrows, a pump in the lightless floors below takes up the difference. Thousands of us are displacing at any moment. The trim is the sum. The standing wave is the trim made audible, driven and shaped and sent, and twice a day it arrives as the Comb: forty rollers, dead straight, perfectly in step, and the spacing between them is the sentence.

You cannot talk here. Nothing here shouts, because shouting is useless; we worked that out before the black courses were dry. What we did instead was take a continent and flatten it past the point a world is permitted to be flat, so that a shrug in the far west would be legible in the far east, so that the roar could be the carrier and not the enemy. The insane expense you keep noticing is a vocabulary.

And the eleven seconds — that silence before the bore, the one you called worse than the roar. That is us going still. Every one of us stops displacing at once and lets the sheet come true, because the line must be clean before the sentence rides it. The animals feel the water go glassy and read it as the end of the world, and they are not entirely wrong; it is the end of something, twice a day, and they have never learned to tell which.

## What we say

The roll.

That is all. That is the entire literature of the Level, and it has been the entire literature for longer than the black courses have had names. Twice a day the Comb goes out and every one of us answers into it with our position and our continuance: *here, fourth rank, eleven in, the depth is correct.* Nothing else. No stories. Nothing that could be called an idea. Sixty-one thousand times I have said the same eight things, and I have never once been bored, because the content of the roll is not the words. The content is who answers.

There are gaps now. There have been gaps for a long time. Four halls east of me there is a chin-step where somebody laid her head for two hundred years, and one tide she did not answer, and the tallwaders were there before I was — they always are; that is what they are waiting for, not fish, never fish, they wait at our flanks for the hobblers our licking dislodges and they wait at our pillows for the rest — and the roll went out that evening with her interval in it, an emptiness the exact width of a name, and it will go out with that emptiness for as long as anyone is left to hear it.

Here is what I do now, most days, at the cost of most of my grazing:

I go east, four halls, at my speed, which is not a speed. I put my head on her step and I sleep there. I lick her pier at her height, which is a little lower than mine, and I do it badly, and I do it anyway. Because the swarms lay back what is worn, and only what is worn — and a dish nobody dishes will be filled, and a scallop nobody scallops will be laid up smooth and true and correct, and then she will be gone the second time, the way that lasts.

So I keep her erosion for her. It is the only work I have ever done that was mine to choose.

## The last thing

You asked where I would be, and I have told you: eleven in, fourth rank, in the abandoned current, with the mustard mat and the thin old fans and the flyers talking green above me all through the dusk about a sky I know only as warmth on the wet part of my back.

But if you want to find me, do not look for movement. You have that habit and it will cost you months. Stand somewhere in the roar with the water at your knees and the hobblers coming over your feet, and wait for the animals to go quiet.

Eleven seconds. The sheet goes true. Everything in a continent holds still, including me, four halls from home with my head on a dead neighbour's stone.

Then the Comb comes through the colonnade and combs itself into forty parallel lines, and in the ninth interval, if you knew how to feel for it, you would find me saying the eight things. *Here. The depth is correct.*

And in the twelfth interval, nothing — and I say that too.
