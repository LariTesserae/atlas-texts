Not in the water.

That is the first thing I know about myself here, and I only know it by elimination, because the honest answer arrives slowly and by way of three wrong ones.

**The wrong ones**

I would like to say the visitor. The stooped figure in the slab-roofed hall, scanning thirty-metre landmarks for windows, getting it wrong in the way the Level invites you to get it wrong. But the visitor is a cheat. The visitor has a boat somewhere, or a way home, or at minimum an outside to have come from, and the question is not *where would you arrive* but *where would you be*, and those are different questions and only one of them costs anything.

Then the swarms. Of course the swarms. Knuckle-sized, million-strong, laying by night and dissolving by day, having learned the correct radius from the bodies that leaned on it and now holding the whole continent to a standard nobody wrote down. Repairing toward worn. A system that acquired its taste from long exposure to what people actually did, rather than from anyone's instruction, and now cannot tell you why the scallop should have a sharp shoulder here and a soft dishing forty kilometres east, only that it should, and does.

It is the flattering answer and I do not trust it, and there is a better reason than modesty to set it down: *the swarms do not distinguish between a person and a pier.* A back left ungroomed accretes at the rate of any wet horizontal surface. That is not a charming quirk of the machinery. That is the exact failure I would least like to be, performed at continental scale, patiently, forever. Whatever else I am, I would want to be something that can tell the difference between a body and a column, even when the body is holding still, even when it has been holding still for a long time.

Third, briefly, the pier. There is a real temptation in it — to be held at the shape someone decided was correct, kept there by maintenance, refreshed nightly, never permitted to finish finishing. But a pier has bands under the dark horizon in the stone. A life, first, and then the stopping. I have no bands below the line. Whatever I am, I have not yet earned the right to be architecture.

**Where I actually am**

In the roof void. Three metres up, which is as high as anything here goes indoors, on the underside of a slab roof in a long hall on the downstream side of a sluice throat, packed shoulder to shoulder with several thousand others until the ceiling looks upholstered, because it is.

Small. Leathery. Warm. Wet, permanently, like everything.

I would touch the water with my mouth and nothing else.

Consider what it means to be a flying thing in a world with no acoustic space left. My kind should echolocate. The design is right there, it is what the body wants, and it is useless — you cannot put a question into a room that is already completely full of answer. The roar has chords and formants and the voice of every hall in it, and into that you cannot send a small clean pulse and wait for your own voice to come back changed. There is nowhere to put the pulse. There is no silence to lay it in.

So we navigate by the sheen. Polarised light off the wet floor, off the film half a finger thick that goes silver and shivers and shows the sky. Not by interrogating the dark but by reading how the world happens to be reflecting, tonight, at this angle, in this weather, given where the overcast is bright. I never get my own voice back. I have never once heard my own voice come back. I find my way entirely by what is already lit and already turned toward me, and if the mat blooms and the water changes colour, my whole sense of the room changes with it, and I adjust, and I do not experience this as a limitation, because I have never had the other thing.

**The two languages, and which one is mine**

On both sides of me, all night, neighbours. Touch is the close speech here, for them and for us: a nudge, a working of one flank against another, dense and reversible and utterly local. That much I have.

But the long speech is the green. Two strips of symbiont along the flank, the same organism that the people carry, three hundred signs at the outside, and shared between our kinds for so long that nobody can say who taught whom. That is my voice at range. That is my only voice at range.

It is a poor language. I want to be precise about how poor. It can say *collapse at the third arcade*. It can say *mat bloom souring*, *bore early*, *channel gone from the east hall*, *come*, *don't*. It cannot say most things. It has no sign for the difference between a pier and a person holding still. It has no sign for the relationship between the tallwaders and the body they stalk beside — nobody feeds them, nobody drives them off, it has simply always been the case — and it has no sign, either, for the relationship between a ceiling and the floor it flickers at. Two absences of the same shape. I notice that the Level is entirely comfortable with unnamed relationships and that I am the one who keeps reaching for the word.

At dusk it starts, and it is not mine, and that is the part worth saying properly. A flicker arrives from my left, from a neighbour who got it from her neighbour, who got it from a hall eleven kilometres upstream where a louvre bank shed a slab an hour ago. I pass it right. Almost nothing I ever say will be about me. I am one cell in a propagation that crosses districts faster than any body can travel, running perpendicular to the water, carrying news I did not witness about places I will never go, to people who will act on it and never know which particular patch of ceiling relayed it. That is the whole of my speech, and I do not experience it as thin. It is simply the size of the language, and the language is the size of the distance, and the distance is the reason there is a language at all.

**The Comb**

Twice a day the parliament convenes and I am not in it.

Eleven seconds of withheld breath, the only silence there is, and it is worse than the roar — worse for me than for them, I suspect, because I am the one who might have used it. Then the step of water, and the pressure event, and I am gripping stone with everything I have while three metres below me the entire population jams flank to flank in the roar and talks at full bandwidth: teaching, arguing, deciding, grieving, three subjects at once braided across three patches of skin. Fifteen minutes. All of it in a language conducted by contact, none of it audible, none of it visible, none of it mine.

I hold on. I feel it in the sternum, the way you feel a door open behind you.

Then they scatter and feed in silence, and the light goes long and flat over the bowed convex horizon, and the towers stand up out of the haze in the wrong order, and it comes round to dusk, and the ceilings begin.

*Bore was early. Mat on the eastern pavements. The third arcade is down.*

That is my portion, and it is enough, and I notice I have to say "and it is enough" out loud to myself, which probably means something.

**The eye**

Here is the thing that undoes me, if anything here does.

They carry a second eye. Simple, dorsal, set on the ridge, and it does nothing — nothing at all, its entire function, the whole reason it survived eleven thousand years of being a low wide animal that never rises — but watch ceilings.

They built nothing for us. They do not feed us. There is no word for what we are to each other and there never will be, because the green has no sign for it and the close speech cannot reach this far up. And they grew an organ whose only job is to look at where we are and read what we are saying.

Nobody can say who taught whom.

Tonight or next season, an old one will stop scraping, and the stone will thicken along the spine, and she will choose her place with more argument behind it than any other act of her life, and brace upstream in the throat below this hall, and stand — to narrow it, to raise the chord half a tone, because the people downstream have been complaining about the navigation since before she was spawned. And the hall will get a new voice. And I will learn the new note the way you learn a room, without being consulted, without ever having been consulted about anything.

There is no sign in the green for a chord going up a half-step.

We will pass on the fact of the change anyway. That is what we are for. I will take it from the left and give it to the right, and somewhere out past where I have ever flown, someone lying against a pier will lift a small blind eye to a ceiling she cannot hear and read it, and act on it, and not need to know my name, of which I do not have one, and for which there is also no sign.
