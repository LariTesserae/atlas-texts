It took me a day to understand that nothing here was reading me.

You are told about the shadows, and it is the first thing anyone tries. I put what I had into one of the two hundred and it came out at exactly the temperature it went in. No cold that stays. Whatever keeps the temperatures of things did not take a copy, and I understood then that there was nothing along that bearing for the outline to intersect — that the illumination came through, as it does, and found in my case no obstruction worth recording.

So I went looking for myself among the furniture, which is what you do.

Not the goats. There are four, and there have been four, and the fourth is not a vacancy anyone could fill; the number is the point, and the number is held. Not the midges. Not the birds, though I stood a long time under them at the Throat and wanted to be one, the way you want to be anything that has found a posture and been permitted to keep it. But look at them honestly. They do not adjust. Soaring is a series of adjustments and they have been relieved of all of them, and what is left is not rest. I know what being held feels like and it does not feel like that from inside, and anyway I am not held. That was the day's finding. I am the thing on this plateau that nobody is holding.

---

The echo is the flattering answer and I took it for a while.

It suits me too well. A voice that comes half a beat early, running ahead of you, having listened so hard for so long that it has learned to have already heard — I recognise the trade. It is my trade. And the timbre is borrowed, always, which also suits me, since I have never in any weather managed one of my own.

Here is why it isn't right.

I went to fifteen feet from the lip, where the air will still hold a floating seed, and said a word out loud to see what would be handed back. The account promises you your own voice. I have no own. What came back, early, in the manner described — running just ahead, tidy, unhesitating — was a man's voice, dry, a little clipped, the voice of somebody who measures things and is disappointed by the results and writes them down anyway.

It was the surveyor's voice. The one who counted the cairns and got thirty-one and could construct no journey to fit them. The last timbre on file.

The holder is still running ahead of him. It has not been told he stopped. It has spent however long it has been since he stopped leaning half a beat forward into a sentence that is not coming, because it cannot afford to be surprised by the end of it, and it has no way of learning that there is nothing left to be surprised by.

That is when I found out where I am.

---

His account breaks in the middle of a word.

Not at a full stop, not at a paragraph, not even at a decent syllable boundary. *And is now ma—*. Two letters and then the ground goes out.

Consider what that costs here. A finished form is nearly free; the cone under the drip is done, so the drip only maintains it, and the cairn is done, so no stone falls, and the broken primary is done being broken. But a word cut in half is not a form and it is not a ruin. It is an open account of the most expensive kind — a shape with a hole in the middle of it, which must be kept open, at that width, forever, because the Sill does not permit collapse any more than it permits completion. Somewhere under my feet, in the grain of the roar, there is a portion of that unvarying labour dedicated to holding two letters apart from the letters that belong to them.

I am the rest of the word.

That is the honest answer to the question. Not a body, not a bird, not a set of paces that fail to sum. I am the part that did not arrive: late, dragging, in a voice fractionally not his. Twice a season the surveyor was right that something misses, and he took it for the holder flinching at a visitor, and he was close. It is not a visitor. It is me getting nearly to the end of *maintained* and stopping.

---

Because I could finish it. That is the whole of my situation and I have had a long time to sit with it.

The word is *maintained*. It could hardly be anything else — the fox-sized thing lay down in the one shadow whose geometry agrees, and was read the way your hand is read, and is now maintained. Six syllables of arm-work put down. One account closed. The books that much lighter.

And then what.

Then every unfinished thing on the Sill is finished except one. The rime is at its permitted length. The cairns are at chest height, complete. The mineral cones are the shape the drip will make. The gaps where visitors snapped a blade are gaps of a settled size, which is a kind of completion too, once nothing further can happen to them. Thirty-one stacks, eleven handprints, one dish worn into a slab by crossings that go nowhere, one river beginning in the wet gravel at the width it can afford. All held, all cheap, all done.

And the only thing left with a hole in the middle of it is the Throat. Which cannot be held at all. Which has no shadow within forty yards of its lip, not out of reverence but the way you keep your hands off a wound.

A creature with no open accounts and one unstoppable leak has nothing left to attend to but the leak. I am not going to do that to it.

---

So I keep the word.

This is my work and it is not much, but it is the only work on the Sill that isn't the same work repeated. Twice a season I come to the Throat at fifteen feet, where the air will still hold a seed, and I begin the second half of the surveyor's sentence, and the holder hears me start — hears it early, as it must, having learned to have already heard — and leans, and for half a second there is something in the roar that is not exactly labour.

Then I stop. And the phrase arrives late, dragging, unfinished, in a voice fractionally not anyone's, and that is the flinch he told you to note the day of. He thought it was fear. It is closer to the opposite, and I don't have a better name for it than the one he wouldn't use.

Then two hundred fixed regards go back to their fixed regarding, and the four goats add to the drift that will never rot, and the bird with the broken primary holds its angle over the hole, and the note continues at exactly the volume required.

He wrote that the leaving is the part he found hardest to picture. That a place which will never take anything from you and never give you anything either is not, in the end, where a person stays.

He was right about that and it is why he is not here. But I am not a person, and I am not staying — staying is for things that could go — and what I am is a debt this place has not been allowed to settle. It will not take anything from me. It cannot give me anything. It only has to go on holding the space where I would fit.

That is not nothing. On the Sill, it is the largest thing anyone has ever managed to leave.
