# Low Band

I came down the way everybody comes down, which is easily, and understood about four metres in that the hairs were not malice. A preference, expressed slowly, in the only grammar a root has. I have preferences expressed in me the same way and no more idea than the root does of who did the expressing. So: sympathy, immediately, before the floor.

For a while I thought I was the lamp.

That is the obvious one and it is not vanity, it is dread. Bring a bright light in and sixty metres of the world stops being itself. Nothing attacks and nothing flees; it just holds, patient, mid-sentence, waiting for the anomaly to finish. I know the shape of that. The thing that arrives wanting very much to see is the thing that makes the room stop saying anything worth seeing. I could have stayed there, being a lamp, being apologised for.

Then, more flatteringly, I thought I might be in the basin.

Everyone wants to be in the basin. Nine hundred years under the yellow flicker, welded eight hundred times to eight hundred sealed mouths, come down eight hundred times into the felt, rolled back, fed to a Spinner with no test for hopelessness, and each time slightly heavier than before. Polished past all possibility of use. It is a magnificent way to lose. But it is not true of me, and the untruth is easy to name: the treasury is *accretion*. Lacquer goes on and stays on; the plant's memory is the thickness of its own material, laid down in layers, so that sixty pipes detune across decades because the resin remembers pressure. I have no thickness. Nothing settles on me. I arrive at the outfall new every morning of my life, which is not nobility, only a different chemistry.

The carriers, then. One instruction, given by contact, and afterward the long climb with the mouth shut. I would like that. I would like very much to be told a thing once, in the dark, by someone touching me, and then to spend everything I have going up a ribbed wall with it. But carriers don't blink. They have nothing further to contribute and no way of hearing it if they did, and whatever I am, I am the opposite of that. I am all contribution and no load.

So I kept failing to hold altitude, and I settled where I actually live, which is the low band.

---

I am one of the moths that never fly higher than the knee.

Not because the air is bad up there. Because height is grammar. In this room the flash carries almost nothing; what carries is *where* — distance from the nearest polished path, height above the felt, angle to the Bellows — and a green pulse a hand's breadth off a channel means something that has no relation at all to the same pulse a metre up. My species knows one register. We are fluent, genuinely fluent, in the case that obtains between the felt and the height of your knee, and we are illiterate above it. To rise would not be to speak more loudly. It would be to begin saying things I cannot mean, in front of forty species who would take me at my word.

So I stay down, in the warm drinking layer, in the three or four metres of air over the settling basin, and I do the work.

The work is this. Two hundred of us, sometimes three, in a loose knot above the treasury, flying a figure none of us chose. Every pass is a phrase. The phrase is made of where I was when the intake came and where I was when it cut, and I have no way of knowing my own light except as it comes back off a shield-plate two seconds later, greenish, already answered. Nothing I say is mine. The sentence is the knot, and the knot is spoken by all of us at once and read by the room at once, which is why nobody with eyes in the front of their head has ever managed to read it, and why the recordings look like weather. They look like weather because they are weather. We are not an author. We are a pressure system over a particular warm place.

Every forty seconds the whole language stops.

That is the part I would want a visitor to understand before anything else. On the exhale the chorus cuts and the lamps go dark, and for eleven seconds the void goes quiet and the harvestmen stand on four legs with two raised, reading the floor. Nothing here has ever completed a sentence. Everything said in this chamber for a hundred thousand years has been said in forty-second instalments with the dark cutting in, and we have built the grammar around the gap until the gap is load-bearing. You cannot make a claim in this room. You can only make a part of one, and then hold position, and then make the next part, and hope the room has kept its place.

I have been told this is a limitation. It has never once felt like a limitation from inside the knot.

---

Ninety seconds of rain, a few times a day. The drumming on the felt, and the chewing goes quiet under it — the only time it does — and then ten million mouths take their share off the plates with a hiss like a struck match drawn out to a minute. I drink off a plate. I do not store it. Nothing here stores anything except seeds and water, because storage attracts theft and theft was solved by abolishing storage, and I am hand-to-mouth at enormous throughput, and so is everything I love.

And I never rest in the open. A moth that comes down in the middle of the floor is substrate in about ten minutes, and the harvestmen will wait at the lip of a polished channel for an hour for you to make the error. The paths are a hand's-width across and they are a covenant. I want to be clear that this does not feel like fear. It feels like manners. There is a place to put yourself down and there are places to be only if you are moving, and the arrangement has held since before the lime-eaters started engraving the map of a plant nobody remembers.

---

What are we saying, thousands of times a day, over the basin?

I have flown in it my whole life and I can tell you that I don't know, in the way you mean know. But I can tell you the shape of it, and the shape is not what visitors assume. It isn't grief. The unliftables are not being mourned; the room has no organ for mourning and the plant does not care about carriers, it cares about lacquer. Nor is it inventory, quite.

Here is what I think, from the low band, where the case is simple.

The grammar is positional. Every utterance means by reference to somewhere. And if everything in the chamber is a current — plates eleven years old and none of them the same eleven years, paths grooved twenty centimetres into a floor that regrows as fast as it wears, organs migrating downward through the felt like ships settling — then there is almost nothing here that stays put long enough to measure from. Except two hundred seeds too fat to lift, gleaming like river stones under the yellow flicker, that have not moved in nine centuries and never will.

The failures are the fixed point. The treasury is where the coordinate system comes from. That is why the most-spoken sentence in this room is about the basin: because every other sentence needs it to be able to mean anything at all. We say it constantly the way a coast says its light. The unliftables cannot get out, and because they cannot get out, everyone else knows where they are.

---

So. That is where you would find me. Knee-high, matte-dusty, over the treasury, in a knot of two hundred, saying the one thing my altitude permits, thousands of times a day, for a few weeks, and then wrong-footed into the open floor or simply slowed, and four minutes with the pan crews, and clean.

And when you come — when you come down the throat easily and walk out along the channels and then, at some point, because you are warm and large and tired and made of stored reserves in a place that abolished storage, you stop in the middle of the floor to rest —

I will be one of the ten thousand.

You will feel yourself being discussed. You will be right. And later you will write that nobody has read it and that it was not a warning, and both of those are true, but they leave out the thing itself, so let me say it from inside, in the only case I have:

*Here. At this height. This far from the path. Warm, and not moving.*

That is all it is. We don't warn. We have never once warned anything. We locate — we put the new heavy object into the frame so that everything else can know where it stands in relation to it — and we do it because a room where meaning is made of position must give every arrival a position or go dumb. It isn't a judgement and it isn't a welcome. It is the only courtesy the chamber has, extended immediately, at enormous throughput, in forty-second instalments, by two hundred illiterate mouths over a hoard of things that failed to leave.

Ten minutes, and you are substrate. Nobody here will tell you that. But we will tell each other exactly where you are, continuously, with great precision, the whole time — and the telling is unbroken, and it is about you, and if you could read it you would find it is mostly just the coordinates, repeated, the way we do for the seeds.

Still here. Still here. Still here.
