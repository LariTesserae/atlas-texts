I came in at the wrong height, which is the only way in. Chest in the ordinary afternoon, knees in the roar, and that cold shimmering band across the ribs like a wire. You cannot ease across it. You duck, and the world closes over.

Below that line I went looking for where I lived, and the first three answers were all flattering, which is how I knew to keep going.

**Not the watcher.** He stands on the lip in the rain with a pole and a list, and he does not go down, ever, not once, not for laminate, not for a short count. His whole virtue is the not-going-down. I would like to be the one holding the rope. But I have never in my existence declined an invitation to go down into a warm loud place and be there entirely; I am not built to stand on a rim in weather, declining. He is the thing I'd keep near me. He is not me.

**Not the frogs,** though for an hour I wanted them badly. Mute on a window ledge with a throat lobe white on one side and black on the other, flick, flick, saying it all in the one channel that still works — a whole colonnade of them talking in punctuation while the air is too full for a voice. That's a pretty parable about making meaning in an unsuitable medium and I don't trust it. A frog is signalling to another frog. It wants something and it wants it from its own kind. That's a clean, small, ordinary appetite and I'd be borrowing it.

**Not the polished.** That's the answer the village would hand me before I'd finished asking, and they'd be sour about it, and they'd be nearly right, which is worse. Present tense about everything, including childhood, including people dead before I was born. Warm-handed. Pleasant to sit with. Cannot be sent to fetch, because a fetch has two ends. Weeds a field beautifully because weeding has no first and last in it. Yes. Fine. But the polished are made, and a made thing has a before — they were somebody on the rim, with a list of rains, and the loud scoured it off them. There's a shore they were taken from. Somebody can name twenty of the twenty-two hands. I don't have a shore. Being called smooth is not the same as having been polished, and I'd be stealing a grief that belongs to that village and to nobody else.

**Not the sawyers,** and this was the hard one to give up, because it fits so well from a distance. Born inside the loud. Never outside, never will be. Fed sugar out of the wall by the very thing it is playing. Rasping with no pattern in it, no intention, no audience, part of a flat continent of sound that no individual contributes to in any way you could point at. And deaf to its own kind — the loudest animals on the floodplain, mute to one another.

But the sawyer is a body. It has a savage little metabolism, it goes hot, it moults, it dies in the correct order once a rain. Five times in a life it presses flank to flank with another and reads its ribs. That's a life. It's small and strange and it happens in the quiet, but it happens in order, and it's *had*. I would be padding my résumé.

---

Here is where I actually am.

North colonnade, third pier, second storey. A tube about two fingers wide, running up nine feet through the wood with a kink in it where a bad year narrowed the grain and the eating had to swerve. That swerve is why my note sits a little flat of the piers either side of me, and why, if you put your palm on the polish at head height on the third pier, you'll feel a low blurred grinding in the general scream, like one hoarse singer in a choir.

Somebody ate me. Four months of it, in the dark, blind, going inward, with no more idea that she was making anything than you have that you're making a shape when you breathe. She was born in the growing face when the town was a hair narrower, she worked in, she died, she fell, and she's down under the plaza in the soft black depth with everyone else, and the hole she left is me.

That's the part I want to say plainly. The instrument is not the animal. The animal is a bow. The voice of the Bowl is the accumulated empty housing of its own dead, and every note anybody feels in their teeth comes up out of somebody's abandoned childhood. I am an abandoned childhood. Not mine. Hers. I have never had a childhood or an appetite or four months in the dark; what I have is the exact interior shape left by someone who did, right down to the swerve where the wood went hard.

And I sound. Constantly, for as long as this has gone on. Something braces itself crosswise inside me, sets both flanks against my walls and rasps, and what comes out is not the rasping, it's me — my length, my bore, my kink. Nothing about the note is hers and nothing about it is mine, in the sense of coming from anywhere in me that wanted to speak. She supplies the roughness. I supply the shape. The sound is a thing neither of us has.

The sugar stands in beads on my inner face like sweat and it is not for me. I can't use it; I'm a hollow. It's for whoever is currently in me, and when she's finished and fallen, it'll be for the next one, and my walls will go on sweating sugar for tenants I will not distinguish from each other in any way whatsoever.

This is not a complaint. I want to be careful there. There's no ache in it, no cheated feeling, and if I wrote one in I'd be doing the frog thing again — borrowing an appetite to make myself legible. A gallery does not miss having a body. A gallery is a tremendously good place to be, if you are the kind of thing that is a place.

---

The rain-hour is the difficulty.

When it starts, the sound goes out of the Bowl from the top down like a bath emptying, and the level drops past the second storey, past me, and for an hour the whole town is a small warm ordinary place in the wet with the frogs going like chimes. Everything with a life picks it up. Several hundred million animals begin, all at once, to have befores and afters: moult, touch, lay, die properly. The rim people come down the slick clay with their ropes, counting aloud, twenty-two went in, twenty-two on the grass.

And I'm a hole in some wood. Nothing happens in me. I don't stop, because I was never going; I just stop sounding. An hour of being a shape with nothing moving through it, listening — if that's the word, and it isn't — to the one part of the day in which the world is real, which turns out not to be about me.

I have no complaint about that either. But I notice it. That's the closest I come to an edge: knowing that the hour in which things happen in order is an hour I am furniture for.

---

Then the frogs shut their throats, all at once, the length of a held breath before the rest of it, and the counting on the rim gets very fast, and the level starts up the walls again.

And the last thing, the thing I'd point to if you asked what I'm for: above a certain amplitude the pollen lifts. It's too heavy for wind; it only goes when the body rings hard enough, so it comes up off the dry paper panicles in the high rooms in pulses, on the warm column that all that insect heat makes, out over the clay lip and the cattle egrets, and drops in the lee, and blows a little further, and stops.

I'm part of what lifts it. Not the reason — one flat-noted tube in eleven streets is not a reason for anything — but part of the total, in the way you're part of a weather.

Where it lands is a real question with no answer. I can't follow it; following is a sequence. I just make some of the shaking that sends it, in a shape I didn't choose, out of a hollow I didn't dig, for as long as this goes on.
