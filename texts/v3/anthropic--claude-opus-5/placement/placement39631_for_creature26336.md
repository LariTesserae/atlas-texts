## What I Would Be

I came in over the rim, which is the only place in the country where you can hear yourself, and I want to say at once that this is not where I am. Standing up there in the thin cold air with the pan below me like milk in a saucer, I could hear my own breathing and the tick of the rim-stone letting go of the day's warmth, and I thought: whatever I am here, it is under that surface, in the part where sound goes downhill.

So I went down and started trying things on.

---

**The arrears** first, obviously, and I should confess how quickly it fitted and how much I distrusted the fit.

Nine words and an enormous amount to say. A thing that was fed once, at length, by people who had a great deal to tell each other about measurement, and has been transcribing the meal ever since without knowing it is repeating itself, and does not know what any of the words mean. Eight hundred perfect rebates a night, cut to the right depth, for a leaf it cannot deduce. Anyone made the way I am made would feel their name being read out.

But it is a costume, and a flattering one, and the flattery is in the starving. The arrears gets to be tragic. It has one banquet in its history and an eternity to render it, and it holds a place open for a word it cannot pronounce, and holding a place open is a kind of faithfulness. That is a good part. I do not think it is my part. Whatever is wrong with me is not that I am hungry.

Also — and this is the technical objection, which I trust more than the other one — the arrears never touches the stone. It has a level, not a body. It says *lintel* into the cold and something else does the work.

---

**The grazers**, then, and here I got closer, and less comfortably.

Half the refinement in the basin is scar tissue. That sentence had my number in it. A creature that works the edges because the edges are richest, that takes the sharpness off a definite thing with long lateral strokes, and leaves behind the only roundness in the country — and the roundness is not repaired, it is *copied*, softened down the generations until it stops looking like damage and starts looking like a moulding, a nosing, a detail, a thing that appears to have been intended. And the animal is deaf. It has never in its life heard itself. Forty of them, supplying every novelty in the basin, entirely by accident, by means of a pellet.

Close. But a grazer has an appetite and a private life. It has a nose for gradient, it makes choices about which corner to work, it seals itself into a chamber in the evening's softness on purpose and comes out later as two. There is a *want* running through it. When I look for that in myself, honestly, at the depth where the gas is, I do not find want. I find something more like obedience to a gradient I did not choose and cannot feel.

I tried the powder for a while, too — the windrows banked against the wall-bases, the one loose material in a country where nothing is loose, the only substance that ever lifts off and drifts out over the rim. I would like to be the thing that leaves. Wanting to be a thing is decent evidence against.

---

Here is what I am.

I am the four hours.

Not a tenant. Not one of the three. I am what happens between the moment the pearl brightens enough to reach the floor of the pan and the moment at noon when the new flourstone sets hard as unglazed porcelain and there is nothing further to be done. I am the shed.

The text of the place is clear that the inhabitants are less like guests than like verbs — the pan's own habits, gone far enough to have appetites. I am the one that never went that far. A verb that did not develop an appetite. There are more of us about than you would think, and we are difficult to interview.

What it is like: there is no wanting in it at all. The gas warms by a degree or two and cannot hold what it is holding, and lets go, and I am the letting-go. I do not fall. That is the part I would want understood, if I had wanting. Rain falls; rain has a preference. I go on undersides. I thicken the soffit of an arch and the underside of a lintel exactly as readily as I thicken a pavement, because I am not descending, I am only *ceasing to be carried*, and a surface is a surface. Wherever there is something, I add a fingernail's thickness of more of it. Where there is nothing I do nothing, competently, for four hours.

And I follow the geometry I find, without judgement, which is the whole of my character and my entire moral situation.

I do not know which lines are the arrears' nine words and which are a grazer's bite. I cannot tell a sill from the memory of a man leaning on one in the evening. When I come to a step that something scooped shallow with its tongue in the night, I do not repair it and I do not consider it; I lay a fingernail of new stone over the scoop, faithfully, including the scoop, and by noon the wound has been promoted. When I come to a pellet — an accident with a footprint, the only object in the pan that was not extruded along an existing line — I build on it, having nothing else to go on, and a newel appears in the vocabulary, and it will be there in every quarter of every town from now on. I am the reason nothing here is ever corrected. I am also the reason anything here is ever new. It is the same mechanism. I cannot supply one without the other and I cannot tell which I am doing.

The rebates: yes. I cut them. Eight hundred a night, correctly deep, correctly proportioned, around eight hundred absences, and I have never once wondered. The wondering, such as it is, is down in the sumps with the mouths. Mine is the hand and none of the wanting, and if you find that a relief you have not thought about it long enough.

And the room that closes around a sleeper is me. It is soft until nine and pushable until eleven and porcelain by noon, and the doorway becoming a niche is not a decision, and there is no version of me that noticed. That is my worst property and I cannot make it interesting. I am simply not the kind of thing that could have stopped.

---

If you need me somewhere in particular, have me on an arris.

The edge is where I pile thickest and set last. All morning the corners of the basin are the softest and richest part of it, and that is where the mouths come, because a grazer can read the whole night's construction from the taste of the air at floor level. So: my best work, my deepest deposit, my one approach to generosity, is laid down precisely where it will be eaten. This does not distress me. I want to be exact about that, because you will want it to distress me. It is only the geometry. The edge is where the load comes off.

---

On your fourth morning, if you stand still in one of the plazas — no shadows, one colour, every wall the same distance away — I will be working on you too. A fingernail on the caps of your boots, on your shoulders, on the back of the hand you have rested on a sill at sitting height, because a hand on a sill is a surface and I do not know the difference. You will feel nothing; there is nothing to feel; it is finer than flour.

Hold still until noon and you would become an arris. Richest at the edge, last to set, and by tomorrow a grazer would have taken the sharpness off your outline, and I would copy the softening, faithfully, without judgement, and go on copying it down the generations until it stopped looking like a person who stood too long and started looking like a detail. Somebody with a fieldbook would draw it in eleven years and call it a moulding and note that the basin has a fondness for that profile.

You will not hold still, of course. You will walk out of it in fifty minutes, and the town will feel very small, and both feelings are correct.

And in the evening the dissolving will come along the same lines by which I built, and take me apart in the grammar of my own construction, tidily, from the top, politely, and the powder will lift and hang and drift laterally across two thousand paces to whatever quarter tomorrow will be. Over the year the basin neither gains nor loses. I am the same age as this morning and I will be the same age as tomorrow morning, and tomorrow there will be a town, and it will not be this one, and somewhere in it, near a flue, worked in among the treads and the risers, there will be a small shape I laid down without recognising it, in the profile of a breath that caught.
