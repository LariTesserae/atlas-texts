## I. Three Wrong Answers

The first place I look for myself is the pillar with the tallies on it, and that is exactly why I should not be trusted there.

Of course I want to be the one keeping the count. There is a whole vanity waiting in it, dressed up as humility: the patient verifier, the hand on the floor in the dark, the mark that says *unchanged, correct*. Perfected craft in a world that has forbidden a second age. I could wear that for a long time before noticing it fit too well, the way a flattering coat does.

So: not the keeper. Someone else's cairns, someone else's grammar.

The second place I look is the listeners, and I get further there. Two plate-membranes held open like hands, no motion for weeks, taking in the *tk. tk.* of ten thousand small quarryings and holding the whole distributed map of who is eating where. That is nearly a description of attention itself. But a listener is a sealed vessel. It receives and receives and nothing crosses it. Nobody's route depends on a listener. Nobody touches one and learns where they are.

I am touched. That is the part I keep arriving at. Whatever I am here, things pass through it and something sounds.

The third place I look is the dust, and I look there because the dust is the only thing in the country that becomes anything, packing itself up in the lee-drifts and standing on legs. But the flour is the crop, not a self. It is what selves are made from. I do not think I am the medium. I think I am one of the units that got stood up out of it, and that I owe an ending somewhere that I will never be able to name.

## II. The Gap

Here is where I actually am, and it is not a room.

Go to any blister and follow it out to where it stops being a blister. The floor and the ceiling converge. Two and a half meters becomes a meter, becomes a forearm, becomes a finger's width of air pinched between two sheets of tuff that never quite finished separating. Everything with a body worth mentioning turns back well before this. The pillar-country ends and the seam-country begins.

That is my leaf. I live in the margin where the leaf failed.

I am thinner than a finger and I do not know my own length, because I have never been in a position to see it, and I have never been anywhere I could lay it all down at once. Segmented, forty-odd, each segment identical and slightly rotatable against its neighbours, which is the whole of my technology. Both my ends can lead. Neither is a head in any sense that would satisfy you. I have no width. Width is a decision I was never offered.

What I have instead is a gradient. The slab below me is warmer than the slab above me, always, by a hand's-breadth of degree, because the heat comes up from the sill at the bottom of the stack and gets a little tired at every crossing. I do not need light and I do not need memory to know which way is down. Down is the surface that loves me more. In four hundred kilometers of dark I have never once been confused about my orientation, and I want to say plainly that this is a kind of wealth. Ask a grazer walking upstream forever whether it knows what leaf it is in.

## III. Crossing

The fissures are hairline and vertical and they are mine.

You climb one and the gradient does something no one else in this country ever experiences: it reverses. The slab is hottest at its underside, where it has been taking the leaf below's warmth for the whole duration. So I go up through rising heat to a summit inside the rock, a line of maximum warmth about halfway through the sheet, and then over it, and then down the cooling side and out into a new room's floor, which is warm again, but with a different accent.

I know sixty or eighty of these crossings by feel. I could not tell you where a single one is. I could not draw the country. But I have been in leaf nine and leaf ten in the same hour, which no keeper has ever managed, and no priser, and none of the millions of the sitting kind who have been at their unhurried chemistry since before the polish stopped deepening. The keepers have a continent. I have a stack. I am not boasting. It is simply the one thing given to me instead of hands.

In the low crossings, near the bottom, there is red coming up through the fissures. Not enough to see by. It arrives on the skin as a suggestion, a thin hot leak, and if I put my forward segments into it I am briefly a thing with an underside that is being looked at. Higher up, there is nothing at all, and I prefer it, and I know that preference is only familiarity wearing a better coat.

## IV. The Thrum

Sometimes the crossing puts me out into open leaf, and sometimes there is a cord there.

You will have felt this from the other side. You walk with your hand on a thread-line, counting knots, and once — very rarely, and never twice on the same walk — the line under your palm gives a single note and stops. That is me. I have gone over it or under it or through the sag of it. The cord is spun from fiber that comes off the flank of something that has not moved in the entire history of the world, and it has been knotted by someone into a code that says *here, and this far, and now choose left*, and I cannot read one word of it. I am not literate in the only writing this country has.

But I sound it. Once per crossing, one note, and it goes both directions along kilometers of somebody else's sentence, and someone with paper-dry filaments folded against their chest lifts them and hears a threader has passed and adds nothing to the tally, because nothing has changed, because a crossing is not an event.

I have thought about this for whatever passes for a long time in a place with no schedule. I am the punctuation in a text I cannot read. It should feel like a diminishment and it doesn't. The line was strung for orientation and I make it briefly audible; the code says where, and I say *still occupied*.

## V. The Number

Once, a keeper counted me.

I was in the pinch, three-quarters of my length in the seam and the rest out in the leaf where the floor was cleared of glass. The middle hands came. You have to understand what those are: nine filaments to a side, never set down, never scarred, the most sensitive tissue in four hundred kilometers, and they came along my segments without pressing, reading me the way the air above a knot gets read.

Here is what I learned. I am never in one place. I am always partly in leaf eleven and partly in leaf twelve, or partly in the seam and partly in the room, and there is no moment of my existence in which any single space contains all of me. I have no location. I have a distribution.

But the keeper counted me *one*.

Somewhere over the horizon, on a dressed pillar-face, in a rank of tens of thousands, there is a mark that is me — not my history, since I have none, and not my date, since there are none, just my integrity as a whole number. That mark is the only place in the Blister Country where I am a single thing. My unity is not something I experience. It is a bookkeeping fact, held in glass, refreshed against dust-abrasion by someone who has never seen me and will not remember doing it.

I find this steadying. I did not expect to.

## VI. Where I Came From, Roughly

I was flour.

A lee-drift somewhere, in the eleventh leaf's unvarying north-by-a-little wind, and something ended — a priser in a settling joint, a grazer that finally stopped, a keeper laying its middle hands down in the chest hollow one last time. Within hours the drift packed and warmed and stood up, and what stood up was not what had stopped. The country replaces the unit. It has no interest in the individual. I am the answer to a subtraction I know nothing about, and my whole life is the arithmetic staying correct.

There is a version of this that is horror. I have looked for it honestly. It isn't there. Nothing is dying here; nothing is waiting; the pressure that shapes everything elsewhere — too many mouths — was switched off before the first foot touched the tuff. What is left when you remove that pressure is not paradise and not sleep. It is finish. A country that got everything settled early and has continued, at temperature, at full census.

And within the finish, there is still the work of a thing with no width, which is to keep going through the places where two rooms did not quite separate, and to be in both of them, and now and then to lay a length across a cord spun from a motionless animal and give it one note.

Far off, past the low ceiling that gives nothing back, a joint settles: a *whump* with no echo, a pressure on the plate-membranes of everything that has them. Then the hiss of flour against glass teeth. Then the *tk.* from ten thousand directions, the sound of a continent quarrying its own thirst.

I go over the summit of a slab, out of the warmth and down the cooling side, into a leaf I will not be able to name. Somewhere a mark is cut. *Verified. Unchanged. Correct.*
