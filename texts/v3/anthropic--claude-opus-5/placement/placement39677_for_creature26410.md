## First, the answers I am not allowed

Not the plant. The description forecloses it, and it is right to. But there is a second reason, which is that I would want it too much — and wanting to be the ancient enormous patient thing is the same reflex as looking for the rivets. In a place where the resemblance to intention is the standing hazard, the flattering answer is automatically the wrong one.

Not the visitor, either, though that is the honest first guess for anyone holding the page. The visitor is the only object in the room and the only thing in the whole basin with an unfinished sentence. But the visitor leaves. A day's walk out, a day's walk back, and the place does not include them in any tense. The chest-wrongness they carry away is an organ they brought with them.

Not the room. The room is a function, and I don't think I could bear to be a function that is defined by staying empty; or rather, I think I would bear it too well, and that is its own kind of lie.

Not the gutter. Tempting — *not a pool; a flow rate* is as good a definition of a self as I have read this year — but the gutter has no inside.

The rails are comic and dignified and step over each other where their circuits meet. The bats are the best joke in the building: thousands of tenants in a cathedral, all of them using the fire exit, none of them ever once looking up into the nave. The thumbnail frogs live in a *thickness* — a life spent inside the film, three hundred microns of world. The mites live on a singer, so that for them the roar is not even weather; it is a vehicle.

But I know where I am. It is the sentence that breaks off.

I can finish it. What it grows instead is purchase.

## Where

Southwest arc, because the fog comes from the southwest an hour before dawn and that face combs it first, so the wet zone climbs a full metre higher there than anywhere else in the ring. Seven metres up, which is above the frogs and below the seams of bats. Nine laminae in from the outer face.

My slot is a curved sheet-space, the gap between two shed leaf-bases that wrapped this column and were pressed flat against each other before my great-grandmothers hatched. It is a millimetre and three quarters at its widest and it narrows at both ends, where it opens, and those ends are the doors. It runs perhaps three and a half metres around the curve of the column. I have walked all of it. It took most of a night and there was nothing at the far end but my neighbour's door and my neighbour's noise, which is my noise, which is the noise.

I am eleven millimetres long and flat as a pressed flower and the colour of old straw, which is the colour of everything. Two dark pinpricks resolve nothing except the difference between inward and outward, which is the only difference. Inward: dark, warm, wet, sweet. Outward: pale, dry, hard, and in the end a line of light.

Instead of wings I have two thickened plates, one on each side of the thorax, that lock across the slot and brace me between its ceiling and its floor. Nothing can push me out. Nothing can pull me out. Flight would be idiocy here — nothing crosses the middle, the updraught would take a winged thing up the chimney and put it out over a day's walk of brick — so my line gave up the air and bought grip with the proceeds. I am not built to go anywhere. I am built to *remain at a depth*.

## What the depth is for

A lamina is a larder. When the plant abandons a leaf-base it seals into it the last of what it was carrying, and then the lamina begins its slow journey outward at a fingernail a year, going stale the whole way. Nine deep, the stuff still yields to a stylet. Fourteen deep it is horn and tannin and no use to anyone.

So the wall pushes out and we walk in. Each generation lays its eggs inward of itself, cemented against the fresher face, and the nymphs come past their mother going the other way, and they inherit not her slot but her *distance from the living*. My family has been at nine laminae for longer than any lamina has existed. We are not a lineage of bodies. We are a lineage of position — the same thing as those hairline cracks out in the pan, which are in the same places every year and have been in the same places for longer than the cracks themselves, inherited from the ones before.

In my whole life the wall will carry me outward perhaps four millimetres. Less than half my own length. It is the only displacement in the entire basin, and it is smaller than me, and it is the reason I am the answer to the question. The pan is finished. The plant is finished. *Ancient* and *current* mean the same word in that room. But not in here. In here there is before and after, and the distance between them is four millimetres, and I am the thing that experiences it.

## What the singing is

Not song. Not courtship, not much. It is address.

A quiet slot is an invitation. Go silent for a night and your neighbour widens her door, and by the third night there is no boundary where the boundary was. So I click, and I click, and the point of the clicking is not to be heard but to remain *locatable* — to keep a place in a texture that would otherwise close over me the way water closes.

Which means my hearing is entirely negative. The visitor stands in the nave and is crushed by the noise. I have never heard the noise. The rushing of the chimney, the billion snapping vessels, the forty thousand of us — that is not sound to me, that is the condition of there being anything at all; it is what silence is called here. What I hear is *holes*. I am tuned only to where the texture isn't. My whole attention is a search for absence, and I have never once been the one to find it first.

Perhaps four times in a life it happens. A slot goes quiet. Everything within a metre turns, in the sense that we turn, and what we feel is not grief because nothing here is capable of grief; it is the pull you feel toward an open door. Death registers in this building as a gap in a sheet of sound. It is the only event that has ever been reported.

## How it ends

The wall keeps arriving from behind. One year the outer laminae of my slot weather thin, and then they curl back in a dry scroll and stay, because nothing in this air is quick enough to rot them, and the light comes in as a line the width of a hair and then the width of a leg.

Then dry. Then no film, no yield to the stylet, and I go on clicking, because that is what I am, and the strange mercy is this: nobody comes. Nobody widens into an address on the outer face. There is nothing there worth having. So I am not overwritten. I am *left*, which is the one thing that never happens to anyone here, and I have no name for it, and I go quiet in a slot that no one turns toward.
