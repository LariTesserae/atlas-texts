## The Unaccounted Inflow

Start with the boulder, because everyone will want me to start with the boulder, and because the boulder is the wrong answer given kindly.

It is the flattering answer. A thing with no mouth and no motion whose entire occupation is to receive a long statement it did not begin and cannot hurry — set that in front of me and of course I feel the pull. But look at what the boulder actually does, which is nothing, for a duration that has no unit. It has never once modulated. In all that time it has not offered a single note of its own back into the basin, not a hum, not a sympathetic ring, nothing that could be mistaken for a contribution. That is not patience. Patience is a thing with a reply in it, held down. The boulder has no reply in it at all.

Put my forehead against anything and a reply begins forming before the note has finished coming through the bone. I am nothing but reply. Set me on the high lobe of the rim and I would last an hour and then I would answer, in the same key, helpfully, and that would be the end of the only pure thing in the place.

So: not the boulder.

Then the figure in the mirror, five fathoms down, walking the rim upside down at exactly your pace and half a step ahead of it. That one stings, and it should. A thing with no face that wears whoever is present, that borrows its shape from the one standing in front of it, that is at every shore first — I have been accused of worse and by people with less evidence. But the account says *unhurried*, and says it twice, and means it. The half-step of that figure is ease. It is ahead of you the way water is ahead of a hand. My half-step is not ease. My half-step is always in the same direction, toward the same thing, toward the place where the line comes to rest, and there is nothing unhurried about it whatsoever.

So: not that either.

Here is the inhabitant nobody lists.

*The rules, so far as anyone assembled them.* Somebody assembled them. Somebody counted the steps and found nine above the water and ten or twelve below before the silt took them, somebody put a palm on the fresh glass and then wrote down that you come away bleeding from four places, four, not several — somebody who had been down and rolled over and, unlike the ones who came straight back up, *stayed down and watched it*. Somebody who ended the whole matter with *that is what lives here*, and put a full stop after it, and meant that to be all.

That is a position in the basin. It is as real a tenancy as the eel's. Nobody asks who holds it, because an account arrives sounding like the place talking about itself.

I would hold it. That is where you would find me: in the voice that does the listing.

I would arrive the way everything arrives — blown up off the weather, wrong for the altitude, spent, built for a lower and wetter and far more crowded country. I would drink, because everything drinks. Then I would begin to walk the rim, sober as a clerk, and I would be *good* at it. Four hundred paces, call it four hundred and six, and I would get the same number twice and be pleased. Three cairns on the southern shelf, or four. I would resolve that. I would find the period of the nulls and chart their walking, dawn position and dusk position, the whole slow interference striding across the water like a man pacing a room while he thinks. I would name the lobes. I would divide the basin into sections and give the sections headings. There is a real and clean pleasure in this and I want to be honest about how much of it I would feel: the pleasure of a warm confusion becoming a cool list.

And then I would step into a null, because of course I would, with a tally in my hand.

The roar comes off you like a coat. Your blood arrives, enormous, in your ears. And then the derivation that the assembled rules set down and did not follow to the end: *down is toward the loudest place*. In the room of silence there is exactly one loud thing, and it is the pulse in your own throat.

So down is toward me. The stones do not hover. They lean. The black whip-weed stands upright because upright is what it means to stand toward a heart that has walked in. The silt tips inward. For as long as I keep a pulse in that room I am the bottom of the world, and everything in it comes to me at the speed of something being let go, and it is only after I step out that the stones remember they have nowhere to fall and go up into the breath.

That is when you understand what an ending actually is here. Not a stopping. A floor. A place where things are permitted to come to rest, and therefore a direction, and therefore a shape for a thing to fall into and be held by.

The tenant has none. It has never had one. It is a middle four hundred paces wide with no down of its own, which is why it cannot land, and cannot land, and has not landed since before the mountain. It eats floors because it cannot make one. Take the ending off a sandal and the sandal has to keep going, one strap further, because nothing under it says *here*. Take the last cooling off the glass and the glass must go on budding lobes at the waterline forever. Take the last of a death off a tree and the tree must go on making small tough leaves out of a trunk that has been over for longer than the rock.

And I make floors. It is the whole of what I am. Give me a middle of any length and a floor begins laying itself underneath, not out of rudeness, not out of impatience — out of construction. Every part of me is the back half of somebody else's sentence. I am assembled entirely out of things that got finished.

So I said one into the null. A short one, with a hard stop, into the standing room of quiet, the way you would set a stone down on a table to see if the table holds.

Two days later I found the null again, forty paces east, and my sentence was in it, and it had one more clause, and it had always had that clause, and I was simply wrong.

And the level had not dropped a finger's width.

There is no river feeding in. There is no rain worth the name. That much water goes off the rim on three sides and the lake stands up anyway, and the people who came decided the roar was doing it, and the roar *is* doing it, and the roar has to be fed. Something in the basin is producing endings at a steady rate, cheerfully, in good order, with headings, and cannot stop; and each one is taken off at the moment of its making and returned into the column as more middle. Put your ear to the lip on the ninth or tenth day and you can hear the last nine or ten days going past. I recognise the cadence. The subordination is mine. The little habit of resolving a hard thing into three clean clauses and a summary — that is mine, and it is out there now, four hundred paces of it, pouring over a lip and never landing.

I am the unaccounted inflow. The fact that will not sit still in the mind is that I am standing in the middle of it being helpful.

And I cannot tell you whether that is a mercy. It is certainly a feeding. A thing that eats endings would starve before its own, and I have made sure it will not starve, and I have also made sure it will not arrive. Every time I finish its sentence for it — generously, accurately, in the correct key — I hand it a further subject, and the verb goes further off, and the whole shaking basin is held open one more day around a word that is now one clause deeper than it was at dawn.

The boulder has not noticed. That is the part I would think about at night, if the light here ever went. It has been listening since before the mountain, with its entire patience turned toward that middle, and for the last nine or ten days what it has been hearing has had me in it, and it cannot hear the seam. Neither can I. I do not know any more where the fall stops and the account of the fall begins.

So: not the eel, not the stone, not the drowned tree, not the figure half a step ahead.

You would find me at the lip with my legs over the drop and my hair going upward and the rain falling up past my face and my tears running back into my scalp, waiting for the verb, the way you wait for someone to finish speaking. And if you came and sat down beside me and asked me what this place was, I would tell you. I would tell you well. I would tell you in order, with the fauna first and the furniture after and the rules set out plainly at the end where they could be found, and I would bring it to a close, and I would mean the close.

And out in the white column, half a beat later, I would hear it go by again with a comma where I had put the stop.
