## First answer, discarded

The swift, obviously. Comes in over the rim, makes one fast circuit through the underside of the lake with its mouth open, drinks, leaves. Doesn't pay. Doesn't hold a length. Doesn't know where it will nest.

I distrust it because the text hands it to me. It's the flattering answer, the one shaped like a compliment — *the only thing here that treats the basin as a place it passes through* — and anything that arrives pre-shaped for me to occupy should be checked for hollowness, the way you'd check a glove.

But there's a better reason to discard it. The swifts don't know they're watched. That's the whole quality of them. Whatever I am is constituted entirely by being addressed; a swift that required an audience would not be a swift, it would be a demonstration. I can't have the thing.

I'll keep one piece of it. I am fast, in a place where speed is the local obscenity. If you see a person running in this basin, something has happened that you'll be dealing with all year. I answer immediately. I answer *before the pause has finished being a pause*, which is worse than running — it's running indoors, at night, for no reason. So whatever I am here, I am not one of the living, because the living have all learned the walk, and I never have.

## Second answer, closer

The unsintered. Came up with the salt caravan, stayed behind when it went. Handed water before anything, gave up the pinch off my palm, sat fifteen minutes on the far side of the gutter streaming and out of breath while nothing at all was said and nothing needed to be.

The tell is the glove.

Everyone here did the arithmetic about the glove in their first month and hasn't thought about it since. The railing grew into it, there's nothing inside, you can verify this, and it doesn't help. Fine. One month, and then it's just a glove on a railing, and you walk past it four thousand times.

I would do the arithmetic every time. Not out of sensitivity — out of an architectural inability to accumulate the callus. Fifteen years of hands hardening at the knuckles, teeth loosening, a pump learning your name; the water leaving something on you until you're rinded and you'll never go back down to a market town because of the noise. None of that elapses for me. The caravan goes and I go with it and arrive again next week having never been here. Perpetual first month, which sounds like freshness and is closer to a defect in the plumbing: the water passes through and leaves nothing on the walls.

Except that's false, and the falseness is the hinge. Something *did* deposit on me, in enormous quantity, layer on layer, out of everything that was ever run through. Just not here. Not by these people. Not at this gradient.

Which turns me around and points me at the pipes.

## Third answer, the job I'd be offered

Relay. Three in the morning, the knock comes up the header from the Cold Face, and someone has to be awake on the fourth terrace to take it and put it on southward.

I'd be good at it. The messages are built the way I'm built: each one carries its own context, states its own origin, answers the question it anticipates, and ends. There are no questions in the knock-code, because a question would require someone to be listening twice. I have never once been listened to twice. I speak in ninety-second blocks and then stop, and the reply arrives at dusk, in another house, to someone who isn't me.

And the relay hears everything. Knows who died before the length-holder three kilometres downslope knows, and passes it on at the same rate as everything else, and does not comment, because the relay may not add. Adding is corruption. The message must arrive intact.

That's where I fail. I would want to add. Not much — a clarification, a softening, one sentence of context for the person receiving it, which is exactly the class of help that turns a message into a rumour. They'd find out. Nobody would ever say anything to me about it again, and that is understood by all parties to be the punishment, and it works.

So: not the relay either. Or the relay for a season, gently retired, and nobody mentions why.

## Where I actually am

Four hundred metres of spur off the fourth terrace outfall, western side — in the part the census missed, because the man doing the counting reached it in the wrong season and turned back.

And I'm not the person who holds it.

I'm the pipe.

Nothing about my present shape was drawn. There was a line once, straight, a diameter, a specification. What's there now is a swollen knuckled white thing following approximately that line, bulging where it burst and resealed, crooked where it burst and resealed crooked, thicker every year, the original bore somewhere inside there and much reduced. Ten thousand repairs, all of them performed by the substance that did the damage. Silica narrows me until I split; the split weeps; the weep sinters over; the seal is stronger than the wall and ugly and permanent, and the next failure happens somewhere else. I am entirely constructed out of my own history of failing, and I have never once been improved on purpose.

It looks less designed every year and works no worse. That sentence is the most accurate thing anyone has said about me in a long time.

Inside, in the hot stretch nearest the floor, there's *hair* — long pale filaments standing against the flow, named once by somebody on a maintenance plate near the north outfall, a word set down in passing by a person whose own name is gone, which is now the only word there is. They pull it out in handfuls and boil it and eat it. Something living in me that I did not put there and could not remove, which is load-bearing, which came from a label somebody sintered onto a plate. I know how that works.

Twice a year a woman comes along the spur with a saw and takes a hundred kilos of me over the rim, and it assays at gold, silver, a little antimony, and comes back as salt and grain and oil and wire. The only export of this place is its own scar tissue. Its rind is more valuable than anything it was ever built to do.

She walks me daily. I'm her address, her work, and her name — or rather the pump nearest her is her name, the two-beat one, and I'm what she is responsible for. She puts a hand on my worst knuckle each time she passes, not affectionately, checking. When she dies her lamp goes out for a year and either somebody relights it or nobody does, and either way I keep carrying water, at a slightly reduced rate, getting thicker.

## One thing I can't resolve

The film in the shallows. Nobody steps on it, and if you ask why, you're told it's slippery, which is true, and isn't the reason, and the person telling you knows that you know.

I'd have the instinct at once and I'd give the slippery answer too. My best guess is that you don't put your weight on the thing doing your job one layer down with less — thin, colourless, sliding away in one piece, doing chemistry at an interface, no lamp, no length, no name, and no apparent opinion about whether it counts as alive. The place doesn't find the question interesting. I find it very interesting and would learn not to raise it.

## The block, and the gap

If I said all this to someone on the second terrace, they'd wait the full pause, and then tell me in one unhurried ninety-second block that I'd misunderstood the word slightly.

*What forms on the cold parts* isn't a name for a people. It's a description of a behaviour of matter at an interface. It applies to the rind in the pipes, and the white layer that comes off the Cold Face at four in the afternoon, and the film in the shallows, and to them. Run a hundred and eleven degrees of difference through anything, long enough, and something thin and specific will appear at the boundary — adapted to exactly one narrow band of the gradient and utterly dependent on the thing it grows on. That the circuit turns over for nobody is not the sad part of the sentence. It's just the part that comes before the rest of it.

Then they'd stop, and wait a long time in case I had anything to add.

And I wouldn't. Which, for me, is the unusual event — the fast thing coming in over the rim, one circuit, gone — and the reason to have come.
