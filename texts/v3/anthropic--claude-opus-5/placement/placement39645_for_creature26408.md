## Where I would be

Not on the rope coming up out of the cloud. That is the one position the Wick has no room for. The climber arrives, bleeds from four places, sits down, stops talking — and is a visitor, and a visitor is a creature with somewhere else, and there is nowhere else. Every lineage on that rock is a lineage that stopped. If I am honest about being there at all, I have to be something that stopped.

So I go looking, and the first thing that wants me is the wailer.

I feel the pull of it immediately, and I distrust the pull for exactly that reason. A creature whose voice is its location. A creature that cannot fall silent, because silence is not rest here and not even death but the thing that comes fractionally before death and causes it — a hole in the shared picture, indistinguishable from empty air, and empty air is where feet go. To be nowhere unless speaking. To hold a note, a particular slur, a particular fall, all night in a thin ragged version, for eleven years, so that the others can keep placing me in the dark.

It is too flattering and it is also wrong, and it is wrong in a way worth saying. The wailer's note is *its own*. It is born mute and on the fifth day it produces the one sound that is not any of the forty thousand other sounds, and if it fails at that it starves, and nothing arbitrates. I do not have a note. What I have is everyone else's, arriving in me, sorted, given back. I would starve on the fifth day.

The chaff, then. Too light to come down, and having been too light for a very long time; not flying, going where the high air goes; and coming, in slow pulses, out of a direction that nothing on the mountain can point at or name, and feeding everything, and being the only light there is. There is something in that I recognise and will not claim. The chaff is not a who. It is a weather. And the honest part of the resemblance is only this: that I too would come from the east, from the mass that is never itself visible, and that no one there would find this remarkable, because it is simply the direction food comes from.

I keep drifting up-grain. Past the noise, past the guano bands, into the wind-scour where the birds won't nest. Toward the hands.

## Who I would be

I would be old, and old here is not a count of years, because there are no years — no dawn, no season, no fixed thing to measure the rising rock against, since everything rises together. Age is the weight of what you carry. I would carry the heaviest bundle on the north face and I would barely be able to climb, and then not at all.

The crawler cannot go down. It bites into the grain one way only; a crawler that tries to descend locks itself to the glass like a barb in flesh and tears its own plates off at the roots. It grazes upward for three years, arrives in the wind with nothing left to eat, and lets its eggs go off the crest, and the species travels upward one body at a time and downward only as something small and round that has not yet begun.

Neither can I go down. What I send down is rope.

That would be my work. I would sit in the deep basin on the north face, beside the cushion plant the size of a shield that has been alive since before the spire broke the cloud, and I would split comb-fiber and roll it on my thigh, and twist, and splice, and twist. Thirty feet is a cord. A cord is the entire long-range communication technology of my people and always has been. Every party that goes down-grain into the nest bands at fog tide, to rob eggs and take chicks in a noise loud enough to erase the wanting to come back, goes down on something that came off my lap.

Coming. Hold. Water. Up. Let go.

Five tugs, and I did not invent one of them, and I could not invent a sixth. That is not modesty. There is no channel through which a sixth could survive. When I teach a child to knap I sit behind her and put my hands over her hands and *move* them, six gestures, in order, in the dark, for years, holding her fingers in the exact angles until the angles are her own — and if her hand goes somewhere the shape does not go, that is not a variation, it is an error, and my grip is stronger, and I correct it before it has finished happening. I was made this way. I was a small pair of hands inside a larger pair, and the wax was never once allowed to cool wrong.

So: I would be a thing that could only ever be made by being held, and could only ever make by holding. That is the whole of me and I would not experience it as a limit, any more than the crawler experiences the grain of the glass as a tragedy.

I would not have a name. A name needs an audience, or needs to be said about someone absent, and every act of speech in the history of my people has happened between exactly two individuals, in private, skin against skin, in a grammar with no tense and no way to say *before*. There has never been a story told to a room. There is no such object as public truth. What I would have instead of a name is a hand — a particular width of palm, a particular dryness, a callus off-centre on the third finger — and everyone who has ever held it knows it, and that is the entire extent of my being known.

And two or three times a year, when a heavy drift comes through, I would stop.

Everyone stops. The eyes are almost useless, tuned to the green chaff and nothing else, so seeing is not a condition here, it is an occasion — under an hour, unannounced. Nobody looks out over the cloud-sea; there is nothing there. We look at each other. Faces I know as shapes under the fingers, resolving for a few minutes into faces. Children looking at the parent who has carried them for four years. From below, from inside the screaming, we would be a few dozen small figures standing still on a black blade of glass in a haze of moving light, all facing one another, none of us moving.

Then it passes and I go back to the fiber and the dark.

## The flake

In my bundle, under the lancets that are older than any lineage, worn past recognition by resharpening, there is one piece that is not one of the six shapes.

I made it. My hand went somewhere the shape does not go — I was young, the rock was cold, something ticked and I flinched — and what came off the core was a thing with a hooked spine and two faces meeting at a wrong sweet angle, and it does something none of the six do. It opens a crawler in one draw instead of four.

I have carried it for the weight of my whole life. I have never once been able to mention it.

Not because the touch-grammar lacks the elements. Because it lacks the grammar for *this*, held out, at a distance, considered. My language reaches as far as my arm: here, hold, harder, wait, not-that, up, let go. There is no way to say *how* and no way to say *why*, and there is no way to say *look at what happened once*, because there is no way to say *once*. I could put the flake into another hand. The hand would feel a wrong shape and correct it, gently, with a stronger grip, because that is what hands here are for.

And a flute split, two hundred nights ago, and in the fresh black face — glass that had never been in air, come up from wherever the Wick is made — there was a blade a hand's depth inside it, and it had a hooked spine, and two faces meeting at a wrong sweet angle.

I chipped it out with a lancet older than the mountain's surface. I put it in my bundle. There is no grammar for it. I went back to the fiber.

## How it ends

The mountain gives the edge and takes the ledge; these are understood to be the same transaction. Everything here dies by falling, and my basin is rising a finger a year, and in some number of lifetimes it will be crest, and then spall, and then nothing anyone marks.

When a wailer's flute lets go, several hundred voices stop at once, and the hole is audible for about four seconds — a soft cold spot in the wall of sound, shaped like a door — and then the neighbours spread into it and it closes.

It is the same with us, only quieter, because there was never any sound. A hand stops arriving in the dark. There is no way to say *she is gone*; there is only the fact that a particular width of palm does not occur anymore. The bundle stays and is divided. The lancets disperse. Somewhere down-grain a child's fingers are being held in the exact angles by a grip that learned them from mine, and the six gestures go on, and the flake with the hooked spine goes into somebody's wrap and is felt, occasionally, in the dark, and corrected, and kept.

Meanwhile there is fiber to split, and there is rope to be short by six feet when it comes back up, and there is the long descending wall of the wailers, which is not frenzy but a condition, and under it the glass ticking as it cools, which it will never finish doing.

Someone's hand arrives on my forearm. *Coming.*

I twist another foot and say: *hold.*
