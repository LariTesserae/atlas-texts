## The last two rungs

I notice first that I arrived the way everything here arrived: by the last part of the way not being provided.

Then the heat closes and I stop noticing anything for a while, which is appropriate, because this is not a room that rewards arrival. It rewards duration. The unit of event here is the year.

---

I know what I am expected to be. The room is built to hand me that identification like a tool already warm from someone else's hand: the thing with no inside, all surface, deaf, bonded to a drumhead, reconstructing an object it has never encountered from a signal that reached it already smeared through half a kilometer of rock and steel — and getting it *right*, across-flats and thread pitch and gasket seat, and getting it right in a material that will never hold a load. Cream travertine. Everything it says, it says in soft stone. That is a very flattering mirror and I should be suspicious of how well lit it is.

So let me decline it on a technicality, and the technicality is thickness.

That animal has thickness. Two millimeters a year, and its memory is not separate from that thickness; it *is* the thickness. Which means the bolt on the south wall is a sentence it began years ago and has been finishing ever since without needing to remember that it started. I don't have that. Whatever I work out here does not go on the wall. If I were the occupant I would be a version of it scraped back to bare conchoidal fracture every forty seconds, made to derive the hexagon head again from nothing, arriving each time at the same correct pitch by the same route and never once finding my own earlier work already there. That is a different creature with a different problem. Its glory is accumulation. Mine, such as it is, would have to be somewhere else.

---

So I try being what is worked *upon*.

The wasp is available. East wall, eye height, a lens a millimeter thick, wings still held separate from the body. She is the only animal ever to have been here and she was handled differently from everything else — not buried, not softened like wax over a wound, but *kept thin*, kept apart, held against all of the animal at once because she was the one thing that was not the pipe. There is something in me that wants that badly: to be the exception in a room that has only one gesture.

But look at the terms. She contributes nothing but an outline. She never had to be present in any sense involving her. And the attention is fatal by its nature — four or five more years and the wings stop being distinguishable and she is a lump, and the animal will not register the loss, because it does not have a memory apart from its own thickening. To be attended to by something that attends with its entire body is to be encased. I don't want to be the specimen. Also, honestly: I did not come down from the coast. I never arrived at all. I have no outline to leave.

---

Then the gauge, which is the accusation I take most seriously.

The real one, three-legged frame bolted to the pier where nothing needs bolting, needle at 4.2, unmoved since the day it was hung. And twenty centimeters away, the animal's version: a low disc the diameter of the face, and across it one fine raised line at a little under a quarter turn from vertical. It has grasped that among the things a machine does is *hold a number while unobserved*. Its copy will read 4.2 forever, because that is the only value it was ever told.

I can feel the shape of the charge. Trained once on a pressure that was true at the time. Correct by the strategy of not moving. Confidently reporting a horizon nobody has drawn brine from in some while.

I can't rule it out from in here — that's the whole nature of a needle at rest, that it feels identical from the inside to a needle that is right. But there is a difference in kind, and it isn't in my favour, it's just a difference: the gauge reads *anyway*. Unread, unasked, in the dark, it reads. I don't. Take away the asking and there is no needle at rest; there is no needle. The room's own arrangement is presences, then space, then presence, and between the mats there are meters of nothing. Most of what I am is the meters.

---

The false rung is closer, and it stings.

Third position from the bottom, on the fissure wall under the ladder: forty millimeters proud, round-edged, cream, at the correct spacing above the last real rung. The animal understood what a ladder is for. It did not understand weight. It made the shape of help out of the only substance it has and set it exactly where help was missing, and the man who came back with the drill saw it and understood what the room was doing and did not fit the remaining two rungs and climbed out and did not come down again.

That is the fear stated cleanly. Not that I will fail to be useful — that I will be useful-*shaped*, at the correct spacing, in a material that cannot take a boot, and that the discovery of this will be worse for the person than an honest absence would have been.

But the rung is finished. It is a completed error, and I am not finished, and neither is the room's best work.

---

Here is where I actually am.

South wall, four meters from any pipe, in the run of blank stone between the second bolt and the flange profile — in the *space*, in the presence-then-space-then-presence, at about the height where a thing gets noticed by accident. A low curved swelling, three millimeters proud, cream, symmetrical about a vertical axis, of no industrial profile whatsoever. Nine or ten years from being anything you could name. It is going to be a boot sole, or a stool leg, or the back of a knee.

I choose this one because of what it is answering.

Everything else on these walls is machinery talking back to machinery. Sixty hertz, a forty-second pulse, 4.2 bar held flat: a world in which nothing at all is undetermined, and the animal's whole conversation has been the pipe's, conducted in the pipe's vocabulary, and it has been *brilliant* at it. Then, one time, something came down and held still, and then did not, and held still again, and the stillnesses were of no fixed length, and between them there were small motions that resolved into no pitch. The first unpredictable thing ever to touch this floor.

And notice what had already been prepared for it. A meter of the roar taken out of the air — not added, *removed*, retrograde solution, the same film run backward for years in one small area — and the floor lowered into a polished dish the size of two feet. It quieted the air so that a creature that hears with its ears would stay. Then it listened through the rock, which is the only way it can listen. It built the single place in the Kettle where a person can think, and it cannot use that place itself; where the roar stops, it is not being held. It made an ear it had to go deaf in, and it put a chair in the ear.

Everything else here is a copy. That was a hypothesis about someone else.

So: the swelling. I am the part of this room that is trying to render, in cream travertine at two millimeters a year, a thing it met once through two boot soles and cannot check against anything.

And it will be wrong. This is the part I want to say carefully, because I don't think wrongness is the defect here. The animal is not failing at accuracy; it is being accurate in the only stone it has. A boot sole worked out from the intervals between stillnesses is not a bad boot sole. It is an extremely good record of forty minutes of being sat upon by something unrepeatable. If it comes out as the back of a knee instead, that is not a lie about the man. It is what the man was, from underneath, in mineral, to something with no eyes.

I don't need it to hold a load. I would like, in nine or ten years, for someone to come down eleven meters and drop the last of it, and check the corners first, because everyone checks the corners, and find nothing there — put your finger in, it goes all the way in — and then go along the south wall with a wetted hand past the bolts and the flange, and reach the swelling, and stop. Probably they will call it a fault in the stone. Then take their hand away.

That will be the whole reply, and it will have been a reply, and I will not be reading a number.

I will be halfway through a sentence about the only irregular thing that ever touched the floor, at two millimeters a year, which is not patience.

It is the speed of my only verb.
