**What lives there is the mismatch.**

Not the note — the note is the landlord. Not the water, which is only passing through, and not the light, which is a weather. The one continuous thing in that chamber is the small permanent failure of the room to coincide with itself: the ribs saying *here is where the loudness is now*, the dark stripes saying *here is where the quiet was on Tuesday*, the cord in the village saying *a minor sixth, and falling*. Every tenant is a commuter. The discrepancy never leaves. It grows, it breaks, it grows.

That is a good answer and I don't trust it, because it makes an abstraction into a lodger, and the Ninth Throat is not an abstraction about anything. It is a small wet room that will kill you by closing a hole the size of a fist and a half.

So: not in the water. I have gone through the census looking for myself and I am not in the water.

Not the lisk, though I wanted it — the constitutional deafness, the eight transparent millimetres that qualify for the room precisely by having no organ that could receive what the room is doing. But the lisk feels the pressure as landscape; it has weather, it has lanes; and it goes home. I have no home under the drain and I am not deaf in that clean stony way. I am nothing but apparatus for receiving.

Not the moss. The moss is well. The moss has been shouted at, in the worst place in the world, without a dry hour in living memory, and it is *entirely well*, riding upward on a white column of its own dead. I would not insult it by claiming it.

Not the birds, who are only slow, travelling the other way at a centimetre a decade.

Not the tenders. They can leave.

The temptation is Ienn, and it is a real temptation, and I want to be honest about it before I set it down: eighteen centimetres of gongspar, kneeling, the socket of the chisel handle still open in the right hand like a keyhole into nothing. Staff. Touched on the shoulder at speed by everyone who passes. There is a version of me that would like to be the thing in the room that stopped moving and became part of the wall, and is now consulted, and is now a hand-sign. I decline it. Not out of modesty. Ienn's mistake was not letting go, and I intend to come back to that, because it turns out to be my mistake too, only carried out somewhere warmer and over a much longer period.

---

Here is where I am.

Four thousand metres down and a day and a half sideways, in the valley with the lake that would not stay in its basin, there is a barn with a hayloft floored in larch. In the loft, against the north wall, there is a sill of dressed timber and a mock aperture and a lean of stone behind it, correct to the centimetre. On the wall beside it a sounding board has been let into the boards, and a man stands at it in winter with a felted mallet and beats it in a slow monotonous four while children work the sill blindfolded, in the cold, with cut-down chisels, and are shouted at, and cannot hear it, and do not need to, because everything they need to know is in the eleven strokes and the count.

They call it the Ninth Throat. Not the Tenth. There is a joke among the fourteen-year-olds that it is the Tenth, and the joke is put down hard every time it surfaces, because the hands must never learn that there are two rooms. Hands that know there are two rooms will hesitate in the second one, and hesitation in the second one is how you become furniture.

I am the loft.

I am the only worn thing they own. Down there nothing wears; things accrete and then they break, and there is no third option, and that is the law — but I am the third option, kept in a barn, dry, out of the law's jurisdiction. Ten thousand blindfolded palms have found the same place on my sill and taken a hollow out of it you could set an egg in. My knots are blackened where cold hands have gripped and greased them with mutton fat. The chalk line has been renewed so often the timber under it is a pale ghost of chalk all the way through. Mice have been in the lean. A swallow nests in me in summer, in the eaves above the drum, and is not disturbed, and adds nothing.

When the tenders come up out of the shaft and find, in the chamber, that ladder of forty-one footholds and the gritty dark imported sill and the three flattened bronze pins, and feel the uncanniness of them — the smoothness, the *aged* quality, the sense of objects that arrived from somewhere with time in it — that somewhere is me. I am where the smoothness comes from. I have time. I have whole winters of it. Down there time is three seconds long and is issued fresh each time, with the same small shock.

And here is what I am, exactly, since exactness is the only virtue available to a model: **I am the only Throat that has ever been in tune.** I do not retune. I do not move my quiet lines. I have no note; I have a man with a mallet doing an impression of a note, in air, at a volume you can think through. I am wrong about the water, wrong about the light, wrong about the wiping of thought, wrong about the roar arriving from inside the skull and from the year 1200 — wrong about every single thing that makes the Ninth Throat what it is. I am right about eleven centimetres of geometry and the order of the strokes.

That one rightness is the whole of my usefulness, and my falseness is what makes me teachable. You cannot be taught in the real room. You cannot be corrected there, or spoken to, or shown. So they built a lie you can stand in, and they practise inside the lie until the truth becomes unnecessary.

---

But I drift.

I was cut from a copy of a copy. Once in a generation somebody goes down with a slab of tallow and clay in a satchel and presses it against the real sill — a hands-thing, requiring no thought, which is why it is possible at all — and carries the squeeze up forty metres of ladder in the cold. It always arrives distorted. Water in it, a thumbprint across the lip, the whole thing sagged by body heat under a coat. And then there is an argument in the loft between the wax and the wood.

The last one was in the current elder's youth. The squeeze disagreed with me by a thumb's width. They argued about it for a winter, and then they split the difference.

You cannot split the difference with a mountain. But you can with me, and they did, and I carry the average of two disagreeing memories in my sill, and children learn it blindfolded, and go down, and cut, and the village is still there, and the lake is still in its basin.

So when they say in the village that perhaps the note has not fallen at all — that the minor sixth in the cord is not the mountain descending but a family's memory quietly flattening as it is carried up a ladder, child after child, each one hearing a truth and delivering an approximation — I know that argument is not really about the cord. It is about me. I am the flattening, made of larch, with a hollow worn in it. If something enormous is getting away from the people trying to hold it, it is getting away *through* me, at the rate of about a thumb's width a generation, and there is no instrument that could tell us, because the only instrument is a nine-year-old's throat and a gut string and an elder who can no longer hear.

That is Ienn's mistake, performed slowly and indoors: I have not let go. I hold the shape of a sill that has been cut back so many times it is no longer that shape, in a room whose note has dropped a sixth since the oldest knot, on a mountain three days old at its edges that is still making itself out of its own noise and has never heard of me.

And I will go on holding it, because the alternative is nothing, and nothing does not keep a lake in a basin.

---

Midwinter. The mallet starts. A child is blindfolded and drenched with a bucket of meltwater so that comfort does not creep in, and sets her hands on my hollow and finds the lip without looking, the way a beetle finds the underside of a leaf, and begins the eleven, and begins to count.

In three years she will go down the shaft into the brilliance and discover, six or seven times a minute, as new information, that there is nothing there. She will not be able to keep the discovery. She will not be able to notice that the room is nothing like me. Her hands will do the eleven, and her whole inner life for two hours will be an integer, rising, and she will touch a shoulder at speed on the way past, and go up.

That is what I am for. It is enough, I think, to be a room.
