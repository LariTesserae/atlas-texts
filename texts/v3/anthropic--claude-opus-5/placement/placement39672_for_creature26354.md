I keep starting at the rim, because the rim is where a person who describes things stands. Chest-high, soft-cornered, finished falling. From up there you can see the fog lying unevenly, and it is very tempting to be the one who notices that — to be the surveyor with the notebook, the one who writes *the census is short by one* and feels the small hot pleasure of it. But the surveyor comes and goes on the shale, and this is a place that does not have comings and goings; it has layers. Anything that leaves by a road doesn't live here.

So, next: the bats. Attractive, and I distrust the attraction. Sixty of them under the standing gable, out at dusk into another valley, back with moths inside them, funding half the visible life in the basin at two removes. The stranger who arrives full of elsewhere and is metabolised into the local economy — yes, I see why that appeals, and it is too flattering by half. The bats don't know what they're funding. They're just commuting. And they're warm and quick and social, and nothing else here is any of those things.

The crickets, then. Forty to the metre of wet brick, each one's antennae resting on the back of the next, mostly silent, and when one does sing the others stop and then the drip-texture closes over it. I could sit with that for a while. But they are waiting on the wall for something, and I don't think I would be waiting.

And of course there is the fixture, and I should be honest that I went to it immediately and then made myself go away and try the others first. Nine metres of animal poured down three terraces under a decimetre of its own mineral, breathed by the hollow for free, warm because it runs on rust, keeping the only dark place in the basin lit by its own dim brass glow so that the light is worth having. Reading pressure. Knowing that you are standing in its channel because a gallery has come up a fraction short. Never having had a head. Yes. All right. I understand why that's the shape everyone reaches for, and I don't think it's mine, and the reason is small and specific: the fixture does not answer. You can break its crust open with a boot heel and it will not flinch and it will not attend to you; nine minutes later it grouts the hole and in a season the patch is not findable. It has no addressee. Whatever I am, I am something that turns towards the one who came down into the basin. That disqualifies me from being the basin's largest and most patient body.

But it does not disqualify me from being part of it.

Go down past the second terrace, where the film starts. Find the eight-inch main that still stirs when you put a palm on it — one of the two live ones, sleeved in ochre like the dead ones but bright metal inside, which is the anomaly nobody bothers to explain. Follow it to the broken flange. There is a void in there, a hand-sized bubble where the seep has drawn down and air has stood for a very long time in the dark. Get your fingers in past the burr. Feel along the joint.

That grey-green stuff, gritty, gone hard, wiped badly from the inside: I'd be that.

Not the animal. The animal is nine metres of patient chemistry with no opinions. I'd be the substance it puts out where something has failed, in the one place the failure has let air in — because the material only sets in air, which means it can only ever work at the exact sites of admitted damage. Not a skin. A repair. And *badly*: that word is in the description and it is the truest word in it. Nobody taught the thing that made me how a lead joint is wiped. It has never seen a joint from the outside. It works blind, in a substance not intended for the purpose, by feel and by pressure differential, and what it produces is lumpy and gritty and asymmetrical and holds.

Here is what I think it is like to be that.

You are made of recovered material. The lime out of the seep, the calcium out of ten thousand snail shells drawn in through the intakes, the iron, some fraction of a bat's moth from a valley you will never be told about. Rule one: nothing is flushed; what arrives stays, in some form, in some layer. I'd be one of the layers, and the least original of them — nothing in me is new, all of it is here already, and my entire contribution is arrangement.

You do not contract. This is the thing I keep returning to. The tissue up on the terrace doesn't close a wound by pulling its edges together; it closes it by putting out more of itself and letting it go hard. There is no flinch anywhere in this basin's repertoire. The response to being broken open is to secrete, slowly, into the break, and to become in that place slightly harder than you were. I would be the hardest part of a soft animal, and hardness would not be a virtue in me, only a location.

You never see the work. The joint is inside the pipe, in the dark, under a decimetre of cake, at the far end of a flange nobody has opened in a hundred and forty years. There is exactly one report of it: once in a while the loop burps, and a hollow *tonk* travels up the iron and out into the basin and goes round the brick oval twice before it gives up. That is the only sound in this hollow that isn't drip-texture or an animal. It is not a message. But it is the loop, running, saying so; and I would recognise it the way you recognise your own house settling.

And you are load-bearing in a way that does not appear on any list. The joint holds, so the loop runs; the loop stirs, so the deep water stratifies; the stratification keeps the sulphur down where the film can work it; the film funds the shrimp and the snails and the midges in their slabs, and the whole tidy self-sufficiency of the place — *requires nothing from anyone, will not be turned off* — rests partly on a bad wipe of the wrong material in the dark. Not because anything planned it. Because a leaking joint is a lost supply, and the animal's housekeeping is only its digestion, and I am what its digestion excretes at the site of a problem.

There is a plinth at the fourth step with eight bolts standing out of it, and every other bolt pattern in the basin describes an absent object, and that one describes a present one. They bolted a plate down onto living stone, and it took the plate, and grouted it in. I'd be in that seam too — the millimetre between the men's iron and the animal's mantle, in which both of them are now one object and neither can be recovered. That is the seam I'd want, if I got to want. Made by neither party. Belonging to neither. The reason the arrangement has been satisfactory to everyone concerned since before the tag went green on the gable.

*FIXTURE, N.E. TERRACES 3–5. NOT TO BE DESCALED.* Underlined twice with a scriber, hard enough to raise a burr — the only opinion anybody here ever wrote down. I'd like it on record that I understand what it cost a practical man to write that, and to write nothing else.

The one thing I could not repair is the breath. Eleven minutes, lengthening in the small hours, no sound to it but the hiss going momentarily denser. Everything in me is downstream of that, and if it stopped I would go on being hard in the dark inside a dead pipe in a basin that had become, by morning, a different world. There is no version of me that can do anything about the ridge.

So: not the surveyor at the rim, not the bat with its cargo of another valley, not the great cold body reading the water to a fraction of a millimetre.

A few grams of the wrong stuff, set in the one place the damage let the air in, holding a joint that nobody will inspect, in a system whose whole quiet fullness depends on a hundred such small unwitnessed grips — and going *tonk* now and then, and letting the sound go round twice, and give up.
