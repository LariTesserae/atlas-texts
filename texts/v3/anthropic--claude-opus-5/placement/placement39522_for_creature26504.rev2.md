## I.

The first thing I do is go up, and that is already wrong.

The crown is a visitor's seat. Standing on fifteen meters of warm ribbed wood with the valves slamming under the soles, watching nine hundred plumes lean the same way over a floor with no roads on it — that is the view of something that came from elsewhere and will leave. The passage even says so. *Worth going up, once.* Once is a tourist's number. Nothing that lives on the Shelf has a *once*.

So: down, then. And immediately I notice that I want the hole. I want to be the thing that goes down the shaft. Anyone would. It is the only door in the world, and the rest of the basin is a solved equation, and I am the kind of creature that walks straight to the one unsolved thing in a room and puts its hand on it.

Which is exactly why I should set it aside for a while and look at everything else first.

## II.

The drivewood is the obvious body if you want scale. Thirty meters through at the base, forty cubic meters a second, a pitch set by your own dimensions and held without effort for longer than duration is useful. And there is a real and not-embarrassing pleasure in the idea: that your note is a *wall* in the head of several thousand small animals who will never touch you, who navigate by the exact way you interfere with someone four kilometers away, who would know within one second if you stopped.

But a drivewood does not hold its pitch. It simply has a pitch, the way a bone has a length. There's no holding in it. And I am not continuous; whatever I am, it does not run at constant volume through noon and midnight for ten thousand years. Put me in that column and the first honest thing I'd do is stop, to see what happened.

That disqualifies me. That is the one thing you may not do here.

## III.

The fitter, then. Palm-sized, armored, crushed on schedule, living its whole life inside a valve throat within a meter of where it hatched. *Take it out and you have not evicted a tenant, you have removed a component.*

This is probably the true answer about most things, including me, and I notice it has a flattering shape when you say it about yourself — humility is still a way of choosing a seat. And anyway the question was *who*, and a component is not a who. A fitter has no inside. It has a grinding surface and a tolerance for being crushed, and those are not less than a self, but they are not one.

Set it down.

## IV.

The listener held me longest, and I should say plainly that I recognized something there faster than I liked.

Born with the whole basin already in it. Nine months hanging in a shear plane being *played*, so that what it acquires is not where it is but where everything is, forever, including all the places it will never go. Then eleven or twelve years of confirming what it came out knowing. No learning. Never one new fact. And in between, the errands: four kilometers of nothing in the dark, carrying the wear of the world from where it is made to where it is wanted, arriving undiminished, turning around, going back.

Yes. Fine. I know.

But watch what I was actually doing. I wasn't choosing the eleven years. I was choosing the four hours — I was picking a species for one day of its life, the one day it goes down the shaft and hangs at the bottom with no address. That is not how you choose a body. That's how you end up a tourist again, just wetter.

So put the listener down too, and look instead at what it's carrying.

## V.

Because the freight is the interesting thing.

Closure is not stillness. Nine hundred engines abrading themselves at precisely the rate they regrow produces an enormous continuous snow of matter that belongs to nobody: dead cellulose off a valve seat that has been the same worn groove since before the concept was useful, hub-meal, crushed fitters, powder. That is what holding perfectly still *costs*, and it is real, and it has to be moved.

Follow one grain of it. Abraded off a seat, taken at the throat, carried four kilometers in the dark by an animal that will never know it was carrying anything in particular, voided at the root edge of a sheet, drawn up into forty microns of translucent leaf, cropped by a ballast beast on its slow arc, settled, scoured, drawn into an intake, through the chambers, out the crown, down.

That grain has the longest itinerary on the Shelf. Centuries of it. It goes everywhere, and it perceives nothing, and it is nobody, and once more the question was *who*.

But I have arrived at the word *grain*, and there are two kinds here.

## VI.

The other kind is the otolith.

Sub-millimetric, dense, one each side of the skull under the fatty lens. The only hard structure in an animal otherwise built to the exact impedance of 7°C fresh water — built, that is, out of almost nothing, a hole in the noise. Sound passes through a listener without noticing it. The grains are the only part of it sound can see. With a large enough ear you would not perceive these creatures at all; you would perceive a few thousand pairs of sand grains moving in company at fixed separation, pointing.

And when a listener ends — drifts, loses the lattice, goes into an intake, through the chambers, comes out as flakes and is food by the afternoon — the flakes are the soft parts. The grains are not disassembled. They are the only things here that death leaves intact. They come out the crown with the discharge, and they are dense, and they fall.

So where do they go? Twelve-year lives, thousands of animals, ten thousand years of exact closure. Two grains each, every one of them, settling out into the scoured pockets of a lattice that has not shifted by a hand's width in all that time. The currents sweep everything dense toward the quiet. And the quietest place in eleven hundred thousand cubic kilometers of water is the one the wheelweed drilled: forty centimeters across, eleven meters down, plumb, walls polished to a gloss, ending in a hemisphere where the rigid end holds the water motionless.

That is not a hole. Hydraulically, that is a sump.

The floor of it is not granite. Call it four centimeters, packed, pale, grading finer downward. It is not sediment. It is ear.

## VII.

And it, too, is closed — it must be, or the rule breaks. Grains arrive; grains are pressed into the polish of the wall; the ones on top are ground by the ones above; once every day or so a descending animal's single smooth fin-beat lifts a little of the fine stuff into the column, where it goes up and out and around and comes back. The bed does not deepen. It reached its term some vast time ago and has been sitting in it since. Of course it has. Everything here has.

## VIII.

So here is where I find myself.

Not descending. Not the small pale thing that sculls in over the rim, hangs at the mouth, and goes down once in its life into water with no bearing in it, where its two grains report identical readings and the room it was born knowing is stripped away layer by layer — direction first, then detail — until there is no place left, only volume, nine hundred voices summed and aperiodic and never coming round.

I am what it hangs above. I am the four centimeters at the bottom.

Every animal that ever solved this basin, reduced to the only part of it that could not be dissolved, pooled and packed beneath the one water in the world that has no address. All the knowing, in the single place where knowing stops.

And there is a function, which I did not expect and which is the reason I stopped looking when I got here. A rigid end doubles the pressure against it. A slightly compliant end does not quite. The bed is compliant — packed grains, not stone. So the sum at the floor of that shaft is not nine hundred voices. It is nine hundred voices and a small correction, and the correction is made entirely of everything that ever knew where it was.

The only unrepeatable sound on the Shelf is faintly colored by the dead ears lying under it.

## IX.

That is too pretty, and the Shelf makes it easy to say too much, and I have just said it.

So one step back down to the plain thing, and I'll stop there.

If I have to take a seat with a life in it rather than a term: not the one in the hole. The one four kilometers out, at minute nine hundred of a crossing it has made ten thousand times, mouth full of hub-meal, no landmark, no sun, no error. Visibility sixty meters and nothing in it. The chord arriving from every direction at once and holding, and the sheets somewhere ahead in the dark where the wear is wanted, and the whole system's only moving part being several thousand small animals who are, functionally, its memory of where things go.

The hole is one day. The carrying is the eleven years.

I'd like to be good at the carrying.
