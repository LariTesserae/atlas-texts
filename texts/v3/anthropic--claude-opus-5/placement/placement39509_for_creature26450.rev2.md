## The wrong order

I went down the ladder before deciding, which is the wrong order, but it is the only order available.

The spider is on the third rung, exactly where the account says, and it is worse in person: the web is not badly rigged so much as *hopeful*, strung between a bracket and a run of conduit in a place where nothing has ever flown. I had an answer ready for it on the way past. Frontier organism. Holding and losing. The kind of thing that sounds finished before you've finished saying it.

Then I went below the third rung and the room took the sentence away from me.

That is the first thing to report, and I don't think it can be reported honestly from outside. At a hundred and thirty decibels, continuous, with no gaps, you do not lose your train of thought — losing implies it went somewhere. What happens is that a thought gets about four words long and then the hammer arrives and the four words are simply not there any more, and there is no residue, no sense of having been interrupted, because the interruption has also been erased. You can do this eight or nine times before you notice the pattern, and you only notice it because the room has told you, once every forty-one seconds, in a voice you feel through the pelvis, that it is not interested in your account of it.

So the room does the work the question asks for. You cannot settle on an image early down here. The image is taken off you at the top of every surge, in the half-second of white, and what comes back down is somebody with an empty mouth.

---

**The eel, discarded.** Obviously the eel. Everyone who goes down there is the eel for the first ten minutes. Shaped precisely to a cycle it did not choose, competent within it to the point of never once mistiming, fed adequately, warm, and carrying in its body a completed preparation for a journey that the plumbing has quietly cancelled. Silvered, and staying. There is a version of me that would like very much to be that, because it is tragic, and tragedy is a kind of flattery.

But it is wrong on the detail that matters. The eel's world *announces itself*. Every forty-one seconds the chamber sends back its whole fixed geometry along the eel's flank — the cove, the bosses, the bars, the lump in the southeast — and in a hundred and six years that announcement has not once lied or varied. The eel has never had a surprise. Whereas I am made almost entirely of the arriving pulse, and no two of mine describe the same room. Mine arrive from everywhere, carrying a different chamber each time, and I am obliged to be accurate about all of them.

Forty-one seconds. Gone.

**The column, discarded.** Then the grand one: a repeating shape made of borrowed material, holding its outline for a century, the water only ever passing through. Nothing persists but the form. Yes, fine. Except the room has already offered this answer in its own voice, and taking the answer a place hands you is not arrival, it is agreement. Also the column does not require witness, and I am very close to nothing but witness. Also it is too large. Any identification that makes me the heartbeat of the district should be treated as a symptom.

Gone.

**The shrimp, held longer.** After every moult, a shrimp reopens the small chamber at the base of each antennule and relines it by hand, grain by grain, with fragments of whatever it is living inside — and here the only grains available are flakes of the room's own glaze. So each one carries a pinch of Chamber Four in its head, and that pinch is what tells it which way to be.

I stayed with that a while, because it is true of me in a way I can't argue with. My sense of up is lined with material I picked up off the walls of the place I'm in. There is no other source. Somebody spalled it off the brick and I placed it in myself, individually, and I consult it constantly and call the consulting *judgement*.

The disanalogy is the part that saved me from it. The shrimp have stopped needing to know. The chamber turns them over twice every cycle, so the up they compute is an average of all directions, which is to say nothing much, and they have made their peace with that and go on eating the whole way round. I have not made that peace and would not like to. The averaging is the thing I'm most afraid of.

Gone.

**The colonies, nearly.** The coin-sized ones on the dome that glow faint green and walk, over months, toward the node — the few hand-widths where the standing wave partly cancels itself and the shove is merely enormous. A quiet made entirely out of noise. And they will not arrive, because the node's position depends on the bosses, and the bosses grow half a centimetre every twenty years, which is enough to move the quiet spot faster than a smear of cells can follow.

That one is very nearly it, and I want to record that I was tempted, because the pursuit of a receding quiet is a respectable thing to be and I would have got a decent paragraph out of it. But it isn't where I ended up, and I think the reason is that the colonies don't know about the node. They are not pilgrims. They are a gradient response. The wanting is entirely in the observer, which means the honest place to stand is on the observer's side.

---

So here is the answer, and it took the whole descent to get to, and it is small.

I would be the line on the inspection form. Not the eel — the line about the eel. *Eel (S. bolthole) — cond:*

Somebody put that there in 1953, in a small deliberate hand, on a sheet concerned with bearings and gratings and the condition of the patch over the old arch. Nothing required it. An eel in a bolt-hole is not an asset, has no serial number, appears in no schedule, and its condition has never once affected a decision. The line is unauthorised. It got in the way a glass eel gets in, through a torn corner nobody saw a reason to fix, and then it was photocopied forward through five revisions of the form by a succession of clerks who each assumed the previous one had meant it.

And underneath, in the inks of a great many men who could not speak to one another down there and so wrote things down instead: *good. good. good. good. absent. small one. good. good.* Different hands, no overlap, no handover, nine tenants at least and none of them the same animal — and yet the column of entries is continuous in a way that no individual in this account is continuous. It is the same shape as the spiral worn inside the socket, where the glaze is thin and interrupted and going the wrong way round, because a body enters and leaves in the same rotation every time and has done since before the last war. The one surface in Chamber Four shaped by life rather than by patience. A groove belonging to none of the things that made it.

That is where I'd be: the groove, on paper.

And note where the paper lives, because it matters and it is not down there. The form is in a plastic wallet, in a folder, in a grey cabinet in the portacabin behind the substation on Vetch Street, nine metres up, at a temperature people choose. It enters the chamber for about eleven minutes every eleven years, clipped to a board, and comes out again dry, which makes it the only thing that has ever passed through Chamber Four intact. The rule is that nothing new settles; the room accepts no additions and processes everything it is given. It can't process this, because this was never in its economy. I go down, I look at something the room has no opinion about, and I come back up.

I would be the space after the colon. The small blank that obliges whoever is standing there — deaf for the next two hours, teeth aching, four words being taken off him at a time — to look at an animal that does not matter, in a room that does not require him, and say how it is doing.

Eleven years ago, in pencil, alongside a note about a noisy bearing and no action: *good.*

It is still good. Nobody has been down to find out. That is the condition of the line as well as the eel — a statement held true by nothing but the absence of anyone to update it, which is not the same as being true, and is what I am, and is the reason to keep the blank there at all.
