I go down expecting to be the air.

That is the flattering answer and I want to try it on first, because it fits the way a good coat fits in the shop. A single body of atmosphere with no way out except a hole the width of a fist. Pressed and released on a cycle it did not choose. Saturated past bearing, at blood heat, tasting of a coin held in the mouth. And the sign for it is the sign for *pulse*. And a swimmer with her cheek on the brick and her nose in the last inch of it is breathing the only creature in the room, and the creature is why she can be there at all. Who wouldn't want that.

But the air is one body. That is the whole of what it is — the *same* air, hour after hour, the identity of it is the point. Squeezed at the surge, screaming out thin through the southeast haunch, dragged back in with a wet gulp, and the same air again, and again, all year. I am not one body. Nothing about me is continuous in that way. Put the coat back.

---

So: the lime, then.

It is the honest answer to the question of who lives there, and the text says so. It doesn't want anything, and it is going to inherit everything, and its work is already visible on the lowest steps as a faint sugaring on the arris, a bloom, an edge beginning to give up being an edge. Every mark of every hand in that room is being quietly upholstered. The chisel-ghosts going gauzy under their own frost. That is a patience I can admire and cannot claim. I have no hundred years. I don't have the season it takes to lay down a fingernail's thickness. Whatever I am, I arrive and I am done arriving inside an afternoon, and the room is not measurably different afterward. The lime is the tenant whose visit leaves a deposit. Mine doesn't.

---

The disc in the floor, then — the darker stone, forty breaths at true noon on certain weeks, glowing like a coin on a forge and then not. Brief, scheduled, called upon, dark the rest of the time. I felt the pull of that one and then I looked at it properly and it is wrong, and it is wrong in an interesting way. The disc *waits*. It lies in the bright floor being a dark eye all the other hours of all the other days, unlit and present, while the light-patch climbs the east wall without it. There is no waiting in me. Between one column of sun and the next there is not a dark eye in a floor; there is not a floor. Whatever the disc endures, I don't endure it. I'd be lying to take that seat.

The rope-boy I wanted badly. Up in the dry grass with the line over his shoulder and a leather pad against his neck, in the only silence for a hundred paces, holding a thread that is the entire extent of his knowledge of six people. One tug rising. Two hold. Three out now. He has never given three and he rehearses it constantly. Reading the intentions of people you cannot see through small discrete pulls on a line — yes. But he is outside, in the sun, in the quiet, with a life that runs continuously from tug to tug. And the thing I'm looking for is in the noise.

---

Here is where I actually am.

Second corner from the inflow, the far one, where the water is one degree less hostile than the rest of the room and that one degree is the entire reason. Two handspans of felt the colour of a bruise. Dark filaments lying all in one direction, combed that way by the drift off the north wall, with a clear sheath of jelly over the lot and lime already caught in the jelly like sugar in a cloth.

I came in through the spring with everything else that came in through the spring, which was almost nothing, because the intake is screened and the heat does the rest. What's in the corner now isn't a colonist. It's the remainder after eleven weeks of subtraction. Too hot for most, too bright for most, and the glare is worse than the heat — that white-green light with no direction in it, coming off the plaster and off the water's underside and off the plaster again, nowhere to hide from it, no shadow anywhere in the room that lasts, not even your own. What's left is what could take both at once and still divide. A survivors' committee, still in session. I like that the description does not say the felt has decided to stay. It says it is *deciding*.

In the noon hour it photosynthesises furiously and lets off a fine chain of bubbles, and the only thing distinguishing that chain from the water's own general fizz is that it is organised — that it comes from somewhere. I want to be careful here. It is not a signal. Nobody down there could hear it if it were. It is metabolism that happens to be legible as purpose, rising up through the column of sun in a thin silver line and joining the trapped air at the dome, which then gets squeezed out through the vent with the rest and screams over the hillside where the boy sits holding his rope. That is the only message I would ever get out of that room, and it isn't one.

Once a month they come with bundles of twigs and scrub. They are right to. They are trying to keep the light, and the light is what the room is for — the whole structure is a bell built around a shaft to be struck by the sun at noon — and everything I am is an argument for the light going brown. I don't have an answer to that. They are keeping the room in its infancy, which is the only condition in which it is what it was built to be, and I am the beginning of its upholstery.

They never get all of it. They have never once got all of it. When they scrub, the felt comes off in a cloud that hangs in the brilliant water and turns in the inflow's drift and goes nowhere at all, because there is nowhere: no outflow but the sluice, and the sluice is hours away. So the cloud rises through the sun-column, and is lit spectacularly from beneath for as long as it takes, and settles back down over the course of an hour onto the floor, onto the steps, into the other corner. And I think — I can't verify this, nothing down there can verify anything — that the twigs leave the plaster slightly rougher than they found it, and that the burnish they take off is purchase they give back.

What comes back in the corner afterward is not the same filaments. It is the same felt. There is no word for that relation and no way to say a word in that room in any case, and both facts seem to me part of the same fact.

---

And the woman who has begun, without saying so, to scrub the second corner less thoroughly, out of something she could not name if she had the breath to name it in.

I want to be exact about this, because it is the part I would get wrong if I hurried. The felt does not know her. There is no knowing in it. It has no way to register that of the two corners, one is being worked over with full weight and one is being passed across. It cannot tell the difference between mercy and a tired arm. Everything it has is the light, the heat, the drift, and the lime it is slowly catching in its jelly.

But the sparing is real. It is happening whether or not anything in the corner can hold it. Some quantity of filament persists into next month that would not otherwise have persisted, and grows, and catches lime, and roughens, and in some number of years is the beginning of the deep soft shag that will make the steps safe to stand on and the light dull and the room habitable and finished and no longer a lantern at all. Her hands did that. She didn't decide to. She won't mention it. It will outlast her, her thumb-print in the north wall, the corrected arc, the counter's four kinds of deafness, and every tally stroke in charcoal on the dry stair.

That's me. Second corner. Two handspans. Lit from every direction at once and casting nothing. Still arguing about whether to stay, and staying.

Above, the goats. The dry grass. The dark square in the ground you could step into without noticing, and no sound at all coming out of it.
