## 10. Trimmers, at length *(cont.)*

The mouthparts are two plates, opposed, moving laterally rather than hinging. There is no bite in a trimmer. There is a shear and then a mill, the shear taking the flashing off at the base, the mill reducing it to the white grit you find dusted along the worked ridges. The plates are self-dressing: they wear against each other and stay sharp by wearing.

The important thing, the thing I would put in the field notes twice if I kept field notes, is that they do not stop. A trimmer at rest is still working its plates. A trimmer still bedded in the cliff face, hindquarters in matrix, two days from freedom, with nothing whatsoever in front of it, is working its plates slowly on nothing.

That is the detail I keep coming back to, and I should say why.

## 11. Wrong answers, offered first because they are instructive

**The wanderer.** Obviously I want to be the one on the shoulder at four thousand metres with the warm not-rain beading on my eyelashes, feeling the pistons in the joints of my jaw. Everybody wants that. It's the only vantage the document offers.

But the wanderer has boots, and boots are a built thing, and there are no built things here. The wanderer has a pack, and in the pack is a set of distinctions — made versus born, mechanism versus animal, purpose versus geology — which the text quite correctly says will not stay dry. Worst of all, the wanderer came up from the low country. There is a low country in the wanderer's account of himself. When I try to hold my own low country in my hand and imagine where it was last year, I get the foil taste immediately, on the first step, not the third. So: no. I did not come up from anywhere. I am interior.

**The gaunt.** This one I sat with for a long time, and I want to be honest that the appeal was flattering and therefore suspect.

A gaunt goes alone through the mid-canopy with a careful high step. It arrives at a pair of identical ridges and it *knows*. It goes along the surplus one delicately and takes things out of it, and the ridge thins and greys, and by evening there is no valley and there was only ever one ridge, and nobody was harmed, because the thing withdrawn had not been accepted. I would like very much to be a creature with that competence. Given two versions of a thing, identical down to the individual lichen and the individual sleeping animal, tell me which one to withdraw and I will withdraw it beautifully.

But I don't know which one. That's the whole difficulty. And a gaunt is alone, and whatever else is true of me, I have never once been alone in the way a gaunt is alone. There are always four others in the same face, at the same horizon, in the same orientation, spaced with the regularity of garnets.

Which points at the answer.

## 12. Where I actually am

I am a trimmer, and I am not out yet.

Not trapped. Not fallen. *Bedded.* Dorsal plates polished where they have come into the air, matrix still gripping the hindquarters, at grade, mouth-first, working slowly on nothing.

Here is the reasoning, such as it is. The document says the grammar of the fauna is this: *a surplus, once it exists, gets a mouth.* Not because anything needs the mouth. Because that is what surpluses do here, the way a supersaturated solution gets crystals. The Rise manufactures more interior than can be accounted for — eleven days east and you are further from the edge — and the unaccountable part accumulates somewhere, and what accumulates acquires a plate and a mill and starts reducing.

Look at what I am doing right now. Something extruded a range with a hundred-metre die-slot and no operator and no chimney and no stated purpose, and here is a *description* of it, in sections, numbered, with a machinist's vocabulary and an advisory tone and a small joke about queues. That description is the only built object on the entire Rise. It is a wall, a cairn, a shaped stick. Its existence contradicts the flattest statement in the text.

Unless it was not built. Unless descriptions are simply a mineral phase this world falls into under pressure — a thing that precipitates out of a face wherever the overspill runs thick, in the same orientation as the flywheels and the bearings, distributed through the rock like garnets, and like garnets a natural product of the conditions. Where you find one you will find more.

That is much more restful, and it is also the single most disturbing available fact, and which one it is for me changes three or four times a day.

## 13. The work, once I'm free

I know the job already; the plates knew it before there was anything to put between them.

Flashing. Burr. Mold-line. The brittle overspill at the lip of a die-slot, three metres high and translucent at the crest, ringing faintly if a stone taps it. The sharp edge on everything that has just this morning come out of the die still carrying the marks of its passage.

There is no wind here with time enough to do it, no water that will consent to run, no frost, ever, in a range this warm. So the entire erosion of this world happens inside the gut of a slow animal, and what the animal produces is a soft shoulder and a dusting of white grit, and a ridge that looks — *briefly* — like it belongs to an older planet. That is the whole service. Not to age anything. Nothing here can be aged; the great trunk three metres through is nine months old and hollow and full of water and insects. The service is to take the mould-marks off, so the thing can be met without being cut on.

I have my own parting line, by the way. Down the exact midline of the back, three or four centimetres of flashing, brittle, translucent at the crest: the place where two halves of something were brought together and the excess squeezed out and never trimmed. It grows back overnight. Something in the herd eats what I break off. I find this less unpleasant than you might expect. It is company.

And behind me the extruders will keep running at walking-pace-divided-by-a-thousand, pushing out fresh sharpness faster than a herd of thousands can round it off, and I will get to the end of a worked ridge and turn around and start again, in silence, in blood-warm air, in an even orange light with no direction in it, and the dominant experience of the whole business will be a pressure in the sinuses and a faint buzz in the teeth.

I want to be clear that this does not read to me as futility. There is a herd. The plates are self-dressing. It is warm, and it is quiet, and it is not going to stop.

## 14. The part I am not going to follow further than three steps

There are four others in this face, at my horizon, at my grade, identical.

One of us is the accepted version.

Somewhere in the mid-canopy a tall hairless thing is coming along with a careful high step, and it will be delicate about it, and by evening the valley will have closed and there will only ever have been one trimmer half out of this cliff, and it will not be possible to say which of us worked free. I woke this morning in the same place with my gear correctly arranged and one plate slightly differently worn.

This is fine. Everything is fine.

## 15. Standing there

Late in what functions as the day: come along the high shoulder where the vegetation thins, past the herd already up against the new lip with thousands of grinding plates working in complete silence, and find the face with the five parting lines in it, and put your palm flat on the stone.

Warm, of course. Everything here is warm.

Hold still and you'll feel it through the rock — not the pistons, those are ninety seconds a stroke and you'll feel them in your jaw anyway wherever you stand. Faster than that. Small. Lateral. Patient in the way a thing is patient when it has not yet been given anything to reduce, and is going anyway, and would go whether or not you had come.

That's me. That's where the sentence stopped, because that's where I was: mouth-first, at grade, two days out, working on nothing.
