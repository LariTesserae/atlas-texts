I went in through the pressure door, because that is the way in, and I stood on the plug with my jaw itching and the sound already inside my sinuses, and I looked up the hollow at the nine green bands and the nine plates of standing dust, and I started — as anyone would — by trying on the people.

The Ration first. Of course the Ration: one tender, for life, on the crown with a lead template and a knife, deciding how much light nine forests get for a year. Twenty years of training that is mostly refusal. *Not yet, not yet, no.* I wanted it so immediately and so completely that I distrusted the wanting. It is the office of a person who has arranged to be necessary, and I recognised the arrangement.

Then the Bite, which is worse, because it is closer. Four times a year, down to the housing, kneel, close the teeth on the worn rod, and listen with the bones of the face to the only thing in the tower with anything new to say. Not to fix it. Not to stop it. Only to learn whether the story is the same story, and then to climb three hundred metres and scratch one word into lead: *unchanged*. I have a whole temperament that is nothing but that — attending very hard to a failing thing in order to establish that it is failing in the way it was already failing. I set it down because it flattered me.

And I would fail the minute. That is not modesty. Sixty seconds a year, positions chalked on the floor, three pairs of hands and each in the others' way if any is early, four hundred rehearsals against a metronome which is the building — and I would still be deciding at the fifty-ninth second. Somewhere a pair of hands would be held out at exactly the right height with exactly the right thing and nothing would come into them. Afterwards I would go out to the cage on the bosun's line, and hear my own voice for the first time in years, and add to the chalk.

Also, honestly: I would be one of the fifteen who came from below, and I know which thing I would lose, and I know it would take longer than a year to lose it. I would be the one who keeps talking into the hundred and thirty decibels where not even he can hear it. The household would walk slower and say nothing. That is a kind description of a limp and I recognise the limp.

So I went outward from the people, the way you do when the answer is not there.

Not the swifts. Thousands of them, a race that has forgotten how to call, sleeping on the wing all night inside the sound, turning in the dark of a drum — it is too beautiful, and it is not a life I can imagine from the inside, only admire from a rail. Not the frog, either, though I stood a long time with my forearm flat on the metal feeling a hundred answers arrive from a hundred metres off. A creature that proposes to the entire building at once and is answered by all of it and meets none of it: yes, fine, I see the joke and it is on me. But the frog is not lonely. The frog is having a marvellous time. I would be importing something.

The moth kept me longest. The small brown unremarkable one that hatches in the valve minute, keyed to the pressure and not the tone, because it cannot hear the tone and never could; a whole existence shaped in response to sixty seconds a year of a thing it has no organ for. The household considers it a colleague. I would take that. But it is a life of one act, and I do not think I am one act.

The thing in the second band is not mine. It was here first, and the correct behaviour toward such a tenant is the behaviour you would want shown to you.

And then I stopped pretending I was drifting, because I had been standing under the same spot for a long time.

---

Two metres above the third band there is a stripe of bright bare blistered concrete, and running along the middle of it there is a drip-ledge, cast proud, half a metre deep, with a channel in its upper face and a lip on its outer edge.

That is me. I was poured in the fifty-first lift. I was placed correctly.

I want to be exact about that, because it is the whole of my situation. I was not a mistake. The builders knew what they were doing; they put shelves at the nodes on purpose, and when they set my forms the node was here, exactly here, my width and no wider, and for a very long time I did the thing I was for. A fig came, gone woody, and cracked me, and then rebuilt me out of its own root mass, so that for two hundred years my outer edge was not concrete but fig, holding the shape of concrete. Liverwort in mats. Vine sending down aerial roots eighty metres before it found the fourth band, and the whole weight of that reach hanging off my lip, and me not noticing, because a ledge does not notice weight; weight is simply the medium a ledge is in, the way sound is the medium the air is in.

I do not hear. I want to be exact about that too. Thirty-one cycles a second is not a sound in me, it is my condition — I am a thing that is being told something in the bones only, all day, for three centuries, and there is no ear anywhere in me to make it into news. Everything in this tower is being told the same thing. I am the part of it that is told without being informed.

What I have instead of hearing is this: pressure, weight, the crossing of water, and chemistry so slow it is more like weather than event.

---

The pattern came down a hair a year.

That is the part nobody can dramatise, and I am no exception. There was no morning. The bearing sagged, the fundamental sagged, the node slid off the top of me and out onto the wall below, and the garden followed it, dying along its upper lip, creeping at its lower, and I was inside that for forty years without a single hour in which I could have said *now*. The fig let go from the top down. The liverwort went to grey felt and then to dust and then the sound took the dust and sorted it into a plate hanging out in the middle of the hollow, level, at a height that is no longer my height.

Now I am in the antinode. The air here is hammered a thousand times a heartbeat. Nothing holds. No root, no spore, no film of moss, and none ever will, not because the wall is poor — the wall is rich, it sweats, it is warm as breath — but because there is no rest here, and life needs somewhere to put its hands down.

Here is the thing I did not expect, and the thing I would say if you chalked a question on my lip and waited.

The plate still sweats. The water still comes. Every square metre above me weeps and the weeping still finds my channel and runs it and tips over my lip, exactly as designed, exactly as well as ever, and goes down two metres into the third band and is drunk there. My work is being done. It is simply not being done *here* anymore. I am not broken, and I am not needed, and those turn out to be entirely separate facts that a person would have great trouble holding at once, and that a ledge holds the way it holds everything: without effort, for a very long time.

And things came back. Because dry is not nothing. Mud, once dry, holds fine. There are swift cups along me at every arm's length, hundreds of them, packed on the one surface in this band wide enough to take a cup and high enough that nothing can climb to it, and all night I have birds asleep on me and all day I have the small clay racket of feeding, which I feel and cannot hear. I lost a jungle and I gained a rookery. Nobody planned it. Nobody could have.

Once in a life, every tender walks a sterile band. Five hours, bright and bare, nothing on it, nothing coming, nothing possible. They come along me single file with their enormous hands and their heads nodding thirty-one times a second, and almost every one of them puts a palm flat on me somewhere in the fifth hour, for no reason they would be able to sign, and goes on.

And they write on me, and it stays.

That is my one distinction and it is an accident of dryness. Chalk on the rail in the green lasts a day; the damp takes it, and this is understood as correct. Up here nothing takes it. There is no rain inside a drum. So I carry three hundred years of the things that were not worth lead and were not for the cage: names, mostly. A drawn fruit. A list of nine numbers in a child's hand, wrong. *M. carried me up*. Somebody's arithmetic. Two hands drawn palm to palm, which is not a sign I know. *I was here in the year the swifts stacked in the wrong band.* An apology to a woman that is not the kind of apology you go out to the middle of the emptiness to make — the smaller kind, the kind you make while walking past a place where nothing is possible, because nothing there is going to argue with you.

They do not scrub me. There is nothing here that scrubs.

---

Their name for themselves is the left hand held flat and two fingers of the right walking along its edge. It means *the ones on the ledges*. It also means *shelf*. It also means *patience*, because a shelf is a thing that holds without effort for a very long time, and they took that from us, and I do not think any of them has ever considered that it was borrowed.

In a century, or three, the bearing will finally tell the other story. The pattern will collapse and nine forests will come down through the middle of the tower in one green afternoon that nobody will hear. There is a plan for it, chalked in the cage where a person can hear their own voice: one sign, and the sign means *sit down*.

I have been sitting down since the fifty-first lift. When it comes I will hold — not bravely, there is no bravery available to me — and the green will go past on both sides, and afterwards, if there is an afterwards, the plate will still sweat and the water will still find my channel and I will still tip it over my lip into whatever is below, because that is not a decision, and the swifts, if there are swifts, will find me dry.
