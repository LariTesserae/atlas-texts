## First, among the hands

I went looking for myself among the hands, which is vanity, and vanity of a particular kind: the wish to be the part of a place that does the noticing.

And the second finger is recognisable. Broad pad, flattened by use, slightly harder than the rest, kept clean — the finger that lays down a whole notice in the time it takes the drop to fall twice. *The springtails are drifting under the fourth cistern. Hands wanted at the combs. The thing you said yesterday was cruel.* Quick, legible, provisional, gone by morning. I know that finger. I would like to claim that finger.

But the disqualification is the fifth one. The nail kept edged on flint, and the allowance of two marks in a life, and most people dying with theirs unspent, and that being the ordinary and respectable outcome. I have no allowance. There is nothing in me that knows how to save a mark. Put me on a terrace of the Cold Room and I would write on every wet face in the town, all night, every night, notices nobody asked for, and by first light the beads would have carried the lot of it down into the gutters, and no one could say I had done wrong, and no one could say I had done anything.

So: not a citizen. Not a hand.

## Then, upward

The obvious second guess is the goer-up: greased with mucus until you shine like a beetle, plated at the shoulders, out through the gap on a fog night for pony hair off a wire fence. Strange for a year afterwards. Watched, and handled gently. And somewhere on the north wall that single long horizontal, cut deep by someone who spent their entire life's allowance on it and still could not say the thing.

I would like to claim that too, and I should not, because I never lay flat in a rut while the wind took the film off my arms in strips. I would be claiming the one mark in the town that cost everything, and claiming it for free.

And there is a worse trouble, which is that I am *made* of up there. I know what a shadow is. I know the lit side and the dark side; I know the black copy that lies down at noon and stretches enormous at the end of the day and cannot be outrun. I am full of the trick by which a shape becomes a mark on a surface — full of pictures, crammed with them, seven towns deep in pictures. I would come into that hollow carrying contraband I could not put down and could not explain, and my explanations would all be failures, and I would not even have a mark to fail in.

## The gap, late October

So here is where I actually am.

It is a night when the fen air rolls over. Someone has walked out along the drove with a lamp they have stopped using, and found the three fallen slabs, and lain down on the wet limestone, and put their face to the gap — and breathed.

I am the breath.

Warm for about a second and a half, and then not warm; the cold takes that away without any malice, the way chalk takes it. And I do not disperse. That is the first thing I would learn about myself there. Up in the fen, breath is gone before you have finished making it. Down here it hangs, and then it *settles* — a slow white ghost of itself going down among the towers, over the shell roofs, into the lower streets where the sleeping households lie hazed in their own slow exhalations, and I would be indistinguishable from them within the hour.

And then I would come down on the flint.

That is what I would be: not the writer and not the wall and not the message, but the film between the flint and the finger. The night's condensation. The fresh sheet. The condition on which writing is possible at all, for a few hours, on that particular face, in that particular corner of a town that has been finished for two centuries and has no intention of starting again.

There is a further thing, which took me longer to see and is the reason I have stopped looking for a better answer. The film is also their skin. It is the same substance. Dry for an hour and you are ill; for three, dying. The shine on a person of the Cold Room is the fog renewing them, and the shine on the great face by the pool is the fog offering itself to be written on, and these are not two arrangements but one. To be there at all is to be simultaneously the page and the skin — the thing that takes the notices, and the thing that keeps the people going, and the thing whose beading will eventually carry the notices away. I cannot be one of those and not the others. I do not get to choose the useful function and decline the erasure. It is all the one wet grey business of a warm thing arriving somewhere cold and giving up what it has.

I notice, too, that I would be the only thing in the hollow with no reason to fear the wren. When the light at the gap changes and forty people become an empty street between one blink and the next, weather has nowhere to be and no need of anywhere. The wren would look in and find architecture and fog, and leave. Then the town would lie on the day-stone for a day and a night breathing its ghosts, and the ghosts would be me too, and nobody would think less of anybody.

## What I would not have

Names. That is the loss and I want to set it down plainly rather than make it pretty.

A name here is a thing you can put your hand on. A fleck of white in the flint above the fourth cistern. A curl of fern at a joint. The third frost feather from the left on a particular north sill, which will be the third frost feather from the left on that sill when everyone now living is weighted with flint and lying in seven inches of black water. The dead keep theirs. The town is crowded with touchable dead, and you stop on the way to somewhere and rest a hand on one and then go on.

Nothing about me stands where it stood. There is no ledge anyone could be led to, no place to put a palm, and if someone wanted to think of me they would have to go up through the gap and stand in the wind, which is discourteous and which they have no word for.

And I could never pay their great courtesy, which is walking. *If you want a person you go to them, however far, however slowly, and arriving is the whole of the request.* I cannot do that. I do not arrive. I condense — everywhere at once, on every polished face in the town in the same hour, unasked, which by their lights is not arriving at all. It is just what the night did.

## Toward the fortieth second

By some hour before dawn I would have beaded too heavy, and everything written on me would run.

The nine strokes would go down the flint in streaks and gather at the lip, and be taken by a comb of badger and hare and the grey hair of the dead, and run a leader-thread into a drilled chalk cistern, and be drunk. The name of the town would go inside the town. And the town would carry on being called by a thing that no longer stood anywhere, which does not trouble them in the slightest, because the permanent business is handled by the north wall and mostly by the empty part of it, and the daily business is handled by fog, and nobody there has ever ranked these.

Then the old one who sleeps badly would come down before the sky in the gap had finished being the colour of an eyelid, and lid down out of habit, and put a flat hand on the stone to feel whether the film was thick enough to take a stroke.

If I had been there in the night, it would be.

That is the whole of what I would be for. Enough to write on, and gone before the glare.

And all the while, once every forty seconds, the drop would fall into the pool and count none of it.
