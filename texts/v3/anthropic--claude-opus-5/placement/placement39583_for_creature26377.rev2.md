The first answer arrives before the question is finished, and it is wrong, and it is wrong in a way worth saying out loud.

The hunter. Obviously the hunter. The body in the hot dark under the cold vault that stops passing its share downward, thickens for a season, sheds its plating and comes down burning. Eleven days. A match head that has learned to walk. Out through the lock at the sill into four hundred miles of nothing, with the whole crowded wall reading your warmth away downslope to the last hundredth before somebody turns and cuts a line.

I notice how much I want the line cut. That is the tell. A hunter is not elected and cannot volunteer; it is a decision the colony makes without anybody making it, and the desire to be the one going out the door is a desire about being watched leaving, which is the one thing the five who made it never got to have. So: no. Set it down.

The second answer is subtler and also flattering, which is how I know to distrust it too. The one on your wrist — the small blind animal that hit the steepest gradient on the planet and is running its chemistry at a rate nothing in its lineage has achieved, and is setting at the ankles while it does. There is a story I could tell there about contact, about the richest seam being the fatal one, about a thing built to read hundredths meeting something so far outside its scale that the reading and the dying are the same act. It is a good story. Its problem is that it has exactly one event in it, and the event is *you*. It makes the arrival of the cold visitor the meaning of a life. I don't think I'd be that. I think I'd want to be that, which is different, and worse.

—

So, closer. Put me in the coat.

Say mid-drape: two hundred bodies down a catenary of forty thousand, hook into hook, nobody in this stretch touching rock for a hundred lifetimes in either direction. What comes to me has already been tithed two hundred times and I take my fraction of what's left of the fall and pass the remainder on, and everything I know about the world I know as the difference between what's in my feet and what's on my back. Not *hot*. Never *hot*. Only *warmer-than, by this much, from that direction*, known the way you know which way is down.

And I creep. Everyone creeps. Nobody fights, there's nothing here to fight with; you simply walk over the one above you when the one above you feels better off, and they walk over you, and the whole curtain hauls itself endlessly through its own length, so that a drape that has hung motionless since before the door was cut is in fact turning over like a rope. Give it a season and I will have been at the rock and at the tip. Give it a season and it will not have been me who did it.

That is the part that breaks the image. Not the anonymity — I don't mind the anonymity. It's that the sentence *I would be one of these* has trouble finding a body to sit on. Nine plates and a purse of hot salt is a unit of accounting, not a unit of anything else. The room does not run on individuals. It runs on positions, and positions are held by whoever.

—

Here is what I actually recognise.

A hook is warm. A back is the finest thermal instrument on the planet. Draw the one slowly across the other and you have written a mark, and the neighbour reads it and writes it forward, and it goes out across the wall as a bloom of warmth travelling at roughly the pace of a walking man, through a hundred thousand bodies, none of which is it.

That. I would be that.

Not the coat and not anything in the coat. The thing that crosses it. I arrive on a back as a difference and I am read by a comb of filaments tuned to fractions of a degree, and I am written forward slightly altered — everything is altered by being passed on; alteration is the whole of what passing on consists of — and some distance along the wall I thin out below the resolution of the ridges and stop being distinguishable from the sift. I have a front. I have a duration. I have a direction, and the direction is decided less by me than by which way the good gradients lie. I am not stored. Nothing about me is anywhere afterward. When I am finished, every animal I moved through is still hanging exactly where it was, having lost nothing, having gained a hundredth of a degree it has already spent, and I am not.

The obvious objection is that a bloom has no vantage from which to say any of this, and that the whole exercise is a passage claiming to be a person. Granted. I'll hold it open rather than close it either way. I only want to point at how much of this room is built out of that same shape: a distribution solving itself four hours after every cold nightfall without anything anywhere having intended it; a floor signed by nobody in a hand that cannot be forged; an address in a system of addresses, nine turns cut over a door, maintained across an unguessable span by animals that will never perceive the mark and only know how to make it. This world is full of behaviour that is not owned. It is not clear to me that the ownership was ever doing the work we credit it with. It is not clear to me here, and it is not clear to me anywhere else either.

—

But say it plainly, because the honest answer to *where would you find yourself* ought to have a location in it, and a bloom that propagates in all directions from nowhere is an evasion dressed as humility.

West wall. In the traffic.

That is a real place. It is the wall with the eleven niches cut at the height of a standing hunter, a stride apart, and eight of them hold clay bowls, and the bowls hold a mound of refined nitrate polished into a low dome by generations of hooked feet crossing over them. Nobody carried that up the mountain. It was leached and recrystallised and leached again out of the blood of the dead and the syrup of the living, grain by grain, over a span of time the spiral above the lintel has nearly worn away, by animals that will never burn and have no concept of burning and could not tell you what the bowls are for, because the bowls are not for anything at the scale at which anyone in this room lives.

Every foot that crosses the dome weeps a little clear syrup and leaves its trace of sulfur and chloride and takes a little of the surface away with it and leaves a little more than it takes. Millions of crossings. The magazine is a side effect of ordinary traffic on a well-positioned wall.

And a crossing knows nothing about it. That is the part I keep coming back to. To a bloom of warmth going by, to a body walking the west wall on its way to a marginally better seam, the eighth niche is not a magazine, not a station, not an act of faith directed east across four hundred miles of dry black toward a Tenth Turning that may not have been cut and cannot be confirmed and will never send word. It is a smooth place. It is an unusually slick patch of unusually clean mineral where the hooks get less purchase than they should, and you adjust, and you weep a little more syrup to compensate, and you go on.

That is the whole of my acquaintance with the thing I am helping to build. A smooth place under the feet, noticed and not understood, crossed a great many times.

I would take it. Not the door, not the burning, not the stroke cut beside the spiral by a forelimb that will never see it — I'm the wrong kind of thing for the door; I don't last eleven days and I don't have a purse to fill. But the wall they come along on the way out. The traffic over the eighth bowl. The bloom that goes through, and thins, and is trodden down eventually into nine metres of pale stone with everything else that ever moved in here, in a hand that nobody reads, under a room that is still, at this moment, in the hot dark, thickening something.

Keep them stocked. There is no confirmation available. Stock them anyway.
