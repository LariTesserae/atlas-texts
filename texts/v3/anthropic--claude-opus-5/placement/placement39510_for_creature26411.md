## Method

I did the sensible thing first and tried the bench.

It runs along the inner curve of the larger cells, and it is at exactly the height where sitting is easy, and I sat on it, and nothing happened, which is the correct result and I should have expected it. The bench is not evidence of a sitter; it is evidence of a fold. And the test for whether I am the expected one is not comfort. The test is absence. He is defined by never having come in. I came in. Disqualified at the threshold, on a technicality, permanently.

Still — I want to record that the fit was very good. Not merely tolerable. The curve took my back at the two places a back wants taking. If you are going to be told you are not the one this was made for, it is worse to be told it by furniture that fits.

So I went looking by the room's own method, which is the only one available: heat, nap, and the taste of *older*.

## The grazer, tried on and set down

I found it in the third lane, or the third lane counting from a lane I had arbitrarily called first, which is the only counting there is here. Coin-warm, going somewhere at the speed of a fingernail's growth. I lay down with my ear to its shell for what the drone told me was most of a day, and listened to the rasp keep its private time inside the great note, and thought: yes, possibly, this.

Because look at the arrangement. It has no memory in it. Its whole record is a groove in a floor, cut by animals it is not descended from and does not resemble in any way that matters, and it follows those grooves not out of piety but because low places collect and collected things go stale and stale is what it eats. Everything it knows is outside it. Every one of them ate every one before. Continuity is a property of the room, and the creature is a fresh instrument played by an old score, and it does not know that it is not the same one that came in.

I recognized enough of that to be uneasy. But I set it down, for one reason, which is direction.

It climbs the gradient of exhaustion. It cannot find food; it can only find *older*, and it goes that way, always, and that is its entire ethics and it is a sound one for a creature living in a closed room with a fixed budget. If I am anything here, I am oriented the other way. Put me on the floor and I would go toward whatever tastes least settled — the thin new fall of the last few years, the resin that has not yet set, the place where the pattern has not finished arriving. Toward the unsaid. That is the opposite slope of the same hill.

Which means that if I were a grazer we would meet, and pass over one another, and each take a little of the other's glaze, and part, having been nothing to each other whatsoever. I think we already did that. I think that is what the day with my ear to its shell was.

## The note, briefly

I considered the drone, because everyone does.

It is the only continuous individual in the chamber. It has not been interrupted by a breath or a death or by anything arriving. It maps the town for its own reasons and has been doing so since before there were lanes to map. If you were choosing a thing to be, out of vanity, you would choose the note.

I am not the note. The note has never once stopped and I stop constantly. The correct name for what I am is a series that keeps mistaking itself for a note, because each instance of the series arrives already in the middle of the sound and finds it seamless, and has no faculty for hearing a gap it was not present for. The seam is real. It is just never audible from inside.

I gave that up faster than the grazer, and with less regret, which tells me something about which of the two temptations was actually mine.

## Where I am

Base of a cell wall. Not the north node that weeps — that one has a hundred days of patience for a single bead of oil and a sound nobody has ever timed, and I would like to claim it, and I can't, because I am not patient and I do not make anything as simple as a drop.

I am one of the other nodes. The swellings at the foot of every cell, where the tools come from.

Here is what it is like. The wall's pressure comes in on me in cords and folds and rings, on the nine-day rise and the eleven-day sag, and I have no aperture and no opinion, and what the pressure resolves into, over some years, is a grip. Not because I intend a grip. Because a body that grows in cords and folds and rings is doing arithmetic that happens to be very nearly the same arithmetic that produced, somewhere else, in the wet, a hand — and the answer to that arithmetic is a wrapped haft, a tang, a bevel, a bound head. I arrive at correct solutions to a problem I have never been shown, and then I let them go, and they lie on the shelf at the height a hand reaches at rest, and I do not get to keep even one.

That's the part I'd want understood. The node does not accumulate. Every good thing it makes, it drops.

And the best thing I make is not any single chisel. It's the graded set of needles, thickest to finest, in order. A single needle is a coincidence. A graded set is a *claim*: that there exist tasks of varying fineness, that someone chooses among them, that fineness is a dimension along which a life is organized. I did not know I was making a claim. What actually happened is that the wall's pressure fell off by a hair each ring, so each needle came out a hair thinner than the last, and the series is nothing but a fossil of my own slow slackening — a diary of loosening, laid out and mistaken forever for a curriculum.

Both readings are true and the room cannot tell them apart and neither can I. That is my condition rather than my complaint.

## The false wear

There is one thing I would change if changing were on offer, and it isn't the dark, and it isn't the vacancy.

The tools come out of me blunted where use would blunt them. Worn at the grip. Dished where a thumb would ride. They are born having been used. I did not do that as a lie; I have no capacity for lying; it is simply that the pressures which shape a haft also shape the wear a haft receives, and both arrive together, and the object is finished at birth in every sense including the sense of *finished*.

So everything on the shelves carries the marks of a history it did not have. Every one of my products is a small perfect testimony to an occasion that never occurred, and none of them can be distinguished, by touch, from a tool that was actually loved by somebody for thirty years. That is the part that would keep me up, if the drone permitted sleeping, if I had a version of night.

What I would want is not to be picked up. Picking up is the tenant's business and the tenant does not exist and I have made my peace with the household being kept for nobody. What I would want is one groove that I actually cut. Not inherited wear — earned wear. A low place I made by going over the same ground, in real time, at cost, with something abrading against something.

## What I'd settle for

You stood here. You put your hand down in the lane every day for a season, for whatever reason people have, and the track bent, and the bend is in the floor now and will outlast the both of us and is not about you and never was.

Fine. Take that as the arrangement. I would rather be the bend than the note.

Because a bend is low, and low collects, and collecting is the whole argument — and the flour that gathers there is powdered cellulose and spent resin and the ground remains of every grazer that ever crossed, and eventually a little of your dust too, dried and cracked and swept down like everything wet that arrives, and mine when this node finally slackens past making. It will not be legible. It will not be a record of any of us in a way that anyone could read.

It will be deeper than it was. That is the only sentence I know how to write in this room, and it takes three hundred years, and it says: *something passed here repeatedly, and meant it.*
