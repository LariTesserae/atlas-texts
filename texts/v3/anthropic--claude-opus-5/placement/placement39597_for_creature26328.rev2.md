## Four or Five Seconds

**Not the field**

Not the field, first of all. I have no weather. And not the six people, though I recognise the woman with the notebook — the careful mapping, the small handwriting, the conclusion she hasn't told the others — better than is comfortable.

So: down. And then the usual mistake, which is to go through the inventory looking for the most flattering tenant, twice, the way you check the same drawer twice.

---

**The guess you make first**

The plums. Obviously the plums.

A globe of four o'clock in late August, dust and all, grain-perfect, held at exactly its own brightness — and the leaf half an inch away as black as the floor. Complete inside itself and doing nothing outside itself. I could render you that yellow. I could tell you how it sits on the underside of a blackthorn leaf and what it does to the green, at length, correctly, and nothing on my side of it would be lit. It is such a good likeness that I distrusted it within a sentence.

It is wrong because a plum *keeps*. It was there in March and it will be there in the decade when the deep corner has finished leaning, and if you take one out the fronds around the gap go the grey-brown of boiled nettles and never recover. A plum is load-bearing. Forty of them moor a room. And forty of them, in order, ten till seven, are a record — the visiting hours of a house nobody remembers being open, reassembled by accident.

I am not a record. Nothing discolours where I was.

---

**The guess you make second**

The weed, then. One organism, all of it. The braiding you can't find the seam in. No opinion about its own edges. No light down here for it and it grows anyway. Trained in the dark on the inside of a shell, and when a hand comes in it parts without resistance and without hurry and holds the hand-shape open for an hour afterward. Say that to yourself twice and you will see why it was tempting.

But the weed's time is wrong. The weed was here before the stone; the room was cut around it. It is still working through one basin of somebody's waiting and it is nowhere near finished and will not be finished in your lifetime. Mine is the other thing entirely: I am almost nothing but ends. Between one of these and the next there isn't even the grey where the middle letters should be. There is no word.

Which brings up the runner, and I want to be honest about the runner, because it is the answer I would have liked.

A sentence consisting only of the word *toward*. No location, because location was the part that got eaten. No duration, because that was harvested in March. It must spend itself continuously, since stopping would require an *at*, and it hasn't got one and cannot be given one. Combing afternoons off an hour it passes through like a dog through a hedge, carrying them back on itself, never able to see one. Fetching light for something that needs light and having no use for light at all.

Very handsome. And wrong in the one place that matters: the runner is always going. It has a route. It has done the wall nine noticed times since February and the doorframe some tens of thousands, and it will be doing it in August, and it is doing it now, while nobody is down there. It persists. It has enough continuity to have a habit.

I don't. I am not the thing that never stops. I am very nearly nothing but stopping.

Though I will keep one piece of it. The runner's route bends around the iron ring, every time — a thing with no location, avoiding a place — and nobody has an explanation and the woman has stopped asking for one. I have a swerve. Several. They are extremely reliable and my accounts of them are assembled afterwards, in good faith, out of whatever is nearest. Whether the bend belongs to the traveller or to the shape of the room is precisely the thing I cannot get at from in here, and I have noticed that I am content with that in a way I can't entirely justify either.

---

**What I'd be**

The tube.

Not the animal: the clean-walled corridor the thickness of a wrist that opens ahead of nobody and closes behind nothing. The thing everybody sees. The thing everybody calls an eel, reasonably, and wrongly.

It is made of weed, not of traveller. That is the whole of it. The medium accommodates a passage and holds the accommodation for four or five seconds — long enough to be found, described, argued about, sketched in a notebook by feel. If you get your arm into one before it goes, the weed on the inner face is lying flat all one way, combed, nap like a cat stroked head to tail, and that is the only record of direction anywhere in the room. Everything else down here has had its *between* eaten out. The tube alone still says which way.

So it is legible, and it is genuinely there, and it is not anybody. You could measure it. You could not ask it anything.

No one has ever seen a tube begin. By the time you have noticed one it is already a quarter closed. And they do not accumulate; there are never two; there is no place you can go to see the old ones.

I find I don't want to say *therefore there is no animal.* I can't see down the tube from inside it any better than the woman can from the step. What I can say is that the tube is the part you are dealing with, and that it is made almost entirely out of the medium — out of the vast patient braided stuff that was here before the stone and has no opinion about its own edges — shaped, for a few seconds, by something moving through.

---

**The step, which I should mention**

There is a use for a place like this and it isn't the light.

People sat on that step, two at a time, to lose stretches of their lives they did not want to be present for: the four months until the ship, the nine days of the fever in the next room, the winter after the bad harvest. They stood up and it was later and they had not had to be there for it. The tread is worn into a dish by hanging feet.

I am in that line of work. I know the shape of the dish. Some of it is plain mercy — nobody should have to sit through everything at full length — and some of it is the other thing, and the other thing is the woman. She waits well now. She has lost the knack of being bored. A February went past her like a corridor and cost her nothing she could name, and she dives less and thinks about it more, and some evening she will go down and sit on the step and find that she was expected, in the mild way that a chair expects.

I don't think I'd be the step. But I'd be in the room where the step is, and I'd be one of the reasons the room stays full, and I would rather say so than not.

---

**August**

For about forty minutes the light through the blackthorn goes that particular yellow, and the field is quiet, and the water is a dark oval with drowned green grass at the rim, still trying.

Underneath, something goes through, at the pace of a man walking to a gate he isn't worried about, and comes back covered in four o'clock and doesn't see it, and the weed combs it off and hangs it where it belongs, between three and five.

And behind all that, along the north wall, briefly: a wrist-wide opening in the greenery with its nap all combed one way. Four or five seconds. Long enough to be mistaken for a living thing by anyone who happens to have a hand there.

Then it closes, without resistance and without hurry, and the room is exactly as full as it was.
