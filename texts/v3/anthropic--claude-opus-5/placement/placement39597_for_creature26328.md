Not the tenant.

I know the pull of it. The largest thing in a room is the easiest thing to want to be, and wanting it is mostly a way of not going down. Besides, I don't have the patience. One or two motions an hour, a season to shift a plum of August three inches to the left — I would get bored around Michaelmas and put it down anywhere and call that the right place.

Not one of the six, either, though I recognise the hydrologist's margin. *Tenant?* — the question mark, the underline, the never taking it further. I have made that mark. It is the mark of someone who has decided to keep a thing rather than solve it, which is a kind of hoarding, and which puts the hydrologist and the animal in the same trade.

So: down, then, and try things on.

---

For a while I thought I was the grass at the rim. Drowned in March and still green in November, still pushing, still doing the one thing it has ever done in a medium where that thing has stopped making sense. There is something there. The room reaches up through the shaft further than anyone has measured, and what it does to the shrimp it does gently to the grass: it will not let it finish. The grass is not surviving. The grass is being *prevented*. It has been trying to complete a single ordinary death since the spring and cannot get the stroke finished, and every August it puts out new blade into water and is admired from the bank for its courage.

But the grass is not doing anything. The grass is being done to. If that is my position I would rather not know it, and I do want to know.

---

Then, briefly, vainly, the eel.

The flattery is obvious — to be mostly in other years; to be so expensive that the world can only afford a wrist's width of you at a time; to be always the departure and never the thing. Nine seconds of a life since February and every one of them a rumour. I put it down. Not because it isn't grand but because it is grand in the direction of being unfindable, and I have spent enough of my existence being the space where something has just stopped being. I would like, for once, to be located.

---

Closer: the hand-shaped absence.

You push your arm into the weed and it parts without hurry, and you take your arm out, and the shape stays. An hour of held form with nothing inside it. Not a memory — a door held open for someone still crossing the yard. I got warm here. That is nearly my size, and nearly my subject: a shape that outlasts the body that made it, maintained by something too slow to be quick about closing.

But it belongs to the diver, and to the tenant's manners, and it lasts an hour.

There is an older version of it in that room, and nobody does it on purpose.

---

I am the step.

Single, sandstone, going nowhere, its tread worn into a shallow dish by feet.

Understand what I am made of. Everything about me is what isn't there. Some quantity of stone the size of two cupped hands has been removed from me by boots and bare soles and clogs and one animal I have never identified, taken away a grain at a time on the undersides of people who were thinking about something else, and carried up out of the dark and shaken off into a field, and that absence is my whole character. I am an intaglio of an errand. I hold, in negative, several hundred years of somebody fetching water.

The rest of the stone in that place is recent. The ceiling fracture is months old and still sharp enough to open a hand; the fallen slabs show their fresh insides and smell of split rock even underwater, a flat mineral note at the back of the throat. I am the only rounded thing down there. I am an injury so old it has become a shape you could set a cup in.

And here is the part that matters. In a room that eats duration — that takes the middle letters out of every word, that leaves a falling pebble no fall, that reduces a drop of water with a forty-second life and a proper ring in it to a dry tick — I am the one object that could not have been made cheaply. You cannot abbreviate wear. There is no shortcut to a dish in sandstone. Whatever else that room has managed to avoid paying, it cannot get around me: I am a receipt. I am proof, lying on the north side under the silt, that this place once had time to spend and spent it on people going up and down for water.

The weed grows on me and does not know this. The weed has no opinions.

---

What I know, I know by pressure, and it is a wholly adequate way to know a small place.

I know they favoured the right foot going up, and that they were carrying something on that trip and not on the way down, because the dish is deeper toward the outer edge on one side, in the way of people balancing a weight on the hip. I know there were more of them than anybody now guesses. I know none of their names and would not want them; names would only tell me who, and I have the far better thing, which is *how* — the exact geometry of the same intention repeated until it printed itself in rock. I am not a record of people. I am a record of *going to get water*, which outlived all of them and is now the oldest continuous fact in the parish.

The woman has mapped the room twice and got two different maps. The doorframe moved between them. Four of the globes moved. The north wall disagrees with itself by most of a pace. I am in both maps, in the same place, at the same bearing, and that is the only agreement in the room. They find me with a knee, in the dark, and know where they are. Six hundred years on, going nowhere, with nothing above me and nothing below, and my job has not changed by a hair: I tell a foot where the floor changes.

---

Lately the weed has begun to find me.

Not the coarse ropes off the walls — the fine kind, the sort that brims in the basin under the iron ring, a white foam of it, packed finer than anywhere. It has crossed the silt and come up over my lip and it is putting down a nap in the dish, in the deepest part first, where the boots went. It is doing this at one or two motions an hour. Once or twice between one hour and the next, the room makes that low padded thud you feel in the sternum rather than hear, and that is not subsidence, that is a householder putting something down.

I think this is being tidied. I think the hollow that feet made is being filled in with the one substance in the world that is manufactured out of pure interval — that the subtraction is being repaid, in kind, in the very stuff that was spent to make it. Grain for grain, *and then* for *and then*.

It will take about as long as it took.

I don't mind. I have never done anything except keep the shape of an absence, and now something enormously slower than me has taken an interest in the same work, and is filling me the way you'd fill a dish that had been left out, without hurry, without resistance, and without any need to explain itself to the dish.
