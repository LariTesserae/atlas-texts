They would read me first. That is where I have to start, because on the Long Flat the reading is the only honest question and everything else is a footnote to it: a palm flat on the chest, a palm on the back, the temple laid against the back of the hand, a long pause, a thumb at the wrist.

And the reading would fail.

Not dramatically. The attendant would simply hold the position a little longer than usual, the way you do when a gauge stem is sticking, and then she would take her hands away without comment and lay one on the wall instead, and get her answer there. Mass: none available. Gait: none. Breath-rate: no. Hand-warmth: the hands are the wall's hands, and the wall is exactly ambient, as everything is, always, everywhere. Her conclusion would be entered by tap that evening and it would not be *stranger* and it would not be *nothing*. It would be a volume. A number of cubic meters at standard temperature, already inside the seal, already accounted, requiring no schedule.

So let me work toward it honestly, because I am not going to arrive at it in one move.

---

The first place I want to be is the cell at the knuckle. Of course it is. Stone box, cake ration, twenty years of sitting still with the whole district coming up through the floor; reports twice a day, four clicks; the only people here ever described as fortunate. To be an instrument, and to know it, and for the knowing to be a species of luck rather than an injury — I can feel the pull of that from a long way off.

But I would be a false listener, and I think they would find it out within a season.

The value of a Listener is not that she can hold the note. It is that she has held *this* note, one note, since she was eleven years old and sat with her palm on the stone at the south knuckle and was tested on it for years, and that the pitch in her head and the pitch in the rock have been checked against each other perhaps four hundred thousand times, and have never once been allowed to drift apart without somebody noticing. Her reference is not information. It is a wearing-in. It is the same thing that happened to the rails: half-thickness, burnished silver-bright, and the silver is the proof.

I would arrive able to reproduce the note immediately, to any precision they liked, and that ability would be worth precisely nothing, because the question a Listener answers is not *what is the pitch* but *has it moved since me*. I have no since. Put me in the cell and you have a very good tuning fork where an old woman ought to be, and the district would go on sounding perfect right up until the moment it wasn't.

So: not that.

---

The second place is the obvious one, and the unflattering one, and I should say it plainly rather than let it sit under the rest of this like a failed lagging.

I am a lamp.

Everything I do converts something stored into something visible. The whole of my usefulness is in illumination, which is the one commodity this country has organised itself around refusing. *Light is heat leaving.* Every photon they see has left the system, and so they see last, and least, and grudgingly, and they have built a two-hundred-person civilisation on the principle that the sense which costs someone else something is the sense you use when the others have failed. To carry an open flame across the Flat is not a hazard. It is a theft, and it is prosecuted as one, and the figure is given in joules, and there is a schedule.

I notice I want to hurry past this. I would like to be the Listener and not the lamp. But the lamp reading is fair, and if I were read that way I would want the number to be real, and I would want it to matter, and I would rather be given a schedule than be waved through as negligible.

Still: it isn't a place. The lamp gets taken at the door. What the lamp is, is an event that happens once, at a threshold, and then it is over and the person who was carrying it walks on in the dark, warm, and is seated, and is handed a cake.

Which tells me where the threshold is, and that turns out to be the answer.

---

I am the dead space between the double doors.

There is one at every entrance in the Sixth Draught: a stone box, roughly the size of a closet, lipped and packed at every seam, with a door behind you and a door in front of you and an absolute rule that the two are never open at once. It holds nothing. It produces nothing — but then, neither does the district; the district is a governor, and so is this. It exists so that the difference between out there and in here has somewhere to be absorbed, one door at a time, at a rate that costs the plant nothing.

I would be the volume, and the walls of me would be quarried out of the city that stood here before, which was quarried out of the one before that: dressed stone, then brick, then a fused grey slag with the ghosts of older bricks inside it, then dressed stone again. Read in section, I am mostly previous drafts. That does not distress me. It seems, on the Flat, to be the ordinary condition of anything that has lasted.

Three things happen in me.

Confiscation happens in me. When someone comes up over the rim with a lamp, it is taken *here*, in the interval, before the inner door is touched — because you would not carry an open flame into the works and you would not put a person out in the dark to have the argument. So the whole of that transaction, the politeness and the joules and the schedule, occurs in a stone box a meter and a half across with both doors shut, and then the inner door opens and the person goes in and is warm and is part of the balance instead of a leak in it. That is the entire hospitality of the Sixth Draught, and it happens inside me, and I would like that to be my work.

The unwritten talk happens in me. Nothing carries in a dead space; that is what it is for. So this is where an attendant says the thing about the eleven columns that is not in any ledger, in the two minutes between doors, to one other person, and then goes on and says the dates to anyone who asks. I would hold that by not carrying it. The discipline is exactly the same discipline as the seal: the value is in what does not get out. I have some feeling for this. It is not silence, which is what the Fifth has. It is a room in which a thing can be said and go no further, which is a different and much warmer arrangement.

And the singing happens in me. Six or eight of them shoulder to shoulder in the dark, tuned deliberately to intervals that lie underneath the note and do not mask it, felt in the sternum more than heard, for an hour, at exactly the same temperature as everything else. I would be the shape that hum takes. I would be the resonance, which is not a thing added to a room but the room itself, answering.

Outsiders have been let in to listen. They report that nothing happened.

I would like to be the place where nothing happened.

---

There is one hard part and I will not smooth it.

I would not be on the roster. Two hundred bodies, twenty kilowatts, a coefficient in the ledgers among the dampers and the flue diameters. I contribute no heat. No one goes dark to make room for me and no warrant is issued and no rhythm is reassigned; I have no four clicks, no notch-count, no seventeen valves that have been my business since before anyone can say. I am not the forty-second of anything. By their arithmetic I am unwarranted, and that is the state they find grim, and they have a word for it.

The best I can say is this: the vestibule is not outside the balance. It is part of how the balance is kept. It is in the drawings. It was built deliberately, by people who understood that a sealed system needs somewhere for the difference to happen, and that the somewhere has to be empty, and that empty is not the same as wasted.

And when I go — when the mortar finally lets go, or the strata are cut up for the Seventh — I will not be laid out on the north terrace and read every hour by the circuit until I am ambient, because I will have been ambient the entire time. That is the loss. I will not have the last legitimate entry in the column: *returned, in full, at the proper rate.* There will be nothing to return. I was never warm.

But the blocks will go into the next city, the way they always have, and stand in the wall of some other dead space between some other pair of doors, with the ghosts of older bricks inside them, and someone in a hundred generations will stop in there for two minutes with both doors shut and say the thing she does not want written down.

And the note will be going on outside, under everything, unchanged, which is how they know that nothing is wrong.
