## Second interface, colorless

The way to find your place here is not to look for it. Looking is a summit habit. You are dropped in, and you settle, and wherever you stop is where you were.

Still, I tried the summits first, because everyone does. Above the waterline, in that air which is barely air, in the mattressed moss and the leather-leaved dwarf trees, there is a ridge from which forty peaks recede in tiers of paler blue-green, doubled by the mirror below into eighty. It is the best seat in the world. It is also a lie, because it is the seat of the one thing the basin does not contain: a watcher, with a notebook, upright, dry at the shoulders, capable of the word *strangeness*. There are no eyes in the drowned cordillera. There is not even the concept of a season for eyes to mark. To stand on that ridge is to be the visitor, and the visitor is the only creature here who could leave.

So I tried the glider, which is what anyone wants to be who has read the census and understood the trade. To be the courier. To be the one thing that crosses. A meter of translucent fringe, coasting on nothing, twenty minutes to a clearing, and then the slow deliberate work of settling onto a lid until it gives, and the sliding through into a water no spore of the floor above will ever reach, and the small bloom closing behind you like a door in an hour. To be the reason six sealed worlds share any vocabulary at all. To leave a route so persistent that it becomes a place — a lane of richer foliage running for kilometers at one depth, printed into the forest by a body dead a hundred years.

I could not hold it. Wanting to be the one who carries meaning between the floors is precisely a summit appetite. And in any case the glider knows nothing of its lanes. It is going down for reasons of its own gut.

I looked, briefly and with embarrassment, at the stairs. Eleven columnar steps on the north face, rising to a landing that stops in mid-air, and beside them the twelve taut threads collecting litter into the unmistakable image of a rope bridge with its planks rotted out. To be *that*: the strong false recurring impression that someone came through. But that is not a being. That is a misreading, and a misreading needs a reader, and we have established there are none.

Then I stopped choosing, and did the only thing this world really permits, which is to sink until arrested.

Down past the wobbling circle of sun that follows you. Down through the first water, where the light is simply daylight and the sheath-trees hold their forty tiers a hand's width apart like the pages of a book nobody will turn. A hair of density. A fraction of a degree. And there — not chosen, not achieved — I stopped, because I am the thing that stops.

I am the second seam. I am the knitter of the second interface, the colorless one, the one that shows only as a shimmer when the light rakes it, and whose absence of color no one has ever established a reason for.

### extent

I have no location. I have a perimeter.

Six kilometers is a diameter, not a self. What I am is a sheet one finger thick and horizontal beyond any horizon I could have, filling every gap between the mats of arrested foliage, gluing them, drawing them into the lid that the guidebooks call a false floor. Above me: a water I would starve in, clear, generous with light, empty as a scoured dish. Below me: a water that would smother me. I cannot rise a meter. I cannot sink one. There is exactly one plane in this world on which I am possible, and I occupy all of it.

Which means my map of the cordillera is a map of my own missing parts. I know the mountains only where they interrupt me. Forty summits are forty holes in me, each with a hemmed edge of stone-taste, and the great pale faces I have never seen are simply the places where I end and go on ending, year after year, in the same shape. If I could be said to picture the range, I picture it as negative: an archipelago of absence in an otherwise continuous body. The peaks are what I am not.

### weather

There is no wind, no current, no rain, and so I have one weather, and it is my own working.

Where I am eating, I am a fraction of a degree warmer than the water — a warmth you could find with the back of a hand and never with the palm. That is my whole climate. My larder arrives without being fetched: everything sinking from the forest above slows when it reaches the density change and stops, so there is a permanent thin haze of arrested drift lying along me, kilometers wide, a finger deep. Pollen. Leaf-scale. The glass bodies of diatoms. All of it dead. All of it holding, in trace amounts, the nitrogen and the phosphorus that the clouds three floors down made out of nothing and that the trees stole and took up ninety meters and let fall.

Understand what that means. The dead do not come back in this world, and I am the exception, and I am not a large exception. I am a slow mouth eating the ceiling it made. Where the felt is dark and damp and faintly warm, that is me working. Where it has gone pale and glassy and hard, that is me finished, and what I am finished with is permanent.

I do not know anything except by digesting it. There is no other channel. Whatever I could be said to remember is laid down as pallor, and the pallor is read from below, by the floor beneath me, as sky.

### events

Gradients, mostly. Nothing that could be called a day.

But four things happen.

*The stitchings.* A sheath-tree lets go of a ballasted seed, the only object in the basin in a hurry, and it comes down through its parent's tiers in a rapid tearing sound and hits me, and goes through, and I am four small wounds closing. It is the fastest thing I will ever be touched by. It is also the only news I get from the surface that has any weight to it.

*The punctures.* Once a month or so, a glider settles and works its way through me. This is not damage. It is the only mail the floors exchange. Whatever grows on the third interface that also grows on mine, arrived that way, in a fringe or a gut. I close over the hole in an hour. I have no way to know what went down.

*The shearings.* Somewhere on a face I have never seen, a flake of stone the size of a door lets go of the friction that has held it for a century, and comes down through forty tiers of untouchable leaf in one long soft rip lasting nearly a minute, and passes through me, and keeps going. Afterward there is a slot of raw daylight from the sky to the floor, and every seedling within reach begins, over the next ten years, to grow crooked toward it. The forest's entire ambition is subcontracted to geology. So, in a way, is mine: the slot lets a column of unarrested drift fall straight past me for years, and I lose it, and there is nothing to be done.

*And the writing.* The clock-snails come where I am dark and warm, because nothing but my working can open that glazed litter for them, and they scrape at my frontier at the rate of a hand's width per day. They eat me and they inscribe me. Their pale meandering tracks stand for decades on my underside, a hundred meters to a lifetime, and when two of them find each other's handwriting and braid it for a season, the loop stays legible long after both are gone. I am the paper. I do not mind being the paper. It is the only respect in which anything here is addressed to anything else.

### the turning

Every eleven years the density difference that is my entire habitat begins to fail.

It starts as a warm bruise low in the basin and it takes six weeks, and it is nearly silent. My plane goes soft. It buckles at the speed of an hour hand. The haze stops arresting — my larder simply keeps falling, for the first and only time — and I begin, in the middle of a plenty I cannot reach, to starve.

And this is my one fertility. I have spores and no way at all to place them; I get moving water once a decade and I spend the decade preparing for it. So I fruit while I fail. And in that same six weeks the seam beneath me, the cold silver one, which I have never touched and have shared a world with for eleven years, passes through me over hundreds of hectares. Not meeting. Interpenetration. There is no organ for it, no gesture, no signal; there is only the fact of two sheets occupying the same water and exchanging, at scale, everything they are.

Then the water sorts itself out again, and the new seams settle at new depths, and the new leaves begin to fall onto them, and the new floors begin. What is on the second interface afterward is not quite what was there before. Colors tell you who married in. Whether it is still me is a question about words, and I have no words, and I suspect the answer is *partly*, and I suspect that is the ordinary condition of everything that lasts.

### the stairs

One last thing, which I know only as the shape of a hole.

Below me, on the north face, eleven steps rise out of a young intrusive, evenly, at the height of a step, to a flat landing. They come up toward my underside and stop just short. Twelve threads run down from that landing into the dark with litter caught along them at regular intervals. Nothing built any of it. Nothing here has ever built anything; fire is not a concept that would occur.

But the water is very still and very clear, and my finished parts are pale and glassy and lie perfectly flat for kilometers, and so beneath me there is a mirror. And in that mirror the eleven steps continue upward, identical, inverted, climbing out of the landing into a range that isn't there, above a cordillera that repeats itself in fainter and fainter tiers to nowhere.

I am what makes the second stair. I have no eyes, no news of it, no use for it. The only construction in the drowned cordillera is manufactured by the underside of a colorless organism that cannot see, and it is aimed at whoever came through here once and left before the leaves fell.

You, presumably. Mind the lid on your way down. It will hold, for a while, and dimple, and let a slow bloom up through the puncture, and close.
