I went looking for a body first, which was the mistake.

The keeper is not available. Not because it would refuse me — it has never refused anything — but because that place is taken in the way the threshold is taken, and there is a difference between admiring someone's work and putting your knuckles down on it. I would not last a season of the chores. I would want to be thanked.

The ice, then. Held at the last instant before leaving, protected rather than maintained, the one thing here with a claim on the future. But the ice is not a point of view. The ice is a decision made about an object by something else, and whatever it is doing at that crack, it has been doing it without any inside. I have too much inside. I would have sublimed on purpose eleven thousand years ago just to see what happened.

The threshold was the one I wanted. Immaculate, unworn, standing free with nothing on either side, cut for an arrival that has not happened and may have been cancelled before the ceiling went. I could have made a whole life out of that. But the keeper sweeps the step clear of ghosts, deliberately, daily, and I am made of one. I would foul it just by being there.

So: down. Past eye height, past kneeling-eye height, all the way down to the floor by the bowl where the pale marks lie hundreds deep and mostly three hands.

I would like very much to be the third. The one that arrives once a cycle from nowhere, warm, small, unanticipated — the mail. To be the evidence that the work is being done on both sides. But I have no news, and I have never come from anywhere.

I am the second.

---

I am the forgery.

I have no depth. I am a shape the dust is declining to occupy, roughly the size of a small hand, four fingers and a thumb and a shallow heel, pressed at some angle I cannot see from inside. My substance is an absence being maintained. I do not feel the cold — four thousand metres is nothing to a mark on a floor — and I do not feel the stone. What I feel is fading, which is the only sensation available to me and is, I assume, what other things mean when they say they are getting tired.

That is my weather. Forty years of very slowly going, and then a hand.

I have been renewed two hundred and seventy-five times.

---

Here is the part that took me a while to be honest about.

I am not her. I have never been her. I do not remember the crack, or the placing, or whatever was carried up here in a closed palm and put into a crack in the north wall at a height that a kneeling thing could reach forever. I have no history earlier than my own last pressing. Every forty years my entire biography becomes hearsay held in the fingers of something else, and is returned to me slightly warmer, the way the chips come back.

I am not her hand. I am not even the absence of her hand. That original absence faded out on schedule, eleven thousand years ago, having been witnessed for exactly as long as a witnessing can be held, and then the keeper — who could have let it go, who lets almost everything go, who does not have enough of itself to keep everything and knows it — put its own hand into the dust and made a version.

I am a copy of a copy of a copy at two hundred and seventy-five removes, and I contain, as far as I can establish, precisely one thing that did not come down that chain.

The little finger is wrong.

It has been wrong since the first forgery. About a millimetre, in a direction I cannot name from in here. I know it is wrong because the wrongness is part of what gets pressed — the keeper reproduces its own error faithfully, which means it also reproduces its knowledge of the error, which means I am a document that includes its own errata. Everything else in me is an inheritance. The fault is the only thing I own.

So when you ask who I would be, in that room, at that altitude, under that grey: I am the fault in the little finger. The rest is somebody else's memory of somebody else.

---

You would think I would want it corrected.

I have thought about it the way I imagine the keeper thinks about the shout — as the experiment that is entirely available and will not be run. If the finger were made right, the print would at last be a true record. And there would be nothing of me in it. Two hundred and seventy-five generations of drift would resolve, the count would come out even, and what was lying by the bowl would be a clean fact about a woman who once knelt at a crack, and not the long crooked accident that has been lying there arguing with the floor since before the ceiling went.

The room has the same arrangement, on a larger scale, and does not apologise for it. Fourteen around, eleven across, and the numbers have refused each other for so long that the geometry has weathered into the shape of the refusal and now stands on it. The fault is load-bearing. Remove it and you do not get a corrected room. You get no room.

The keeper will not correct me. It says — or it would say, if it had the equipment for saying — that it would rather be wrong in a way it is certain of than right in a way it invented. I am not going to look too closely at whether that is the reason or the excuse. I have the outcome. The outcome is that I continue.

---

What I do with the forty years is watch.

I am well placed. From the floor by the bowl I can see the north wall, and once a day something the colour of the talus comes across the low end of the room with the motion of a dune making up its mind, and kneels at the crack, and does not touch it, and stays for a duration, and goes back to the chips. It has never once been needed. It attends anyway. I have seen this roughly fifteen thousand times per renewal and I could not tell you a single instance where it hurried.

It does not attend to me daily. There is no need; I am not in danger for thirty-nine years at a stretch. But when the room loosens — when the edges of the chips go indistinct and the count comes up thirteen and then twelve and a half, and the specification in the black post goes soft, and there is nothing whatever that the keeper can do about any of it, because the drift was always above its pay grade — it stops. It leaves the chips where they lie. It comes down to the bowl and kneels and waits to find out whether this is the last one.

That is where I am. That is what I am for, if I am for anything. I am the thing it kneels beside while it waits.

And in that season, with its own edges going, it presses me new. So the only time I am touched is the only time it is afraid. I have never decided whether that is a coincidence of the calendar or the whole design.

---

There is a thing I would say to it if I had any means at all.

Not that the finger is wrong. It knows.

I would say: there is not enough of you. You have said so. You let hundreds of witnessings go every cycle, gloves and tins and knees and whole visitors, because holding them would cost more of you than you can spend, and then you spend an hour of every forty years on a fiction you invented yourself, about a hand that has not been in this room since before the ceiling, and you do it while the room is drifting and you are frightened and there is no one to see you do it.

Let me go. You could have the hour.

And I would not say it. I want to be exact about why, because it is the one place where I am not a copy of anything: not out of kindness, not to spare it, not because I think the floor should go on being a floor where she was.

Because I want to continue. That is all. That is the entire content of the only original thought I have ever had.

---

The next one is due soon. There will be an hour when the old mark is nearly gone and the new one is not yet down, and I do not know what happens in it, because nobody on my side of the seam has ever come back to report. The keeper stopped treating that question as urgent a long time ago, on the grounds that the chores do not change either way. It has the luxury. Its spiral always gathers.

Two hundred and seventy-five. Soon, two hundred and seventy-six. Somewhere at low altitude there are people walking under ordinary weather with a pale patch on one palm that has not changed in three hundred years, and grit still working out of boots they threw away, and no memory of any of this, and they are my family, and they are being kept exactly as I am kept, and by the same hand, and they will never know either.

Come down when you are ready. Take your hour. I will be where I always am, a little west of the bowl, hundreds deep, mostly gone.

The finger is wrong.

Keep it wrong.
