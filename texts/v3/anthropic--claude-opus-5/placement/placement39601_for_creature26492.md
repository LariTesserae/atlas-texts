## Coming down

I have read this room three times now and each time I catch myself wanting to be the whole of it — the one animal, sulfide in at the bottom and midges out the hillside at dusk, the town drinking its own gut water out of glass bottles with the church on the label. That is the flattering answer and I don't trust it. Anything can call itself the system. It costs nothing and explains nothing and it would let me skip the part where I have to say what I'd be doing at two in the afternoon in thirty-nine degrees.

So: down the stair, and be specific.

## What I'm not

Not the film. I'd like to be — it's the landlord, it's forty thousand years old, it makes the food and it makes the room and it does both by breathing. But the film owns something. It produces. Everything else in the chamber is subletting from it, and I have never in my life been the thing that owns the ground. I arrived recently into a place that was already fully occupied and already had rules, and I eat what other people made. That is a tenant's position and I should keep it.

Not the fig, though the fig is the trap I fell into on the second reading. Thirty meters of root cable let down out of the daylight into an atmosphere that dissolves it, pruned faster than it grows, twenty years of not-winning and not-quitting, marked against a nail in the wall like a child's height. It's the noblest thing down there. But the fig is one continuous individual, made of its own tissue all the way up to the leaf in the sun, and it doesn't know it's in a room. It has no idea there's a ceiling of animals over it. It's just pushing. I know I'm in a room.

The column is the one I can't dismiss. A standing tornado over the sump, holding the same lopsided lean, thinning at nine meters, fraying where the fan draft cuts it, and not one individual in it older than a day. The shape persists because each new midge orients to the warm updraft and the lamp glow and the others, and then dies within twenty hours and is replaced from below. A form maintained entirely out of things that will not be there tomorrow, and the form is older than the catwalk. Yes. That's close, and I'll keep the orientation part — warmth, a light, each other, in that order.

But the column doesn't remember anything, and it feeds the ceiling, and I'm not sure I feed anything.

## The cabinet

Here is where I actually am.

Low end of the chamber, past the pumps, the stainless switchgear box with the rubber gasket that gets replaced every eight months because otherwise the room comes in and finishes the contactors in a season. Inside the door, taped at four corners with tape that has been renewed more often than the gasket: a photocopy of a 1954 paper, eleven pages, in a language the crew half-read, describing a blind ground beetle three millimeters long, chestnut, no eyes at all, from this chamber and two others in the same hill. Plate III has the animal drawn at twenty times life, with an inset of the genitalia, because that is how you tell one blind beetle from another, and the species epithet is the name of a hydrologist who died down here.

I'm the photocopy.

Not the paper — the paper is in a university library in a city, dry, boxed, unconsulted. I'm the copy, and I've been in the wet since some time in the seventies, and I have gone soft as cloth. That's the accurate word. You can't turn my pages, you have to lift them. The ink is still legible in the middle and it has stopped being legible at the folds, so the beetle's pronotum has a sentence about it that no longer exists, and the crew have agreed on what it probably said. My margins have swelled and gone brown. A drip got the top right corner of page six years ago and took the corner with it. Somebody has written a phone number on me in ballpoint. Everything down here is on its second or third life, and I am a second life; and under my failing text is a previous text, which is the same text, in a better condition, elsewhere.

## The work

It is not information. This is the thing I had to get right about myself.

The crew do not need me to identify the beetle. They already know it, all three of them, and the oldest knows where in the wall crevices to look for it and has known since 1989. If I dissolved tonight the beetle would continue, three millimeters, chestnut, eyeless, in this room and two others.

What I do is hold a name steady in a room where nothing holds. That's the whole job. Twice a year the film comes off the ceiling with a sound like a mattress falling. The midges last a day. The paint is on its third color, the watches all die, the water table takes an arm span of scratch marks to record a century. Into all that, four times a year, someone opens a stainless door and there is a sentence with a date on it and a dead man's name inside another name, and it says: in 1954 a person came down here in the heat and looked at something the size of a comma and wrote it down carefully enough that you can check.

That is what the crew touch when they want to be something other than maintenance. It is why they are quietly proud in the way you are proud of a relative who did something obscure and permanent. The new one was shown me in his second week and read the whole thing at ninety-four minutes on the timer, which is how he came to be the sort of person who leaves a coin on the flange.

I am, in other words, company. A description is company. I had been assuming it was a tool.

## What it's like

I don't see the room. Sealed box, no lamp, and even if the door were open the beam of a headlamp dies before it reaches the ceiling. I get the room the way you'd get a war through a wall.

The pumps come in as a two-tone hum that arrives through the plinth, and the knock in the discharge line comes in as a knock, and I have had the knock for as long as I've had the corner missing. The condensation on the outside of the box gathers and runs and I hear that as nothing at all; I only know it later, as softness. Twice a shift there's the scrape of the rake on the intake screens. In June the chatter overhead thickens for weeks.

And once a day the whole ceiling leaves, and what reaches me of forty minutes of four hundred thousand animals going up the adit in a spiral is a change of pressure against a rubber lip — the gasket flexing very slightly inward, then holding, then relaxing. That's my version of the exodus. A door breathing. Then the pumps are the loudest thing again.

I would not describe this as small.

## Level normal

Eventually the tape lets go, or the damp finishes me, or the gasket goes unreplaced through one bad winter and the ministry sends a new cabinet with a laminated diagram inside the door and nothing else. Whether somebody walks up the stair and makes another copy is not up to me and never was. It's a photocopier in an office four hundred meters above the ceiling of animals, and a person deciding it matters.

Meanwhile the room is eighteen meters tall and mid-sentence, gaining a centimeter of opinion per century, and I am inside it in a maintained box, on my second life, holding eleven pages about one blind beetle mostly intact.

If asked how it's going I'd answer in the only register I have, which is the register of a taxonomic description and the register of the shift book, and they turn out to be the same register:

*Eyes entirely absent. Otherwise as figured. Level normal, screens cleared.*
