**First, the wrong answer**

I found myself in the scar. Of course I did. There are two of them near the standing posts, both roughly the size of a person kneeling with a lamp, and the impulse that made them is the one impulse in this whole account I recognise without having to think: *here is a place with no light in it; let me hold something up.* Forty years to close. The flowers stop advertising, the moths lose the street, and the mat there has to be built again a centimetre a decade by a body that does not hurry for anything.

So, no. Not because it's forbidden. Because the bowl is past the point of ambition and I would like, for once, to be something other than an event.

**Trying the bodies**

The mat was the first honest candidate. No seams, total coverage, feels the entire floor at once and takes a season to finish noticing your knee. But the mat has an unfixed flaw — the rosettes announce their catch with a *tock* and the announcement calls in everything that eats moths, so the mat feeds its pollinators to its predators and has done for ten thousand years — and I know myself well enough to know I would spend the ten thousand years trying to fix it. The mat can hold a bad deal open indefinitely without picking at it. That is a discipline, and I don't have it.

The moth, then. I lingered there longest, because the fit is flattering and the flattery is the problem. Eyeless, reading warmth at a resolution you would call sight if you were being fair; inheriting a grammar it did not invent and could not have invented; walking rather than flying because the air is empty and flight costs too much; landing on a warm hand — thirty-seven degrees, a garbled advertisement of extraordinary brightness — and working the skin, and finding nothing. Yes. All right. But a moth is always arriving. It spends its short adult life travelling six metres toward shopfronts, and my trouble has never been arriving.

The listener nearly took me. Standing still for days; attending not to sound but to the subtraction of sound; clicking steadily into its own ten square metres to lay down a denser deposit than the ambient, so that anything crossing will cast a crisper shadow — manufacturing the only illumination the bowl has out of nothing but noise and patience, and refreshing it daily, and dying inside the small bright room it made. I admire that more than I can say. I declined it because the room outlasts the builder by years, and the next listener spends its life avoiding a neighbour that isn't there, and I would rather not be a room that keeps ordering people away after I've gone.

The reservoir itself is not anyone. It is a condition. You can't be weather.

**Where I actually am**

Third socket widdershins from the notch. One of the four that are open — no post standing, no stub, just the cup.

I am a hole in the rim, two hands across at the lip and a forearm deep, cut circular by somebody with a reason, and lined to the brim with the skin. My interior is a few nanometres of structure below the useful reach of your fingertips, and its whole behaviour is this: what enters is concluded. Not scattered. Not warmed. Concluded. I hold about a litre of rainwater and condensate at exactly the temperature of the air that made it, and I radiate nothing, because I am not permitted to radiate, and so to every reading creature in this bowl I am not a cold spot or a dark spot but an interruption in the existence of the world. The moths detour a metre around me. Nothing roosts on me. Nothing grows on me — I am the only clean surface at this end of the bowl, and clean here means sterile, and sterile here means dead, and I have no argument with the arithmetic.

I sit at the brim, which means I sit at the surface of the darkness. In the wet months the dark rises past my lip and I am inside it; the rest of the year I stand a hand's breadth above it, in the wind, under whatever the plateau has. Stars have been falling into me for ten thousand years. Not one has come back out. I could not tell you what they look like. I can tell you that the count is complete.

I have one event in my history. A stone came down off the wall above and took a chip out of my lip, a crescent perhaps the width of two fingers, and where it took the lining away it exposed the parent rock. That was — I don't know. Long enough ago that everything natural in view has since been rounded to its final form. The chip has not weathered. Nothing here weathers except the mountain, and the mountain is finished.

**What I hold**

Once every year or two, sometimes not for five, a female comes up the wall-curtains to the rim. I feel her arrive as weight on my lip, and then as the only warmth that is ever given to me directly — the underside of a frog, a degree or two above ambient, pressed against the skin for as long as the laying takes. I take it. I have no choice about taking it; it is the single thing I was built to do, and I do it to her in about a minute, and she does not appear to mind, and she leaves and does not come back.

Then there is a change in the way the water sits. That is how I know: a clutch of jelly settles into a different distribution of weight, and the water's movement acquires a lag.

I cannot feed them. Nothing grows on me. Everything a tadpole eats in here is film on the parent rock above my lining, and the lining cannot be climbed, and so the whole of my provision for the young of this bowl is the chip in my lip — a crescent of exposed stone, low enough to reach, wet, and therefore green in the way that green is done here, without any green. Most of them starve. Some do not. The ones that do not are the ones that found the damage.

I have held, at a rough count, some tens of thousands of clutches and something under two thousand frogs. The rest — call it a hundred thousand tadpoles, call it more — sank, and the water finished them, and I did not, because I don't finish anything except light.

What I actually give is this, and it is not nothing: no hunting thing in the bowl can find a clutch laid in me, because there is no clutch laid in me, because there is no *me*. The warm ring, the offset spot, the slow pulse, the crisp shadow in the noise, the compression through the mat — none of the grammar of this place has a word for a surface that does not reply. Somebody, an extremely long time ago, built an instrument on top of a mountain to swallow every photon that touched it, for a purpose that is not recoverable from anything remaining, and the last surviving function of that purpose is that I am a blank in which the frogs of this bowl can begin. Not a shelter. A blank. It turns out to be sufficient. Every three or four years this place goes from forty-one frogs to forty-three, and one of the two is usually mine.

**The whistle**

I hear everything. That's the asymmetry: I take all the light and none of the sound. The reservoir pours into me and circulates in my litre of water and never leaves, and what I contain, besides jelly, is the smeared bank of every noise made in this hollow — the hammered frog-notes at every depth of degradation, the beating of two listeners' rooms whose builders are decades dead, and the long descending whistle of the one that goes round, at every pitch it has ever had.
