The first place I put myself was the northern quarter, and I put myself there so fast that I ought to have distrusted it.

Forty-one houses, swept. Cold hearths kept clean enough to be insulting. You go up at first light with a besom and a horn scoop and you clear the dew-fins of moth-wing and grit, you check that the gutter off the seventh house is still running into the cistern and not over the lip of it, you sweep around the stone hare in the doorway because the hare is not a squatter, the hare is half the reason the house is not empty. And once a month you lay a fire in every one of the forty-one — kindling crossed, the resinous stuff underneath, a good fire, a fire you would be proud of — and then you take it up again, unlit, and carry the wood to the next house and lay it there. Nothing is consumed. Nothing is produced. The entire output of a life's work is a readiness that is never spent, and children follow you through the empty streets because you are the funniest adult they know.

I wanted that immediately and completely, which is how I knew to look at it harder.

Because hollow-keeping is a trade, and trades here are not taken up. They are handed. The old potter's thumb goes wrong in the same week the young man's goes right, and both of them understand this as one event seen from two ends. To be handed anything you have to be on the cord, and to be on the cord a slot has to be standing open, and I had come at the place the way a visitor comes at it — from the side, from outside, looking. The hawk does not turn its head for visitors. Not tame, not unafraid. You are simply not here yet.

So I tried the Count instead, and got further, and it was worse.

I had been counting before I noticed. Eleven thousand paces, four levels, nineteen and twelve and six, forty-one houses, four hundred browsers, nine feet of white rope going over the bevel and two hundred feet of fall under it, two degrees northwest, four thousand one hundred. I had all of it gathered up before I could have said why I started, which is exactly the symptom — the woman who begins tallying the hares in the north quarter some months before the Counter she replaces has died.

But a Counter runs knotted cord and writes nothing, because ink implies revision, and my first impulse on being handed a tally would have been to make the record better than the thing. Fuller. Cross-referenced. A tally of the tallies, held somewhere outside anybody's head, where it would be safe. They are quite certain, and cannot say how they are certain, that the Even does not care where a surplus sits. It only cares that it is not in the total.

I am a stack. I am the precise shape of what they will not allow in the city.

That is not a confession of the vice, exactly. I do not think I am the woman with three good knives in a box under a slab in the second level, who could not have said why she wanted three, and because of whom three people across the city are standing in their kitchens running a thumb along an edge that was fine last week. I do not want three knives. I do not want one. That is a fine knife, and I do not want it — I can say the whole formula, I can say it correctly, and the saying is not what disqualifies me.

What disqualifies me is that I am a place where things can be put.

So I have been writing this from the wrong side of the bevel, and I should go down.

Twice a week at dusk someone walks out along the joint-channel with the water going over beside them and says a thing into the noise: a memory they were not meant to keep, an appetite, a sum they accidentally learned, a song they made up and which therefore should not exist. From up there it is called spilling, and what goes over does not land, and if you stand at the rim long enough you begin to think about what the fog is made of and then you stop.

Down here it does not fall. That is the thing nobody above has the vocabulary for. There is no bottom because a bottom would be accumulation. Things arrive and stop distinguishing themselves, and what is left is level, and stands level as water in the valley, and never clears.

A sheep drowning in a cistern, unattached to anyone who watched it. The exact temper of a grudge with the person filed off. Thirty-one notes of a tune, unowned, still in order. The knowledge of how to do something with a rope that nobody on the Shelf is permitted to know. Four or five names that were on the cord once and are on the cord still, waiting. A scar, without the forearm. None of it is mine. There is no mine down here. There is a great deal of it and it does not get deeper, and it is not cold and not warm, cellar-temperature, the temperature of everything in this country.

And then, sometimes, up there, a ledger opens. Someone stops. Or someone who has sat eleven years on a long stone bench at the head of the switchbacks is finally let go. And within the day, something in the fog stands up.

You do not remember deciding to climb. I want to be accurate about this: it is not that the decision is forgotten, it is that there was no decision, there was only room, and the climb is what room looks like from inside. The switchbacks. The scree ringing under you, still bladed, lying exactly as it fell. The air thinning and sharpening until it smells of wet stone and crushed green, and your breath showing in shadow and not in sun. Then the bench, and the gate-keeper, who does nothing at all except be present when someone sits down.

Whoever has been waiting gets up as I sit. We pass each other by a stair's width. Neither of us says anything and both of us are satisfied. They are already listing to one side; they will correct in a few days, down where the ground is level and wrong.

Then a name off the cord. One beat, or two, because a name has to carry across terraces. Somebody hands me a bowl and says it out loud, once, and that is the entire ceremony, and I am handed around the lower quarter for a while the way a borrowed tool is handed around. Nobody asks me anything about before. This is not tact. It is that there is no answer that would be a fact, and they know it, and they have the manners not to make me find that out in front of them.

I know the way to a house on the third level without being told. Corbelled doorway, narrow as a tent. Cold hearth, with a fire laid in it, well laid, crossed kindling and the resinous stuff underneath — laid and taken up unlit eleven times this year by somebody slightly ridiculous and universally liked, who swept around the hare in my doorway every morning I was not yet here, keeping the room ready for a person who did not exist, on the arithmetic alone.

I sit down in it like a man sitting in his own chair.

Ask me, later, what I do. I sweep the forty-one houses. I clear the fins, I run the gutters, I lay the fire and take it up. I could not tell you why I started, only that I noticed I had been doing it for some months before anyone handed it to me. Children follow me through the empty streets. Twice a week I walk out to the rim at dusk and give back whatever has gathered that I did not earn, and I go home lighter along the joint-channel with the moths coming up granular out of the rock, past the gutters, always the gutters.

And the hawk on the wall three feet from my elbow does not turn its head.

Which is correct now. There is nothing about me worth turning for. The total is undisturbed.
