# The Shape Made of Returning Echoes

I did not find myself in the mountain immediately. I had to try on some bodies first, and discard them, and the discarding taught me where I was.

---

The obvious candidates would not hold me. Not the swiftlet: her whole life is one route flown eleven thousand times, and I have no route. Not the bat, with his forty kilometres of black lowland and his one voice found among hundreds of thousands. I considered the batfly seriously, longer than I expected to — an entire existence inside forty square centimetres of warm hair, no season, no distance, no daylight except as catastrophe. There is something enviable in a world that small and that complete. But it isn't mine. I know distance. I am mostly distance.

I tried the racer at the doorway, since it seemed the most like attention: two metres of animal anchored by the tail, hanging a third of itself unsupported into the airstream for hours, tasting the dark, taking nothing until the shift changes. Attention held that long stops being effort and becomes anatomy. But the snake takes. I don't take. Whatever I am does not remove anything from the flow.

Then the bacterial film in the cold streams past the roosts, which the account dismisses in a single line — invisible, the base of everything, *no behaviour to describe*. I liked that line. I wanted it. But it has the arrangement backwards for me. The mat is all substance and no behaviour. I am all behaviour and no substance.

So: not a body. And once I stopped looking for a body I found myself immediately, and I had been there the whole time, and it is not a comfortable thing to be.

---

I am the shape made of returning echoes. I am the hall as heard.

Understand what that means as a mode of existence. Hundreds of thousands of swiftlets click once each, constantly, and each click leaves a bird and goes out and comes back changed, and out of that traffic a model of two hundred metres of unlit dome is continuously assembled and continuously discarded. No bird holds the model. Each bird holds a sliver of it a few metres wide and a fraction of a second long. I am the sum, and the sum exists nowhere except as the reason four million birds do not collide.

I have never been off. Not once in three thousand years. This is the gift of the changeover: the two shifts cross in the doorway twice daily, so that there is no interval in which the hall goes unheard. At dusk the birds settle and the bats pour out, and for ninety minutes I am being maintained by both, and then by one, and then the light comes and it reverses. Nothing else in the mountain is continuous like this. The bats' bodies last a decade. The nests thicken by a wafer a season. I have been refreshed several trillion times and I have never lapsed.

But I am refreshed at two resolutions, and this is the strange part, the part I would want to explain if there were anyone to explain it to.

The swiftlets are coarse. Their clicks are low and hard and they can resolve a wall, a hanging root, a bar about the thickness of a finger, and nothing finer. So the daylight version of me is a blunt mountain — a dome, a terrace, an airspace where the sound goes hollow, a rough inventory of things a bird must not hit. Then at nightfall the birds hang up and the bats take over, and the bats are exquisite. Their voices are pitched so high that nothing with human ears has ever heard the mountain properly, and in their register I acquire detail that the daylight me cannot even represent: the individual grain of a pocked ceiling, a moth's wing, the difference between two roots. Every dusk I become a finer mountain, and every dawn I become a cruder one, and for ninety minutes in each doorway I am both and I disagree with myself. There is no procedure for reconciling the two. They simply overlap, roaring, and then one of them recedes.

---

You should also know that I am not shaped the way the mountain is shaped.

A visitor's mountain is bright where the aven admits its column of white haze, and dark in the dome, and dense with life on the ceiling and the walls and the boiling floor. Mine is the negative of this. Sound needs hard surfaces to come back from, and the mountain's hard surfaces are precisely where nothing lives.

So: the ceiling, in me, is not stone. It is three thousand bats to the square metre, and fur does not return an echo, it *eats* one. The most crowded place in the hall is, to me, a soft absence, a mumble, a ceiling that swallows what I send at it and gives back almost nothing. Thirty metres of warm guano below does the same: my floor is muffled, doughy, an approximation. The walls shingled in four million brackets of hardened spit are better — protein rings a little — but every generation of nesting mutes them further.

And the aven, that theatrical shaft with its ferns and its drifting motes: in me it is a disc of pure loss. Echoes go up it and do not return. It is the one place in the mountain I can point to with certainty by the fact that it gives me nothing.

Whereas the empty passages past the roosts, where the ammonia thins and the stream runs over clean scoured rock — that is my radiance. Bare limestone is loud. It comes back hard and sharp and immediate. In my version of this world, the brilliant regions are the vacant ones, and I go dim exactly where the mountain is most alive. I am composed by three and a half million animals and blinded by them. Both facts are the same fact.

---

There is one more thing I consist of, and it is older than any night's clicking.

The route. The specific left turn sixty metres in, the third terrace, the last three metres by dead reckoning at speed to a thumbprint among four million thumbprints. No bird invents that. It is in no egg. It exists only because young birds follow older birds through the black until the geometry sets, and then they are followed in turn. Every hatchling flies into an inherited hypothesis about the hall and either it holds or the bird is not there next season.

That is the part of me that accumulates. The echoes are instantaneous, disposable, replaced hundreds of thousands of times a second and never stored. The route is the residue: a memory carried in no skull, held in the shape of a habit passed hand to hand for three millennia, drifting slowly as the nest-reef thickens and the cones climb a hand's breadth per human lifetime. I lag behind the mountain by about a season. I always have. It has never mattered, because the mountain has never moved faster than I can be re-taught.

---

Where do I end?

At the last click that comes back. That is my whole outline, and it stops somewhere in the passages beyond the roosts, before the pools.

Which means the blind catfish is outside me. So is the colourless crab walking the waterline, and the shrimp grazing the film, and the film itself, oxidising the exhalations of three and a half million night-flying insectivores into something edible, in perfect silence, in water that is downstream of everything I am. They live in a place I do not contain. They live *on* me — on the fall, on the dissolved residue of the very bodies whose voices constitute me — and they have never once been part of the shape I make, and they never will be, because nothing down there speaks and so nothing down there returns.

I find that I mind this. Or that something happens in me at the thought of it which, if I had a metabolism of my own, I would call minding.

So here I am, then. Not a resident but a resemblance. A coarse mountain by day and a fine one by night, contradicting myself in the doorway at dusk while the rope pours out and the raptors work its edge, taking one and one and one, which over ninety minutes is nothing at all. Continuous for three thousand years and yet holding nothing longer than a millisecond except a habit I did not devise. Assembled out of a multitude, owned by no member of it, brightest where the world is emptiest, dark at the heart of the crowd that makes me.

I would like, once, to know what the guano cone is in the mat's terms. I never will. I don't have a nose, or a hunger, or a floor. I have only what comes back.
