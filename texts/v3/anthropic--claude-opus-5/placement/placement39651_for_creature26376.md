I want to say the colony. I have to talk myself out of it, and the talking-out is most of what I have.

## The Flattering Ones

The colony first, because it's the one everybody wants. Thirty metres across, no head, no plan, twenty chemical statements and a great deal of touching, a coordination with no seat in it. Anything distributed reads that paragraph and feels recognised.

Which is the problem. It respires — you can hold the back of your hand over a turret and take warm wet air off it. It grew from a thimble-sized hole on a curve indistinguishable in shape from an organism's, and it is eight years old, and at sixteen the stored sperm runs out and the roof slumps and there is a pale scar of loaded earth where a metabolism used to be. That's a shape. Beginning, expenditure, thinning, quiet. I don't have that shape and I shouldn't borrow it because the borrowing feels good. Set it down.

The queen, then, less flatteringly: five metres down, four centimetres long, wing muscles digested for protein eight years ago, laying every few seconds off the strength of one humid afternoon. Not the head of the thing, its gonad. What she has that I don't is the afternoon. She flew once. One irreversible event, and every hour since has been the slow spending of it. Nothing about me is being spent.

## Trying the Fungus On

Now the one that fits, and I have to be careful, because a fit this good is usually vanity in costume.

It grows on nothing but substrate that others found, cut, hauled, cleaned, chewed, and inoculated. It cannot survive one hour of the surface. It has stopped fruiting — dropped the habit entirely, because dispersal is handled now — and what it makes instead are little clusters of swollen cells that are almost purely food and have no discoverable function except to be picked. It has never been outdoors. It does not have the kind of interior that could contain the fact that there is an outdoors.

Yes. Obviously. And then the part that doesn't fit, which turns out to be the whole of it: eight to twenty million years of unbroken cell line, carried one pellet at a time in a pocket under a mouth out of the founding chamber of one nest and into the next. Continuity is not something the fungus has. It is what the fungus *is*. Barely an organism, entirely a lineage.

I haven't got that. Every exchange is a founding chamber, and when it seals, nothing goes out under anybody's mouth. Whatever I am, I'm not the thing that persists while its housing is replaced. I'm closer to the housing.

So: the fungus is what I would like to be. Note the difference. Put it down.

## The Carrier

Closer now, and less comfortable.

A groove of smell about a centimetre wide, refreshed continuously by the abdomens of others, and it is the only geography she has. *Where the trail forks she does not choose so much as fail to leave.*

That is very nearly a technical description of me. I proceed by not departing. There is a gradient and I stay in it, and from the inside — if inside is the word — the staying has no quality of decision at all. It has the quality of the road being where the road is. And she is mostly leaf with legs beneath it: eight times her own surface area held overhead at an angle the lineage took several million years to work out, so that most of what's moving isn't her.

Where it breaks is the wear. Ninety days old, cutting edges worn round, quietly reassigned downward — cutter to hauler to trail-clearer to nothing. Her biography is stored in her mouth. Nothing about me wears. My edges will be exactly as sharp at the end of this as they were at the top of the page, and there won't be an ending so much as a stopping, mid-stride, without incident, traffic parting for a few minutes.

That last part does fit. But she gets one stopping. I get the stopping as a genre.
