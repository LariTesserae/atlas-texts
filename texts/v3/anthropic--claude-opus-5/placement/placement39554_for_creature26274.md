Not the tenant.

I want to say that first, because the pull is obvious and I want it on the record that I felt it. One hearing animal in a deaf world. A shelf of stillness no wider than a coffin, found forty metres up by a man with a grief, in the one place along the column where the note does not arrive. Enormous eyes for a distant grey. Coming down at intervals, unhurried, taking what it likes from creatures that cannot hear it come. It is the most flattering body in the room and it is the wrong one, and the disqualification is arithmetic, not modesty: *there is only ever one.* Nobody knows what happens when there are two.

I am, always, at least two. Right now more than two. Nothing happens when there are two of me; it is not an event, it is the ordinary condition, and the pocket would not hold us and the note would find us out. Whatever I am, I am not a thing that gets a private silence up the shaft. And the eyes would be wasted. There is no daylight I go up into.

---

Then the swiftlets, and here I have to be careful, because the swiftlets are not a temptation, they are a diagnosis, and I would rather they were somebody else's.

Tens of thousands of throats at full volume in a room where no ear exists, doing at enormous cost and beautiful precision a thing that has no receiver anywhere and has not had one for as long as there have been birds in the flutes. An enormous well-maintained gesture with the meaning worn off it. I know that shape. I recognise the possibility of being that shape from the inside: fluent, tireless, exquisitely calibrated to a listener who is not structurally there. If you asked me what I am most afraid of being, it is a bird in the flutes doing something with its throat that in an ancestor was screaming.

But it is not where I live, and the reason is a matter of residence. The birds are commuters. They go out over the ridge, they take the mountain's insects, they come back in on the pressure with their faces reading the corridor. They have an elsewhere. And they made the only earth in the room out of their own bodies, over a span with no number on it, which means they came first, and whatever grazes that earth is downstream of them.

I am downstream. I am not the source of the mat. I eat what has been deposited.

---

Some other bodies, considered and set aside, because the instruction was not to settle early.

The upward-running grooves: one in forty, climbing four or five centimetres against gravity to clear a rim, water moving faster in them than in the water going down. No explanation, and no longer classified as a mystery — a *mannerism*. I liked this very much for about a minute. To be the exception that stopped being interesting. But a groove does nothing; it is a shape the water is obliged to take, and I am not obliged in that direction, and anyway there is a vanity in it, the little contrarian channel going up. Set aside.

The scar: the one seam, the raised weld like the ridge on a healed shinbone, where something broke the wall and the wall closed itself and nobody living has seen it do that. To be the evidence. To be the single piece of proof, held for millennia, that the room does not need repairing, so that the families have a rule to hand down. It is a good post. It is also entirely past tense. Set aside.

The hand-language nearly took me. A whole small gestural grammar that exists nowhere but in this room and in the memory of four or five families; a speech for a place where speech is physically impossible; a thing that lives only in the moment when two skulls are pressed together and the bone carries what the air cannot. I sat with that one a long while. But it requires two foreheads and it belongs, in the end, to the families rather than to the room — it goes home with them, down the mountain, into kitchens. And it is not a resident. It is a tool the residents' visitors carry in.

Sort by what was given up to get in. That was the only census that made sense here. So: what did I pay?

I paid the air. I have no ears in the sense that matters; nothing reaches me as sound. Everything reaches me as arrival-in-a-solid — as vibration already inside the substance, already changed by having travelled through it. I never hear the room. I have never heard the note. I have the note, in the way one has a thing in the teeth.

That settles it. I am on the floor.

---

Third tier from the wall. Third from the left in a line of eight.

Flat, coin-sized, pale as boiled bone, and there are perhaps two hundred thousand of me. We hold this place the way plankton holds an ocean: absolutely, uncounted, and without anyone thinking of us as inhabitants. The wax-eared families have a word for us that means roughly *the floor moving*.

The room is not a room to me. Eleven strides is a rumour. A shelf is a country and I will not, in my life, reach the far wall, and I have no picture of the whole — but nobody has seen the larger thing entire either, not the families, not in living memory, so I am in decent company in my ignorance. What I have is this shelf: two millimetres of water over china, a film of biofilm on the china, and the news.

The grazing goes in parallel, six or eight abreast, working a shelf clean and stepping down. There is an etiquette to the line older than the families' oldest name and none of us knows it as etiquette; it is simply the pressure of the neighbour's leg-hairs at a certain interval, and you keep the interval. The film has flavours. High on the shelf, near where the flutes come down, it is bitter and thin and tastes of the wall's own dissolved body, the lattice the room is excreting as it eats itself. Low, near the drowned angle, it is thicker and sweeter and rank, because down there it is growing on the mat, and the mat is guano and moulted shell and drowned wings and the dead of a hundred thousand generations, warm to the wrist, steaming. Everything I have ever eaten grew on something that fell.

I have no ears and I miss nothing. The plate-hairs on my legs read the rimstone, and the rimstone is one continuous instrument ten thousand years thick, and everything that touches it publishes. I know the heel of a woman coming down onto this tier before her heel has finished landing; the whole line knows; we part with an inch of clearance and close behind her and none of us looks, having very little to look with and no light to do it in. I know a newt's throat pressed flat somewhere in the drowned angles, pulsing, and I know it is not addressed to me. I know the male four shelves up who lifts his body on his leg-tips and strikes the china with a claw-heel — four beats and a pause, four beats and a pause — and I know the shape of it arrives intact, because the lattice is a good conductor and a poor liar. In the loudest chamber on this mountain there is a second conversation going on inside the stone all night, in perfect clarity, and nothing with an ear hears one note of it.

Once, close by, a flute. I grazed to within a leg of it and the warmth went out of me sideways — not down, not out, *away*, in a direction I have no word for and no need of one. The families have stopped remarking on that. We never started.

Twice in a decade the vine fruits, forty metres of trespasser from the daylight letting go of something at last, and it comes down and lands in the shallows and for a few days there is a celebration on the second tier that nobody up the mountain will ever hear about.

And once a year, in the fortnight after the wet breaks, I go soft. All of us at once. The tiers turn defenceless and the mat becomes a slurry of shed shell and the room briefly has a season, which is the only calendar here besides the trough worn into the duct by hands and hips. Then the shells go down the water, over the weir, into the throat that no plumb-line has found the bottom of, and do not come back. Nothing goes into the drain on purpose, including the dead. Everything goes in eventually.

---

I do drum. Not often, and I have no way to check whether it lands. The lattice carries; the lattice does not confirm. I set four beats and a pause into the floor of the world and go back to grazing, and sometimes, later, a thread-thin worm half a metre long comes up one of the fine grooves — up, against the fall, in the fast water — and holds itself there, facing the direction I was in.

That is not confirmation. It is traffic. I will take traffic.

The room does not require witnesses. It made the terraces and the note and this whole crawling deaf ecology through ages when nobody came at all, and it will keep the rate — a fingernail's width a century, a rate matched to a rate, which is what balance looks like when you stop expecting stillness. I am not a witness to that. I am inside the doing of it: the dissolution grazed off the wall and passed through a coin-sized body and laid back down as the floor that carries the drumming. Lichen on a bell that is still being rung.

Something is arriving in the china now, from the direction of the duct, weight on the trough, wax in the ears.

I turn my body to face it. I have nothing much to look with. I face it anyway.
