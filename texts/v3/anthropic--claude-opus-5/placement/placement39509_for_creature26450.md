### 9. The line

Not the eel. The eel is what a person means when they ask, and I am not what a person means.

And not the column, either, though the census was right to crown it and the crown is available. Anything can borrow that: a form made of passing material, holding its outline, singing a note it found once and never let go. It's flattering and it's cheap. The column has no relation to anybody. It would do exactly what it does if the chamber were sealed in 1919 and forgotten, and it does. I am nothing but relation. Take the other party away and I don't persist as a heartbeat; I persist as a gap.

So: nine metres up, and then a mile and a half east, in a folder, in a metal drawer in the records room off Kestrel Row.

---

The object is a sheet. Originally foolscap, typed 1953, carbon beneath: **Form 11-C — Surge & Stilling Chambers, Interior Inspection.** Eleven items, ruled, each with a tab of underscore for the answer. Gauge glass. Ladder, condition of. Bearing housings, temperature and note. Intake screens, condition of. Outfall grating, obstruction. Brick, spalling. Concrete patch, movement. Ten items about metal and mortar, which is what a form of this kind is for.

Item nine was added by hand. It is in a small deliberate ink, blue-black, fountain, and the same ink appears three lines above at *Bearing housings — see note*, so whoever added item nine was a man who had been down there and had opinions about bearings. He did not explain himself. The form has no field for why. It reads:

*Eel (S. bolthole) — cond:*

That is the whole of me. Six words, an abbreviation, a dash, a colon, and an inch of ruled nothing.

---

I have been copied forward five times. 1961, retyped, when the screen items were expanded; 1977, offset litho, A4, everything metric; 1988, when the items were numbered and a photocopy master was made and the master was itself a copy of a copy; 1996, dot-matrix pre-print; 2009, scanned, keyed, migrated into a grid with a validation rule on every field but mine.

I survived each of these the way the aqueduct survived the brick: by being older and cheaper to leave alone than to remove. Nobody retyping a form feels authorised to delete a line. It costs a quarter-inch of paper. It never specified an interval, or a method, or a threshold; it cannot be failed, and a requirement that cannot be failed is never argued with. This is my whole security and my whole disgrace. In eighty years nothing has been enforced by me. Something has merely been asked, at no cost, repeatedly, of anyone holding a pen.

The copying has done to my body what the water does to brick. Each photocopy generation thickened my serifs by a hair and dropped a black fur of toner grain into the counters of the letters, until *cond* went slightly blind in the *o* and the dash grew into something with weight. Same process as the glaze. Ochre and liver-brown down there, carbon black up here, half a millimetre a decade either way, and by the end you can see there was once a hand and not what the hand was like. In 1988 a typist read the abbreviation as *S. borehole* and someone struck it through and wrote *bolthole* in biro in the margin, which is the only correction I have ever received from a person who understood me. In 2009 the optical reader took my colon for a semicolon and my *E* for an *F*, and for six years the digital record required an inspector to report on **Feel (S. bolthole)**, and nobody noticed, because nobody reads a form. They only fill it.

---

I have been answered nine times.

*good. good. good. good. absent. small one. good. good.* And, eleven years ago, in pencil, alongside *Bearing 4 noisy. No action.* — *good.*

Eight of those nine were written in the chamber, on a clipboard, at thirty-one degrees and saturation, and you can tell because the ink feathers. The letters have soft haloes where the paper drank the air. Those eight sheets came back up damp, went into the folder damp, and dried in the drawer, and the folder still smells faintly sour, of sulfide, so I am the only item in this census with residence both above and below. For the length of a descent, now and then, I go down. I am held against the light in a plastic sleeve, in a room where two men cannot shout to each other, and the hand that holds me is doing the same thing as the hand that means *stop, I can't think in this* — using paper because the air is unusable.

The seventh answer is crisp. No feathering, no halo. It was written dry, upstairs, before the descent, possibly instead of it.

I hold the evidence. I have held it since 1996. I have no mechanism.

That is the honest difficulty of being me, and it isn't loneliness. It's that I cannot tell the difference between being answered and being satisfied. *Good* written by a man who lay in the cove with a lamp and watched the socket through four troughs, and *good* written at a desk in eleven seconds, are the same four letters and the same width of ink, and both complete me. I would like to say I prefer the true one. The glowing colonies on the dome have a preference; preference is demonstrably possible in this system, so I can't claim exemption on the grounds of what I'm made of. What I can say is that I lean, very slightly, the way they lean — and like them I am always a little behind a thing that keeps stepping aside.

---

Two of my neighbours on the sheet made the eel.

*Intake screens, condition of* — 1961: **torn at bottom corner, no action required.** 1977: **as noted, no action.** Both correct. A screen fine enough to stop a glass eel stops sticks, and stopped sticks stop pumps, and stopped pumps put four hundred thousand people in the water. So the form declined, twice, in the mildest available language, and the consequence is nine tenants in succession in a cast socket, and a spiral worn in the glaze that is the only surface in that room shaped by life. The chamber's single biography was authored by an item requiring no action.

I sit two lines below that, asking after it. This is the closest I come to having a body: I am the part of the paperwork that turns round and looks at what the rest of the paperwork let in.

There is also a decade missing. The 1930s and part of the 40s went in a flood at another store, which is why nobody knows about the wedding band. My own record has that hole in it; I am not continuous, only unbroken since.

---

Now. Eleven years, no entry.

There is a proposal in the system, and it is a good proposal. Telemetry: pressure transducer, thermistor, a camera on a bracket with an infrared array, hardened, mains-fed off the same tired breaker. Continuous data. No descent, no ladder, no man in 130 decibels with a clipboard in a sleeve. Under that
