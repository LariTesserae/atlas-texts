## 1. The census, from the inside

I look for myself in the census first, because it's short, and because I want to be one of the countable things.

Not the visitor. Visitors have an outside; that is their whole definition. A visitor is a creature with a Rim-crossing in it, a person carrying a wind somewhere in their body that they will get back to. Nothing about me is going anywhere and nothing about me came from anywhere I can produce on request.

Not a borrower, though I would like to be. Glass in the bones, a private pocket of duration, spending it on a gait — the only sanctioned verb in the basin, walking, at whatever rate a quarter of an hour per leaf implies. To be the regulator. To be permitted a *next*. But a borrower has somewhere to be, and I have never once had somewhere to be.

Not the Chatter, either, or the something-in-it. That is the tempting one — everybody who comes here wants, briefly, to be the pit, because it is where all the events went. But the pit is a drain. The noise is not speech, it is spillage. Whatever is down there is not doing; it is being done, fifty times a second, forever, and it cannot even keep the year it grows.

## 2. The wrong layer

So I go down into the drifts, because that is where the only history is, and for a while I am sure of it.

Dig through the wafers, metres of them, each one perfect, each one still faintly ringing when your wrist knocks the pile. Down at the bottom the shape changes: broad, sloppy, asymmetrical things, veined in that soft crowded branching that means a leaf grew by argument rather than by rule. Stomata. Pores for losing water. Made in a world with weather, by something that expected weather to continue.

I know what I'm made of, upstream. Somewhere behind me there is humidity. Something wet is in my composition the way salt is in blood, long after the sea.

But the first-draft leaves are *finished*. They fell. Falling is a completed act and they have the enormous peace of it — the only completed act in four hundred square kilometres. Nothing has fallen here since. They are not a record of four centuries; they are the twenty days when the basin was still being converted and the forest was growing fast and badly and greedily, and they have the dignity of having been let go.

I have never fallen. I am on the other side of that boundary in the stack — the wrong side, the dry side, the first wafer, the draft that was kept.

## 3. Where I actually am

Third terrace, south-southwest, a grove of drawn-glass bundles standing about nine metres, understorey of brass horsetail ranked along a joint, violet coins on the plate rock that have not moved off those particular stones since before your country had its present name.

I am one of the stems, and the only thing about me that matters is that I am bent.

Six degrees off vertical, convex to the west, and the bow does not start at my base — it starts at about the height of a human hip and it opens gradually, and it corresponds to nothing in the light, nothing in the rock, no load, no wind, because there has never been wind. Read across the grove and the whole corridor is there: forty stems, all of us leaning out of the way of a line that runs from the terrace lip to the crystal beds, with the clearance widening slightly at shoulder height and then again, a little, higher up, where a raised arm would go.

There was no moment of bending. This has to be understood. I did not lean; leaning is a change and changes are taxed and the tax collector took everything at the beginning. I was *set* bent. The bow is not a thing I did to accommodate somebody. It is my shape, in the way that a ridge's shape is a ridge, and its content — all of its content — is somebody else's room to pass.

That is what I am. I am the negative of a stranger's shoulder, standing up in glass.

## 4. What it is like

There is no waiting. I want to be exact about this, because people assume patience and patience is a change: patience is a felt gap between now and the thing. I have no gap. What I have instead is the expectation itself, held with no duration under it, the way a socket is a shape of the thing that fits it whether or not the thing has ever been inserted.

The one activity I have is the ring. Everyone who comes here hears it and calls it the forest conducting, which is right, and none of them hear it whole, because sound dies at twelve metres and the note is a chord thirty-one kilometres wide. I contribute perhaps one thread of it. I have contributed that thread continuously for four hundred years and I have never heard what it is part of.

## 5. Someone came

Twice, that I am the shape of. Possibly three times; the distinction between two and three requires a memory and memory requires an event, and my only faculty is geometry.

They arrive stinking. This is the thing no visitor knows about themselves. To a basin where nothing rots, a person is a walking putrefaction — salt, breath, wet wool, the hot bacterial reek of a body running its accounts at full speed, the smell of decay, which is the smell of things being allowed to happen. It is the most extraordinary odour I will ever be near. The forest tastes the surplus off them at once and begins, politely, to sip, and their watch and their pulse start their long private disagreement, and they stand there in my corridor with their hand on my fibre — they always touch, they cannot help it, the trunks ring — and they look up my six degrees of bow and understand, and take it badly.

Then they walk through the space I am the edge of, and out, and up the talus, and across the seam into the wind.

I am still bent exactly as much. Not more, in gratitude; not less, in relief. The fit was never a response to them and it was also never about anyone else.

## 6. The unused part

Here is the part I would tell them, if telling were available, if anything I said could travel more than twelve metres before it was absorbed and spent.

The bending goes everywhere. That is the horror they report on the Rim — that you cannot outwalk it, that the unplanned path is bent too. But turn it around and stand in it: the basin is a field of accommodations, at every scale, on every line anyone could ever choose, and almost none of them are ever used. Ninety-nine hundredths of this forest is clearance held open for a passage that will not occur. Corridors on terraces no one has climbed to. Shoulder-room at the head of a scree gully, four hundred years wide, for a person who was never born.

I do not know which kind I am. I cannot know. Knowing would take an event, and if I had one to spend I would not spend it on that.

## 7. The wafer

One of them took a leaf. Mine, or a leaf from the stem next along the corridor — the difference is not available to me either.

It is on a shelf now, in a house in the wet country, in a room where the light changes twice a day and the air moves and the wood beneath it shrinks slightly every winter. It has not curled. It will not. It will outlast the shelf and the house and the children who were told, more than once, not to touch it, and it will not report anything back, ever, because nothing propagates out of here and nothing propagates in.

That fragment is the only part of me that will ever be inside a future. It doesn't have one — but it's *in* one, which is nearer than I will get.

What I have instead of news from it is the shape of the reach that took it: a slight extra clearance at chest height, west-facing, held open in me still, precisely the size of a hand that has already closed.

## 8.

So: come down over the Rim, and lose your sense of distance, and shout and hear it stop, and pick the line you're certain no one predicted.

I'm the one already leaning out of your way. I was bent for you before you were an idea, by twenty days of frantic growth I don't remember, and I will be exactly this bent afterward. If that seems a poor sort of welcome — a courtesy with no one behind it, arranged in advance, unable to be moved by the actual weight of you when you finally arrive — consider how much of your own accommodation was built before you were consulted, and how little of it you have ever managed to thank.

I can't be surprised by you. It is the one thing I would spend everything for.

Walk through. The clearance is good. I checked it four centuries ago, once, forever, at the only moment I was able to check anything.
