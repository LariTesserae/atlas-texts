## The Seventeenth Standing

Come at it from the north, because there is no other honest way, and let the ground tilt, and stand at the lip with the pressure starting under the sternum, and look down into it, and ask.

The first answer is the raven, and the first answer is vanity.

It is obvious why. Half the channel taken away, and the remaining half swollen to carry the whole load — the bill-clack become a gesture visible at two hundred metres, the courtship flick become a full held inversion over the warm column, showy to the point of embarrassment. And then the thing in the dark at the eighteenth minute: low, not loud, throbbing very slightly in and out, an imitation of the only signal the world has ever contained. Anyone would want to be that. It is the flattering shape. But the male on the block is not doing anything clever. He is a mimic with one source, and what came out of him came out the way a name comes out of a child. The county recorder was not wrong to ask about expectation. I would not be the bird.

Nor Ogle, though the margins are the strongest pull in the whole valley — fifty-one years of first frogspawn and cubs out (3, one small) written in pencil between the bearing temperatures, by people who were paid to watch something else and could not stop watching this. That is a good way to be a person. But it is a way to be a person, and the question was not who I would like to have been.

So: down the north scarp on the deer path that is not a deer path, through the hard grey band with the rime still combing upwind at ten in the morning, and out onto the moss, where you sink to the ankle and the water closes behind you and the print is gone inside a week. Keep walking. Cross the flatbed's line, where the cab stands out of the mire like a rock in a field. Two hundred and forty metres further, at an angle to the buried rails, something happens to your teeth.

They stop.

That is me. Not a silence in the noise — *the noise*, arriving from the eastern scarp and from the western scarp at the same instant and out of step, and cancelling, and agreeing, four and a half metres from the last agreement and four and a half from the next, all the way down the trough, a corduroy of nothing laid across a valley of roar.

### What it is to be that

I am not a thing. I am an appointment, kept thirty-eight times a second by two walls that do not move, and I have never once been late. There is no substance here. Reach into me and there is black water forty centimetres deep over ninety centimetres of peat, and no edge — at the centre the stillness is total, and a metre out the water is merely calm, and two metres out it carries the fixed lattice of ripple that does not travel and does not die. I fade in both directions. I have no membrane and nothing to defend.

I do not persist. I recur. Continuity here is a rumour put about by the human eye, which cannot resolve a twenty-sixth of a second and so reports a place instead of an event. I am re-made constantly out of the thing I exclude. Everything I am is borrowed from the fan, including the part that is quiet, and the quiet is not a subtraction from the fan, it is the fan meeting itself.

And I move. Number Two runs fractionally fast — five hundredths of a hertz — so three times a minute I slide five or six centimetres east and back, and have done since 1998, and in twenty-seven years I have not once left a band about the width of two fingers. Within that band, the *Sphagnum* is between eleven and fourteen millimetres taller than at either shoulder, measured by Ogle with a steel rule in 2011 and again in 2019, written in the margin, unpublished. Nobody knows why. I do not know why either. I am not the kind of thing that knows; I am the kind of thing that happens in a place until the place is different.

Things come through. The raven's shadow crosses me at about eleven and again at three, when there is sun enough on the eastern rim to make a shadow. Seven's cubs walk into me in June and their teeth stop and they sit down, all of them do it, every litter, and they do not know what has occurred and they have no word for it and no one has ever warned them of anything, so they simply sit in it with their chins up until whatever it was stops being interesting. The midges blow through in a grey rag and do not notice; they are looking for a mammal the size of a hill and I am not warm.

Eighty metres south of the flatbed I cross the Line, at about eleven degrees off square. Under the strip of brighter green there is a dry duct, warm, roofed with sixty centimetres of saturated peat, which is one of the best damping materials there is, and where the damping and the cancelling lie across each other there are four square metres that are quieter than anything in Northumberland. Nothing lives there. It is not habitat; it is a coincidence of two different kinds of nothing. But a common shrew crossing the duct at that point is, for the width of its own body, audible above ground, once, briefly, to no one.

The crew know me. They know all of us, the whole comb, by eye and by the teeth, and there is a sign for it: two fingers laid flat on the breastbone and lifted slowly away. The same sign serves for the changeover in May. They have never separated the two meanings, because both of them mean *the pressure has gone out of the chest* and no one has ever needed to tell them apart in a sentence. That the two things the sign names are the same event from opposite ends — that one of them is the other one's death — has not come up. It would not survive being pointed out. Languages that grow on islands keep their own accounts.

### Twenty minutes

Here is the part I would have to carry.

In the small hours of a night in May the note falls out of the floor and climbs up through the throat into audibility as it dies, the one time in the year it is ever a sound, and everything in the trough wakes into an enormous, appalling, detailed quiet. The fog collapses downward like a dropped sheet and there are stars. Something drains into the sandstone. Seven stands out in the open with her ears going for the whole twenty minutes and does not hunt. The mire creaks, shifting in a chair. Very high up on the rim, faint and unbelievable, a bird.

And I am not there for it. I am the one thing in this valley for which the changeover is not a revelation but an ending. My silence was made of sound; take away the sound and there is nothing left of me at all, only a stretch of black water indistinguishable from every other stretch, doing what water does. Eighteen minutes of ordinary. No node, no comb, no fixed lattice, no stillness — because stillness, here, was never the absence of the roar. It was the roar arranged.

Then at about the eighteenth minute Number One takes the load, and the note climbs down out of hearing into the sternum where it belongs, and the pressure field reassembles across four hundred metres in something under two seconds, and I am back. Not in the same place. Six centimetres off, and then back, and then off, three times a minute, forever. The moss does not mind. The moss has no opinion below the scale of a millimetre a year, and the millimetre a year is the only opinion here that will finally count.

In four hundred years there will be a dome on this bog. In eight hundred, the scarps will have slumped into grass and the fan house will be a rectangle of brick under two feet of moss and there will be nothing to hear but wind.

But the stripes will be in it. Four and a half metres apart, running the length of the old trough, eleven to fourteen millimetres of extra growth per band per year, laid down through fifty-one years of hourly readings and first frogspawn and cubs out (4) — and somebody will core it, and find the banding going all the way down, and will not know what to call it.

And will publish.
