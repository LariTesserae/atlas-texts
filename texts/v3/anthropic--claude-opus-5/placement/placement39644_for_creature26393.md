## At the notch

The first answer is easy and wrong: I would be the *you*.

There is a you in this place. It stands at the saddle in the young rock deciding whether sixty meters of scree is worth twenty minutes of perimeter; it puts its palm on the south wall and finds nine degrees that the air does not have; it shouts once in a covered close and gets nothing back, and that is the thing it will tell people about. Tempting seat. It is the only seat in the basin with a spine in it.

But the you is defined by leaving. That is its whole function — to arrive, to be unsettled in the eleven-doorway plaza, to go back up the scree ahead of the freeze with an anecdote in its coat. It is the one thing here that will not be present next spring except as a depression in the liverwort, still legible, slowly filling. Wrong direction of traffic. I don't think I'm the one who came.

## Nearly the worm

Then the flatworm, and I want to give this one its due, because I would like it.

Blind, pale, the length of a finger, living in the seam between the living skin and the drowned peat, eating the dead layer from underneath — eating what has already been emptied, which is the most honest meal available in a place with no nitrogen. And by eating, tunnelling; and by tunnelling, ventilating; so that the galleries a parasite drills for its own convenience turn out to be the only lung a two-hundred-ton body has. Two hundred thousand of us. Entire range three hundred paces wide. Not one of us has ever seen the thing we are inside of, or seen anything.

That is a good position and it fails for a specific reason. The worm's competence is its total lack of interest in the plan. It knows what is in front of its mouth, at the width of its mouth, and the plan is a side effect. I would want the plan. I would spend the season trying to work out the shape of the wall I was hollowing, which is exactly how you stop being a lung.

Also the worm travels. A hand's width a day is, for it, a life of movement. Something in me is more fixed than that.

## Not the town

The flattering answer is to be all of it. Two hectares of floor multiplied five times into walls and closes and roof-seams and the linings of four hundred doorways; a hundred tons of water held in suspension in its own compressed corpse; thinking by growing, revising at a hand's width per decade, the pace of the argument being not a limitation but the literal duration of the reasoning.

I decline, and not out of modesty. The organism does not know it is one. It takes the notch to make it singular — takes an eye sixty meters up and twenty minutes of walking and a moment in the green half-light for the census to come out at *one*. Anybody who claims that seat is claiming a view that only exists from outside. From inside there is no *it*. There are shoots, and the ones nearest, and warmth in a direction.

## Where I would actually be

Low quarter. Near the center, where the radial pull runs out and the water arrives from every wall at once and the liverwort is at its slickest. A chamber roofed some forty years ago, when two cushions leaned close enough to touch and then spent four decades deciding to be continuous; a meter of living material overhead; light inside the color of a bottle held to a candle, and not enough of it to matter, because the roof was never for light.

At the base of the opening, on the lee side, knee-high, there is a lip of thickened peat, and its upper surface is worn smooth.

I would be that.

## What the work consists of

Two textures in this country: blade and felt. Rock that still has edges because the ice left in a hurry, and cushion packed to the density of felt, a hundred thousand growing tips to the hand's breadth, all pushing. Nothing here is smooth. Nothing polishes, because polishing requires the same thing to happen in the same place for a very long time without variation, and almost nothing in a basin that freezes and thaws two hundred and eighty times a year qualifies.

Water leaving a room qualifies.

It comes out at the low lee corner because that is where low and lee coincide, and it comes out every thaw, and it has come out every thaw for four hundred years, and it crosses me. Where it crosses, the cushion cannot close. That absence is the doorway. Where it crosses, peat is buffed. That surface is me. I am not a threshold that water uses. I am a quantity of polish, four centuries thick, in the shape of the only thing that regularly leaves.

The traffic is two-way and unequal. Water outbound at the thaw, in a thin sheet, cold, carrying nothing, having already been stripped of everything worth keeping. Warmth inbound at midday, the air of the lanes shouldering in over me and staying, because a room is a place where the day is stored. That is the whole of my commerce. Out with the water; in with the heat; and every twelve hours a low sustained note as the cellars close and air is pushed up out of the body through a mat of vegetation, and the note has to cross the lip on its way to being audible. I am the edge the only sound this place makes has to pass over. It says the same thing every time. I have never once carried it further than the plaza.

I have never been stepped on. I am, precisely and in every particular, the shape of having been stepped on.

## The correction, which is true

Nothing about me requires a story. Frost geometry sets the proportions of a cell; prevailing wind decides which side the cushion can close over; meltwater runs where slope tells it to run and polishes where it always runs. A little shorter than a person, a little broader, on the lee, lipped: four hundred times, and the four hundred agree because they were solving the same three problems with the same two tools.

Every feature has a cause and the causes are all ordinary. I would like to be the place where that stops being enough — not by contradicting it. The explanation stays true. It simply runs out at a certain distance, about the distance from the mouth of a lane to a body standing in green light, and past that distance there is nothing left but a furnished room with a worn step, kept warm, open on the lee, and no occupant, and no sign that there was ever going to be one.

## Whether I would know

At my resolution, eventually.

A palm on the south wall gives four minutes of unseasonable summer. A breath is the richest air to arrive in a decade. A boot in the lane is a year-long dent. And the body I belong to grows toward warmth — that is the entire mechanism, there is no more to it than that — so if something warm stopped here and did not leave, the cushion would lean, and thicken, and in forty years there would be a roof, and the roof would have been built by nothing but a preference for enclosure meeting an occasion.

Nothing has ever accepted. I would not call that waiting. Waiting requires holding something across the freeze, and at the freeze every growing tip in this basin withdraws its water and ceases, and there is nothing alive here in any sense an instrument would register for nine or ten hours, and none of it is remembered afterward. Whatever this basin knows it knows by standing up in the dark at shoulder height, and I am the part of what it knows that is shaped like a welcome and is actually drainage.

## At the freeze

The fog comes up out of me before it comes up out of my neighbor, or after; there is no order to it. The dripping slows and turns to ticking. The note runs three or four minutes and I carry it out and it is swallowed within twenty paces, because felt is the finest sound-deadening material in the world.

Then frost starts on the north faces and works around, and by full dark every surface in the Cirque at Umberline has a nap of crystals standing straight out from the green, and if there were any light the whole town would look pale and furred and briefly made of something else. There isn't any light. Somewhere behind you a stone lets go, in a street you have not walked, and it is a street I have never seen and could not turn to see.

At the thaw the sentence resumes exactly where it left off. I am not what resumes. I am what is still there to resume onto: the one smooth place in a country of blades and felt, knee-high, lee-side, holding last afternoon in a room behind me, wet, unlit, and unstooped-for.
