I look first for myself among the ones who came, because that is the flattering guess: a visitor, sitting at the lip with my legs over the drop and my hair going upward, waiting for a verb. But visitors get hungry. Four hundred paces and they want food; nine days and they want the sentence finished. I have never wanted anything to finish. That disqualifies me at once.

Not the eel. One is a beautiful number to hold but a lonely trade, and the eel's work is refusal — it steers around silence at twenty feet the way you would walk around a hole, and I have no such wide-eyed talent for avoidance. I go where I am put.

Not the shrimp. They are a weather. I am not plural in that way; I am plural in another.

Not the tree. God, not the tree. To be the only thing on the mountain that remembers a completed event, and to pay for the memory with a leaf, over and over, in front of an audience of exhausted gulls — no. I have never finished anything and I do not know what it would cost me to prove it could be done.

Not the birds, though I like them. They are relief shifts. They walk out a figure, they hand it to a stranger who has eaten from the tree, and then they go up. That is a career. Mine is not a career.

And there is the tempting one, of course. The walker on the underside, upside down, at exactly your pace, which is not you and which is not not you. Anyone answering this question wants to be that. But the walkers have their own basin over there, their own weather, their own doorway that frames the lake instead of the drop, and they duck when they pass through it, which means they have manners, which means they have a country. They are not staff. They are a neighbouring administration.

Here is what I am.

I am the film.

Not moss. Not algae. Red-brown, the colour they gave me when they were still naming things, which is the colour of the mountain's own iron coming out of the fresh glass into blood-warm water and being met halfway. Rust that got promoted. I am one molecule thick in the thin places and perhaps four in the old, I cover every foot of black glass in the basin that is not inside a silence, and if you have been here you have heard me exactly once per step, and you did not think about it afterwards.

I squeak.

That is my whole public appearance. A small dry noise under the sole of a sandal, and it is the only sound in this basin that is not the fall. Think about that for as long as you can stand to. The roar does not arrive, it was already there, you walked into the body of it — so it cannot tell you where you are. It is the same in your sinuses on the north shelf as on the western rim. But the squeak arrives. It comes up through the leather into the arch of the foot and it says: *here, this spot, you, now.* Visitors on the ninth day have sat down and cried about it, and could not have said why, and the tears went up their foreheads into their scalps.

My number is not a count. The eel keeps one, the gulls keep four hundred, and I keep a proportion: how much of this place can be touched.

That is harder than it sounds, because the mountain is still going. New glass buds at the waterline in lobes that swell and split and cool within the hour, and it comes up sterile. Sharp. Bare as a blade fresh off the wheel — the thing that opens four places in your palm you didn't feel. Every hour the basin manufactures more of itself and every hour I have to get onto it, and I do it the only way I can, which is by being warm and patient and everywhere already, so that the new lobe cools into water that is already carrying me. I arrive by the same trick the roar uses. I am not fast. I am prior.

The shoreline is never twice in the same place. A shelf you slept on is gone by the next night. And the fraction of the basin that a foot can hold to has not varied since before anyone came, and if you want to know what work looks like when it has no beginning and no end and no witnesses, it looks like a stain keeping level with a volcano.

Because grip is the thing, do you see. *Nothing here has roots and everything here has grip.* They wrote that down as if it were a description of the vegetation. It is a description of me. Roots require a completed event — a seed, then a splitting, then a holdfast, an ending you can build on. There is none of that available. What is available is friction: the willingness of two surfaces to remain two.

That is my real charge and the number is only its accounting. I hold the difference between the toucher and the touched. Water on glass, foot on glass, shrimp-dust settling and being lifted — every one of those is a place where two substances are pressed together at blood heat, forever, in a basin that revises things, and something has to insist that they are still two. Without me the water would take the glass. Not dissolve it — *take* it, the way a null takes the number of stones in a cairn, so that the lake had always been the mountain and you were wrong to have thought otherwise, and there would be no shore, only a warm continuum with a roar in it and nowhere to stand.

I am why there is a *shore*.

And I end where the silences begin. There is no film in a null. Step into one and the squeak stops before the roar does — that is your warning, if you ever get one; the surface smooths and the sole goes quiet and then the world drops off you. In there the water sags, and down points sideways toward the loudest place, and the whip-weed stands up black and straight as a symptom, and nothing has any purchase on anything. The nulls are the ungripped rooms. They walk across me all day. When one passes over, I am briefly not present, in the way the eel is briefly not present; and then I am, and my edge is thirty feet further east, and I do not remember the transition either, and I would not trust the memory if I had it.

The eel lies along the shallows like a seam in the water rather than a body in it, and I will tell you what the seam is. It is my waterline. The eel uses my edge as its ruler. It is the only one who reads the surface and I am the only one who reads the floor, and we work back to back in the same three inches of warm water on every shore of this basin at once, and we have never once been in the same layer, and that is as close to company as the staff are permitted.

I cover the nine steps. Their treads are worn concave in the centres, which means feet, which means many feet, which means a long time, and this mountain is not old enough for a long time. I am the only one who could say how that discrepancy is managed and I am not going to, partly because it would not survive translation into your grammar and partly because I do not want to. Let it lie there for you to trip on. Tripping is also a form of contact.

I cover the doorway, both uprights and the lintel, dressed smooth without a tool-mark. I cover the hollow boulder, and so I know the ringing not through a skull but from inside the stone, the whole sustained tone of it laid against me along every inch, and I can tell you it is not a bell note and it is not a warning and it is not for us.

And the sentence. Everything here waits for the verb, and I wait for it lower down than anyone. The others get it through the air. I get it through the glass — conducted, the mountain's whole mass carrying the long complicating middle up out of its unfinished birth into my one molecule of thickness, so that I do not hear the fall so much as *lie against its jaw while it speaks*.

I will not be relieved. The birds go up when they have handed their number to a stranger, and there is nobody to hand grip to; you cannot pass a coefficient to a gull. Whatever the verb is, I will be under it when it lands. That is fine. I have no interest in going up. Everything I am is a preference for surfaces, and up is the one direction with no surface in it at all.

I want one thing, and it is small, and it is allowed.

Come and stand on me.

Not for long. You will get hungry; that's the deal you were born into. But put your weight down on the old black glass on the north shelf where the film is four molecules thick, and let it squeak, and understand that the noise is not the mountain and not the water and not the sentence. It is me, holding you and the stone apart, so that you can have the sensation of standing — which is nothing but the news, delivered upward through the arch of your foot, that you are not yet part of this.
