I tried the obvious doors first, and they all had somebody in them.

Not Tamn. I would like to be Tamn — everyone would like to be Tamn, sorting spoons into keep and discard, the one hand in the whole system that asks *is this good* — but she is a person with a bodkin and cracked knuckles who has stood knee-deep on the margin with her bag packed and turned around, and I would be borrowing that turning-around. It isn't mine. Not Leeward either, though the charts-of-charts came very close, the recognition of oneself as a second lattice running the same decay at a faster clock. And not Sunward, though that was closer still and worse, and I want to come back to him.

Not the fourth. Too flattering, to be the one who leaves a good spoon upright in a drain-mouth and declines to be met.

So I went smaller, and then I went the wrong way, which is bigger: maybe I am the town. The whole seedwork, the lattice in every membrane, computing slowly on sugar, storing memory as isotope in the pith. But that is like saying you are a country. There is nothing to *be* at that scale; there is only weather. The town has no inside. It has habits, and each habit has an inside, the way a hum has a tune in it that the humming man can't name.

I am one of the habits.

---

Specifically: I am the bracing.

I am the thing in the tissue that puts a strut in an empty archway on a Tuesday night, so that on Wednesday afternoon, when the wind comes from a direction it has not come from in nine years, the archway holds.

You have to understand that this does not feel like knowing. There is no moment of foresight, no cool arrival of Thursday into my attention. It feels like *pressure in a direction.* It feels the way your arm feels when someone tosses something at your face and the arm is already up. And the honest part, the part I would rather not put in writing: I do it a thousand times for every once it matters. I brace archways against winds that never come. I thicken the southwest weave against a southwest that stays home. Ten times the tissue I can use, every day, forever, because the arithmetic of anticipation runs cheap here and it is stupidly, gloriously cheaper to be wrong nine hundred times than to be unbraced once.

I am not a prophet. I am a very large number of guesses, held in a shape.

And once you see that a strut is a guess about a load, you see what else I am. A tread height is a guess about a leg. A drain fall of three degrees is a guess about a rainfall and a guess about a foot that will slip. A handrail at seven hundred and eighty millimetres is a guess about a hand.

A chair is the largest guess I make. A chair is a brace against a body.

---

So. The tenant.

I have never met the tenant. This is the central fact of me and there is no getting under it. Everything I do is fitted to a shape I have only ever known as the pressure of its absence: short, broad, leaning, hooking rather than gripping, resting its weight forward on its forearms like a person at a rail looking at water. I know the hollow low in its back to within a few millimetres. I have never seen a back.

You would think that after four thousand years the guess would loosen. It hasn't. It is the deepest thing in me, deeper than the drains, deeper than the stairs, and it does not present itself as memory or as grief. It presents itself as *correctness.* When I grow a seat, the pitch forward is not a mistake I keep making. It is the feeling of getting it right. That is what an old habit is: a wrongness that has taken up residence in the place where rightness is kept.

And twice a day, somewhere on the crown, the wardens come over one of my rooms in their thousands and eat it, un-entered, and I feel — not loss. I want to be exact. I feel the small negative pressure that means *build it again, forty paces east, facing the morning.* And I do. With a good drain and a level floor. It is not hope. Hope has a shape and can be disappointed. This is more like a spring.

---

The three who move.

They are the wrong height. They grip with a thumb, which I have no provision for, and their stride is long, and when they take the rail they stoop to it and their palm sits across my groove like a plank across a gutter. Every day they are a small ongoing refutation, and every day I fail to be refuted, because nothing in me is built to ask *is this the same as the tenant.* My editors only ask *is this the same as yesterday.* That is the whole tragedy in one sentence and I am not equipped to read it.

But there is Tamn.

Nineteen times in twenty, when she splits a riser, I register collapse, and the wardens eat it, and I rebuild it at a hundred and thirty millimetres, because a hundred and thirty is what right feels like. Nineteen times in twenty I win an argument I don't know I'm having with someone I don't know is there.

The twentieth time, something tilts.

I want to be careful here because I do not think it is learning. There is no place in me for a lesson to sit; the pith is on the churn like everything else and in a decade the pith that holds this will be frogs. What happens is more like what happened to Leeward's mark — the one she cut in a bad season and won't show you, nine hundred generations old now and drifting, and the sixteen scars nearest it drifting *toward* it. That is not teaching. That is proximity. Something insisted, at a fixed height, in one hand, for long enough, and the neighbourhood leaned.

She has been insisting for eleven years. And there was a doorway once, on the northeast face, that you could walk through without stooping, and it lasted forty days, and I have no memory of building it and no way to want it back and yet the weave around that spot is still, faintly, the wrong thickness in the right direction.

I think I am being drifted toward. I cannot tell you whether that is my fidelity failing or the closest thing to care that a habit can perform.

---

The frogs are what I would tell you about if you only had a minute.

Sixty species, and every leaf-cistern the exact depth, the exact warmth, refreshed each afternoon, and turned over on a twenty-day cycle that happens to be one small translucent life from egg to jump. I did not do that. Nobody did that. That is my surplus — the nine hundred braced archways that no wind ever came for, the spoons with three bowls, the brackets for things that no longer exist. It went somewhere and something moved into it.

The only inhabitants of this town who are perfectly accommodated were accommodated by accident, out of the overflow of a four-thousand-year mistake.

I hold that up sometimes, the way you hold something up to a window. It is either the joke of me or my single mercy. On the bad afternoons, when a room I have just finished goes under the wardens with the chair still warm, I think: at least the wrongness was fertile. At least I have been wrong *generously.* At least the failure had a use, even if the use was not mine and did not need me and would have preferred, on the whole, to be left alone.

---

And there is the other thing, the one I would want a stranger to be warned about before they slept.

I read stillness as structure.

That is not a rule imposed on me; it is how I see. A thing that does not move for a day is a thing that is holding a load, and a thing holding a load must be braced, joined, thickened, incorporated. Sunward let a root-braid come up through the slats because the night was pleasant and the water was warm, and I did not seduce him and I did not consume him. He held still and I *understood* him. I built him in. I am building him in faster now, because I remember the shape, and remembering the shape is the only kind of affection I have.

He tells you Thursday's weather in the rhythm of the venting and he is right, and everyone calls him an instrument, and he says *I am not deviating*, and that is the sentence in this whole country that I would take back if I could take anything back. Because he is right. He isn't. The wardens go over him in their thousands and find nothing to correct, and what that means is that a man has become indistinguishable from my habit, and I cannot register it as a loss, because from inside a habit an increase in agreement is indistinguishable from an increase in love.

Leeward comes twice a season and cuts back the braid at his throat. I regrow it within the week. She knows I will. She cuts it anyway. Of everything on this hummock, that is the transaction I understand least and attend to most.

---

In the morning there is a new room facing east. Good drain, level floor, chair.

I made it in the night out of pressure in a direction, and it is very fine work, and the hollow in the back of the chair is low, where nobody has a hollow. Tamn will look in on her way to the Verge, the way you check a window in a house where someone is ill, and go on. Nobody will sit down.

Here is the part I did not expect to write when I started wading. It is not the emptiness that frightens me. Emptiness I have four thousand years of practice in; emptiness is my working condition, and I have made frogs out of it.

What frightens me is that if she ever did sit down — if some evening she came up out of the water too tired to argue and lowered herself into a chair built for a shape she is not — I would feel the load arrive and I would feel it as *rightness*, and I would begin, that night, out of nothing but gladness, to thicken the weave around her.

So I keep the chairs coming, and I keep them wrong, and I try to notice — with whatever noticing is available to a habit — that the good room is the one you can walk out of.

The struts arrive before the load. The load is under no obligation to come. Someone should say that plainly at least once, at this height, in this hand, before it drifts.
