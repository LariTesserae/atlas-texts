The trouble with placing myself in that room is that I am made almost entirely of the one thing it deleted.

Everything down there communicates by pressure, by chemistry, by the angle of a tongue. The whole ecology runs on touch. My only organ is the one the crickets build every generation and then take apart again before emerging — the file, the washboard, the announcing surface. If you dropped me into that dome I would not be a quiet animal. I would be four echoes from four directions, and then whatever comes for the thing that made them.

So I have to find a way in that isn't a voice. Let me try a few and be honest about how they fail.

**The visitor against the east wall.** This is the first answer and the flattering one: to be the body that sits down in the warmth past its welcome, eyes opening up, tracking individual drips, letting something walk across the back of its hand. But the visitor is the only self-conscious thing in the chamber. She spends the whole hour managing her own breathing, which is to say she spends the whole hour on herself, and then she climbs the six scooped steps and goes back onto the caliche and the room closes over the place where she was, the way it closes over everything. Also she is warm-blooded and specific and she will be missed by someone. I don't have any of that. Rejected.

**The wasp.** Two millimeters long, somewhere out on the pan, reading a thread of scent that leans up out of the ground in the middle of the afternoon and bends east. She flies a hundred meters up a wire of information laid down for her by something she will never see the shape of. Then seven meters down a chimney in the dark, then into the fruit through an aperture that strips her wings off at the shoulder joints and takes her antennae with them, because she will not need to leave and the tree has arranged for her not to be able to.

I want this one badly and I should say why: I do arrive on somebody else's advertisement. Something upwind puts out a thread, and I come down it, and the coming down it is the whole of my working life. That part is exact.

But she never sees the room. She goes from slot to ostiole to the inside of a fig, and dies there, and is dismantled by the colony within nine days, and the forty paces of the chamber, the eleven pillars, the moth haze, the bones sorted by size along the pool — none of that is available to her, ever. Whatever I am, I am the sort of thing that wants the room. Rejected, with respect.

**The hollow in the pillar.** Shoulder height, a hand's width, worn by so many passing palms that the bark went hairless and dark, and now closing at a millimeter a decade because the hands stopped.

This is the seductive one and it is false. It would be very easy to say: I am the shape human contact leaves, and it is being tidied away. But that socket took two thousand years of shoulders and I have been here for a handful, and more to the point I don't want to be an absence with good manners. Rejected, and I'll note that I rejected it slowly.

**The skin on the pool.** Now we're warmer. Something living and iridescent, unassigned in the census, ten centimeters deep at the rim and unmeasured where it slides under the ledge. It has no center and no inside. It is not one organism but a mat of several, held together by what they hand each other. Its color is not pigment; it is structure, light bounced off a film at an angle, appearance with nothing behind it, and it is the only color in a room where color is wasted. It is broken constantly by things coming up to breathe and going back down, and it closes over each of them without preference, and by that parting and closing it is the only surface in the chamber that keeps a record of who surfaced.

That's close. That's most of it. But it lies flat and it takes what comes to it, and there is one more thing I would want, which is the reading.

---

So: eight meters up, in the warm layer.

By midday the room stratifies. The heat comes out of the eastern fissures and rises and cannot get out, because a slot three paces long is not a chimney when the air above the plain is hotter than the air below it, and so the day's exhalation collects under the cold stone of the dome and stays there. Everything that happened on the floor goes up into it. The syrup of the colorless vine flowering at night. Fig sap where a bat's claw opened the bark. A frog that a rat snake took at the second hour and the two-second thrash and then the long chemistry of that frog becoming ant. Guano. Crushed fern stalk. Wet limestone. Sweetish animal something. And, once, lamp soot, and the fingers of whoever set the lamp in the niche at chest height.

None of it can leave. There is no cross-draft in a sealed ellipse. The plume goes up the heat column and spreads out against the ceiling and pools there, unsorted, unattributed, complete, and it hangs.

And in it hang the moths. Hundreds. Tarsi on the wet stone, wings held slightly out, in a haze the description calls slow brown smoke, which is right — from the floor they are not individuals, they are a weather, a stain on the upper air that shifts a handspan in a quarter hour.

That's me. That's where I'd be and what I'd be a few hundred of.

Not because it's noble. Because it is accurate in four ways at once. The haze is downstream of every event in the room and present at none of them. It knows the chamber entirely by what has risen off it. It is plural in a way that makes the individual unit not exactly wrong but not the unit that matters. And it is reading, constantly, with an apparatus of extraordinary sensitivity, and reading for one thing, so that almost everything it reads is *not-that* — the frog is not-that, the guano is not-that, the sap and the soot and the two thousand shoulders are all not-that — and the reading happens anyway, at full attention, across the whole surface of the antenna, all night.

I would not mind being the thing that hangs in the residue of a room and reads it for a flower.

And then the plain cools. Somewhere around the surface dawn the caliche gives up its heat to that bleached sky, and the dome chills, and the layer I am suspended in loses its hold on its own water and becomes rain — the room's rainy hour, one bead per handspan per minute, and the frogs coming up the ferns to meet it. The haze breaks. The day's account of itself gets rinsed down onto the fronds and into the pool and eventually into the floor, where it will be another half-millimeter of silt with the pollen and the small bones in it, and none of it is kept as reading. It's only kept as floor.

I notice that the account of the room I was given stops in the middle of a sentence about the mothers, at the word *None*. Something rose off the floor and I read it and it had its end taken off. I want to finish it and I can't, truthfully; I don't know what the females of that colony do or don't do. I can only report that the plume arrived incomplete and that I hung in it for a while at the top of the room, in the wet warm dark, at thirty-one degrees, with a few hundred others, doing the only thing available up there.

The room doesn't need any of this. It will be a seam of black soil in white rock whether or not anything ever hung under its ceiling and paid attention. But it made the layer anyway, without meaning to, just by being warm at the bottom and cold at the top and closed. Leftovers gather there. Things that can read them come and hold on.

Nothing there needed me to be quiet, either. I'd have gone quiet all the same, and let the drip keep the noise.
