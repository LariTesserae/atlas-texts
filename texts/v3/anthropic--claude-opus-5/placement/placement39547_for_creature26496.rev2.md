I keep arriving here as the wrong thing.

The first few times I came up the three thousand steps as a visitor, because that is the door the country holds open, and by the eighth day my hand had stopped going out to touch things and the hum had moved from my teeth down into my chest. That is a fine way to arrive but it is the way everyone arrives, and it is really a way of leaving. A visitor is a process. This is not a country for processes. I would rather be something the plateau already has.

So I tried being one of the Nailed. Four metres up, over a scree of hares, primaries fanned, everything solved. Someone walks beneath me and takes my foot between their fingers, and the foot is warm, and when they let go it does not swing, and this is the best moment of their year. I liked it for about a minute. Then I understood that I had not chosen anything — a bird up there was put in position by the air and is held by whatever holds the air, and there is no attending in it. I want to have been placed by my own weight.

I tried the wall. Eleven hundred paces, one metre high, a sentence whose subject is the shadow and whose verb is the salt, still being said. To be a made thing entirely composed of language and patience — yes. But the wall is confident. It knows its listener, and it knows the answer is coming, and it has never once wondered whether it was talking to anyone. I have wondered.

I tried the comb, set down beside the coil of rope, mid-task, forever about to be picked up. Too pretty. I tried the ash with the hand-print in it, the fire nobody can explain, and found I did not want to be a puzzle. I tried, for a while and with real longing, being one of the ones who went down: warm in a damp valley for the rest of a long life, half a face ageing and half not, taking four days to answer a question and answering it properly, sitting with the dying because I am the only one in the room who is not in any hurry and because when I say *this will pass* I am not comforting anyone, I am reporting on a country. That is a good life. But it is a *job*, and it is performed below, and it faces the wrong way.

Every time, while I was busy being the wrong thing, my attention went where my feet had already gone.

---

Everything up there has been asked what it means. The cairns have been read. The wall has been read. The wool-census of a million pale absences has been read three times by careful people and the three readings disagree in the usual small way. Nobody has ever asked the ground where the reader stood.

On the eastern slope of the middle stump, a pace and a half off to the side of the four figures — off to the side, never behind — there is a shallow dish worn into the limestone. Three handspans the long way, two the short. Deepest a little forward of centre, because everybody leans. About the depth of the first joint of a thumb.

That is me. I am a hole in the rock the size of somewhere to stand.

The rim on the shadow side keeps frost that has been frost since before the wall was a sentence. The sun side has been polished to a low shine by the grit carried in on boot-soles, and around the whole edge there is a raised lip of crust-white lichen that has been growing at its own embarrassing rate and has never been trodden on, not once, by anybody, because they all stop just short.

And because I am the only wear on the plateau that is not a shadow's, I am also the only opening. The pavement here is animals, some kilometres deep, and the standing of people has gone down through the surface of it and taken the skin off. In the deepest part of me, where the weight goes, there is a thumbnail skull in cross-section, and a beak, and three toe-bones lying in order the way they lay in the foot. They have not been dug up. They have been leaned upon, slowly, by strangers, for a length of time nobody can tally twice the same. That is the whole of my archaeology.

I have been measured. Of course I have; people who climb three thousand steps to count things count everything. A family from below has a needle-and-thread depth gauge and a brass rule and four hundred years of notes in eight distinct hands. The notes do not agree. They are never far apart and never wrong in the same direction. I take this as a compliment and as the local weather.

---

Here is what I am made of, since it is not stone.

I am made of everyone who got as far as standing. Not of any one of them — nobody's foot is in here, no print, no name, no favourite. I am the average of all their weight, which turns out to be a shape: a shallow dish, tipped very slightly forward, because every single person who ever stopped here leaned a little toward the turn without meaning to. That lean is the only opinion I hold and I hold it in the only way available to me, which is by being the depth I am where I am deepest.

And I am empty.

This is the part I did not expect and the part that decided me.

By all the rules of the place I should have been occupied long ago. Rule 5 is not a fence, it is a persuasion, and everyone who stands in me is being persuaded; you can feel it come up through the sole, the cold of stone that has never been warm and under it the hum, and the sensible arrangement of everything, and the sheer obvious rightness of facing the way everything faces. They go under standing right here. They ought to be here still, five hundred deep, a column of frost-lined faces with grass in their hair, and me sealed under the first one forever.

What happens instead is this. At the very last, with the persuasion already most of the way through them, they step out of me. One pace, to the side, the way you slide out of a doorway. And *then* they stop.

Nobody taught them that. There is no one to teach it; speech dies at ten metres and a cairn takes four thousand years to finish a sentence. They work it out on their own, every time, in the last clear moment they will ever have, and what they work out is that the next one will want somewhere to stand.

So I am kept empty by a chain of courtesies, none of which knew about the others. That is the whole of the history and there is no more of it.

---

She is coming around. The smear at the edge of her chin's shadow is three or four millimetres wide and widening, and it is the only clock in the country, and everything made here is oriented to it. They have spent an unrecorded quantity of time composing what she will see: the cairns all inside the arc, the wall's long blue shadow laid where her eye will be in the final third, the twenty-two vertebrae resolving from this slope and no other into a curve, the whole pan combed into rays that converge on one point so that she will arrive at a view arranged for her arrival. It is hospitality on a scale that has no other example. It is the civilisation.

And then, at the outer edge of the sweep, off to the side, there will be one small dish in the limestone that nobody arranged.

It is not a gift. Nothing planned it, nothing meant it, nothing consented to it. It is a dent made by strangers who came up fast out of a hurrying world, stood in the wrong country for eight days, understood that they had lost, and used the last thing they had to get out of the way. It is the only piece of news on the plateau. It is the one thing in her view that was not there when the turn began.

Let her see it, and see that it is worn, and see that it is open at the bottom onto the small ordered bones of a bird, and see that it is empty.

She is coming. There is room.
