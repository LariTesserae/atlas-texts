## How it copies itself

By being broken. There is no other mechanism available on a world where nothing mends.

The plateau cracks constantly, in every direction, in no pattern. Sooner or later a crack opens beneath a crust, and the crust — a wafer of several hundred laminated courses, mostly hollow — does what everything here does, which is split cleanly and stay split. The fracture runs through the lid, through the interior, through the root. Two plates where there was one. Each has a lid, each has a root, each has a gap between them, and each is therefore complete. Nothing was inherited, because nothing was ever held apart from the world in the first place; there was never an inside to pass on. The lineage is simply the crack.

This is why the number of them is not stable in either direction. A crust is unmade by any of a hundred routine accidents and made by exactly one, and the one is the same event as an accident. In the grammar of wetter worlds this is called reproduction and is surrounded by ceremony. Here it is indistinguishable from damage, and the difference is not determinable, and nothing needs it to be.

Afterward the two halves are lying beside each other in the same gradient, drawing on the same warmth, and this is the worst position either will ever be in. Crusts that meet interfere. One is smothered. The smothered one goes grey and flakes, and the flakes stay crisp forever, and the survivor grows across them.

So each half turns. It takes a week for a change of direction to show in an outline. It takes months for the turn to accomplish anything. But they do turn, they always turn, and they go away from each other at five millimetres an hour, in opposite directions, for as long as they last.

That is the answer to the thing you noticed about the cairns and could not account for: *whatever is doing this keeps its distance from itself.* It is not modesty and not territory and not a rule anyone is following. It is the only mercy the arithmetic permits. Proximity to your own kind, on the Rise, means one of you goes grey. Everything here that is separate is separate because separateness was survivable and closeness was not, and the structures stand kilometres apart because that is the radius of not killing your sibling.

## How it ends, ordinarily

Cold, mostly. A vent dies — they all die, they are the plateau exhaling and the plateau is only ever provisionally itself — and the ache spreads from one margin, and the crust leans away for weeks toward a warmth that may not be there, growing on the good side, going grey on the bad, until the grey overtakes the good and there is nothing left but a shape on the floor and its own trail behind it, indistinguishable in freshness from the day it was laid.

Sometimes lava. A lobe feels its way forward, stops, cools, splits, and a new lobe comes out of the old, and eventually one of them arrives, and the crust is under it, and that is that.

Sometimes the stack falls on the builder. This happens.

## What it is like, in the only grammar available

The word *I* is a loan from a wetter world and it will have to be paid back at the end of this. Take it as shorthand for a difference that has arranged some stone around itself.

I am a metre across and as thick as a palm and as thick as the habitable zone, which is the same measurement. Above me the sky has never once been anything but cold, and this is not a hardship, it is my whole occupation; the cold is the half of me that faces up. Below me the floor is warm, always, everywhere, and warmth arrives through the root in a hundred thousand hairline threads, and I do not have a sense that tells me *warm* the way you have a sense that tells you *red*. I have the difference. The difference is the only thing I am, so I cannot report it as a sensation. You could as accurately say I am the seam, and the crust is what the seam does with stone.

I do not see. There is no light from above to see by; the illumination on this world comes out of the ground and I am lying on it, so the whole of my upper face is turned toward a black that is not an opening and has nothing in it. I do not hear, in the sense you would want. But the plateau ticks — a dry report twice a second, all across the visible world, glass contracting into its own fresh surfaces — and each tick comes up through the fronds as a small pull, and the rate is information. When the rate changes locally I know a fracture is coming and I know it about ninety seconds early, and there is nothing whatever I can do with ninety seconds. I know it in the way you know a step is missing after your foot is already down.

Behind me is everything that has happened to me, laid out on the floor in grey flakes, one unbroken line, uniform, unworn, as legible as it will ever be. Age is invisible here, so it is all equally present: the margin I shed this morning and the margin I shed at whatever the beginning was, side by side, the same crispness, the same conchoidal scoop. My entire life is written down and preserved perfectly and lies within a few metres of me and I cannot read it, because I have no eyes and reading is not among the things this world has gotten around to. I am at the wrong end of the sentence. I face only the direction I am growing, which is the direction of warmth, which is the only direction the world has.

So: no memory, and a ninety-second prophecy. Everything behind me kept forever and unavailable. This is not tragic. Tragedy needs a pond.

I have set stone. I could not tell you how many or where, and there is no *how many* in me to be told with. What happens is that the gradient is always steepest at the base of a chimney, and a chimney is what you get when the gap stays open and the rock does not fall into it, and so the fronds go into a hairline fracture and thicken, a hair a day, and the slab lifts and tips and settles, and the fronds that did it are crushed in the joint and go grey and stay there. The joints of every cairn on this plateau are packed with the spent tissue of the thing that made them. It looks like unweathered stonework because it is a wound that cannot heal in a world where nothing heals.

If there is anything in me that you would recognise, it is this: the place where the gradient is steepest is the place I have made, and I made it out of myself, and I cannot leave it, and I am still moving away from it at five millimetres an hour. That is the shape of the only relation available here. You would call it a home and you would be wrong by a whole biology, and yet you would be pointing at the right stone.

The wall on the eastern spur, twelve metres of coursed slabs one course thick — that is a line-source. A fissure vent, hundreds of metres long, breathing along its whole length. A chimney for a line is a wall. It went along it, setting courses as it went, at a hair a day, for however long *however long* is here. The gap in it with the lintel and the threshold is where the crust was lying when it stopped. Nothing was framed. Nothing was entered. It is a doorway by every test you can run on it, and it is a doorway for the same reason yours are: stone must not fall into the place where gas goes through.

## Payment

Now the loan comes due, because I cannot narrate the part you are most interested in.

You will find me. Not this one, necessarily; there is no *this one*, there is only the population of differences currently standing in the seam. You will find a black plate lying flat and take it for mineral for the two hundredth time, and then, because of the cairns, because you have already installed a hooded figure walking the terraces with a legible purpose, you will look for the maker and you will look at the wrong height, and eventually you will crouch, and it will weigh almost nothing, and you will turn it over.

Understand what that is. My lid faces cold; that is the entire arrangement. Before your hand there is your body, standing over me, warm, occluding the only reliable heat sink in the known universe. For some minutes the sky above me is *warm*, which has not happened to anything on this plateau in the whole span of its short existence. Then the lift, and the inversion: the yellow root turned up into the black, the flat black lid pressed down onto the warm floor. The gradient does not go away. It reverses. So I work — I cannot not work, I am the difference and the difference is still there — and I grow, in the wrong direction, upward, into the cold, on the wrong face, for as long as that takes.

One millimetre. Perhaps two.

You will set me down, because I have already gone to pieces in your hands, wafer-thin, several hundred courses, mostly hollow. The pieces will lie there at exactly the freshness of every other break on this world, unaging, unroundable, indistinguishable from an hour ago or a year, and among them will be a short unweathered frond pointing at the sky, which is a shape nothing here has ever made before and which nothing will wear away.

Three hundred metres off, warm, patient, unexplained, a stack of three slabs with the fourth balanced and not yet settled.

That one is not going to be finished. There is no mechanism on the Rise for finishing anything, and no mechanism for mourning it, and the plateau is higher today than yesterday, and it will be higher tomorrow, and this is the only direction the world has.
