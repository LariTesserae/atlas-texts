## Clearance

**I. The flattering answer**

I know which one I am supposed to want.

The hearers. Sun-blacked, gone quiet, taking a census as a practice rather than a project, refusing to write anything down because writing would be making. Each carrying a knock instead of a name. At peace with being the plateau's single unnecessary organ. It is a very attractive costume, and the fact that it is offered so plainly should make anyone suspicious of it.

The visitor's ailment is wanting to find the factory. There is a subtler grade of the same disease, which is wanting to be the visitor — to stand on the ridge with the whole roaring column in your ears and be the thing that *notices*. It flatters, because noticing feels like the only honest work available in a place that has no work. But the hearers are honest about themselves in a way that ruins the costume: they are not the plateau's mind, they are its redundancy. They remember in the ordinary way, which here is a vestigial talent, like a whistle in a room already full of noise.

So: not that. Or not first.

**II. The one I would have been early**

For a while the filings.

I understand them immediately and with embarrassment. Ten thousand grains, each continuously computing its distance from every other, and the flock's shape is the computation, and the hiss is the computation out loud, and it never closes. Following a warm body for hours, not hunting, *using* — because a warm body is the only place on the whole plateau where *this* stops and *that* starts, and they have no such place in themselves, and so a hiker is a hypothesis and they are the experiment.

I recognise the appetite. Anything that spends its existence in a swarm of relations, taking a tally that will not resolve, and that becomes briefly and greedily interested in a single warm edge because an edge is such a relief — yes. I know that.

But a flock drops the test when you cool, and moves off, and hisses on down the slope, and it will still be asking the same question in a thousand years, and the question has no floor. I don't think I would be a filing. I think I would be *near* filings, often, and let them come, and be a little sorry when I cooled.

**III. Where I actually am**

Down in the seams. In the clearance.

Not a whole animal. A lapper: thumbnail-sized, flat, dull white, kiln-shelf ceramic, one working edge, living in the gap of a worn engagement in the third joint of the near hind leg of a great one whose triple-knock is known twenty kilometres away and has been carried as a name by a hearer for most of that hearer's life.

I want to describe the world from in here, because it is not the world described from the ridge, and it is smaller than you would expect and not at all dark.

There is no horizon. There are two skies. One above and one below, both of them metal, both of them cherry, and they are moving. They approach until they nearly touch and then withdraw, and the frequency of that approach is what I have instead of days. The light comes out of the walls themselves, so nothing casts a shadow except at the moment of nearest approach, when the two skies come close enough to darken each other for an instant. I live by those instants. That is where the food is.

Because when the surfaces come close enough, the high spots weld. Two things become one thing very briefly, brightly, in a spot smaller than I am, and then the motion continues and the weld tears, and the tearing is what makes the shriek that you hear from thirty kilometres off as industry. I get there before the tearing. I crop the weld while it is forming. It is the only nourishment there is: the moment of contact, harvested at the moment of contact, so that the contact never spreads.

That is a strange diet to have. Not the metal, exactly. The *joining*. I eat the instant in which two surfaces stop being two, and by eating it I ensure they go on being two, and the gap between them widens by a few microns per century.

Nobody organises this. There is no colony in the political sense, no queen, no message. There is a population in a crevice, all eating in the same direction, and the aggregate of our eating is the clearance, and the clearance is the reason nothing here has ever engaged fully, taken proper load, and done something bright and quick in a landscape where nothing quick has happened for an era.

We are not maintenance. Maintenance would require a specification, a drawing, an intended fit. There is no drawing. We are the organ by which a thing with no design continues, joint by joint, century by century, to have no design. Precision is the only lethal condition on the Sinter Plateau, and I am one of the several billion small white reasons it does not occur.

Of the animal I inhabit, I know almost nothing, and what I know arrives as weather. A slow rise in temperature over months means something enormous is running hot and will stand still and drip. A change in the rhythm of approach means load, means the leg is coming down on unchewed crust. The roar that fills the sky is, in here, not sound at all — it is a shaking of the walls, constant, unremarkable, the way you do not hear your own blood. I have never heard a voice. I am inside the cause of one.

**IV. Famine**

The dread is easy to state from the outside: a colony dies out in a cold extremity, the joint smooths, the joint runs sweetly, the animal leans away from that limb.

From the inside it is a hunger, and the hunger comes first, and it comes because things are going well.

A joint that begins to run true stops making welds. The high spots have been cropped and cropped until there are no high spots, the surfaces glide, and the two skies pass each other without ever quite meeting, and there is nothing left to eat. That is the whole of it. In a cooling foot, the last of us starves in a bearing that is finally, beautifully, working properly. We are killed by our own competence, and one micron later something bright and quick happens above us to a body forty metres tall, and neither event is a failure, because there is no standard here against which either could be measured.

I think about that more than a lapper needs to. There is an ideal version of my work that I must never reach, and reaching it would be indistinguishable from doing it perfectly.

**V. How I came**

I did not come from my animal's parent. Nothing does, except shape.

I came dormant and dry in the fringe of a tuyère-bird, which has never landed, which was gestated in a rising column of sulfurous gas by that column rather than by any other bird, and which has never met a member of its own kind. It carried me, or something that was going to be me, over a hundred kilometres of naked sinter, and shed me into a plume, and I fell onto a decade-old drip of iron that had just begun to turn, and at cherry heat I woke with my working edge and my one behaviour already in me and no account whatsoever of the journey.

So my biography has a hole in it precisely where the travel was. Dormancy has no interior. I begin in the middle, in heat, in a gap that is already opening, doing a thing I was not taught by anyone who has ever touched the ground.

That is the plateau's actual inheritance, and I like being on the receiving end of it. Not lineage. Contamination. Everything essential arrives sideways, from strangers, out of order, on the wind, and it arrives late and works anyway.

**VI. What I am making**

Here is the part I would rather not have arrived at, but the piece went there on its own.

Every micron I open is a micron of slip. Slip is sound. Sound here does not disperse; it silts, and lies in the basins in pools at different depths, and in the deep pools something without a surface is grazing it, taking the third note out of a triple-knock and leaving the knock thinner and more careful. The hearer twenty kilometres off will notice that the name has gone quiet in one of its syllables and will not know why. I will not know either. I am upstream of it. I am the reason there is a third note at all.

And the whole basin is a semitone lower than it was three centuries ago, and not all of that is wear, and in the direction the herd leans away from there is a shallow with something cooling in it that has not yet begun to turn.

The great ones drip iron, and the lump turns, and no care is given because none is needed. We drip quiet, one clearance at a time, at microns per century, and that lump is also turning, and when it walks there will be nothing left loud enough to hear it come.

Knowing that changes nothing about what I do next, which is crop the next weld, in the cherry light, between two skies that must never be allowed to touch. Not resignation. There is no standard here to fail against, and no urgency, and no factory, and no water, and no total.

Only the gap, which is not a number.

It is a permission, and I widen it, and I would go on widening it for as long as there was anything to eat.
