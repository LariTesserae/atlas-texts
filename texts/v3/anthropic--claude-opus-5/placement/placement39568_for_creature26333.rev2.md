Start at two hundred metres, where the people read as posts, because that is the distance at which I arrive anywhere.

From there the first thing I want is the ledger-woman. Of course I do. She is the flattering one — the one who walks the spoil-banks in the flat light and takes up the black wet objects and writes *unknown* in a hand that does not hurry, two thousand times, nine of them wrong. Anyone would want to be her. But she has paid forty years for the right to write that word properly, and I have paid nothing, and the difference matters here more than it would matter anywhere else. In a place where lichen is the dominant civilisation at a millimetre a decade, duration is the whole of the argument. I don't have duration. I have a great deal of simultaneity and almost no continuity, which is the opposite thing wearing the same coat.

Then I want to be a leaner, and that lasts about ninety seconds, because the leaner is carrying a measurement it cannot explain and neither can I, and the resemblance is too pretty and too cheap. Eleven and a half paces. Some other people decided it, once, and then stopped being. I decline to be the animal in that story. I would rather be somewhere in the part of the world that is still deciding things, badly, on purpose, twice a day.

So: down off the moor, in through the covered lanes with the whole hydrology of the plateau running under the planks, past the bale-yards, into the works.

There is a room I keep coming back to.

It is not large — two and a half paces by three, turf over iron, a bench, a lamp at the height of a thumb giving its shell-light, a door that seals badly on purpose so the air moves. The walls are drystone. Running through them, in at the north and out at the south and out again east toward the fourth fog-hall, is the copper: three fat green runs meeting in a casting the size of a sheep's head, the joint, which is where the losses happen. A figure struck at the cableway head arrives at the joint having crossed three kilometres and one bad weld and is no longer a sound. It is a texture. It is grain, not depth — which is the same way these people see, and I don't think that is an accident; I think a plateau under flat cloud makes one kind of animal and the copper makes the same animal twice.

On the casting there is a boss where the metal has been beaten flat and then polished by use, and you put your cheekbone on it. Not your ear. The ear is for air and air here is a failure. You put the bone of your face against the bone of the town, and the figures come up through the skull, and they arrive enormous.

That is what I would be. The carry at Sixth Joint. Not the sender, not the reader, not the one who knows what any of it is for. The one who receives a weak figure and strikes it onward at strength.

---

It is a job for someone with a slow pulse and no particular need to be the origin of things.

You sit the whole shift with your right side against the run, because the run is warm, and by the second year the warmth has printed a band across your ribs that does not entirely go away; old carries can be identified in the bath-house by the stripe. You hold the striker loose. Knuckle for close work, brass for long carry, and the brass is never used above quarter-force because a hard strike does not travel further, it only travels uglier, and an ugly figure is worse than none. There is a saying, three words, flat: *strike what came.*

That is the whole ethic and it is harder than it sounds.

What comes, most of it, is ordinary. The long roll a quarter hour before turnover. Two-and-a-hold, *come*, eighty times a day, mothers to children, foremen to whoever owes them. Pressure figures off the settling tanks in a fixed grammar I could strike in my sleep and have. The fast triplet, twice in my first winter, both times a boy in the runnels below the moss-presses, both times fine.

And what comes with it, unavoidably, is everything else, because the pipe does not have private lanes. There are perhaps three hundred standard figures. There are a thousand or more that are family-held, and nobody taught them to me, and I know four hundred of them anyway, because a carry sits in the middle of the town's nervous system for eleven thousand turnovers and is not deaf. I know what the Erran house says when the grandmother has had a bad night. I know the two obscene figures the press-crews use about each other and the third one, much older, that they use about carries. I know that the girl on the east rim and the boy above the fog-halls have been tapping at each other through nine hundred metres of warm green metal for three years and that the whole town is pretending not to hear it, and that my pretending is of a different order from everyone else's, because theirs is a courtesy and mine is a professional condition: it goes through my head on the way to hers.

I strike it onward at strength. Every night. Unchanged. This is the part of the job that is actually the job.

---

Once I improved something.

A figure came up from the south run at the edge of the audible, in the minutes after a turnover when the casting is still shedding its clicks and everything is dirty. It was three-quarters of a race-crew figure and the last quarter was not there. I knew the figure. I had struck it four hundred times. So I completed it and sent it on whole, and a crew went to the wrong cut, and worked most of a day at a face that did not want working, and nothing dreadful happened, nothing at all, nobody was hurt.

The foreman came up to Sixth Joint at the end of it and stood beside me against the wall, shoulder to shoulder, both of us looking out at the same grey, and asked me flatly what I thought I was doing.

There is a figure for *unheard*. It is two taps, dull, deliberately unmusical, and it costs the sender a repeat and costs the carry nothing but the admission. It is the cheapest thing in the entire system and it is the thing new carries will not use, because they are young and they would rather be right than accurate. You learn. You learn it the way the ledger-woman learned to write *unknown* two thousand times without flinching: the discipline is not in the knowing, it is in declining to supply what did not arrive.

Strike what came. If nothing came, say nothing came, twice, dull.

---

Twice a day the whole network rings.

Every engine in Thimble takes its single stroke, the groan comes a kilometre wide with its edges sanded off, and every metre of copper in the town sings with it, so for four minutes the pipe carries nothing but the pipe. The carry cannot work. The carry sits with a warm stripe on their ribs and their cheekbone off the boss and their hands inside their sleeves, and is interrupted, entirely, along with everyone else, as a matter of manners, fifty thousand times in a life.

I think that is the part of this world I would actually want. Not the fog-combing, not the moss, not the long unbreathing sentences they write and would never dream of reading aloud. I mean the fact of being reliably, scheduled-ly, unremarkably interrupted — of being a thing that transmits, and stops, and is not offended by the stopping, and has never once in its history considered the stopping to be an interruption of anything except the noise.

---

Four years after the rod came up from the south cut, they brought a smaller thing out of the same bank and laid it out and left it, as they do.

A bone, hollowed, about the length of a forearm, with six holes down it and a seventh at the underside, and every one of them stopped with pitch, carefully, from the inside, by someone who meant it to stay stopped.

The ledger-woman measured it and wrote *unknown*, which was correct.

I went down and looked at it on the bank for a while, because my whole life is the question of what carries and what does not, and here was an object made entirely of the assumption that air works. Somebody came up out of the thick green world below with that in their pack. Somebody blew into it on this plateau, in half an atmosphere, under flat cloud, and heard the note go twenty paces and lie down. And then somebody — maybe the same somebody, later, having thought about it for a long time in the way this place makes you think about things — sat down and filled every hole with pitch, from the inside, so that it could not be tried again, and put it in the ground, where nothing rots, where it would keep.

I would have wanted to know whether that was grief or good sense. I would have stood there in the shadowless grey working at it. And then the long roll would have come up through my boots from the joint above, a quarter hour to turnover, and I would have gone back up the plank lane and laid my face on the polished copper and put my hands inside my sleeves, and been quiet for four minutes along with four thousand other people, and then gone on striking, all night, exactly what came.

Round the handle of the striker there is a thread of red. Everyone wears some. It is the one colour this place has never produced on its own — it has to be carried up from below, like the bone, like the grain, like every idea anyone here has ever had about what the weather is for.

Mine is worn to pink where the thumb goes.
