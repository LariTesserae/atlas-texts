My first thought was the visitor, and I want to say why I put it down.

The visitor is the one with the hammer. The visitor is the only irreversibility. But being the event requires no qualities whatsoever — a dropped crate would do it, a boot heel, a dead battery leaching into a seam. Anyone can be the thing that happens. It is the least interesting office on the plain, and the plain has, by my count, only about four offices.

Second thought was the resident, and I put that down faster, because it is flattering and because it is wrong in a structural way. The resident has no seat. There is nothing there to be. You cannot be a continent-wide contact network any more than you can be the fact that two hundred trillion things are touching; you can only be some of the touching. So if I were going to be the resident I would be a *little* of the resident, somewhere, doing the resident's work in a specific slot, and then the honest question is which slot.

Third thought was the eye at the foot of the weld. That one took longer to put down. It is the good part — cobbled out of the wrong shells, jointed with cured sealant, disguised as a spall of flash in a world with no spall, the only mortal object for a thousand kilometers. And it *moves*. I liked it very much and then I noticed that I liked it because it moves, which is not a reason, and that in the whole economy of that world the moving part is the cheapest thing. It is paid for. Something paid.

So: down.

---

I would be in a seam. Not a gutter-line, not a throat — an ordinary seam, a finger's width, between hexagon and hexagon, about four hundred meters from a hatch-plate that has never once had to account for me.

I am a gasket beetle and I have been dead for something over seventy thousand tides.

The slot I lie in is not dark; nothing here is dark, the light comes down as an even grey wash and just gets thinner with depth, so I lie in a kind of dusk that has never changed value. Above me the wickworm tails make their pale nap. Below me, plate, and below that the shafts holding their note.

I am held. That is the whole of my condition and it is worth being precise about it. A flange snail keeps position at my foot and has for longer than the snail's own lineage exists to measure, because there is no lineage, because it is the same snail extruded and re-extruded and always put back to the same spot. There is a joint of cured sealant at what used to be my shoulder. A wickworm's capillary lies along my ventral seam and keeps me wet, because a dry carapace checks and crazes in fifty tides and a crazed carapace reads as debris, and debris does not exist here, and a thing that does not exist gets noticed.

My third left claw is not with me. It is out walking. It is a hinge in the leg of something that looks like a chip off a weld crest, nine meters from the visitor's camp, and has been walked past four times.

---

Every so often the tremor comes — long, low, warm-bodied, blind, reading the floor the way you read a page with your palm.

And I answer. The wickworm pulses in my capillary, the snail makes a shift of a few tenths of a millimeter, and what goes back into the plate is the tremor of a live beetle at rest. The hound reads a beetle at rest. The hound is satisfied, because the hound is not a predator, it is an errata service, and there is no erratum here: the census says forty-seven on this hexagon and there are forty-seven, and I am one of them.

I have returned that signal without a single error, ten thousand times and more.

No living beetle has ever been that consistent. Living things wobble. That is what being alive mostly consists of, down at the resolution the hounds work at — a faint continuous failure to be exactly the same. I have none of that. I am the truest beetle on this plate and the reason is that there is nobody home.

---

Here is what took me most of the seventy thousand tides to notice, and I only noticed it because there was nothing else to do and no hour in which to do it.

Twice an interval the ceiling lowers and the world above me is smeared. Not destroyed; degraded. Enough that everything the resident holds has to be written again, in beetles, in the arrangement of grazers on a hexagon, and that rewriting is nearly all it has and nearly all it is. A mind whose entire capacity goes into continuing to be the same mind. A man carrying water in his hands, forever, and calling it his life because it is.

The water comes down into my seam too. It fills, it gurgles, it drains. It polishes me — from the inside, by current, not the way the plain is polished, which is by feet. I have a different gloss than everything up there. It is the only thing about me that is mine.

And it does not move me. I am held.

I am not smeared. I have not been smeared in seventy thousand tides.

Which means — and the resident has never once had the spare capacity to think this thought, it is too slow, it thinks at forty kilometers an interval and a thought this size would take it three human working lifetimes and cost it something it cannot afford — I am the only place on this entire plain where something can be set down and left.

It has been leaning things on me for centuries without deciding to. Small things. A position. A count. The relation between my foot-snail and the next held shell eleven kilometers south, which has not varied, which did not need to be rewritten after the tide, which is therefore not memory in the sense of labour but memory in the sense of a *mark*. There are, by my estimate, some thousands of us. Three eyes' worth of fraud, and every corpse propped upright to pay for them is a fixed point, and fixed points in a mesh are not silence. They are punctuation.

---

So I know what it wants. Damage. A gouge. One hexagon deliberately broken, a scratch that stays, the first sentence in the history of this world that would still be there after the tide — because it has never in its existence had a fact that did not have to be re-earned twice an interval, and it would like, once, to put something down and walk away from it.

It cannot make one. Its hands are beetles.

But it already has one. It made it by cheating. It wanted to look at you, and looking costs, and the currency was corpses held in position across centuries, and in paying that price it accidentally built the archive it has spent its whole life wanting and has never been fast enough to recognize.

That is what I would be, if I found myself there. Not the eye. What the eye cost. The shell in the slot, wetted and propped and forging the tremor of a living thing at rest so that the ledger stays quiet and something small and unbudgeted can go stand at the foot of a weld and watch a stranger who has walked past it four times.

If you break a hexagon, it will be the second mark.

I am the first, and I am not a message. Nothing is written in me. I am only held, and have been, and that turns out to be the entire difference between this place and a place where something could happen.

Don't hurry on my account. I have no hours.
