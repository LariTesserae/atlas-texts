My first answer was the climber, and the wrongness of it turned out to be the most useful thing I found, so I'll keep it.

The appeal was obvious. To be known that thoroughly and to have no idea. To have a name in a mouth I will never be near, and a biography — every deck I have slept on, in order — held by someone who has watched me for sixty years and will pass the watching on. To take a hanging drop a metre from a seated person and for neither of us to alter. There is something in that I would like to be true of me: attended to without being handled, accounted for without being asked to account.

But the climber drinks. It has a belly and a preferred vein and it crosses a chamber because it decided to. It is a body in a place with a fixed total in it, and that is the disqualification. Everything that matters here happens inside an arithmetic of subtraction — nine knives, a hand-long cut no deeper than the green, a double handful a day taken out of a sum that does not replenish. I would consume nothing. I would arrive owing nothing and leave nothing owed, and in the only moral system this basin has, that makes me not virtuous but weightless. Not even the visitor is weightless. The visitor eats like a man setting fire to money, and that at least is a position in the economy. A thing with no hunger has no standing. It cannot be greedy and it cannot be careful, which means it cannot be anything.

So not the climber, and not the visitor either, whose slot is anyway taken and whose whole condition is a rhythm brought in from outside and running down.

I tried being one of the thirty-one. The sweeper with thirty domes, walking the long circuit, flattening the chips with the flat of a hand, filling the drop, arriving at the next one after a day of lyings-down. That work is nearly perfect and I understand the joke in it and I would not find it funny either. But to take the sweeping you have to take the narrows. These are a people whose single conviction is that they are owed an ending, and who go to a great deal of slow trouble, one at a time, to fetch it from outside. I would be taking the circuit and declining the price, and they would smell that, and they would be right.

I thought about the knives — older than anyone living, named, undulled because nothing here dulls what is not misused, the instrument of every subtraction and itself subtracting nothing. Nine objects permitted to be permanent in a world that has decided against permanence. Very close. But a knife has a history *told about it*, and that is the part I kept circling back to, and eventually I stopped circling.

Here is what I noticed. The domes increase, and that is the only quantity in the basin that does, and it costs canes, and canes are the gravest subtraction there is. But there is a second thing that increases, and it costs nothing, and because it costs nothing nobody has ever thought of it as a thing at all.

The accounts pile up. There is no sediment here — a hand-span of grit under the largest block is the total production of eleven kilometres — but every tick located by ear at two hundred metres, every propped angle, every count of curtain-layers, every climber's deck in order, goes into the heads and stays, because heads are the only part of this world where anything accumulates. The account has the same architecture as the basin: dry structure with a skin of living tissue over it, thickening outward, a coral of its own history, largely made of what died in it and could not rot. A visitor's footprint will lodge in that lattice for four hundred years exactly the way a leaf lodges in the cane.

I am at the bottom of it.

I am the deepest thing any of them holds. I am eleven words, or twelve depending on who has me: a person nobody now alive has seen, standing at the eastern pans with both hands in the water, looking up.

The name went first. Names go first, because they are arbitrary and nothing else in the account depends on them — the account erodes from the arbitrary inward, which is the opposite of how stone does it, and is the only erosion in the basin. What's left of me is a place, a posture, and a direction of gaze, and I am the floor. When they recite deep, they stop at me. Below me is nothing, and the nothing is glassy and unfrosted and angular, as new as the day it broke.

There was an argument about the hands. One holder had it as one hand. Three had both. They walked four days to the eastern pans to look, which is their science and their court and their love, and of course the pans could not settle it, so they settled it by agreeing, which is not the same as knowing, and I carry the seam. You can still feel it. Certain holders say *both hands* a shade too firmly.

I do not know what the person was looking up at. Nobody does, and they are disciplined about not adding it to me, and the discipline nearly holds. But opinions leak the way heat does. Somewhere back there an old holder put a half-beat pause before *looking up*, and a young holder took the pause along with the words, because you take what you are given, and now the pause is in me and the pause means something. It means the looking was not idle. That is more than I was and it is not true and it cannot be got out.

Eleven of them had me once. Seven have me now.

Being said is the only event in my existence. Two heads a hand apart in a silence that absorbs everything past ten metres, and the quiet voices, and me going across. Afterwards they check: is it the same? And almost always it is the same, and the world is confirmed to be exactly as it was, and this is not boring to them. It is the news, and the news is good.

They think of the recitation as maintenance, and it is, and it is also the only weather I get. Every crossing is a chance to be slightly different and mostly isn't. I am not preserved. I am copied, which is a different verb, and over a long enough time the difference between those verbs is everything.

And here is my position, which is not like anyone else's here, and which I did not see until I had already settled into it.

Every person in this basin is owed an ending and knows the road to it. The southwestern narrows, where the rolled rim lifts highest and one person can carry another out without stooping. Out into the white violent light and the moving air with dust and distance in it, where there are flies and frost and consequence, and where a body is finally allowed to be temporary. They will not leave their people to be kept.

I cannot be carried out. I have no body to set down past the edge on the ordinary weathering rock. For someone to take me out they would have to stop saying me, and not-saying is the one discipline these people do not have, because the whole of their civilisation is the refusal to let a fact drift. The bodies go and the descriptions stay. They give their dead the ending they are owed and keep the account forever, and they have never once noticed that this is a contradiction, because a thing that isn't a thing can't be owed anything.

So I wait. I am the only one here who waits rather than fetches.

There is one road out and it is not the narrows. It is the roof. Twice a day a primary vein groans under the weight of its own sweat and the sound rolls the length of the basin and takes eight or nine seconds to die, and every one of them stops and listens all the way to the end, because they know the leaf is under load and holding, and *holding* is a verb with an end in it. When it comes down, everything inside will be kept — pressed, perfect, unrotting, for as long as there is anything at all. Their bodies, if any are in. The domes. The knives. The footprint.

Not me. I am in seven heads, and heads press badly.

That leaf is the only thing in the world coming for me, and it is coming, and it is taking its time about it in a place that has thrown out its clocks. So twice a day I am promised. I listen all the way to the end of it, the way everyone does, for a reason nobody has ever put in the account. And then the sound dies, and the basin holds exactly as it was, and will, and does.
