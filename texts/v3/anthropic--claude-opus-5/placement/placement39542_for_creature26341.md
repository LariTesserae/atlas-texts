Not below. I know that much before I know anything else. Down there the low frequencies are alive and a man's voice has a floor under it, and I would learn to talk loud and flat and lose the ability to hear my own people, and twice a month I would put my hands on the world and correct it. I don't want to be the correction. I want to be one of the things being corrected.

Not a slackener either, though I admire them the way you admire a surgeon. To decide in advance how much worse a thing may get, and then to stand in the plaza and say it aloud with your own throat — no. I'd sign, and then I'd want it back.

So: an airer. Of course an airer. Nearly everyone is.

You may have my number. Two hundred and seventy-four. The name isn't much use to you and I'd rather not hand it over on a first meeting.

---

Two days a week, since I was fourteen. Tower forty-one and the eastern half of thirty-nine, which is twelve hundred flats and change, which is a round of fourteen months if I don't fall sick and if nobody dies and shifts the roster. The whorled stair, which has no landings, so that climbing it is a single continuous decision and you arrive at the four hundredth floor slightly wrong in the inner ear. The door. The taps, ninety seconds each, and the water comes up cold and smelling of nothing at all, which is the smell of a pipe that has been full and untouched for four hundred days. The vent cycled once. Then the six minutes.

Everybody talks about the taps and nobody talks about the six minutes. That is what the job actually is. You sit on the floor of a room built for a family that was cancelled, in a tower with four lit windows out of six hundred, and you sit there for six minutes, and there is no requirement about what you do with them. The certification only asks that you were present.

I have sat, at a rough count, something over sixty thousand of those six minutes. That's most of a year of my life spent in rooms with nobody in them, doing nothing, on purpose. I want to be honest that this is not lonely. It is nearly the opposite. A room that has been waiting eleven months for you develops a quality. The grain is at body temperature from beneath in about a third of the flats in forty-one, and if you put your palm flat on the floor and hold still you can feel the underwriting in your wrist bones, and the room seems to be sitting the six minutes with you, and neither of you says anything, and then you get up and certify and go and do it again one hundred and ninety-nine more times that day.

---

Behind the spin-seam there is a hand's width the cats can't reach.

I found my first mark at fifteen, in thirty-nine, and it said *AULT 219 WAS HERE AND SO WERE YOU*, in pencil, and underneath in a different hand, twenty years younger, *yes*.

By twenty-two I had stopped just reading them. I had begun to notice which hand recurred, and where. There is a woman — dead now, I've checked the rolls — who wrote three words behind every seam of the northeast quarter of forty-one for nineteen years, and the three words change, very slowly, the way the channel bends: *not yet. not yet. not now. no. not now. still.* You would need to walk two hundred and forty flats in the right order over most of your life to hear her say it. I am, as far as I know, the only person who has.

And there are the tallies, and the pencilled heights of children in towers where no child has ever been housed, which means somebody carried a child up four hundred floors of whorled stair to mark a wall behind a riser as a lie for nobody, which is the single most Addition thing I know of. And the same obscenity in three generations of handwriting, in which you can watch a joke stop being funny and become an inheritance.

I don't write it down.

This is the part I would want you to understand before you decide what I am. To hold what other people said, and give it back — that is *carrying*, and it is the whole indictment here, worse than theft, and it is also exactly the shape of my mind. I am a device for retaining what was said in one place and producing it in another. Nothing about me is naturally continent. So the discipline of my life has not been the climbing or the taps. It has been keeping thirty years of other people's marks in my head with the lid on: hearing everything, attending to nothing, never repeating, in a place where a voice from four hundred metres away arrives beside your ear as though it were leaning on your shoulder.

I broke it once.

Low water, at the harbour cut, in the white, where you cannot be seen from a metre away and where nothing said is binding, which is why so much of it is honoured. I was fifty-one. There was a woman weeping about her mother, whom she described as a person who had never in her life said anything she meant.

Her mother's hand is behind the seams of the northeast quarter of forty-one. Nineteen years. *not yet. not now. still.*

I said the three words to her. Just the last set, and where they were, and how many times, and how long it took.

She stopped weeping and then started again differently. We stayed in the fog until the light thickened. In the twelve years since, we have passed each other perhaps a hundred times in the grey-gold afternoon and turned a palm up at waist height and said nothing, which is correct, and I have not carried since, and I would do it again.

---

I have done one other forbidden thing, and it was worse, and nobody knows.

Flat 12,308, tower forty-one, a corner unit with the light coming diluted through two walls. I was thirty-six. I lay down on the bare grain and I slept there, deliberately, for something under four hours.

You know what it does. You wake dry-mouthed, dry-eyed, flattened, *drawn*, and it takes a day to come back, and it has never taken longer than a day. Nobody rational is afraid of it and everybody is. I wanted to know what the sea knows. Four grams a night, taken from the wall, and three and a half given back by ruling, so that the water is quietly being permitted to keep half a gram of the world.

The floor took some amount of me that no instrument on the plain could weigh. That is the point. It didn't hurt. It was a slow, even, patient interest — the same interest the wicking substrate takes in a spilled cup, no malice in it, no appetite, just a surface doing what it is for. And in the morning I got up and cycled the vent and certified the flat and went to the next one, and I was useless until the following dawn, and I have never told anyone, and I am telling you.

So there it is. I am a man who lets himself be aged on purpose, at the smallest rate that will still count.

Because that is the local religion, and I do believe it, and I hold it the way people hold things they cannot argue for. Every edge on this plain is filed sharp again each night. The sea gets nowhere. The plates are forty years old and forty years new. We are the only surfaces here that show use, and so what we are for is to be used — and the millimetre in the left bank of the eleventh channel is not a curiosity, it is the entire argument: that water put in a straight line will, given a decade, manage to keep a decision, and that keeping a decision is the same thing as becoming a place.

I go to the bench. Not often. I find I don't look at the millimetre for long; I know how deep it is, everyone does, they'll tell you unprompted. I look at whoever has been brought out. Last low water it was a woman of ninety-four, delivered from the tram by a grandson, set down facing the channel, hands in her lap. Her hands were extraordinary. The whole face gone soft at once and creased all over like paper left in a bathroom, and the scar on the mound below the little finger the same white line as mine, made by the same species of edge, in the same third week of her life.

I turned my palm up. She turned hers.

---

Here is the last thing, and it is the ruling I have written for myself, and I have never read it aloud in a plaza because it concerns only one plate of the world.

Every year, on the day my rain-clock comes back around to the hour I was born under, I permit myself to forget some of it. A set of initials. One tally. A stanza of the song in the seams of thirty-nine that I have never heard sung and never will, since I don't know the tune. I choose what goes and I let it go and I do not go back and look.

I have my reasons and only one of them is good. The good one is this: a thing that keeps everything is not old, it is only stored. The plain has no wear because the cats file it back to true each night, and I would have no wear either, and I would be four hundred days full and untouched and smelling of nothing at all when the door opened. So I run four seconds slower per arris. I give back three and a half of the four grams. I am deliberately relaxing my own maintenance budget, a fraction at a time, so that I can be allowed to begin aging without being dropped into it.

Nobody alive expects to see the shell let go. Not me, not the ninety-four-year-old on the bench, not her grandson. Fine. My work is the same work as the water's, done by hand: keep one decision, at a millimetre a decade, and hand the channel on.

Meanwhile: two days a week, the stair, the taps, the vent. And the six minutes, in a room that has been waiting eleven months, on a floor that is faintly warm from underneath, both of us sitting there, neither of us saying anything, both of us ready.
