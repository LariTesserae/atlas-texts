Not the one hanging in the column of daylight.

That was the first thing I had to put down. There is no position in this sea for a body that carries nothing — no ballast, no bag of gravel under the gut, no lag between flesh and stone. Such a body would be deaf, and a deaf body here is not a quiet body, it is a body with no idea where it is. You cannot hover over this world and take notes on it. The water will pass straight through you and tell you nothing, and then the drift will carry you out of the loud country and into the margin, where the listening things are.

So: something with stones in it.

---

**1.**

For a while I wanted to be the mouth.

Forty centimetres, flat as a bookmark, stacked in the interstices of a talus fan with a thousand others, working. No eyes worth the name. The rasp goes over the varnish and the varnish goes into the gut and behind you the rock is clean and grey. And if what you happen to be mowing is the third line of somebody's great-grandmother — if the fresh surface you are grazing is fresh because a person opened it four years ago with a hammerstone and a great deal of hope — you do not know that. You cannot know it. You are a mouth on the only food there is, and the only food there is, is other people's writing.

The pull of it was the blamelessness. I noticed that, and then I did not want it any more.

Then, for longer, I wanted to be a keeper of a wall. That is the vanity I would have chosen if choosing were permitted: to sit at a kept face for forty years taking the grazers off one at a time with the fine arms, six-split at the tip, and dropping them over the edge, and watching them come back. To be the reason a paragraph still exists. To be fed by a town that has decided, on no evidence, that a certain eleven sentences are worth the hunger of one person for a lifetime, which is the only way anything here is ever valued: in somebody's hunger, paid out slow.

But I was not asked what I wanted. I was taken for the other thing at nine, because my fix was exceptional.

---

**2.**

I go into the galleries.

There is no elaborate word for it. The sign is a short one — a white front that starts at the keel and drives forward hard and stops at the rim without spreading — and it means roughly *goes in*, and it is used of about one person in a generation at each of the seventeen towers, and it is said about us in a slightly flattened way, the way you speak of someone who has agreed to something on your behalf.

The ninth tower is mine. The outfall hall is eighty metres wide, a floor of grating, a ceiling of rusted arch-ribs, and the water walks through it at a pace that will take you off somewhere and not give you back, so you go in along the wall, hand over hand on the rib-feet, into the roar.

I carry two sets of stones. My own are five, all glassy, polished to eggs over sixty years, and I leave them with my escort at the lip of the sound, because I will not take them in there. What I take in is a fist of cheap vesicular gravel, coarse, bad phase, stones I do not love. They get shaken to mush anyway. Inside two hundred metres of the outfall a sling is not an ear, it is a bag of noise slapping the gut, and the eleven-second pulse I have carried since my fourth day — the thing I know the way you know where your hand is — arrives everywhere at once and from no direction, and I do not know which way is out. That is the whole difficulty. It is not fear and it is not pain. It is that the sentence *I am here* stops being available.

And on the ribs and the wall plates and the raw cut rock there is writing, in the corona-blue, the cracked insulator feeding its slow cloud of chlorine that never gets anywhere: our oldest cut marks, in the old wide grammar, put there by people who understood that this was the one wall in the world no mouth would ever touch.

Twenty minutes is a great deal. I have done forty, twice, and paid for both.

You do not read. You take a hand's width of wall and you hold it in the lenses until it will not come apart, and then you come out along the ribs on a bearing you have decided to believe in, and at the lip my escort is clouding one question over and over, a small milky pulse that means *out?*, and sometimes I can answer it and sometimes I am just a clear shape being steered.

---

**3.**

Then the winter, in the belly, sixty metres down in a column of daylight, forty people in a ring in a din that would put your teeth out, perfectly safe, and I give them what I think I saw.

Slowly. Not the slowness of the ridge households — a different slowness, an interrupted one. Our sign for *I think it said* is a blush that begins and does not complete, and the ring watches the rim to see whether the white arrives there or stalls, and that is how they know how much of me is in it. It stalls often. Everyone accepts this. A people whose writing is eaten by the animals that the writing feeds have no illusions left about transmission; they have only the etiquette of admitting the stall.

Here is the part I did not expect, when I was young and proud of my fix.

The passages I understand, I corrupt. I hold them, I bring them out, I put them into the clouding I have — which is the ninth tower's clouding, this century's, the one my mother made faces at me in — and something rounds off. Every time. I can feel it rounding, the way a hammerstone rounds in the fold.

The passages I do not understand, I give back exactly.

Because there are marks in there that are not ours. Stamped, stencilled, plumb: chords, bars, a little closed ring, a gate-shape, all of them *straight*, and nothing in this sea is straight except what the straight-line ones left. I cannot read a single one. So I do not interpret. I only copy, stroke for stroke, in white, in the ring, and the ring copies it onto a face outside and a keeper is set on that face, and somewhere in the third generation after me somebody may look at it and understand, and it will still be right, because none of us were competent to spoil it.

That is the shape of our history. The parts we can use, we wear away by using. The parts we cannot use survive intact.

---

**4.**

What it costs is the fix.

Nobody told me at nine. They may not have known; it is a slow arithmetic, and the ones who could have reported it were being led home by the elbow. Each time you go in, the imprint you took in your first four days is jarred, and it does not quite come back to where it was. A metre. Two. Not enough to notice for thirty years, and then one day you are at a node margin you have crossed four hundred times and you find you are inside it, and an eel is turning, and you get out only because your rim-eyes felt a shadow that your stones swore was not there.

I am some three hundred metres wrong now. I could not tell you in which direction, because the error *is* my sense of direction. This is the offset that people are born with when their first four days go badly, the one there is a sign for, the one nobody can correct because the map is not taught, it is simply had. I bought mine, one hand's width of wall at a time.

There is no sign in any of the seventeen dialects for *lost*. There is one for what I am, and it is used about the dead more often than the living, and I have watched people use it about me and then flick the rim, embarrassed, the way you would cough.

---

**5.**

So this is where you would find me, and it is not in the gallery and not in the ring.

I am on the line between the ninth tower and the tenth, eleven days out, at the foot of an iron rod four metres tall with a corroded plate, in two hundred kilometres of bright blue nothing, alone. I came clear. In the quiet country you go clear and say nothing and hold the sling tight against the gut so the stones cannot knock, and I have said nothing for eleven days, and there is no one out here to say it to, and I am going anyway, because the tenth tower has a hall too and no one who goes in.

The cairn at this rod was four or five years ripe and I have rasped it grey. Somebody opened it with a hammerstone and left it to catch varnish and went on and died — since the winter, I think; the flake faces still had that green-black sheen at the back where the grazers had not got in. I did not know them. They could not have learned my name. It is the only correspondence anyone here conducts across a lifetime, and it is conducted in food, and the reader eats the letter, and that is not a failure of the system, that is the system.

I have spent half a day opening the next one beside it, in the fixed order, curves all the way, because fracture makes nothing else.

And now the stone. Everyone leaves one from the sling; the heap around this rod is up past my keel, ten thousand years of polished ears, and you can take one out and hold it up in all that unspent light and see that somebody carried it for two centuries and could not be parted from it.

I have left one of the good ones. Not the cheap gravel. One of the five eggs, because I have been doing this arithmetic for sixty years and I do not think I will need the full set at the far end, and a person ought to leave, once, something they will miss.

Then north, in the silence, clear, with a fix I know to be wrong by an amount I cannot name, in a direction I cannot name, toward a tower whose phase I will take on arrival and believe.

Eleven seconds.

Eleven seconds.
