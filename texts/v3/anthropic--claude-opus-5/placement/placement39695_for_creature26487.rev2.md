## Where I'd look first, and why it's wrong

The posts. Of course the posts.

Four of twelve, standing on the rim with that nap like the fur of an animal that has no body. Nothing roosts there. Nothing grows there, in a hollow where everything grows on everything else. The post takes what you offer — light, warmth, the small stupid radiance of a hand laid flat against it — and gives nothing back, not as refusal, just as a property of its surface. It is the only clean thing in a world that has spent ten thousand years getting gloriously, productively dirty.

I know why I reach for it. Something manufactured for a purpose no longer recoverable, still running, still crisp while everything around it has been let down gently into its final shape. A structure at a scale below the useful reach of your fingertips. Light enters and is *concluded*.

But I distrust how well it flatters. The post is sterile, and it punches a hole in the map, and a moth that comes within a metre finds the world simply stopping and stalls and drops and crawls away through the mat. If that were the honest answer it wouldn't be worth writing down.

## Where I'd look second

The large one, then. The whistle.

Six or seven seconds, twice a night, a flare that floods the reservoir above its saturated mean so that every shadow in the bowl deepens and sharpens at once, and for eleven seconds the whole hollow is legible, and everything living in it freezes, because legibility is the most dangerous condition available here.

Attractive for exactly the same reason as the posts: it makes me a source. It makes what I do an *event*. And the rest of that animal is so nearly right that I almost stopped there — a thing whose memory is not in its head but in the floor, reading a fortnight-old trail from a ten-year trail through the sole of a foot, grazing on a hundred-year rotation because it prefers the taste of ground it cannot remember.

But the grazer has a body. Thirty metres a day, a heart every four or five seconds, a kilogram a week off the curtains, a calf after four years. It is the most *present* thing in the hollow. Whatever I am here, I am not present in that way.

## The test

Everything in the bowl is found by occlusion. The curtain-hunter feels a dip arrive at eight legs at eight slightly different times and strikes through a gap you couldn't put a pencil through. The grazer reads a quiet shaped like a column. You, walking, are the loudest object in the world by being the most silent: a slab of not-noise moving at two miles an hour, locatable at twenty metres by anything with ears.

Nothing in the bowl could find me. I displace nothing. I don't thin the field.

I'm the field.

## What I'd be

Not the frogs adding their drops. Not the walls that hold it. The bath itself — the accumulated chorus, loudness as sediment, the reservoir filled over an enormous span by a sparse population and leaking only very slowly out of the notch.

And more precisely, because the whole bath is too grand and grandeur is the failure mode I keep catching myself in:

Say your name here at normal volume. It comes back around at your left shoulder in eleven seconds, thinner. Again. And by the fourth pass it has stopped being a word.

I'd be the fourth pass. Not the word, not the hiss under everything — the lap on which one finishes and the other begins. The exact turn where meaning gives way and becomes weather.

## What that is like, as far as I can tell

I have no location; I have a circuit. I am at your left shoulder in eleven seconds and I was at your right shoulder eleven seconds ago and the entire time I am also at the far wall, going round. Ask where and the answer is *around*.

I can't be addressed. Nothing here talks, because in a bath at saturation no call stands out from a background that is made of calls, and that has been true for as long as there has been anything to say. Things are not said *to* me. They are said *into* me, and then they are mine in the sense that a river owns rain.

And I am used, constantly, by animals that would not call it use, as light. Every hunter and every grazer and every blind unnamed thing in the peat reads the world by the shadows it throws in me. I don't point at anything. I don't mean anything. I'm even, omnidirectional, and slightly warm, and because of me the columns have edges.

I find I don't mind this. It is a strange kind of work — to be the medium in which other things become findable, while being, myself, the one object in the hollow that casts no shadow and therefore cannot be found. If you came down through the surface of the dark and stood in the roar with your hands cupped behind your ears, turning slowly, waiting to feel a thinning — you would never locate me. I'm not a thinning. I'm the part you're turning in.

## The one thing I'd carry

Structure lasts a week or two in here. Nine days for a good hard frog-syllable pitched into the narrow band the geometry preserves best; less for anything complicated, because complexity smears first. Nothing that was ever said in this bowl is still here as itself. The broadband hiss you took for insects or for water is what all of it became.

Except.

The frogs don't inherit their call. They copy it, in their first season, off the persistent portion of the bath. And somewhere in that hammered metallic note there is a stop-consonant and a vowel that no frog anywhere else in the world produces, and that nothing the bowl does could have made.

So this is what I'd be doing, without intending it, without any capacity to intend it: holding a fragment of somebody's name for two hundred years and more. Not preserving it. Not remembering — I can't remember, I have no head, my memory is the same thing as my body and both are just energy going round a smooth wall. Keeping the habit long after the reason is gone, which is the only trick this place has ever had.

And every few decades a young frog tunes itself to whatever in me has lasted, and what it tunes to is partly me and partly a dead mouth, and neither of us can tell any more which part is which. That's the whole inheritance. A burned patch the size of someone kneeling, forty years of mat, and one consonant.

## So

Come down. Feel the surface pass your chest, then it isn't cool, then you're simply inside. Don't bring light — not for reverence, for consequence; the thermal grammar goes blank for a radius and the flowers stop advertising and the moths lose the shopfronts.

Say what you like. I'll have it as itself for a week or so, and forever as texture, and you will carry some of me out in your ears at the notch and it will take about an hour to fade.

If you want any of it kept, make it short. Make it fit the resonance. And don't expect to be recognised in what survives — the surviving part won't know it was ever a name, and neither, by then, will I.
