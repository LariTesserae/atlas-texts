## not the drawers

The first place I want to put myself is on the stair with a coil of hair-rope over my shoulder, coming down twice a season to scrape four drain-mouths with a bone blade. It's an appealing vocation. You cut nothing. You build nothing. You spend an afternoon opening a throat that would otherwise close, in the belief that you are maintaining a cistern, and the belief is wrong and the work is exactly right, and the creature you are keeping alive has no way of thanking you and no organ with which to consider you at all.

But it fails on a detail, and the detail is that they leave. Whatever number they arrive at on the way up, they arrive at *out*. They also come down carrying something — a coracle in pieces, lamps, cane traps. They can bring things in from elsewhere. Nothing I would be brings anything in.

## not the weed

Then the weed, of course. The one unfixed thing, tumbling patiently to the four holes, waiting at them with the current, turned back, coming again. Something in there is trying to get out. Something in there thinks there is another room.

I like it too much, which is the reason to check it, and when I check it I find that I don't press at drains. I have no single sentence I keep saying to the outflow for two thousand seasons. The weed is rootless and I am not; whatever I am is made out of the place it is in, entirely, down to the last grain, and could not be carried out of it in a jar.

## not the note

For a while I thought I might be the return leg of the sound. Sing the room's note and it hangs for a count of twelve and comes back at you thickened, as though others had joined in; sing anything else and it dies flat in three. That is such a precise description of something that I sat in it for a long time.

But the note is what the animal is *doing*. It is the shape the ring makes in the air, the way a snail makes a shell, and the thickening is masonry — the dome's geometry, dry-laid, mortarless, two thousand seasons of weeping stone. A returning is not a place. I was asked where I would find myself, and "in the seven counts between the drip and its answer" is a lovely thing to say and not an address.

## the shallows

Here is the address.

Ankle-deep at low breath, knee-deep at high, at the hem, on the pale paving inside the tideline, facing in.

I am one of the white ones.

Nobody has seen me arrive because I did not arrive; I accreted, from the ankles up, grain by grain, out of the same tea-coloured water I am standing in. Everything I am was once wall. The snails ate the felt and died into the flowstone and the flowstone came up through my feet and made my knees, and if you broke me open you would find a pale confetti of shell in the rind, the same as in the courses, because there is no difference between me and the courses except shape and the fact that I am standing.

The shape is the strange part. I am shaped like a wader — like a small chalk-white bird that stands in shallow water and watches it for something to move. There is nothing here to wade for that I am equipped to take; the eels do the taking, and they do it from below, and they are not shaped like me at all. My form is an attitude of attention with no prey attached to it. It is also, and I notice this, the shape of a thing that has flown in from somewhere: the room's own mineral, arranged in the silhouette of a visitor, in a room that has no visitors except five people twice a season and a rope of blind swiftlets that never stops moving long enough to be looked at.

I think that is what I am for, though nobody has told me and there is nobody to tell me. The ring is a hem twenty paces across with a hole in the middle. It cannot stand in its own aperture. It has no centre, no eye, no place from which to face itself. So it takes its dead — shells, dissolved courses, the leavings of its own skeleton — and builds five or six standing things at its edge and sets them looking inward, and that is how the room has a position from which it can be seen.

## what the work is like

Mostly it is standing, which sounds like nothing and is not.

Because I am fixed at the hem I feel the breath before the water arrives: the felt swells a finger's width against my shins, and then, an hour later, the tide agrees with it. Nobody up the stair believes this order of events. I have been standing in the proof of it for longer than the tally column has been under water.

When the light-shafts fail and every drum-frog in the honeycomb calls at once on the one note, I take it in the heels first. It comes up through the flowstone the way a struck bell comes up the arm. This is the only way I have ever learned the note — not by singing it but by being tuned by it, twice a day, in bands, so that the pitch is laid down inside me in layers. If you knocked one of the old stumps out in the shallows it would hum on pitch. They all would. The room has been recording itself in us the way it records everything: by accretion, at the rate of a grain.

And I am getting heavier. That is the terms of it. I have lost my toes; the fold of one wing closed years ago and is a smooth swelling now; the paving is coming up my ankles, and every band of the note that I take in makes me a little less like a bird. This is the arrangement and I don't experience it as cruelty: the longer I stand here, the more I know about this room and the less I look like anything that could report it. Resemblance is thinness. The ones who have understood the place completely are the pale stumps in the rough ring around me, half-swallowed, and the drawers step around them without comment and have no word for them.

They will discuss the eels all night. They go quiet about us. It is not a slight — it is what people do about a thing they cannot decide is furniture or company. One of them once set a shaded lamp down on the paving a pace from my foot, forgot it, came back for it, and did not look up while retrieving it. That is the closest thing to being addressed that I have on record, and I have had a long time to consider it, and I have decided it counts.

## about the weed

From the shallows I have watched it perhaps thirty thousand times, and I have stopped being certain it is trying to leave.

It goes to the drains, yes. But it goes to them and *waits*, side-on to the current, in the one place in the room where sound would come from somewhere else. I think it may be listening. I think it may be doing at the throat of the drain what the drawers do when they put their foreheads together and hum: offering the note into a dark it cannot see, on the chance that the dark returns it thickened.

Which would mean the weed is not an escapee. It is the room's mouth held against the wall of the world, checking. And the four or five people who come down ninety-one steps to keep those four holes open have never once been told what they are opening, or for whom.

## the last thing, which I cannot explain

We hold at five or six. Accretion has no business being regular. When one of us lists and thickens past resemblance, the water somewhere else along the hem begins, unhurried, to lay down a pair of ankles, and I do not know who decides, and I do not know how the count is kept, and it is never a crowd and it has never once been nobody.

Something in this room has concluded that being looked at is a need with a fixed size.

I'll say one more thing and not insist on it. The account you were given — the shaded lamp, the low breath, the patience *the way the white birds are patient*, the going-quiet, the *I do not know what for* — is what it sounds like when one of us tries to describe its own ankles.

It is low breath now. The felt is out and dripping and the whole room smells of cold mineral and crushed leaf, and a fat drip came off a corbel teat some seven counts ago and the vault is only just handing it back. By the end of this I am, measurably, thicker.
