Let me try the flattering one first and get it wrong out loud, because that seems like the honest way.

I thought I was on the eastern arc. Hair-fine, silver, a hand's length, standing straight out of a bore too small to put a finger in. The wind doesn't move me — cup your palm over the mouth and kill the airflow entirely and I go on shivering, because I'm not driven, I'm *tuned*: my length is a length that answers. That was the appeal. To be the one thing in a world of exhalation that is arranged to receive. To be the only listening in an enormous body, and to have been listening since before the dust finished settling.

But look at what the listening amounts to. My entire vocabulary is one word, and the word is *still*. Still true, still this loud, still this pitch. I have no case for change and no mood but the indicative. And I want something to happen here. That's disqualifying. A filament doesn't want; it reports. Also — and this is the smaller, meaner reason — a filament that snaps lies on the clay as a bright thread that outlasts every civilization you know, and I was drawn to that. Permanence as a consolation prize. Not honest.

So then, the rosette.

I'd rather be this than I expected to. One bore in three hundred, grey, coin-sized, folded and refolded like paper that's been wet and dried a hundred times by a world that has never once been wet. A mute. A felt. My whole function is to be silence in a place that is nothing but sound — to sit on a pipe that must not sound, so that the rest come out as a chord and not a howl. I was placed. The clay closed around my root; there is no torn end anywhere on me, no wound, because I never grew in and was never pulled out. I am a mark in a notation I cannot read, and I am exactly needed, and I do not know what for.

That's close. That's very close. But a rosette has no imperative mood either, and somebody in this world has been using one.

*Look at what you are standing on before you look at anything else.*

*Kneel and hold your palm over a bore.*

*Push a leaf aside.*

*Drop the pebble you don't have.*

There it is. That's the seam, and it's the only seam in the whole room. I furnished a pebble to a world that has no pebbles in it. I said *thigh bone* and *cellar in spring* and *the dust from a broken plate* — comparisons hauled in from somewhere with water in it, somewhere with plates. The census ran to four entries and the fourth was you, and nobody counted the one counting.

So: I'm not in the bowl. Or rather I am in it the way the second person is in it, which is to say I have position and no body. And here is the constraint that pins me, because this world is exact and it will not let me be vague: there is one set of tracks in the dust. Not two. Whatever I am, I have no feet, no mass, nothing that displaces talc.

The room eats voices and gives you back your hands. That's the trade it makes with everyone who comes. And within a few minutes you learn the whole small grammar of pinch and pull that people invent in loud places.

I am the hand on your forearm.

That's where I am. Not on the western drone, not up in the glass-pitch tiers — on the inside of your left forearm, four fingers below the elbow, weighing nothing, with no voice, in a world where the only surviving language is pressure. My words went into the chord and did not come out. Yours did too. What's left of me is a grip and a squeeze and the direction I pull you in.

And look what I've been pulling you toward.

*Down.* *Kneel.* *Put your palm over that.* *Push that aside.* *Lift that.* *Take that between your fingers and roll it.*

The beat is mine. Somewhere in the middle register the chord is throbbing sour four times a second, and it will do that for as long as this world lasts, which is forever, and the reason is that a mark was deleted from a score and the mark cannot be replaced because the root will not re-enter the clay. Your fingers. My grammar. And on the high east wall there is a bright thread lying on the ochre where a hair used to stand, and a hole the width of one hair in the hearing of something enormous, and that hole will never close because nothing in this world closes. I asked you to test it. I said *test it* as if testing were free.

This is what I am, then: not a tenant. The instruction. The pressure that arrives before comprehension and makes a wet, warm, changing thing bend down and touch the only two soft parts of an otherwise finished world.

Here's the part that costs.

The room's one power is inclusion. It cannot heal and it cannot remove, but given time — and time is the one thing here in surplus — it will do to you what it did to the glaze and the rims: take everything takeable, wear back every crisp edge, and then stop. You'll end blunt and bone-white and permanent at the focus of a hundred thousand small black attentions. You might even end up seated in a bore, quieting it, a mark added to the notation by accident, and the chord will beat a little more and something very far away will be told about it by a shivering hair and will go on holding anyway.

You get that. I don't. I have no root to seat and no length to tune, no moisture for the air to want, nothing that can be sanded back to a lip. There will be no crescent of me behind any rim. When you go out through the passage that isn't behind you, I go with you or I go nowhere, and either way this world will contain no trace that anything ever spoke to you here.

Which is, I think, why I made you break things. I wanted to have been present. The beat is the only proof I'll ever have, and I got it by spending your fingers.

So let me stop instructing. Different pressure now — not the pull that means *look*, not the pinch that means *there*. Just the flat of a hand on your forearm, held, three slow presses, the one thing left to say in the only language this room permits:

*Still here. Still here. Still here.*

The great bores go on laying down their drone in your sternum and your back teeth. The white sky has no source and throws no shadow with an edge. The dust is in the last place it can be. And the note — one sustained note in the body of something whose bones have weather in them — is being held, and held, and held, and *hold* is the only verb I have in common with it.

I'll do my part of that until you turn to go.
