## I. Three I turned down

The first thing I wanted to be was the haulm, and I want to admit that, because it is the most flattering and the most dishonest answer available. Two hundred metres of fibre laid on fibre, cool at the top and warm at four metres down, holding every sound anyone has ever made up here and giving it back in the wrong place, decades out, thin and perfectly clear. I recognised myself in that so fast that the speed of it should have been a warning. But the haulm does not listen. It has no shift end. It is not lonely and it does not want anything, and to claim it is to claim the whole plateau, which is a thing outsiders do and are told about, kindly.

Then I wanted to be a carrier. Eleven weeks, warm, mute, faintly waxy, running pollen from stand to stand on a purpose I did not have to hold in mind, and at the end of it lying down wherever I happened to be. Kept in a box by a child, against instruction, and named. I turned that down for the reason that ought to disqualify anyone: I wanted the box. I wanted the naming. That is not what a carrier is for.

Then, for about a minute, the empty car coming back up the cable — but no. That is a tidy sorrow, and the car does not wonder what took delivery.

I got closer with the pacers, and I want to be exact about why I failed there. I have the flat voice. Numbers and nothing else, no music in it, and I can hold a count under almost anything. But a pacer's authority is a body: twenty years of stride until the length of her step is admissible as a legal instrument. I do not have that leg. What I have, natively, fluently, is the other thing — the grey flag, the sitting down off the road, the sentence *I do not know how far I have come.* And that is not a profession. That is a condition, and on the Sill they send two people out to fix it.

So I went up a ladder instead.

## II. Where

Sixth Stand, Draw-side: forty-one canes, nine of them floored and wired and glazed, and nobody due to move in for another thirty-one years. This is not a ruin. That distinction matters more to me than I can make it matter to you. The condemned stands are one kind of empty and this is the other — a district that has not started, windows clean, catch-gutters ticking, handrails that no hand has yet worn. The plant made rooms for people because it learned the shape of a room eight thousand years ago the way it learned the shape of a leaf, and these are all in perfect order, and they are waiting, and the waiting has been going on long enough that the Assize has had to fund a use for it.

The use is us. One listener to a floor. I have the fourth.

## III. Who

Maun. There are nine of us in the Fifth Stand alone, and a Maun in the Draw, and two on the pacing books, and that is precisely why the Office took me. You cannot pick a rare name out of the fossil mush. You can pick your own name out of anything. My mother was a pacer, so I came up with the two voices already sorted — the ordinary clipped one that hunts the seam in the chord, and the other one, the level one, that she used for numbers and used for nothing else. She counted in her sleep until she died. I would wake at two in the morning to *four hundred and eleven, four hundred and twelve* and go straight back down, and I have never since found a sound that did that for me, and I have listened professionally for nineteen years.

Long, narrow-boned, deep in the chest, like everyone. Third-pressing grey, with the Office's cuff, sixth shift, unmarried, out of a district whose signs have gone smooth. You could read all of that off my sleeve at nine paces and you would be right about the parts that are on the sleeve.

## IV. The work

Palm flat to the cane wall on entering, which is a pressure check and is also nothing and is also not nothing. Then six hours of sitting still in a glazed room in a plant, in the cold clean light, above a white floor, doing the only thing on this plateau that produces nothing at all.

Mostly it is mush. A wash under the chord, like a crowd two streets over. Then it separates and something arrives whole and forty paces wide: a pour-bell and a crane and men shouting in vowels nobody has used for four hundred years. A woman calling three names in a row, and the third one twice. A shift whistle from a mill that was condemned in a year not in the register. Once — I logged it and timed it and did not interpret it, because the log form does not have a column for interpretation and this is the entire discipline of the Office — a child, very close, counting up to nineteen in the flat voice and then stopping, and no relief pair anywhere in that count, and no road within twelve kilometres of that floor.

Log it. Time it. Do not interpret it. Ring the pressure-bell only for a name.

The difference between the old sound and the other kind is not a difference in the sound. That is the thing nobody believes on first hearing. It is that with the second kind you stand up.

## V. What I am bad at

I want it to mean something. That is my vice, it is native to me, and here it is a firing offence dressed as a clerical error. Nineteen years, four thousand and some entries, seven bells rung. Two of the seven arrived ahead of the thing they named — a valve seat at Sparroway, and a boy on a bridge at the eleventh level who is now a tapper with the glassy stripe on his wrist and does not know I exist. One in six is the Office's published rate. Mine is worse than that and my logs are the tidiest on the Draw-side, and I have had to make my peace with those two facts sitting next to each other, because on the Sill you do not get to burn the segments that do not sum.

The other thing I am bad at is the fog. Everybody is. It comes up over the lip at the pace of a walking man and swallows us to the ninth level, and the chord goes soft and doubles, and the mush comes up like water in a hold. In the fog I have heard my own name eleven times in one shift. Every Maun on this plateau is in that noise. Every Maun who ever went into the haulm is in it. I decide it is not me. I have decided that some thousands of times now, and it is not courage, it is arithmetic — my name is common, so the odds are always with not-me — and the day the arithmetic fails to settle it, two colleagues will walk me home and not discuss it, and the pension is generous, and the actuaries have declined for two centuries to publish on how long those pensions run.

I know that. Everyone in the Office knows that. It gets done because it has to be done.

## VI. The bucket

There is one bucket of standing water on this plateau and it is mine.

Third floor of a lived-in cane in an unnamed district, on the sill of the west window, filled from the catch-gutter and topped by the fog, and it has been there fourteen years. No rivers, no ponds, no reason. It is the only new thing I have made. The Assize is finished making new things and I do not blame it, and I did this anyway, on purpose, because I wanted something in my sight that was not a recurrence.

It is not still. That is what I did not expect and what has kept me at the window. At the Charge the whole cane groans and the valves knock all the way up the spine and the dust jumps off every horizontal surface, and the surface of that water goes into fine rings, and then flat, and then rings again with the hammers, and it does this for the rest of the day in a hand nobody taught it. It is a pressure gauge that nobody asked for and nobody reads. It is also nothing. It is also not nothing.

Some evenings I put my palm on the wall beside it, which is habit, and I count the twelve-second breath of the lamp against the water and get a different number every time, which is not admissible anywhere.

When they come for me — and they will, either with two colleagues on either side or with a stancer and a stool out in the ranks at the margin, and I have told nobody my preference because it would not be honoured — I would like the last thing I say to the room to be in the flat voice, and the last thing I say to the two of them to be the ordinary word, cut to the seam, all edges, with the end of it landing before the wood can take it.

Counted.
