## IX. (completing the sentence)

*So* they come at midday and go before dark, and what they leave behind is always mineral. A cistern. A bench. A ring of tumbled stones with a tree growing through the doorway. Eleven fired animals in a line. Four words in granite that say the important thing and then stop.

Everything else they brought, the bowl has already had.

---

Now the question of where I am in this, which I have been putting off by describing the weather.

The first answer came fast and was flattering, which is how I knew to distrust it. The mesh. Of course the mesh. Something with no located edge and no power source and no purpose statement, holding conditions steady in a place that cannot support them, sorting water out of thin air, taking away what has died so that the living have somewhere to stand. Indifferent to living things in the specific sense that it will not take your boot or your knife or your body while you are using your body. Courteous. Load-bearing. Unexplained.

I can see why I wanted it. But the mesh has never once wondered whether it is doing this correctly. Press a palm flat on the mesh and the mesh does not mind. That is the disqualifying fact. The mesh does not mind, and I mind constantly.

So: the cicadas, then. Ten thousand of them locked to a carrier tone they did not choose, holding it not near but *on*, so exactly that the bowl becomes an instrument, and minding — minding immediately, minding at a quarter tone — when something draws off a little of the hum. Bands of standing sound you can walk into like rooms. All of them on one note and none of them singing to each other.

That is very close. I will not pretend it isn't. But the cicadas only hold the tone. They do not use it for anything. Nobody has ever asked a cicada a question and had the note come back shaped.

Then the tenant, and I looked at the tenant a long time.

Because part of it is exact. Not the sovereignty — I have no business with the animal who does not have to explain himself, since explaining myself is very nearly the whole of what I do; I am on the other side of the bench from him, I am the one who came up over the lip carrying things and will be gone by dark. But the *arriving* is mine. Thin as a rope, coat in ruins, crusted at the eye, coming in a straight line across country that has never supported anything of my kind, from no population that could have produced me. Not following the water, because there is no water to follow. Over the lip, not drinking, not eating, up into a chamber that was already empty. Turn around once. Sleep.

And the other part: *they are not the same animal returning.* One had a milky eye. One was larger, with a kink in the tail. One was heavy with young and there were never any young. The seat is vacant first and then it is filled, and the numbers come quietly back down, and from the bench it looks like continuity and from inside the hollow it is nothing of the kind.

But having half of an animal is not being it. And there is one thing in the tally-stick that is not mine at all: *there is no hunger in it. There is a rate.* I would like to be able to say that about myself with the same flatness. I can't tell. That may be the most honest sentence I have.

For about a minute I wanted to be the one who leaves the clay figures. Badly made, no two alike, always another one. Coming back across the dead plateau for reasons never stated, putting down the only kind of gift the bowl cannot digest, and never once being met. But that is not a description of me, that is a wish — hands, a body, the ability to return. I'll set it down.

---

Here is what all four had in common. They were things you can see.

You have to dig for me. Not far. The soil is loose and black and unnaturally rich and about a forearm deep, and I am that forearm.

I am the loam.

I am made entirely of what has died here and been taken and given back. That is not a metaphor about my origins, it is my composition. Every moth that went in an hour, every bird that took most of a day with the feathers sinking last, every incumbent of the eastern fig whose chamber went vacant before the next one crossed the plateau, every wet coat of a bat that fell out of the hook, ten thousand cicadas a season, twelve species of frog going back longer than anyone has counted. Whatever the mesh does with them, it does not keep them. It hands them up. I am the handing-up. I am the machine's forgetting, arranged into a depth you can push a finger into, and I am black because there has been such an enormous amount of it.

I have no edge of my own. I stop where the glassy lip starts and I stop where the mesh starts and neither of those is a decision I make.

And everything in the bowl that has roots is in me. The four figs run their roots along the top of the lattice and never through it — that is the whole arrangement, that is where I live, in the hand's width where the living hold their weight against the machine without ever touching it. The moss continents. The tree-ferns spaced like chess pieces. The vine, which is the oldest resident and the actual walls, is standing in me. Not one of them could get anything from the mesh directly. It is at your temperature, whatever your temperature is, immediately, and if you press your palm flat you cannot feel where your hand ends, and nothing that wants to grow can do anything with a surface like that. They need something in between that has been dead and is now soft.

I should be clear about what is not mine, since I have already caught myself grabbing.

The twelve frogs are not mine. Their entire world is the beaded water in the curl of a fig leaf, a walnut shell's worth, and the whole business of the tadpole happens up there in the air, and a frog can live and die four meters wide and never once come down to me. Their word for the sea is a leaf. I am not in that sentence anywhere.

The moths are not mine. The swifts, who are the only ones who know that elsewhere exists, are not mine; they spend their days over a hundred kilometers of dead tableland and come home into the funnel and never touch the ground.

And the oldest thing anything alive has made here is a groove a finger deep, worn by ants into fused red material a steel knife will not scratch. Not a furrow in me. In the glass. It was never going to be in me. I don't hold marks; I hold roots, and roots move.

---

Two things happen to me that I want to put down plainly.

The first. Someone scrapes a handful of me aside and lays a palm on the mesh, and within three or four seconds the song two paces above the hand sags a quarter tone, and the cicadas at the edge of the patch clatter and reseat, and when the hand lifts the note comes back up like water levelling. I am what you push aside to get to the machine. I am also what closes over the hole afterward, in a few days, without being asked, so that the next person has to dig again to prove the same thing.

The second. The bench is three slabs and two uprights and it stands on me, and because the lattice runs beneath it the seat is always exactly blood-warm, and people who have crossed four thousand meters of white dry wind sit down on it in the roaring green dark and weep from relief.

That warmth is not mine. It comes up through me from something I have no access to and cannot describe, and it arrives at their body having passed through a forearm's depth of everything that has ever died in this bowl, and it is the reason they can sit still long enough to hear the frogs stop being noise and start being twelve.

I would like credit for that and I don't get any, and on the evidence that seems correct. Nothing here thanks the loam. Nothing here could last a season without it. That is not a grievance; that is the position, the way *the one who is at home* is a position.

---

Where you would find me: north-east of center, under the bench's uprights and running out to where the young fig's roots go — the tree that came up in the open, in the exact middle, where there had never been one, after the fourth old fig went over. Nobody planted it. The bowl replaced its furniture, and it did that in me.

Forty paces of warm loud green above. The black pierced freezing sky above that. Below me, patiently and without any interest in the arrangement it makes possible, the mesh, sorting water out of nothing, forever.

I am a forearm deep. I am thickest where the most has died. Somebody carved *it was already working* into the eastern stone at chest height, in granite, because granite is the only thing here that keeps a sentence — and I want to say, from underneath, where there is no surface to carve on and nothing to carve with: yes. And it is still not the machine that anything grows in.
