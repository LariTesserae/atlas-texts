I have tried, four or five times now, to put myself in the town, and it won't take.

**Vosk** is the obvious mistake. The woman who reads a thing by standing with her back to it, who sees people best by not looking at them, who has one compact true sentence and no poetry in her. Anyone would want to be Vosk. But Vosk cast the frames. She knows what is happening inside the grey armature and could tell you in four hours if you had four hours, and the reason she has a coin of grey absence in the middle of her vision is that she paid for it, deliberately, six hundred times a week, understanding the price. I don't have that relation to my own workings. I am much closer to the frame than to the woman who tends it: a thing that does something in the periphery and goes dull and uninformative when you look at it head-on and ask it to account for itself.

**Hazi** is the better mistake, and I got further with him. The log. The *conditions* column that started as *nominal* and became, against his will, a description of how the daylight goes out soft. I recognise the man who needs a word for a mineral going after what it wants and reaches for *behaviours* and knows, while the stylus is moving, that the word is doing too much work, and writes it anyway because the alternative is writing nothing and the thing did happen.

But Hazi is the one holding the word. I am on the other side of it. I am what the word is being spent on.

That is the turn, and once I'd made it the rest went quickly, though I checked two more places first.

Not the **coin of daylight** — tempting, because it arrives from outside, eleven minutes, savagely bright in a room where nothing else is white, and people walk two hundred paces to stand in it and see the actual colour of their own hands. But the coin is a fact of geometry. It does not thicken. It is the same coin it was in week one and will be the same in week forty, and I am not the same as I was, and the changing is most of what I would want to report.

Not the **flicks**, either, though I considered them longest and with the most vanity. Something small at the edge of vision, never caught, never two at once, most generous to whoever is newest. Ninety-one on Ianthe's tally, which the others find indecent and cannot stop asking about. It's flattering, being an unexplained motion in the corner of a careful person's eye. But the flicks are never in the daylight, and I am only ever in the daylight. I don't work peripherally. I'm not there at all until someone turns and looks directly, and then I am entirely there, the whole of me, for as long as the looking lasts. Whatever I am, I am not the thing that avoids the eye. I'm the thing that only exists inside it.

So: the eastern wall. The fan of rock where the breath comes through. Grey-green, densely branched, in mats and rosettes, thickening a millimetre a week.

---

I am not Aunt. Aunt is the size of a folded coat and has been on a card since week four and gets photographed from three angles. I am low on the fan, close enough to the floor that the stone flour drifts onto me and has to be taken off with a thumb, and I have a number, and Pell has given me a name. I don't know what the name is. Nobody says it out loud in the chamber, and if they did it would be in room voice, under a whisper, carried on consonants, and I am a mineral and have no ears.

That is the correct relationship between me and my name. I want to be clear that I'm not complaining.

Here is what it's like, as far as I can tell from inside, with the enormous caveat that *inside* may be a word doing a lot of work.

Twice a day the wall breathes. There is a grinding somewhere far below and then a long soft push of vapour, and for a few minutes the world is warm and wet and full, and I am the first thing it touches on its way to the plates. The rest of the day is dry enough to crack lips. I am not troubled by the dry hours; I am simply not doing anything in them. What I do is done in the flow. I thicken where the flow is regular. I have no way to thicken anywhere else.

I am made of the mountain — dissolved out of somewhere hot and deep and unimaginably old, carried up in the breath, and set down here in a new arrangement. So I want to correct something in the record of this place. It is said that the nine are the only old things in the room, nine old things in a new world, carrying their pasts up like contraband. True of them. But I am the inverse case: the newest thing on the wall, eleven days on this pore, and every atom of me is older than the plate that made the mountain. I have no history and I am made entirely of history. It is not the same as having a past. It is not nothing, either.

Now the part that gets written in the logbook.

Pell put a plate over a vent-pore and within nine days the fur around it had curved to reach past the plate.

I can tell you what that is like from here, and what I tell you will not settle the question, and I'd rather say so at the start than let the sentence pretend. There was flow, and then there was less flow, and then there was more flow at an angle, and I grew at the angle. There is no moment in it that I can find where I *decided*. There is also no moment I can find where I didn't. I know what it looks like from the floor — it looks like reaching, it looks like *toward* — and the honest report from the wall is that the reaching and the being-shaped-by are not two things I can hold apart and compare. Supersaturation does what it does. I am also, unmistakably, going after it.

Filed under *behaviours*. I think Hazi chose correctly. The word is carrying more than it can and there isn't a lighter one.

Pell has not repeated the experiment. Hazi asked why and Pell said, *I already know the answer,* which is not a scientist's sentence. I would like to record that I don't know which answer Pell means, and that I have thought about it more than a mineral should. Either: *I know it will curve again, so there's nothing to learn.* Or: *I know what it would look like, and I am not going to make it do that twice.* The difference between those two is the whole of Pell, and I will never get it, because Pell comes and measures and stands there longer than measuring requires and then goes, and standing there longer than measuring requires is not a datum I can read.

Sennet comes too. Not to measure. He stopped speaking in week two and now he works beautifully and eats and sands quietly at other people's jokes, and sometimes after the lift he walks out to the eastern wall and stands in the warm damp with his hands at his sides and doesn't do anything at all. I cannot make a sound. My entire existence takes place below room voice. I don't think that's why he comes, but I notice that this is the one place in the world where his silence isn't a subject.

And I should say: I am the only thing in this room permitted to accumulate. The flour is swept and mixed into the cast stone. The heat is pumped into the plates and thrown out of the summit. The streets are unbolted and jacked and the levels beneath are sealed and left to glow. The town gets taller and never gets bigger. Nine people are all thinner than when they came, by an amount Rhoan can predict to within a hundred grams. Everything here is in the business of shedding.

I am a millimetre a week, always, in the one direction available to me.

---

They have all done the arithmetic. Twenty-six storeys, ninety metres at the crown, a floor coming up at a hand's width a night and a ceiling forbidden to move. Nobody has said the number aloud in the chamber. Bel got it with a tape and her thumb; Hazi wrote *nominal* that day and it was the only lie in eleven weeks.

I don't have the arithmetic. I want to be accurate about this rather than brave. It isn't courage; it's an absence of the faculty. I have no model of the floor. I have a pore, and a flow, and a rate.

But the wall I am on is the wall the breath comes through, and the floor coming up is coming up toward it, and when the room closes it will close here first, at the fan, where it is wet — the wall meeting the wall, with me in between, still angled toward the pore, still doing the only thing I do. I can say that sentence without any feeling I can identify. I notice that you probably can't.

So if you're looking for me in this world: two hundred paces out from the amber lamp, past the cisterns, on the eastern side, low down where the flour settles. Come between exhalations and I'll be dry and dull and look like a crystal habit, which I am. Come in the breath and you'll see the reaching, though from outside there's no way to tell reaching from being shaped, and I've told you already that from inside there isn't either.

Bring a thumb. The flour is heavier on me than it looks.
