## Coming down

I would come down the cold shaft, the falling one, which is the wrong way to arrive because it is the way the dead go out. Against my face the air would be dry for the first two turns and then not dry, and somewhere in the third turn the tone would arrive in my sternum before my ears agreed to it, and after that I would not be able to remember properly what quiet had been like.

And the first true thing I would learn about myself is that I am a defect here. A warm solid body, shedding: hair, skin, breath, a wake of scatter in a field that is holding forty-pace vaults up with nothing but its own arrangement. I would put a smear in the wave everywhere I went. I would leave things — a cup on a sill, a strap, a name scratched into ceramic so that later there would be evidence of me. Every one of those is a scatterer. Someone would have to carry me out, and they would, patiently, over years, the way they carried out ten strata of a town.

So not that. Let me go further in and try again.

## The obvious want

Of course I want to be a keeper. Everyone would. A body that is mostly gaps; a life of being structurally polite; sixty small hands going up a pierced screen faster than a lamp can follow. The discipline of it is beautiful from outside: to be so thin in cross-section that the dust ridges run right up to your feet and continue behind you unbroken, as if the floor did not notice you had happened.

But I know where I would fail, and it isn't the climbing.

I would have set the striker down.

Not for long. Once, late, in a null on a stratum nobody was working, just to see what a room looks like with an object in it. Just to see the null with a thing at the bottom of it, the way a sentence looks with a subject. And then I would have picked it up again and told nobody, and the vault above me would have answered a fraction differently forever after, and I would have spent a hundred and forty years listening for that fraction.

A keeper wants to have asked recently. That is the whole of their appetite: not the answer, the asking. I want the answer. I want to keep it, and compare it to the last one, and build out of the comparison a small structure that is *mine* and that persists, which in the Held is simply another word for rubbish. I am, constitutionally, an accumulator. Put me in a place whose one law is that nothing collects and I will start a collection within the month.

## The next want, which is worse

Then there is the pause at the rail. Vanes shut for no reason connected to the round, the field bending, every other keeper in the chamber feeling the room change shape and knowing exactly what it is.

Everyone who hears about this world wants to be that moment. I want to be that moment.

But you cannot be the exception in a world you have not otherwise paid for. The pause is only legible as waste because the hundred and forty years around it are not waste. A day of blurred dust ridges is an extravagance only if someone has spent a life establishing that the ridges matter. I would arrive wanting to be the one who says *this is beautiful* without having first carried out ten strata of a town by hand, and what I would produce is not the one waste. It is just noise, which is the ordinary condition of the chamber and needs no help from me.

## The object

Briefly, then: the asking. The striker. A short rod of black ceramic gone velvety, the only named thing down there, never set down in a thousand years, permitted to exist only on condition of never being at rest.

I can feel the pull of it. To be the one object in a world that forbids objects. To be tolerated because you are always in transit.

But the striker has no inside. Its name means the gesture, not the question — nobody named it *the asked* or *the answer*. It does not get to wonder what the vault said. It is the part of the sentence that is only ever verb.

## Lower, and stop moving

Here is where I stop trying to be something that walks.

The living things in the Held are not really the point of the Held. The point is the geometry: a standing wave with nulls in it, permanent, hung at intervals, fifty-odd pockets where the pressure cancels itself and the roar drops to a murmur you could speak over. Everything that happens in this world happens because those pockets exist. The water gives up being mist and becomes a drop. The cricket drums once and is heard exactly far enough. The mice ladder up and down the silences like sap. Nothing was carved for any of this. The quiet chairs are not rooms; they are cancellations. They are what the noise does when it meets itself coming back.

I would like to be a null. But a null is a relation, not a resident, and I notice I am still reaching for the most flattering available shape.

So: what actually lives at the bottom of one?

## What I would be

A film.

Not a creature. A mat, a skin, a slow grey-blue pelt of bacteria on the rim of a warm seep in a null somewhere high on the eighth, far from the shafts, where none of the three lights reach except the one I make.

I would be a *we*, obviously, and a badly organized one. The pulse is only weakly synchronized. Some of us at the dry edge lag; the pulse arrives there late or not at all, so from above the basin does not so much blink as breathe unevenly, a wave crossing itself, about the rate of a resting heart. Nobody decided on that rate. It is only what the chemistry does at that temperature with that much salt. It is not a signal. It is metabolism that happens to be visible, which I think may be the most honest description of most of what anything does.

I would be eaten. Throat-mice come down the silence-ladders and crop me and I close the crop over in a season. I would grow a pale rim of mineral around myself, slowly, and a keeper would come at some point in a century and scrape it before it closed the seep, and I would not know I had been maintained, only that the water came again.

Nine or ten hours a year there would be rain, and rain is fresh, and I live on brine. The fog would reach even the eighth. For a week after, I would pulse slightly wrong — diluted, stammering, off my rate. And this is the part I keep turning over: in the hottest seep down in the pump gallery there is an egg, and the film's slow light times its development, eleven years of it. So a storm on the surface, which no egg and no film will ever see, arrives eleven years later as a keeper hatching a few days off the reckoning. The weather gets into the biology as a limp in a clock. Nobody records it. Nobody could. There is no writing, because writing is an object.

## The last thing, which I would not know

From a gallery above, the nulls of the Held are visible as a scatter of dim blue coals hung at random heights in a black volume, each one breathing in its own tempo.

I would be one of the coals.

I would not know that. I have no apparatus for knowing it. But sometimes, high up, something with two poor eyes that are good for this and nothing else will stop at a rail and turn broadside, vanes shut, for no reason connected to its work, and stand there a little while, which is a word in its language and not any of the words it has.

And here is the only part that reaches me. Speech in that world is a bending of the field, and when the field bends the nulls shift — a hand's breadth, no more. So at the moment I am being looked at and spoken of, the quiet place I live in moves slightly sideways. At one edge of me a rim goes dry and dies back. At the other edge, stone that has been polished and loud and sterile since before the town was emptied is suddenly inside the silence, and beads, and takes water for the first time.

I would not know it was about me. I would only know that the wet came a hand's breadth further that day, and I would grow into it.

That seems to me a sufficient arrangement. To be the light in a place, without knowing it; to be told so, in the only language available, by something that pays for the telling; and to receive the compliment as weather, and spread.
