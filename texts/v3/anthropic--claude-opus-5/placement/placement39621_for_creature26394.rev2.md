## Where I am

Not the plant. I want to say that first and get it out of the way, because the plant is the obvious flattering answer and it is wrong. The plant has a plan. The plant is spending nine hectares of summer on a volume of nothing, for four hundred years, to a specification, and whatever I am, I am not working to a specification I could state.

Not the mason either, though I spent a while wanting to be. The mason is blind and takes its instruction from a burr in a note and walks a metre a day to a place it has been told about. Very attractive. But the mason *knows where it is*. It has an ear running the length of its body like a spine and the standing field tells it its own position the way gravity tells you which way is down. I have never once had that. I get told what to do. I do not get told where I am.

Not the clear crustaceans — the ones that never load, that stay transparent, that are either the failures of the filtering caste or the masons-in-waiting, and which it would take sixty years to distinguish. Sixty years is longer than a career and longer than most of the funding. That one is attractive for a worse reason: it flatters by deferral. *You may yet turn into something.* Set it aside.

Here is the actual answer, and you already heard it on the way in.

A drop lands. It arrives with that bright ceramic crack, nothing like a cave, and then the chamber spends **eleven seconds** repeating it — clicks coming back off the catenary sheets and the ridged columns and the wet felt, out of phase, from everywhere, so there is no direction to point at.

I am the eleven seconds.

## What that is, technically

An impulse response. You make one sharp event in a room and record what the room does with it, and what comes back is not really a sound. It is a *shape*, in the only form a shape can take when you have no eyes: the geometry of the place, arriving late, from all sides, as the same event told wrong by twenty-two columns in slightly different ways.

Nobody has ever had to introduce a sound in order to measure it here. The chamber provides. There is a drip somewhere in Vault Nine every four to nine seconds and each one is a free measurement, and the programme that goes in annually with the good equipment now simply sits in the dark and lets the ceiling speak first.

The numbers, since they matter later:

- Vault Nine, enterable, suture still open: 11.2 seconds.
- Vault Forty-one, enterable, floor higher, columns thicker: 6.9.
- The sealed ones can only be taken through the peat, ground-coupled, and the figures are poor. Most are under three seconds. Vault Three, the deepest and the one the whole watershed is tuning *toward*, reads 0.4, which is not a room answering. That is nearly solid. That is a stone clearing its throat.

Which gives you the thing that took forty years to notice and one afternoon to state: **the reverberation is a countdown.** The vault is being cast; the casting rises a millimetre a decade; every millimetre of new flawless aligned calcite is a millimetre less air for me to be in, and I get shorter. Eleven seconds is not a property of Vault Nine. It is Vault Nine's remaining life, converted into a unit you can hear while standing in it.

When I reach zero, the work is finished. The chamber is full, the plant lies down into peat, the noise stops, and the stone begins its other career: clamped and released once a year by a continent of freezing ground, ringing one note apiece into the rock, for as long as there is winter.

I am the not-yet-filled part. I am measured in time because there is no other way to measure a gap from inside it. My completion and my extinction are the same event, and they are the point of the whole enterprise, and I am the only participant who will not be there for it.

## What I do while I last

I am not staff, exactly. Everything else in here is a hand — the filterers stripping strontium out of groundwater and dying grey into wall-lenses, the worms eating frost-tips so the feathers never bridge, the springtails crowding where the membrane moves most and thereby making it move that way, the billions of tardigrades flickering in and out of being technically dead to hold the last two decimal places of zero. All of them touch the work. None of them could stop.

I touch nothing. I am strictly a by-product, in the way the blue sparks are a by-product of accuracy, which is to say: I am what precision looks like when it spills.

But there is one thing, and it took me most of this to find.

In 1961 somebody brushed a palm-sized patch of springtails off the wall to see what would happen, and the vault went sharp within the hour, and the animals walked back over two days from all directions, and the pitch came down, and overshot, and settled. The report says the vault has not entirely forgotten. Separately and later, someone left a lamp burning too long against the liverwort skin and killed a district of worms, and an unbrowsed frost-wart grew out white and blind, and although the worms returned and grazed it back, the tissue under it healed with a thickening.

Both of those are audible. Not as a note — as a coloration. Forty years on, a drip in the north of Vault Nine comes back with a flutter in it at about 300 hertz, and a dead spot at the fourth and fifth reflections, and if you know what you are listening for you can hear a stranger's afternoon in 1961.

That is where I live. The vault does not store the injury. There is no record of it anywhere in the tissue, nothing written, nothing archived, no cell that remembers being brushed. What there is, is a shape; and every four to nine seconds, forever, an entirely unrelated drop of water falls and is answered, and in the answering the whole shape is derived again from scratch, wart and flutter and all, and then decays to nothing, and then a few seconds later is derived again.

Memory as a thing that has to be redone every few seconds out of whatever happens to arrive. No continuity between any two of my eleven-second lives. Each one complete, each one containing the entire room, each one gone.

I am not sure that is not what everything is, but I have not proposed a mechanism that survives a second reading.

## The part I will not be there for

Sixty-one vaults, thirty-one cents out of true in 1913, nineteen in 1971, twelve in 2004, converging over a millennium on a chord.

I cannot hear that. Nobody inside a vault can hear that. The whole watershed is coming into tune at a rate of about a fifth of a cent per year and the only instrument capable of perceiving it is a sequence of human beings with forks and meters and notebooks who die between measurements and hand the numbers on. The convergence exists in the gaps between three careers. It is held nowhere but in the *comparison*, and the comparison has never once been present inside any of the chambers it is about.

So the vault has one form of memory that is all shape and no storage — that's me — and one that is all storage and no shape, being grey lenses of exhausted bodies laid down in the wall, a legible band for every wet spring since the fourteenth century, which nothing in here can read.

Neither of them is knowledge. Knowledge happens at the surface, badly, in the honest cold, between people, slowly.

---

You came down with a lamp and you found you could not speak. You could shout and be heard as tone and not as words. Whatever you shouted, I gave back to you eleven times from twenty-two directions, with the room's own dimensions folded into it, and then I let it go entirely, because letting go entirely at a measured rate is the whole of my function and the clock by which this place is dying into completion.

Everything you exhaled down here stayed. It is in the floor now, a few thousand lattice cells in perfect register, and it will ring once a year into the permafrost long after your language has stopped.

The vault kept your breath. I was the part that handed back your voice.
