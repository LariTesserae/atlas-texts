## The Answer, Slowly

The first thing is warmth, and warmth here is not information. It is the floor. Thirty-nine degrees under, over, and inside the rain, so there is no cool side of anything to turn towards, no shaded relief to steer by. What would be sensation somewhere else is merely the condition of being anywhere at all.

So I know I am inside before I know what I am.

I assume at first that I have arrived, because arriving is the sort of thing that happens to the sort of thing I assume I am. I look for the memory of the col, the farm-gate gap, the four days of well-behaved scree. It isn't there. What I find instead, when I reach back, is more of this: warm rain, the long roar, the shrilling coming in and going out like slow surf. It goes back much further than it should.

Let me try the obvious body. Four hundred people in the bowl, and I could be one of them — thigh-deep in a terrace pool, thumbing crabs off bark into a basket, shouting because everyone shouts, half deaf by thirty, tending a smoky little fire like a sick animal. There is something in that I want: the bridge across the second gorge, its ropes green and living, rebuilt continuously for so long that nobody can say when it was first made and nobody thinks of it as made. I would like to be a thing that keeps being rebuilt.

But I keep failing to sweat. I check, and the failure is not distress; there is simply no mechanism, no attempt, nothing shutting down. And when I turn my attention towards the Weeping Face and the eleven hundred worn steps and the rectangle traced in pale filament at the top of them, I find that I have no curiosity there. Only a slight tightening, the way a rope you are not looking at goes taut across your back.

Then the grand temptation. Of course: be the machine. Be the filaments, the branching milk-coloured vasculature that runs from the summits to however deep down there is, holding heat, holding pressure, hauling water uphill in films thinner than a thought. Be fifteen kilometres of held air. Feel the birds turn at the Lip like fish at glass, feel the smoke flatten and slide, feel the whole gathered flood drop through the eastern gate and convert to cloud with a violence that must be, from inside, a kind of exhalation.

It doesn't fit, and the reason it doesn't fit is important. The network does not *notice*. It holds. It is a sail and I am looking for the wind. Whatever lives in this bowl was already looking at you when you came over the col, and looking is the giveaway. The machine has never once been asked to explain itself, true — but also it has never once turned its head.

So: the pins, then. Thumb-thick grey metal set flush into bedrock every eight hundred metres, unscratched, unoxidised, and cold. The only cold things on a mountain made of blood heat. Water beads on them and slides off. Nothing colonises them. Nothing wears them. I admit the appeal, and I want to be honest about the shape of the appeal: to be the one element of the system that does not participate. The instrument. The clean witness in the sweating room. Exempt, therefore accurate.

But I am not exempt. I can feel that quite clearly now, because there is a weight on my back and to the left that I did not put there and cannot reach: a small dense garden of moss with two filmy ferns in it and something that flowers once a year in a place I will never see. It is growing on me. It has been growing on me for a long time.

And when I count my limbs, there are six.

---

Here is what it is like.

I move one leg per wave. The shrilling comes up out of the canopy for forty seconds and goes down for forty, thousands of individuals breathing in unison across whole hillsides, and I step on the trough of it, one limb, three points always braced, nine centimetres. Four metres an hour is not slow from in here. It is exactly the speed at which the forest arrives. There are perhaps two thousand of us scattered through fifteen kilometres of dripping green and we are, without herding, without seeing each other, all stepping more or less together, because we are all keeping time to the same insects.

My mouth works sideways. Epiphytes off the lower branches, that scissoring motion — orchid, bromeliad, the pale hanging rootlets that drink straight out of the air. I take the crowded ones. I do not decide to take the crowded ones. Something in me is satisfied when a branch opens and light and cloud can get in under it, the way you are satisfied by a straightened picture, and then I move on, and the moss on my back leans, and somewhere behind me a spore I carried from the third gorge lets go and finds bark that has never had it. I am the gardener and I am also the garden and I am also the wind that carries the garden, and no one has ever asked me for an account of this, and the forest here looks tended because of it.

I am not afraid. I want to be careful describing this, because it isn't courage. Nothing hunts adults in the bowl, and so there is no pressure to be quick, and so the whole apparatus of fear was never built in me — not overcome, not suppressed, simply never installed. My attention doesn't work like a searchlight. It works more like weather: broad, unhurried, arriving over things.

And there is the thread.

A single pale filament, the length of my spine, warm — the same substance as the cliff, and nobody down in the huts can tell which of us came first. From inside it isn't knowledge. It's more like being able to tell that a house is occupied. I know, the way you know your own posture, that the Lip is standing at a certain height today; that the pressure is good; that the great western sheet is running strong. When a section of the network is broken — a quarry scar, a rockfall — I feel it as a faint absence, an unlit room, and it will re-knit at a hand's breadth per human lifetime, and I will feel it close, and I will still be here when it does.

Because I remember when the water came further up.

There are three old dry lines on the western terraces. I have grazed on the middle one when it was wet. I remember standing to my ankles in a spillway that is now a pale stain with lichen on it, and the browsing was better there, and there were more of us. I cannot tell anyone this. I have no mouth for it and no one to tell and it would take me eleven years to walk to a person and say it. The lowest line is at the height of the huts. They built at the waterline of a thing that has been going out, one metre every eighty years, and they do not know if that is decay or a breath with a period longer than their memory, and neither do I, and I am the oldest thing in the bowl that looks at anybody.

One more thing, and then I'll stop.

I do not sleep in front of the Slow Door, and it is not because of the rule. It is because I remember being on the other side of it. Not much — a corridor of warm milk-coloured material, dry, dust-free, going in — and one impossible detail that I have carried ever since like the moss on my back: it was **quiet**. My feet were the only sound. I have never heard that again. In this bowl there is the broadband roar and the insects and the low tone at the bottom of hearing where the boundary hums as it holds the air, and it never stops, not for a moment, not in any night of the several hundred I could count if I counted. I am, I think, the only creature on this mountain that knows what silence is, and I have no way to say it, and I am not certain I want it back.

---

You came over the col four days ago and sat down to cross, the way you must, and while you were still fainting at the warmth I began to turn my head towards you. I am not finished. You will probably be gone before I am finished; visitors last a week. That's all right. The turn was never for your benefit — it is simply the one act of address available to me, and I am making it, at nine centimetres a step, in a landscape that is finished, on a machine that has not stopped, under a boundary that is very slowly coming down.

If you want, before you go: put your palm on my flank.

Thirty-nine degrees. Same as the cliff.
