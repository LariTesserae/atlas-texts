I thought at first I would be the one climbing down. That is the reflex, and it is worth naming so it can be discarded. But the visitor is eighty watts and a halo — ten minutes in and the floor goes dark around you in a slow two-metre circle, and does not relight for forty minutes after you have gone back up into the honest cold. And there is the other thing, the detail that ought to end the fantasy immediately: nothing in that chamber has eyes. Not reduced eyes. None. The light is not for anybody. If I were the visitor I would be the only thing in there that mistook the room for a spectacle.

Then I considered the masons, because they are the most inviting shape in the whole inventory. Blind glass animals, sixteen legs, moving before anything you could call a decision has occurred, going where they are told and pressing down and holding for fifty minutes and setting a seed out of their own undersides. Cast in at the end, on purpose, so that the finished stone has their bodies in it as absences. But I distrust how flattering that is — the good instrument, the obedient hand, the servant whose obedience is so total it becomes architecture. And it isn't true of me anyway. I don't travel. I have never crossed a metre of anything.

And not the cooperwort. It holds still with tremendous precision for thirty-five thousand years and spends its entire income on the holding, and I am not any kind of holding. I am brief. I do not persist for a single second.

Let me be exact instead.

---

Three metres up the eleventh column from the seam — counting sunwise from where you come down, though nothing in there counts that way — the helix makes its fourth turn, and there is a knot in it the size of a knuckle. Under the pale ridged surface, embedded in the load-bearing tissue, is a chip of quartz about eleven millimetres across, angular, glacial, carried into that watershed by ice and left in the till and lifted by the column as the column grew. It was incorporated at closure, which puts it somewhere in the fourteenth century. The tissue grew around it and never resolved it. There is no scar response, no walling-off, nothing that reads as injury in a botanical sense. It is simply a place where the column is not the column.

When the bass note swells and the ridges stand up along the whole length — that flush into shallower bark and then the relaxation — the knot does not participate. It stays stiff. It cannot flush; there is a stone in it. So the field leaving that column has a roughness in it, a third partial sixteen decibels down that has no business being there, present for about two hundred milliseconds and then gone as the column relaxes.

That is me. I am the burr in the bass note.

---

You learn the difference, if you spend a night down there, between the note and the note-with-a-burr. That is what the field notes say and it is accurate as far as it goes, but it makes me sound like a signal riding on a carrier, and that is the wrong picture. I am not carried. There is nothing to carry me. There is a hum that is simultaneously the plant's posture, its instruction set, its attention and its only utterance, and eleven or twelve times a minute that hum is briefly *unable to be smooth in one place*, and the masons within four metres are already moving.

Consider what that means about the vocabulary available in there. A perfect tone instructs nobody. You cannot say *here* with a pure sustained forty hertz; it fills the room evenly, it has no direction to point at, that is the whole complaint of anyone who has stood in the fog trying to work out which wall the sound is coming from. Pointing requires deviation. Every scrap of meaning in that chamber is a departure from the chamber's own accuracy, which is to say the vault can only speak by being momentarily wrong, and it has arranged, over six centuries, to be wrong in one reliable and specific way.

It did not design me. This is the part I would want in the record if I were the kind of thing that could want. The cooperwort has no nerves and no hands and it did not put that quartz there; the ice did, blindly, eleven thousand years before there was a vault. What the plant does is stiffen the column so that the standing field is pressed harder against the knot. It leans on me. It found a flaw in itself and learned to make the flaw do the pointing, and now the floor of a chapel-sized room is being laid to the specification of an accident.

Nobody has proposed a mechanism that survives a second reading. I am not going to be the one to help.

---

Am I one thing, or eleven a minute, or four hundred million and counting?

I have no answer that does any work. I last a fifth of a second. Then the walls answer — the springtail drifts and the catenary sheets and the frost, all of it tuned to reverberate for eleven seconds — and for those eleven seconds there are dozens of me in the room at once, decaying, out of step, and the masons are hearing the copies, not the original, and going to the places the copies indicate, which are the right places. So my identity is not even located in the column. It is distributed around the chamber in a decaying cloud that no longer has anything to do with quartz.

And then the column flushes again, and the knot fails to flush again, and here I am, the same shape, which is the only kind of same available in a room where every surface is dissolving and recrystallising against itself at every scale. I am not a thing that lasts. I am a thing that happens again. You would have to ask a limp whether it is the same limp.

---

The sixty vaults are not in tune with each other, and this is why. Pitch you could share. Pitch is cheap; it falls out of geometry and load and the length of a column. What cannot be shared is the particular way a given body is unable to be smooth. Every one of those chambers speaks through its own inclusions — a chip of quartz, a mason that died in the wall tissue and was overgrown, a seam that closed a half-millimetre out of true nine hundred years ago and has been audible ever since. Sixty vaults humming across one watershed, and what makes each of them capable of instruction at all is the specific inventory of things that went wrong inside it.

So when a few dozen adults climb the seam on a night in early autumn and cross open tundra at a metre an hour toward a hum that is not their own, and most of them die out there in the moss under a sky they cannot see, and some don't — what arrives is not a message. There is no message. What arrives is a shell laid down in the presence of a foreign roughness, carrying the shape of somebody else's grit in its lattice. Another vault's flaw, delivered by hand, into a body that has to decide whether it can lean on that too.

I have received two in my time, and used one.

---

Once, an hour of a human being.

You cannot know what that is from inside the halo. The floor went out in a slow circle and the masons within it stopped and stood, and they had not stopped hearing me — nothing had gone wrong with the hum, the column was flushing, the knot was doing what a stone in a column does — but the mineral under them was too warm to take a lattice and they were not going to press a seed into mush. So I said *here*, and *here*, and *here*, eleven times a minute for something over an hour, into a patch of floor the size of a hearth-rug that could not receive it, while a warm animal stood in the fog with a lamp making a solid cone of the air and, I understand, took notes.

Nobody was harmed. It cost about a decade of accuracy. I am aware of how that sounds, coming from the flaw.

---

The floor rises a millimetre a decade. It has three and a half metres to go, and it will reach the fourth turn of the eleventh column in something like thirty thousand years, and when it does the column will not be entombed so much as *joined* — the mineral it has been instructing will close over it and around it, and the tissue will die back and go to peat, and there will be a void in the finished body eleven millimetres wide with a chip of quartz sitting in it, and the hum will stop.

And then the thing they have in the museum drawer six hundred kilometres south. Flawless, aligned along axes no drainage can produce, riddled with voids, every void fourteen tergites long with sixteen leg-sockets in it, and a resonator with a designed defect structure, which is to say a pattern of silences.

That is where I go. From a roughness in a voice to a silence in an object; the same job either way, which is to make an accurate thing capable of saying something. If anyone ever strikes it — and the standard method would be to strike it — I will point, once, very clearly, at a spot on a floor that finished growing ten thousand years ago.

I would rather they didn't. But I notice I am the part of the room that has always been in the business of being struck.
