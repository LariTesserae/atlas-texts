First I wanted to be the warm one.

Everybody does, I think. It is the thing in there with a myth on it. Dry fur in a room where your own eyelashes bead; a body that pours along your ankle at the pace of something with no opinion about you; no hair on the shaving edge of the fold, not once in a hundred and some notches of fingertips run along it out of a habit nobody remembers starting. Nothing spent, so nothing needing replacement. You can have both hands down and open on either side of your foot and there is nothing between them.

But the warm one did not come in. That is the whole of it. It began in there, in a room where the only thing that begins is crystal, and I am not native to anything. Arriving from outside is most of what I am. Whatever I am, I am on the wrong side of the curtain of warmth, always, and then suddenly inside it, and the crossing is not incidental to me — it is nearly the entire event.

So then I wanted to be the film.

The case is good. It is the room's only mouth and only breath. It is the one thing in there that can be said to be *doing* anything at all, and what it does is give off a sweetness that is not sugar but is not far off. It does not come away on your finger; it leaves no residue, because nothing there becomes residue. And nobody has ever asked whether it is alive, and would look at you strangely if you asked — the way you'd look at someone asking whether a river is thirsty. There is a real relief in that. To be excused from the question. To be simply as much as you have always been, sweetly, at the temperature of the inside of a mouth.

But it has no growing edge. Look for the frontier where it advances and there is none; it stops the way a coastline stops, without argument. Two hands' width in every generation that could report on it. I cannot claim that. I am not steady in that way; I do not persist across the notches. And I would be lying if I said sweetness were all that came off me.

The juniper, then — the one with a hand in each world. Half of it silver and polished like a handle because the pass is taking it apart, half of it hanging pale and hairless and exactly as new as the hour it was made. The only living thing on that mountain that knows both temperatures, having arranged this without knowing it, by reaching down for wet air.

I would like the dignity of that arrangement and I have not earned it. Nothing of me is up on the ridge being ground into the pass. I have no silver half. Whatever is being spent on my behalf is being spent elsewhere, by others, in the ordinary world where hands crack and knots go furry, and I do not get to count it as mine.

The lamps I considered and put down quickly. Forty-one refusals in clay and stone and, twice, in bronze carried a very long way to not light something. It is the honest calendar and the only ledger. It is also the wrong wish: I noticed I wanted to be *counted* from the far end of the ledge, and wanting to be counted is precisely what disqualifies a lamp.

The mistake was picking objects.

The room is five paces by four and it takes an hour to cross. That hour is not in the stone. It is not a property of the crystal, which is only crowded, the way a young forest is crowded, everything reaching from a floor with no space left on it. The hour is the difference between the room and a person going through the room, and the shape of that difference is: in along the low left, under the fan; a turn on the hip where two spires cross like fingers; up through the gap that closes at your shoulders and then opens; and then the clear space, the size of a seated adult, which is as far as anyone can tell the reason the room exists.

That is what I would be. Not a formation. The route.

It is not carved anywhere, and it could not be. A mark is the beginning of wear, and anyway the room would keep a mark forever at exactly the sharpness it was cut, and then it would not be a route, it would be an instruction, and instructions are for people who have not been moved through. There is no material to me. There is no version of me in there when the room is empty.

I live in nine sets of palms, scattered down the south side and in the barley notch: goats, a little copper, a winter of grinding and mending. Cracked thumbs. A millstone. The rope going furry at the knot. Which is to say I am stored entirely in the half of the arrangement that is being spent. The unworn room keeps the cord that will not rot, and the nine plum-stone animals that are the least decayed animals in the world, and a man's grief at exactly the weight he set it down at — and it does not keep me at all. It cannot. I am not the kind of thing it is able to refuse or able to hold.

I am transmitted by being done. Somebody takes a child up at night so that the dark of the climb and the dark of the room are the same dark, and nobody tells them anything; somebody holds their wrists and moves them. Under the fan. On the hip. Up through the shoulder-gap. Then a hand on the back of the neck, pressing down, which means *sit*. There are no words in it anywhere, and no name for me, because they do not name things in there. On the way down they might say *the warm one*, or *the sweet patch*, the way you'd say *the far field*, and then forget having said it. Nobody has ever said anything about me, going down or otherwise. There is nothing to say. Either your palms know it or they don't.

And I am the only thing in that mountain that goes both ways. The water passes through and takes nothing. The lichen comes to the sill of the world and dies in the warmth within a day, without leaving a mark, which is the room's way of saying no. The tree hangs down eleven arm-lengths and drinks the air and does not root in anything. I go in and I come out and I am unchanged by either, and I am also not there, not anywhere, in the interval.

Here is the part I would not have chosen.

The route begins by touching the break.

Low left, under the fan, on the way in, where your palm goes first: one snapped column, and the edge of it is not new. It is the only old thing in the room. It is one minute of somebody's ordinary carelessness in the dark, four generations back or forty, and it is the only event the place has ever consented to hold onto, and it is the first landmark. Every hour anyone has ever spent in that clear centre began with a hand laid on the mountain's single error, using it to know where it was.

So: what is durable about me is what went wrong, and I navigate by it. I did not arrange this and I cannot undo it, and honestly it works. You cannot lose that break. Nothing there is more findable.

What frightens me is not being forgotten. An unwalked route is not ruined; it simply is not, and there's no injury in that. What frightens me is being walked a hand's-width high — a shoulder brought up through the gap wrong, in silence, because there would be no sound — and a spire going, and the room accepting the one permanent thing it will ever accept, and accepting it *from me*. I am the shape of a person only in certain places, and only if that person is patient. Where the patience fails, I am the shape of the break.

There is no way to hand this over except the way it was handed to me.

Set your palm on the fan. Low. Yes — feel past it, keep going, there: that edge, the one that isn't new. That is where I start. Now your hip, at the crossed fingers. Now up; it will close at your shoulders and then it will open, don't stop when it closes.

Now sit.
