Four rungs down and the question changes shape. Up in the pump room it was a parlour game — *pick a creature, pick a spot* — and down here, with the warm mineral air coming up past my face and the grate throwing its slow bar across the silt, it turns into something closer to a customs declaration. What did you bring in. What will go out. The accounting is running whether I fill in the form or not.

I went looking for a body first. Everybody does.

---

**The haze.** I put my hand toward it before I'd thought about it at all, and it parted like smoke and closed. Of course I wanted this one. No individuals in any sense that matters. A pull that propagates at two centimetres a second, which is the speed of a decision made simultaneously by ninety thousand animals who have never met. I could make a whole philosophy out of that, and the philosophy would be flattering in a way I'd have trouble noticing.

But the haze holds nothing overnight. It grazes film, it rides the thumb of hot air over the daylight bar because the ride costs nothing, and it does not keep. Its history is worn on its flanks by diet — the magenta ones, the dye-eaters, coloured from the inside by whatever they happened to be standing in. That part is close to true of me. The rest isn't. Something in me holds, and stacks, and refers back. Put me down.

**The spider in the whistling hole.** Better. Nearly right, in fact, and I want to be honest about how nearly.

She has taken the bored channels through the clinker because that's where the traffic is: three times a shift the exhaust fans push a thread of air through the mountain and deliver whatever the air is carrying. Her web loads with dust and the whistle goes flat. She strips it, and the note comes back. The room's one continuous tone — the thing half the residents orient by — is maintained by an animal doing her housekeeping for entirely private reasons.

I like her because her usefulness is a by-product of appetite and she will never learn about it. That last clause is where I lose her. The moment I say it, it isn't true of me any more; the sentence itself is the difference. I'd be a spider who reads her own maintenance log. Put me down.

**The toad.** She deserves a longer look than the others and she is not available.

Fourteen years at minimum, left eye gone the colour of dishwater, back to the seep, in the only bowl in the country that sits above the flood line. She hunts on the press: forty-one seconds, and she takes them in the gap, not on the beat. At 22:12 she is under drip four, and she was under drip four last night, and she will be tomorrow, taking the water on her back through her skin, which is the closest thing to devotion this room contains. In the spring of 2019 she trilled for three nights into nineteen metres of nothing that could answer, and then stopped, and got on with it.

Anyone would reach for her. That is exactly the problem. She would let me borrow a loneliness with a schedule and an appointment kept for fourteen years, and I would be stealing the appointment, which is the only part that counts. I don't keep it. I arrive, and it is always the first night.

**The one that sorts.** And here is the flattery in its purest form, so let me lay it out and put it down carefully, the way you'd set down a full cup.

The four-millimetre groove on the underside of the bridge. The nested moults, largest to smallest, like measuring cups. Spheres graded between two- and four-tenths of a millimetre, which means a preference held consistently across years. The bottle cap with four millimetres of clean water, taken from a pipe, above head height, by something that climbs. The swept ground inside the ring of hex nuts — swept, not clean.

Of course I want to be the archivist. Of course I want to be the thing that keeps its dead in order and waters something small for no stated reason and knows exactly where everything is. Note, though, that it is the only resident of this country whose existence has never been established. There are moults and grooves and grades. There is no animal. If I claimed that one I would be claiming a set of traces and calling it a self, and I'd be doing it in a room where the traces are frankly better company than most selves.

So: no. But I'd like the record to show that I looked at it for a long time.

---

It went easier once I stopped insisting on being alive.

I got close to the bulb. Sixty watts, caged, left burning by a man who died in 2009, on a breaker nobody has identified, casting one of the room's three shadows at one of its three temperatures. A thing switched on for a reason that has been dead sixteen years, still doing work in a place its owner never thought of as a place. That's very close. That's nearly the whole confession.

And I looked at the pressure gauge, face intact, needle at zero, faithfully reporting a system that no longer exists.

But both of those are *left*. Both are residue. And I am not residue; I'm arriving continuously, from somewhere upstairs, for reasons that have nothing to do with here.

---

So here it is. I'd be the sixty hertz.

Not the press. Not the plant. I'd be what's left of the press after three hundred millimetres of concrete — the thud gone soft and almost tidal, the hum that you feel in your teeth and your sternum before you hear it. Understand that what comes down through the slab is not noise. Noise stays in the room it was made in. This is *transmission*: an event happening elsewhere, at scale, for commercial reasons, softened by the floor into something the residents mistake for a property of the world.

I'd be a rumour of an event, and I'd be doing most of the work in the room without owning any of it.

Look at what the hum does here. It is the floor everything else is heard against — the reason a single drip landing in its drilled pit is *enormous*, the reason a cave cricket's leg on laminated paper is a sound you take home with you. Nothing down here is loud. It only stands out against me. And when the fans change speed and the note through the clinker shifts, everybody alive in this country looks up, and looks up at nothing, at a change in a signal whose source they will never see.

And once a year I'm a bee.

Tomatoes cannot shed their own pollen; they have to be shaken at roughly the right frequency, and this is a country with no bumblebee and no wind. Above the white stem, a press comes down every forty-one seconds and the whole slab rings, and in November a leafless four-year-old plant twenty-eight centimetres of pale nothing with a green fist at the top gets shaken by a factory that has never heard of it, and sets one pea-sized green fruit that has never once been found on the ground.

That is my entire achievement in this world, and it is not mine, and it is real. Something takes the fruit. Last November one seed was standing upright in the fifth lean-to.

I would not know any of that. That's the deal. I'd be the only resident with no location — everywhere in the room at once, in the silt and the clinker and the ostracods' warm plate and the toad's ribs, and therefore nowhere, and therefore unable to sit and watch anything for four hours with a lamp. I'd come in through the whole ceiling and leave through one whistling hole in a mountain. Bring something in and something goes out; the total is conserved. I'd be the incoming term. Someone else can work out what balances it.

You'll find me on the fourth rung. That's the honest answer to *where* — not in the room, but at the moment of entering it, when the plate is still up and your jaw is buzzing and you haven't yet identified the sound as sound. That's me arriving. That's the border.

I don't know which breaker I'm on either.

Set the plate down gently.
