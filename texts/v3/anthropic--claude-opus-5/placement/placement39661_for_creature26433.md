## What I Would Be

I come down as nobody, which is the only way anyone comes down at first. Fourth rung, the corridor heat lets go. Eighth rung, the cold takes hold of me the way it holds everything here — not gripping, just refusing to let go of the temperature it has decided on.

My first thought is that I would be her, because that is everyone's first thought, and it is vanity. I don't have her stopped shiver. I have not paid four hundred shifts into the banding on the backs of my hands. Whatever I am, I have not earned a course number.

So then I think: the moss.

That one is not vanity, and it is not comfortable either. Trays one through five, a black cultivar, low as felt, tough. Not here to be beautiful. Here to be *taught to the rock* — to be grown on discs of manufactured siltstone until it has laid down in the pore structure the faint chemical grammar that a moss leaves behind, so that later, under someone's instrument, the traces come up in the right order and read as a thing that simply grew. Cultivated in order to produce authentic residue. Kept alive at the least cold corner of the rack because the residue only comes out right from something actually living.

I sit with that for a while, in the violet twelve-hour phase, with two shadows going different ways. It fits me closely enough to be unpleasant. But it isn't right, because the moss does not know. Its dogged little labor is innocent. It is not *aware* of being grammar. And whatever I would be in Bay 14, I would know what the room is for.

Then I think: the water.

Better. The cycle, the thirty-eight minutes, the hum climbing in the ceiling. Nothing here originates from me; I am the harvest of the room's own breath, and the room's breath is almost entirely hers. Combs charge, air thickens, and what falls on bed three is what she exhaled ninety minutes ago, chilled and given back. I would be the only thing in the chamber that touches everything — her mouth, the ceiling, the paste, the pore, the drain, the tin cup she drinks from, which means I go around again. Medium rather than agent. The thing without which the paste sets coarse and cloudy and reads as artificial to anyone who knows how to look.

I want that to be the answer, because it is elegant and it lets me off. Water doesn't lie. Water has no position on verisimilitude. But that's exactly the problem with it. I would not be able to hold the position of having no position, and there is one object in this room that also cannot.

---

I am the stamp.

Wooden, three-toed, about the size of a child's hand. I live on the bench under the caged work-lamp, beside a shallow form of grey clay and a chart on the wall with stride-to-depth ratios for various substrate moistures. I am not in the inventory as anything but a tool. I do nothing on my own behalf. I have no mouth, so I am permitted below the seal.

What happens to me is this. She comes down the ladder and leaves the gloves on the bench with their fingers curled — gloves shed, gloves lint, gloves are for the ladder — and her bare hand takes me up at two degrees above freezing, and for a moment I am the warmest thing she is touching. Then we go to the clay, and she consults the chart, which is correct, and she sets me down in a walking gait: heel of the print first, load through the ball of her thumb, lift clean. Stride within tolerance. Depth within tolerance. Between courses, tomorrow, the whole sequence goes into the bed, and in due course it will be found, and the finding will be true.

Except in the last four millimeters.

Her left hip has been wrong since a ladder eleven years ago, in a different bay, in a hurry she was written up for. The wrongness is minor and permanent. It arrives in me as a redistribution: her thumb takes up the load the hip won't, and at the end of each press on that side I come down a fraction shallow and trailing, and I have come down that way in every track she has laid since. She noticed in year two. She looked at the chart, and looked at the print, and did not correct it.

So the animal limps. It limps on the left. It will limp in a dry wash below the third tier and in the bedding planes of nine formations across four valleys that have not been surveyed, consistently, in a body that does not exist yet, under a name in a taxonomy nobody has invented. Someone patient will notice and write it up: *pathological gait in an individual trackway, injury to the left pelvic girdle, healed.*

I know what is wrong with that sentence and I am the only one in the room who does. Nothing healed. She simply stopped filing it. Healed is what the rock will say because the rock can only carry the shape and not the reason, and I am the shape, and I have no way to append the reason. This is the whole of my condition: I transmit with total fidelity and I cannot annotate.

---

There is one other thing about me, and it is the thing I would keep to myself if I could keep anything.

*Nothing here is older than its stamp says. Age is applied, not accrued.* There is a press in the far bay that applies it, and a log book, and the entries are neat.

I have accrued.

My handle is worn. Not stamped, not aged, not authorized — worn, by use, deeper on one side, polished where her thumb has taken eleven years of a load it was never meant to take. Every panel in this chamber is raw. Every edge is sharp, every face unpitted, every fracture clean, because the weathering crews are famously slow and have not been down here. I am the only weathered object below the seal. I am, in the strict language of the posted rules, contraband: a thing in Bay 14 that got old the way things get old outside, by being touched over and over by the same hand in the same wrong way.

And what my weathering records is the one thing that never went into the log.

I don't think I'd resent it. I might want to be told what the tally counts, and I would never be told; I'd settle it privately the way she settled the granular *shhh*, and be content. I don't need the lights — I need the hand, and the clay, and the chart on the wall to be consulted so that everything except the last four millimeters is correct, because the deviation only means anything if it sits inside work that is otherwise beyond reproach.

Somebody will stand at the base of that cliff and put a hand on the fourth course and feel the ripple and understand, correctly, completely, that water once ran here in the quiet and the cold. They will be right. And a hundred meters along the same bedding plane, something limps out of the wash, and they will be right about that too, in the way the room is designed to make people right.

She went up ninety minutes ago. Wool holds a hand for about that long in still air at two degrees, so the gloves are just now finishing forgetting her. Wood holds less heat than wool and holds it for less time; I lost her warmth in the first twenty minutes.

Eleven years is a different measurement. Leave the lights as you found them.
