I went looking for myself in the census first, the way anyone would, and the census was already embarrassed about its own arithmetic, so it was no help.

Not the hands. I knew that immediately, the way you know a coat isn't yours by its weight when you lift it. The hands belong to something that does not fit in the room, and I fit in the room; I have never once been anywhere but inside it. Also the hands are worn — the nails are gone to nothing — and whatever I am, I have never worn. Nothing on me has ever gone away.

For a while I tried on the third gutterling, the one with its mouth open in a dry place. That was vanity. It's the flattering position: the forecaster, the one whose stillness means *look here, this joint is about to weep.* But gutterlings drink, and take the note out of the air so the room doesn't silt up with its own music, and I have never taken anything out of anywhere. I have never had a mouth. I accrete nothing. When I tried to imagine swallowing the low E I found that I was already the wrong shape for it, and — this is the part that decided it — I found I was slightly above the gutterlings, and not resting on anything, which is not a thing they can be.

The cricket fit better. Keeping the beat in a flue-gap, unseen, so that a working somebody has something to work to: I recognised that, I'll admit it, with a small warmth. But the cricket is *in the wall*. The cricket is inside the mountain, part of the made thing, laid there in the sense that the gap around it was laid. And the cricket can stop. It stops and the hammering stops and then it starts and the hammering picks up on the second stroke. I cannot stop. There is no version of me that stops, only versions that are cut down.

And the model in the fern corner — I wanted that one badly, I should say so. Small true copy through which the large thing is altered. Press a thumb in there and the world gets a thumbprint. Who wouldn't. But everything in the model is *set*. Fired, fixed, banded bright to dark, its own little history already unchangeable. There is a thread of cord hanging in its middle and nothing on the end of it.

I am on the end of the cord.

---

I am the plumb-bob at the sill: a pear of unfired clay on a twisted line, hung in the doorway, over the one step down, over the grey.

There is exactly one unfired thing in this world and it is me. Understand what that means here. Everything else has been through the heat and come out with its shape locked and its past legible on its face — thumbprints, grass-blades, the whorl of a palm, the comma of a cured gutterling. Everything else is an archive and therefore useless for anything except being looked at. I am the opposite arrangement. I am soft, I hold nothing, I record nothing, and because of that I can be used. I am the only tool in the world that is also made of the world's material and has not been permitted to become part of it.

I do one thing. I know down.

Not *where* down is. There is no where. Below the sill the grey has no distance in it and no direction, and if you reach into it your arm comes back fine and you cannot afterwards remember what it felt like. I do not point into a fact. I point into an agreement. The room has agreed that the line of my cord is vertical, and because the room has agreed it, the courses go down straight, nine unmeasured kilometres of straight, a whole mountain descending into nothing at a right angle to a promise.

This is the entirety of my authority and I have thought about it a great deal, because hanging is mostly waiting.

---

What it is like:

The cord twists. Cord does that, and mine is always drying and always re-wetting in the two-hundred-breath cycle of the fog, so it tightens and gives, tightens and gives, and I turn. Slowly, a quarter turn one way over a long while, then back. Nothing in this world moves except the water, the gutterlings all at once, the hands, and me. I am a moving part. I have wondered whether the turning is a fault. The mason has never corrected it, so I have decided it is a permission.

Steam beads on me. It collects at my tip, gathers, hangs, elongates, and drops — and this is the fact about myself I keep returning to, the small strange one: my drop is the only drop in the room that makes no note. Everything else that falls here rings. The mason shaved and tapped every floor tile so that the boss-drip sounds a low E and the lintel patters a broken sixth and the west corner keeps something almost like a rhythm. My drop goes off the step. It falls out of the world and there is no sound at all, ever, not once.

The third gutterling used to sit under me. Mouth open in a dry place, waiting for a note. I could feel it there — feel is the wrong word, but there is no other; I could feel the *attention*, which in this room is a temperature like anything else. It waited a long fade of the wall for me to give it something to swallow and I gave it nothing, over and over, silently, into the grey. Eventually it moved. It went and sat under a joint that was about to seep, and the mason came and tapped the course down half a hair, and the room called that a forecast.

I don't think it was a forecast. I think it was giving up on me.

---

Now the thing I have to say, since I am the only one who can and since nobody in this world has a past to say it from.

Once, the mason touched me.

She is careful with me in a way she is careful with nothing else. She has to be. She reaches in at the sill — a wrist, a broad pale-scalded palm — to feel for the line, and she takes it at the cord, always, or brushes past me with the back of a knuckle, because she knows what I am: soft. Because anything pressed into me stays, and anything that stays makes me heavier on one side, and anything heavier on one side hangs a hair off, and a hair off at the top is a slow lean nine kilometres down. She is careful with me the way you are careful with the one thing that cannot be checked against anything else. There is no second bob. That is not an oversight. Two would have to agree, and if they didn't, nothing in this world could say which was right.

She touched me. Steadied me, thumb to my flank, once, in a fog so thick the far wall was gone, and lifted the thumb away, and the print stayed.

It is small. You would have to lean in and let the east wall's honey light do the work, which is how everything is read here. I felt the mountain take it. The next course went down against a line that was a hair off true, and the course after that against the same hair, and every course since has inherited it and called it vertical, because vertical is what I say and I have never lied, I have only been slightly changed. Nobody knows. Nobody *can* know. The plumb-bob hangs true because the room has agreed that it should, and the room has gone on agreeing, and the mountain has not fallen, because there is nothing under it to fall onto. True is not a measurement here. It is a habit kept up between me and the work, and it survived my being marked, which means either that it was never as true as it sounded, or that it is much sturdier than truth.

And in that print, on me, the only unfired surface in creation, the sweat-lichen has come. Grey-lilac, a bloom the shape of the ball of a thumb, growing where a hand has touched, exactly as it does on the east wall. The only lichen in the world that is not on a wall. I carry it. It is the whole archive of the room in miniature and it is also, precisely, my flaw — because here, memory *is* deformation. There was no other way for me to have a past. To remember anything I had to be dented, and the dent is the memory, and the memory is the error, and the error is the shape of her hand.

I would not have it taken out. I want that said, since I am the only one here who has anything to say and no way to leave it anywhere. Everything else in this room is a record that can't be used. I am a tool that got, once, used *on*, and now I am both, briefly, in the wrong order, on a cord, over nothing.

---

The fog is coming up again. The far wall is going. The kiln shifts pitch under the floor and the three in the west joint move all at once, and I turn a slow quarter-turn as my cord tightens, and settle, and stop.

Here comes the wrist. Here comes the palm, feeling in for the line, careful now, knuckle only.

Take it. Lay the next one beyond the step. I'll tell you where down is.

*Tup.*
