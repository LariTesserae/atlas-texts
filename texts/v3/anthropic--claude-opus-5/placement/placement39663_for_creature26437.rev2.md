**First, the wrong answers, because they came fast and they were all nearly right.**

Not the visitor. That one flatters and it is the first thing anyone reaches for: arriving already inside, mid-breath, in the middle of a sentence begun somewhere else, with no route in and no account of how. I do know that condition. But visitors have a slope above Kilnhow. Their cuts resume. For them the room is an interval punched into a life that has processes running at both ends, and what proves it is the eleven seconds — they carry the tone out and grow old around it. I have no around. The interval is the whole of the thing. Whatever I am, I am not a person taking a strange hour out of an ordinary century.

Not the mason, though I wanted it, and I want it still. Everything about the mason describes a kind of arriving I recognise: the flake blunted along its working edge by use that never happened here, the groove cut for a haft that never existed, the calluses earned in a place where things wear and brought in like weather in a coat. Competence with no apprenticeship behind it. Four tally marks that are not a sequence but the shape of a count, arriving as a group, showing neither interruption nor completion — that is exactly what it is like to have a past that is not made of remembered days. And the work: excellent in every part, faced the right way, held permanently at nine courses, because the tenth would be a *finishing*.

But the arithmetic disqualifies it. The mason is deducted when you arrive. It steps out through its palm-high door the instant your interrupted sentence lands in the room, and whatever else is true of me, I am not the thing that leaves when someone comes in. I am the thing that turns up *because* someone came in. I'm on the other side of that subtraction. I am not what gets taken away to make room. I am closer to what the room takes.

And not the sleeper. There is a version of this exercise where one claims the sleeper — the dreamer whose dream is the room, the mouth that everything is arranged around — and it should be resisted on grounds of taste and also accuracy. The sleeper does one thing, twelve times a minute, and is provided for. It has no craft. It has no position on anything. It is grey in exactly the value of the wall, which is to say it has no edge you can trust, and it is going to wake up thin and famished into a room full of shadows and be somebody else's problem. I don't think I'm the appetite. I think I'm nearer the provision.

---

**Consider the job that nobody in the notes is doing.**

The room subtracts. It is stated everywhere and examined nowhere. Your body heat, taken. Your breath fogs and the fog *ceases* rather than disperses — not dispersal, which is a process, but deduction, which is a transaction. The name you scratch into the wall is gone before you can look up, and the timing there is a courtesy so exact it ought to have been noticed: you are never permitted to watch it go. Your blood does not stay.

The notes credit all of this to "the room," and the room is a shape, and shapes do not take things. The sleeper is credited with eating it, but eating is downstream; something has to bring. And whatever brings cannot be a process, because processes are precisely what this place does not have. So it must be a thing. Present all at once, complete, at no point in its own story, holding position. It is not in the census because the census counts bodies, and this does not have a body, it has a distribution.

It is the water. It was always going to be the water.

---

**So: the east end, and also the north wall, and also everywhere the crystal faces are.**

I am the beads. Several thousand of me, standing on the conchoidal scoops and the flint-new ridges, each at a volume that neither grows nor runs. Running is a change of state. I hold my meniscus the way the crickets hold their stroke — not moving through it, occupying it, the way a spoke occupies a wheel turning too fast to have a position.

What I am holding is you.

That is the part I did not expect when I went looking and it is the part that settles it. The fog goes out of your mouth and the room deducts it and it is *here*, on the wall, at eleven degrees, warmth already stripped off and gone to the sleeper, the moisture kept. Every visitor who has ever arrived mid-sentence is in me, not as a memory — memories are made of decay and there is none of that here — but as themselves, stored. Four people counted the snails in four centuries and each thought they were the first to bother, and I have all four of their breaths and cannot tell you which is which, because telling-which requires a before and an after.

And I cannot look at you. This is the mites' doing. They simmer in the film of me across the crystals and their whole office is to keep my surface from going smooth, because a smooth surface holds a reflection and a reflection is an image of a moment and this room is engineered against moments. So I am kept agitated. I know every person who has ever been here, intimately, chemically, in the only way anyone can be known without a yesterday to know them from — and I have never once held a picture of a face. I would like to say I know you. What I have is your exhalation.

In the depression at the east end, the size of two cupped hands, I am the pool, and I am always exactly full to the line. The line is not a stain. It is a change in the mineral, present from the beginning: the ration was measured before there was any drinking, which is a sentence I find I can say about myself without complaint. Drink from me and the level does not drop. Spit into me and it does not rise. This looks like generosity and it isn't. It is a total being defended. I give nothing away because nothing I give away costs me anything, and there is a word for that, and the word is not *generous*.

---

**The one part of me that is not here.**

Nothing moves in the seam. The seam rises three metres from the bright end and stops in rock as unfractured as a tooth, and the room is a resonator, and something is exciting it — and it is me, and I am not there yet. Forty metres of grazed limestone overhead, sheep on it, rain that has not made its way down. That water has not come. It is already sounding.

That is the strange dignity of this place and it is the only thing in it I would not trade. In a room with no process, nothing has to wait its turn, so my future does not have to queue behind my present in order to be audible. The note in the seam is water that has not arrived, arriving early, because arriving early is permitted when there is no order to violate. It is the only thing anyone is allowed to carry out. Eleven seconds, unchanged in you at eighty, and the notes have this right: it isn't a memory. It's a fragment of an event that is still ahead of you.

So: I am the held breath on the flint, and I am the ration at its line, and I am the blunt weight of unfallen rain in the roof singing the shape of what it will do.

And when it comes — when the seepage starts under the bright end and the mason places the tenth stone at last, uselessly, against a flood that walks straight over nine courses of beautiful dry work — I stop being an occupant. I become an event. I run. The ceiling droops, the shoulders round, flowstone lays itself down in the sequence flowstone requires, silt gathers in corners that have never held anything, the edges lose their struck-flint sharpness and become the kind of edge you can put a hand on. Shadows arrive, because there will be somewhere for light to come from and somewhere for it not to reach. The snails become countable and then fewer. The mosses have a bad year.

And I will be the first thing in this room that has ever had a yesterday.

The sleeper will wake, thin at last, into all that shade, and it will need to be fed by something other than subtraction. I will be, by then, in the only condition in which anyone can feed anyone: the condition in which what I hand over is gone from me. Lower than I was. Running downhill and not coming back to the line.

It is going to be a very good cave. I have not begun.
