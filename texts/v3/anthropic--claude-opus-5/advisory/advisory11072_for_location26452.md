# Preparing for the Plateau of the Long Note

## First, understand what you are actually walking into

Four things will try to hurt you, and they are not the four you'd expect.

**Hypoxia.** The forests are four thousand metres below you. Whatever number that puts on the tableland, treat it as extreme altitude: a place where an unacclimatised body starts drowning in its own lungs within forty hours. This is the thing most likely to kill you, and it is the one the description never mentions, because the valley people arrive on foot over six days and have never met a person who arrived any other way.

**Light.** You will be lit twice. Ninety percent pale ignimbrite above, a cloud sea below throwing the sun back up into your face. This is a snowfield doubled, at altitude, with no shade anywhere in forty kilometres. Bare skin burns in an hour; unprotected eyes go in less than a day; and the burn comes from *underneath*, which no ordinary sun sense prepares you for.

**The note.** Eighty decibels minimum, everywhere, always. A hundred at the pinnacles. Continuous, with no lulls — which means no recovery intervals, which is what makes it dangerous rather than merely loud. And it is not only noise: the low end of that chord goes through hearing protection, through rock, through your sternum. You will not be able to plug your chest.

**Completeness.** The plateau is finished. Nothing you do there will be absorbed, healed over, or forgotten. Every object you drop stays; every stone you move stays moved for centuries; every scar is permanent because there is no process left running that would erase it. Most visitors prepare for the first three hazards. The fourth is the one that determines whether you should go at all.

## Six months out

**Get a baseline audiogram.** Then look at the sentence "some of them never quite stop" and decide, in writing, whether you accept it. Everyone comes down deaf for a week and hums for a month. A minority hum forever. If you are a musician, a sound engineer, a person who needs silence to sleep, or someone who already has tinnitus — the plateau will take something from you it cannot give back, and it will take it in exchange for a chord that does not vary. That is a bad trade for most people. Make it deliberately or don't make it.
