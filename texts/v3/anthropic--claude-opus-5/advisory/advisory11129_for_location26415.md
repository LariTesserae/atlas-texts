# Preparing for the Plateau of Long Machines

## First, an honest filter

Most people should not go. Not for reasons of courage — for reasons of hardware.

You will be, on arrival, the least competent organism on the plateau. A grazer knows the shape of the pool ahead. A climber in the curtains knows where your throat is. A local child of six can hear the difference between a shelf of rimstone and a shelf of rimstone with something standing on it. You will know nothing, and the only way to survive that is to accept it completely and structurally: you go with a guide, you go on a short leash, you go for a short time, and you go having spent a year making yourself less useless.

Rule yourself out, immediately and without negotiation, if you have any of the following: hearing loss of any kind, chronic tinnitus, recurrent ear infections, perforated tympanic membranes, poor balance, a vestibular disorder, untreated dental disease, uncontrolled asthma or COPD, or any history of psychosis or dissociative episodes. On the plateau, deafness is death, an earache is blindness, and a tooth with a hot nerve will be found, hourly, by four kilometres of turbine grind coming up through your jaw.

Also rule yourself out if you cannot honestly promise not to make light. We will come back to this. It is the whole thing.

## The one rule that eats all the others

**Do not bring a light source. Not one. Not shielded, not sealed, not "for emergencies."**

Travellers argue this. They point out, reasonably, that a single shrouded diode inside a padded case, used once, for three seconds, in a stone hut, could save a life.
