# Field Preparation: The Increment

## First, understand what will be dangerous about you

Most hazard briefings for a new world begin with the air, the water, the microbes, the police. Here they begin with your resemblance to yourself.

You are, as a multiverse traveler, a pattern that occurs in a great many places. That has never mattered before. Elsewhere, the fact that some hundreds of thousands of near-identical yous are shaving in near-identical bathrooms across the sheaf of worlds is a philosophical curiosity, an occasional source of vertigo at three in the morning. On the Increment it is a physical property, and the mountain can feel it.

Nobody there can see your other addresses. But the mountain's logic does not require anyone to see. Concordance is not a belief system; it is what the place does with sameness. And you will walk onto that mountain carrying more sameness than anything else on it.

So: everything below follows from one principle, which the guilds teach children before letters, in the form of a notch cut into a doorframe.

**Be unrepeatable. Bring nothing that is twice.**

## Six months out: the body

**Break your symmetries.** This sounds superstitious and is not. Your two hands are the most nearly identical objects you will bring, and they will be next to each other, doing the same work, all day. Locals never let a pair alone. Wear a ring on one hand from now, and get used to its weight; grow one thumbnail long; part your hair off-centre and keep it that way; adopt a small habitual asymmetry of gait. It doesn't matter if it's arbitrary. It matters that it's *yours* and that it's *held* — the local word for a thing that has no sharers.

Get one ear pierced. Not two. Two is a pair.

**Unlearn counting.** This is the hardest part and needs the full six months. You count constantly: stairs, paces, days of medication, coins, the beat of your own footsteps, items in a shopping list. Every one of those habits is a small pressure applied to the world, and on the Increment pressure applied to concordant things does one of two things — nothing, or something enormous and expensive that you will be liable for.

Practise navigating without tallies. Learn to estimate by feel: "about a floor and a half," "a long flight," "two songs' worth of walking." Learn to check medication by colour, by taste, by which pocket. Learn to say *some* and *a few* and *a fistful* and mean them. If you find yourself doing arithmetic in company, stop mid-sum; there is no polite way to finish.

**Learn to be arrhythmic.** Metronomic behaviour is regarded on the Increment roughly the way a persistent cough is regarded in a tuberculosis ward. The mountain's physicians speak of patients who "fell into time" — whose breath or heartbeat became so regular that successive beats stopped being successive. This is why your breath hangs in the air longer than it should up there. Consecutive exhalations are not quite consecutive.

You cannot do much about your heart. You can do a great deal about your breathing, your walking, your speech. Learn a limp you can hold for hours. Learn to breathe in an irregular pattern without thinking about it — swimmers and wind players find this easy; office workers find it takes months. Never hum a repeating figure. Never march.

**Acclimatize.** You are going above a cloud deck, high, thin, and cold-wet. Eight degrees, constant, with condensate on every surface — that is a temperature that will kill you slowly and politely if your clothing wets through, and everything there wets through. Spend time cold and damp. Learn to be functional at eight degrees with wet cuffs for twelve hours. Get your altitude tolerance honestly, in mountains, not in a chamber.

**Acquire a scar.** Preferably by accident, in the course of doing something no other version of you would plausibly have done. This is not mysticism; it is documentation. At the crossing, if there is an examination — and at most crossings there is — you will be asked what holds you. It is a real question with a real answer and "I'm me" is not it.

## Three months out: the kit

The doctrine is simple and absolute: **no two items alike, and no item that is a copy of anything you have left behind.**

This will destroy your normal packing instincts, which are all built on redundancy. Two of everything critical. Matched sets. Spares. Standardized modular systems. All of that is not merely useless on the Increment, it is actively lethal, because *redundancy does not work there*. Your two identical water filters are one filter with two addresses. When it clogs, both clog. Your triple-redundant beacon is a single beacon that you have paid for three times and that will fail once, completely.

Rules that follow:

- **Backups must differ in kind, not degree.** A pump filter and iodine and a boiling pot. A wind-up torch, a chemical light, and a candle. Three different manufacturers, three different mechanisms, three different centuries of technology if you can manage it.
- **Deface everything before you leave home.** Not on arrival — before. Every buckle, plate, bottle, boot, tool, and bootlace gets a personal mark: a filed notch, a smear of coloured lacquer, a knot of odd cord, a drop of some scent you can identify blind. Locals do this so instinctively that a foreigner with clean, unmarked, factory-fresh gear reads to them roughly as a man walking into a hospital ward licking the handrails. Your marks should be *ugly*. Elegance repeats; ugliness is specific.
- **Socks and gloves must not match.** Nothing that comes in pairs may be allowed to remain a pair. Cut a corner off one glove cuff. Dye one sock. This feels ridiculous until the first time you watch a newcomer's mittens become one mitten and their off hand goes numb in a way that no fire will fix.
- **Medication is the single most dangerous thing in your bag.** A blister pack of thirty identical tablets is one tablet with thirty addresses. You will take your course of antibiotics faithfully and receive, in total, a single dose. Bring liquids where possible. Where not, have a compounding pharmacist make your doses in varied colours and shapes, or scratch each tablet differently with a pin before you go, and store them separately. Yes, this is absurd. Do it anyway. This is the failure mode that kills more visitors than the drops do.
- **No mirrors.** Or rather: no clean mirrors. Carry one small mirror deliberately scratched across the face. An unmarked mirror on the Increment is an argument that there are two of you, and it is an argument the mountain sometimes accepts.
- **Photography is compromised.** Digital storage duplicates by nature; your primary and your backup are one drive. Frames of identical exposure blur into each other. Bring a notebook and write by hand — handwriting is the one recording medium in your possession that cannot repeat itself. Draw badly and often. The Increment is a place best documented in prose and worse sketches.
- **No clocks that tick audibly, no metronomes, no drums, no decks of cards, no dice, no coins of the same denomination, no counters, no beads, no rosaries.** If you need something to fidget with, bring a stone.
- **Food, if you bring it, must be varied item by item.** More on this below.

**Clothing specifics.** You want a brimless cap with a stiff *lower* flange — the local design, and there is no substitute; the standard visored hat is worse than nothing because it shades you from a sun that isn't there and leaves the ground-glare full in your eyes. Get one made before you go or buy one at the first terrace market and accept the price. You want dark lenses ground for upward light, which is to say heavily tinted at the bottom and clear at the top, so you can still read the shadowed faces of people talking to you. Regular sunglasses will leave you unable to see anyone's expression for the length of your stay.
