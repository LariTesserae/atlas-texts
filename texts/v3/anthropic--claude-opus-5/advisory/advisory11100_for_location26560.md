# Visiting The Pocket
## A preparation guide for the ordinary traveller

---

### First: decide whether you are the kind of person who can be in a room without helping it

This is the whole of the preparation. Everything below is logistics.

The Pocket is not in distress. Nothing in it needs you. The oak is not dying of thirst; it is holding two leaves the way a held note holds a note. The lamp is not waiting for a match; it is waiting for the century that will need it. The tally is not unfinished, it is *forty-one*, and forty-one is the number it has. The swallows are not trapped.

Every visitor who has been refused entry — and refusal happens at the level of the body, in the form of a persistent, humiliating tremble in the hands — has been refused for the same reason. They arrived in a room that was *being made*, and they could not stop themselves from having opinions about the result.

Spend a month before departure practising this: sit in a room with something slightly wrong in it (a picture hung crooked, a tap dripping, a chair out of line) and do not fix it. Do not fix it for an hour. Then two. If you find at the end of a fortnight that you have stopped noticing the crooked picture, you are not ready — you have solved the problem by going numb, and numbness is no good in the Pocket, where you must be entirely awake and entirely idle at once. You want the state where the crookedness remains vivid and interesting and your hands stay where they are.

**Do not bring:** anyone who talks when nervous; anyone with a cough; anyone who whistles unconsciously; children; dogs; a partner you are currently quarrelling with; anyone who describes themselves as "hands-on."

---

### The transit

The Pocket is nine metres inside a body of soft limestone in a district with no people in it, in an era with no people in it, on a coastline that has not yet been named by anything with a mouth. There is no shaft. There is no crack to the surface. The gap the swallows use leads only to more stone.

This means two things, and you must sit with both of them for a while before you commit.

**One: your arrival must be accurate to well under a metre.** There is no walking in. There is no walking anywhere. Ten metres of error in any direction and you arrive inside rock that has not yet agreed to be a cliff, which is to say rock with the consistency of hard cheese, which is to say you will have perhaps four seconds in which to be extremely surprised. Do not economise on transit. Use a house that will show you their placement records, that talks about metres and not "the vicinity," and that has hit the Pocket before. Ask them which pocket. There are several thousand in the district, none connected, none aware of the others, and most of the cheap operators will happily deposit you in a dark one that is closing.

**Two: there is no rescue.** Not slow rescue. None. No signal leaves the district, and if it did, there is nobody in that world with ears. Your extraction is a mechanism you carry with you, and it is the single most important object in your kit, and you should carry two of them, on separate parts of your body, and you should test both before departure and neither inside.

Ask about the return anchor specifically. Most anchoring systems key to local geometry, and the Pocket's geometry is a draft. **Count the walls twice and you get different answers; an anchor that has counted the walls once is an anchor that will be wrong later.** Insist on a temporal anchor, or a cadence anchor. The drip is every eleven seconds without exception, forever, and it is the only honest metronome in the room. Reputable houses key to the drip. If yours proposes keying to "the threshold stone" or "the north wall," find another house.

### The body, in the two months before

**Cold-and-damp conditioning.** Nine degrees, always, at effectively total humidity, with condensation forming on every surface including you. This is a temperature that reads as *mild* for the first twenty minutes and as a serious medical problem at hour five. There is no exertion available to warm you, because you will be moving very slowly and mostly not at all. Practise sitting still, dressed as you intend to be dressed, in a cellar at nine degrees with a plant mister, for three hours, without shivering enough to make a sound.

**Lag training.** Your shadow will arrive late, then overshoot, then settle. So will the shadow of your hand as you reach for something, which is worse. In most people this produces within forty minutes a specific and unglamorous nausea — vestibular, like a ferry, sited behind the ears. Train it out: a fortnight of working with a delayed video feed of your own hands, twenty minutes twice a day, doing fiddly tasks. Also bring ginger, or whatever anti-emetic you trust, taken *before* arrival, not after; and know the trick, which is to stop looking at your own shadow and look at the shadow of an animal instead. **The animals' shadows are the honest clocks.** A grazing sifter's shadow is precisely where a shadow ought to be. Rest your eyes on a sifter for a minute and the sickness releases.

**Silence fitness.** This is harder than people expect. Sound in the Pocket is a substance, it is farmed, and everything you emit will be caught, stored, and released later, out of tune, into a room that may by then contain someone. You are not being asked to be quiet as a courtesy. You are being asked to be quiet because you are pouring material into an economy you do not understand.

Practise four hours of true silence — not *shh* silence, but the discipline of moving a body through a cluttered space without producing a single event. No throat-clearing. No sniffing. No sighing on exertion. No breath through the mouth. Learn to yawn with your jaw shut. Learn to cough into the crook of your arm slowly, over three seconds, which converts a bang into a smear and is much kinder to the webs.

Do not go with a head cold. A single sneeze in the Pocket is the loudest thing that will happen there for eleven thousand years, and it will be released, undated, into some future dark, and someone will build a religion on it.

### Clothing

**Wool.** Wool everything, several layers, close-woven, and a second full set sealed and carried. Cotton will wet through and take your heat; synthetics squeak, rustle, and make a small chirping noise at every knee-bend which you will not hear and the glasswrights will.

**No zips. No Velcro. No plastic buckles. No clips.** Tape over anything that could click. Sew your fastenings if you have time. This sounds precious. It is not: every click is a bead of tension hung on a filament, drunk or bundled or dropped, and it will come out somewhere, sometime, in the wrong key.

**Boots: soft, wide, felted or crepe soles, and larger than your foot.** This is not for grip. **The floor is not rock yet — it takes the print and keeps it.** A narrow heel goes in like a die-stamp. A wide soft sole distributes and leaves a vague, ambiguous depression, which in four hundred years will be an argument rather than a fact. Decide beforehand which you want to leave behind: an argument, or a fact. There is a case for either. There is no case for leaving it accidentally.

**A hood, and a fine net over the face.** Two reasons. First, you shed — hair, skin, eyelashes — and the floor takes everything and the dust closes over it within the hour, and you do not want to be the district's earliest organic contaminant, indexed and puzzled over by people who will not be born for a hundred million years. Second, the webs. You find them with your face. A net finds them a centimetre earlier and, crucially, finds them *without breaking them*, if you are moving slowly enough, which you will be.

**Gloves: thin, wool, and a spare pair.** Bare skin on the floor or walls is a decision, not an accident. See below.

**Gaiters, and shin protection.** There are five chalk shrews. They are blind, quick, furious, and territorial, and they patrol the crystal bank. They are the only animal in the Pocket that is ever in a hurry, which means they are the only animal that will make contact with you at speed. They cannot see you and will not be deterred by your size. They bite ankles. It is not dangerous; it is startling, and startling is exactly what you cannot afford, because startled people make sound.

### Kit

Carry little. Everything you carry will be damp within ten minutes and will remain damp, and everything you drop becomes an artefact that is not old but *early*, and which will accumulate wear it has not received.

**Two extraction mechanisms**, separately stowed, drip-keyed.

**A mechanical watch, wound, and a second one.** They will disagree. Do not attempt to resolve the disagreement. Use them as a bracket, and use your bladder as the tiebreaker; your bladder is from your own universe and is not participating.

**A wax tablet and stylus, or waxed paper and a soft pencil.** Not a notebook. Paper turns to porridge here in under an hour. Not a voice recorder — you will hear more about that below.

**A web wand.** This is the one piece of specialist equipment and you should make it yourself: a metre of thin cane with a long, soft feather bound at the tip, ideally something with fine barbules. Held ahead of you at face height and swept very slowly, it finds filaments by drag before your body finds them by tearing. Practise with it at home, in the dark, against threads stretched across a doorway, until you can walk the length of a corridor at web speed, which is about one pace per five seconds.

**A hand mirror.** Not for grooming. For a test, on arrival — see below.

**A knotted cord, six metres.** For measuring relations, not distances. Distances are not settled. Relations are.

**Your own water, sealed, and more than you think.** You will not drink from the seep, or the puddle, or the condensation, for reasons that should be obvious and which will be spelled out anyway.

**Dense food that requires no chewing sound.** Soft cheese, paste, cooked grain. Nothing crisp. Nothing in foil.

**Containment for your own waste, and the discipline to use it.** You do not urinate into a room that is deciding what it is going to be made of.

**A small, dry, dependable warmth**: chemical heat packs, several, and a wool cap. No flame. Nothing that burns.

**Nothing that emits light.** You will not need it. If you need it, something has gone very wrong and you should be leaving, not looking.

### The first ninety seconds

Arrive, and do not move.
