# Field Preparation: The Dry Canopy

## A Traveler's Brief for Ordinary Humans

---

## 0. What kind of trip this is

Understand first what you are, in the frame of reference of this place.

You are a bag of warm salt water. You are, by mass, sixty percent of a substance that has never existed on that plateau in four billion years — not in the rock, not in the air, not as a trace in the mineral lattice. You breathe out a fine mist of it. You leak it through your skin continuously. You are also warm, which nothing there is, and conductive, which is the local definition of *edible*.

You will be the strangest thing that plateau has ever hosted, and the plateau will notice you, in the specific, non-conscious, thoroughly attentive way that a hundred thousand square kilometers of interlocked living circuitry notices a short.

So: this is not a wilderness trip. It's closer to a clean-room visit, run in reverse — you are the contaminant, and you are also extremely fragile. Prepare accordingly, on both counts, and hold both ideas at once. Travelers who only prepare for their own survival damage the place. Travelers who only prepare to be respectful die.

Plan seven to ten days on the surface for a first visit. Water mass is your hard limit, and you will hit it before you hit boredom.

---

## 1. The four things that kill you, ranked by how fast

**1. The air.** Nitrogen and carbon dioxide at roughly one bar. There is no free oxygen worth measuring. Unlike inert-gas asphyxia, which is gentle and stupid and kills people who don't notice, a CO₂-rich breath is instantly, violently unmistakable: air hunger inside two seconds, panic inside four, unconsciousness within twenty to forty. This is good news and bad news. You will always know when your loop is breached. You will not be calm about it.

**2. Desiccation.** Zero humidity — not "arid," *zero*. The relative humidity of that atmosphere is not low, it is undefined. Any unprotected mucous membrane begins failing in minutes: corneas fog and abrade, lips split, nasal mucosa cracks and bleeds. An unmasked human on that plateau, given oxygen somehow, would still be in serious trouble within the hour and dying within the day. Your suit is not primarily an air supply. It is a *water* supply.

**3. Electricity.** The ground is at a wildly different potential from the sky and the entire biosphere lives in the gradient between. There are megavolt columns on a schedule. There are predators whose whole hunting strategy is completing a circuit through a body. Walking there is like walking across a working substation the size of Britain, wearing a hat made of meat.

**4. Cold, slowly.** Six to eight degrees sounds survivable. It is not, in a dense cold CO₂-nitrogen atmosphere with about 1.6 times the density of Earth air and correspondingly greedy convection. Six degrees there strips heat off you the way twelve degrees of moving water does. Add wind. Add the older stack interiors, which are *colder than the outside* and radiate nothing at all, in flat defiance of accounting, and which will pull the heat out of you at a rate your instruments will report and your body will not warn you about.

## 2. Your body, in the months before

**Cardiac screening, done properly.** You are going to spend a week inside continuous 8–60 Hz infrasound at amplitudes that move your sternum, in an environment where a defibrillator is a nine-day round trip away, occasionally being investigated by animals that hunt with electricity. Get the full workup. Any conduction abnormality, any history of arrhythmia, any implanted device — this trip is not for you, and no amount of shielding changes that. Pacemakers and the Dry Canopy are simply incompatible; the field environment alone is disqualifying.
