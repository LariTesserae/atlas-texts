# Preparing for the Ninth Throat

## 1. First, decide honestly whether you are going

Almost everything about this trip is survivable. The part that is not negotiable is that you will spend some minutes inside a small, brilliant, freezing room where your mind does not work, and you will have to have arranged, in advance, for a body that can finish the job without you.

Hard contraindications, in the plain sense that people have died of them here:

- **Photosensitive epilepsy.** The sunlight bar splits on the falling column and the whole chamber scintillates at some rate nobody has ever measured. There is no shadow to look into and no way to shut your eyes and still know which way is up.
- **Any middle-ear, sinus, or vestibular disorder.** Air spaces are where sound of this intensity does its damage. Your sinuses are cavities in the bone plate the note plays through.
- **Recent dental work, cracked enamel, an unfilled cavity, a loose crown.** The note couples through your jaw. A bad tooth is a nail being driven.
- **Recent eye surgery, retinal fragility, high myopia.** Your eyeballs will vibrate in their sockets. Everybody's do.
- **Pregnancy.** No one who understands the place will take you down.
- **Panic disorder, or any relationship with claustrophobia that you have not tested.** A room four metres across, entirely full of water, with forty centimetres of shuddering air at the top and one exit the width of a shoulder.

Also worth admitting to yourself: you will lose hearing. Not all of it, not necessarily much, but some, permanently, and you will acquire a tinnitus that is not a hiss but a pitch, and it will be *that* pitch. Old tenders can hum the note of the year they were twenty. Get a baseline audiogram before you leave your own world, so that afterwards you can know exactly what you traded.

## 2. Twelve months out: the body

**Altitude.** The mouth of the shaft is above four thousand metres and the approach is longer than it looks, over unweathered spurs with no talus and no path, on a mountain that is still assembling itself. Plan three weeks of acclimatisation minimum. Understand that altitude also cuts your breath-hold to something humiliating — perhaps half of what you can do at sea level — and cold cuts it again, and terror cuts it again. Whatever you can hold in a warm pool, assume fifteen seconds of it survives the descent.
