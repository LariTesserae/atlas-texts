# Preparing to Visit the Throat

## I. First, the honest accounting

Understand before anything else what this trip costs, because unlike most destinations, this one takes payment in advance and does not refund.

**You will lose some hearing, permanently, and you cannot prevent it.** Foam plugs plus muffs give you thirty-five, maybe forty decibels of attenuation on paper, and paper lies at sixty cycles — low frequency is precisely where hearing protection performs worst. Worse, above about fifty decibels of attenuation you run into your own skeleton. Sound at these levels arrives through your jaw, your mastoid, the top of your skull, the soles of your feet. Plug your ears perfectly and seal them in a helmet and you are still standing inside roughly a hundred decibels of bone-conducted noise for as long as you are out in the loud. A week's visit, managed carefully, will cost you a notch in the low mid-range and a tinnitus that never entirely goes. A month will cost you conversation in restaurants for the rest of your life. The locals are stone deaf by fifty and consider this the natural shape of a life.

Get an audiogram before you go, so that afterward you know what you spent.

Also get your teeth checked. Cracked molars and old fillings do not enjoy sustained sixty-cycle excitation, and there is no dentist in the pit.

## II. What to leave in the cache on the other side

More important than what you bring.

**Every source of ignition.** Lighters, matches, ferrocerium, stoves, chemical hand-warmers, anything with a lithium cell in it, anything that can arc. The garden is silica-stiffened tubing full of terpene oil, standing in drifts of finely divided sulfur, in air with free hydrogen in it, at oven temperature, in a country with no water. This is not a prohibition, it is a physical fact wearing the clothes of a law. A single flame in the reedwood ends forty-one lives and two centuries of survey work in about four minutes. The penalty is expulsion onto the gypsum, which is the same as the other penalty. Wear cotton, linen, leather; the air is bone-dry and synthetics will build charge you can feel crawling on your arms. Ground your tools against the plaster before you touch a stem.

**Anything that records sound.** The entire reason this city exists is that speech in a still stays in the still. That property is their courthouse, their confessional, their marriage bed, their diplomacy, and their only export. Carrying a recorder across a threshold is not rudeness; it is arson of a different kind. If you must carry a device for other reasons, declare it at the lip, open it, let it be handled, and expect it to be kept for you.

**Anything that measures a pressure field.** This is the subtle one, and the reason thoughtful travelers turn back. A pocket sound-level meter, a phone with a microphone, a cheap array of piezos — any of these would let a deaf sixty-year-old mason survey a node in ten seconds, with no young ears to verify the sand. The whole social architecture here rests on the fact that the young must hear for the old, and the old must build for the young, and neither can do the other's work. You would be carrying the end of that in a hip pocket. Perhaps it should end. It is not yours to end in an afternoon, on your way through.

**Steel chisels.** Same argument, softer. Their copper is soft enough to sharpen on a stone, and their limestone yields to it, and their pace of building is matched to the pace of the pit's dissolution. Leave the gift-set at home.
