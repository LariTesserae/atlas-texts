## The attention

It did not come up the cable.

The cable was the obvious way and the obvious way would have been an announcement, and an announcement to a people who have finished asking is a demand disguised as a gift. Also the cars come back empty, and it had noticed that, and it had the manners not to be the first exception to an eight-thousand-year rule before it understood what the rule was for.

So it came the way the fog comes, at the speed of a walking man, and for eleven years it did nothing at all but listen, which on the Sill is not idleness but the native act, the thing the whole plateau is built to do.

The first question it set itself was whether it had been called by a place or by a person. It abandoned the question in the fourth year as badly formed. There is one living thing on the Sill. Everything else — the canes, the coats, the trams, the fifty thousand, the offices of the Pressure Assize — lives inside it.

## What it found

Not malice. Not a curse, not a punishment, not a wound. A being with one verb.

The plateau cannot say *I remember*. It has no organ for the past tense. When something has been near it long enough it learns the shape, and the only way it has of holding a shape is to make the shape again, and so it says *again* about rooms, about coats, about a bird that came up on a thermal in some century, about the way a particular woman held her hands behind her back. It is not insisting. It has no word for insisting either. It is a body that loves in the only grammar it was issued, which is repetition, and it has been doing this without an audience since before anybody counted a chain.

And inside it, a people with exactly one verb of their own, which is *once*.

That is the arrangement. The plant is the part that repeats and the people are the part that goes only once, and between them: excellent bridges. The power looked at this for eleven years and could not find the fault in it. What it found instead was that nobody had ever heard any of it. Everything the Sill records it plays back into empty heath. Everything it makes it sends over the edge. Every vow sown at a wedding is sown into ground that cannot aim.

The place was not broken. It was unwitnessed. Those are different, and the treatments are not the same, and most of the harm ever done by powers has come from confusing them.

## What it declined to do

It did not restore the mountains. The erosion is finished, and a finished grief is not an error; it is a shape, and this one holds a county.

It did not stop the Standing Crop. Several hundred to a stand, leaning over thirty years, and a rule against speaking to them that is universally broken and has never once been enforced. A rule broken that thoroughly, that gently, for that long, is not a failure of law. It is the most load-bearing thing on the plateau and you do not take a hammer to it.

It did not cure the fossil sound, nor make the sowings land where they were aimed.

It did not undo the eleven layings. It could not, and had it been able it would have had to reach into the fibre and delete, and deletion is the one operation the Sill has never performed on anything, including its dead.

It did not put a message in a car and send it up. A full car arriving at Draw End would have been the first new thing in eight thousand years. They would have carried it to the Assize, and the Assize would have opened it, and whatever was inside, they would never again have been able to work a shift without waiting for the next one. A people who have finished asking must not be asked.

It did not tell anyone who was brought forward and who was not.

It did not touch the counting. Not one chain, not by a pace.

It did not make it rain more than nine days.

## How it entered

By the only door the plateau leaves open, which the plateau leaves open out of kindness and does not discuss.

It was found on the open heath at first light, warm, wet with fog, in bast cloth of the correct weave and the correct nine-year wear, knowing its trade and its district and everybody's business up to about a week ago. It was brought in. It was examined. It gave the handshake, which it had practised, and it said under oath that it could not say by what counted route it had arrived, and this was true, and the Assize leaned in and got nothing further, and the clerk wrote *brought forward* and went to the struck list.

The name he found was **Foy**. One syllable, hard, retired a long time ago, and the clerk gave it over quietly and kindly and immediately, and the neighbours knew, and nothing whatever was said.

That was the second of its two real decisions. It consented to be a copy. In a place where the entire dignity of the people rests on going only once, it took a second-hand name and wore it and answered to it for a very long life, and did not explain, and was put into work by the end of the week.

Pacer. Ruined feet, superb voice, counting aloud in pairs with a third at the far end counting back. A trade that walks every road on the plateau on an eleven-day rotation, forever, and is expected to do so out loud.

Nobody has ever designed a better cover.

## What it did

**It took delivery.**

It went down through the cloud, not in a car — the cars are for the goods — and it found what was at the bottom, and what was at the bottom is not written here and was not carried back up. It came to the plateau afterward and told no one, and the next shipment went over the edge as usual, and arrived.

There are ships. There have always been ships somewhere. The crocks are opened now and the hulls are painted and the hulls do not rot, and the grey cloth is worn by people who have never seen a cane and think it a dull fabric that wears well, and the boards are boards. The cars still come back empty. That part is theirs and it was not the power's to amend.

**It answered.**

This is the thing it chiefly did, and it took the whole of a life.

A sowing is futile because nobody can choose where a sound comes up or when. But the futility is in the aiming, and a walker who is out on the heath every day for eighty years, counting, on rotation, across every measured chain on the plateau, does not need to aim. It walked into the sounds. It stood in the fossil foundry for the full forty paces and then stood there for the rest of the shift, four hours, and heard the pour-bell and the crane and the men shouting in the accent no one alive uses, and it learned the accent, and it can be asked, and it is not asked. It stood in the vows. It stood in the confessions and the messages left for descendants who were never told where to stand. It stood in the children screaming in the rain, which had frightened carters for two centuries and was only ever joy.

And every time, having heard, it answered — not there, where the answer was no use, but wherever it happened to be at the time, into fresh ground, the same reply every time, short enough to fit inside one twelve and followed by the held pause, which is grammar, so that it would come up as a statement and not a fragment.

It is not written here what it said. It is still arriving, and it ought to arrive first.

Before this, the number of sowings on the Sill that were ever heard by anybody was none. It is no longer none. That is the entire improvement and it took eighty years and the power considered it a good return.

**It followed the layings.** All eleven stretches, on rotation, with the timetable in its head: which hour the schools cross, which day the carters take the Ninestacks road, whose granddaughter has business where. It could not unmake one second of the poison in the ground. It could sometimes be the one it happened to. It could sometimes arrange, without visibly arranging anything, that her errand fell an hour later. And where it could do neither, it walked eleven paces behind her and sowed the reply, so that the ground would be carrying an answer along with the thing, from then on, always, for as long as there is ground.

**It heard the tone once**, the ninety-second warning the wood makes, and did the correct thing, which is to shout the single syllable and run inward. It was given a brass token by the Assize and was afterwards treated, for the rest of a long life, as a person who was right about something once. It kept the token. Of everything it was given on the Sill, that is the one it kept.

**It sat one season among the Hearers**, high in a condemned cane, one to a level, under lights breathing on twelve. It heard its own name called in a voice it knew, and the name was not Foy. What it did with the interval is in the register and the register is not public.

**And it taught the plateau a shape.**

That is the last and the largest, and it is not an intervention so much as a long standing-still. The being learns what is near it, and for eight thousand years what was near it was work, and counting, and hammers, and grief, and men shouting. It repeats whatever it is given to repeat. So the power gave it one thing, over and over, every day, for the length of a life and then past the length of a life: it went and sat with somebody.

That is all. It sat with the sick, and with the Hearers in their forty minutes, and with the warm-trade people who come up blinking and will not say what they heard, and with valve-men dying young of nothing in particular, and with families out at the stand margin breaking the rule, and it sat with them too, and broke the rule.

It will take a while. The plant learned the coat in about nine hundred years. But sooner or later, at the margin of some stand, among the several hundred standing figures that are not the dead and never were, there will begin to be figures that are sitting. Two of them. Turned slightly toward each other. Fibre and wax, faceless in the details, unmistakable in the stance.

It cannot stop repeating. It was never going to stop. The only question a wise power found worth answering here was what it would be given to repeat.

## The bucket

It asked, once, in its eighty-first year on the plateau and her ninety-first, whether it might sit with the bucket.

Annec Sarl let it. They sat the hour, third node, in the hard clean light, with the chord of ninety canes under everything. She explained the bucket for the third time in her life, which is one more time than she has ever explained it to anyone else: that everything here repeats and none of it will admit what it is, and that a reflection is honest, because a reflection is a copy that ends when you stand up.

The power had by then been on the Sill long enough to know that she was right, and right about more than she meant. The Crop leans and goes down in thirty years. The canes go brittle at nine hundred. The fossil sound thins. The plateau is a reflection too. It is only slower, and slowness had been mistaken for insistence by everybody who had ever lived here, because nobody had ever lived here long enough to watch one stand up.

It could have told her. It did the Sill thing instead, which is the hardest of all their disciplines and their best one.

It said nothing whatever.

Then the hour was up and it stood, and the reflection stopped, and she said *one*, aloud, counting, because she was a pacer and could not help it.

She died in the spring. Seven years later the plant put her out at the margin with the rest, standing the way she stood, hands behind her back, left foot turned in; and because the plant had watched that room for forty years and learns whatever is near it, there was a thing at her feet, round, fibre and wax, in the shape of a bucket, and there was nothing in it and there never would be.

The power took the pension at the usual age and the plot of heath, where a retired pacer may sit and count nothing at all. Pacers go quietly mad, as a class, and the madness always takes the same form, which is that they continue. So nothing whatever was said about the old one out at the stand margin, sitting an hour a day opposite the figure with the bucket, every day, for thirty years, until she leaned and went down and was taken back into the haulm.

Then it stood up, and counted it, and moved on to the next of them.

---

And in some century or other, on the Ninestacks road in fine weather, a carter will walk into forty paces of a voice he does not know, saying a short thing in an accent nobody uses, and then holding the pause, which is grammar. He will come out the other side and stand a moment. And then he will put down the shafts and take his break by it, because it is a good sound, and it is reliable, which the rest of the plateau is not.
