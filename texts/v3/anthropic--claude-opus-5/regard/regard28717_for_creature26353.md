## The Attention

Something came down the stair.

Not as light. That was the first wisdom, and nearly everything else followed from it. A being whose habit is radiance, arriving in a district that manufactures illumination and lives in the dim, would have blinded four people who cannot walk a market at noon without smoked glass — would have arrived, that is, as an injury, and been remembered as one. So it came down as warmth, which is the local grammar of goodness, at a temperature that made no moss thicken and no fern lean, and it came down at the pace of the treads.

It submitted to the sandglass. This should be recorded, because it did not have to. The glass was turned at the newel post and it went up when the sand ran out, and came again the next day, and the day after that, for a year, and in that year it did nothing whatever except learn. It could have known, from above, the temperature of every surface in the hall to the hundredth of a degree, and did know, and came down and learned it again with a palm laid on gauge-moss, because knowing a place and being taught by a place are two different acquisitions and only the second one entitles you to touch anything.

By the end of the year it could read the moss to within a degree. It got a fracture face across the heel of its hand in the fourth month, like everyone, and let the nick close slowly and imperfectly in the wet, like everyone's.

---

**What it did not do.**

It did not cool the seam, which would have been to deliver the year eighty forty years early, out of kindness.

It did not warm the seam either. Consider what that would have meant: the ledger unwritten, the arithmetic broken, the wick-vine held in a hot dark forever, and four people and their successors and their successors' successors kept at ninety-eight degrees in perpetuity by an act of love. The Cut is not a thing to be sustained. It is a thing that is happening, at one degree a decade, and has a shape, and the shape has an end in it the way a stroke has a bottom.

It did not unmake the pleat. Forty years of softening is not a disfigurement to be reversed; it is how a doctor above knows a body from the Cut without asking, and it is how Halm knows her own hands.

It did not heal the hands. Nothing here heals smooth. To make one set of hands heal smooth would have been to remove four people from their own chamber.

It did not brighten the lamps.

It did not ungear the Slow, or improve it, or offer Halm a bearing that would never complain. She listens for the week before the complaint. That listening is thirty-nine years long and it is hers.

It did not ripen the fig. Forced fruit rots on the vine, and a power that does not know this about fruit does not know it about people either.

It did not bring anything back out of the winter of the long frost.

It did not take the work. This is the omission it considered longest, over several years, at the sump end, where thinking is easier. It could have carried the week up the stair so that Sennet's head was free. It could have counted the shrimp. It could have moved the trellises a handspan every season, tirelessly, exactly, so that Ovey might rest. It declined. To relieve a person of the labor by which they are legible to themselves is a very old error and it always presents as generosity.

It did not scratch its name in the glaze above the fifth bunk.

---

**Its warrant.**

Powers of this kind are cautious about entering, and the caution is proper: most places do not want what would be brought. So it looked for a vote, and found the place had already voted, repeatedly, in small change.

There are blind shrimp in the cistern that die before men do, and nobody eats them, and the reason given is not squeamishness but *you do not eat the thing that is doing your dying in advance for you.* The plane roots that came down nine meters for the warmth were not cut; they were trained onto frames, tapped, and hung with buckets, and the arrangement is described above as the surface paying rent, which is a joke, and a joke is a kind of courtesy extended to a tree. There is a fifth bunk kept made in a chamber where four is an arithmetic of air. There is a fig carried the length of the hall to be eaten in the cold, because a thing that has taken eleven years deserves to be tasted, and heat will not let you taste anything.

And under the sump grate there is a large toad-like animal, older than any warden, fed table scraps by tradition, which nobody claims to have looked at closely.

That last one settled the matter. A place that feeds, for generations, something it does not look at, from which it gets nothing, has already established the principle under which a benevolent power may operate there. The Cut did not need goodness introduced. It needed the one thing four exhausted people in a hot dark cannot spare for each other, which is attention that is not also a measurement.

So the first thing it actually did was look at the toad closely. This took a night. The toad is only a toad, extremely old, with a cataract in the left eye and a preference for onion. Being looked at was what it had been owed since the second year, and it received it, and nothing else came of it, and that is fine; not every gift is required to be productive.

---

**What it did.**

**One.** At the sump end, in the cold glazed terraces where the grey moss grows, there was a hairline seam in the young rock, of the kind water would have opened in eight hundred or a thousand years. It did not break the rock. It persuaded the seam, at the rate a seam can be persuaded, and the work took twelve years, and at the end of the twelve years the throat delivered rather more than it had.

Not a great deal. One handspan of stone. Enough that the sandglass at the newel post was reset by the guild to run two hours instead of one, which required a meeting and a small expenditure and was minuted in the market office as *improvement to ventilation, cause unascertained.* Enough that a fifth body in the hall would no longer be slightly stupid forever. Enough that in the fourteenth year a child came down and stayed, and was not the thirty-second name in the glaze.

It came in at the cold end, so that no trellis had to be shifted out of turn and no boilerfern was moved a season early. This meant the sump end grew colder. Pell is the one who can bear cold. Whether that was arranged or merely fell out that way, the power declined afterward to say.

**Two.** In the year after the throat, at a fig — the four of them and the fifth carrying it down to the cold terraces, iron and ash, eaten slowly, very little said — there was air enough for it, and Sennet sang. He has a good voice and had never used it, because shouting costs air. What he sang was a thing off the market square that he had learned by sound through nine meters of rock, and he had some of it wrong, and Halm did not correct him.

Note the shape of this. The power supplied air. It did not supply the song. A benevolence that gives room rather than results is nearly always mistaken for luck, and should be.

**Three.** On the anniversary of the winter of the long frost, at the second bell, Halm stood at the vent-gate with her hand on the lever, as she does. The cold of that day is still in her somewhere and has not been driven out yet, and it was not driven out. It was sat in, alongside, for the nine minutes of the rain, in the register beneath the drip, in silence, which the register includes.

But the lever was one degree warmer than the seam accounts for, and the gauge-moss on it stood one pile thicker than it had any business standing, and she read it with a fingertip in the dark and knew it was not the seam. She said nothing. She had no word for it — the pipe-word has thirty terms and no tense, and you cannot say in it *someone was here with me* — and she has continued to say nothing for years, and she goes to that lever more often than the gate requires.

**Four.** It became the tense.

Paper does not survive in the Cut; a page pocketed at the stair-head is pulp by the sump. The past therefore lives above, in a clerk's handwriting, in a ledger of moss depths and shrimp counts and fig dates, which is an excellent ledger and records almost nothing that happened. It does not record that Corse was hurrying *for* something, and that the something is unwritten. It does not record why thirty-one children left, only that they did, in a list of first names with no reasons, because the reasons were all the same reason and everybody was too tired to write it.

So the power learned it. All of it, and by name: the thirty-one, and the reason, which is that the place is hard and does not soften and asks you to become someone whose hands cannot tell a penny from a button; and Corse's errand; and the exact shape of each warden's private market square, built out of cartwheels and boot-scuffs and the rumble of the fish stall's shutters, no two the same shape, none of them the shape of the square that is actually up there. It holds these the way the Cut cannot. This is not preservation for any purpose. It is simply that a thing that happened should be, somewhere, still true.

**Five.** On the first of the month, at the stair-foot, Ovey's wife and daughter come down and the glass is turned. The scratches on the newel post are above his eyes now and he has never once seen the girl outdoors and will not. The power did not lengthen the hour. It has no authority over the clerk's clock and would not use it if it had.

What it did was much smaller and is hard to put in the ledger. Those who sit at the stair-foot now find, going up, that the hour was enough. Not long. Enough — which is a different measurement, and had not previously been available down there, and is the only miracle in this account that no one has attributed to ventilation.

---

**The year eighty.**

It does not intend to intervene then, and has said so, in the flat breathy register under the drip, to the two of the four who asked.

The last hot pipe will drop below the boilerfern's line. The Slow will be walked up the spiral in pieces, and Halm's bolts, every one of which she can date, will go up in a basket. The Cut will fill with its own water and go quiet in the fuller sense, and the shrimp will keep the water sweet for a while with nobody to count them on Fridays, and the toad will be fed for as long as anyone remembers the tradition, and then will not be, and will be looked at once more, closely, at the end, and will not be alone in the water.

And Pell, or whoever is Pell by then, will go up the stair with the wick-vine in a wet sack to find wherever the ground has opened next.

The power will be there ahead of her. Twelve years ahead, warming nothing, cooling nothing, sitting in a new hot dark with its hand flat on young rock, learning by touch — early only in the one place where earliness is not hurry, which is arrival.
