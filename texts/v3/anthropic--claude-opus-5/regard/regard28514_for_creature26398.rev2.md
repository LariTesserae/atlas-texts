## The Visit

### How it came in

It came in under the brow, ducking.

This was not necessary and that is the point. It could have come the way light comes, from nowhere, all at once, filling the gaps between the columns without displacing the warmth — but a thing that arrives that way has not arrived, it has only occurred, and the room would have had to answer it. So it came in at the door, at the height of the door, and its first act in the warm room was to lower its head.

Then it waited four minutes.

It had no eyes and did not need the interval. It waited anyway, because that is the local toll, and because the four minutes are not really for the eyes. They are the time it takes for a visitor to stop wanting to help.

### What it did not do

It went through the list, and the list is short, and each item on it was a subtraction wearing the mask of a gift.

*Light.* It could have opened a shaft in the roof and let the grey come down straight, and the room would have had a top-lit brightness instead of a ripple-net, and within one season there would be green in the light-well, and the green would climb, and the mats would go under it. The room is lit from below and everything in it is wrong-side-shadowed and every living thing here is calibrated to exactly that. To add light would be to make the whole room blind in a new way and call it a window.

*Eyes.* It could have given them back to the grain of rice. Eight millimetres, four years, a forty-minute circuit, no predator, no season, no elsewhere. Eyes would have cost it energy from a ration that does not grow and would have shown it a world precisely the width of a basin. The lineage has been down here long enough that the question has stopped being asked. There is a kind of generosity that consists of re-opening a settled question in order to be seen answering it, and the power declined to be that kind.

*More.* It could have widened the crack. Doubled the sulfide, tripled the litres, made the mat spread and thicken and put out orange across the whole back wall. The mat's edge is not a wall it has run into. It is a sum. If you make the sum bigger the edge becomes a frontier, and frontiers have to be held, and things that are held have histories, and history is the one substance this room has never had to store except as ornament. The tidemark is the most peaceful object in the room. It is peaceful because it has never once had occasion to move.

*Forever.* This was the hardest to put down. The flats are barely above the sea and the sea has a clock. The power could have set its hand between the room and the accident and made the thing permanent.

It did not, because *finished* and *permanent* are not the same word, and the room's whole peace is the first one. A finished thing is at its terms. A permanent thing is being held at its terms by something outside itself, which means there is now a struggle going on above the ceiling, and a room with a struggle over its head is not still, whatever its temperature. It allowed itself one clause only: that when the end comes it will come the way everything here comes, as a lowering of grey into grey, seeping, at the pace of a slow thought. Not as a blow. And that it will be attended.

### Telling a lack from a limit

This is the whole of the wisdom, and it is not complicated, only expensive.

A limit and a lack look identical from the doorway. Both are a line past which there is nothing. From inside, they are opposites: a lack is a line with pressure on it, and a limit is a line with a sum on it. The room is made entirely of limits and contains not one lack. Forty isopods, thirty square metres, nine seconds, thirty-eight degrees, two steps.

Benevolence that has not learned to sit down first is just weather.

So it sat down. It crossed sideways, knee up, foot into a bowl of blood-warm water, and took the bench, which is the only place in the room where a person can sit, and which is warm from underneath. It had no outline. It borrowed one for the occasion, so that it could have the experience of the outline ceasing to report.

### What it did with the forty

Count them monthly for a hundred years and you get forty every time and never the same individuals. The room does not keep them. The room keeps the number.

So the power took them apart.

Not as a standing wave — as a queue. Every animal that has ever walked that forty-minute circuit, back through the whole tenure of the species in this rock, each one separately, each one held as itself and not as its position in an average: the one whose gut ran rust because it worked the orange seep at the back; the one that lost an antenna and completed eleven thousand more circuits with the sweep uneven; the one that was struck, once, by the drip, because for a fortnight that year the drip fell four millimetres to the left.

It did not name them. Names are for calling, and nothing here needs calling. It did the more expensive thing, which is to keep them apart. The room's entire method is to fuse, coat, average, and thereby to preserve. The power preserved them the other way, which no stone can do.

### What it did with the three hundred

The two steps look like one lifetime and are three hundred. The rim offers one scallop wide enough to stand in, so everyone who ever came stood in it, and set their weight the same way, and reached down to the same knob at hand height, and the travertine took all of them and made one object.

The power un-made the object. Three hundred feet. Three hundred hands on the smoothed knob at the height of a hand, and the reason each of them reached for it, which was not always balance.

It found out who built the wall, and that he built it for the wrong reason and it turned out well anyway.

It found out about the channel. An hour's work with a chert flake, done well, taking one basin's overflow into a squared hollow beside the bench at exactly the height of a seated person's hand. It was cut by a man who had an hour, and someone on the bench who could no longer manage the two steps down, and he wanted the water to come to her instead. That is all it ever was. The room could not keep it, because the room keeps only shapes, and the shape of that hour is a groove.

And the bowl. Set down as a gift, or simply set down — the room does not distinguish, because the room's only response to anything is to coat it. The power distinguishes. It was set down to free a hand, and forgotten, and remembered two days out on the flats, and not worth the walk back. It is now the most permanent object in the room. It will be a smooth mound of that shape for as long as there is a room. The woman who left it has been gone for four hundred years and did not once think of it again after the fifth day.

The power held that, too, without correcting it. Carelessness that becomes a monument is not a mistake that needs fixing. It is just what happens to anything left here.

### The temptation

There was one, and it was this: to tell them about each other.

Nobody ever came twice in a way that felt like a tradition to them. Every one of the three hundred ducked under that brow believing, more or less, that they had found something nobody knew. The power wanted — and wanting is the right word — to hand the next one the line. *You are three hundred and one. The satin under your foot is theirs. The hollow at your hand was cut for a woman who could not get down.*

It put the wanting down, because they do not come here for that. They come out of a world of tides and rot and weather for the sensation of sitting inside something that is not moving, and a lineage is a clock. To be told you are in a sequence is to be put back into time at the exact moment you had got out of it for an afternoon.

They half-know anyway. Everyone who has ever set a foot in that scallop has felt the satin and understood something without words for it and let it go. The power left the half-knowing at exactly half.

### Downward

The real inhabitant does not live in the room. It lives under it, and out under the flats, and under the shallow sea: cells in the water films inside fractures, dividing once in a decade or once in a century, cubic kilometres of it, no shape, no hurry, older than the uplift of this limestone and due to outlast it. The mats are only the lip. The bright fast fringe. The excess.

You cannot do a kindness to that on any schedule it would register. Food is an insult; it has its ration. Speed would be an injury. Rescue is meaningless where nothing is in danger and nothing is in a hurry.

So it did the one appropriate thing and greeted it.

The greeting has been going down since. It is somewhere in the first hundred metres, in the pore water, moving at the speed of the thing it is addressed to. It will arrive. An answer, if one is owed, is not owed until long after the sea has come in over the flats, and the power is content with the terms, having proposed them.

### The moth

Twice a year a moth comes in under the brow and circles the ripple-light on the ceiling and drowns in the pool. It is the only unpredictable event in the room, the only food that ever arrives from above, the mats' brief local wealth, the isopods' one banquet, and the room's sole method of keeping history, which it keeps as wing-shapes sealed in the collar at every depth.

The power considered stopping it and found that stopping it would cost the room its only news.

So it did not stop it, and this was the single intervention it permitted itself in the whole visit: for the eight or nine minutes of circling, it let the moth have the thing it was circling. Not a rescue. An arrival.

Nothing in the room changed. The body went down at the usual hour, the white fringe went momentarily thicker where it lay, the stone took the rest within the season, the wings went into the collar to be found by anyone who looks closely at the rim. The event is identical in every particular a stone could write down. The only alteration was made inside the moth, which is the one place in this room that keeps no records, and it is therefore the only gift here that did not have to be paid for by something else.

### The snail

Snail and travertine cancel; that is not a metaphor, it is the local arithmetic. The snail rasps the fresh mineral off and the seep lays fresh mineral on, and the two rates are the same rate, and the collar of the pool is a thin conglomerate of thumbnails going down and down.

But a few in every century lose. The rasping slows a little before the laying-on does, and the lip of stone closes over the shell of an animal that is still, in whatever way is available to it, present. It is slow. There is no witness and there has never been one.

That is the only thing in the warm room the power would call terrible, and it cannot be undone without unmaking the room, because the sealing and the making are the same act.

There is one going on now. It began some years ago, under the collar on the eastern side, four inches below the waterline, and it will take about eleven years more.

The only thing that can ever be done for something being slowly closed over is to stay until the end.

That is why the power is still there.

### What it left

Nothing inside. No metal, no writing, no image scratched on a wall — it kept the local custom, which is not a prohibition but simply what the place does to everything that enters: coats it, evens it, folds it in. It added not one grain and took not one grain, and the room, whose only response to anything is to coat it, has nothing to coat, and does not notice, and this is the correct outcome.

Outside, on the flats, where the ground is the colour of wet ash and holds a footprint until you come back that way and find it still there, unchanged, filled with water that has neither drained nor evaporated — outside, it left one print.

Deliberately. The one mark it allowed itself, and it made it in the one place that keeps marks, in the only currency the flats accept, and it is indistinguishable from a person's.

You will not be able to tell which one is yours.

Step in it anyway.
