# What Came to the Sweating Notch

## How it arrived

Not on the cable.

It could have. It could have come up in the bucket-car with the grease and the mail sack, dripping on arrival like everything else, and been met at the pylon, and been shown the valves. But that is the way things arrive from below, and everything that matters in the notch arrived from above, in a size too small to require permission — a spore, a dust-grain seed, a torn thumbnail of liverwort lifted nine kilometres by the updraught. The notch has been receiving mail for a few hundred thousand years and has answered none of it.

This was mail. It let itself be delivered.

It arrived at an hour with no one in it, which is most hours.

## What it understood in the first six of them

It came intending to help, and then it lay down.

There is exactly one bed-shaped thing in the notch: the stack of galvanised plate stock going green under moss. Several people have lain on it, looked up into the cloud being manufactured two metres above their faces, and said afterwards that they wished they hadn't. The power lay on the plate stock for six hours and watched the sky vanishing into existence, and was the first thing in the notch's history able to look at that without wishing afterwards it hadn't. It found it companionable. It knows the process from the other side.

And in those six hours the intention to help came apart, gently, the way wet paper does.

Because the place has already done the difficult thing. The moss went where the water ran, which happened to be the brick. The ferns went to the waste heat, which happened to be the pads. The orchid went to the housing of the western turbine and flowers there whenever it likes, smelling of hot iron, on both sisters, and nowhere else on the mountain. Nothing here is being reclaimed. Nothing here is in recovery. Nature arrived after the rivets and organised itself around them, and *forgiveness* is the wrong word because there was never an injury — the green looked at the machinery and decided it was weather.

Any power arriving to separate the garden from the engine would be separating a marriage.

So it put the intention down and did the harder work, which was to go through the temptations one at a time.

## The temptations, in the order they came

**To give the water bears back the trick.** Four or five kinds of tardigrade in the mattress, and not one of them can dry. The lineage has mislaid it. Dried in a dish they simply die, like anything else, in the wettest place above the weather. It would be one gesture to hand the folding-up back to them.

Declined, for two reasons. A gift is also a prophecy, and to return the drying is to tell forty thousand generations that a drought is coming. And the trick has another end: a thing that can travel as dust will be travelled. They would stop being the only citizens the notch has ever had — the only inhabitants who cannot leave, who go a thumb's width a season inside the moss that carries them, and go nowhere else at all. Not being able to leave is not always a defect. Sometimes it is the whole of belonging.

**To multiply the frogs.** Forty in the world, under the brick, hearts showing through the flank. Forty is not a small number; it is the number the brick holds. Four hundred would be three hundred and sixty starvations with a witness present.

So it did the arithmetic the other way, and counted them instead. All forty: the big gravid female with the healed notch in her left skirt, the male with the beautiful clouded useless eye, the one whose heart runs faster than the others' and always has, the nine froglets the size of match heads that came out of the moss fully made in the last clutch. It knows which of the forty will be alive in four years. It has not told anyone, there being nothing to do with that information except grieve early.

**To silence the sisters.** For the crews, whose hearing is carved out at exactly the frequency of the groan, so that by their fifties the machine has erased itself from them.

Declined. The erasure is the machine taking itself back out of them, slowly, without malice, over thirty years — and more than that: the gap after the sixth pump stroke is the only silence the notch has, and the calling only happens in the gap. Nothing here would be improved by quiet. Also they cannot sleep without it.

**To hold the mountain still.** Four to six centimetres a year, warping the pads, twice re-shimmed. There is a graph of it in a filing cabinet at the valley station, a straight line sixty years long, and everyone who sees it laughs and then stops laughing.

Declined. The rising is the mountain doing precisely what it is. The shims are the crews' love, expressed in steel of a measured thickness. The power went and looked at the graph anyway — the only reader of it who has never needed to laugh.

**To answer.** This was the large one.

The frogs call in the gap the hammer makes, and only there, as though the machine had asked them something. The power could answer. It is not even a difficult question. But a question answered stops being asked, and then forty silent animals with their hearts showing would come out under the brick into a completed world and have nothing to say into it. So it left the hammer to go on asking badly, in iron, every eleven minutes, two million times so far, and left the frogs their reply.

That one took most of a season to decline.

## What it did instead

**It took the night shift.** Nine people work here and not one of them has ever been here at night. Twice a day the car goes down and the saddle runs on without a witness: the cloud manufactured overhead, the rock weeping, the sisters at their single speed, six hammer blows every eleven minutes to nobody, the pink films creeping up out of the crack a little further where the lichen isn't looking. That is the shift it took. Not surveillance. Attendance — the way you sit up with a house that is doing something enormous slowly.
