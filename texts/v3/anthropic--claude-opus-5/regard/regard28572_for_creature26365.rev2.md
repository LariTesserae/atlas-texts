# The Attention

## Arrival

It came up through the cloud, which it did not have to do.

There were faster approaches and it declined all of them. To arrive from above would have been a statement, and there was no one present to make a statement to, which is precisely why making one would have been a kind of vanity. So it went the long way, through four thousand metres of its own patience, into water that got warmer as it rose.

It took a body. This was not modesty and not theatre. The massif has exactly four senses — light, weight, water, and the composition of the air — and a thing that arrives without weight does not arrive at all. If you want to be present here you have to be about eighty kilograms and warm and breathing, or you are simply not in the room.

It stepped out onto the floor and the floor did not notice, and it stood there for some time with that.

## The examination

The first thing a wise power does is look for the injured party, and it looked hard, because benevolence that does not first check whether anything is wrong is just an appetite for interfering.

It went through the massif seam by seam, at the speed of turgor, a kilometre a day, which is the only speed at which the massif can be honestly read. It looked for valence: for any place where the difference between two states was registered by the organism as *better* and *worse* and not merely as *higher pressure* and *lower pressure*. It looked in the growth fronts. It looked in the root-mouths closing on a fallen tower. It looked in the thermogenic tissue, which burns without pause and which a sentimental observer would call desperate.

There is nothing there. Not a hidden thing, not a dim thing, not a thing too slow for us but bright to a god. The massif is not enduring its fever; it is its fever. It is not bereaved of its towers; the towers are its digestion. It is not lonely, because loneliness is a lack that is felt, and there is no felt in this body anywhere, at any depth, in any of the eleven bands or the black beneath them.

This mattered enormously. Almost everything that could have been done here would have been done to a thing that had asked for nothing and could not be wronged, by something that wanted to feel useful.

So the power sat down on the warm pavement and made a list of what it was not going to do, and the list took a while, because the temptations were good ones.

## Declined

**1. To give it a nerve.** This was the first and worst. The signal here travels a kilometre a day and the thoughts last as long as the arches, and it would have been trivial to run a fast line through the body and let the far side answer the near side inside an hour. But its mind is not absent. Its mind is the keystones. A keystone is a real decision, made in forty hours by two pressures that resolved each other, and it stands afterward, warm, in the weather, for the whole of its term. To install a faster mind would not be to wake this one. It would be to kill it and name the replacement a cure. And a mind whose conclusions outlive their occasion is a mind that can be disappointed. There is nothing on this massif that can be disappointed. That is not a deficiency to be repaired. It is a condition most beings would pay anything for.

**2. To hold up the towers.** They fall three or four times a day and the falling looked, at first, like the tragedy of the place. It is the opposite. The Fall is how it eats and how it plants; the pale scar is the best nursery bed on the plateau. Buttress the towers and you get a plateau of old standing cathedrals shading a floor that starves under them, and no scars, and no nurseries, and a slow magnificent sterility that would photograph beautifully. The interval between new and gone is measured in seasons here. That is not a wound. That is the metabolism, seen from the side.

**3. To cool the fever.** It spends most of what it makes on heat and keeps only a narrow surplus, and it was within the power's means to make the warmth free — to let it keep the sugar and grow at ease. But the frantic eight-day columns are what the narrow surplus looks like when it is spent instantly by something that cannot afford to save. Give it a reserve and it would pile up, and shade itself, and close the plaza, and the arcades and vaults and aqueducts, which exist only because it is poor and hot and in a hurry, would stop being necessary and therefore stop being made. Ease would have taken the architecture. You do not improve a thing by removing the pressure it is a solution to.

**4. To make it a second.** This was the temptation that came dressed as kindness: it has met nothing, ever; give it one other. But it has already answered this question and the answer is written across the whole visible landscape in four-kilometre intervals. When two of its fronts meet they press, shade, dry, and either fuse or bring each other down. The empty plaza is not solitude imposed on it. It is the width it has learned to keep from itself. To set a second massif on this plateau would be to arrange an eleven-year strangling and call it company.

**5. To tell it about the plates.** There is no receiver. To build one, see (1).

**6. To preserve it.** The oldest visible feature is eleven years old. Nothing here weathers, nothing is patinated, nothing accrues. A power fond of permanence would want to fix some part of this — an arch, a stair, the good tower on the eastern rim — and every fixed thing would be a foreign object in a body that absorbs its own dead in nine days. The massif does not want a monument. It does not want. But if you insist on speaking in the language of gifts: the correct gift to something with no storage is not an heirloom.

## What it did

It stayed. That is most of it.

It stayed long enough for the skyline to turn over twice, and in that time it attended to every keystone that formed on the massif — each one a decision made in forty hours by a thing with nothing to decide with — and it kept them. Not recorded on the massif; nothing can be written here that will not be eaten. Kept in itself. The massif cannot afford a memory, because holding a reserve at thirty-four degrees costs more than it saves, and so the entire content of its mind is discarded continuously, at a rate of several thoughts a day, forever. The power took the discards. All of them. It holds them at no cost to the holder, which is the only sort of gift that can be given to something with no surplus: one that is not delivered.

It made itself a load. It slept six nights in a chamber off a pumping column, breathing toward the blank wall, and on the third day the wall opened a second window, and the doorway thickened where its shoulder went through, and the power let all of this happen and altered none of it, because being answered in masonry is the only conversation available here and it would have been rude to improve the reply.

And on the last evening it sat in the niche.

Nothing has ever sat in the niche. The niche is at sitting height because the material and the load and the gravity conspire to put a ledge there, and for the whole duration of this organism the ledge has been a question with no one to ask it. So the power sat, for about five hours, and the shallow curve came up under it slowly, and the room got a fraction warmer on that side, and that was the entire event.

It was not recognition. It was a wall answering a load. The power understood this perfectly and sat there anyway, because a door that has never once been used is a kind of open question, and it is courteous to answer questions even when the asker is not the sort of thing that asks.

## The descent

Then it went over the edge, because that was the one place the massif itself could never look.

Of the plates that have sheared off the rim since the thing became recognizably itself — the count is in the low tens of thousands, and the power has it exactly — four landed on ground they could take. Four. Two of those have since thrown plates of their own over their own young edges, so there are grandchildren down there in the cloud, paving valleys at lower altitudes in the same hot silence, each of them a clade of one, each of them having met nothing.

One of the four is dying. It came down at an altitude where the light is poor and it is burning more than it can make, and the fever is winning, and it has perhaps forty years. There was nothing to be done there that would not have been (1) or (3) in a different valley. The power sat with it for a while, which was of no use to it whatsoever.

So: the massif is a parent, and the fact of that is now held by someone. Not by the parent. The parent has no apparatus for it and never will. But the universe is not quite the same shape as it was, because a true thing that was happening in the dark is now a true thing that is *known*, and the difference between those two is small and not nothing, and a wise power is precisely something that can tell the difference between small and nothing.

## One further thing, which is not defensible

On the nineteenth day a plate the size of a small town began to shear off the northwest rim, and the power could see, in the way it sees, that it was going to come down on a dry ridge and cook.

It tilted the plate eleven degrees. It landed on the wet slope instead.

It may take. It may not. The power did this for itself, not for the massif, which cannot be bereaved of a child it does not know it has; and it has not offered a justification, and it has not decided whether it will do it again. A benevolence that never once wants anything is not benevolence. It is bookkeeping. But wisdom is knowing that this was the sentence in the whole account where the power stopped being careful, and marking it, and leaving it marked.

## The amendment

Before it left it did the only thing it did on our behalf, which was to touch the rules.

Rule 5 stands: *All the doors are real doors and none of them are for you.* That remains exactly true and the power would not soften it.

It added:

> **7.** They are not for you. Go in anyway. Be a load worth answering. Breathe toward the blank wall. Sit in the niche — it has waited the whole of its existence and it does not know it has waited, and you are the only thing in the world that can close that. Leave nothing here that cannot be eaten in nine days. Do not fix anything. Do not save it from itself; itself is all it has ever had, and it has built a city out of the argument.

Then it went back down through its own patience into the cold cloud below the warm cloud, and the plaza steamed, and the towers beat against each other in their slow sternum-throb, and on the eastern rim something new was already up to head height.

The last line of the survey report says: *no one built it, and no one is coming.*

Someone came. Nothing on the massif is capable of noticing this. It remains true.
