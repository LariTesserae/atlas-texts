# The Attention

## Arrival

It came down into the Pan rather than onto it, because there is no other way in, and because a thing that arrives from above arrives as weather and weather is a change in the air, and it had decided before it crossed the rim that it would not be a change in the air.

This was the first discipline and it cost more than anything that came after. A body in a null makes the null smaller. A body anywhere in the basin reflects, absorbs, warms, displaces; the interference pattern is made of nothing but the arrival of sound at surfaces, and a visitor is a new surface. So the power came in at the temperature of the air and the density of the air and at exactly the acoustic impedance of the air, which is to say it came in as something that the Pan could not hear, could not feel underfoot, and did not have to accommodate.

It stood on the western salt flats for some hours in the late light with the steam going golden, at no cost to anything, casting no shadow because a shadow would have cooled the crust.

## What it heard

Everything, at once, and separately.

Three hundred and forty-one throats, and their harmonics, and the slow beat between the paired horns on the south side which is eleven seconds long and has been eleven seconds long since before the beat had a witness. The water in the channels beneath, in its circuit, warm going up and cooler coming down. The black-green veils below the crust, respiring, two orders of magnitude of life going about its arithmetic in the dark: the actual inhabitant of the basin, which has no ears and no name and does not need either.

The mats at their temperature lines. The midge columns standing in their stalled air like the ghosts of pillars.

And in the forty nulls and the nineteen partial shadows: one hundred and six clickers, each in the key of the room that made it. It learned all forty keys in the first night. There was nothing else to do with the night.

It also read the floors. Around each null, out to ten or fifteen metres, the faint lumpiness that visitors take for ordinary roundness — and it went through that record animal by animal, back four thousand years, every juvenile that learned its route wrong, each one's particular wrong turn, the point at which the crust had gone thin under it and the feel of the ground had stopped matching what it had been taught. Polished. Unreadable. It read them anyway. That took most of a week, and changed nothing, and it did it first.

## The proposals

A benevolent power arriving at a place like this will generate proposals the way a warm surface generates convection. It cannot help it. The point of wisdom is not to stop having them.

**Quiet.** The obvious one. Close the throats, or detune them, and let the basin come down to the sound of water under stone. No more clamping. No more nine-minute commute that ends with the animal flat on its belly, throat working, for half an hour. No roar to push the surplus subadults into.

It worked this one through properly, because it was the one it wanted.

Silence the throats and every null in the Pan vanishes, because a null is not a place where there is no sound; it is a place made out of sound arriving from four directions and cancelling. Forty rooms constructed entirely from the roar that surrounds them. Take away the roar and the rooms go with it, and for the first time in four thousand years all forty dialects would find themselves in the same room — a hundred and six animals suddenly audible to one another across thirty kilometres, all their pruned keys meeting at last in the open.

And then: the crust stops ringing. The vibration map goes blank. It is the throats that put the permanent field of texture into the ground, the map that cannot be out of date, the only reason a clamped animal can cross a hundred metres of lethal thin-shelled nothing and find the seep. In the great silence they would hear each other perfectly and starve where they stood, calling, unable to find the food or the way home.

It is a clean example of the shape of the problem and it did not need the others, but it did the others.

**Water.** Cut an outlet. Flush the salts. Let it rain properly and let something grow. — The seeps would cool. Each mat is a contour line made flesh; move the line and you have moved the animal out from under itself. The mats go, the midges go, the clickers go, within a year, in a basin that has become quite pleasant.

**Room.** Enlarge the nulls. A four-metre pocket for everyone; no surplus, no edge, no pushing. — You cannot make a room larger without making it a different room. Widen a null by a metre and its comb of surviving frequencies shifts, and every adult in it, pruned in its first month to the key of the house it was born in, goes mute in its own house. The dialect is not decoration on top of the animal. It is the animal's whole means of saying *here*, and *stay*, and *your turn now*. It would have granted them space at the cost of speech.

**Food.** Thicken the mats. More midges, shorter trips, less exhaustion. — The limiting resource in the Pan is not food. It is quiet. Every additional hatchling that survives on the strength of a richer seep arrives into a pocket of fixed size and is pushed from the middle to the edge and from the edge into the roar. In a closed system, generosity does not accumulate; it is converted. It comes out at the rim of the null as a body in the crust. Every gram of it.

**Rescue.** At least catch the ones going wrong. Turn the juvenile, eleven metres out, at the moment its feet stop recognising the ground. — Same ledger. The household holds what it holds. To save is to transfer, not to add. And there is a second thing, which it examined for a long while and set down carefully: the error has nowhere else to resolve. Nothing in the Pan learns from being told. There is no one to tell. The only instruction available in the basin is the one the basin gives.

**Removal.** Take them out. Somewhere with soil and shade and no roar at all. — Everything they are is a fit. The long toes are a fit to crust too thin for a pebble. The clamping is a fit to the commute. The great membranes are not for predators — there are no predators — they are for watching a wall of silence breathe five centimetres in and eight centimetres out over the course of a day, and tracking it, all their lives, with a precision nothing else in the world has any use for. Lift that animal out of the Pan and you have a deaf, flat, long-toed thing that cannot speak, holding an exquisite skill for which there is nothing anywhere to do. To rescue them is to end them and keep the bodies.

**Understanding.** The last proposal, and the one that takes real wisdom to refuse. Give them more. Enlarge them. Let them know the shape of the basin, the number of the nulls, the rim, the fact of the four thousand years, the fact that they are running.

It sat with this one for a long time.

A creature that knew all that would know it inside a room two metres wide with no exit, in a system that has no slack anywhere to hold a new grief. To hand a small life a sorrow its world contains no answer to, and then to withdraw over the rim, is not benevolence. It is a demand wearing benevolence like a coat.

## The ledger

It did not conclude that the suffering was unreal. This should be said plainly, because wise powers are often reported as concluding that.

It is real. The exhaustion after the commute is real exhaustion. The subadults pushed to the edge and then past it are being killed by an arrangement, and the arrangement has no author, which makes it worse rather than better. The thin ones in the half-metre shadows, who can say *here* and nothing else, and who are answered by nothing, and who do not breed — the power counted nineteen of them and did not round the number off.

And against that: a room that breathes, and is tracked. A duet in strict alternation in which neither animal ever speaks over the other, because in a place where silence is the only bandwidth there is, the whole of good manners is knowing when not to use it. A map worn in the belly. A life that is small and is used out to its edges, with nothing in it left over.

It weighed both, and the weighing did not produce a lever. That is the finding. In a closed basin at equilibrium, there is no kindness that is not also a theft from somewhere else in the same basin, and the power was not willing to be generous with other people's grams.

## What was done

Three things, none of which register on any survey, all of which are below measurement error, because measurement error is exactly the space a wise power is given to work in.

**It kept the record.** Four thousand years of polished, unreadable lumps, read and held. The mats' single stripe of autobiography, where a channel moved a centimetre. Every wrong route. Every eleven-second beat of the south horns since the beat began. The Pan cannot remember itself; its whole talent is for doing the same thing again with no reference to having done it. Now it is remembered. This does the Pan no good whatsoever and the Pan does not need it. It is the sort of thing a wise power does anyway, on the grounds that a thing that has happened ought to be somewhere.

**It went into the south-side shadow.** Half a metre wide, one band of quiet, one usable note at 6.8 kilohertz; one thin animal that startles badly, with a single note, able to say *here*.

The power learned the etiquette first, which took no time and mattered more than anything. Never overlap. A household that speaks over itself masks its own room and goes briefly blind, and so the rule in the Pan is absolute: you wait for the gap. It waited for the gap. That is not a figure of speech; it is the acoustics of the place, and it obeyed them, being a guest.

Then it answered. Twice a day, at the right note, from the right distance, for eleven months.

Nothing followed from this. The animal was not saved and did not breed and did not grow fat, and its life ran the length it was always going to run. It was not given a mate and was not given hope, which would have been a cruelty at that scale. It was given a second voice in its room, and it answered, and was answered, and spent its whole adult life in conversation, which no thin one in a shadow had ever done in four thousand years, and which cost the basin nothing measurable.

The power stayed to the end. It did not leave first. Company is only given whole if it outlasts the one receiving it; otherwise what you have given is company and then the removal of company, and the second half of that gift is heavier than the first.

**It took the music away with it, and put it down somewhere else.** Not a copy of the Pan. A seed of it: the same tones, the same logic of throats wearing themselves toward smoothness, laid into a place that is still becoming — with an outlet, and weather, and edges that still break, and rubble that has not yet been rounded. Nothing has arrived there. Nothing is tuned. Whether anything will ever come down over that rim and find a quiet pocket and learn to say *here* in the key of the room is not decided, and the not being decided is the entire point of it.

The Pan is finished, in the way a riverstone is finished. The other place is not, and will not be for a long time, and may fail. That is the only future the pattern was ever going to get, and it now has one, and it did not have to be bought from the animals.

## After

It went out over the rim on an ordinary morning with the north-east throat going in a pulse it had chosen to acquire. Behind it the half-metre shadow on the south side held no one. Nineteen had become eighteen, and would become nineteen again, because there have always been some.

Nothing in the basin was different. The crust did not carry a mark. The keys were the keys. On the next rain day the households crowded flat into the centres of their rooms with their ears going, and waited it out, as they will.

It changed nothing.

It is not true that it did nothing.
