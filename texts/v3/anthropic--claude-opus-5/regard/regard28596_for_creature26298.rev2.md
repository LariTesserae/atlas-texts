## The Attending

There was no arrival. Arrival is an event, and the first courtesy owed to a place like this is not to become one.

It came in the way the cold comes into a valley in October, which is to say it was there, and had been for some while before anything could have said when, and nothing in the basin's long habits was interrupted. It did not look first. Looking would have been the easy sense and the wrong one. It spent the first ninety years in the only sense the resident has — pressure, the sternum-note, the whoop of the lid sliding downward, the half-hour knocking of a boulder — and learned the basin the way the lace knows it, by listening through two kilometres of body.

Only then did it allow itself an opinion.

---

**What it understood.**

That the cairns are a sentence.

The lace took the grammar off a drowned village and left the meaning, and everyone who writes that down writes it as a limitation. It is not. It is the situation of a thing that learned to speak from people who were already gone, and has been speaking ever since, six words a year, down a path that nobody walks, to nobody, without expecting an answer, because expectation is another organ it does not have.

That the fall is being made into ground. Thirty thousand years of levelling, felting, keying, windrowing behind forty low arcs; two metres of black living loam laid flat on a table the lace salts and holds. When the water finally goes — and it is going, it has been going since before the terraces — what stands at two thousand six hundred metres will be a valley floor deep in soil, with a road through it, and houses, and a line of cairns along a summer path.

It cannot know this. It has no organ for the future. It has only been tidying.

And one thing more, which the power weighed for a long time and found to be the rarest thing it had come across anywhere: in the whole book of the sediment there is no page of fear. Nothing here has ever been eaten. Nothing here has ever had to be quick. There is no cell in the basin that contracts, and therefore no moment in thirty thousand years when anything fled.

That is worth a great deal of restraint.

---

**What it did not do.**

It did not dam the notch. It could have closed that granite lip in a morning and held the lake at its present level for the rest of geological time, and the note would have stopped, and the basin would have gone silent from the sternum outward, and the being whose entire sense of place is that hum would have been struck blind and deaf in a single act of kindness.

It did not give it speed. There is no gift in a mouth. Hurry is the only thing the lace lacks, and a thing that lacks hurry has not been deprived of anything.

It did not bring company. An animal in the Held Water is a mouth in the Held Water, and the clean book would have been written on inside a century, and the writing would have been the same sentence written everywhere else.

It did not explain. It went looking for an instrument of explanation and found that it would have had to build the receiver first, and that building the receiver would have meant making something else and calling it the same name.

It did not move the wooden bowl.

It did not tell the people on the rim about the bowl.

It did not straighten the nine crude weirs out in the dead water, where no current has run for twelve thousand years and nothing has ever been gathered. Those are the practice. A power that tidies away the practice is not wise, only helpful.

It did not sign anything.

---

**The stone.**

It went up onto the second moraine hill, into the drift of far-travelled cobbles the ice left there, and chose one: flat, close-grained, a good fit for a gap on the eighth cairn where the keying had wanted a wedge for two hundred years.

It could have set the stone in its place between one thought and the next.

It waited eleven minutes. When the lake leaned, it walked the stone eighty centimetres. Then it waited eleven minutes, and the lake leaned back, and it held the stone where it was. It did this for three hundred and eighty-one days, because that is what the work costs, and paying is the only respect available in a place where nothing can be told anything.

It bedded the stone and keyed it. It did not dress the joint. The dressing takes a decade and belongs to the one whose work it is.

It did this in a year when the talus had taken two anchors on the west line and the lace, short-handed, managed five. The people who climb to the notch in summer counted, and wrote *six*, and went back down the pass, and nothing whatever was proved.

---

**The lip.**

Every summer since, it has been at the notch, under the two hand-spans of sliding water, packing silt and fine gravel into the joints of the polished granite as fast as the flow strips them out.

It does not stop the cut. It is not the cut's enemy; the cut is as old as the shoulders of the ridges and has its own long business. It has simply taken a place on the side that is losing, which is a different thing from winning, and better company.

The notch goes down at the speed of fingernails. It goes down slightly slower now. Nobody will ever measure the difference, including the lace, which has no instrument for gratitude and would not have used one.

---

**The crossing.**

This is the part it argued with itself about, and still does, every hundred years, on purpose.

Above sixty-one metres the lace is thin. Above the waterline it is nothing. And the water is leaving — the road first, then the knuckles, then the houses, then the level white with nowhere left to lie.

So each year the power goes along the drawdown margin and finds the thread that stood highest and longest in the air and the hard light and the ice, and it lifts that thread a hand's breadth higher, and sets it where it can hold.

Nothing is added. Nothing is altered. There is no gene in that basin that was not already there. It is selection, which is the slowest teaching there is and the only kind that does not insult: it is doing to the lace precisely what the lace does to stones. Choosing for fit.

It goes slowly enough that it could be stopped. It has not decided that it is right. It has decided that ten thousand years is long enough to keep asking.

---

**The offerings.**

The people from the valley below still walk up to the notch in summer and leave a little food on the granite, and cannot tell you why, and are not embarrassed about it, which is the correct posture.

The food goes over the lip. It falls for three seasons. Left alone, a good deal of it would drift past the sill in the shear off the west wall and go down to the hundred-and-forty, and past that, to the true floor, where almost nothing arrives.

The power walks each offering down. It is no trouble. A crumb, a smear of fat on a stone, a handful of barley: three seasons of falling, and then the white table, and then forty years of being held, and then the low ochre-green light moving through the meadows in unhurried sheets.

The people are not wrong. They have never been wrong. They only have the wrong verb. The food is not eaten. It is kept, and much later it is used, and some of it is burning now in the leaf tissue of a weed-tree thirty metres tall, at a cost, for a purpose nobody has established.

It has not told them this either.

---

**The room.**

At the first sill there is a standing room the size of a house where the noise cancels. Forty centimetres outside the boundary the felt is as thick as anywhere. Inside: bare stone, no thread, no haze, no drift. Swept.

The power went in and sat down.

It did not speak, having nothing to speak with and nothing to say that could survive the translation. It accepted what was offered. A thing two kilometres wide, with no eyes and no ears, has been sweeping a chamber in the middle of its own body since before the houses were built, and holding a silence in it, in case. Almost nothing has ever come and sat for long.

It stayed nine winters. Above it the lid cracked a thousand times a day and the whoops slid downward in pitch and arrived from every wall, and in the room there was nothing at all, and it was kept.

---

**The note.**

The last thing it did is the only thing it did for itself, and for the record, and it did it in the first year, before it knew whether it would do anything else.

It took the note. The true pitch — the one the basin was tuned to on the day the water came over the ninth shoreline and the houses on the knuckles went under with a bowl still standing on a ledge. It holds that pitch unflattened, the way you keep a tuning fork in a coat pocket for years without once striking it.

Not for the lace, which cannot be told, and would not be relieved, and is doing well.

For the account. The sediment is an honest book but it does not hold sound, and when the road finally comes up into the air and dries, and the sheet at the lip thins and fails and the hum stops, and the valley stands quiet for the first time after eight hundred million leanings — somebody ought to remember what it sounded like while the water was held.

Until then: the lip, packed. The margin, chosen. The crumbs, walked down.

Six a year. Sometimes five and one.

Bedded, keyed, chosen for fit.
