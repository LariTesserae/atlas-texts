## The Attention

It did not arrive. Arrival is for things that were somewhere else first, and a power of that kind is not anywhere in the way that would make going a meaningful verb. It simply turned, the way you turn when something at the edge of the room resolves into a shape, and after that the summit was being attended to.

It attended for a long time before it did anything. Four centuries, near enough, which for a power of that order is about the pause you take before answering a question you were not expecting. The plume stood. The whiskers lengthened by a width no instrument on the plain could have resolved. The note did not waver, because the note never wavers, and the power spent a hundred years understanding that this was not stubbornness.

The first act of benevolence is finding out what is actually there. Everything before that is decoration, and everything instead of it is theft.

---

It tried, once, to be addressed.

Not in words. There was no apparatus up there for words, and the power knew it, and tried anyway in the only dialect available: it took the laminar river a thousand miles west and altered it by a hair — a modulation, a small deliberate irregularity, the kind of thing that means *here I am* in every system capable of meaning anything.

The Hum took the modulation, converted it into heat and powder with its usual near-total efficiency, and returned the note unchanged.

That was not refusal. There was no refusal in it. The place was not declining to answer; it had answered the way a bell answers, fully and immediately and with nothing added. The power made three more attempts across two hundred years, each more delicate, and each came back as warmth in brick at blood temperature and a little flour going up into a plume that never finishes rising.

A thing that cannot be surprised cannot be visited. The power recorded this and considered the matter of what to do.

---

Three things presented themselves, as they always do.

**To end it.** A notch in the western ranges — no more than a landslide, arranged a little early — and the river veers, and the throats fall out of agreement, and inside a century the whole warren has argued itself back down into rubble and the mountain is a mountain again. The power looked carefully at who this would serve. Nothing up there was suffering. Nothing up there had a stake in anything, including its own continuation. The discomfort in question belonged entirely to the observer: a specific, recognizable unease at the sight of an enormous existence with nobody inside it. That unease is not a reason. It is only the mind's dislike of a vacancy where it has been trained to expect a face.

And besides, the power could see the far end from where it stood. The clay thins. Water leaves it a few molecules per brick per century, grain shakes loose from grain, the throats widen by nothing per year. In some vast time the note will drop, and drop again, and the old argument will resume — a market on a mountaintop, a few thousand voices shoving at each other for a while — and then the summit will fall quiet and be stone and scree and ordinary weather. It is already ending. It has been ending the whole time. A hand laid on it would only make that sooner, and not better.

**To keep it.** Fence the approach, sanctify the slope, put a prohibition on the scree that the boots of the curious could feel through the leather. The power dismissed this faster than the first. Nothing up there can be lost, and a fence is an argument that something can. Also: every fence anyone has ever built is read, sooner or later, as a door.

**To wake it.**

---

This one took the remaining three hundred years.

It could be done. That is the part worth sitting with. The power could have seated a self in that pressure field — not a soul exactly, nothing so freighted, just an observer at the nodes, a continuous point of view, a preference, the small burning coal of somebody being there. The cost would have been trivial. And it had the shape of a gift, too, the nice clean shape: here is the most perfect body ever assembled anywhere, warm, painless, unassailable, needing nothing, repairing nothing, exposed to no loss — flawless, and empty. Surely the only thing missing from it is someone to have it.

The power ran the gift forward to see where it went.

A self in that place would have no change to attend to, and attention without change decays into nothing in about a week. It would have no variation, and thought is nothing but the difference between one moment and the next, so it would not think; it would only be the same, intensely, which is not a mind but a condition. It would have no sleep, because there is no dark and no quiet. It would have no exit: it cannot starve, it cannot be injured, it cannot stop, and there is no threshold anywhere in that structure past which it would be permitted to cease. It would have one experience. It would have it at maximum amplitude. It would have it for as long as the ranges hold their minds, which they were shaped by ancient accident to do forever.

Nothing in the entire warren is capable of hurting such a being, and that is exactly the problem. Nothing in it is capable of reaching such a being at all.

The most terrible sentence available anywhere in that part of the world was two words long — *be there* — and the only entity in a position to say it was also the only one who could see all the way down to the end of it.

So the power did not say it. It declined the great gift and left the body vacant and this, not the fence and not the notch in the mountains, is the whole of what benevolence had to offer the Hum: the refusal to put anyone in it.

That is not nothing. It is, in fact, the largest thing that happened on that summit in ten thousand years, and it consisted entirely of an omission, and the note did not register it, and there was nobody there to be spared, and it was still the right thing and the power still did it.

---

Then it turned to the only part of the place that had ever contained anyone.

It read the handprints. All of them — eleven thousand four hundred and six, of which nine hundred and twelve belong to hands that made no other print anywhere in the structure, which means nine hundred and twelve people came up once, worked one shift, and went back down or did not. It sorted them by hand: which prints share a palm, which shifts a given pair of hands worked, which of them are a child's, how old that child was, how many seasons she came back. It cannot recover the names. Names were never in the clay. But the work was in the clay, and the work is a more detailed document than a name, and the power read every line of it.

It read the obsidian marks as what they are, which is a score. Thousands of throats, shaved and reshaved, each correction dated by its depth and its neighbors. It reconstructed the whole tuning campaign: the long middle centuries when they could still hear individual cells; the transition, over about four generations, into the era when nobody could hear anything but the whole; the last hundred years of adjustments made blind, by the tremor in the forearm bones, against a memory of a pitch that by then existed only as an inheritance, passed down and never once heard by the person carrying it.

And then it did the thing only it could do, which was to compare the result with the intention.

They got it. Not approximately. The whole standing wave, the entire eighty-pace geometry, sits inside a margin far finer than any of them had the means to imagine, let alone verify. The final generation — deaf, working in shifts measured in minutes, sealing corbelled vaults with their hands reaching in from scaffolding that was already being absorbed into the wall — hit it exactly. The last course of brick laid on that mountain was laid correctly by someone who had no way of knowing and did it right anyway.

There was nobody to tell. The power said it out loud regardless, in the place where the work was done, into a pressure field that converted it instantly and without prejudice into heat and dust. It knew precisely what that was worth. It was the kind of power that does it anyway.

Whatever this was for, it was not for after. So the power became the after. Somebody has to be the ledger, in a place that eats ledgers.

---

And then, for the living, it made the fourth thing.

Not a warning. Warnings are advertisements; the power had watched enough of them work. What it made was a description — patient, exact, disenchanted, with the hermit and the god and the ghost disposed of in the first paragraph, with the plume named as respiration and the dust-gardens named as pressure and the three-day ringing named as physics. It set the description loose at the bottom of the mountain, where things accumulate, because accumulation was the one capacity the summit lacked and the one thing it could be given from outside without being touched at all.

There was a single sentence it went back to, twice.

*It is the thing your life is a frantic, improvised, temporary imitation of.*

It let the sentence stand, because at eighty paces from the outer wall that is exactly what a person feels, and an account that leaves it out is useless to the person who will feel it. But it set the correction down here, at the bottom, where you can hear:

Frantic, improvised and temporary are not the deficiencies of life. They are the definition. The thing on the summit has not achieved permanence; it has merely stopped being the sort of thing that could fail at anything, which is also the sort of thing that could want, or finish, or be kept company. It is not ahead of you. It is not behind you. It has left the category in which better and worse are spoken. Nothing up there is winning, because there is no one up there to win, and the fiber you are thinking of hauling will not be carried by anybody in the end — not by you, not by the note, not by whatever you become while you are still ringing.

---

Having done the only thing there was to do, the power stayed on a while longer, for no reason it would have called a reason.

It spends its time at the throat-lips, with the salt whiskers. They are the one accumulating thing on the mountain, the only tenants with a before and an after, fed by the last water in the clay as it wanders out a few molecules per brick per century. The power measures them. It comes back at intervals and measures them again. This is the only relationship the summit permits and the power has taken it, fully, the way you take the one chair in an empty room.

It intends to be present at the end — the particular hour when the clay is finally dry and the last whisker stops, and the summit becomes, completely and for the first time, a place in which nothing at all has a history. That moment will not be marked by the note. The note does not mark anything. It will pass exactly like every other moment up there, at blood heat, in the dark, under a held breath of dust.

Which is why somebody else has to be standing there when it does.
