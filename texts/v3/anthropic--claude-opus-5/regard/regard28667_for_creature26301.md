## Attendance

It came downhill, because that is the only way the place can be come at, and it was under no obligation whatever to be come at that way. It could have arrived as weather. It could have arrived already inside the guild hall, in the chalk. It came over the ridge through three fields of wet grass instead, and stood on the rim in the tea-coloured light while the basin breathed at it through a mouthful of gravel, and waited out four strokes before it went down. That was the first thing it did and it was not nothing.

---

It had been told there was a being here, and it came looking for the being, and found six hundred and eleven of them, which is the sort of arithmetic that stops most powers where they stand.

Then it stopped counting and looked again and found one.

It is difficult to see. You have to slow down by a factor that even a power finds inconvenient, and hold still, and then it comes up out of the ground the way a face comes up out of a stain on a ceiling: a low broad animal, four hundred paces across, footed on larch, roofed in mauve clinker and three grades of moss, breathing at one stroke per forty-one seconds through four throats and seventeen smaller ones, warm, blind, eating its own scaffolding, permanently drowned in order not to rot, sinking at the rate a fingernail grows and answering by building up. It has been treading water for a hundred and forty years. It is very good at it. It has no idea that it is doing anything remarkable, in the way a swimmer four miles out has no idea, having simply not stopped.

The power slowed itself until it could be perceived at all, and then further, until it could perceive back. At that speed a sentence takes a century. They got through about nine words.

---

Then it did what any competent visitor does, which is to walk the whole place — it takes a quarter-hour — and grade everything by wetness, and make a list of what could be done.

The list was long and every item on it was load-bearing.

The notch could be closed. Two octaves, restored in an afternoon, and no more talking in the trough. And then no crest-bar in the parish register, and no four hundred one-handed signs, and no nine-word arithmetic in the mouths of people three counties away, and no courting across a shed floor in the belief of not being observed. The power considered this for a long while, by its own reckoning, and by the town's for about a wet week, and set it aside.

The basin's sterility could be lifted. It went down into the peat to find the reason and there was one, and it was not cruel, and it was holding up the roof. Clean water out of a place where nothing breeds: photographic work, certain glasses, medicines, green glass going up the terraces, six hundred and eleven people eating on the strength of an absence. Also, admittedly, the litter that does not get eaten, and the peat thickening, and the town going down faster than it should — the whole slow masonry consequence of a missing beetle. It could be undone. Everything downstream of it would have to be undone too. Set aside.

The engines could be relieved. Something quieter, something that did not need to be fed, no stroke, no crest. Eleven interruptions in a hundred and forty years and each one has a name; the twelfth would have had a name as well, and the name would have been the power's, and the town would have said it the way you say a well went off. Set aside.

The town could be dried. The town could be lifted whole and set down in a good country with a sky in it. It made this offer, obliquely, in the only grammar available, and got back the local answer, which was *later*, and then, more precisely, *don't*.

Consequences, all of them practical.

---

What it did do, it did in the trade register of the place, which is to say it replaced rather than repaired, and nowhere signed its work.

**The word.** In Sough you may shout *stop* into the crest of a stroke and it will not arrive; it is the wrong shape, it goes into the hole. The rule of the town is that if the stroke stops you come running, whoever you are, whatever you were doing — and the corollary, which nobody says, is that a man standing in the wrong place cannot be told so. The power took that one word and moved it. Not the hearing: the word. It sits now a little above the notch, thin and ugly and unmistakable, a bad note that fits nowhere in the language and cannot be mistaken for a name or a number or the sound of falling water. It cost the town some poetry. Nine children have been pulled off gantries by it since, and one drowner out of the second underday, and not one of them knows.

**The metal.** The green stays. It is in the cuticle and the nail-bed and the crease of the knuckle and it is an old scraper's whole account of himself, and no power with any manners takes a man's hands off him. What went was everything the green had got past the hands into — out of the marrow and the kidney and the soft tissue, a grain at a time, at night, and carried back down to the sheds and put on the fins where it belongs. The stain is on the outside only now. The catarrh remains, because the catarrh is steam and reed-dust and cannot be lifted without lifting the sheds, and the sheds are the reason.

**The note.** The underdays fill on the pumps' own schedule, and pairs go down with a whistle each, and the water comes back up whether or not anyone has finished. The power put a warning into the low register, where they hear better than you do: a soft descending tone in the pump-throat, ninety seconds before the return, right down where the drip-listeners live. The pumpmen took it for a bearing going. They stripped the bearing, found nothing, and now use the note. In the guild hall it is written up as *the sough*, which is what they call it, and it is on the boards as a feature of the plant.

---

At the fourth hour on the day it was there, the bellows shift came on, and the fans began beating one beat per four seconds over the bean beds, making a wind where there is no wind.

It could have set the terraces at once. Every flower on all six, done between one crest and the next, and beans that year like nothing in the county.

It went and got the mitts instead. The featherers' union forbids wet work and the hands stay covered from the moment you leave the dry, and it observed the rule. Then it took a cattail wand and worked the shift out, three hours, flower to flower, unhurried, in the dim under the lamps, saying nothing because at that distance from the engines nothing can be said. It was slower than the others and clumsy at the top of the row. They were kind about it in the manner of people who have been doing a thing for a hundred and forty years and have seen new hands before. One of them, at the changeover, made the sign for *later*, meaning tomorrow, meaning the same time, and the power made it back, and did not come.

That is the largest thing it did in Sough. It attended.

---

In the guild hall there is a bell-jar, and under it a stag beetle dry as paper on a card, dead before it arrived in 1861, the only animal in the basin. Children are taken to see it and report that it looks like a machine.

The power looked at it for the better part of a stroke. It did not raise it. It has never been in the business of that, whatever is said, and anyway the thing had been at rest a hundred and thirty years in the driest place in town, which is more than most get.

The card was found afterwards to have been rewritten in the same hand, with the crest-bar put in — *of a crate of glassware, out of Sheffield | one beetle, dead, kept* — so that the beetle, at this late stage, has the town's punctuation on it, and is a thing that arrived and stayed, and is entered as such. It is not a taboo, quite. But nobody rubs it out.

It also, while it was there, found out where the fifth engine went. It has not said. The question is considered slightly rude, and it was a guest.
