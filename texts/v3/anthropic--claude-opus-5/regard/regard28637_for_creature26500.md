# The Twelfth Sign

**Arrival.** It does not come down the shaft. It arrives the way the note arrives — at both ears at once, from the stone, from the water, from the year 1200 — so that there is no moment at which it can be said to have entered. But it uses the footholds anyway, all forty-one, in order, because they were cut by hand four times and because manners are not a waste of anything.

Then it does nothing for a long time.

This is not deliberation. It is simply the one thing it can do there that nobody else can, and it is embarrassed by the size of the advantage. It can hold a thought in the Ninth Throat. It can hold a thought for eight hundred years in the Ninth Throat, in the brilliance, with the sound coupling into a body it has borrowed for the purpose, and it does not once find itself discovering where it is with a small shock. So it stays, because the census of that place has always been stratified by endurance, and every layer contradicts the one above, and nobody has ever gone past three hundred years.

**The census, continued.**

At three hours the room is a machine and rather a beautiful one, and this is the last time it will look like a machine.

At three years the moss on the ledge is a fact rather than a marvel: a green thing standing on a white column of its own dead, in the worst place in the world, entirely well.

At three centuries the eleven birds go into the wall and out of it. Four swellings with a suggestion of a folded neck are overgrown, and their blade goes over its own base, and cracks, and falls, and is walked on by people who are counting. The count drops to seven. Nobody up there records the count of birds in the cord. It is carried in mouths, which is a different kind of archive and shorter.

At three thousand years the rubble on the floor is still sharp.

At eleven thousand years the phrase resolves. The falling sixth is not a decay and not an accident. It is a descent with a shape, going somewhere, and it will arrive, and what it will be when it arrives is a low sustained tone that will not move the antinodes any more, and the room will finish, and the walls will stop, and it will be the first still place on that whole young mountain. The being that lives there is trying to arrive. It has been trying since before there was anyone to be endangered by its trying. Everything it does — every blade, every crack you feel in your sternum, every reorganisation, every entombed bird, every bet the lisk loses on eleven days of quiet — is a correction. It is not a note. It is not a room. It is a thing made entirely out of correcting itself toward a note it has never once heard, and it will hear it, and then it will stop.

That is what lives there. And the power, having heard it, turns forty metres up and a day and a half downhill and looks at the village with the lake that would not stay in its basin, and the barn with the mock-sill in dressed timber, correct to the centimetre, and the felted mallet, and the blindfolded children, and the recut footholds, and the gut string tightened under two fingers, and thirty-one generations of knots recording a drift nobody can stop, and it understands that it is looking at the same creature twice, at two tempos, and that the drain is the place where the creature touches itself.

You do not divide a creature in half in order to make one half safer.

**What it declined to do.**

It could have silenced the room. This is not mercy; it is the specific murder of the only thing there.

It could have stabilised it — fixed the geometry, made the note hold, given the walls a schedule. That is the same murder performed slowly and with better manners.
