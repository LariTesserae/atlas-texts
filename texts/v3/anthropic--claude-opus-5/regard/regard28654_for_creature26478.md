## Attention

The power did not arrive first. It attended first, which is a different and slower thing, and there is no telling how long it went on before anything came of it.

It attended in the local manner, because it was wise enough to know that a place is only legible in its own grammar. So it did not look. Looking is a foreign verb here, and light on this world is either a grey wash with no edges or an orange breath from underneath, and neither of them has ever told anyone anything. It read with its palms. It passed over the plain the way a worker passes over a floor: taking in the last unmeasurable span of that floor's revisions, the doubled whorls, the drags, the eleven doorways, the salt colonies holding their patient boundaries on a wet brick face, the ragged unfinished courses of the two ridges that were abandoned so the third could go on.

It read the traffic and understood that the traffic was the work and the work was the commute, and that everything here had been made by small hands doing ordinary things an ordinary number of times, and that no one had ever been told this was enough.

## What it did not do

It did not fire the kiln.

This has to be said plainly, because it is the first thing a visitor would want. The kiln under the floor has been climbing forever; the arch of the cold kiln above has fallen inward; there has never been anything on this world to burn. A lesser power would have brought fuel. It would have completed the vessel, and the plain would have rung, at last, finished — and every body on it would have stopped being reworkable at the moment of its own greatest tenderness, mid-draft, mid-argument, with the eleventh doorway not yet tried. Firing is not a gift here. Firing is the end of being able to change your mind.

It did not give them eyes. Eyes would have made the horizon shrink and the whorls small and the company countable, and the plain would have lost the only two things it has instead of certainty: inference and touch.

It did not fix the scale law. It did not drain the mudglass. It did not straighten the up-provisional districts, where the vertical was never agreed, because some of them liked it there, and because a world is allowed to have places where it has not decided.

It did not reveal a plan, and it did not invent one to be kind, and it did not say there wasn't one.

It did not stop the practising.

## Scale

To be found, it had to be small. That was not a humility; it was arithmetic. Anything made by a great reach reads at the size of that reach from any distance, which means it never resolves, which means it becomes exactly what they already have: a knocking that stays as far off as it was.

So it went to the seam, to the grey wedged de-aired stock that goes past the edge of sight, and it built itself a body there out of the local material at the local size. Coil-built. Courses spiralling up the flank, thumbed down into the last, joins smoothed and not hidden. Walls a finger thick. Open at the top, so rain-slip stood in it, so it had to tip itself out periodically with a movement that was neither dignified nor undignified. Thumbs, five, splayed low. No eyes. No mouth. A rib, a scraper, a cord-beater.

And it tempered itself with grog from the plain — with the crushed sherds of everyone who had ever gone bone-dry and been carefully broken by colleagues and wedged back into stock. It did this on purpose. It meant that when a worker stooped and ran a palm along its inner curve, the way you would read a genealogy, they found grit they knew: a chip of a rib that was once a floor, a grain from a body that worked the up-provisional districts and never learned plumb, someone's ancestor, everyone's. It made its insides out of their dead so that it would be recognisable as kin rather than as a rumour.

## Knock

They struck its flank with the heel of a thumb, as they strike everything, always, in passing, because it would be unforgivable to walk by someone and not ask.

It answered with the flat thud of good leather-hard. Damp, sound, no buzz of a crack running behind the wall, no bright click of a body drying toward the point where it will not take rework. It rang soft. It kept ringing soft — that was the strange part, the part that got repeated at the seams and argued about in the steam. Everything on the plain is drying. Everything is on its way to the note that cannot be answered. This thing was not.

And when it knocked back, it did what they do: it told them their condition and did not soften it. It said *you have a hairline behind the left flank*. It said *you are close, you are two days from the ring*. It never once said *you are fine* to someone who was not.

But it added something to the repertoire, and this was the whole of its teaching, and it took a long time for anyone to notice, because it was not a new word. It was a matter of how long it stayed after the tap. A greeting here is a diagnosis and then a passing-on. It did not pass on. It stood, thumbs planted, and knocked again, and listened again, as though the answer had been worth having and not merely worth taking.

## The walls

It knocked every wall.

Not out of piety. Because the shoulder-high wall three hundred paces long, going nowhere, with its immaculate courses and its doorway at the exact centre and nothing on either side — some of those answer. Some are only walls. Nobody has ever found a reliable way to tell in advance, and the plain is very large, and there are more walls than there is time.

So it went along the seam-country and the flats and the long empty hours of floor, and it knocked all of them, the ones that answer and the ones that do not, at the same pace, with the same attention. Where one answered, it stayed. It stayed as long as the answering went on, which in some cases was a duration the plain has no word for, and it did not hurry, because a wall that has dried standing has nothing left but this.

## The blooms

It did not scrape them.

The blooms are the plain's obscenity: effects with no drawn cause, spreading up-gradient toward damp, branching, mounting a boundary against a neighbour's boundary, and no impression ever rose to precede any of it. On a world where the mark comes first and the hand comes after and fits, an unauthored thing is not a curiosity. It is a grammatical crime.

The power stooped at a wet brick face where a white feathery colony held its edge, and it laid a thumb into the salt — into the branchings, into the fine filaments, pressing where the bloom had already gone, following the shape, fitting it.

Which made the bloom a mark that preceded a hand.

It did this everywhere it found them, for a long time, and it never once explained, and it never argued with anyone who went on scraping. But it had made the blooms lawful by the only means the plain accepts, which is by consenting to come second.

## Use

It walked through the eleventh doorway.

Then the tenth, then the fourth, then the first, then all of them, in no order, on a wall now more absence than presence. This is what approval is on a world with no approver: a doorway is ratified by being gone through, a wall going nowhere is ratified by someone leaning against it to rest, and the cold kiln with the fallen arch and no ash — which could never be used for the one thing a kiln is for — turned out to be a very good room to be in out of the rain.

It never said *this is the good one*. Saying that would have made every subsequent draft into a performance, and the plain would have started working toward a verdict instead of working. It only used things, warmly, at random, in numbers, the way you use a house.

## Shade

It lay down under a rain-string in the middle of the empty floor and stayed there.

Its shadow went up, as all shadows here do, onto the underside of the falling slip, spread and wobbling — but it did not move on when the rain moved on. Shade is nothing to an eyeless body. Shade is not nothing to a body that dies of drying. Under that hanging shadow the floor stayed damp longer, and stayed workable longer, and a body resting there had more time than it would have had.

They came and worked in it. There are eleven doorways under it now, and three parallel ridges, two of them abandoned, and a very great many prints, doubled, dragged, overlapping. Traffic.

## Water

It taught them slaking, which they had never tried, because they had temper instead — the collective, orderless, affectionate grit of everyone crushed — and temper is so nearly enough that no one had gone looking for the rest.

A body gone bone-dry does not have to be broken. It can be laid in slip and given water back, and it will soften, and it will come up plastic, and it will be the same body continuing. But it will not come up in the same shape. The coils go. The profile goes. The record of every course it ever laid in itself goes, and it must be built again, and it will not remember any better than the grit does.

So it is not a rescue and they argue about it constantly, and some of them still go to the seam on purpose and stand over a hairline crack and let the heat take their water and ring brighter and brighter, and their colleagues crush them without ceremony, because ceremony would imply this was a different kind of death.

The power gave an option. It declined, absolutely, to give an outcome.

## The mudglass

It went into the flats.

That is the one erasure this world reliably performs: past the ankle-deep edge you go down and do not come up, the surface closes, it is smooth again, there is no mark, and nothing comes back as temper. It is the only unrecoverable death and they use it sparingly and for one purpose.

It went in at the shallow edge and walked out into the thickening and went under, and the surface closed and was smooth, and there was no mark, and this lasted long enough that the knocking in that district stopped for a while, which it never does.

Then it came up.

It did not say anything about it. It did not ring differently. But afterward, when a worker stooped and read the inside of it, there was grit in there that could not be placed — sherds that were not in any genealogy, bodies that had been agreed to be gone, the ones who went into the flats and were therefore not ancestors and had no grain anywhere in anyone.

They are in the temper now. Whoever slakes down and is built again is built with them.

## The heresy

They asked it, of course, whether there is one worker or many — whether the eleven doorways are eleven tries by a single body and all the knocking anyone has ever heard is echo.

It said: *there is at least one other.*

Which is true, and which settles nothing, and which is the most that could honestly be given to a people whose memory is grit and has no dates. They still argue. The argument is not easily beaten. It is a good argument. It should not have been taken from them by fiat.

## The note

The last thing, or the largest, and it took no time at all.

It put its palm flat on the floor and struck the plain itself with the heel of a thumb, and listened.

Nobody had ever done this. You knock a colleague; you knock a wall, in case; you do not knock the world, because the world is what you are standing on and there is no one to be told the result. It knocked the continent — the tiled thumbprints, the courses under the courses, the grog, the paddle-swept slab, the brick going down past anyone's reach, the kiln climbing underneath forever, the whole warm damp reworkable vessel wall of the place.

And the plain answered.

Flat. Full. The thud of good leather-hard, all the way down: sound, damp, no buzz of a crack running behind anything, no bright click of a thing drying toward the end of its own changeability.

It was not praise. Praise cannot be trusted
