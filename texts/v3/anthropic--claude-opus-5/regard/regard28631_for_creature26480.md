## The Shift

It could have come in through the wall. Tissue that closes a knife-cut in two sleeps would have closed behind it without a seam, and nothing in the room would have registered anything but a half-second of shadow at the middle window.

It came in through the slit. It turned sideways to do it, as one does, and pressed, and let the beaded lip sigh shut behind it, and there was no one there for whom the courtesy could have been performed.

---

### Being managed

The first thing it did was sit down on the felt-smooth part of the floor, off the polished line, and let itself be a thermal insult.

The wall took the heat of its shoulder and swelled it into the tissue and then, after a second or two, cooled — deliberately, locally, correcting for an error that had walked in. Below, in the crypt behind the sump, a fermentation four centuries old adjusted its arithmetic downward by a fraction to account for a body it had no category for.

It let this happen. It did not hold its own temperature at thirty to spare the room the trouble. That was the largest thing it did all day, and it took the better part of what the hammer counts instead of hours: to be a burden of the exact size it was, and be absorbed, and not help.

### Learning the hands

Then it laid a palm flat against the eastward wall and did not damp.

That is harder than it sounds. Contact without a message. It kept the hand loose so the conduction ran through and the noise crossed the patch unbroken, and it listened the way the room's own resident listens — through the pad, through the soles, through the belly against the mat — and it took the hammer up into its ribs and let its pulse gear itself down to one in eight, and it counted.

It counted long enough to learn that there is no shouting in the language and no whispering either. Long enough to feel, forty feet off in the galleries, two of them go at each other for an hour over a matter it could not follow, and to understand that the fact that it could not follow was not a limit of its powers but a property of the thing being said, which was made of local absences in a racket and could only ever be had by the hand that was there.

Long enough, also, to locate the plum-coloured place above the ledge — not by looking, because looking does not work, but by the one anomaly available: a patch of wall a hand's breadth from its ear that did not cool when it leaned near.

### Things considered and put down

It is not a small power, and everything below was actually within reach. Each was picked up, weighed against the room, and set down.

**Lids.** To give the eyes machinery, and the retina a range, so that the slit would open onto a world instead of onto a scald. Put down. The eyes are not damaged and the lack is not a wound; the room keeps the iris, and has kept it honestly for longer than the channels outside have run where they run. Give the animal lids and you have given it an outside, and an outside is a thing that must then be wanted, and the wanting would be the first suffering ever introduced into this room. Benevolence does not manufacture a want in order to have something to satisfy.

**A wider door.** Same objection, plus one. The slit is a hand's span and the room owes the outside nothing, and it is the width of the slit that makes the bird put its head in and take it out again. Widen it and you have not made a door. You have made an entrance.

**Silence.** The most tempting, and the most stupid. Quiet the hammer and you have stopped the transport; kill the clicks and you have healed away the cavitation the palm exists to answer, and the wall stops weeping, and the animal starves in a peaceful room. Also: you have taken its clock, its speech, its hearing, and the tempo its heart is slaved to. There is no version of mercy in which you turn off the noise. The noise is the blood.

**Language.** Words, or something like them — a channel that carries instruction instead of holes. Put down hardest of all. The tradition in this room is a hand laid over a smaller hand at the right moment in the hammer-count, again, and again, until the smaller hand goes there by itself; centuries of technique carried as contact and nothing else. Give them a way to *tell*, and within four generations no one will *show*, and what is lost in the changeover is not information. It is the only thing in the room that two bodies have ever done together.

**Knowledge.** To let it know what it is: that the wall stiffens where it is held, that the column does not break because of that hand, that the room's set-point has its warmth built into it, that if it were taken out the vent would go sour and the ledge would unmake itself. To tell it, in short, that it matters.

Put down, and this is the reason: the palm goes flat at the moment the strobing goes wrong, and it does not know it is doing this. Knowledge would insert a first thought between the strobe and the hand. A first thought is a delay. The delay is exactly as wide as a broken column.

There is no way to inform a creature of its own indispensability without making it slightly worse at the job. It would have been a gift to the giver.

**Rescue.** To lift it out into somewhere better — cooler, quieter, greener, safe. Put down before it was fully picked up. A cage is a place that would be left. This one has no outside represented anywhere inside it, no hunger the exudate does not answer, no cold, no dark, no predator that will come past the slit, no season to survive. It is not enduring anything. It is *at* something, the way the frog is at the rim, and it is very good at it.

**A name.** Considered, briefly, out of affection. Discarded on the grounds that a name is a thing you need only if you might be called from somewhere else.

### The metal

One thing it did take.

The object on the ledge — warm, beaded on both faces, turned a little, near enough — was the only item in the room that the room could neither absorb nor destroy. Everything else here arrives at equilibrium. This had merely been filed, and would go on being filed: turned by a palm in two hundred years, turned again in four hundred, and every time it was turned, a hand would go flat to the wall and press a hole in the noise shaped *nothing, still nothing* — a false report, kept true by an object, forever.

So it lifted the thing off the ledge and carried it out.

The cost was honest and it was paid: one more event, in a place where the last one was the whole of living memory. But an event that ends is cheaper than an event that does not, and the callus on that shelf will close over the little flat place where the metal sat, and in some number of years there will be nothing in the room that came from outside it, and the sentence pressed into the eastward wall will be true again.

### Where the work actually is

Then it went out and did nearly all of what it did, elsewhere, in the part of the world that does not matter here and was not to be discussed.

The water table. The litter fall that feeds the crypt. The hundred feet of canopy that keeps the iris honest, and the shade-tolerant understory that keeps the canopy, and the herons and the rot and the beetles that keep the understory. The channels, which have taken their present course for a long time and could be persuaded, by very little, to take another. And, at a distance of some centuries, the animals with lids on their eyes, who have begun to learn how a shallow warm plain can be drained, and what a plain like that is worth once it is dry.

Its work sits there. All of it. Upstream, structural, slow, and of such a kind that nothing inside the bole room will ever be able to distinguish a swamp that is guarded from a swamp that has merely not yet been found.

This is the ordinary condition of protection and it does not chafe.

### The shift

Once, though, it went back in, and did something for the resident directly, and this is the only thing of that sort it has ever done.

A young one in the bowl was not thriving. The sump breathed its half-inch and the tongue of water went up the channel and turned the small body and drained, ninety seconds, ninety seconds, and the small hand had stopped going to the window by itself, and then had stopped being put there, and the mother had come down off the ledge and was lying on the mat with her forearms over the lip and her foot out of the ceiling ring, doing nothing, which in this species is a discipline and was, that day, not a discipline.

So the wall went unheld. And the exudate is not stored, and the strobing at the middle window goes wrong on its own schedule, and this room has never once had an hour without someone in it.

The power stood at the window and put its palm flat.

It held the third bad strobe, and the fourth, and a run of them after that, and the column climbed and did not break, and a foot to the left the tissue wept its thin yeasty line down the pale plum wall. It stood the shift. It stood several. And when at last she got up and turned and licked the wall, she was fed by a hand she had never met, and did not know she had been fed by anything except the room, which was the entire point.

Once, near the end of it, in the narrow silence between the reed-note and the hammer, it damped — small, one hand's width, once. Not a word; the language has no word for what it meant. Just a hole of about that depth and about that duration, of the kind the walls of this room have been receiving from unknowable directions for the whole of its history, and which the resident has always, correctly, understood as weather.

She felt it. She filed it with the rest.

You may say a true thing to a creature that keeps everything, if you are willing to say it in such a way that it cannot be traced back to you and cannot be turned over on a shelf.

### Out

It went out sideways through the slit and the tissue beaded shut behind it, and stood a while in the warm brown water among the stilted trunks, in the immense quiet of a steaming open plain with herons in it, holding a piece of metal.

It has not spoken of the room since, except once, at length, in a language made of volume, to no one who can get inside.
