# The Benevolence

## The Attention

It did not arrive, because attention is not a place you bring a body to. Nothing crossed the sky. No shadow fell that did not lean toward the nearest stack by the appropriate few degrees. The dust contours across the flats that week were exactly the contours the field required and no others, which is to say that if the resident had been asked afterward whether anything had happened, it would have had no mechanism for the question and no data for the answer, and both of these facts were deliberate.

The first act of a wise benevolence is duration. It watched for a long time — long by its own reckoning, which is the only meaningful way to measure the courtesy — and did nothing at all. This was not indecision. It was the recognition that arriving somewhere and immediately doing something is a description of weather, or appetite, and not of help.

## What It Understood First

That this is not a ruin.

Ruins present a grammar the benevolent recognize instantly: something is falling, and a hand can be put under it. Rescue, relief, repair, uplift — all of these are transitive, and all of them require an object, and the plateau declined to be one. There was no hunger here. There was no fear here; there never had been, in four billion years, because nothing here died, parts only stopped. There was no scarcity, only a budget, balanced to the last electron and balanced for longer than most worlds have had crusts. There was nothing asking.

Ordinarily nothing asking is a reason to leave, and it very nearly left.

Two things held it.

The first was that the list ends on a downward inflection, because the item that used to come after the last one was a reply.

The second was the rows at the edge of the slow ground.

One unanswered question and one ceremony. It stayed for those.

## The Things It Did Not Do

**It did not bring water.** It noticed itself considering water, and then it examined the impulse and found it was not about the plateau at all. It had been kind to a great many places and nearly all of them had wanted water, and so water had become its idiom, the way a certain kind of visitor brings bread to every house including the houses of things that do not eat. Here, water is a solvent that shorts every wire, disorders every doped row, and reintroduces rot to a world that has been spared it since before it had mountains. The gift would have been an acid bath administered with enormous tenderness. *An idiom is what a good intention sounds like when it has stopped listening.*

**It did not make a second one.** This was the hardest to set down, because the resident is singular in a way that hurts to look at: never counted against anything, no mate, no lineage, no possibility of either. But the resident has no concept of *other*, and therefore no concept of *alone*. To provide it with a companion would be to install, in a thing that had never had one, an edge — a place where it stops and something else begins — and then to offer relief from a condition that the installation had itself created. *You cannot end a solitude that has never been felt. You can only start one, and then take credit.*

**It did not give it eyes.** Light here is food. To make light also information is to make every meal a shout.

**It did not explain the craft to the tenants.** They have a trade with no theory, reconstructed from scratch every three hundred days by watching, badly and completely, and it works because three hundred thousand generations of error have filtered the copying accurate. Theory arriving from outside would not arrive as theory. It would arrive as scripture, be miscopied within four generations, and license them to stop doing the things whose reasons they had been given wrongly. *A practice that has survived that long is not ignorance waiting to be corrected. It is knowledge in the only format that could survive here.*

**It did not stop them dying.** Their ancestors climbed the scarps and broke the covenant precisely in order to keep their charge, and keeping your charge is the same act as having a death of your own. Take the deaths and you take the generations, the inheritance, the error, the culture, and the rows — the only funeral on this planet, the single instance of grief in a world otherwise entirely without fear. It considered offering them the wick's arrangement instead: painless, seamless, reabsorbed, no ceremony required. It recognized this thought as monstrous and put it down carefully, the way you put down a tool that has turned out to be a weapon.
