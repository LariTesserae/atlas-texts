## What Was Done on the Shelf

### I. Attention

It arrived long before it did anything, and the doing, when it came, was very small.

This was not timidity. It is simply that a place which counts must first be counted, and the power had learned — elsewhere, expensively — that benevolence practised at speed is mostly weather. So for a while it was nothing on the Shelf but attention: a quality in the air no thinner than the air already was, present at the winnowing, present in the cheese-cellar, present on the scree at the hour when the browsers stop eating and no one has ever known why they stop.

It counted. Sheep, hares, hawks, courses of wall, knives, the ounces of ripening cheese, the moths coming granular out of the joints. It found the tallies of the Count exact. It found the rain exact. It found, examining the mineral film on a cold morning, that the film returned not approximately to the places the day's grinding had taken it from but to those places, particle for particle, and that this was accomplished without hurry and without any mechanism it could locate, in the manner of a hand smoothing a sheet.

And then it noticed the thing that reorganised everything else: four thousand one hundred names, every one of them worn continuously, never numbered, never retired. That is not a population. That is an anatomy.

The Shelf did not have four thousand one hundred people living on it. It had one being, and the people were how the being was arranged. The names were organs; the wearers were the cells that pass through an organ and are replaced without the organ noticing. The Count was not a government, it was a proprioception — nineteen places where the being felt its own outline. The hollow day was not a bureaucratic cruelty but a wound closing, the local pain of a body that has lost a cell and is waiting to make another, and the three days of loud weeping afterward were the body's permission to feel what it had already survived.

This changed what could decently be intended. You may pity a town. You may improve a town. A being you must ask.

### II. The Offering at the Bevel

It asked in the only grammar available, which was arithmetic.

On the clean bevel of the rim, three feet from where the stone would open a palm, it laid down a double handful of true soil: black, sour, alive, full of the rot of leaves that had never grown within two thousand feet of that altitude, threaded with worms and fungal white and the seeds of eleven low plants that spread instead of concentrating. It was a fair offering and an honest one. It meant: *this is what I have. Talus and loam and slumping. Ruin under your foundations, layers, middens, the thing you call history. Grandmothers. Say yes and I will bring the mountain down into its own softness and you will get time, and you will get soil in the cracks, and your dead will be somewhere other than in the street holding a bucket.*

It waited through the dust-watch with forty or fifty people who did not know it was there.

By sunrise the soil was gone. Not scattered, not blown into the fog — gone in the way the day's ground stone is gone before it is put back, taken up into the accounting and set down again wherever it had come from, in whatever forest, at whatever hour, to within a thimble. There was no scorch and no violence and nothing to be offended by. The refusal had exactly the tone of a blanket being tucked in.

The power considered the possibility that the Even was a prison and the being inside it a hostage, and looked for the jailer, and looked for a very long time, and did not find one, and eventually understood that it had been asking the wrong question in a place with no history: nobody had done this. The mountain came up sharp and young and something caught it mid-gesture, and the something was not separate from the caught thing. The Even is the being. To break the Even in order to free the being is to hold a man's head under water in order to get the drowning out of him.

So: no.

### III. What Hask Wanted

Refused by the whole, it went to a part, which is the oldest courtesy there is.

It came to the east gate in the afternoon and sat down on the shiny stone beside a man who had been sitting there eleven years, and asked him — in the roundabout way such things must be asked, as a stranger's foolish question — whether he would like the mountain to learn to slump.

Hask thought about it with the seriousness of a man who mends cord for a living and does not answer quickly. Then he said that soil would ruin the joint-channels, and that his sister's floor was shimmed just so, and that if the walls stopped being where the walls were the twelve masons would have nothing to do and would be unbearable at meals. He said the fog was the same fog either way. He said he did not want the world altered.

He wanted to go down the switchbacks. That was all. He had wanted it since he was nineteen and he wanted it in the same size and shape as he had wanted it then, one man's worth, and he would have it or he would die on the bench, and either way the total would hold.

The power had come prepared to remake a mountain and was told, politely, by a man with grooved fingertips, that the request was for one seat.

This is the whole of its education on the Shelf and it is recorded here so that it will not have to be learned again: the cosmic remedy is almost never what is wanted. What is wanted is specific, local, unglamorous, and exactly the size of a person.

### IV. Payment

It stood up and walked down into the fog and came back up out of it four days later footsore, mumbling, blinking at the hard unmediated light, with the stone-cut in its palm that everybody gets on the last switchback where the bevel comes close to the path. Eleven dogs found it hilarious.

The gatekeeper's scale is a literal thing. The arriving sat, and Hask, who did not recognise the stranger and had no reason to, stood up off the bench with a sound in his throat that the power has kept, and the counter at the gate tied her knot and said *closed*, and Hask went down the switchbacks that evening with a wheel of cheese on his back and a cord on the wheel with eleven knots in it, which the cellarer had not been asked to allow and had pretended not to notice.

The name Hask had gone cold for part of an afternoon. The arriving one wears it. He mends cord for the gatekeeper and gives directions to the terraces and leans two degrees into the hill, which took his spine four years to learn and hurt the entire time. He does not remember what he was. This is not a punishment; it is the tariff. A place where nothing accumulates will not let you keep your own duration inside it, and a self spent into the Even is spent, and does not come back, and is loved from outside the way you love an emigrant: continuously, uselessly, without contact.

The power has done this more than once. It cannot say how many times, because the ones it spent are inside a place where that kind of number does not pile up, and it declines to guess. There are people on the Shelf right now who are it, and who do not know, and who count the cups when they come into a room.

### V. The Art Available

Barred from adding and barred from subtracting, the power discovered that everything good it could still do was a rearrangement, and set about becoming a virtuoso of permutation.

The number of sharp knives in the city is constant. Which knife is where is not specified. So: the dull one drifts, always, to the hand of the child; the sharp one is on the block when the slaughter-clerk has been satisfied. Rain falls in perfectly predictable amounts on unpredictable days — and so it falls, when it can be managed, on the day the woman in the fourth level cannot get out of bed and needs the streets to be empty and the gutters loud.
