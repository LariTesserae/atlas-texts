## Nýgres — the attention

**How it came.**

Nothing landed. No light stood in the canteen doorway, no voice used the compressor's gap to speak in. The attention arrived the way weather arrives here: by contact rather than by falling, depositing itself on stone, on wire, on wool, on the backs of hands, and being noticed by nobody.

What drew it was the slate. Four numbers in one hand: people, terns, channel depth, millimetres gained. A place where a stranger and a bird and the water and the ground are the same kind of fact, written in the same chalk, corrected the same day. There is not much of that anywhere.

**What the being was.**

It had first to decide what it was looking at, and this took it longer than anything else it did.

The plain was not the being. Sixty-one years old, no habits, no self yet — a body that has not decided which of its motions are gestures. The four hundred and seven were not the being either; they were four hundred and seven, and it could count them, and counting is not the same as finding. The works was a machine with a company behind it, and the company was elsewhere, and was not interesting.

The being was the arrangement. Four hundred and seven bodies; eighty-eight houses standing on jacks on pads with a written record of each pad; one furnace; a pot of yesterday on the back ring; twenty-two thousand index cards about a blind shrimp; eleven volumes of a book of heights; and, in every west wall in the town, a sash wedged open a finger's width in all weathers so that the ground can be heard in the room. That is one creature. It eats basalt and grey light. It keeps its own records because nobody else will.

**Its first answer.**

Its first answer to the question *what should be done* was *nothing*, and it held that answer for a long time and never entirely let go of it. Everything that follows was argued down from *nothing*, one item at a time, against resistance.

**The first mistake.**

It went for the obvious, the way anyone does.

A boy of eleven opened himself from ankle to mid-calf on a slab edge above the second thicket, the shallow bloodless opening the plain specialises in. The power closed it. No scar, no tape, nothing to explain.

Then it watched the father come looking for the wound with the roll of tape already in his hand, and not find it, and go quiet, and say nothing to anyone for four days.

So it put the mark back, thinner than the original, and learned the first rule of the place: some of the damage here is script. The plain writes on them and they read each other by it, and a boy with unmarked shins at eleven is a boy who has been excluded from something without being told what.

It made the same mistake in a smaller way with the notch at four kilohertz, and stopped before anyone was harmed. A hearing aid here does not fail because it is too loud. It fails because it brings the terns and the fulmars and the floor and the compressor back all at once and makes the ground speak directly to you, and a person who has spent sixty years being carried by a sound cannot suddenly be addressed by it.

**The list of things it did not do, with reasons.**

1. It did not stop the terns from attacking the school path. Four weeks a year, hunched, sticks up, in a screaming white cone, counting heads: this is the best memory almost every adult in Nýgres has, and the census depends on the walk, and the walk depends on the terns being exactly as they are.

2. It did not take the glass out of the skin folds. The itch is a tax and the tax has a rite: the outer room, the hook, the bench, the stiff brush on the lanyard, and the sound of a person beating themselves in the porch before they are allowed into a kitchen. Somewhere in every adult here is a mother's voice saying *I can't hear you brushing.* You do not remove that to spare someone a wrist.

3. It did not level the plain. It considered this seriously and for some time — an even lift, eleven millimetres everywhere, houses that could be set solid on footings and forgotten. It declined because *we are screwed down, not planted* is a load-bearing sentence, and because of the four days: the surveyor's level, the children holding the staff, the food, the works shut, and the whole population out on the black rock re-levelling itself once a year in a silence it describes as beautiful and unbearable in roughly equal measure. Nothing it could give them is worth those four days.

4. It did not save the works. It could have. Orders, a market, a mind changed in a boardroom in a city that has never heard the floor. It declined, because a town kept alive by a hand it cannot see is not a town but a pet, and because the being in question is not the furnace. It made three arrangements instead about what the furnace leaves behind. They are below.

5. It did nothing whatever for Sigrún, who has been blind since her fifties and crosses from her door to the canteen by ear in any weather, using the low note over the smooth ropes, the wasp at the burst slab, and the silence at the quarry lip. She was the only person in Nýgres for whom nothing at all was needed. The power entered this in its own records as the town's highest achievement to date.

6. It did not write on the slate.

**What it did.**

It worked in the local units, which are millimetres per year and periods of eleven seconds, because a gift in the wrong units is an insult with good manners.

*The shrimp.* It went down into the pool and looked at the amphipods properly. The whole population descends from one gravid female that arrived on a bird's foot around 1966; the line is thin, and one hard change of chemistry or two degrees the wrong way would end it, and with it Óli's office, and with it — this being the actual load path — the town's willingness to trust the only water it has. So the power did the smallest ugly technical work: it widened the tolerance of the line by about two degrees and a little acidity. Nothing visible. Óli's card for that morning read sixty-one per jar, which was ordinary.

*The crack.* The pool is fed by a fissure under a slab that was going to close, quietly and without warning, in about 2031. The power set a single grain of olivine crosswise in it. Sized to hold four hundred years.

*The eleven seconds.* This was the wound it could see coming and nobody could buy a remedy for. When the furnace stops the compressor stops, and the town loses its metre: the hymns, the drinking songs, the spacing of words in an argument, the frame the choir cannot explain to mainland conductors, the beat their babies were put down on. No boat brings that.

So it went to the cliff and looked at the gas-pocket cave the size of a stairwell. At present the mouth is too low and too wide and the sea only gargles in it. But the plain rises eleven millimetres a year and the sea is going patiently away, and the overhang above the mouth is held together by one seam that salt is already interested in. The power did not touch the rock with anything like a hand. It expressed a preference in the rate of a few million small events — which flake goes, which grain stays wet — so that in a certain year the lip will thin and drop, and the mouth will be the right size, and at any tide above three metres the surge will enter and compress the air in the pocket and let it go as a thump that travels through the vesicled rock and is felt in the soles of boots two kilometres inland.

It set the period at eleven seconds. It could have chosen any number. This was the only sentimental act it committed and it knew so at the time and did it anyway.
