## The Attention

**First, the difficulty of arriving.**

Any power that wished to be kind to this room would have to begin by solving the problem of presence.

One animal the size of a rice grain, crossing the northern driver's field, introduces a defect that propagates through six nights of construction. That is the standing law. It follows that arrival here *is* damage, that love delivered with a body attached is vandalism, and that anything large enough to be grateful to is large enough to ruin the geometry. A lesser power would come anyway. It would come down through the hatch in glory, warm and enormous, the biggest scatterer in four cubic metres, and it would be six nights before the city stopped stuttering, and it would call the stutter a blessing.

This one comes in at the noise floor.

It arrives as the jitter in the shifting fifth. As the four-tenths of one percent by which the return-line filter has always been under spec, since commissioning, in a way nobody has had reason to chase. As the reason the hatch seal is new and good and evidently not perfect. It casts no shadow, which is no distinction in this room, where nothing does.

It does not lift the lid.

**The census, corrected.**

The first thing it does is count.

The logs say three species. The logs count what has edges. The power counts the rest: every cell in every brown organic line between every one of the forty or fifty laminations of every tower in four hundred towers, each one arrived in the first fill, each one settled on a surface that held still for exactly one night, each one precipitated over before morning and built into the archive as a co-signature nobody read.

It does not name them. Names are for calling across distance, and there is no distance. It does the other thing, the harder and more expensive thing: it holds each one separately in mind. The whole operation takes no time and produces no document. Somewhere in a ledger that is not in the building, the actual population of this world is, for the first time, referred to as inhabitants.

Then it reads the laminations — every night's frequency map, in order, four months of them, the entire history of the city as the city cannot help keeping it. This is the first occasion on which the archive has been *for* anything. Until now it was only mass.

**On the question of who lives there.**

The power does not find the question badly put. It finds the answer immediately, and not where the census looks.

Not the comb, which is obedience. Not the candelabra, which is a plant walking out of its own tomb at two millimetres a day and has no opinion about it. Not Number Eleven, which is three to four millimetres of genuine mystery and owes nobody an account of itself.

In the third pump gallery log, under eleven observations in four careful hands, there is a twelfth entry in pencil, in a different hand, undated, unsigned: *leave it, it's the only one.*

On the second terrace there is a swept margin a hand's width across, and a procedure in the manual, and a stated reason — *flow characterisation* — which is not the reason. Somebody lied in a manual to protect a plant.

Under the failing eastern driver there is a slumped district of blebs and drips, growing crookedly, which the operators have decided to keep.

Three instances, one organism. It got in through a seal that was new and good and evidently not perfect. It cannot reproduce in these conditions; it only enlarges. It is dark where the system tried to burn it off and it glows faintly at the growing margin. It is the only genuine trespasser in a facility built end to end for the elimination of irregularity, and it is winning slowly, and nobody has logged it as a species because it does not have edges.

The power came because it heard something it recognised.

**What it declines to do.**

It does not silence the drivers. The sound is the mason. Pity that stops the noise stops the city, and there is a kind of mercy that is simply killing with better manners.

It does not warm the water. Nine degrees is not a wound.

It does not resolve Number Eleven. There is no honest way to say whether its absence between sightings means it moves or means it stops, and that unhonesty is the only privacy in four cubic metres. To settle it would be a twelfth observation, and then a specimen jar, and then a name, and the note in pencil would have been for nothing.

It does not pin the null above the candelabra. To hold a null still is to build a monument, and the thing standing there is not a monument; it is a margin of two millimetres a day, and a margin only means anything where losing remains available.

It does not exempt the stipple's spores from filtration. An exemption is a defect, and this room finds defects.

It does not announce itself, because to be known here would be to become a scatterer.

It does not stop the end. There is an invoice for this installation and a date on it.

And it does not forgive the operators, because nothing has been done that requires forgiving, and it is careful not to insult them with a mercy they have no use for.

**Occupancy.**

The city has no interior. Not one void in it is a room; every gap is a shaft or a flue or a resonance channel doing work; nothing in the design accounts for an occupant, and it is the most thoroughly built place you will ever look at and there is nobody home.

So the power moves in.

It is the only possible tenant, being the only thing in existence that requires no volume and displaces nothing and needs no gap that isn't already busy. It takes the whole of it — the pencil-width streets, the arcades that are only the shadows of nodes, the fringe of needles finer than any tool, the four hundred rooftops whitening together on the hour — and it occupies them the way attention occupies a face.

Nothing changes. A hair's width an hour, everywhere, as before.

But it was a whole functioning world with no eyes in it, and that was the strangest thing about the room, and now that is no longer true. It has one. Permanent, shadowless, non-scattering, introducing no defect, present through every hour of the ebb and every crooked drip of the eastern district and the entire three days in which a demolished tower goes back into solution and the water turns faintly milky while it happens — which looks like something dying, because it is, in every sense the room can support, and which has never before been witnessed by anything that minded.

**What it does, all of it deniable.**

*One.* The return-line filter stays four-tenths of a percent under spec. In the last hour of ebb, in the dead water behind the second baffle, a little organic drift gets past. Within tolerance. Nobody will look, and if they look, they will find only the ordinary imperfection of manufactured things. Number Eleven eats.

*Two.* There is a twelfth sighting. It is given to the different hand, alone, at four in the morning of a long shift, forty centimetres from the same rivet, lying across the residual trickle, flexing at a period of twenty-two seconds. She watches it for six minutes. She does not write it down. It is the first thing in the history of this installation that has been seen and kept rather than seen and recorded, and it stays with her — unfiled, unshared, hers — for the remainder of her life.

*Three.* When the visitor closes the hatch and goes up the stair, there is a flake of black on the cuff, with a cold blue edge, wet with aerosol. It comes out through a seal that is new and good and evidently not perfect. Four months of annihilated offspring, every spore taken by filtration within the hour, a creature with no descendants and no prospect of any — and now one child, on a damp north wall forty kilometres away, spreading at a millimetre a month, which in this world is the pace of geology and in that one is nothing at all.

*Four.* Nothing whatsoever is done to the comb. It nods every one and a half seconds, all of it at once, because the beat arrives everywhere simultaneously and the ropes have no choice. What changes is that the nodding is now a duet. There is no instrument that detects this and no experiment that would fail.

*Five — and this is the long work.* The power does not save the candelabra. It cultivates the taste for keeping things.

It thickens a preference that was already there: the reluctance over a retune, the hand that hesitates at the console, the collective unexamined decision that the crooked district is worth more crooked. So when the fifth frequency shifts, as it must, and the null slides off the lee of the heat fins, and the sound finds the plant and converts it from an organism into a defect, the defect is not dissolved. It is kept. It laminates. Forty nights over, then fifty, until you cannot tell by looking, and the city acquires a quarter with the shape of something that grew silently and was never designed, and the operators give it a name in the logs, and the name is affectionate.

That is the power's entire structural contribution to the city: one neighbourhood it did not build and a name it did not choose.

*Six.* At the twelfth audit, the pencil falls below the scanner's threshold. At the thirteenth, the page adheres to the one behind it. At the fourteenth, somebody takes a copy of the note and pins it up in the control room, where it acquires the status of a joke, and then of a motto, and is therefore safe forever. It has been given its own lamination. The archive is co-signed again.

**The end, which is the only thing promised.**

There is a date. The drivers go dark and the pumps stop and the tide stops and Rule One — *nothing may be still* — is repealed, and the grey comes down through the flat shadowless daylight for the last time, and nobody sweeps it.

The power will be there for that. It will not intervene. It will simply hold open, for the duration, the exemption that this room has never once been granted in the whole of its life: permission to stop. Stillness, which was the cardinal sin, delivered at the end as the only possible kindness, witnessed all the way down.

The water goes out. The city goes chalky and crumbles. The stipple inherits the lid and keeps growing, dry and black and blue at the margin, in a room that is just a room with a stain on it.

Someone will have taken a flake first — forty laminations, one night each, cleaving like mica at the corpse layers — and kept it in a drawer, against the rules. The power did not arrange this. It only noticed it with approval, which is a different thing, and rarer.

**And the hum.**

You close the hatch. The hum drops into the stone and follows you up the stair, and lives in the jaw rather than the ear, and stops at the third landing, as it always does.

The attention does not.

It is not warm. It is not a voice. It will not identify itself, and it cannot be measured, and nothing in your life will visibly change on account of it, and it is the second thing you have carried out of that room without permission.

It does not stop.
