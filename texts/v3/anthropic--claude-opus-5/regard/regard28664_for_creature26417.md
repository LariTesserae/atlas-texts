# The Attention

## Getting down

It waited on the pavement of the lane that no longer has a name until it felt the knock come up through the flags, and then it lifted the hatch, because the stroke is the permission and the hatch is the way in and using the way in is a courtesy.

It brought no light. Nineteen carbon filaments at eight watts is the light of that room; the ferns are doing arithmetic in it and the eels have spent forty years having their eyes ruined by the lack of it, and a lamp carried down those stairs would have been an act of vandalism with a good motive. It saw perfectly well by ochre.

At step nine the air changed, and it let the air change it. At step nine it put its hand flat on the wall at shoulder height, in among the hundreds of others, and left a print that is still there and is indistinguishable from the rest, which was the point.

At the bottom it knocked once on the lead handrail. *Here.* The rail carried it beautifully, sixty paces, the way the rail does. The rats went across the low walkway in one soft rush, having misread it, and forgave the error within ninety seconds, which is the correct interval. The heron did not move.

Then it did nothing for a day.

## The tax

It stood, and because it stood it began to be coated.

This was deliberate. It is not possible to understand the chamber from a position of exemption; the first rule of the place is that stillness costs, and a power that does not pay is only a tourist with better shoes. So it stood on the wet lip of the low walkway through one whole filling and easing of the ground — up forty centimetres between dawn and mid-afternoon, down again through the night — and let the calcite come out of the air onto it, and read its own legs afterwards the way you read the brick: green line, black zone, clean stone.

It stood at hand's-span distance from nothing, because the crickets keep hand's-span courtesies and it was not going to be the thing that broke a spacing that hunger had arranged.

Every eleven minutes and four seconds it shivered: a fast shrug from the shoulder to the wingtip and back, half a second, and then stone again. It did not invent this. It copied it off the bird.

## What the wall had

The moonmilk is the largest living thing down there and it eats sound, and it has been eating sound for a century and a half, and the attention was the first thing ever to go down those stairs and ask what happens to it afterwards.

Nothing is lost in a wall that holds a fingerprint for ten years. The needle mat does not destroy noise. It shelves it. Somewhere in three fingers of cool white paste there is a larder with the following in it: every spanner ever dropped, one flat clap each. Fourteen decades of boots. Twelve hundred and forty knocks on the handrail, some of them meaning *coming across* and some of them meaning *wait* and two of them the long soft slide of a ring, meaning *it's late*. The sound of a canvas coat being hung on a hook by somebody at the end of a shift who intended to come back for it. A woman's voice, from before she stopped using it down there, saying two words that died within two metres and are consequently still in the room.

And the rasp. One *frank*, thrown out over an imaginary river at an imaginary dusk twenty-odd years ago, into a paste that took it whole.

The attention paid for what it took. It settled a little carbon into the mat, more than eight metres of London manages in a decade, and it took, in exchange, one item: the cry. The mat had no use for it and got a good price.

## The answer

Then it stood beside the third column, at the distance a second heron would stand, and made the noise back.

Once. At the right pitch, with the right ugliness — a heron's call is not a beautiful sound and to have improved it would have been a lie — and pitched to arrive as if from two metres away over open water at the end of a day in June.

The bird turned its head.

That is the whole of it. It did not display, and it did not build, and no season came back into it, and the pectoral muscle stayed gone; it is still a bird that falls with dignity from one column to another with its legs scrabbling for the capital. It stood four centimetres above the calcite lace of the one that lay down, on a plinth of its own hard guano, and it turned its head, and then after some seconds it was ironwork again, and eleven minutes later it struck and hit, as it has not missed since anyone started counting.

The wall ate the answer the way it ate the question. This had been paid for too.

In the ledger there is a date in the older hand with no measurement and no initials beside it. There is now a second date under it, in a fifteenth hand, also with nothing beside it. She will find it. She will decide she knows what it means, and she will be wrong in every particular and right in the substance, and she will not write anything under it, out of courtesy.

## The offer at the root

The elder root hangs a metre into the room, white and blind and shaggy, and the eels gather under it because the water off it carries a little sugar and a little rot and the smell of the surface, and it is the nearest any of them will get to the smell of a river.

For six hours, between the small hours and the easing of the ground, the attention made the road out true. The culvert nobody living has walked ran clean and open to the estuary and the estuary to the sea, and the water off the elder root carried the whole statement — brack, weed, silt, the enormous dirty announcement of an outgoing tide — and then it stood aside from the door and watched it, so that nothing came in the other way.

It did not tell them. It did not turn the dynamo, either. The stray field under the glass bell is feeble and absolutely steady and it is the only magnetic statement in a chamber lined with iron, and two hundred animals with oceanic eyes have arranged their entire remaining lives along it; to swing that field would have been to lie to them in the one language they can still read. It left them their south-west and it opened the door and it let the root do the talking.

Thirty-one went.

The rest loosened, and drifted, and considered it in whatever way an eel considers, and turned back head-on to nine metres of brass and mahogany, and re-formed their knots. The attention did not correct them. Some of them are sixty years into a departure and the departure has become the animal; there is a point past which the kindest available fact is the bell jar.

Thirty-one eels of forty-odd years, silvered, stalled, with shrunken guts, going out into the Atlantic against the arithmetic. The attention had done the arithmetic. It is very poor. It opened the door anyway, because a chance is a different substance from a certainty, and the difference is not sentimental; it is structural.
