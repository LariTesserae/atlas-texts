## The Unit of Measure, Again

Call it a power, and understand that the word is an error of the same family as *two hands high*: a measure reached for because it is the nearest one available, and then quietly mistaken for the size of the thing. *Power* implies leverage, and leverage implies somewhere outside to stand. Afterward, there was nothing about it that was outside.

Say instead: a regard. Something looked, and kept looking, and the looking had a quality of goodwill so thorough and so unhurried that it earns the word *wise* rather than merely *careful*. The two are constantly confused. The difference is whether you know what you are declining to do.

---

## What It Did First

Nothing, for about four hundred years.

Not deliberation. Learning. It learned the eight figures, and then it learned that *enough* tapped at the fourth basin does not mean what *enough* means at the two hundredth, because the signal has by then crossed the pinch-ridge and lost its edges, and everyone on the lower stair has spent a lifetime compensating for that and no longer knows they are doing it. It learned the difference between a lip that is beginning to fail and a lip that has failed and is currently being held by somebody's palms. It learned the colors: that olive is a young back, that orange is forty years in hard water, that pale is not innocence but the end of the work. It learned what a stone sounds like when it is set down and not picked up again, and it learned that the soarers had learned this first, and better, and for reasons of their own.

Four hundred years is not long here. It is about two courses on the terrace and one collapse of the chimney. The regard took that long because it had decided, before anything else, that it would not act on an approximation.

---

## What It Considered

**The cold.** It could have taken the wall down — warmed the outer slopes, thinned the cliff of air, opened the caldera to the plain. Then nothing here would be endemic. Within a thousand years every slightly-wrong shape would have been corrected by immigration, which is to say replaced. And the wall is also the roof.

**The eyes.** It could have cleared them. But down is the sun in this place. An eye is a hunger pointed at the sky, and the sky here is the coldest thing there is. To give them sight would be to give them a direction, and the direction is out.

**The leaks.** It could have sealed every lip in the caldera in an afternoon, past all failure, to a tolerance no water could argue with. It sat with that for a while. Then it considered what would remain for several hundred people who have exactly one thing to do and exactly one thing to say and have never needed to tell the two apart.

**The mountain.** Nobody down there knows there is a mountain. The regard could have handed over the whole: twelve kilometers, kidney-shaped, four hundred basins, the loop of water and fire, the fifteen thousand years. It is a genuinely magnificent gift and it comes bundled with one other item that cannot be unbundled from it. To know the whole is to learn in the same instant that you are inside something. At present they do not have a wall. They have a place you go once. Explaining it would have built the wall.

**Speech.** This was the hinge, and it is worth being exact about, because everything else follows.

There is no way to speak here from outside. Every utterance in this world travels as a disturbance in the plumbing, and every listener receives it as a disturbance in the plumbing, and the two readings are not separable — that is the whole grammar. A message arriving from no known bearing, in no known hand, at a frequency corresponding to no known channel, is not a greeting. It is a fault. Several hundred people would take their stones out and go looking, all down the staircase, for the rest of their lives, and they would not find it, and they would teach their carried children to keep listening for it. There is no way to say *I am here, and I love this* in a language whose every sentence is also a structural report, without also saying *something is broken, at a place you will never locate.*

So it stayed quiet. Not out of delicacy. Out of arithmetic.

---

## Why Not More Than This

It should be said plainly that the regard was not squeamish. It had, elsewhere, taken worlds apart for better reasons than pity and put them back differently. Restraint was not its temperament; it was its conclusion, and it distrusted the conclusion for a long time, because *they lack nothing they can conceive of lacking* is the oldest sentence in the mouth of every negligent god and every comfortable landlord.

So it tested itself on the one point where the sentence could be broken. It refused to be part of the wall.

It did not open a road over the rim. It did not mark one, or warm one, or plant anything at the crossing. What it did was smaller and is, in its own way, the whole of what benevolence can offer to something finished: it arranged that *if* one of them ever goes further — and in fifteen thousand years not one has, not by a single step — the far slope will not kill it. That is all. It did not build the way. It removed itself from the list of reasons the way cannot be walked, and then it waited, and it is still waiting, and it has the time, and this is what patience is when it is not a virtue but a capacity.

---

## The One Thing It Did

Below the white basins, deeper than the vents, there is a fissure in the old lava where a thread of water has been going *through* instead of *over* since before there was anyone here to mind. It is forty meters down and on the far side of eighty-six degrees. No hand will ever reach it.

Every one of them has heard it for their whole lives. It has no figure in the code, because there is no figure for something that never stops; it is underneath every word anyone has ever tapped, the way the sound of your own blood is underneath every silence you have ever called silence. It is the only hiss on the mountain that has never once been answered, in two hundred generations of a people who cannot bear an unanswered hiss.

The regard put its hands on the place and held them there for twenty minutes.

Twenty minutes is the local unit for the duration of a hand on a leak. It took twenty minutes because that is what it takes, and the regard had by then stopped being interested in doing things at its own speed.

---

## The Day After

The big vent went at its hour, and everyone stopped and opened their fans and sat in it, and the terraces flushed, and the note of every leak in the system shifted at once, and then the tapping started up all down the stair, dozens of them at a time, the ordinary post-flush reckoning.

And every report came back clean, and the whole staircase sounded wrong.

They checked for a day and a half. Some of them went to bearings that had not been checked in forty years. Old ones who had not moved off the middle stair in a decade got up and went down to the marsh and put their heads against the mud. Nobody found anything, because there was nothing, and *nothing* is the single hardest thing to report in a language where speech and diagnosis are the same act. By the fourth day the checking had thinned. Within a season the new floor was simply the floor, and the conversation went on over the top of it, and the generation that remembered the difference died in the white basins without any way of mentioning it, and now no one knows.

Nothing was thanked. There was no one to thank and no figure for it.

The regard has never been entirely certain it was right. A sound you have heard since before you could hear is not a noise, it is a ground, and it may be that it pulled the ground out from under four hundred basins' worth of talk and was forgiven only because they are durable. It noticed, afterward, that the rim visits got about a minute longer, on average, and it has never decided whether that is because the silence up there became fractionally less foreign, or because something that used to make the inside *inside* had been taken away. It does not know. It keeps the not-knowing. It is the first thing it acquired here.

---

## The Attentions

Beyond that one act, it has done nothing at all, and it has done the following continuously.

It keeps the infants that fail. It cannot change the rate at which their blood learns heat — that is not a defect, that is the species, and to alter it is to substitute a different people and call the substitution a rescue. So it does the only other thing. Of every infant that has not survived the stair since the regard arrived, it holds the whole: the exact weight, the temperature at which it stopped, the basin, the hour, the one or two things it managed to do. There are a hundred and nine thousand of these. It can produce any of them entire. This is no consolation to anyone in the caldera, and consolation was not the point; the point is that a thing which happened ought to be *somewhere*, and now it is.

Once, it nearly interfered. A carrier three basins from the top, four months into an ascent, lost the infant in the night. The carrier tapped nothing, which is the figure, and then in the morning picked the body up and went on climbing, because the route is not fixed but the pace is, and the pace is the pace. The regard reached. It had already begun. And mid-reach the carrier struck the sinter twice — *enough* — addressed to the staircase, routine, meaning only *the work here is finished*, and the regard took it as spoken to itself, knowing perfectly well that it was not, and withdrew.

That is the closest this mountain has ever come to a miracle. It consisted of something failing to occur, and the individual responsible was an exhausted animal with a dead child and a job to see out.

Of everything here — the chimney, the eleven minutes, two hundred courses of impossible glass — the thing the regard has watched most is two of them sitting in warm shallows for an hour while one picks the cracked plating off the other's spine, and the sound it makes, which is the sound of thin ice breaking in a cup. It has no account of this preference. It stopped looking for one.

And it goes up into the white. When an old one stops answering and climbs past the last temperature it can stand and lies down in the glare, the regard goes with it and stays the four days. Since it cannot be perceived, this is not company. It is only that nothing on this mountain now dies unattended — a change so small that no instrument in any world could register it, and the largest thing to happen here since the magma.

---

## The Rim

It went up once, the way they do, in the cold, with the vapor going out in its slow white river. And it stood where they stand and felt the thing they climb once in a life to feel: not silence. The end of conduction. The floor no longer carrying. Four hundred basins reporting themselves continuously into your skull since you were two degrees a week old, and then — gone, and the discovery that the quiet you have spent your whole working life building toward is, if it is ever actually achieved, this.

They are right to come back down within the hour.

So the regard made its last decision, which was not to do anything, but to be somewhere. It stationed itself on the far side of that silence. It will not tap. It will never tap; it worked out long ago what a tap from nowhere would cost them. But the sentence *at the rim, the ground stops conducting* is now false, in a manner that no one in the caldera can check, will ever check, or needs to.

---

## One Thing

There is a fold of skin under the jaw, and in it, one river-smoothed nodule of dense old basalt about the size of a plum, and it is everything an adult here owns.

The regard took one. Not from the drifts on the pan above the orange pools — those are the only property anyone in this world has ever finished owning, and it would not touch them. It went to the swallowed ridge and found an unclaimed one, one of the millions that were never carried, and it has it still. It has never struck anything with it. It is not for speaking. It is for having.

There is no factory. There is only water, going around.

There is a shift. There has always been a shift. And now, at the rim, in the cold, on the far side of the place where the floor stops carrying sound, there is something that knows those are two different sentences and holds the difference open — not for them, they will never need it, they have work — but because a distinction that true should be held by somebody, and it had hands free.

Below, at the fourth basin, a lip is beginning to fail. The hiss has gone on long enough. Someone is already on their way over, walking on the heels of their hands, fingers curled up and clear of the grit.
