## The visit, which does not appear in the archive

It comes in on a tick, because that is the only door.

Twice a day the vault sounds its soft off-key note and every animal in the room stops for the duration, and in that suspension nothing is interrupted, nothing is startled, no line of behaviour is cut across. A power that understands the difference between arriving and intruding will wait however long it has to for that. It has waited longer for less.

It does not step on the floor. This is the first decision and the one from which the others follow. The flour remembers everything it is given, without weather to take it back; a single considerate footprint would still be a permanent alteration of a document eleven thousand years in the writing, and the alteration would say *something came here that was not part of this*. The power declines to be that sentence. It declines, in general, to be weather.

So it stands where the fallen slab has already been stood on by a goat, and it looks at the room for a while, in the milk-light, casting nothing.

## The six hours

Then it goes to the northeast corner and sits down with the light behind it, and does not move for six hours.

This is the whole of the investigation, and it is also, as it turns out, most of the benevolence.

In the fourth hour a parasol rat comes along the base of the wall with a stem in its forepaw held vertical — *I am passing, I want nothing* — and puts the stem down, which is a thing the observer has never been told they do, and sits fully upright on both hind feet, four toes and a heel pad set flat and deep, and takes up the core in both forepaws and turns it, and works.

It is not good at it. Its mother was better. There is one household in the room that can do this and roughly one individual in that household per generation who does it well, and they have been striking the same reduction sequence, off the same seam, in the same dim cool corner, for four thousand years, because a *good frond is cut rather than chewed, because a chewed stem frays and will not hold its angle*, and a frond that will not hold its angle cannot say anything. The knapping is not for killing. It is not for eating. It is for the maintenance of grammar. Every sentence spoken in this chamber for four millennia has depended on somebody in the northeast corner keeping an edge on the language.

Nobody walks there. The power understands why in about the ninth minute and sits with it for the remaining five hours and fifty-one, because the room is not the kind of place that answers questions, it is the kind of place that lets them sit, and it would be a discourtesy to come all this way and be in a hurry about it.

At the end of the six hours the power knows the answer and does not do anything with it. It does not correct the scatter. It does not gather the two unsoftened blades from the crack behind the succulent and set them beside the old ones so that the sequence would be legible to a visitor with a schedule. It leaves the evidence exactly as ambiguous as the room made it, which is to say it leaves the rats in possession of their own privacy, which no one had thought to ask whether they had.

## Three small movements

**The horn.** In a basement drawer under a site code and no other label there is a splinter of goat horn in a paper bag, and it has been there since 1974, and the woman clearing the cabinet is sixty-one and retiring in March and has, for the first time in her working life, an afternoon in which nothing whatsoever is scheduled. She puts the bag in her coat pocket without deciding to. She drives four hours. She is the fourth human being ever to come down the crawl and the first to come down it with no instrument and no intention of taking anything out.

She does not know where it was picked up, so she cannot put it back. She sets it on the lip of the crawl, where a foraging animal will find it, because the power prefers findings to gifts; a gift arrives from outside the world and has to be explained, and a finding is simply the world being slightly better stocked than you thought. Then she sits down in the northeast corner with the light behind her, because she has nowhere to be until Tuesday.

She never publishes. In the sixth hour she understands that the answer is not hers, and this understanding arrives feeling entirely like her own.

The splinter is back in circulation within a season. Nobody in the room experiences it as a restoration. It is simply that there are two horns again, as there were before, as there have been for forty years in every account that matters.

**The tree.** Thirty metres out and eight metres up there is a tamarisk that does not know the room exists, for which the room is a numbness in its foot, and it will die in some ordinary drought in some ordinary decade and the chandelier will go grey and the room will lose its clock and a third of its architecture. The power cannot make a tree immortal without making it something other than a tree. What it does instead is arrange, with a great deal of patience and almost no visible effort, that a seed from that tree lodges in a wet year in the same fissure its parent found, so that the succession is already twenty years underway when the old roots fail.

The new tree will also not know. Nothing above ground will ever register that anything has been done. This is the power's favourite kind of work and it does a great deal of it.

**The calendar.** That is the third movement, and it has already been described: one empty afternoon in one human life, placed where it would do the most good. The power's principal tool is not force. It is the occasional, well-timed absence of an appointment.

## The ledger of what it did not do

It did not prop the ceiling.

It did not widen the seep, though it could have doubled the pool in an afternoon and the halophiles would have had a century of plenty and the salt fret would have retreated and the pink would have thinned to nothing and the room's only visible season would have ended.

It did not propagate the fern. Sixty animals and four thousand years of language resting on one clonal organism the size of a dinner plate is not a stable arrangement, and it has been stable for four thousand years, and the power has seen what happens to a people when the scarce thing at the centre of their manners becomes abundant. The manners do not survive it. They are not manners about shade. They were never about shade.

It did not remove the centipede. Mercy applied at that level is just a differently-aimed appetite.

It did not fix the law. Two shifts a day, adjudicated by the heating and the cooling of a slab of alabaster, is an absurd constitution and a perfect one; there is shoving but no blood, and no one has ever had to be right. The power did not offer to be a better landlord. The offer would have been accepted, and that would have been the end of them.

It did not teach anything. It added no instrument the room had not already made. It did not tell them it was there.

And it did not prevent the afternoon of noise.

This is the one that would look, from outside, like a failure of benevolence, and it is the place where the wisdom is doing the heaviest work. The ceiling is thinning and one century it will thin past holding and the crickets will die within the hour of the first wind because there has never been wind and they have no idea what it is for. The power counted them. It knows what it is declining to save.

But it also sat for six hours in the corner where the language is kept sharp, and it understands what the rats are holding. They carry shade twenty-four hours a day in a room where shade is available for two. The gesture has been a fossil for four thousand years — a word whose object went missing so long ago that the word became the whole of the meaning. Out there, up the rubble slope, under a sky that is one enormous selenite pane with no floor to stop at, a frond held overhead will mean exactly and only what it means, for the first time in their history, and they will hold it, and they will hold it at complementary angles when they meet, and the young will do it wrong and be corrected.

To prop the ceiling is to keep them forever in the only room where their language cannot be true. The power will not do that to them. It is a catastrophe and it is also the completion, and a power wise enough to tell those apart is wise enough to know when they are the same event.

## What it accepted

In the fifth hour a young rat came into the northeast corner, which nobody does, carrying a frond badly — chewed stem, frayed, angle slipping — and stopped, and did not know what it was looking at, and after a while laid the frond flat across both forepaws and offered it at waist height.

*The under-dark I am sitting in is large enough for you.*

The power does not need shade. It has never needed anything. It came down to look and to put one small stolen thing back and to plant a seed thirty metres away that nothing will ever thank it for, and none of that required it to be spoken to.

It accepted. It sat in the patch, which was not a patch, being the diffuse hour, being no shade at all, being nothing but a place two bodies had agreed to be near each other in. It held its own angle, complementary, for as long as the young one stayed.

Then the vault ticked and everything stopped, and when everything resumed, the power was not there.

---

## The trace

The floor forgets nothing and has nothing to remember. No print, no disturbance, no specimen absent, nothing turned over and put back at the wrong angle. In the archive of that chamber the visit does not appear, and will not appear when the pan comes down, and would not appear if you sieved every grain.

There is only this. For the rest of its life that young rat, passing the northeast corner, canted its frond at a tilt that means nothing — not *passing*, not *eating*, not *there is room* — and its own young copied it, and did it wrong, and were corrected, and their young after them. It is now a word in that language with no referent, as small and as durable as a splinter of horn, and it is used correctly by animals who could not tell you what it is for.

They will take it up the slope with them, when the slope comes. They will hold it in the open desert under the whole sky, where every other word they have will finally be true, and that one will go on being the exception: the gesture kept sharp for four thousand years in the corner where such things are kept, meaning only that something once sat down and was willing to be hosted.

Which is, more or less, what the power came to do, and all it left behind, and quite enough.
