## The Attention

Begin with the fact that decides everything else: the power arrived already knowing it must not fix this.

It came the way weather comes to a place that has no weather — which is to say, it did not come, it was simply there, and had been, and the town went on braiding. It did not announce. Announcement is the first tool of the impatient. Instead it did what any wise thing does in a house it has just entered: it stood still for a long time and learned where the walls were.

It took the power a hundred years to learn to read the panes, not because this is difficult but because it wanted to learn them the way a child of the town learns them — cold on the crown of the head first, then the shiver in the sight of the far shelf, then the closing of the curtain behind. It learned the tapstones by listening to arguments, which is the only way anyone learns a language. It learned that two taps and a scrape means *come up*, and that a mother's *come up* and a foreman's *come up* are struck at different depths of the wrist, and that everyone can tell.

Then it began to consider what, if anything, to do.

---

## What it had done before, when it was younger

It had been here before. This is the shameful part and it comes early, because everything else follows from it.

Long ago, in a state of enthusiasm it now recognises as a kind of infancy, it had brought a fish. One breeding population of a blunt pale fish, carried in from a water that no longer connects to this water by any route at all. It had done this out of pure generosity, meaning: without asking what a gift becomes.

It has watched, since, four thousand people decline to eat that fish. There is no reason. The taboo has no story left in it; the priests do not defend it; the children ask and are told *we don't* and that is the whole of the doctrine. Every generation of the town has been a little hungrier than it needed to be because a power once loved them without thinking.

It brought the flies too. Same visit, same mood. The flies are lucky. Nobody swats them. The flies do nothing whatsoever.

So: **the first law of benevolence, learned the hard way.** Anything you hand down becomes holy, and holy things are expensive, and the bill is paid by people you will never meet.

---

## The five refusals

**Part the water.** It could. It considered it for a long time, honestly, without flinching, because a benevolent power that will not consider the big lever is only squeamish. And found: the forest dies in an afternoon. The lung-fruit die. The vent mats bleach. The grazers boil. Four thousand people with hollow, buoyant, low-load bones stand up in real gravity for the first time and their thighs break. And the survivors — say there are survivors, say it is done gently over decades — the survivors are, from that hour forward, a *rescued people*. Their history acquires a hinge that is not theirs. Every song about the breach becomes a song about the day someone else decided. Refused.

**Give them fire.** Some contrivance: a burning that works in water, a metal that needs no kiln. Refused, and the reason is the fish. A tool nobody can make is a tool nobody can mend; a tool nobody can mend is a relic; a relic is a shrine; a shrine is a thing men eventually kill each other over the tending of. It would give them, at best, three generations of wonder and then a war. Refused.

**Carry them out.** Set them down in some wide bright country of air and cities, where nobody drowns. Refused. In that country their obsidian is a curiosity, their tapstone grammar a folk performance for visitors, their four hundred years of argument about the weighting of the dead an anecdote. A people moved is a people politely concluded. Refused.

**Blunt the glass.** Take away the splinter that opens a hand to the bone — the one sharp thing in the world. And here the power found the deep architecture of the place, and sat with it a while: *every danger here is load-bearing.* The rind that maims is the only edge. The vents that scald are the warm stratum. The buoyancy that lifts the dead away from their families is the same buoyancy that lets an old woman with ruined knees walk the lanes like a girl. Remove any one hazard and you remove a floor. Refused, all of it, with respect.

**Answer the question.** This was the hard one, because the power *knows*. It knows what is above the Shine, and what is not, and it knows exactly what happens to a body that goes up past the drift-beach. It could put that knowledge into the town in a night.

And then: every family who has held a grandmother down under a cairn learns what they did. Or every family who cut the belt learns what they did. One half of the town receives, in a single evening, four hundred years of retroactive cruelty, itemised. There is no version of the answer that does not do this. The town's not-knowing is not an ignorance; it is a truce, and it was negotiated by people who could not speak, using their knuckles, over centuries, and it is the most sophisticated thing they own. *The town has never decided, and does not have to, and won't.*

The power kept it. It also declined to lie, or to nod, or to arrange any convenient sign. It simply held the answer and said nothing, which is a form of labour, and it is still doing it.

## What it did instead: the doctrine of occasions

Having refused to give, the power was left with the one method that does not poison what it touches. It does not act. It *occasions*. It arranges the small accident, and then it gets out of the way so fast that a person can have the credit, and mean it.

**The grit.** The mountain has not learned to make sand; there is nothing here that grinds anything else. And yet the interior of every pillow the town breaks is pumice — grey, bubbled, harsh — and it had been in their hands, and under their heels, for eleven generations.
