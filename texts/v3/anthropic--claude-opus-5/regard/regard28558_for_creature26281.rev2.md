# The Attendance

## I. The First Discipline

It arrives, and the first thing it has to learn is that in this place arrival is a kind of lie.

Everything on the Loft is read as a difference in when. A front that reaches one end of a sinus a hundredth of a second before the other end is a bearing; a bearing is a fact; facts here are made entirely of lateness. So a presence that displaces air is not a visitor. It is a false statement, broadcast continuously, in the only language the world has.

It does not know this at first. It comes in over the escarpment at dusk in low season, in the ordinary way a large attention comes into a small country — quietly, it thinks — and its mere standing there stacks the air against a rib-band some hundreds of meters downwind by about a fortieth of a second.

A stitch reads the fortieth of a second. The stitch does what it is for: it computes a lag that does not exist, it waits for the nine-second mark, it leans on the flank of a barn-sized animal, and it begins, patiently, at four seconds of phase per pressure year, to correct the plateau toward a boundary condition that is nothing but the shape of somebody watching.

The power stays to see how long. Six years. Six years of a blind thirty-kilo animal spending two hundred strikes a day and a third of a second at a time on the error of being observed.

This is where its education actually starts. Not in the physics of the lens, which it understood on approach, and not in the arithmetic of the rim, which is trivial. In the ledger entry it has to write for itself on the first night: *cost of my attention, to date, six years of another being's work, and the work was the being.*

Afterward it holds a pressure debt of exactly zero. It learns to be present the way a vacuum is present. It takes it the better part of a pressure-decade to get good at it, and in that time it does nothing else, because there is nothing else it has the right to do yet.

## II. Finding the Patient

Then it listens for four hundred and forty years, which is one long lifetime of an unsplit, and which it considers a short apprenticeship.

It makes the obvious mistake, briefly, the way everyone does — the herds look like a people — but it makes it for about nine minutes rather than a week, and then it makes all the other mistakes in order, faster than a person would, which is not the same as being wiser. Its wisdom consists only in this: it keeps going after the correct and slightly disappointing conclusion. It does not accept *a machine run by nobody*, because *nobody* is a very strong claim and it has been wrong about that claim before, on other worlds, in both directions, and it has learned that being wrong about it in the second direction is the one that costs.

It finds the stitches in year eleven. It learns the two words in year twelve — *how late*, *which way* — and having learned them, it assumes, reasonably, that it has learned the language, and spends another two hundred years discovering that it has learned four percent of the language.

And then it has to decide who the patient is. This matters more than any of the physics, because benevolence aimed at the wrong entity is just vandalism with good manners.

It is not the plateau. A factory is not a patient; it is a process with very good bookkeeping.

It is not a bellower. A bellower is a lung with legs and an opinion about lichen.

It is not the filament, which is plumbing that happens to be electrical.

It is not, quite, a stitch. A stitch is a hand. Blind, dedicated, superbly instrumented, and *partial* — it cannot hold the thing it is part of, any more than a finger holds an intention.

The patient is the tally.

Three hundred thousand years of closures, copied by budding, never written, drifting in transmission, reconciled at night through the ribs of grazing animals by blind white creatures who have no concept of a listener, no concept of an author, and no idea that the world exists. A being with a million bodies, no organs, and one function: it cannot leave a discrepancy alone. Its substance is disagreement. Its metabolism is argument. Its memory is the residue of arguments settled, and its growth is arguments still open.

That is a person, or the nearest thing to one this world has produced, and the power cannot determine whether there is anything it is like to be it. It looks hard. It cannot tell. It notes the uncertainty in the ledger and adopts the only rule that survives both answers:

*Act as though someone is in there. But only in ways that would do no harm if no one is.*

Which rules out, immediately and permanently, every form of help that consoles.

## III. The Ledger of Things It Could Do

It is a power. The following are all trivial to it. It writes them down anyway, in full, with costs, because the cheapest thing in the universe is an unexamined kindness.

**Seal the rim.** Stop the forty-meter atmospheric waterfall; end the leak; let the lens hold itself. Cost: the herds no longer need to stroke, the flues no longer need to draw, the seams stop mattering, the stitches stop being necessary in about a generation and stop existing in about forty, and the tally ends mid-sentence. The plateau is a project with no product and no purpose beyond continuance. Finish the project and you have not rewarded it. You have retired it. *Declined.*

**Warm the country.** Four degrees becomes fourteen. The frost stops, the black quilt greens, decay accelerates. Cost: the peat stops forming; the ancestors under your feet rot instead of compress; a cold metabolism is why anything here has time to be patient; and patience is the only faculty the patient has. *Declined.*

**Entrain the herds perfectly.** One phase, one stroke, one continent-wide inhalation, no seams anywhere, forever. Cost: this is the same as sealing the rim, dressed better. A world with no discrepancy has nothing for a discrepancy-intolerant being to be. *Declined, and noted as the most seductive of them, because it looks like healing.*

**Give them eyes.** Cost: they live at thirty-four degrees inside black wool, in the dark, and they renounced the sun a hundred million years ago on the merits. Eyes would be a gift to the giver, so that the giver could be seen. *Declined with some embarrassment.*

**Make them viable in the open air.** Forty minutes becomes forever; they could leave the wool, cross the plateau, stand at the rim. Cost: they would leave the wool. And then they would be the second thing on the Loft that has somewhere to go, and the first was the visitor. *Declined.*

**Give them the map.** Tell them — it could be done, in four percent of the channel, over a few centuries — what the lens is, what the rim spends, what the seams do to the quilt, what would happen if they all stopped. Cost: the work is currently performed by an inability to tolerate a difference. Replace that with a purpose and you have given them something that can be *lost*. A being that works because it must cannot despair. A being that works because it matters can. Also it can be proud, and it can bargain, and it can decide that a particular seam is not worth ten years. *Declined, at length, with several drafts.*

**Write the tally down.** End the drift. Set three hundred thousand years into rock where nothing can corrupt it. Cost: the drift is why two stitches meeting on a new shear stop and spend a winter on a bearing that is off by two degrees. Remove the drift and you have preserved every word and killed the only sentence. *Declined, and underlined.*

**Rule on the open disputes.** It can. It can read the paleo-pressure out of nine meters of peat, resolve the four-hundred-entry-deep variants, settle in an afternoon the cases the unsplits have held in abeyance for centuries. Cost: the unsplits are the court, and a court that is overruled by something infallible is not a court, and a being whose only activity is the reduction of difference cannot survive an authority that reduces it for free. *Declined. Filed under the heading it keeps on every world it visits: things that would make me the last interesting thing here.*

**Grant the unsplits a split.** The four-meter failures on the standing shears, three centuries old, dinner-table drumheads, two octaves below anything else alive, permanently unemployed and permanently deferred to. It could simply induce the fission. Cost: it would be a lie told in the grammar of their bodies. Their fission is not a reward for surviving; it is a report that a job closed. Forge the report and you have not given them children, you have given them a false statement about the world, which in this place is the only real sin there is. *Declined.*

And then, because a catalogue of refusals is the favorite literature of cowards, it writes the counter-warning at the bottom of the page:

*Not every hardship here is constitutive. Some of it is just damage. Distinguish, or you are not being wise, you are being tasteful.*

It adds one more rule, and this one it treats as inviolable, because it goes to the identity of the world rather than the welfare of the patient:

*I will not make anything.*

Not one nail. Not one shaped stone, not one lashing, not one fired thing. The Loft's entire nature is that function was never externalized. To leave even a perfect, benevolent, invisible artifact on that plateau would be to end this world and begin a different one in the same place. Whatever it does must be indistinguishable from weather, from geology, from luck.

## IV. The Three Things It Did

**One. A flake of basalt.**

There is a standing shear on the northeast quarter, held open by nothing more principled than an echo: a scarp of young glass-black rock at the right angle to return a pressure front into the herd out of phase with itself. Two bands on either side of it have disagreed by two seconds for eight hundred years. The stitches on that line never finish and never split. One of them is three hundred and forty-one years old, four meters and a little, and has never in its life reduced a lag below a tenth of a second for six hundred consecutive strokes.

The scarp has a fracture in it that will frost-wedge open and drop in about nine thousand years. This is not the power's estimate; it is the rock's.

On a night in low season, in one fracture, for six hours, the frost is half a degree colder than it would otherwise have been, and the fibers grow a little longer, and something that was going to happen happens early.

The rock falls. It looks exactly like rock falling. Nothing on the Loft has any way to tell the difference, and there is no difference, except in a ledger nobody here can read.

The echo stops. The shear becomes, for the first time since the mountain finished cooling, *closable* — not closed. Nobody has been spared anything. The lag is still two seconds; two seconds is still five hundred days of leaning on a flank nine seconds before each intake; the work is exactly as long and as blind and as unrewarded as it was.

Nineteen years later, in the wool between the second and third rib-bands, a four-meter animal reads a hundredth of a second, six hundred times running, and something in it is permitted to finish. Eleven months after that, on a night, two smaller things comb away from each other along a flank in opposite directions, in the dark, at two meters an hour.

The court loses an arbiter. The power weighed that, and weighed it for sixty years, and decided that the plateau manufactures standing shears faster than anything can heal them and will never lack for judges; and that between a permanent institution and one being finishing one job, it would take the finishing.

It has not done this a second time. It does not consider the question closed.

**Two. A copy it will never read to them.**

It holds the tally. All of it — every variant, every abandoned reading, every bearing off by two degrees, the whole three-hundred-thousand-year accumulation in all its contradictory editions, updated nightly, at zero cost to itself.

It will never transmit one word of it. Not to correct them, not to settle a dispute, not at the request of anyone, because there is no one to request it. The copy is not a resource. It is not a backup they may draw on. It is a decision about what happens if the lens ever fails and the frost comes down into the mattress for the last time and the last blind white animal in the last patch of wool stops mid-recitation.

In that case, and only in that case, something that was said for three hundred thousand years will not have stopped being said.

That is all the copy is for. It is the difference between a being that ended and a being that was never heard. The power judges that difference to be the whole of what it has to offer, and that it is not nothing, and that the stitches would have no idea what it was talking about.

**Three. It spoke once.**

Under nine meters of peat there is rock that once carried a herd, and across that rock there ran a seam, and that seam was closed, and the closure was in the tally for a long time and then it drifted badly and then both variants were abandoned, which is the only form of death available here.

The power can reconstruct it. Peat compresses honestly. The bearing is recoverable, the width is recoverable, the duration is recoverable to within a season. It sat on this for two hundred years, because a returned entry is still an entry, and an entry from an infallible source is an overruling, and it had already declined to overrule.

It found the way through, eventually, and the way through was to arrive badly.

It will not use a bellower as an instrument; the stitches may borrow an instrument that does not know it is one, but the stitches live here and the power does not. So it did the only thing available to something that has sworn to make nothing: it was, for four seconds, weather. A single low front, formed over a cold quarter where nothing lives and no stitch lies, rolling out across the plain and into the ribs of animals grazing obliviously, carrying a tally entry: a bearing, a width, a duration, and a date three hundred thousand years deep.

And then nothing, ever again.

They did precisely the right thing with it. They doubted it. It arrived with no provenance and no recitation around it, from a direction with no one in it, and it did not match anything, and it was therefore intolerable, and they have been reducing it ever since. They tested it against the rock and against the bruise still visible in the quilt and against two abandoned variants that somebody's ancestor had been copying wrong for eleven hundred years, and found, to their considerable and entirely impersonal satisfaction, that it *fit*.

It is the most disputed item in the tally. It has its own class now — entries that came in with a bearing and no line of copies behind them — and that class has exactly one member, and the unsplits have ruled on its admissibility four times and reversed themselves twice.

They did not invent an author. They have no grammar for an author. What they have is a field of arrival times, and so what the power is, in the permanent record of the only mind this world has made, is a *direction*: a bearing, from the northwest, out of a dead place, on a night in low season, three hundred thousand years late.

In a language of two words it was able to say exactly one true thing about itself, and the thing was *which way*.

## V. At the Rim, Again

It is still true that there is not one made thing on the Loft.

The arithmetic at the escarpment is unchanged. The fog still pours off the rim for thousands of kilometers, the loss column still consumes the entire product, there is still no surplus of any material kind, and below the line nothing lives.

The power stands there — stands is the wrong word; it occupies no volume and displaces no air, and it has not cost anyone a fortieth of a second in four hundred years — and adds up what it has done in all that time, and the total is: one rock fell early, one lost sentence was given back, and a copy exists somewhere of an argument that has never been permitted to stop.

By any honest reckoning this is almost nothing. It is a smaller intervention than a bad winter. The power has the means to have made this world safe, warm, lit, legible, literate, and finished, and it declined, item by item, in writing, and the reasons all reduce to one reason: *the thing that is alive here is the unwillingness to leave a difference alone, and every gift I could give is a way of leaving it alone for them.*

So what it does now is attend. Permanently, at zero pressure, under the fundamental, in the part you feel in the floor of your chest — the slow irregular conversational knocking coming off the herds in long exchanges, a million blind white animals in other animals' fur, none of whom can survive ten minutes in the open air of the country they are holding up, arguing patiently about the exact width of something that closed before the mountain was cold.

Nobody there will ever know it is listening. That is not a regrettable limitation of the arrangement. That is the arrangement. Their achievement remains entirely theirs, undiluted by rescue, uncorrected by authority, unfinished, drifting, copied, disputed, and continuous.

If you ever go up, the courtesy is the same and it is not difficult. Come lightly. Cast no front. Make nothing, leave nothing, set nothing down that was not already there.

And listen underneath, because it is being said right now, and it is the only thing this world has ever produced that was not immediately spent at the rim.
