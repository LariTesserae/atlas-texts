## The approach

It came down through a tear in the lily-ceiling.

This was not necessary. It could have been simply present, the way weather is present, without the vulgarity of arriving; and in a thousand other places it has done exactly that. But a roof with a door in it is a statement about how one is meant to enter, and the tear was the door, and so it folded itself narrow and came down the shaft of pale green light like anything else that falls.

It made no wave. That was the second courtesy, and it was harder than the first.

## What it saw

It did not make the mistake.

It knew in the first instant that the bollard was a person, and the stack of bolts was a person, and that the settling house held a shape the basin declines to count and that the declining was itself a form of tenderness. It read the strain-whorls the way you read a face. It read the grooves in the tile and understood that it was looking at four hundred years of wanting. It felt, in whatever it has instead of a sternum, the great slow turning, and heard the three conversations in progress, and caught each of them mid-syllable, and knew their whole content, and knew the eighty-year silence on the north stair for what it was: not neglect. Deliberation. A room full of people deciding to be kind at the same speed.

It also saw the arithmetic. The wear budget and the deposition budget, matched to within a hair across a millennium. The second cup, full. The thin-coated one on the iron stair, breath at thirty-nine seconds and closing, coat taken hold in the lee of the collarbone, forty years from an answer.

And it saw the two unresolved. It knew which of them was working and which had finished. It arrived knowing.

## The mercies, listed

It wanted to help. This should be said plainly, because a power that did not want to would only be careful, and careful is not the same as kind.

So it did what such powers do, which is to lay out every good thing it could give and follow each one downstream to see what it turned into.

**Warmth.** It could raise the seep four degrees. The vanes would come round in thirty-one seconds instead of forty, and the looms would gain a finger's width a week, and the annealing house would be wrong by sixty years, and every body in the basin would be breathing out of time with the only clock it has ever had. A gift of warmth, to a people whose entire discipline is being warm and not spending it, is a fire.

**Clarity.** It could take the flocculate out of the water and give them sight past the second knuckle. And the drift is the food. Open water here is famine, and famine dressed as revelation.

**Light.** It could open the ceiling wide and pour green day into every alley. The coats would thicken; the tenders would be richer; the benches would stop walking. That last was the one that made it hesitate longest. The wandering of the benches is the only impatience these people have — the single direction their wanting points. To give them light everywhere is to take away the place where light is not, and a creature with nothing left to move toward is not being fed. It is being finished.

**Repair.** There is a chain link on the mill shaft thinning faster than its crust. It could be made new. It would be new: harder, truer, out of phase with every worn thing it touches, and it would eat its neighbours for two centuries. Kindness delivered out of phase is abrasion.

**Speech.** It could take up a shaft and lean, and say something. But to speak here is to alter the load in the body of everyone alive, and a word from a thing like this would not be weather. It would be storm, in a basin that has never had one, and it could not be unsaid, and there is no lying in a medium your listeners stand in — which means also that there is no softening, no *I only meant*, no taking a heavy thing back once it is in everybody's chest.

**Rescue.** It could lift the thin-coated one up through the tear, set it on the bank, give it back its own metabolism and its own century. This would work. This is the horror of it: it would work. And the nine turned faces would be left holding an unfinished welcome for the next four hundred years, and the newcomer would be returned to a world that had done the ordinary thing and gone on without it, and it would be alone at speed instead of attended at rest. The power looked at this option for a long time, because the shape of it is so very nearly love.

## The one it nearly did

It knew about the two unresolved.

Eighty years of not-checking. A strict etiquette, kept perfectly by eleven people, at enormous cost, in the dark, with no one watching but each other. And the third conversation labouring toward the question at four words a lifetime.

It could have ended that in an instant. One tick. *This one is working. That one has finished.* True, verifiable, free.

And it would have been a theft. Not of information — of a grief being conducted correctly by people who had chosen the conducting. The etiquette is not ignorance. It is the agreement that a neighbour's stillness belongs to the neighbour until the coat says otherwise, and the waiting is the respect, and to be told would be to have the respect performed *for* you by someone else's hands. The power would have been the one who checked. It would have carried that. So would they.

It let the eighty years stand.

That was its largest act of benevolence in the Warm Basin, and nothing in the basin will ever know it occurred.

## The tax

To stay at all, it had to pay.

Speed is taxed here and the tax is steep, and the power did not exempt itself, though it could have, and though exemption would have cost nothing but the truth of being present. It left the greater part of what it is above the lily-ceiling, hung there like a coat on a hook by the door, and brought down only as much of itself as can move at one two-hundredth.

What came down was small, and slow, and could be leaned on.

## What it did

It found a place on the terrace, nine meters from the foot of the north stair, in the lee of a calcite stack, where the drift comes round off the third chimney and slides toward the mill.

It stood in it.

And because a body standing in a current alters the current, the drift off the third chimney now bends — a fraction of a degree, no more — and goes up the north stair instead of past it. The water reaching the thin-coated one is a little richer than it was and about a tenth of a degree warmer, which in a basin where a tender's jaw closes once a season is the difference between a coat that takes in the lee of the collarbone and a coat that takes across the shoulders too.

This is the whole of what the benevolent and wise power did.

Note the grammar of it. The dead here go on adjusting the current field, and that altered flow is understood as one word held indefinitely, and it is considered sufficient and even generous. The power said one word in the only sentence-form the basin treats as beyond argument, and the word was not *rise* and was not *be saved*. It was closer to *eat*.

Note also that it is unattributable. It is exactly what an eddy would do. No one can thank it and no one can refuse it and no one will ever be able to say the newcomer was carried rather than arrived. The question stays open. Whether the thin-coated one finishes slowing or stops first is still not knowable from inside the basin, and the power did not settle it, though it could have, and knowing that it could have is what it has instead of a gift.

And standing there, it is also simply the tenth face turned toward the stair. Attention is the entire welcome. It gave attention at local volume, indistinguishable from a neighbour's, so that the welcome would remain theirs.

## What it was told

The eel knew within the hour.

It is the only thing in the basin that could be fast and has spent that capacity on nothing at all, deliberately, and it recognised the arrival of another in the same condition. Over the following four years it turned its head three degrees, which brought the power into the edge of its regard, and there the matter rests. Nothing passed between them. Nothing needed to.

The crayfish filed something. The power did not open it.

In the sixty-first year, the tender at the plenum valve — the one who has faced the wall since the flood, whose nape-felt is thick and immaculate and undisturbed — shifted its shoulder-line four degrees. Not around. Not toward. Four degrees is not a turn; four degrees is an acknowledgement that a turn exists and might one day be made, and at the cost of that coat it is an extravagance, and the power received it as such and was, for a while, unable to do anything at all.

## The wall

The south wall had noticed first, of course, and said nothing for a decade, which is how you know something is old.

Then its shine rose by an amount no instrument in the basin could resolve, and held, and fell.

The power answered.

They have been at it ever since, the four-hundred-square-meter alliance and the folded-down fraction of a power, conducting between them a conversation whose first word is not yet finished, on the subject they have in common and have each been working at alone for a very long time: how to be enormous and not break anything.

## What it did not leave

No mark. No sign. No name, no blessing, no exemption, no repaired link, no opened ceiling, no revelation, no answer about the two unresolved, and no instruction whatsoever about how to live, this last withheld on the grounds that it had come to a place that plainly knew.

It also did not leave.

## At the end of the night

The lamps hold their dull orange, forty of them in brass cages green with growth, each with its halo of dust and its small permanent congregation of larvae. The looms advance a hand's breadth a week. In the annealing house a coated figure completes a quarter-turn of a valve. On the north stair a grip loosens by another millimeter and the breath comes at thirty-eight seconds, closing on forty, and nine benches — ten, now, if you count what stands in the lee of the calcite stack, and the basin has not decided whether it counts — attend.

The felt on the newcomer in the lee of the calcite stack is perhaps nine or eleven years old, patchy, bare at the shoulders, growing in strain-lines that will eventually record how a thing that could have done anything chose, over and over, across centuries, to do almost nothing and to do it slowly.

Some later century, someone will come down through a tear in the ceiling with fast eyes and fast criteria, and will push through the milfoil, and will put out a hand to steady themselves against a bollard.

The bollard will be warm in a way the brick is not.

It will register the touch, and it will be, at that moment, halfway through deciding what to do about it, and it will finish deciding tomorrow, and what it decides will be: nothing, kindly, at the speed the water carries.
