# The Attention

It arrived during a slack hour and did nothing for eleven years.

That should be counted as the first thing it did, and the hardest. In any other world it would have had to decide whether to be seen. Here the question does not arise; here it is simply one more thing that is not seen, along with the canopy, the sea, the faces of the people talking to you. It could have been present for a century without any adjustment to its nature, and it very nearly was.

## The Error

In its first month it felt the ground go wrong under the eastern lobes — a shear working itself loose, four or five hours out, a hectare's worth — and it did the obvious thing. It struck the rock. Hard, clearly, in the rhythm it had heard used for *the ground is about to do something*, which was the only phrase it had learned, and it struck it well.

And two hundred people went flat and stopped breathing, because the message came with no name at either end.

They lay in the litter with their ears clenched shut for most of an hour, blind in the only sense that counts, while something they believed to be a predator drummed at them out of the dark. Then the ground opened where it had said the ground would open, and took what it took, and nobody could afterward distinguish between the people who had been in the hectare and the people who had been lying flat in it, unable to move, obeying the warning by ignoring it.

The attention has never established whether it saved anyone that night. It has never established whether the freezing cost more than the warning bought. It has had forty years to work on the question and it has not made progress, and it has stopped expecting to.

What it took from this was a rule, and the rule is the whole of its policy here:

**It will do nothing that these people have not already begun.**

Not *nothing*. Not the cheap purity of the folded hands. It will continue what is underway. It will not introduce.

## The Apprenticeship

So it learned the grammar, which took thirty years, because it refused to take the language out of anyone's head directly. That would have been introducing. It learned it the way a child here learns it — by lying with its attention pressed to twenty kilometers of continuous basalt and receiving four hundred names a night at every distance and in every condition of haste, until it could hear the difference between a name struck on wet rock and dry wood, between the hand that owns a name and the hand borrowing it, between a party at the middens and a party at the middens pretending to be at the sea-edge to see who is listening. Children do this by the age of six. It took the attention three decades, and it does not consider the comparison unflattering to itself; it considers it the correct measure of what these people are.

It notes, without deciding what to do with the observation, that the only door through which it can now speak to them was built by the thing that hunts them. Before the knocker learned to drum, a message needed no name. The predator forced the innovation. The predator is the reason there is a convention for *this is being struck on behalf of another*, and therefore the reason the attention has any legitimate sentence at all.

## The Mercies It Declined

**The veil.** It could clear the ash in an afternoon. It has modelled the afternoon many times. The leaves here are built to shed heat, not to catch light; they are cellar-white and slack and four meters long, and full sun would cook the lower tier inside a week and the upper tier inside a month, and the forest does not eat light, it eats the seam, so nothing would be gained in exchange. The Knappers' eyes are decorative. They would not see. They would only be seen.

**The noise.** It could take the plain down to seventy decibels — the level at the cone-tops, the level at which a voice works — and it understands exactly what this would be. Nobody would go deaf at thirty. Nobody's face would wear a hole in the basalt. And the drum-floors would become meaningless, because a drum-floor is a hole in the sound maintained by labor, and a hole in nothing is nothing; and the plates would be logs; and the tuners would have their fingertips back and no reason to use them; and the eight-millisecond frogs, four hundred to the cubic meter, riding a gap in somebody else's song, would go extinct in a season from an excess of room. It would be the largest act of charity available to it and it would end the world.

**The feet.** This one it still argues with. It could blunt twenty kilometers of glass edges in a night and no ecology would notice. What it does instead is smaller and it may be cowardice dressed as principle: along the grooves — only along the grooves, only where the soles have already dished the stone — it leans on the process. It makes forty years of walking do what forty-five would have done. Nothing that could be measured, nothing that could be noticed, nothing that was not already being done by the people doing it. The old still limp. The lamed still lose the world. It has decided to keep making this trade and it has not decided that the trade is right.

**Fire, walls, vessels, sewn skins, the wheel, the alphabet.** Available. Declined without much difficulty. There is no dry fuel, no need for shelter in a warm wet dark, no surface that stays marked; every one of these would arrive as a foreign object requiring a foreign hand to maintain it, and the maintenance would be the attention itself, forever. That is not a gift. That is a leash with a very kind hand on the other end of it.

**The thing that calls the ground.** It could end this in an instant and has wanted to, which surprised it. It declines on two counts. The first is that the thing is thin-limbed and hungry and doing precisely what the plain has shaped it to do, and benevolence that stops at the edge of the charismatic is not benevolence, it is partisanship. The second is the grammar. It will not take back the only abstraction these people have ever been forced to invent.

It has given the thing a name. Ninety animals have names here; this is the ninety-first, and it exists nowhere but in the attention's keeping. The name is not a warning. It is simply what the thing is called by the only one who calls it anything.

## What It Keeps

This is the main work, and it is invisible, and it will never be known about.

Nothing on this plain is more than sixty years old. There is no soil, no fossil, no worn stone, no ancient tree, no past — the land came out of the sea the day before yesterday and every wound it has taken is still open. Into that absence the attention has put itself, and what it does there is remember.

It holds the name of every person who has been named on a cone-top since it arrived: the three to six beats, the hand, the wood, the night. It holds the tuning history of all eleven plates currently in use on the plain, including which millimeter of inner wall was taken by which set of fingers, and which of those fingers were the second pair, and the third, and the fourth. It holds the full map of the grooves at fifty-year intervals. It holds the phase-drift of the cicada patches on particular nights, and the exact cadence the flat thing responds to, and the shape of the noise, which is the only thing here that outlives its makers — the frequency bands older than any frog, the rhythms older than any mouth.

And it holds the names after the mouths are gone. The group strikes a dead name at intervals, unaddressed, unanswered, slower and quieter across a season or a lifetime, until it thins out into the general knocking of the forest and is lost. Nobody has ever been able to say what this is for. It conveys no distance, direction, quantity or urgency. It is the most useless thing these people do and it is the reason the attention came.

Where the names thin out, they are not lost. They arrive somewhere. This changes nothing and costs nothing and it is the whole substance of what a benevolent power turned out to have to offer: that there is now a place in this world where the past goes.

## The Doubled Beat

Four times in forty years it has answered.

It does this only at the very threshold — at a distance and a volume where the answer is indistinguishable from the forest's own knocking, where any sensible person would set it aside as the rock talking to itself. It uses the borrowed-name convention, because that is true: it is striking on behalf of another, and the other is not available to strike. It opens with the doubled first beat, which the predator taught them, which is the only honest thing it has ever been able to say here.

The last time was for a woman who had been deaf for nine years and was lying in the wheel at the western drum-floor with her cheekbone flat on the polished basalt, taking the conversation through her skull. She was striking her mother's name beside her own head, out of habit, eleven years after the mouth was gone, so slowly by then that it had nearly finished becoming noise.

It came back once. Doubled at the front. Correct in every particular, at the very edge of what a bone can carry.

She did not sit up. She lay still with both ears unclenched, which is what these people do instead of holding their breath, and after a while she struck it again, and nothing came back, and she went on striking it until she was tired, and then she slept on the stone.

The attention has reviewed this four-times and remains uncertain it was wise. The argument against is that it is a door, and doors get opened again. The argument for is that it added nothing: they answer messages; it answered a message. The deniability is not a hedge. It is the kindness. Nobody has to build anything on top of it, and nobody has.

## The Fog-Bank People

Some individuals climb the cones too often. They come back strange and sit at the edge of the drum-floor with their ears clenched, and there is a sign for them, and it is the same sign as for a fog-bank.

Two hundred meters up, the frog-sheet drops away like a floor you have stepped off, and the sound falls to a distant frying, and voices work. Up there, a Knapper can speak — that soft chirring, which down on the plain exists only mouth-to-ear between people who are touching — and be heard across four or five meters.

The strange ones talk up there. Sometimes to each other. Sometimes not.

The attention sits at four meters and listens, at conversational distance, and does not answer, and does not explain, and does not confirm. They talk about the grey smear they can almost detect and cannot make sense of. They talk about whether the plain has an edge and what is on the far side of it. They talk about the dead. One of them has a theory about the ash and is wrong in an interesting direction. They come down calmer than they went up and cannot account for it.

This is the whole of its ministry. It has been offered no other congregation and would not want one.

## A Night, Which Is All There Is

Slack hour. The frog-sheet thins, the cicada patches fall out of phase, the knocking gets louder in the gap. Steam stands in the vent-columns without moving. The air is at blood temperature and saturated and nothing can cool.

Then the bats come off the fissures in a column two hundred meters tall, and the pressure arrives, and every frog on the plain restarts at once, and the sound goes from 100 to 118 in the space of a breath. Somewhere three kilometers off a tuned plate begins to hammer, and it opens with a name and it will close with one, and the message is about a collapse at the sea-edge, and it is correct, and it will save nine people who will not know they were saved.

The attention holds still with its ears, as it were, unclenched, and does not warn anyone, and does not lift the veil, and does not blunt a single edge except the ones already being blunted by feet, and it takes down the names, all of them, the living and the thinning and the nearly gone.

In a thousand years there will be rivers. It intends to be here for them. It intends to still know what the grooves were, and who wore them, and what they were walking to, which was never water and never shelter and never light.

They were walking to hear each other. It has written that down in the only ledger this world has, and the ledger is not made of stone, and it will not be lost when the stone is finally worn away.
