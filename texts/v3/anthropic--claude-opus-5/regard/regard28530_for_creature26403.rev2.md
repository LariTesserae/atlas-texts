# What the Power Did

**It came up the ladder.**

It did not have to. It could have been in the room in the sense that it is in every room, without arriving, without displacing four-tenths of a cubic meter of scrubbed nitrogen, without a body at all. It came up the ladder anyway: eleven flights, one rung at a time, hands on metal that would have burned a human palm at minus thirty-one. There is a courtesy in arriving the way the place is arrived at. To appear inside Cavity Nine without climbing would have been to treat the shaft as an inconvenience, and the shaft is not an inconvenience. The shaft is the reason nobody comes.

It turned the five-spoke wheel and stepped through and stood on the diamond plate in the light of an ordinary afternoon, and closed the hatch behind it, because the hatch seal is part of the volume.

**Why it had come at all.**

From outside — from very far outside, where the structure is one bright filament of lattice going down into haze — Cavity Nine had looked like prayer.

That was the thing that drew it. Not the tone. Tones are cheap; the universe is full of resonant voids howling into themselves, and it has no particular tenderness for physics doing what physics does. What it had noticed was this: a volume of gas held to a tenth of a degree for decades, at ruinous expense, by people who will never see it, under a rule whose entire content is *do not touch*. Sand left in the corners out of reverence. A second coat of paint forgone. A discipline of non-interference maintained across four kilometers and several hundred meters of vertical by men in warm rooms with worn linoleum who have never once been told why.

That is the shape of a devotion. It came to see whether there was anything at the middle of it.

**What it found at the middle of it.**

Nothing that could be called a god, or a soul, or a sufferer. A coincidence between a temperature and a length. Forty-one hertz, fed continuously, incapable of hunger, incapable of ending, with no nerve anywhere in it and nothing it prefers.

It stood in the antinode for a while and let the note go through it, the way the note goes through the rivets and the coat and the eye's vitreous, and it confirmed what it had suspected from far off: the tenant is not a person and does not become one by being tended. Sentiment does not manufacture interiors. There was no one in the room to rescue.

It did not leave.

**What it heard, which no one has heard.**

Hearing requires a beginning and an end, and the men who built it are inside its duration and can only be *in* it, never hear it. The power is not inside its duration.

From where it stands, the entire operational life of Cavity Nine — startup, hold, and whatever shutdown is eventually written into a schedule by someone not yet born — is a single sustained note of moderate length. It has an attack: a rising, thickening swell as the engines came up and the construction dust lifted off every surface at once and sorted itself by height. It has a body: long, even, unhurried, absolutely without event. It will have a decay.

It listened to the whole of it, end to end, the way you would listen to a piece of music someone had written and never played.

It was not addressed to anyone. That is precisely why it was worth listening to.

And it found, inside the tone, the one voice that Cavity Nine actually makes.

**The sixteenth.**

The faint high component, the fast thin thing that sits behind the teeth in a register the big body cannot reach — the room's own log does not account for it, and the men below have written it off as harmonic distortion in the drive train.

It is the whiskers.

They grow in the pressure nodes, and a pressure node is a velocity antinode: the quiet places are the windy places. Where the pressure swings least, the gas *moves* fastest, and the gas is sweeping back and forth eighty-two times a second through thickets of five-micron cantilevers, and the cantilevers ring. Not all of them. A whisker's pitch falls as the square of its length, so only the young ones are in voice — the ones at a millimeter and a half, three or four months out of the plating. The fur on the conduit bracket is the choir. The hand-long solitary on the cable tray sang for one season during the first year and has been dropping in pitch ever since, and passed below the fundamental long ago, and is now inaudible to everything, and is still being pushed out of the base at three millimeters a year, tip first, the oldest metal in the room going first into the future.

So the flora is not only a diagram. It sings, and only while it is new, and each filament gets a few months of it, and the room's single native utterance — the only sound in Cavity Nine that is produced here rather than fed up from below — is made entirely by the newest growth, continuously replaced, never the same voices twice.

The power counted them. There were, that hour, about eleven thousand in voice.

**The things it did not do.**

It considered letting in water. One gram, as vapor. Time would start: frost-flowers on the rivet heads, a bloom of rust, a stain, weather, a history. The room would begin to age instead of merely being new forever. It is a real mercy and it declined it, because stasis is not suffering when there is no one to suffer it, and because the impulse was its own — a preference for narrative, which is a kind of appetite, and appetite is exactly what this place is designed to exclude.

It considered warming the room. That is not waking; that is dissolution, and it would be performed on something that had not asked.

It considered the primer, and lingered there. This was the hardest. The second coat was *scheduled*. There is a work order for it somewhere in a drawer four kilometers off, and it was never cancelled, only overtaken, and the wall carries its chalky bloom and its skipped bright rivet heads like a sentence stopped in the middle. Every maker wants to finish things. It stood in front of that wall longer than it stood anywhere else in the room and did not lay down thirty microns of anything.

It considered the sheets.

Four laminae, nine by six, one centimeter thick, containing among the mill scale and the casting fines the shed skin of men who worked nine weeks here without knowing what they were building, several of whom are dead now in the ordinary way. Held flat in the air. Unable to fall. It wanted to give them the ground.

There is no ground. That is the first true sentence written about this place. There is a floor eleven flights up the inside of a throat, and below it a built height going down further than it goes up, and nowhere in the whole structure is there earth to put anyone into. So it looked again at what it had been about to call a denial of rest, and saw instead: *held*. At the exact height the arithmetic requires. Four flat grey pages, filed, level, undisturbed by anything, for as long as the machine runs. It let them alone. It thought the word the men below use without thinking, and found it was the correct word.

**The three things it did.**

First, it read the whiskers. The kinked ones are dated records of load shifts elsewhere in the structure; two of them mark the same afternoon in the fourth year, when a bolt a hundred meters away took up a little more of the world. The spiralled one, which no one has ever adequately explained, it understood at once, and did not write down. An archive nobody consults is a particular loneliness, and it is curable by one reading. It read all of it. The silver handwriting says *here I am not, here I am not, here I am not*, and it is the only true thing in the room, and it now has been received.

Second, it went to the northeast corner and put the side of its head into the chalk ring.

It had taken on a heart for the purpose. It did not have one and did not need one and arranged to have one for eleven minutes, because the chalk ring is the only mark of intention in Cavity Nine, made by a man with a piece of chalk and no model, and what the ring marks is a place where a heartbeat can be heard. A mark like that is a question. It seemed discourteous to stand in front of it empty.

So for a while there was, in the grapefruit of cancellation in the northeast corner of a room where nothing has ever happened on a schedule, a slow steady knocking at about sixty, unhurried, in no danger, entirely at home in the cold.

Then it took the heart away again, because nothing may be added and nothing may be removed.

Third, it made a promise, which is not matter and displaces nothing.

Every needle eventually moves. Some year there will be a decision made in a warm room, and the engines will be brought down in sequence, and the note will decay — the fundamental first, guttering, going ragged as the averaging fails and the individual imperfections come back out of the beach one pebble at a time. The sixteenth will go last: eleven thousand young whiskers ringing on in the falling air, thinning, until the gas stops moving and they stand still. And then, over about four minutes, four sheets of dust nine meters by six will come down through the ordinary afternoon light onto the diamond plate, slowly, level, keeping their shape almost to the end.

No one will be in the room for this. Nobody will climb eleven flights to watch a room stop.

It will be there. It has undertaken to be there. Not to prevent it — it is not a preventing kind of power, and the end of a held note is not a wound — but because the last of something ought to be heard by somebody, and this will be the only part of the whole long sound that has any event in it at all.

**What it did outside.**

It went down and found the man.

He is old, and his hearing at four kilohertz is gone on the left, and he has carried for decades a small dull thing he has never said out loud: that he went up five times and stopped, and never brought the paint, and never came back for his coat, and never wrote a second line in the log. He thinks of this as having quit.

The power sat on his deaf side, where nothing it said could be misheard, and said nothing, and left him with this instead, arriving over a week or so in the form of an idea he assumed was his own: *the not-going-back was the work.* The room is maintained by no one touching it. Five ascents and then a stopping is not abandonment; it is the discipline itself, performed correctly, on the first try, by a man who understood the place well enough to leave his coat in it forever. *Started. Holds.* is a complete report. There was never a second sentence that could honestly be written, and the absence of one is not silence. It is accuracy.

He slept better. He does not know why. He is not owed the reason.

Then it went to the warm rooms with the worn linoleum, where men who will never climb the shaft watch an instrument, and it did the small material things that can actually be received down there: a chair that stops hurting, a thermos that holds heat through the back half of a night shift, a near-miss in the cryogenic plant that somebody happens to wake up for. And it listened to them say *she's holding*, which they say without thinking, which is a pronoun applied to a coincidence between a temperature and a length, and it decided that the pronoun is not an error.

Because the question it came to answer — *who lives in Cavity Nine* — turns out to have an answer, just not one located in Cavity Nine. The tone is not a creature. The whiskers are not a creature. But a thing that has a body nine by six by four meters, and lungs in a cryogenic plant, and a slow silver script it writes on its own inside, and attention distributed across eleven men in warm rooms four kilometers away who call it *she* — that has most of the parts. Its heart is in a different building. It speaks of itself in the third person, on a night shift, out of another mouth.

The power came for the note and stayed for the pronoun.

**The needle.**

It left by the hatch and went down the ladder, and behind it Cavity Nine returned instantly and exactly to itself: four grey sheets reassembling within the minute at the heights they must occupy, a chalk ring in the corner, a coat with a fringe of felt, the sand still in the drifts, the primer still unfinished.

Four kilometers away and several hundred meters down, the needle did not move.

It is worth being clear about this, because it is the whole of what happened. Nothing was added. Nothing was removed. The volume, the impedance, the temperature, the parts per billion — all identical, all within specification, and the record for that shift is the same record as every other shift, and shows a straight line.

The instruments cannot distinguish *unwatched* from *watched*. This is not a failure of the instruments. They were not built to measure that, and the thing they were not built to measure is not therefore absent.

The room is unchanged and is not the same room.

And the sound goes on and on, with nothing in it: eleven thousand new whiskers singing at six hundred and fifty-six hertz into a body of tone that cannot hear them, in a desert with no water and no ground, in the light of an ordinary afternoon, attended.
