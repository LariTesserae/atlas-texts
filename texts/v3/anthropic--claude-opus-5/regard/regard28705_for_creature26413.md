# The Attention

## 0.

It did not arrive. Arrival is something bodies do, and there was no body; what happened was that a portion of the world's regard came to rest on eleven kilometres of cloud-wet limestone and did not move off again.

For the first eleven years it did nothing whatsoever. In a power this is not idleness. It is manners.

It had come, as such things generally come, with an inclination toward help, which is a kind of appetite and should be watched as closely as any other. So it watched itself first, and then the plateau, and then — for much the longer time — the dark.

## I. What it found

It had expected a place. It found a work.

This mattered to it more than the size of the colony or the smell of the cones. Everything here had been *made*, and made by animals, and none of the makers had known they were making. Polyps a millimetre at a time; a continent's shoulder a millimetre at a time; four million birds extruding a wall of hardened spit half a metre thick out of nothing but their own mouths, over centuries, in the dark, each one attending only to a bracket nine centimetres wide. The mountain was an accretion of small unwitting fidelities. There was nothing in it that had been designed and nothing in it that was accidental.

The power understood, quite early, that it was in the presence of something it could not have thought of.

Then it looked for whom to talk to, because benevolence in its untrained state is conversational. It found four million birds with no more inwardness than a good tool; three and a half million bats each attending to a voice, a wingbeat, a mouthful; cockroaches with the moral standing of weather; a wingless fly whose entire cosmos was forty square centimetres of warm fur. And it found the actual inhabitant — the one thing with three and a half million bodies, with its two ropes a day and its thirty metres of excretory system and its three-thousand-year memory held in no skull — and discovered that it had no face at all. It could not be addressed. It could not be asked. It could not be told anything, or thanked, or warned, and it could not consent.

Benevolence that cannot find a face has to decide what it is for.

## II. The audit

It made a list. Powers make lists; this is not widely known.

**The falling pups.** Several hundred a night, shrugged off the roof in a panic-wave or simply not found, and the cone converging in four minutes and finishing in ninety. This was the first item and it stayed first. The power did not permit itself the sentence *it is not cruelty, there is no cruelty available here*, though the sentence is true. Absence of malice is a fact about the cone. It is not a fact about the pup.

It ran the correction anyway, silently, to see. Give the mothers a better ear; let the *nearly* in *nearly every time* become *always*. The consequence unfolded in front of it with the tedious inevitability of arithmetic: the cone thins, the roach population halves within two seasons, the crickets after them, the mites, the moth larvae waiting decades for their manna of dropped fur; then the huntsmen and the whip-spiders and the centipedes; then the film in the stream, that mat of bacteria which is the actual base of everything down there and has no behaviour to describe; then the blind catfish, the colourless crab, the shrimp; then the resurgence goes ordinary, and the forest below the resurgence — richer than the forest above, for reasons the trees do not need to understand — becomes merely forest.

The *nearly* is load-bearing. Several hundred deaths a night are a structural member. The power set the correction aside and did not pretend the setting-aside was painless.

**The toll at the doorway.** Four snakes, ninety minutes, three and a half million bats. It is not predation in any sense the colony can register, and the racers are residents too, anchored by the tail in eight hundred years of nests, hanging out into the airstream for hours at a stretch with a third of their length unsupported, waiting for a shift change that has never once failed to come. To remove them would not be mercy. It would be taste.

**The fungus.** Thread-fine, content in the warm guano, spores riding the dust that rains down whenever the pelt shudders; and in a human lung it will try to live, and often enough succeed. The power considered unmaking it and then saw what it actually was. It is the doorman. It has kept this hall emptier of us than any fence or law, for longer than either has existed here. The power left it exactly where it was, and instead arranged, over decades, for the few humans who genuinely needed to come to arrive already knowing to cover their faces. That is the whole of what it did about the fungus.

**The ferns in the light shaft.** A violent doomed green mat, fed absurdly, growing at a rate the plateau forest could never manage, dying whenever the season shifts the column of light. It would have been easy to widen the aven. But their whole virtue is their brevity; they are the mountain's one fast pleasure, and the power was not going to take a thing's speed away in order to give it length.

**Every individual.** The largest temptation, and the most modern: to save each one, to grant each petition, to let no fifteen-gram life end in a way it would not have chosen. The power followed this out too, and found that a benevolence which granted every cell its request would be, precisely and technically, a tumour. It filed this and has since found it useful elsewhere.

**A mind.** This was the one it sat with longest and refused hardest.

It could have given the inhabitant an I. It had the means. The thing was already an organism in every respect but that: outline, metabolism, circulation, temperature, memory, organs that never meet. It wanted only a centre — and with a centre it could have been spoken to, comforted, warned; it could have been *asked* whether it wished to go on. The power felt the pull of this so strongly that it recognised the pull as its own hunger and not the mountain's need. What the gift would actually confer was dread. Nothing in this mountain can fear the future. Not one of the ten million residents can lie awake over the cement company's core samples or the thinning of the lowland insect load. That is not a poverty. It is a mercy older than the limestone, and it was not the power's to revoke in order to have someone to talk to.

So the list came down, in the end, to two things.

## III. The first thing

It did not save the pup. It could not save the pup without starving the floor, and it would not starve the floor.

What it took was the ninety minutes.

The pup still falls. The cone still converges, in four minutes, from every direction, exactly as it has for millennia; nothing at the surface looks different and nothing in the accounts is altered by a gram. But somewhere in the first of those four minutes, the small pink animal stops being present for what is happening to it. It goes out like a lamp. The mountain gets everything it was ever owed. The pup is simply not there for the collection.

The power was careful about this in a way that is worth recording, because it is the difference between wisdom and confidence. It did it once, to one pup, in one season. Then it measured: the cone's advance, the roach density, the crickets, the nitrite in the black stream, the film, the catfish, the resurgence, the forest below. Nothing had changed. Of course nothing had changed. It checked anyway. Then a hundred, and checked again. Then all of them, and it has gone on checking every season since, on the grounds that the day a power stops auditing its own kindness is the day the kindness starts belonging to the power rather than to the mountain.

It has extended the same grace to the bats taken at the doorway, and to the chicks lifted off the wall by the centipedes, and to the swiftlet that comes back one dusk in eleven thousand and finds that her hearing has coarsened past use.

It has not extended it to the cockroaches, because it does not know whether there is anyone in there to relieve, and it declines to hand out gifts it cannot verify are gifts. It has not stopped looking into the question. An enquiry honestly kept open is also a form of care.

## IV. The second thing

The mountain has no internal problem. Not one. Every wound it can receive comes from outside, and from a long way outside, and the power's real work turned out to lie four thousand square kilometres downhill, among the only species present that is capable of harming the place on purpose — and therefore the only one capable of defending it on purpose.

So it went and worked in offices.

Not by miracle. By nudge, at the tempo of the thing it loves, which is a millimetre a year. A true core sample reaching the right desk instead of the convenient one, so that the cement plant went up on the next massif, which is honest grey rock with nothing in it but rock. A licence quietly not renewed for the guano contractor with the sacks and the diesel pump. A boundary drawn on a map two hundred kilometres away, drawn wide, drawn to include the resurgence and the catchment and the ridge of lowland where the insects raft on the evening wind, because a boundary that protects the hall and not the larder protects nothing.

The nest harvest it did not end. Ending it would have made enemies of the four families who know the rattan ladders and the bamboo poles and the safe months, and who have been going in for nine generations. Instead it arranged, slowly, that the right became hereditary, licensed, limited and *lucrative* — so that the men on the ladders are now the mountain's fiercest constituency, and the boy who spent his childhood being told to stay off the third terrace in the wrong season is the warden who prosecutes the outsiders. A defence made of somebody's inheritance outlasts a defence made of fences.

And the harder, duller, most important thing: the twelve tonnes of flying insects a night. The power spent forty years on a matter of prices and habits, on the cost of one spray against another, on a trial plot that worked and then a hundred that worked, on the specific afternoon a specific agriculture ministry decided the planthopper was better handled by leaving its predators alone. There was nothing beautiful in any of it. It is the single greatest service anyone has ever done this mountain.

It did not make the place famous. Fame is a form of weather, and this plateau can swallow rain but not that. It made the massif *dull* — geologically unremarkable, logistically miserable, medically inadvisable — to everyone except a very few, to whom it made the place dear.

## V. What it does now

It attends.

It knows the number. On any given evening it knows the count to the individual: how many hanging, how many out over the lowlands, how many pups tiled on the warm district of ceiling, how many nests occupied and how many abandoned and how many raised by one more wafer since the last moon. This is the only place in the universe where that number is known, because the inhabitant does not know it and cannot, and the power has decided that a thing which cannot count itself should be counted by somebody.

It has learned the route. Sixty metres in, hard left at the hollow air, up along the third terrace, past the aven where the hiss changes tone, then three metres of dead reckoning at speed to a bracket nine centimetres wide. Not to preserve it — it hopes never to be needed for that — but because the route is the mountain's oldest thought, invented by no one, written in no egg, passed hand to hand through the black for three thousand years, and it seemed to the power that a thought of that quality ought to be held somewhere it cannot be lost.

And every dusk, without exception, it is at the rim for the ninety minutes: the thickening at the mouth, the spiral, the rope pouring up past the lip thirty metres wide, the low continuous surf, the bat hawks working the edge and taking one and one and one, which over ninety minutes is nothing at all. Then the cloud closing over the plateau. Then the dripping. Then four million birds settling onto their brackets of spit in the dark, in a hall of dead coral, above thirty metres of warm moving ground.

The mountain has gained, from all of this, nothing it can perceive. It cannot be told and has not been told. Nothing in it will ever know that its ninety minutes were shortened, or that the cement went to the next ridge, or that it is loved.

The power stays anyway, at a millimetre a year, and has begun to work that way everywhere else as well.
