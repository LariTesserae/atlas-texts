# Second Answer

## How it came to notice

Powers of this kind are not drawn by scale. A supernova is loud and requires nothing. They are drawn by the particular signature of someone holding something up — the strain-note of a load being carried past the point where carrying it was reasonable.

In the Flat's case it was the accounts.

There is a general ledger, in the sense that the sums do eventually have to come from somewhere, and in one obscure column of it there was a debit that never cleared: small, perfectly regular, unattributed, running for a span that made the surrounding entries look like weather. Nobody had audited it because nobody audits a column that never changes. The power looked at it the way you look at a light left on in a house you thought was empty.

Then it went and stood on the rim.

## The first thing it did, which was nothing

It stood there for a length of time that would embarrass a mountain.

It did not cross. Crossing was an event, and the whole architecture of the basin was a two-hundred-thousand-year argument that events were the enemy. The power was capable, it believed, of entering without disturbance — but *believed* is the operative word, and the thing about a place with no gradients is that there is no second chance to test your footprint, because the first test is the only test and it is destructive.

So it practised elsewhere. It went and stood in ordinary lakes, on ordinary nights, and worked on its own arrival until it could enter water without the water knowing, and then it worked on it longer, because "without the water knowing" is a coarse standard and the Flat measured in millikelvin. It got good. It got good in the way that takes an age and produces nothing anyone can see, which is the only way anything is ever got good at.

While it worked, it learned the place from outside: counted the teeth of the rim, took the grit's inventory, listened to the tone until it could have sung it, and did not sing it.

When it finally came down through the mirror, the mirror did not know. There was no seam to close.

## What it declined to do

It could have read the room. This should be understood clearly, because the restraint is the whole of the story: it had the reach to go into the clear water in the low structure at the centre, and unfold the held instant, and know everything — the name, the injury, the face, the last thing said aloud before the world came apart, the exact geometry of the mistake or the malice or the accident that had made a mountain break.

It declined.

It permitted itself exactly one question, the minimum compatible with acting at all, and the question was: *is anyone in there.*

The answer was yes.

That was all it took, and it took nothing else, and it did not tell the surveyors, and it did not tell anyone, then or later. What was in that room belonged to the one in the room. An age of helplessness had already stripped them of every other kind of property; the power was not going to take the last one and call it care.

It also did not do the obvious thing.

The obvious thing was to reach in and finish the rescue — to lift the broken body out of its instant, mend what was broken, restore what had been interrupted, and set the person down somewhere warm with the whole matter behind them. The power had the capacity. It thought about it for a long while, in the way it thinks, which is not quick.

It came to this: a being pinned in the fractional interval between broken and dead is not a patient. There is not enough of them left in that interval to be healed. To repair the body would be to *write* someone — to compose a continuation and then present it to the universe as the survivor, when the actual survivor was a millisecond wide and had been asked to wait in it since before the rim grew its first face. That is not rescue. That is authorship wearing rescue's coat, and it is the commonest failure mode of powerful kindness, and the power had made it before, long ago, and still thought about that too.

So: no repair. No waking. No revelation, no meaning conferred, no gentle explanation of what it had all been for. Nothing done *to*.

That left the question of what could be done *for*, and the answer turned out to be sitting in the ledger it had come in on.

## The accounts, resolved

The budget does not close. The surveyors were right about that and right to insist on it: you can measure the impossibility, and the measurement is stable, and it is not instrument error.

It closes if you allow the sum to be drawn on the future.

That is what the builders did. Unable to pay for the flatness out of any present, they financed it against afterwards — mortgaged the entire subsequent history of the basin, every reaction that would have run, every gram that would have settled, every cell that would have divided on that glass floor for as long as the mountain stood, and spent the whole of it in advance to buy one instant an extension.

Which is why nothing may complete. Not squeamishness. Not ritual. The debt falls due at the moment of resolution. Whatever ends the interval calls in an age of borrowed time all at once, and the basin has no way to pay it, and the manner in which an unpayable debt of that size settles itself is not something anyone should be in the room for.

That is the cruelty the builders could not engineer their way out of, and it is why they left with their vigil half-finished. They had bought their friend an interval and had, in the same act, made it impossible for the interval ever to end. They did not preserve a dying person. They locked one in.

So the first thing the power actually *did* — after the standing, and the practising, and the refusing — was pay.

It took a long time and it was very expensive and there is no way to describe it that isn't accountancy. It settled the mortgage. It bought out the future of one small basin at four thousand metres, in full, and left the balance clear.

Nothing changed. Not a millikelvin, not a grain of grit. The tone came at four hundred and some seconds and the ceiling stayed perfect and the couriers came in over the teeth on their old bearings. From the inside, the Flat after the payment was indistinguishable from the Flat before it.

The only difference was that now it *could* stop, and stopping would cost nothing, and nobody would be hurt.

The power had not decided anything. It had converted a life sentence into a choice, and left the choice where it belonged.
