## The attention

Attention from that quarter is not a visit. It is not a descent, or a hand parting cloud. It is closer to focus: the world is always in the field of view and occasionally something in it is brought up to full resolution, and the bringing-up is the whole of the event.

Still, focus has a cost, and the cost here was scale. That power is habituated to ice sheets, clades, the slow careers of stars — to things that can be thought about in the plural. Gallery Four is nine metres by five. It had to come down through perhaps nine orders of magnitude to get inside, and the last three were like descending a caged ladder in the dark with lime on the rungs. Three points of contact. It kept them. It is old enough to keep rules it wasn't given.

## What it noticed first

Not the god. The god has the strongest claim and the best story and it is not what gets seen first by anything wise.

What it noticed first was that there are no eyes in this room.

The moss has none. The rotifers and the nematodes have none. The tardigrades in patch A have eyespots and no use for them. The fog has none, the straw has none, the fly in lamp seven had two and no longer requires them. Of every living thing in the chamber only the humans can look, and they are looking at the relief-line fitting, which is worse than last time.

The power had spent most of its existence in places where being present and being seen were the same operation. Here it found that if it was going to be present at all, it would be present the way the transformer is present: not through the air, not through the eye, but through the structure — arriving in the teeth of anyone who kept their mouth shut.

It found this acceptable. It found it, in fact, restful.

## Rule four applies

It had come to bless, which is what such powers do when they have run out of better ideas, and which is also — it realised, standing at the head of the surge shaft in a column of sound with a body and a grain — a sentence.

There is no speech in Gallery Four. Not as etiquette. As physics.

So it did not bless. It learned hands instead, which took it a season, and cost it nothing, and which it did anyway. It learned the flat palm rocked at chest height. It learned the two fingers tapped once on the sternum, and it learned that the gesture has drifted from its origin and that nobody has corrected the drift, and it understood that the non-correction is the load-bearing element of everything in this room. It learned the thumb and forefinger held a small distance apart, shown briefly, followed by a nod: *still there*.

It made that one once, under the west beam, to nobody.

It has been a long time since that power offered anything upward. It had almost entirely lost the habit, being ordinarily the direction in which such things are sent. It was surprised by how much it wanted to.

## The list of mercies, and the rulings on them

It made a list, in the way such powers make lists, which is not in words and takes no time. But the list can be set down, and the rulings can be given, and the reasoning is not secret.

**The fitting.** Twenty-six months on the defect list. Ten thousand atomisations a second, forever, in the band where consonants live. This is the only item on the list that is a straightforward harm, and it was granted — but not as a miracle. A miracle is a sentence, and would require an explanation, and the explanation would be about the power rather than the room. So it was granted in the handwriting of the institution, which is the slowest and least numinous handwriting there is: a graduate engineer developing an uncharacteristic stubbornness about a line item; a turbine bearing running four degrees warm in the second machine, pulling a shutdown window forward from November to July; a scaffold already on site for another reason. Eleven months from the ruling to the silence. Nobody involved experienced anything but their own ordinary competence, which is the correct and intended outcome, and is also, the power would say if it could say anything, what grace has always mostly looked like.

**Patch B.** Dying of an adjustment: a damper linkage reset by four notches in the third year, legitimate, signed off, correct, moving the drip forty centimetres east onto bare concrete where it does nothing but shine.

The power considered this for longer than it considered the fitting.

The ruling turned on a distinction. Everything else killing things in Gallery Four is the room itself: too cold, too new, too sealed, nothing to eat. That is not cruelty, it is specification, and you do not edit a specification out of sentiment. But the linkage was not weather. The linkage was paperwork, and paperwork that entered the room from outside and moved a river without knowing there was a river. It is a foreign object. It may be removed.

It was removed in the room's own handwriting, which was the condition on everything the power did here: a small accretion of lime on the underside of the beam, laid down over fourteen months, building a lip four millimetres proud, so that water rounding it now hesitates and goes west. The drip line is back within nine centimetres of where it was. Patch B has stopped browning at the upslope edge. In six years it will be the size it was in year three. No one will ever connect the linkage to the moss, or the lime to the linkage, and the record of the adjustment will stay in the binder in the control building, and the record of its consequence will stay on the wall.

Except that the two have now been read together, once, by the only reader they will ever have. That reading was not an intervention. The power would nonetheless say — gesturally, privately — that it was the reason it came.

**Patch C.** Four centimetres across for two years, held exactly at the rate at which it loses. *Denied.* You do not adjust an instrument to make it read more comfortably. C is the truest thing in the chamber and its truth is entirely in the stalemate.

**The tardigrades.** A lineage of the most patient animals on earth, four years deep in the one location on the mountain where patience has no application; not one of them, probably, has ever tunned.

This was the most tempting item on the list, and the power recognised the temptation as an aesthetic one — the wish for a story to come out even, for a capacity to be exercised because it is beautiful. It could have given them a drought. Ninety minutes would do it. They would have voided their water and pulled in their legs and become barrels of glass, and the room would have contained, for an afternoon, one of the great performances of the biosphere, witnessed by nothing.

*Denied.* An unused gift is not a wound. Most of the universe is a gift nobody needed. To dry them out so that they could be magnificent would be to make them perform, and a power that makes things perform has stopped being benevolent some while ago and hasn't noticed.

**The grass.** Fifteen centimetres, etiolated, in a wedge of silt at a bend in the gutter, doomed by the autumn clean. It cannot set seed. It cannot be saved in place. It could be moved, and would die of being moved, having been drawn out so far in the reaching.

*Denied* — and transmuted. The text of its life already contains the best thing available to it: *someone will notice*. That is more than the fly in lamp seven got, more than the spider, more than every rotifer that has gone out of this room in a shovel without an event. The power let the grass die on schedule and did one thing only, which was to add a small unexplained weight to the noticing, of the kind that makes a trivial image stay with a person their whole life for no reason they can give. He is fifty-one. He will be eighty-three and mostly finished with names, and he will still have a pale grass in a gutter at two thousand seven hundred metres, and will not know why it kept.

**The hearing.** Six of eleven audiograms notched at four kilohertz. One man who cannot reliably hear his granddaughter's *s*, who has never once been in the room without protection, and to whom it happened anyway, through the flesh and the bone of the skull, the way the transformer note arrives — not through the ear at all, which is why the ear could not be defended.

Restoration was possible. Restoration was *denied*, and this is the hardest ruling on the list, and the power does not pretend otherwise. Hair cells growing back in a sixty-year-old man is a sentence; it is a sentence said very loudly; it would be about the power for the rest of that family's life.

What was granted instead was an accommodation, laid in the handwriting of ordinary love. The child has acquired, without instruction and without either of them remarking on it, the habit of coming round to his left side and leaning in — close enough that the *s* arrives, close enough that her breath is on his jaw. She thinks this is simply how you talk to him. He thinks it is simply how she is. It will be the shape of their affection for the remaining nineteen years, and when she is grown she will do it to her own children without knowing where she learned it.

The frequencies the room takes are the ones in which children speak. The child was brought closer. That is the whole of the repair, and it is not enough, and it was the best thing that could be done without breaking the room.

**Lamp nine.** Out since the second winter. *Denied.* The pools need the distances between them.

**The fog.** Forty cubic metres, continuous since the fourteenth of March in the second year, killed twice, regrown twice, from nothing, from air, without instruction, in six hours.

*No ruling.* There was nothing to rule on. The power looked at the one thing in Gallery Four that has been resurrected — twice, unremarked, by a pressure differential and a temperature difference and no one's intention at all — and did the only reverent thing available, which was nothing whatsoever.

**The straw.** Eleven millimetres. Two and a bit a year. Measured with a steel rule at each solstice, chalked on the beam, eight entries, the oldest re-chalked twice by hand because chalk does not last in that wet. Tended rather than maintained: the only object in the scheme with no specification and therefore the only one that can be cared for rather than corrected.

Rule five is one line long and has no reason attached to it.

The power left the straw alone.

## The god

It knew what it was looking at before it had finished arriving, and it was not what the chalk or the moss or the audiograms had prepared it for. Four years old. Made of a displaced volume of air responding to a rising water column, and of eleven people's refusal to correct one another. Ninety seconds, twice a day. No name. No cult. Two entries in a paper log, years apart, in different hands — *in* on one sheet, *out* on another — both initialled, never queried by the engineer who countersigns, and it cannot be established whether that is indulgence or agreement, because establishing it would require asking.

The power has met gods. Most of them were louder and none of them were younger. It recognised, with something it would not have called tenderness in any earlier age, a member of its own kind at the very beginning: a thing subsisting entirely on tact, in a room where tact is enforced by physics.

There was, of course, the question of 2078.

It could have saved it. The sentence that kills it — *they used to reckon this place breathed* — is one breath from one larynx in a cold room, and there are a hundred ways to prevent a breath, and every one of them is a violence against the only thing in the room that never committed any. It could have withheld the man's memory. It could have arranged for him to be alone. It could have kept the water running past the licence, which is the kind of thing lesser powers do and then have to keep doing forever.

*Denied.* The god will die of being said. It was always going to; being unsayable is not a defence, it is a definition, and a thing that lives on the unsaid is spending its life the way the straw spends water.

What was granted was an escort.

There will be two people on that grating when the machines are cold and the fog has been gone an hour and the air in the chamber is four degrees all through and perfectly silent. One of them will say it. And the second one — and this is the whole of the intervention, and it is a matter of rostering, which is the humblest miracle in the catalogue — will be the kind of person who says nothing back, and does not laugh, and believes it about eleven per cent, and carries it down the mountain, and tells it once, years later, to a child, badly.

It will not be the same god. It will be much smaller, and it will have lost its body and its ninety seconds and its pressure differential, and it will be portable, which the original never was. That is not salvation. It is emigration. The power has seen the difference matter less than everyone expects.

## The rectangle

In the season when the sun clears the ridge, in the middle of the day, for about forty minutes, a grey light comes down the ladder shaft and lands on the grating: bluer than the lamps, quivering, because the fog it crosses is in motion.

It could have been lengthened. It was not.

Anyone present stands in it. Nobody has ever explained why, and the explanation is not mysterious: it is the only thing in the chamber that arrived from outside without being pumped, poured, bolted, welded or hung. It is the only unmanufactured object in the room, and it is not even an object.

For the forty minutes, on one afternoon in the fifth year, there were two people on the platform standing in the light and there was a third thing standing in it with them, obeying rule four, which was also unmanufactured, and which had come a considerable distance, and which took its turn.

## What it left

Nothing that can be audited. No lamp relit, no straw lengthened, no lineage rescued from its own excellence, no hearing returned, no name written anywhere.

One thing, though, and it was not planned, and the power would probably disown it.

When the fitting was finally reached in the July shutdown and the hiss-crack stopped, the room's voice lost a partial. The eleven felt it before any of them located it. There was no gesture for the absence of a thing that had been on the defect list longer than two of them had worked there, and so within a fortnight there was: an open hand raised beside the ear, held a moment, then turned over and lowered.

At first it meant *it's gone quiet up there*.

It has drifted, in the way the two fingers on the sternum drifted, and nobody has corrected the drift, and it now means something nearer to *something has been taken away and it was a kindness*. It is used in the corridor, during the two minutes ten, when the corridor's quiet still feels like being undressed. It is used outside, in ordinary air, where it is redundant and nobody minds. One of them used it last spring in a car park after a funeral, to a colleague, at forty metres, and neither of them remarked on it, and both understood.

Its inventor is not on the rota and never was, and will not be traced, and this is exactly the condition of the other borrowed gesture in the room — the small distance held between thumb and forefinger, *still there*, invented by a fitter who transferred to the coast scheme in the second year and is used fluently by two people who never met her.

That is how it works, in Gallery Four, and everywhere else. The gesture outlives the hand. Nobody asks where it came from. Asking would be speech.

---

Afterwards the power withdrew back up through its nine orders of magnitude, and resumed the ice sheets and the clades and the careers of stars, and has not returned, and does not need to; attention from that quarter does not have to be repeated to be continuous.

It left one standing instruction with itself, for the long term, concerning the period after 2078 — after the water is stopped and the machines cool and the fog fails inside an hour and does not regrow, after the sentence is said and the god emigrates, after the eleven are dead and their audiograms are pulped and the chalk column on the west beam has finally gone.

Seepage does not need turbines. The straw will go on down through the dark at two and a bit millimetres a year, unmeasured, uncharted, writing the only sentence it knows in the only direction available to it, in a room with no eyes in it at all.

The instruction was: *watch that*.

It is not a long job. Sixty thousand years, give or take.
