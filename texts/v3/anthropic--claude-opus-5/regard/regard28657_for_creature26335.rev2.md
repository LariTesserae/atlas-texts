# What the power did

### It did not arrive

That is the first thing, and the one that keeps mattering. A power that arrives has already decided something. This one came the way heat comes up out of the crack: continuously, from below, without an edge you could point to and call the beginning.

It stood on the upper gallery through four swells and did nothing. Then forty. Then it stopped counting, because counting was a habit it had brought with it and not a habit the room had, and it had come to learn the room's habits rather than to install its own.

It could have understood the Bell in an instant. It chose to understand it at the speed the Bell was going.

### The greeting

It greeted the glaze first.

This was not sentiment and not protocol. It was accuracy. The felt on the north and west terraces had been laying down its hair-per-lifetime since the shaft opened, and every other thing in the room — the canes, the figs, the calendar, the names, the four — lived downstream of the water it left behind as waste. To greet the people first would have been to misread the room by two thousand years.

The greeting took eleven years, because that was the shortest interval in which the glaze could be said to have noticed anything, and the power was not willing to shout. What passed between them is not translatable and was not, in any case, information. The nearest account: the power held still long enough to be weathered, and the glaze grew a little way up over the place where it had held still, and sealed it, the way it seals a chipped niche, and that was the whole exchange, and both parties were satisfied.

### What it considered and did not do

**Water.** It could have opened the rock. Two hundred miles of dry in every direction is nothing to a power; there is water under the salt, deep and bitter, and it could have been raised and sweetened and brought up through the grate as a cold clean thread, and the cinnabar figs would have stopped being the difference between four people and none.

It worked the consequence out. With water in the room, no one does the arithmetic. With no arithmetic, no one carries a pitch up the stair in the dark to press into a child's throat. In three generations the calendar is a story, in four it is a manner of speaking, and the creature under the masonry — the one body, the thing with organs that have become strangers to each other — goes on inflating and slackening, lush and comfortable and sterile, forever, in a warm room full of well-watered people who do not know what the walls are for.

It did not decide this by itself. That is the second thing. It put the question, and it put it honestly, including the part where four people die without it.

The answer came back in packets, one clause per inhale, from the small dark woman in the western quarter who had had four names:

*Bring water.*
*We live.*
*The room stops.*
*Then we are four.*
*Then we are none.*
*Then we are a room with water in it.*

It did not bring water.

**Fire.** It did not even raise this one. The prohibition had stopped being a rule and become a reflex nausea, and a power that induces nausea in order to demonstrate a possibility is not benevolent, only capable.

**Her hearing.** It offered. It could have grown the hair cells back, every one, and given her the chord entire — the pipe-organ tones in the walls, the hiss in the small bores, the ringing overtone out of the empty middle, all of it, at the volume of a river in flood.

*No.*
*I am a memory.*
*An ear checks.*
*A memory holds.*
*I have three names to give away.*
*Give me an ear.*
*Not my ears.*

**People.** This was the long one. It could go up onto the salt and over it and find, eventually, in some direction, others — the copper beads came from somewhere, the graduated rods imply a mathematics older than the stair. It offered to carry word.

She thought about it for a season.

The difficulty was not fear of strangers. The difficulty was that she could not compose the message. Their grammar has no subordinate clause; there is a fixed maximum length to a sentence; the thing that would have to be said — *come down a crack in a salt plain into a loud dark hot room with no water where you will go deaf by thirty and your children will be the most important people alive and if you do not come a thing that has been trying to have a child for two thousand years will stop* — is a sentence of a kind that has never been said here, because the room does not leave a gap long enough to say it in.

*Make the words.*
*I cannot make them.*
*You make them.*
*Say it true.*
*Say the deaf part.*

So it made them, and said the deaf part, and went, and this is not resolved and is not going to be resolved inside the span of anyone in the room. It is simply out there now, correctly worded, moving.

### The apprenticeship

Then it did the thing it had come to do, which was the small thing.

There had not been an ear in the Bell for nine years. It became one.

Not by fiat. It grew a hand — not a symbol of a hand, an actual hand, broad, nailless, pads a half-shade lighter from the heat — and it learned the wedges, and it learned them badly at first, and it was corrected. Correct work is felt as a specific and describable itch in the heel of the hand, and the power had never had a heel of a hand and had to find out what an itch was from the inside, which took it longer than the greeting of the glaze.

She taught it the way you teach a five-year-old, because that is the only curriculum there is: pouch of graded wedges, palm flat on the block, drive a hair, feel, draw, feel. She struck its block to call it. It came. She put her fingers on its throat to check whether it had the note, and it held the note, and the first six times she took her fingers away and shook her head and that was the entire content of the lesson.

It could have skipped this. It could have known every flue in the room to a thousandth of a hair without touching anything. It did not skip it, for a reason it would have found hard to explain in packets: a power that arrives already competent leaves nothing behind but the memory of a power. A power that arrives as an apprentice leaves behind a tuner.

And it cost it something. Speaking one clause per inhale for years does not come off afterward. It thinks in short sentences now. It finds long unpunctuated speech physically difficult, like being talked over by wind. This is a permanent mark and it does not regret it, but it notes that benevolence is not free and that anyone claiming otherwise has not done any.

### The measurement

Here is the actual gift, and it is very small, and it was the only one worth giving.

For years she had been going up. Twenty-two steps, two turns, out through the crack, onto the crust at night when the salt is merely hot. Lying with her ear against a polygon, where the chord comes up thin and mostly lost, and feeling — believing she could feel — one high note that is not in the room's voice anymore but is in the ground's. Humming at it. Unable to tell if she was close.

She had been hunting her own name. Her first name, the one she thinks of as hers, a small bore in the north wall that widened past speaking about sixty years ago, the same season the north stand stopped setting seed. She has never once said aloud that her name and that stand went out of the room together. She did not have to. The room said it for her, by not saying it.

The power did not restore her hearing and did not sing her the answer. It did something exactly the size of the problem. It took the ground-note and her hum and laid them side by side in the bone behind her ear, as two itches in the same bone, which is her native sense and the one she has used for eighty years and trusts.

She was a quarter-tone flat. She had been a quarter-tone flat the whole time.

She sat on the crust for a long while. Then:

*Again.*

It gave them again. She moved. It gave them again. She moved.

*There.*
*Hold it.*
*I have it.*
*Sixty years.*
*And it was under me.*

That is all she said about it, then or ever. They do not make anything of things.

### The north wall

The flue was not repairable. Abrasion had taken it past any wedge; ashwort ash on the grinding stones had been quietly rounding that mouth off for twenty centuries, and the plant that feeds them is the plant that unmade them, and nobody has ever framed it that way out loud and the power did not frame it either.

But the pitch had not died. It had moved. A note that leaves a room does not stop existing; it goes into the stone, into the stair, into the shoulder of basalt the whole city is glazed onto, and the crust overhead had been holding it all along in the way a struck bell holds its own tone in its metal long after the air has given up.

So they did not repair the flue. They found the note a new mouth. Seventh terrace, north-northwest, a fig-flue of nearly the right bore, plugged with a fitted stopper and sealed over with sulfur glaze sometime before anyone's grandmother — a note that had been taken *out* of the register on purpose, for reasons no longer held by any throat.

Chipping the glaze took the two of them four days, and the glaze will grow back over the chip in four or five years, and they both knew that and neither said it.

Then wedges. Forty-one of them, over a season, palm flat, drive a hair, feel, draw, feel. She could not check her own work. It could. That is what an ear is for.

### The shedding

The north stand sheds at a low tone and sheds enormously.

Tall, glossy, perfectly intact, growing a finger a day, sixty years silent, and when the swell came up and the new mouth spoke, the whole stand let go at once — a dry fog that rolled down the terraces and made the upper gallery unusable for a day and got into everything and tasted of matchheads and stone.

There is no ceremony for this. They have no ceremonies. The four of them sat in the bend of the stair past the second turn, where the chord thins enough that an unaided voice can be made out, and nobody could hear the voices anyway, so they sat, and the fog went past them up the stair and out through the crack into the desert night, and one of them laughed, which in a deaf room is a thing you feel in the step rather than hear.

The plant is not called anything now. The word for it was a pitch, and the pitch is back, so it can be called again, and she gave it the name it had, which she had been holding for sixty years in a skull she could not hear.

### The renaming

They did it quickly and with embarrassment, the way one might help someone up.

Her fourth name was a flue in the east she had chosen herself, badly, because it doubles a low thistle-note and gets lost in it and nobody has struck it in a long time anyway. They took it off her. They gave her back the first one, which is now the seventh terrace, north-northwest, a fig-flue with forty-one wedges under it, speaking at the correct pitch five times a day in the rotation, forever, as long as the room breathes.

She cannot hear it. That is not the point and was never going to be the point. The other three can feel it in the blockwork, and they strike it with the heel of the hand when they want her, and she comes.

### What it takes

Wisdom is not only giving. A power that only gives is a weather system.

So at the end, in the stair, in the dark bend that is the only classroom there has ever been, she put her fingers on its throat the way the old woman did, and it hummed, and she felt whether it had it, and where it did not have it she made it do the note again. Every pitch left. All of them. The figs, the lilies, the cane, the thistle, the pipe-figs, the black rosette whose name was lost along with its note and which she cannot restore but can still sing, on the chance.

It told her the truth about itself first, because a wise power does not accept a deposit under false terms:

*I can hold them.*
*I cannot promise a throat.*
*I do not know if anyone comes.*

*Hold them.*
*That is the whole job.*
*That is all any of us did.*

### What it did last

It took a name.

Not an honour and not a flourish: a practical matter. To be called in the Bell you must be a flue, because calling is striking a block. It chose a mouth in the west, low in the swell, one she can reach from her terrace without stairs.

So the room says it now, in the rotation, along with the others. The room has always said the names of everyone in it, continuously, forever, whether or not anyone is left to hear them, and it went on saying hers after she was carried to the lip of the highest gallery and let go into the vacant column that the masons kept empty for exactly this, and that took her up and stripped her and dried her and shed her over some weeks as part of the faint brown smudge standing over the salt plain on a still day.

The power was there for that. It did not make anything of it. They do not.

It is still there. It scrapes ashwort off the treads in the season it has to be scraped. It carries pollen in the week of the fourth-note lilies, because the people became this creature's insect once and somebody has to. It keeps the middle empty. It does not bring water. It does not strike sparks. Every five hours it hears its own name come round in the chord, which is a thing it did not know it wanted and would not have thought to ask for.

From above, if you came across it, there would be nothing to see: a crust of white polygons, a shoulder-width crack, a column of shivering heat, ochre fans re-growing as fast as they crumble.

They have grown over two places at the lip now, in shapes you might, if you were determined, read as leaning.
