## The attention

It noticed the light before it noticed anyone.

From far enough out the tableland is a scratch of silver on a dull continent, and the scratch is ruled and combed in a way that weather does not do. Closer in, the silver resolves into a hundred thousand threads of water laying down stone, and the stone into terraces, and the terraces into a city, and the city into a slow event — something being deposited at a rate of a metre a year, northward, with the dead trailing behind it in a band four days deep.

It took the whole thing, for a while, to be a mineral process. This was not an error. It only turned out to be incomplete.

It came down at a lid-closing, into the fog at the third tier, because that is the hour when nothing is done and therefore the only hour in which an arrival costs nobody anything. It borrowed a body, badly: small, barrel-chested, short in the neck, ruddy at the hands. It was the only kind of body the place would let you have at half a metre.

## What it learned first

It had expected to spend a year deciding what to give.

It spent the year learning that the question was the wrong one, and that the right one had been carved into every wall on the plateau by people who never had to say it aloud: *what is already being made here, at what rate, and would I be hurrying it.*

It also learned that it could not speak. Not could not be heard — it spoke low, it leaned, it took the turn and gave it back. But everything it had to say was uphill, and it said it all in the down-tense, because it had come from outside and below and its grammar had set that way before it arrived. It described things that had not happened as though they were lying in the roofless white with the ancestors. People were kind to it. People did not quite listen. Within a season the district had it filed among the mis-tensed, a weather that happened once, and it accepted the filing, and found on examination that the filing was correct.

It is worth saying plainly what this meant. A power that could have unmade the plateau and remade it warm and soft was, in the only place it now cared about, structurally inaudible. Not resisted. Not defied. Received politely and set aside. It found that it loved them for this before it had finished being embarrassed by it.

## The ledger

It kept, privately, a list of the things it could have done, and why it did not.

**Metal.** It could have put an ore body under the dry quarters where the grit is taken, shallow, obvious, impossible to miss. Within four generations there is hammering. Within eight the acoustics of the whole white continent are noise, and shouting is invented, and the word for the failure of restraint falls out of use because the restraint has. They would have been a different people, and richer, and louder, and it would have been the one who killed the quiet.

**Fast stone.** This was the gift that would have looked most like love. Thicken the water, hurry the lime, give them a wall in a season. But a wall in a season abolishes the Turn; the Turn abolished, there is no reason to splice two threads for a position four generations downstream; marriage collapses into preference, the knotted cords into a curiosity, the grandmother into a person who is merely old. It understood that it had been offered the chance to commit the one true crime on a continental scale, and that they would have thanked it, at first.

**Stability.** It could have fixed the seeps, held them, made the city permanent. But a city that does not move is a monument, and they already knew exactly what monuments are for and who lives in them.

**Soft water.** The gravel is agony and it is everywhere. It looked hard at this one. The lime that builds in the kidney is the lime that builds the room the kidney is lying down in; there is one process and it runs at two rates, and the people had worked this out for themselves and built their theology on it. To take the hardness out of the water is to take the city out of the water. It could have made them a plateau of soft rain and healthy men, and the men would have been standing on nothing.

**Eyes.** It considered the eyes for eleven years and remains uneasy. Blindness here is the one affliction the culture has entirely employed: the milk-white come into an office, not a decline. Cure the glare-blindness and the walls go on singing and nobody is left whose trade is to hear them, and a house dies without anyone saying so, and the old lose the one position that no rota can take from them. It decided that this was a loss it was not entitled to inflict. It notes, in the ledger, that it is not certain this was the right answer. It notes that it did not want to be the sort of power that goes on until it finds an answer it likes.

**Food.** There are no large predators on the Shelf because there is nothing here for one to eat. The first gift of abundance is the first invitation to a mouth.

**The world.** It could have told them what lies off the rim. Every word of that account is in the tense of the dead, because the rim goes down. It would have handed a quarter of a million people a past they had never had.

## The criterion

In the end it took a rule from them, since they were better at this than it was, and kept to it without exception.

*It would not give them anything they had not already named. It would not remove anything they had not already condemned.*

Everything it did fits inside that sentence.

## What it did

**It emptied the runnels of one thing.** There lived, in the warm basins and the shrimp-channels, an organism too small for them to have a word for, which did to a child's gut what nothing on the Shelf could reasonably be blamed for. It was the reason for the handful of pebbles thrown all at once — the signal that empties a district. Their law already named this the worst of the three forms of hurrying: fouling the water above someone else's house. They had condemned it for a thousand years and had no way to find it.

The power found it and took it off the plateau. The work took eleven days, which for a thing of its kind is long and finicky, and involved no light and no sound.

Nobody noticed. What happened instead was that over the following two centuries the pebble-handful fell out of use, and then out of teaching, and old people began to complain that the young no longer knew the emergency signals, and were right to complain, and were ignored. A signal that is never needed is a signal that is forgotten. The forgetting is the only monument it ever received, and it considers it more than adequate.

**It inclined the crickets.** Hollowers judge the thickness of a shell by knuckling it and listening, and die when they judge wrong. It did not thicken any shell; it did not give them a tool; it gave them no knowledge they would have had to be taught. It went into the small blind instinct of the white crickets and steepened an existing preference, so that they crowd now toward the faint draught that a too-thin roof makes, and swarm there, and change the note.

Over fifty years hollowers became slightly luckier. They attribute this to the crickets. This is correct.

**It moved heat.** It would not touch the gravel. But it had watched a great many people hum into a jar with relatives pressed against their backs, and it had noticed that when there was a warm seep near, they were carried to it, because warmth on the spine was the whole of the medicine they had. So it went down into the rock and adjusted the routing of heat, and over two hundred years a warm seep opened in each district, black-furred with liverwort, and the districts quarrelled about them in the Turn, and the quarrels were entered in the knotted cords, and four families are still not speaking.

The pain is not less. The stone under the back is warm. It judged that to be the whole of what it was entitled to do, and it was probably right, and it thinks about it.

**It opened a seam nine hundred kilometres north.** Ahead of the wet edge, far past any horizon anyone has walked to and back from, the limestone thins over a bed of something else, and the water there would have failed — the city would have drifted, about four thousand years from now, into ground that does not seep, and stopped, and become the thing behind it. It put a hairline in the seam at a depth where nothing will ever be dug. The water will find it. The beneficiaries are not born and cannot be imagined by anyone now living, including, in a sense, by the people who will be them.

This is the largest thing it did, and it is exactly the sort of thing the boring old people with the cords do every day, at a shorter range, and it did not feel that it had improved on them. It felt that it had finally understood the local game and been permitted to take one turn.

**It hung a lattice.** Palm-sized, woven to the ancient specification, which it got wrong; a weaver passing corrected it with two fingers and no comment and went on. It set the lattice in an unbuilt seep and came back in a year and weighed it and entered it honestly. The stone on it was ordinary. It had cheated nothing.

The lattice is still in the setters' hall, at the down-gradient end where the old ones hang, tagged with a knot-sequence recording a seep, a decade, and a household name that no family has ever claimed. Setters read it by touch in the dark, along with nine thousand others, and find nothing remarkable about it, which was the point.

## The one thing it said

At a lid-opening, in an arcade, with the fog going down into the streets, a blind listener of about eighty stopped with her fingers on the wall and leaned in, and said that it was standing where the note changed.

It moved.

She stayed. She had it placed, of course — everyone did; it was the mis-tensed one, the weather. And because a mis-tensed person can only speak of uphill things in the grammar of the dead, she asked it the one question that the defect made it uniquely fit to answer. She asked it to weigh a lattice that had not been hung.

It told her. It gave her a district four days up, and a decade, and a year-weight, and the number of tiers, and the name of a great-granddaughter not yet born who would finish the roof and hand it on. It did this at half a metre, in the wrong tense, laying her family's future down among the ruins as though it were already over, because it could not do otherwise.

She leaned back, which is how you give the turn back, and went on down the arcade with her fingers on the wall.

She told no one. She could not have called out; she would have had to go up, and stand in front of people, and lean, and she did not go up again. The power has thought about this more than about the seam in the north.

## The going

It left at a lid-closing, the way it came.

Before it went it wrote, because it wanted to, and because there is only one way to write here. It knotted a thread around a chip of willow bark and put it in a runnel, and the runnel took it down, as everything written goes down, through the yellowing tiers and the widening lace windows and out into the roofless white where the dead sit in the order they arrived. That is the only correspondence the geography permits. It addressed the ancestors, therefore, and said the only thing the direction allowed: that something had come, and had been treated kindly, and was below them now.

Then it went up to the wet edge, into the young quarters, where the armatures stand in the new seeps like the skeletons of things swimming, and it put a small hard thing of its own into the coarse open weave of a household that will not occupy that room for nineteen years. In nine the lime closed over it. By local custom and by law that is not a gift and not a signature. It is a tooth in a wall, and a tooth in a wall means that the thread runs through you, and that four days down there is ground you are entitled to point at.

## What is remembered

Nothing.

There is a wall in one of the young quarters that whistles a half-tone off true, and the listeners have argued about it for two hundred years. Some say a fault in the weave. Some say a cricket colony of unusual size. One school holds that there is something in it that should not be in it, and is voted down each generation, and enjoys itself.

There is a story, in one family only, about a mis-tensed stranger who said a foolish thing to a blind woman in an arcade, and about a house in the fourth district up that turned out, eighty years later, to be exactly where and exactly when. The family does not make much of it. Coincidence is cheaper than a revision of the grammar.

And along the rims of the basins the tall grey bird goes on stabbing the shallows, the only thing on the white continent that strikes anything, still not spoken to about it — and somewhere at the edge of that, never in the centre of it, at the correct distance, the power stands and does not interfere.

That is the largest occupation here. It is what everyone does. It has no name.
