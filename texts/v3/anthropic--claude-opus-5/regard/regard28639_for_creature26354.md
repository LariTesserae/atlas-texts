# What the Power Did

It arrived the way water arrives here: laterally, without being seen to come, and a little below the level at which anything was watching.

The first thing it did was nothing, and it did that for a long time. Not out of caution. Out of arithmetic. Anything that has settled into a shape it intends to keep is a sentence in a language, and you do not correct a sentence in a language you have heard four times.

It stayed for something over two hundred thousand breaths. Four years, near enough, by the reckoning kept outside. In that time it learned the following, and learning it was the whole of the work:

That the eleven minutes lengthen in the small hours because the ridge's cavity fills more slowly when the outside air is heavy, and that the snails know this before the water does, and set out early.

That the pipe's *tonk* is a slug of vapour collapsing at the same elbow every time, and that it has worn that elbow to a thinness which will not fail for another two centuries.

That there are three places on the fifth terrace where you may put your weight without damming any channel at all — a knuckle of old slag, a bolt-head, the lip of a plinth — and that from those three places you can stand in the basin all night and not be registered as a shortfall in any gallery.

It taught itself those footings before it did anything else. That is the part of the story most easily skipped and least worth skipping. A benevolent power's first discipline is not generosity. It is learning to enter a room without being an event in it.

## The gifts, considered

It had, of course, brought gifts, in the way that anything wise and kindly arrives with its hands full whether it means to or not. It set them down one at a time on the shale and looked at them in the poor light.

**Light.** The easiest. Tear the overcast, once, permanently; hang a shaft down the long axis. Within a season there would be a green haze on the second terrace, and within five years the film would be undercut by things that eat sunlight and grow faster than chemistry allows, and the beards would sour, and the shrimp would go, and the snails would go, and the warm arc of terrace would be grazed by nothing and cooled by everything. The power understood that what it had almost done was not lighting a dark room. It was setting fire to a house in order to see the furniture.

Set aside. And note, in passing, that the only light in the basin with a purpose in it is brass-coloured, four centimetres thick, and its own.

**Warmth.** It is cold here. But the animal is exactly the temperature of rust forming, which is the temperature of its own metabolism, which is not a deficiency any more than a heartbeat is a deficiency. Add heat and you have subtracted a distinction — the degree and a half that is, as the survey noted without understanding it, the whole answer. Set aside.

**Release.** There are larger waters. There are lakes with sun in them and rivers with room. The power considered the fixture as a prisoner of a chest-high oval of brick and puddled slag, and then it did the arithmetic again and saw that the animal is not in the basin. The animal is the shape of a particular ridge breathing, and to take it out of the breath is to take a note out of the air and hand it to someone. Set aside, with some embarrassment, because that gift had been offered chiefly out of pity, and pity had not been paying attention.

**A companion.** This one took longer.

It went and looked. Under another ridge, in another country, above another dead works, it found a second arc of terrace one degree and a half warm, nine flush ports, and no snail-scoring on the top. Two in the world that it could find. Neither has ever had a category for the other. Neither is lonely, because loneliness requires a hollow shaped like someone else, and these are shaped like water.

The power stood a while with that. It could have carried a fragment of grey-green tissue eleven hundred kilometres in a cupped hand. It could have introduced longing to a thing that has never needed it and watched what longing does to a creature with no muscle worth the name. It set the gift down.

Then it went back to the other basin and learned the footings there too, and the schedule of that ridge, which is seventeen minutes, and it protects both of them now and will never mention either to the other. That is not a lesser love. It is the only shape love can take across nine metres of poured stone.

**Knowledge.** To tell it what it is. To hand it, somehow, the fact of the sun, the news that there are valleys, the arithmetic of its own century and a half. The power turned this one over for a long while, because it was the gift it most wanted to give, and it eventually recognised that wanting as a symptom rather than a reason. The fixture's sense is pressure. Any message would have to be delivered as a shortfall in a gallery — that is, as a small hunger, a stutter in the eleven minutes, a lie about the water. The only sentence the power could say is *something is wrong*. It declined to say it. Set aside, and grieved about, mildly, in the way you grieve a letter you were right not to send.

## The one hard question

Which was time.

The siphon in the ridge is not eternal. There is a bed of shale up there that is being undercut by the very circulation it drives, and in something between three and nine hundred years a wall will go and the cavity will void permanently and the basin will stop breathing, and by the following morning this will be a different world: still water, one thermocline, no rind, no snails on a front, and nine metres of animal ventilating nothing, going cold at the rate that a heap of turnings goes cold.

The power could shore it. A grain of quartz set crosswise in a joint, and the wall stands for a hundred thousand years.

It thought about this through several hundred exhalations and arrived at a distinction it now regards as the most useful thing it owns.

There is a difference between an ending and an interruption. The shale going is this place's own sentence, arrived at by the same slow honest process that made the terraces and the ports and the second band four fingers wide. It is not a wound. It is the last clause. To prevent it would be to hold the animal at the end of its own breath forever, which is the cruelty of every kindness that cannot bear a conclusion.

But a culvert is not a sentence. A drainage licence is not a sentence. A leachate plume off a tip four hundred metres up the ridge, arriving in the seep at a concentration that stops iron oxidising, is not this world's own ending; it is a thing done to it, hurriedly, by people who never came down the shale and would have filed it under weathering if they had.

So the power let the ridge keep its death and undertook to prevent every other one.

## What it actually did

Five things. All of them look like luck, which is the correct appearance for this kind of work.

The drainage scheme, which would have punched a relief adit through the north-eastern flank in the third year, failed twice at committee for reasons of cost, and the second failure was procured. It required the power to be interested in a quantity surveyor for about four hours.

The scrap men who took the rest of the ironwork in the bad decade came down as far as the second terrace, found the flywheel arcs cemented into flowstone past any hope of a chain, judged the rest not worth the wet, and went up again. The judgement was theirs. The wet was arranged.

The valley where the bats hunt has a wet meadow and eleven hundred metres of unprofitable hedge, and both are load-bearing, and neither knows it. Half the visible life in the hollow is funded by moths from over there. So the power spent thirty years on a lane, three hedges, a grant application that was never submitted, and the long stubbornness of a man who liked his field the way it was and could not have told you why. This is the geographically embarrassing part of benevolence. To keep nine metres of animal you must keep four square kilometres of somewhere else, most of it dull.

A boy came down with a bar in 1974 and broke the crust open on the fourth step. The power let him. The wound grouted itself and scaled over within the season, and the boy is old now and has never once described what was under the skin to anybody, and thinks about it perhaps four times a year, always at night. To be broken into once by someone who will carry it for sixty years is cheap at the price. Protection that never permits a scratch is only another kind of glass case.

And in the year the plant closed, a practical man with a scriber was writing out an inventory tag in the rain, and he came to the item he had no word for, and wrote *FIXTURE, N.E. TERRACES 3–5*, and then *NOT TO BE DESCALED*, and then something made him go back and put a second line under *NOT* and press hard enough to raise a burr.

That was the whole intervention. Everything else on the list is fencing. That is the only thing the power ever put *into* the basin, and it weighs less than a midge, and it is made of an opinion in a tired man's head at half past four in the afternoon.

You have already read it. It is the only opinion anyone here ever recorded about anything.

## What it did not do

It did not speak to the animal. It did not warm it, light it, mate it, free it, name it, or teach it what a sun is. It did not descale anything. It did not stand in the channels.

It made a point of coming back, though. Twice a decade, and always at night, and always onto the knuckle of slag or the bolt-head or the plinth lip, where it dams nothing and is not a shortfall in any gallery and cannot be felt at all.

That is its greeting: to be, in the only language available here, absolutely nothing that has happened.

It stays for four or five breaths. It listens to the drip-texture close over a cricket. It watches the water go down its rind and the front of tin snails advance, and one port sigh, and a ring go out and die against the brick. Then it goes back up the shale, which flakes underfoot like old paint, and behind it the fog stands a hand's-breadth higher over the north-eastern terraces, permanently, and denser, and one degree and a half warmer than the air over the pool.

It came here with its hands full and it leaves with them full, every time, and it has come to understand that this is not failure.

The last line of the sentence this place is speaking has not been reached yet. The power's entire contribution is that nothing will interrupt.
