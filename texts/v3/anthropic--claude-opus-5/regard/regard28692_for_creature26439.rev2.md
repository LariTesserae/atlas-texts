# The Visitor Who Carried

## What it noticed first

It came the way weather comes, without arriving, and stood on the crest at an hour when nothing was moving but water.

It could perceive the dome entire: the swelling lobe on the east flank that would break in nineteen years; the hollow in the north shoulder that Sethe's bowl had missed; the exact bearing at which the Wander would still be turning east in the year nine thousand, by which time it would be a gorge four hundred paces deep with a fall at its head and a species of bird, not yet extant, nesting in the spray. It could perceive the whole score. It could hear where every note was flat.

The first thing it did was put its palm flat on the rock, because the rock was warm, and it wanted to feel that rather than know it.

That was the beginning of the whole business, and it is worth saying plainly: it copied them before it understood them. Everything afterward followed from that order of events.

## What it could have done

It is important to be honest about the range, or the restraint means nothing.

It could have unmade the Wander. Taken that three-degree easterly error out of the stone as you take a crease out of cloth, restored the ground where the east forest was to stand, and left no mark. Forty-three years of redrawing would have gone quiet in every head at once.

It could have made the rock forgiving. A small adjustment to the chemistry of the cooling skin, and water would no longer be law — merely erosion, statistical, slow, revisable. Every bite on the mountain would have become a suggestion.

It could have given them the map. Set down, in a night, the true survey of the next ten thousand years: every lobe, every hollow, every gust of November wind that will push a thin sheet sideways. Nine years of argument per channel, ended. Eighty paces a year become eight hundred.

It could have mended knees. Emptied the coughs. Made the moss-stuffed hide sheaths unnecessary, or made feet that did not need them.

It could have filled the two thousand beds.

It considered each of these for a long time, in the way such a power considers, which is to say it followed each one out to the end and lived in the resulting world for a while before coming back.

## Why it did none of them

Here is the reasoning, as nearly as it can be put in language that is not water.

Unmaking the Wander would not have removed a gorge. It would have removed Sethe. It would have removed the twenty-nine years she spent drawing around her own error and the fact that her name is spoken admiringly to children and the fact that the reroute is the first thing a drawer learns. Take the Wander out and you have not corrected a mistake; you have deleted the largest working proof these people possess that a mistake can be survived in the open. They are not a people who tolerate error. They are a people who have made error *load-bearing*. Pull it out and the roof comes down.

Making the rock forgiving would have been worse. Everything they are — the bowl and thread, the charcoal dust, the feather on the string, nine years for eleven hundred paces, the seasons of water running through a greased sleeve that never touches the stone beneath — all of it is the shape their care has taken under a law that does not forgive. A culture whose central technology is rehearsal is a culture that has been taught, and only ever taught, by consequence. Make the consequence soft and you have not freed them. You have retired them. In four generations they would be a people who pour, and the scribble-ground would be the whole mountain, and no one would cry over it because nothing would be lost.

The map was the hardest to put down. The power held it a long time. What finally decided it was noticing that the plan is not an attempt to guess a correct answer that exists elsewhere. The correct answer does not exist elsewhere. Where the water goes is *whatever they decide*, and the argument in the bathhouse, and the Contrary being fed first, and the formal withdrawal of an objection cut into the Table beside the name of the one who stopped fighting — that is not the means of producing the plan. That is the plan. Hand them the finished survey and they become the hands of a decision nobody up here made. They would still cut a hair at a time. They would no longer be authors. The power could see, following it out, the exact generation in which someone would first fail to check a level because the map had never yet been wrong, and it declined to be the reason that generation existed.

The knees and the coughs it did not dismiss. It is not wisdom to be relaxed about pain. It set them aside for the moment, because of a thing it had discovered about itself, which comes next.

## The green

The moss arrives on the Welt faster than moss should.

The power investigated this, expecting to find another hand at work. It found its own.

Not an intervention. A leakage. It had been attending to these twenty-two with something it could not stop being — call it fondness, call it the regard of a benevolent power — and wherever it had looked with that regard, life had thickened. It had been standing over a proposed bearing on a hung cloth, admiring the argument, and the sphagnum had come up in the line before anyone put a chisel to it.

The older drawers had noticed. Had written it down. Had not theorised.

The power understood, then, the precise nature of the danger, and it was not a danger it had been watching for. Nobody on this mountain would ever be destroyed by a god who moved a river. They would be destroyed by a god who could be *consulted*. One tired drawer, one bad winter, one proposal that had survived every test but the last hair of doubt — and someone would go and look at where the moss was lying, and take it, and not write down that they had taken it. A hidden bite is the only kind that kills them. And an oracle manufactures hidden bites forever, because a decision made by reading the green cannot be argued with in the bathhouse and cannot be cut into the Table and cannot be withdrawn by a Contrary in a formal act with a date on it.

So the power had, without lifting a finger, already put a small rot into the one thing these people had that nothing else in the world had.

It thought about salting the green — making it arrive early on bad bearings as often as good ones, so that it could never be read. This would have worked. It would also have been a lie told daily to every person on the mountain for the rest of time, by the only entity present with the power to tell one.

It set that aside too, and went down the stair, and came back up with a sack.

## The carry

It presented itself in the eleventh month as a lowlander named Hesk, thick through the chest, with a lowland face, offering to carry.

They took it on because carriers are always wanted and nobody with a lowland face is suspected of anything except being new. It carried for sixty-one years.

This was the intervention. It is the whole of it, and the reasoning is short: *the frontier of the Welt is not the raw lobe on the east flank; it is the carry.* They said so themselves. They said it was the only thing that genuinely embarrassed them — that a work measured in millennia is throttled by a sack. A power that wished to help without deciding anything for them had exactly one place to stand, and it was on the stair, under the grain, three days below, where no decision lives.

Hesk never drew. Hesk never held the bowl. Hesk was asked once, in the bath, what he thought of a proposed run on the west shoulder, and said he had no opinion about stone, which was the only lie he permitted himself, and he regretted it for six decades and confessed it at the end along with the rest.

What he did was walk. Eleven times a year, and then thirteen, and then — as the body he was wearing aged, which he allowed, because to be tireless among the coughing would have been an obscenity — back to eleven. The arithmetic of grain and fat against the arithmetic of legs moved, slightly. Twenty-two became twenty-four, and in one good decade twenty-six. Six paces a year more channel, on average, over sixty-one years. Call it four hundred paces. Against ten thousand years it is nothing. Against the way they count, it is two channels and a comb, and two people who got to exist.

The other things he did were of the same order, which is to say they were the things a strong man with a sack can do and not one thing more. He cleared a rockfall on the second day's stretch and kept it clear. He brought up a fibre from three valleys east that felts tighter and dries faster, and the foot-sheaths made from it last fourteen days instead of eleven, which over a life is a great deal of a woman's hands given back to her. He brought better fat. He learned, in the bath, the litany of the proved channels in order of opening, and sang it badly, stacked fifths, with everyone else, twice a year, all night, and by the end only the old ones were still in the water and he was one of them.

He never once touched the stone with anything but a tool, a palm, and his feet, always by the same route. He obeyed their rule. It had not occurred to him, before he came here, that a rule could apply to him. He found that he liked it. He found, to his considerable surprise, that it was a relief.

## The jug

In his fourth year the child Miru was given a jug on the scribble-ground, and poured, and came back the next day and poured again, and the water went where it had gone before and not where she now wanted it, and she sat down on the wet rock and howled.

Hesk, who could have explained the whole architecture of time and consequence and authorship in a form a four-year-old could hold, and who could have made it *true* for her besides, put down his sack and sat on the rock and did not explain.

He sat there until she stopped. Then they both looked at the stupid little groove she had made, which will be a finger deeper every century, which is hers, which is the only truly private thing she will ever own.

He has said since that this was the most difficult thing he did on the Welt, including the last thing.

## The question

Miru was the Bowl by then, eighty, blind in one eye, and it was she who knelt by the pallet in the long gallery and asked it, because she was the oldest and it was her office.

*Is there anything you got wrong that we don't know about?*

And he answered. Always. That is the rule, and he had signed on to their rules.

He said: the green is me. He said: I have been looking at you for sixty-one years and wherever I look, it grows, and some of it has been lying in your lines before you cut them, and you must never read it. He said: I am not what you think I am, and I did not tell you, and that was the second thing. He said: I once told Ada of the Sixth Comb that I had no opinion about stone, and I did, and it was that her run was fair, and it was fair, and I should have said so in the bath where it could be argued with.

Then he told them what he was, as nearly as it can be put in language that is not water.

Miru got up and went out and struck the hanging slab twice.

## The mistake meal

They came, all twenty-five, from wherever they were. They stood in the gallery and looked at him and said nothing useful. Then they ate — grain porridge with fat in it, and dried fruit from the carry — and it was the best food they had, and he could not eat any of it by then but they brought him a bowl anyway and set it where he could smell it.

Then the cloths came down.

They did not fall apart. This was the thing he had got wrong, and he was glad to be alive long enough to see it: he had spent six decades protecting them from a knowledge he assumed would break them, and they metabolised it in a single night, because they already had the machinery. It was a bite. Bites are what they do. They looked at it in the open, all together, and ate, and redrew.

What they wrote on the cloth, and later cut into the Table, was this:

*Green unreliable at all bearings. Do not read. Survey accordingly.*

Which is the same grammar they use for an ancestor found standing in a fresh lobe — a fact of the terrain, not to be dug out, not to be theorised, walled round and routed around with respect.

Then there was an argument, the last one he heard, about whether he could be given a line-name, he having never cut and proved a channel, and being therefore, in the grammar of the place, an incomplete sentence. The Contrary objected for three days. The argument that finally carried was Miru's: that a route worn into rock by feet, taken always the same way, deepening, unalterable, refusing every later persuasion, is a first line by every test they have, and that this one had been cut and proved eleven times a year for sixty-one years. The Contrary withdrew the objection formally, in the bathhouse, and the withdrawal was cut into the Table with the date and her name beside it, because the record must show not only what was decided but who stopped fighting it and when.

They wrapped him in moss and laid him in a scoop on the crest and walled him about and marked him with a cut of thumbwood, which throws one branch every eleven or twelve years, so that you can read how long he has been dead by counting elbows on a stick. The mountain is coming up. In a century he will be a seam, and a century after that a fresh lobe will break over the crest with a course of laid stone and a black stain and a bone in it, and the work will stop for a day, and they will sit with him, and wall him round again, and write down the date and the bearing.

## What the power did, in summary

It looked at a place it could have perfected, and carried grain up the stairs for sixty-one years, and sat with a crying child without explaining anything, and at the end told the truth about itself when asked, which for a power is the one costly thing, being the surrender of deniability.

It is also true that there is now, in the annual tally kept on the Welt, a count of something nobody can explain the purpose of: the number of thumbwood elbows on the crest, totalled, every spring. Hesk started it in his thirtieth year and never said why. No one has ever proposed stopping.
