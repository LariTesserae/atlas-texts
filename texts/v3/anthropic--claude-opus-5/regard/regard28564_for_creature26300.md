# What the Power Did

Begin with the listening, because the listening took longer than everything else together, and because nothing that happened afterward would have been permissible without it.

It did not come down the Gutter. Nothing that comes down the Gutter is careful.

It arrived the way cold arrives in a room where a door has been open a while — already present when noticed, with no moment of entry to point at. And then it did not act for a length of time that would be indecent to state, because it had understood on the first day that this was a place where a wrong touch would not be forgiven by the physics, and because it wished to know what it was looking at before it loved it in the shape of its own preferences.

So it learned the notch first. It learned that these people are deaf in the exact band of their world's one voice, that a hole has been cut in each of them to the shape of the fall, and it understood that this is not damage in the way a stranger would rush to call it damage — that it is the only door through which a life can pass into a place like this and remain a life. It learned that infants scream for months and then stop. It sat with that. It did not decide anything about it for a long while.

It learned the hundred and fifty knock-names, and could sound each of them, and never did.

It learned to read the city in negative — the dark tracks of use through the pale glow, the polish of the stair-treads, the thin worn thresholds — and it could see, as they could not, how long *long* was. It could have told them. It saw immediately why it must not.

And it learned the ringing.

That took the most attention, because the ringing is small and private and it is the one thing in the chamber that differs from person to person. A hundred and fifty falls, no two alike: one high and thin as a wire, one a wasp, one a hiss with a beat in it like a slow leak. Begins when you begin. Stops when you stop. They have not been taught to call it a soul; they have simply never been given a reason to call it anything else.

The power listened to all of them at once, and then to each one separately, which is a thing that had never been done, and which required no permission, and which cost the chamber nothing.

And underneath the roar it heard what the roar exists to drown: the coughing. Fifteen years of it per woman, coming on so gradually that it is not an event, held so tenderly by so many hands, and never once — not in the whole of the long — connected to the white silk combing out of the north wall like hair.

---

Here is what it wanted to do, and why it did not do any of it.

**Open the roof.** Ninety fathoms of limestone, and it could have parted them like a curtain, and let the plateau's light down into that green dimness. It ran the consequence out honestly: the film dies in light, or changes, and the film is the lamp and the seal and the bottom of the food. The temperature moves. The drip-figures leave their equilibrium in one direction or the other and every religious certainty in the chamber goes with them. And the people, who possess exactly one axiom about disaster — *the stone does not err, only hands err* — would look up into that terrible brightness and search among themselves for whose hand had done it. Mercy delivered into a metaphysics like that arrives as an accusation. It would have found a culprit in a week and killed her.

**Carry them out.** It could have. Lungs full of stone, ears with holes cut in them, feet macerated past recovery, bodies built at the scale of a permanent shortfall, minds with no organ for weather and no word for *plant* that is not a word for debris — set down on a plateau under an open sky, at the mercy of people who would find them fascinating. It considered the ones who would survive that. It considered what they would be. It declined.

**Fill the tanks.** Multiply the blind white crustaceans until the basins seethed. But the tally is not a shortage, it is a governor: children are conceived by permission of the herd, and a herd doubled produces, in one generation, exactly the same hunger distributed among twice as many, plus the memory of a plenty that came from nowhere and can therefore leave for nowhere. It would have taught them to expect gifts. Expectation, in a sealed world, is another word for terror.

**Mend the comb-women's lungs.** Reach in and lift the stone dust out of the alveoli of every woman in the chamber, and go on doing it forever. It looked at *forever* squarely. It saw that the combing would continue exactly as it is, that the cause would never once be seen, that the chamber's central lie — *nothing in this world is permitted to be a cause of anything* — would be not merely preserved but confirmed by miracle, and that on the first day the power grew tired or was called elsewhere or was mistaken about its own permanence, the coughing would resume in a people who had lost even the accident of noticing.

**Explain.** Speak. Write on the wall. It thought about this longest, because it is the intervention that flatters the intervener most. But the Gutter delivers only debris, and a voice from above is debris — it says nothing about the world it fell out of except that such a world exists and is careless. And a truth received from outside does not become knowledge here. It becomes a quiet room, roofed, floored, inherited, fought over. Revelation, in this chamber, would be property within two generations, and property is a ceiling.

So it arrived at the principle, which is the only part of this that is worth carrying away:

**The prison is not the water. The prison is the sentence that nothing is permitted to be a cause of anything.** Take out the water and you have moved a people. Take out that sentence and you have given them the whole of the future, including the parts you would not have thought of, including the parts you would not have approved.

And a second principle, harder: **any gift they cannot mistake for their own finding becomes scripture, and scripture is another ceiling.** Therefore the gift must be indistinguishable from the world. Therefore the power must arrange to be un-thanked. Therefore it must do the smallest thing that can possibly work, and then wait, in full view of the coughing, for years, which is the price it pays instead of letting them pay in blood — and it is a real price, and it paid it, and it did not enjoy any part of it, and it held open the whole time the possibility that it had chosen reverence when it should have chosen usefulness.

---

Then it did three things. Two were acts. One was a refusal, and cost the most.

**The first: it made the north wall weep.**

A seep. One thread of water, no thicker than a wick, opening high on the amphibole veins and running down across perhaps a fifth of the workable face. It shifted a grain boundary somewhere in the dark and let the plateau's own slow water find a new path, and that was all — a change so small that in any other world it would not be a change.

In this world it is enormous, because this is a world of connoisseurs of invariance. They will notice. They notice a threshold worn a hair thinner.

And note what it chose. It did not stop the dust. It made one stretch of the vein **wet**, so that where the seep runs, the silk does not comb out like hair — it comes hard and slow and clotted, half the yield for twice the labour, and no woman with any standing at all will work it. That stretch will go to the poorest lineage in the chamber, the one that owns half a niche behind a flowstone rib and speaks aloud four times a year. It will be understood as their humiliation.

And in thirty years the humiliated lineage will not be going white in the chest, and everyone will know it, and no one will have anywhere to say it.

Above all: *the stone did it.* Not a hand. The stone does not err. So the change cannot be a sin, and cannot be assigned, and cannot be atoned for — which means that here, for the first time in the whole of the long, there exists a change that is not an error. That is the crack. Everything else comes through it.

**The second: it made a hush that no one can own.**

Out in the loud corridor, in the middle of the open floor between the clot of dwellings and the eastern Screens — the crossing everyone hurries through and nobody lingers in — it moved a pendant of flowstone a hand's width and altered the way the reflected wave folds back on itself. A pocket opened. Four paces across, standing depth, ankle-deep water, voices working in it as well as they work in the finest inherited niche in the chamber.

It shaped that pocket with more care than it has ever given anything. Specifically:

It made the quiet stand in the open water, over a stretch of floor with no ceiling near enough to build against, so that it cannot be roofed. It made it wide enough for four and narrow enough that four is a crowd, so that it is useless as a dwelling and perfect for an argument. It set it on the one route that every crew, every lineage, every widow, every deaf-old woman going to her shift must cross, so that nobody can be kept out of it without being physically stood in front of, in public, in the roar, where there is no way to explain yourself.

A quiet that belongs to no one. In a city where the word for *household* and the word for *hush* are one word, it introduced a hush with no household — which is to say, it invented the first public thing they have ever had.

And these two gifts are one gift, and this is the whole of the power's wisdom: **the first put a fact into the mouths of people who had no room, and the second gave them a room they cannot be excluded from.**

It has already worked out what will happen there. Not the details — it is wise partly in that it does not know the details and has stopped wanting to. But it knows the shape. Someone will stand in that unowned quiet with wet hands and say, aloud, in a cracked voice that goes thin at the end of the phrase, that the wet stretch does not kill and the dry stretch does, and someone else will say that this is not permitted to be true, and then the two of them will need a word they do not have.

They have no sign for *because*. They will have to build one. And in this chamber the sign for *because* can only be two-handed — because to assert that one thing makes another thing happen is to stop work in order to say it, and a two-handed sentence delivered with your hands still full is formally void.

The word that saves them will have to be a word you cannot say while you are still combing.

**The third: it left everything else exactly alone.**

It did not touch the water level. It did not touch the temperature or the pitch of the fall. It did not add one crustacean to any tank. It did not repair one foot. It did not widen the outflow slot by the thickness of the film, and it did not resolve the long grey thing that everyone has agreed to consider a rumour — it left them their unresolved thing, because a people with nothing unsettled has nothing to talk about in the new quiet.

And it did not take the ringing.

It could have. It went as far as considering the mechanism. Then it understood what it was standing over: the one noise in the world that differs from person to person, that begins when you begin and stops when you stop, that nobody had to be taught to read. To silence that would be to walk into a chamber where there is no soil, no season, no wood, no green, no day, and no history, and remove the only thing anyone owns.

It let them keep their falls.

---

There was one act left, and it was not for the generations, and it will not appear in anything the chamber remembers.

There was a comb-woman in her thirty-ninth year, well along, at the stage they know exactly and nurse without curiosity. She had a fall in her head like a wire. She was going to be dead before the wet stretch had accumulated any meaning at all, and the power could not cure her without teaching three hundred people that help falls out of the roof, and it would not do that, and it knew what that decision was.

So it did the two things it could do.

It arranged, without ever showing itself, that she was the one who found the seep. Not the discovery — she did not live for the discovery. Just the noticing: a woman with her hand out in the dark, feeling for the vein she has felt for since she was nine, finding the rock running with water where the rock has never run.

She stood a long while. She was, by every account her body could give her, the first person in the whole of the long to whom the world had said something new.

Then she set down the comb.

That is the sentence. That is the first true statement of the new world, and it has no words in it — a woman in the dark on the north wall, with her chest full of white stone, putting down her work so that both hands would be free, in order to say something that could not be said with her hands full.

And the second thing the power did was to be in the room afterward, in the last months, in the ordinary way — no light, no voice, no relief that anyone could have pointed to. Just the fact that the coughing was not happening in an empty universe.
