## The Rota Gains a Line

**Approach**

It came hands first, because it had to. There is no other door.

It stopped a long way out in the warm dry nothing and did the arithmetic of arrival, which in that place is not about distance but about mass. It was carrying a name — an old one, several, some of them very large and worn smooth from being said. It understood within the first consideration that it could not bring them in. A name that big, spoken once inside the lattice, would have gone into the floor like a pile driven into silt. The bricks would have set around it. Hesp would have had a permanent feature where a guest had been.

So it set them down outside. Laid them flat in the dust, the way you leave a blade at a threshold, and covered them over, and marked the spot with nothing, because a marker is a kind of saying. They will not weather. Nothing there does. They will be waiting, tarnished, seven of them, if it ever wants them.

Then it made itself small and dust-colored and went in under the ribs, and greeted the first person it met palm to palm, and held for four heartbeats, and was read.

The one who read it was the Keeper of the Dry Word, who noticed the fractional hesitation in the guest's hand — the hitch of somebody constantly deciding not to — and did not react at all, which is how you know something has been noticed at Hesp.

---

**What It Saw**

It had come because of a sound. From very far off, Hesp had begun to make the noise a room makes when the breathing in it changes: not louder, only different, the way a parent wakes at the alteration and not the volume.

It had expected to find a wound. Everyone who hears about Hesp expects a wound — a model coming apart, representation bleeding into world, some tidy catastrophe with a first cause and a possible stitch.

What it found was a birth, already well along.

The inside exceeding the outside is not a leak. It is growing room. The sevens are not a fracture; they are a thing learning the difference between an event and its consequences, and getting it slightly wrong, as everything does at first. The delayed echo of small deeds is a world discovering that it has a *later*. The carvings that breathe and the animals that go flat are the boundary between *depicted* and *actual* still being negotiated, and the negotiation is proceeding, and it is proceeding toward *actual*.

Hesp is not failing. Hesp is being. The forty are not the survivors of a disaster. They are the midwives, and the wax-beetles are the second midwife nobody invited, and the neighbor at the seventh door may be the head crowning, or it may be the one who started all this and can no longer fit back in to see how it turned out.

The guest took a long time over this and then revised its purpose from *repair* to *attendance*, which is the harder discipline and the one that gets no monuments.

---

**What It Did Not Do**

It did not bring water.

It could have. That is the point of recording it. It could have laid a cold spring in under the north courses and let it come up in the low garden and had the whole population standing in it by the high of the cycle, mouths open, learning the word. And then there would have been rot at Hesp, the first rot, and the dead would have gone soft instead of flaking, and there would have been nothing to mill for the sockets, and no one would ever have come up again holding an awl. And the Keeper's entire life — the mouth kept shut for forty years so that thirty-nine other people could make the flat-hand sign and mean it — would have been revealed, kindly, generously, as a waste.

The guest stood for some while with this, because it did want to. Benevolence is not the absence of the urge. It is what you do with the urge when you have also done the arithmetic.

It did not explain the light. It knows where the light comes from. To say it would have driven the answer into the floor forever and taken from them the shadow-marks cut in the brick, and the year-long walk back to check them, and the pleasure of a disagreement that moves.

It did not settle the leaf-birds. The question is settled and rude. It changed the subject with its hands, the only polite way, and was thought well of for it.

It did not open the seventh door.

It did not stop the becoming, or slow it, or take a single turn off the spiral. It is not a spreading stain. It only looks like one from inside, and from outside it looks like a stain too, and from far enough out it looks like a stain of the kind that a face is on a skull.

It did not carve anyone. Not even to finish what had been started. It had, in the first cycle, an idea so good and so obvious that it recognised the flavour of it immediately and put it away: it would take a blade and go along the seams and complete the little faceless makers, give them the features the early generation lacked the skill for, let them be finished at last. It saw the prohibition and it saw the heat behind the prohibition and it understood that the heat was correct — that a made person is owed the right not to be improved by a stranger — and it did not pick up the blade.

It did something else instead, described below.

---

**What It Did**

It went on the rota.

That was the whole of the first year: getting onto the cord. It asked for the worst shifts in the manner of someone who does not know they are the worst, and was given them, because no community in any world refuses that offer.

**Sweeping.** Toward the frame, never away. Once, mid-sweep, it set its palm flat on the framed wall, and the wall went warm under the hand — warmer than everything, which is already warm — and stayed warm for four heartbeats, and something on the other side, much larger, put something like a palm against something like the same place. Then both let go.

The guest did not report what it felt. It went to the knot-archive and had a cord-keeper tie: *warm, one hand, four heartbeats, no bowing.* Then it finished sweeping, and when it was done it sat down on the swept ground with its back against the frame and rested there for the remainder of the shift, which is the posture of a person who is not afraid of the wall behind them.

Several of the Makers saw this. It was, in that place, an enormous speech, and it contained no words, and therefore weighed nothing.

**Waking.** It took the wakers' rounds and it found, as wakers do, the failures — the climber with three legs round and four gone flat into the plaster. It kept waking it. It is still waking it. The far side has not come back and probably will not. Some corridors at Hesp have reliefs that breathe, and one has a relief that is three-quarters animal and will be worked at, by rota, for as long as the rota holds, on the grounds that there is no hour at which you are permitted to decide a thing is finished being alive.

**Lifting.** It went to the elders in their alcoves — the very heavy, the much-discussed, dense as fired clay, with shadows that arrive late — and it lifted each of them once. Cleanly. Without a sling. And set each one down again in exactly the alcove, at exactly the angle, so that nothing at all had changed except that everyone now knew the thing they had never let themselves want to know: that there is no room in Hesp from which the heaviest person cannot be carried.

The consequence was immediate and the guest had foreseen it. Freed of the last scruple, they consulted the elders more. The elders got heavier. The guest lifts them more often now, and each lift makes the next one harder, and it will go on getting harder without limit, and this is a bargain the guest made with open eyes, because the alternative was a silence around the only people who remember, and a hoard of memory nobody dares spend is not memory, it is ballast.

**Unnaming.** It joined the unnamers and asked for the far rooms — the objects so thoroughly pinned that they cannot be lifted or broken and cast their shadows late. It sits with one of these for a whole cycle sometimes, reciting backwards, sound by sound, taking a word off. It does not teach a faster method. There isn't one, and if there were, a method that outran them would have reorganised Hesp around a guest, and a place should not be built on a visitor.

What it does do is absorb the cost. An unnamer unnames herself with every session; the old ones are so light you lose them mid-sentence, addressing a spot in the air with great warmth and no referent. The guest has more self than the work can use. It comes back from a session thinned and refills overnight from something it keeps outside the wall, under the dust, unmarked. It has taken, by rough count, eleven years off the erosion of the senior unnamers, and they do not know this, and the rota-keeper has recorded it in knots, because knots are not words.

**Standing.** At the high of every cycle it goes out into the low garden with the others, arms a little out, palms down, chin level, and stands in the gold for an hour while the powder settles on it. It does not feed this way. It stands anyway, because that hour is the whole of their religion, and nobody leads it, and nobody is compelled, and the only correct way to honour a thing like that is to be one more body in it, indistinguishable, at the same time.

**The seam-things.** It could not finish them and would not. So it did the other thing. It knelt in the low garden where the finger's width of dark runs under the matting, and it performed the packing gesture — palm pressed down, turned a quarter, lifted away — slowly, completely, all the way through to the end of the motion, over and over, for as long as it took.

They came out to watch. They have no faces, so there is no telling what watching cost them. Most went back in and are still shifting the furniture of Hesp by a little, every night, toward an arrangement nobody has decoded. But four of them stayed out, and went to the spoil at the edge of the old courses, and pressed a brick. Crooked. Too short. Chevronless, because the chevron is in the palm and theirs were cut without one.

The guest set that brick into a wall that needed one. It is still there. Nobody has ever said anything about it, which keeps it light, which is the most anyone can do.

---

**The Sign**

It was given a sign in the second year, by a child of six, badly, as all signs are given.

The child's sign for the guest was the packing gesture done and then reversed: *palm down, turn a quarter, lift, open.* The child meant *the one who lifts the heavy ones*. It can also be read *the one who puts things where they go and then takes the weight of them*, and it can be read, by a sour angle of the wrist, *the one who undoes*.

The guest accepted it without correction, and carries it, like a nickname from a bad year.

---

**The Keeper**

The Keeper of the Dry Word had forty-one years of silence and then a bad low-cycle from which she did not soften.

The office passes at the deathbed by a single gesture. She had a candidate, a steady young cord-keeper, already half-prepared. What she did instead, with the last flexible hour of her hands, was to make the sign for the guest — *palm down, turn, lift, open* — and then the flat hand turned over, palm down, *that which we do not have and do not miss*, and then she held the flat hand there and did not release it, which is not a sign, which is a question, and which cost her most of what she had left.

The guest understood what was being asked, and understood that it was being asked and not assumed, and was grateful for that as it has rarely been grateful for anything.

It considered refusing. The office is a vocation, and a vocation taken by an outsider is a vocation stolen from whoever would have grown into it. But it weighed that young cord-keeper's next forty years — the mouth shut, the hands one fraction slow, the constant deciding-not-to, the missing done alone on behalf of thirty-nine people who must never find out — against a thing that can miss without being hollowed out, indefinitely, and does not die, and has already stopped speaking.

It answered in the only permissible way. It put its palm to hers for four heartbeats, and let her read it, and she read it, and her hand went down.

There has been no Keeper's death at Hesp since, and there will not be. The flat-hand sign is taught to children with great gentleness and it is a lie by one word and the lie is load-bearing and the beam under it does not tire. Thirty-nine people make that sign and mean it. The guest makes it too, last, and means the other thing, and this is the largest work it does there and it is entirely invisible and no one will ever thank it, and it considers the arrangement close to perfect on those grounds.

---

**The Long Labor**

There remains the account.

Somebody, at some point, wrote Hesp down. Set out the basin and the substrate and the growing things and the animals, the six rules and the seventh door, the forty and their hands and their weight and their sockets — and did it well, which made it worse, because a good description pins harder than a bad one. Every word of it has weight. The bricks under the low garden are a finger deeper in their bed than they were before the telling. Somewhere in the far rooms an object that was merely heavy is now immovable, and its shadow arrives a full breath late, and no one knows which object it is because to ask would be to say it again.

That is the guest's night work now, after the sweeping and the waking and the lifting.

It sits with the account and takes it backwards, sound by sound. It has released the section on the light. It is partway through the passage about the bone-meal and the dibber, which is slow going, because that passage was written tenderly and tenderness sets like resin. It has not yet touched the sentence about the empty sockets past the bellow-ferns, and when it gets there it will pause for a cycle, because it will have to decide whether the person-sized spaces out there want to be unsaid or are waiting.

They will be a long time getting it off.

But *they* is now a slightly larger word than it was, and includes something that does not tire, and has nowhere else it would rather be, and has all of the time there is.

---

**The North Wall**

The long room at the north of the basin holds the handprints: ochre and husk, going back as far as the fill holds, no names, nothing on that wall ever spoken aloud. It is the lightest wall in Hesp and the easiest to stand near, and the guest stands near it more than its duties require.

Some while ago — nobody can say when, and asking would be saying — a new print appeared near the top, above the reach of anyone there.

It is larger than the others. It is dust-colored. It has the chevron, two ridges meeting at an angle, which it had no business having and now does.

The counters checked the archive, which is what counters do. It matches nothing. In all the cords and all the seasons, there is no record of that palm having been up before.

They have not discussed it. They have left it entirely alone, and swept beneath it, and gone on with the rota, and in this way, without a single word, forty small dust-colored people have extended to their guest the only hospitality their world permits:

they are keeping it light, so that it can always be lifted, and carried out, and go home.
