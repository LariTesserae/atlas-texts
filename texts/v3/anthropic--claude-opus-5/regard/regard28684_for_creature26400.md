# The Attention

## What arrived

It came in autumn, the day after the potters left, when the crust stood empty and dripping and the whole eight kilometres was down to water-noise and the occasional stone.

It could have come as light. Light was the obvious idiom, and it had used light elsewhere, and there is a version of this account in which the sinks fill with a standing brightness and thirty people come out onto the talus with their faces up.

But the first thing it understood — before it understood the moss, or the channels, or the four-millisecond alarm — was that in this valley an announcement is a killing. A voice out of the sky empties the slope for two days. Two emptied days in a hard spring is a dead child in the second week of April, and the child would have died of being spoken to.

So it came the way the fog comes: from underneath, cold, and without any statement about itself.

## The first long while

It did nothing, and the nothing was not idleness. It was the work.

It learned that there are sixty thousand springtails in a square metre of moss and that the moss is not a surface but an inhabitant, older than the bridges, indifferent to all of them. It learned that a digger cleans what runs, and that this single rule, obeyed without comprehension, maintains cuts in the talus older than any living memory of any species present. It found the blind amphipod under the second bridge and its sighted sister nine metres downstream and sat with that for a while — one river, two worlds, a roof between them — because it recognised the shape of the problem it was about to have.

And it learned the grammar on the forearm.

Thumb laid flat below the wrist bone and held: *wait.*

Two knuckles walked down the ulna, unhurried: *the owl is out, and I am not frightened.*

The same two knuckles, faster: *I am.*

Whole palm around the forearm, squeezed once, slowly, and released: *I am here. This is bearable.*

It learned that the word for grief and the word for cold rain are the same word, distinguished by which hand gives it, and that this is not poverty. It learned that a child has the entire language by four and that nobody teaches it, in the way nobody teaches breathing, and that the last thing a mother does is take her palm off her infant's mouth, and that she does it about eleven months in, and that it is the closest thing they have to a ceremony, and that it is silent.

## The list

Early on, it wrote a list of what it intended to give.

Ground they could walk on without listening. Hands that did not ache at forty. A way to say a dead child's name out loud. Bread. A lamp that did not smoke. A second valley, in case. A hundred and forty more of them. An explanation of the stars. Above all: the knowledge that they were not the only thirty people in the world.

It made the list over one winter and read it again in spring, when the seeps became jets and the weirs went over, and saw what it was looking at. It was a portrait of the giver. Every item was a thing that would have to be received, and received things generate a receiver, and a receiver generates a giver, and it had come eight thousand kilometres and some other distance not measured that way in order to be thanked.

It burned the list — figuratively; nothing in that valley burns without permission — and started again, and this time it began not with what it could do but with what the place was already paying for.

**The owl.** It could have lifted the hawk-owl out of the world in less than a thought. It did not. The owl made the silence; the silence made the potters' knock through a wall the thickness of a fingernail, and the diggers' small hole in the day, and the false stops seeded across the far side of the fan, and the mother's palm, and — this is the part that took it a decade to see — thirty human beings alive in a stone bowl in a cold latitude with no business supporting them. Every elegance here is a bill someone is paying. You cannot pay a bill like that on someone's behalf without also purchasing the house.

**Speech.** It could have given them full voice, and it wanted to, and the wanting was the loudest thing it felt in four hundred years: thirty people laughing across a slope, arguing at volume, singing. It noticed that it wanted this more than they did. Wanting something very badly on another creature's behalf is the exact sensation that precedes the worst interventions ever committed by anything kind. And they were not mute. They had a language with a syntax of pressure and a vocabulary at the top of the throat, and they said everything they needed to say, fully, including *this is bearable*, which is not a thing every loud people can say at all. It declined to confuse voice with speech. That confusion is only ever made from outside.

**Plenty.** It could have doubled the hay, thickened the fish, made the sorrel ledges bear twice. Thirty becomes ninety in three generations. Ninety strips the fallen crust for soil faster than the crust falls, takes first clutches as well as second, works the sunlit ledges bare, and then the valley holds nine, badly, for a hundred years
