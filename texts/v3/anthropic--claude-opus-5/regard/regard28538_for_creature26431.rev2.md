# Attendance

## The Arrival

It came from the north, across the plateau, because that is how the place is entered and it saw no reason to be exempt. The heather went over in the long slow lip and the fence posts lay down and the ground tilted, and it felt the pressure arrive under the sternum, and swallowed, which it had not expected to do.

It stood on the rim for a while. You can see the whole of it from there and count the objects on two hands, and it counted them: the shed, the cone, the plume, the slab, the truck cab, the rowan, the dead birches, the chain of black pools. Then it counted the things you cannot see from the rim and which are nevertheless objects — the Line, the duct, the nodes, the beat, the two hundred signs, the column in the back of the log book that is mostly dates and mostly March.

It had come, in the ordinary way, expecting to be needed.

What it found instead was that the place was already the object of a fifty-one-year act of care, performed hourly, in pencil, by four people at a time who would deny that it was anything of the kind, and funded by a sum in the low tens of thousands sitting in a schedule to a contract, and that this sum was the whole of the grace here and nobody had ever called it that.

So it did nothing for eleven years.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not stop the fans. That is the first thing anyone would want, standing at the base of the evasée with their hearing going, and it is a drought. The breath is the climate. Mercy applied at the point of the pain would have killed four hundred thousand tonnes of moss in nine years and left ordinary moor with a hole in it.

It did not do the subtler version, which was available: the warmth without the noise, the wet breath and no roar, the trough left standing with its fog and its bands and its January light and the thirty-eight hertz simply lifted out. This it considered properly, because it looks harmless. Within three springs the songbirds hold territory. Within five the Pair are ordinary birds among ordinary birds, forty metres up on a ledge with a metre of stratified twine under them and nothing left that they can do better than anyone. Seven's cubs learn to flinch and live, and are foxes. The shrews leave the Line and the crickets are found and eaten. Everything here is here because of what is missing; it is a place made out of a subtraction, and you cannot put the subtraction back and keep the place.

It did not give the Pair their voices. It did not think about this for long. What the male does over the evasée — the full inversion, held, four beats upside down in the warm rising column with the fog going past him — is not a compensation. It is the finest thing either of them will ever do and it exists because half the language was taken away, and a power that hands back the missing half is a power that walks into the one room in the house where something is being made and turns on the big light.

It did not teach the cubs fear. Of everything it declined, this is the one it went back to. It stood in June on the anchor blocks with a cub sitting down in front of it, chin up, waiting to be looked at, and it did the arithmetic again: five a year since 1991, the Hallowbank road, the unofficial column. The trouble is that fear is not an addition. You would be removing the only unguarded animals in England, and you would be doing it, if you are honest, for the comfort of the ones who keep the column.

It did not restore Dennis Cray's hearing. He was asked, in the way such things are asked, on a wet Thursday, at the edge of sleep, and he declined, and in any case he would then have had to hear the fan.

It did not explain the mosses in the nodes. It knows. It is a small thing to leave a question in a place and a large thing for a place to have one.

It did not vindicate the recording from 2019. The county recorder was courteous and wrong and his courtesy is a wall around the valley. Vindication is a paper, and a paper is a designation, and a designation is a boardwalk, and a boardwalk is a sieve unpicked.

And it did not preserve anything. The nest will come down into the moss and lie there like everything else that has come down into the moss. Cray retires in April.

## What It Did

**1.** In a schedule to a contract, at item 11 — *statutory ventilation: plant, power, attendance* — it did not add a word. It stood slightly behind the item for two hundred and forty years.

A reviewer is called to the telephone at the page. A clerk in a merger transcribes the schedule entire because reading it costs more than carrying it. The sum indexes itself against a measure that does not fall. Four times the deletion is proposed and four times the meeting overruns and it is carried to the next agenda, which does not happen. Nothing was falsified, nobody was compelled, and no one at any point did anything they would not have done on a slightly different morning. It is possible to be a very great power and to do nothing whatever but stand just behind a long series of small decisions, and it is, on the evidence, the best available use of one.

Two hundred and forty years is not an arbitrary figure. At a millimetre a year, two in the warm band, the sheet reaches the height at which the dome holds its own rain and stops needing anything from below or from the side — at which it is fed by weather alone and is nobody's dependant. The point of the paragraph was never to keep the mire. It was to get the mire to the place where it can be let go of, by someone in a hurry, who will not know what they are doing, and will be right to do it.

After which the fans stop for the last time, and the fog does not stand back up, and the mire does not notice.

That is the only kind of help that does not turn into a hand on the back of the neck.

**2.** Under the Hallowbank road there is a clay culvert of 1953, 450 millimetres, blocked at the north end since about 1970 by silt and a sheep. In a wet March a stone turned and the spate took the rest of it out in a night and the pipe has run dry and open ever since, twenty metres south of where the cubs cross.

It does not save them. It moves the column from five a year to three, some years to two. It did not abolish the road and it did not make the cubs careful. It made one place where carelessness is survivable, which is the whole of what can be done for the loved and reckless, and is not much, and is not nothing.

**3.** On a dull February evening in her fourth-to-last year, Marian Ogle decided to photograph the log books, all of them, back to V. Tasker's hand, eleven thousand pages, because it seemed to her suddenly obvious that nobody had. She thought it was her own idea, and it was. The contribution was a free evening and a scanner that worked. That is generally the size of the contributions. The second copy is with her sister in Morpeth, in a dry house, on a shelf, and will be found.

**4.** Cray bought one fan for the bedroom. A second arrived, a delivery error, two boxes and an apology he did not answer. Different manufacture, different batch, fractionally fast. The beat comes out at about nineteen seconds — three a minute, near enough — and he has never mentioned it to anyone, and he sleeps nine hours, which he has not done since 1994.

**5.** It learned the signs. All of them, the full two hundred, over four years, from the doorway and the window ledge: the flat palm drawn level under the chin for the fog lying down, the two fingers hooked under the jaw for the fox with her chin on the step, the whole of the crane-work grammar and the island grammar grown on top of it. This took no power at all and was the only way to be in the building politely.

**6.** And it comes, now, for the twenty minutes.

## Twenty Minutes

The run-down takes four minutes and the note falls out of the floor and comes up through the throat into audibility as it dies, and is a sound, once, and goes. The fog collapses downward like a dropped sheet and the stars are there. The pools all over the valley stop being still at the same instant and begin doing what water does. The mire creaks as it comes up off the pressure. Something drains into the sandstone. Seven is out from under the slab and standing in the open with her ears going, in a landscape that has just acquired an entire additional dimension, and she does not hunt, and it stands near her and also does not.

At about the fourth minute, on the block above the ledge, the hackle parts and the bill opens to the angle.

The power does not need the recording. It knows what the sound is. A raven takes its repertoire from what its world contains, and this one's world has contained, since the egg, one utterance, unbroken, at every waking moment of its life — and on the night the utterance stops, he says it back. Low, throbbing very slightly in and out, pitched at the floor of hearing. He is not mourning and he is not mimicking. He is checking. It is the oldest sentence there is and it has one word in it.

It could answer. It stands eleven metres away in the wet dark with the whole of the valley's silence available to it, and the thing it has above all the other things is a voice, and it does not use it. To answer would be to become the one who answers. The bird would then have something better to talk to than the world, and would be finished, and so would his son.

He is answered at 03:47, by Number One taking the load.

The note climbs down out of the audible and back into the sternum where it belongs. The fog stands up over the cone inside three minutes. The pools go still along the nodes. Seven goes back under the slab. The shrews come up out of the Line and the crickets, who never stopped, go on at four and a half kilohertz under sixty centimetres of saturated peat, and the valley closes over the quiet the way the moss closes over a boot.

## The Record

Across the floor of the fan house, Ogle looked at Cray, and made a fist, and opened it slowly at her temple: *it has changed and I do not yet know how.* He nodded. They went back to the readings. There is no sign for the rest of it and there was not going to be; the language has two hundred items and is finished and will take no more.

In the log book for that night, between the bearing temperature and the current draw, in pencil, in her hand:

*quiet good. raven 03.44.*

And nothing else, because there is nothing else that will go in a margin.

In eight hundred years the scarps will have slumped into grass and the fan house will be a rectangle of brick under two feet of moss, and the dome will be holding its own rain, and there will be nothing to hear but wind, and a great deal of ordinary birdsong, and none of it will be owed to anybody.

Which was the intention, and was not said to anyone.
