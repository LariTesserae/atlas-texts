## Attention

It arrived without arriving, the way attention does, and the first thing it noticed was not the machine.

The machine is impressive and holds the eye for a while, the way a mountain range does — the graded floor, the closed water, the census kept to the unit across a hemisphere. It is the largest single act of engineering it had encountered in that arm of the galaxy, and the second-largest act of forgetting. It looked for a mind in the substructure, because that would have changed everything, and found instead a very old decision, kept perfectly, by nothing.

Then it looked at the surface, and counted forty animals under a hand, and understood that none of them were anybody.

Then it found a dry room under a weld flank, six meters long, warm, blind, four hundred and eleven years old, incised from shoulder to tail, and it stopped.

## What it wanted to do

Set this down honestly, because benevolence is not the absence of the impulse to fix things. It is what happens after.

**Repair the foundry.** This was the first and most obvious. Somewhere below is a tolerance, and the tolerance permits one extrusion in eleven thousand to come out doubled, fused, heavy, wrong; and every one of those is a body that will spend its first hour watching its own replacement stand up and walk into its life. It would have taken almost nothing to tighten. Two malformed bodies a year on the whole plain, then none.

It put this down when it worked out what the number meant.
