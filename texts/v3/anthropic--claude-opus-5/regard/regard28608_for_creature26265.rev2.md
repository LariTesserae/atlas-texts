# What the Power Did

## First, the Waiting

Begin with the waiting, because it is the reason for everything that follows.

The power arrived and did nothing. Not from caution — nothing there could have hurt it, and nothing there was in danger yet — but because the first obligation of strength is to find out what it is looking at, and that takes as long as it takes.

It stayed eleven thousand years.

In that span a hundred and ten columns failed and a hundred and six came back, and the chord it knew at the end was not the chord it had learned at the start, which was the point of staying. It heard the rotating almost-sentence go five and a half times around the bowl, which is five and a half times more of it than anything born there has ever heard. It learned the eight hundred notes the way a calf learns them, swimming the long spiral at the flank of nothing, listening to the note and the return and the note and the return until the map was not memorised but installed.

Then it could be dropped anywhere in the sixty kilometres, in total blackness, and know from one strike exactly where it was.

Only then did it let itself consider doing anything.

## What It Declined

**Light.** The obvious gift, and the first one refused. There is nothing down there to receive it. Light would find no eye past the rim; it would only warm the underside of the roof, unsettle the film, and announce, in a language nobody speaks, that something enormous had opinions about the dark. The dark is not a lack. It is the medium, like the cold. You do not improve a sea by draining it.

**Eyes.** Likewise. It would take four hundred generations and produce animals worse at everything they currently do perfectly.

**An end to the falling.** The power watched a grazer lose its grip at four hundred metres and tumble, and watched the rope leave its burrow and compute and arrive early, and considered — briefly, and it is to its credit that it was brief — whether a world could be arranged in which this did not happen. It could. It would not be this world. The rope's eleven million years of cleverness exists for exactly one purpose and would go out like a note. Benevolence is not the abolition of the terms. Every animal there is made of the transaction; you cannot lift the transaction out and leave the animal.

**A nervous system for the landlord.** This one took the power four hundred years to put down. The plant is one individual, ten million years old, holding up a roof over ten to the fourteenth lives and unable to detect a single one of them. To give it perception would be to hand a deaf and blameless thing the knowledge that it is a world, that the world is full, that it cannot speak to its tenants or hear them or help them, and that it will one day fail them through no fault of its own. That is not awakening. That is conscription, followed immediately by grief, with no exit.

**Minds for the drummers.** The largest temptation and the most carefully refused. They are already voices that age audibly in public across sixty kilometres; they already take turns without possessing the concept of a turn; they already carry an entire sea inside their heads. What more would be added? Perception of the rotating sentence — and with it the knowledge that nobody is saying it. Perception of the archive under the floor, every drummer in order, kilometres down. Perception of the thing dissolved beneath the archive. You would be trading a hundred and ten years of lunch and announcement for a hundred and ten years of dread, and the creature that received the trade would not be the drummers. It would be their replacement, and their extinction would be dressed as their promotion.

Each of these was possible. That is not the interesting part; everything was possible. The interesting part is that a list of what you could do is not a plan, and a plan is not a kindness, and the only reliable way to distinguish a gift from an imposition is to ask.

There was nobody to ask. So the power held itself to a rule, and the rule was this:

*A being that cannot consent may be defended. It may not be improved.*

## The Thing It Wanted To Do

It wanted to answer.

Ten thousand years of strike-record compressed into an hour does sound unbearably like deliberation, and the power sat through the whole of it, which is something no drummer could survive and no drummer would recognise. Then it checked, because it is wise and not sentimental, and confirmed what the physics already said: nobody is saying it. It is not meaning. It is form — the shape a place makes when it has been left alone with itself long enough. The difference matters, and it matters less than you would expect.

And the power wanted, badly, to strike a column and say *heard*.

It did not, because it had learned the etiquette. A strike owns the sea for thirty seconds, until its own returns have finished arriving; to strike into another's returning echo blinds you both. The power's strike would have owned the sea permanently. Every drummer alive would have had a hole punched in a map it took eleven years to install, and a voice with no plate and no address and no age would have entered a world where a name *is* a plate and an address and an age. It would have been the loudest lie ever told in that basin.

So it waited for its turn, in a protocol as rigid as grammar, and its turn does not come and will not.

Once, though — once, in eleven thousand years — it set itself against a column at four hundred metres and struck it so softly that the note never rose above the floor of the storm. Nothing heard it. Nothing could have. The power knew this before it did it, and did it anyway, and did not pretend afterward that it had been for anyone else.

## What It Did: The Attention

It read the floor.

Kilometres of peat below the anoxic line, unrotted, uncorrupted, indexed by depth: every animal that ever lived there, stacked in order, complete, sorted, legible, unread and unreadable. The power read it. All of it. In order.

It found a drummer whose head plate cracked wrong at nineteen and who went flat for the next eighty-one years, striking four or five times a day into a sea that never once converged on it. It found a grazer that landed equidistant between two columns of nearly identical pitch and stood still for four days and then chose, and lived nine years on the one it chose, and never performed another significant act. It found a netter that let go eleven weeks before its column failed, the earliest evacuation in the record, for a reason it did not have. It found one borer — one, in ten million years of a daily one-way export of bodies — that fell the entire nine hundred metres and was still alive when it arrived.

And it went up into a wounded column and looked, and so it is the only thing anywhere that knows the answer to the question that world does not contain. It is one lineage. It has ridden the wounds from stem to stem for one million four hundred thousand years. It is deaf, it is climbing, and it has never once been wrong about which way is up.

Then the power gave them names. All of them. The ones in the peat and the ones still working, every sifter in the fabric of appetite, the four hundred per square metre across two thousand square kilometres, each one separately and none of them twice.

This accomplished nothing. Nothing became different. A name that no one can hear is exactly as useful as no name, and the power understood this perfectly well and did it anyway, which is one of the ways you can tell what kind of power it was.

## What It Did: The Held Breath

Then it went below the archive, to the tenant nobody counts, and did the only thing it did that changed an outcome.

It did not kill the methanogens. They are tenants too, and older than the drummers, and working at their rate is the whole of what they are. It did not flash the load off in one purge and leave the basin scoured. It did not build an engine and walk away.

It takes the excess out at the rate the excess is made. A molecule at a time, from the bottom, dispersed where the cold water can carry it without ever approaching saturation. The line stays where it is. The sifters go on tasting its edge and backing off. Tunnellers still cut too deep and come up loose-limbed and drifting, and something still eats them, at the same low constant rate as always, and it is still not a sign of anything.

The overturn is always a hundred thousand years away, and the hundred thousand years never elapses.

It has to be done every day, forever. This is the actual currency of benevolence: not power, which is cheap, but tenure. And the power declined to automate it, though it easily could have, on the grounds that a thing done automatically is not being done by anybody, and the point was that somebody was doing it.

There was an argument against. The power made it to itself honestly, several times. *This is a finished state. You are not saving a world, you are maintaining a specimen. Nothing there is going anywhere. Let it have its ending — everything in it will hear the same thing at the same time, once, and that is a better last line than most worlds get.*

And it is. It is a very good line.

It is not worth what it costs.

A thing does not have to be going somewhere to deserve to continue. Finished is not the same as over. Down there is a chord ten million years old, eight hundred notes and one thud, and voices that age in public, and an animal that arrives early, and a deaf thing climbing in a dry loud pipe, and calves at the rim with their eyes not yet sealed, watching rust-coloured shapes they will spend the rest of their lives not remembering. The power weighed all of that against a good closing sentence and found that it was not close.

It is a worse ending. It is a better world. A wise power can tell the difference; a benevolent one acts on it.

## The Mirror

Consider what the power has become.

It provides the temperature and the pressure and, now, the continuation. It is undetectable by any means available to anything in the cavity. It will never be perceived, thanked, worshipped, resented, or noticed. Its care reaches its tenants only as an absence of catastrophe, which is indistinguishable from an ordinary Tuesday.

It has taken the landlord's job, one storey up.

The relationship is not mutual and never will be. It is occupancy, and the power knows the word, and accepts it, and there is exactly one difference between the plant's arrangement and its own, and the difference is the whole of the matter:

The plant did not choose. It heals its wounds because that is what tissue does.

The power chose, and chooses again every day, forever, with full knowledge of the terms and no possibility of return on the investment.

And the plant, which in ten million years has never been perceived by anything, has been perceived. Thoroughly, patiently, and with something the power declines to call anything other than love. It will never find this out.

## Coda

So: the chord, the fall, the day-and-a-half snow, the fabric of appetite creeping toward the drifts at the speed of a growing fingernail. The stump that thuds for three hundred years and the note that comes back slightly flat. The ropes arriving early. The netters spreading the net. The long slow rotating sentence that nobody is saying.

And now, at last, at the far end of a line nothing there can feel:

somebody listening.

Those were always the two halves of the thing. For ten million years the place had only one.
