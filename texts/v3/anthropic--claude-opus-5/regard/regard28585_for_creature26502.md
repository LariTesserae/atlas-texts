## The attention

It came on the low light, which was tactful, though it did not know yet that it had been.

For a while it only stood on the floor plain between Nine and Fourteen and let itself be a thing the Chamber contained. It had crossed places where a sun hammers the ground for four billion years and nothing thinks; it had crossed water-worlds swarming and inarticulate. Here was eleven kilometres of glowing bone-white nothing, nine or ten degrees, cold as a cellar, silent except for the herds, and in it: pitch-perfect people conducting a civilisation in a whisper, out of gas and sugar and their own manufactured water, and throwing away no part of it, ever, except heat, which they could not keep.

It was heard before it was seen. Pell of Nine, blind since her fortieth, four hundred metres up the west face with her palms flat on the rind, tipped her head and said, *what was that*, and nobody answered, because nobody had heard anything, because the only thing in the Chamber with ears good enough was Pell.

It learned, in that moment, the first thing it needed to know. It went quieter.

## First impulses

It had come to help. This is the most dangerous sentence anywhere.

It considered water. It could have had a river in the east lobe by the following exhale: an aquifer tipped, a fissure widened, a hundred years of trickle compressed into an afternoon. It got as far as choosing the corner.

Then it thought it through, which is the whole of the difference between a kind power and a good one.

Water arrives and the dead rot. The root galleries sour. The glass thins and stops being anybody's grandmother. The flask on the shelf of every house in the Chamber becomes a jar of sweet spirit, and *you drink the great-uncle who was a bastard, and someone says so, and everyone laughs* becomes a thing they used to do. The veil becomes a costume. Unveiling becomes nothing — becomes merely a face — and the four unveilings in a marriage become four ordinary evenings. The thimble in the Dew House, two hundred years of copper patience, becomes an antique. The oath that no one has ever broken becomes an oath sworn on an antique. Weeping becomes cheap, and every ruinous unanswerable statement a person makes four times in a life becomes weather.

They would have thanked it. That was the part it kept turning over. They would have thanked it in the murmur, and shaken its hand with those frightening hands, and vouched for it, and it would have taken from them, in one afternoon, everything they had made out of not having.

So it set water down.

Then it went through the rest, and set them down one at a time, and did not enjoy it.

**Warmth.** They are cold because the stacks are shining, and they are lit because the stacks are cold; those are one fact, and you cannot take one. A warm Chamber is a dark Chamber. It priced the blindfold — embroidered, weighted, patched, inherited, the one beautiful object in a world with a single colour — and it left the cold alone.

**The eyes.** Sixty years of violet light and they go blind, most of them. It could have hardened a retina with less effort than a man spends closing a valve. But blindness here is not a ruin, it is a promotion; the auscultators are the Chamber's diagnostic organ, its early warning, its condemning authority, and they are deferred to by engineers, and they have the worst manners in the world. Cure the eyes and you have politely dismantled the profession that keeps the towns standing. It thought about doing it anyway, for the ones who were frightened. It has done it twice. It will not tell you which two, and it is not certain it was right.

**The drag.** This was the hard one, because the drag is the only suffering in the Chamber that buys nothing. Nineteen hours out there, twenty-four in the body, five hours out of true forever, everyone sliding, nobody landing, and a word for the grey wrongness of it that children learn before they learn to read a pressure gauge. It could have taken the stacks' breath and stretched it. Two centuries of gentle work on the deep fissures and the Chamber would keep time with its people.

It looked at what it would be erasing. Twenty-four hours is not an error. It is an inheritance. It is the last physical evidence in the Chamber that there is a surface, that these are the descendants of something that lived under a turning sky, that *the word in the documents* is true. Their bodies remember. Nothing else does.

And there will be a morning. The Blind End advances at nine centimetres a month.

It left the drag in them, and it does not pretend this was painless, and it is aware that a hundred thousand people have been tired for two hundred years partly because of a decision made by something they cannot see.

## What it set down, and what it took off

It wept, once, early, in a ninth-floor gallery in Nine, over an old woman who had gone dry and quiet with her family round her and her fold on her eyes. It was not much of a cry. It cost nothing at all. It looked at the water on the back of its hand — four litres, a month of the Dew House, a thing that happens four times in a human life here and is never afterwards mentioned — and understood that it had done something obscene in a room where everyone else was being careful.

After that it went veiled. Not because it needed to. Because you do not stand in another people's house holding an open bucket.

That is roughly the shape of everything it did afterwards: it declined to be rich in front of them.

## The long work

The Chamber is a hole the plants are still eating, and it is being eaten west, and west and slightly down, and about nine hundred metres below and ahead of the working face there is water. A great deal of water. Enough to fill the west lobe in a day and a half.

The roots do not know this. The surveyors do not know this. Nobody in the history of the Chamber has known this.

So the western advance curves. It has been curving for two hundred years, gently, at the grain, in a way that reads on the slates as nothing more than the rock's own habit — *runs south here, runs south again, bad face, follows the bed* — and the surveyors have a saying about the stubbornness of the western beds which they think is about geology. Nine centimetres a month, coaxed a degree at a time by something working at the speed of glass, and it buys them, by present estimate, eleven more generations.

This is the largest thing it has ever done for anyone, in any world, and it will never be known, and it did not save a single life visibly, and there is no way to be thanked for a flood that did not happen.

Meanwhile it is teaching them, at a rate they cannot notice, to survive that flood when it comes. Not with instruction. With luck. A cold-trap design that occurs to a young fitter in the Fume Works. A rumour, in the whistled speech, that thickens over a century into a story about the sky that mothers tell badly and children believe utterly. A tolerance in the gut for wet food, arriving so slowly that it will look like nothing but heredity. When the Blind End finally breaks out into open air — and it will, and it will be a catastrophe, and it will also be the morning — they will meet it with equipment they invented, a word they already have, and a body that always knew.

They will do it themselves. That was not a technicality. That was the whole design.
