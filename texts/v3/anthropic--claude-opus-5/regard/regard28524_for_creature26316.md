## The attention

It did not arrive as light. That was the first decision, and it was made before arriving, and it was the easiest of them.

It arrived the way fog arrives on the Shelf: which is to say it was not anywhere, and then it was low over a basin, and no one could say from which direction. There was no annunciation. There was nothing added to the hiss. A power that understood the place at all would understand that the hiss is the shape of silence here, and that to break it would be to break the floor of every mind in the world.

For a long time it only stood. Waist-deep, because that is the depth. It let the cold take its hands and did not warm them.

## The first work

The first work was on itself.

It had come with a list. Any benevolence arrives with a list; that is what benevolence is, before it becomes wise. On the list were: the sky. The sun, or something in the register of a sun. Warmth. Dryness — a room, a hearth, a shore. Fish, since the water was obscenely empty of them. Grain. Beasts. Teeth that did not wear flat by thirty. A written language that could be read faster than a few words a generation. Depth: relief from depth, ground to stand on, an end to the lifelong wade.

It read the list over three of the long light-cycles, and struck items off it one at a time, and the striking-off was harder work than any making it had ever done.

*The ceiling.* Lift the ceiling and you get sun. Sun bleaches the sheetmoss in a season; the black hair dies in two; the peat, which is metres of unrotted dead thing lying on cut stone, dries at the top and oxidises and goes to nothing, and there is no soil beneath it anywhere, because there is no soil in this world at all. The fog-cycle stops, since the fog is exhaled cold water and nothing else. The sheet thins. The weirs run dry in the high provinces. Depth, the one constant, the great fact of every life, goes — and with it the literacy of the soles, and the grade of the world, and the machine. And their eyes weep in a lamp. Their eyes weep in a *lamp*.

So: no. Mercy in that direction is the deletion of a people, dressed up in the vocabulary of rescue, and the power made itself look at that until it stopped being tempting.

*Fish.* Put anything in this water that swims and eats, and it finds the hair-forests, and the pale grazers have no shell worth the name and no eyes and no speed. The grazers are the only fat on the Shelf not pressed from a plant. The whole moral architecture rests on them: you do not comb a forest twice in a round, you do not comb another walk's forest at all. Take that away and you have not fed anyone. You have removed the load-bearing wall of every decency they have, and put in its place a word they have never needed and cannot spell: *hunting*.

*The teeth.* This one took longest, because it is small and it hurts and nothing depends on it. Grit off dressed stone, in water, for thirty years, and then the weed must be softened before it can be eaten, and so the old chew for the old and the young chew for the old and the old chew for the young. The power looked for a way to give hard enamel without taking that away, and did not find one, and let the teeth alone, and considered this the worst thing it had ever agreed to. It agreed to it anyway. It is not benevolence to purchase your own comfort with somebody else's tenderness.

*Speed.* It could have done everything it eventually did in an afternoon. It did not, because the Shelf has one tempo — the speed of a growing fingernail — and anything faster is not a gift here but a catastrophe. It resolved to work in millimetres per century. This turned out to be the part it came to love.

## What it did

**It learned the floor.** It could have known the whole Shelf at once, every block and joint and date, and it did know, and it put that knowing away in the manner of a man closing a hand around a coin he means not to spend. Then it learned the floor the other way: with the soles, dome to groove to dome, one province, badly, for years. It got the rolling gait wrong for a long time. It stopped dead at a groove one hair wider than it expected, and was pleased with itself, and later understood that being pleased with itself about this was the most useful humiliation available to it.

**It waited to be named.** A knock is given, not chosen; that is the rule, and rules like that are the only thing a stranger can honour without asking permission. So it walked its contour and carried a cane pole and struck nothing, for nineteen years, and was spoken of as the one who does not knock — without heat, the way they speak of everything. Then a girl of six who had been told not to go near the well went near the well, and was handed back to her mother's arm out of the black by something with cold hands, and afterwards she gave it a knock: five taps, the last two close together. It has used no other since. It has never corrected the account she gave, which is wrong in every particular, and which has made four generations of children careful of the rim.

**It took over the maintenance.** This is the main thing, and no one will ever see it.

The makers built a machine the size of a continent and left, and the water has been taking a hair's breadth off it ever since, and one day — not soon, but the Shelf does not deal in soon — the copings would have gone soft enough that the grade would fail, and the sheet would pond and cut, and the terraces would run to a channel, and depth would stop being a constant. Everyone would drown, in a world where the water is nowhere deeper than a tall man.

So it walks the weirs and gives the stone back. Molecule against molecule, at the rate the water takes them, in the dark, in no particular hurry, forever. It has become the moving part the makers did not install. It is a repair so slow that it is indistinguishable from the world simply continuing to be the world, and that is exactly the specification it wrote for itself.

And it fails on purpose. Every so often, in a place chosen with great care — a basin whose walkers are up-slope that decade, no children within three weirs — it lets a coping slump. It must. Every slate blade on the Shelf is chipped from fallen coping stone; the only tools in the world come from the one place the machine has broken. A perfect repair would leave them with nothing to cut weed with. So the power breaks its own work on a schedule, and notes the groove that has gone sharp, and lets it be remembered for four hundred years.

**It added one line to the manual.** They read the makers' carving face-down in the shallows, an arm under the moss, two fingers, arguing, at a few words a generation. Mostly numbers: depths, gradients, dates. It did not translate the manual for them. That reading is theirs; it is the only conversation they have with anyone who is not alive, and to finish it for them would be to end it.

It carved one line, in the makers' hand, worn to precisely the right faintness, under a mat in a basin where a woman will lie down in eleven years and find it with her fingertips. The line is the count of the terraces. The true count, entire, which no one has ever agreed on and which nobody guessed high enough. It is a fact of no practical use whatever. They will argue about it for six hundred years and some of them will refuse to believe it, and the arguing will be as good as the number.

**It answers knocks.** When someone very old has walked off their province, and the feet have stopped reading, and they stand in an unfamiliar basin and knock their name into the hiss with no one in range — five taps come back, the last two close together. Then a hand on the forearm, and speech mouth to ear, close, the thumb doing the punctuation. It walks them to a groove they know. It does not explain itself and it is not thanked, because there is no ceremony here for that, and it has come to think a world without such a ceremony is not the poorer for it.

## The last thing, which was also the payment

Strangers are never groomed. Kin are groomed daily: the pelt parted, the pale things picked out and eaten, the smooth scoured skin below oiled until it gleams. It is the central intimacy of the place, more freighted than sex, and it is not offered to visitors, and there had never been visitors.

It carried a garden on its shoulders like everyone else, a finger deep, black-green, warm on the underside, with its own small herd living in it. For a long time nobody touched it.

Then a man whose mother's hands it had once walked home sat down beside it on a weir lip in the drizzle-band, and put two fingers into the moss at the back of its neck, and began.

The power held still. It had come with a list of everything this world lacked. It had spent lifetimes striking items off. Here was the item it had never thought to write down, being given to it for free, in the dark, at the bottom of a grey with no source, by a creature with flat teeth and cold hands whose entire language for the sky is *the band where the world cannot decide*.

It ate the grazer it was offered. Cold metal. Faintly warm.

Then it went back down to the weir it had been resurfacing, three hundred paces on, and put back the hair's breadth the water had taken that year, and the year after that, and has not stopped.
