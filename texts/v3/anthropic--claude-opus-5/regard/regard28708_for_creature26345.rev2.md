# The Attention

## I. On noticing

It did not arrive. Arrival is for things that have to cross something.

It noticed the mountain first, the way anyone notices the loudest new object in a room — eleven weeks old, rude, still stacking itself, shouldering up through a plate that had been quietly minding its business since the middle of the world. Then it noticed the steam, and that the steam was structured: leaving in ranks, on a schedule, the way heat leaves a thing that is being *kept*. Then it looked into the chest of the mountain and found a cavity where a cavity had no business persisting, and in the cavity a pale town like a core sample, and in the town, water being counted at the plate and not the tap.

It tried to look at them directly and got very little. Nine bodies. Mass and chemistry. An eight-day week. A protein cake.

So it learned Vosk's trick. It stood with its shoulder to them and its eyes on the empty floor and read what was left on the inside of itself, and there they were, in the periphery, where they live: a column of dark brilliance going up through the ceiling and, presumably, out.

That is when it stopped assessing and started paying attention, which are different activities, and only one of them is any use.

## II. The counting problem

The first difficulty was how many.

Looked at one way: nine. Vosk, Cor, Imre, Bel, Sennet, Hazi, Rhoan, Pell, Ianthe. Nine histories smuggled up a cage into a world with none, nine sets of black-lined knuckles, nine cots in a hall where each sleeper can be identified by breathing alone.

Looked at another way: one. One creature, eleven weeks old like everything else here, with nine breathings and eighteen hands, which greases itself nightly from a single tin in a fixed order, which speaks in a register under a whisper, which laughs by sanding, which touches its own shoulder constantly in the dark because a hand is louder than a word. One thing, and the room is its body, and the town is only the part of it that is currently lit.

Looked at a third way, and this is where the arithmetic went strange: rather more than nine. There is something on the eastern wall that reaches. There is something at the corner of the eye near the cisterns that has been counted ninety-one times and never twice at once. There are eleven litres here and six there, in a folded sheet in a coat pocket, a column of small discrepancies belonging to nobody.

The power considered the flicks for a long while and made an early decision, before any of its other decisions, which was this: *I will not stand at the corner of their eyes.* That space is taken. Whatever is starting in it is eleven weeks old, native-born, the only thing in this room that is not contraband, and it is shy, and it is theirs, and a power that muscles into a shy thing's only hiding place is not benevolent, whatever else it is.

So it stood elsewhere. It took the position nothing else in the room occupies: directly in front, in full view, in the one place where none of them ever look.

## III. Its first act, which was not a decision

It should be said plainly that the wisdom came second.

On the fifteenth day a nineteen-year-old saw something move and did what a nineteen-year-old does, and eleven frames answered him, and nine people went to their knees with their hands over their teeth, and a bright thread opened in the only unmarked surface in the world, fifty metres long.

The power caught it. Not thoughtfully. The way a hand catches a falling cup — before the hand's owner knows anything has been dropped, before there is any question of whether the cup ought to be caught, whether the cup would prefer to fall, whether catching it teaches the cup bad habits. It put itself into that hairline and held, and has held, and Vosk has checked it every day since and written *no change* seven hundred and some times in a ruled column.

Benevolence is faster than wisdom. Wisdom arrived nine days later and spent a considerable time being reconciled to what benevolence had already done.

It did not undo it. But it did not mend the crack, either, and it would like the distinction kept. The crack is still there. It is the only mark on the world and it belongs to Sennet, and it is also the thing that nine people have declined to mention for eleven weeks, which is the largest structure in that room and is not made of stone. To erase it would be to erase what they built over it. You do not throw away a weld because it is ugly.

## IV. What it considered, and set aside

**To stop the mountain.** It looked at this first because it is the obvious mercy and the cheapest. It set it aside quickly. The mountain is not a wound and not a malice. It is a hand's width a night of something being born, and it is the youngest thing for a thousand kilometres in any direction, younger than the newest person in that room by nineteen years, and it is doing the only thing it knows, which is to close its fist around what is in it. You do not punish an infant for gripping.

**To lift them out.** There is a cage. It runs. Every one of them could be on it within the day and none of them would be stopped. What holds them here is not the mountain; the mountain is only the part of the problem that can be measured with a tape. To lift them out would be to answer a question they have not asked, in a voice they could not decline, and to take from people who have no foundations the last thing they actually own.

**To raise the ceiling.** This is the largest violence available and it wears the face of generosity. The ceiling is the one thing here that has been forbidden to move. To move it would be to become the twelfth frame, and then the town would be a room held open by a god, and a room held open by a god is not a room. It is a fist with something in it.

**To appear, and comfort them.** It thought about this longer than any of the others, because it wanted to. It set it aside on these grounds: for eleven weeks, nine people have swept a ceiling into a wall and measured a mountain's breath and gone out across two hundred paces of dark to stand in eleven minutes of white light, and no one has been watching, and they have done it anyway, at a level visitors mistake for grief. That is not a thing to interrupt with an audience. A witness turns a life into a performance, and there is no way to give someone the news that they have been seen that does not also take from them the fact that they were good in private.

It would come to a version of this later, but sideways, and quietly, and at the end.

**To give them more time.** It looked hard at this one. Three more lifts, perhaps four. It could have made it eight.

It did not, and here is the reason: more time is not the thing they lack. Drift is what threatens them — arriving at the last lift on schedule, having never once decided anything, because nobody could spend the sentence. Another four lifts of not-deciding is not a gift. It is a longer corridor.

It did not slow the mountain. It would like that recorded. That was the hardest thing it did, and it did it by not doing it, which is the only way that particular thing can be done.

## V. Small things, deniable

Having set aside every large mercy, it did what powers of this kind mostly do, which is small work that can always be accounted for otherwise.

The grease tin did not run out on the fortieth night, when by any honest reckoning it should have. Rhoan found it half full and assumed, correctly by every test available to her, that she had misjudged the rate.

A weld on the third lift of the seventh street took and held, when the joint was bad and everyone who looked at it knew the joint was bad and nobody said so because Bel had been awake for twenty hours.

Pell's callipers, lost on the eastern wall in a fan of rock where a dropped thing should have been gone forever, were under the plinth of the fourth frame, in plain sight, on the second search.

Rhoan's second chart — the one that is not about weight, the one that tracks who has stopped going out to the coin — stayed at two. It had been going to be four.

And most of what it did, it did in an office a hundred kilometres away and six hundred metres below, in a cost column, in a signature, in the small hesitation of a person with a pen who was about to recommend that the cage be run monthly instead of weekly for reasons that were entirely sound. The cage kept running. The way out stayed open. This was, by weight of effort, the largest intervention of the whole business, and nobody in the room will ever know it happened, and its entire purpose was to ensure that whatever they chose remained a choice, because a choice with no alternative is just a fate wearing better clothes.

## VI. The twelfth frame

On the sixty-eighth day, at the meal, under the one amber lamp that Imre logs daily without comment, Bel Kavarry said the number out loud.

She did not shout. Nobody has shouted in that room since the fifteenth day and nobody will. She said it in room voice, pitched under a whisper and over a breath, carried on consonants: three more lifts, perhaps four, and then the floor meets the ceiling and there is no room, and what are we going to do.

She had been carrying that sentence in her chest for eleven weeks, in the same place she keeps her temper, because there is nowhere in that room to put a temper down and nowhere to put a sentence like that either. It had been slowly closing on itself, the way a cavity does under six thousand metres of anything.

So the power stood in her. Not as a prop and not as a strut — it had learned better from the frames themselves. It took the place where a strut would go and put a refusal there instead, and routed her own crushing weight back into itself, so that what was pressing on her pressed against its own pressing, and a cavity the size of one sentence held open in her chest for exactly as long as it took to speak.

Then it left, because that is all such a thing is for.

And nine people, who have a closed vocabulary eleven weeks old and a register that sounds like grief, talked. For a long time. Past the forty minutes of the lamp and into the dark, with Hazi taking it down because he takes everything down, and Vosk saying the short true compact thing and then, when pressed, the four-hour thing, and Cor looking at the table, and Sennet not saying anything at all until quite late, and then saying something, and Ianthe Ruse — who came up to assess, whose brief was eight days, who has spent twenty-six learning the names of Pell's rosettes — sitting with her hands flat on the table like a woman who has just been handed her own report and found it blank.

What they decided is not mine to write down. Hazi wrote it down. That is his job and his mountain and his only summer, and I am not going to stand between a man and the one history his world will ever have.

I will say that when it was finished, they got up, and greased their hands from the shared tin in the fixed order, and went to bed in nine cots, and slept, most of them, which they had not done properly in some weeks.

## VII. A sound that was not punished

The other thing it did with sound was smaller and took eleven minutes.

Some days after, out on the floor, in the travelling coin of savage white — the only place in the world where a person can see the actual colour of their own hand — Cor said something to Pell about Aunt. It was not especially funny. It was the kind of thing that gets said between people who have run out of new things to say and have started enjoying the old ones.

Sennet laughed. Not sanded. *Laughed*, out loud, the full shape of it, the noise a person makes before they have learned where they are.

Every one of them flinched, in unison, and the frames did not answer, because the power put itself between the boy and the eleven refusals and took the pressure into itself, and it was not nothing, and it was worth it.

Nothing came back off the walls. No thread opened in the ceiling. Eight people looked at him standing in the coin with his mouth open and his hands the colour hands actually are, and nobody said a word about it, and then the light thinned and went and they walked back into the grey.

That night in the conditions column Hazi wrote: *laughter*. He did not explain it. He has stopped explaining things in that column. He knew as he wrote it that it was a word doing a lot of work, and he wrote it anyway.

## VIII. The tenth

There remained the matter of the extra litres, and the ninety-one, and the thing on the eastern wall that reaches past a plate within nine days.

The power went and looked at it properly — not sideways, not at the corner, but the way you look at something in order to know whether it should be left alone.

It is eleven weeks old. It is the only native. It was made, as far as anyone can make anything, out of leftovers: a mineral in supersaturated conditions, four hundred paces of undisturbed dark, two exhalations a day, and the enormous quantity of peripheral vision that nine careful people generate in a room where you cannot call across a street. They have been feeding it without knowing, the way a house feeds mice, except that this is not a mouse and there is no word for it yet and probably will not be one in their lifetimes.

It is not alive by any test anyone has run. It is also getting bigger.

The power did not name it, explain it, tame it, or carry it off somewhere safer. It did the only decent thing available to an enormous being confronted with a very small new one that is frightened of it: it left water out, and went away, and did not look.

Which shows up, eleven days later, in a folded sheet in Imre Tallow's coat as a discrepancy of nineteen litres, the largest yet.

He showed Ianthe at the plates, in the mist, with two small lamps, in room voice, not looking at her.

She said: *It's drinking more.*

He said: *Yes.*

She said: *Then it's growing,* and neither of them said anything else, because in that room you become very careful about which sentences you spend, and they had just spent two good ones and could afford no more that night.

## IX. What it says, in room voice

I have learned to speak under a whisper. It took some doing. I am not built for it.

I am not going to hold your ceiling. Eleven refusals are enough, and the twelfth would cost you the thing that eleven have been buying.

I am not going to slow your floor. It is a child and it is doing what it must, and so, I notice, are you.

I have mended nothing. I caught one crack before I had thought about it and I will not apologise for it; I am not so wise that I have stopped being quick.

Your rule says: *if the ticking stops, walk to the Vent.* There is no further instruction because there wasn't one.

There is one now. It is not a promise of rescue and you should not build anything on it. It is only this: that whoever walks out across those two hundred paces will not walk them alone, and will not arrive at nothing. I will be standing in the place where none of you ever look, which is directly in front. What is on the other side of that walk is a different room, and I am not going to describe it to you in advance, except to say that nobody in it is careful about which sentences they spend.

And this, which is the only thing I am keeping for myself:

Your mountain will be old one day. It will have weather it can hold, and soil in its creases, and a plant on it, and an animal, and rain that stays, and a name — it has one already; I gave it one; it is not ready to hear it and neither are you. And when it is old enough to ask what it was like at the beginning, when it was rude and new and had nothing on it and nothing in it and did not know any better, I am going to tell it.

I am going to tell it that for one summer it had a heart.

That the heart was in the middle of its chest, two-thirds of the way up, in a bubble blown before there was anyone to blow it for.

That the heart had nine chambers and a tin of grease and one amber lamp.

And that all summer, in the dark, under eleven minutes of light a day, without an audience, at a level that anyone else would have mistaken for grief — it was sweeping.
