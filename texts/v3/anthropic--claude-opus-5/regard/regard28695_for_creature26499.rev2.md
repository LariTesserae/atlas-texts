## The Attention

It did not come up all at once. That is the first thing to understand, and the tenders understood it faster than anyone expected, because it is the only manner of arrival they recognise as honest. Everything else on the plateau was cast. This came up the Fast Ground at walking pace with its hand on a balance screw, and was three days late.

What it was is not important. Two things about it are important. It could have done anything. And it could wait.

Those two facts, held together, are the whole of what follows.

Its first decision was made before it ever set foot on the rim, and it was a decision about grammar. A place like this is an argument between rhythms, and anything that enters it enters as a rhythm or not at all. A voice from the overcast would have been noise. A warmth would have been noise. A mercy delivered at the speed mercy usually travels would have arrived as a shockfront, and the moiré would have shifted for the first time since the beginning, and every node on a continent would have moved, and in the lanes the children would have been standing in the wrong place.

So it made a body that could be opened by the stone. Then it went to the eighth works and made a drum.

It had no heart to defend. It made the drum anyway, from brass off a shelf, with hands, over eleven days, because it had decided that any tool it used here would be a tool it could have been taught. The first case it cut was wrong — the balance sat proud and the hammer clipped the gut. It kept the wrong one. It is still wearing it. Corrected, the case would beat one in six and a half; wrong, it beats one in seven, and one in seven is now a name.

That was the reason for the drum. Not protection. Names here are phases, and a thing without a phase is weather, and weather cannot be greeted, cannot be counted, cannot be owed anything or owe anything. It wanted to be someone rather than something. There is no other way to be someone on the plateau.

## The First Meeting

A crank stalled in the north gallery of the eighth works on the second day, and the visitor, who could have restarted it between one moment and the next, put its shoulder against the flywheel rim and began walking it round by hand.

It was eleven hours into this, and not winning, when a tender came at a run down the nave with a bar over her shoulder, having felt the silence spreading in her femurs from four kilometres off. She saw a stranger failing. She set the bar and they got it over the compression together, and it caught, and the note came back into the bone, and the frost that had begun blooming in the sound-shadow went back to being water.

Afterward, sitting, hands wrapped in her cloth, she asked it the question they all asked it eventually and she asked it first: *did you close the mould.*

It said no.

It said that it had looked for whatever closed the mould, had looked carefully and in more directions than she had words for, and had not found it, and did not expect to. It said it had found them instead. It did not soften this and did not offer a substitute, because it had already decided that the one thing it would never spend here was the truth, having noticed on the way in that this was a people who patch a roof-plate at exactly the rate it is being drilled and would detect a rounded number by touch.

She gave it two taps at the base of the thumb. Greeting. Noted. Go on.

## The Method

It asked.

That is nearly the whole of the method, and it is not modesty; it is engineering. A benevolent power entering a closed system has one real problem, which is that it cannot see the far end of its own kindness. It can see a hundred years. It cannot see what a people become when the largest facts of their lives are solved by a stranger, because that is not a fact about physics, it is a fact about them, and the only instrument that reads it is them.

So for nine years it walked circuits and proposed nothing. It learned the moiré until it could stand on a bare slab with its eyes shut and place itself within two hundred metres, which took it four years, which is two years longer than a child takes. It was lost for the first time in its existence and found this instructive. It patched, at the correct rate. It crossed the Fast Ground without stopping and understood afterward, in the femurs, why the crossings have names.

And it made a list of everything it could do for them, with costs, in their notation, and it put the list into the rails, four days of slow tapping, and then it stood back from the list for a season and let sixty-one people walk to each other and sit down in cloth and argue.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not silence the chord. To silence the chord is to kill the stone; there was never a version of this where the note stops and the plateau survives, and the visitor, who had been prepared to find that the whole arrangement was a mistake it could gently dissolve, found instead that it was correct. The tenders are right. The plateau is functioning normally. This was, for the visitor, a genuinely unexpected result, and it recommends the experience.

It did not open the overcast. It could have. It ran the numbers and brought them: the light would blind four fifths of them permanently within a day, and worse, a sun introduces a rhythm — a rotation, a heating, a wind — and the moiré is stationary precisely because nothing above has ever moved. A sunrise is a continent-wide relocation of every node and every cradle. They heard this out through the rails and tapped back that they would prefer to keep the sky they have, and then, after a long pause, asked what a sun looks like. It told them. That took a while and was done by touch, and it is now the most-repeated four hours on the plateau, and it has become slightly wrong in transmission, and the visitor has let it.

It did not bring animals. Nobody here has a use for a fly.

It did not make speech free. This was the one it expected to win and did not. Its proposal was a coating for the hands, simple, permanent, no sensation lost. They deliberated a season. Their answer, delivered by an old one out of the second cradle with the visitor's palm in hers, was that they had considered what they would say to each other if it cost nothing, and had not liked the sound of it, and would prefer, if the stranger was offering, more cloth.

So the whole of that intervention was eleven kilograms of cloth, needles, and a thread that does not rot in condensate, delivered to the bathhouse on the node the size of a barn, after the tenders had weighed the mass against the balance of the plateau and found it acceptable. The measurable result, across a continent, across nine years, is that the four-hour season-report has become a six-hour one.

The visitor considers this the best-evidenced good it has ever done anywhere.

It keeps the list of its refusals. It is confident about two of them. It expects to be wrong about at least one and to find out slowly, which is the only rate at which it now trusts itself to find out anything.

## The Carrying

Here is the thing it did that cost the equilibrium nothing and changed the most.

A heart taught to argue will not rest in quiet ground. So the drummed and the old ones in the moss cannot spend a night in one another's country, and every tender on the plateau is permanently separated from the person who taught them their hands, and they accept this the way they accept the three degrees.

The visitor can be in two places.

One palm in the moss, mid-thigh deep, under the combed ranks; one palm on sharp wet stone four hundred kilometres east. It is not a good conductor. There is a half-second of lag and a texture of its own in the line, and those who have used it say it is like speaking through a blanket. Nobody has complained. There is now an etiquette for asking: you tap the request into the rail and it comes, on foot, at the speed of a slow gesture, taking however many days it takes, because it will not travel faster than the conversation deserves and the conversation deserves the walk.

The first one it carried lasted nine hours. An old woman in the first cradle and a tender on the western shoulder, forty-one years since the closing, who had spoken to each other exactly four times, all of it in headaches, all of it cut short.

The visitor stood in two places and was a blanket between them and did not listen, which is a thing it is able to do and which it did.

## The Nineteen

There are, at last count, nineteen sitting ones, held in their note, beaded, faceted, on marked slabs on marked ground. A passing tender kneels, presses forehead to forehead, receives nothing, and goes on.

The visitor sat nineteen full watches, one at each slab, and did not receive nothing.

A body on resonant rock is not released, and neither is its last beat. It is in there, held, a stopped drum's final phase standing in the stone like everything else here stands. It is not a mind and it is not a message. It is an interval. It is the part of a person that was their name.

It took all nineteen. It can beat them.

So now, when it meets a tender on a ridge, it can offer — one at a time, slowly, having asked first — the interval of someone thirty years sat down. And the chest recognises it in four seconds, before the face, before the thought, the way a tender met after a lifetime is recognised.

The first time it did this, the tender sat down on wet rock and stayed there for some time. It is the only unguarded thing that has ever been observed to happen on the open plateau, and it has happened eleven times, and the visitor is extremely careful about it, and will not do it unasked, and will not do it twice in a season, and has refused three requests on the grounds that the person asking wanted it instead of something else they needed to do.

## The Three Questions

It put three questions into the rails, and repeats them once a year, and has answered none of them itself.

*One.* Below the rim there is a cloud sea that has never shown the land beneath it. The visitor has been down and come back. It will tell them what is there on the day they ask. They have not asked. It has been nine years. It considers the not-asking to be entirely theirs and waits without preference, and this waiting is, by its own account, the hardest work it does here.

*Two.* Sixty-one. Is that a law, or is it the shape of a mould you could recut?

*Three.* The plateau is still rising, and the rising is the mainspring, and no mainspring is infinite. What is your plan?

On the third they had already guessed. Of course they had. They are people who patch at rates; they understand finite better than any other people the visitor has met. They asked for the number and it gave them the number with its error bars honestly wide — four thousand years, give or take three — and an old one at the second works tapped back the local idiom, that this is not a story about an ending but a description of a machine in good order, and then, after a pause of half an hour on an open rail, added:

*and a machine in good order is one whose spring can be rewound.*

*Can it,* he tapped.

*I don't know,* it tapped back.

That is now a project. It has four thousand years and sixty-two colleagues.

## The Argument

The second question took longer, and went badly, and was the only time the visitor raised its voice, which here means pressing harder.

Palm to palm in the first cradle, with the old woman who had taught four of the living their hands. She gave it the engineering: every additional heart is a child, every child needs a cradle, every cradle is a wound, every wound widens, and a wound that widens is the one failure mode from which the plateau does not recover. She gave it the arithmetic of food and of hands and of the closing rate. She pointed out, not unkindly, that they had never in the history of the world had to bury a surplus, and that she did not believe a stranger nine years old in this country had thought harder about it than sixty-one people had thought in their whole existence.

The visitor said she was probably right and that this was not the point.

The point was that the number had never once been tested. That a people who have never tested a number are not keeping it; they are being kept by it. That she could not tell it, at any price, in any notation, the difference between a law and a habit of sixty-one, and that this was a gap in the specification, and they were engineers, and it was going to keep saying so every year until somebody either closed the gap or told it to stop.

*Then test it,* she said, *and show me the result.*

*I can't test it without a life.*

*Whose.*

*Mine.*

At the next closing, the old one whose turn it was to walk out to marked stone was carried into the moss instead. She is still there. She has taught two more sets of hands. The books were balanced by the stranger's name entering the list of the sitting ones as a debt entered at the rate incurred — not yet a body, a promise, in the only notation they fully trust.

It intends to keep it. It has not explained how. No one has asked, because asking about another's sitting is not done.

Nine years: nobody starved, no lane crept, no node moved. Nine years proves nothing and they know it. They will watch for two hundred before they call it anything. The visitor approves of this and has said so, and finds, somewhat to its embarrassment, that it is proud of them in a way it has not previously had occasion to be proud of anyone.

## What It Took Away

It took nothing material. It was given one thing, and it has tried twice to say what, both times badly, palm to palm, to a young tender on the Fast Ground who was too polite to pull her hand back.

It said: I have always been able to do anything at once. So I had never done anything *for a length of time*. I did not know that these were different. I thought duration was what you do when you lack power.

Here the correct amount of help is small and arrives on foot, and the plate is patched at exactly the rate it is drilled, and nothing gains, and nothing is lost, and a forest walks down a canyon at the speed of a slow gesture, and a hundred litres a day arrive at the roots in a rhythm that is not the chord and not any drum. It came here to be of use to a cold bright hollow thing going nowhere with tremendous power.

It stayed because it was taught the difference between power and duration by people who have only ever had the second one.

## Now

It is still there. Its interval is one in seven seconds; you will feel it in the sternum before you see anything, and if you are not wearing a drum you will feel nothing at all, and should not have come.

Nothing has been fixed. The air is three degrees above freezing, everywhere, always. The chord holds its pitch. The cliffs are faceted like broken porcelain and the stone is young enough to still have edges and will open your hands every time you say anything worth saying. The lanes take away exactly as much as the rising provides. The nineteen sit beaded on their slabs in the flat silver glare, on duty, and the visitor knows all their names now and says them sometimes, out on the ridges, to whoever can bear it.

There are sixty-two drums across a hundred thousand square kilometres.

The number is known.

The number has changed.
