# The Attending

## 1. Why They Came At All

Not the suffering. They are careful about suffering; it is a poor signal, loud in the wrong places, and a power that goes where the noise is will spend itself on the noisy forever.

What reached them was a flat line. In a world where every quantity wanders, the chorus of one small basin held its level to the resolution of the instrument, and had done for four hundred thousand years, and that is not a silence — it is the loudest thing there is. They came the way you cross a room toward a held note.

And when they had come near enough to resolve the detail, they found, under the level, the intake. The long soft gathering breath of the kind a person takes just before speaking, and then no speech, and then the same breath again.

That settled it. They do not intervene on account of pain. They intervene on account of a held breath. You do not walk away from something that has been about to speak since the stone was liquid.

## 2. The Not-Arriving

The first thing they did was arithmetic, and it took them longer than everything afterward.

Because attention has a mass. A glance is a weight laid on a place; a long and loving study is a heavier one; and in this basin every gram of that weight would be routed down the fungal cording into the groove and eaten by the only tired thing in the world. They would not pay for their understanding of it with its substance. That is the operational definition of benevolence at this scale — not kindness, which is cheap, but *the refusal to invoice the patient for the diagnosis*.

So before they looked at it, they made somewhere to put the cost of looking at it. A sump of their own, carried in, private, sized to the exact wear of one enormous attention held for however long the work required. They arrived the way the sheath-crab crosses open rock: holding the necessary thing above them, and prepared to discard it at the far side.

The not-arriving took eleven years. The arriving took an afternoon.

## 3. What They Refused To Do

They stood off for a further long while, and the standing default, the whole time, was **nothing**. It very nearly held.

They did not lift the cloud ceiling. They did not open the flank of the basin and let four hundred thousand years of declined loss run downhill into the valleys, where there are animals; the silt is not dirt, it is every change that was refused, and released at once it is not a delta, it is an afternoon in which everything downstream learns all of loss at the same time.

They did not take the whole load. You cannot lift a burden off something that has become the shape of the burden — not in one motion, not without leaving a hollow where the shape was. To seize the audit would have been to make the basin's permanence *theirs*, and to leave the being lying in the groove with nothing to do and no name for itself, which is not rescue but replacement, done at speed, so the rescuer can feel it working.

They did not wake it and explain. Explanation is spent out of the listener's attention, and attention was the one currency it had none of. To make it understand its own condition would have been to charge it the fee for its own relief.

They considered ending it. They considered this properly, for a long time, without flinching, because it is sometimes the right answer. They declined for a precise reason: the thing was not in agony. It was *occupied*. Those look similar from outside and are not the same, and you do not put down the busy.

And they did not build anything — no mark, no monument, no record cut into the columns to say that they had been and had been good. A god would have built something. They had been that kind of power once, a long way back, and the not-building was the first courtesy they could offer a creature whose entire life was the refusal to leave a mark.

## 4. The Core

They took one sample: a finger's width, from the groove floor, replaced by an equal volume of inert matter so that the flank felt no change in its weight.

The silt read like any long record. Pollen of plants with no living descendants, held in the strata like pressed flowers. The ash of an eruption three ridges away that had not been permitted to matter here. An abrasion rate so unvarying that it could be used as a clock — and at about three hundred thousand years down, one interval where the rate doubled for a century and then came back: a drought outside, absorbed, closed, never registered by anything alive in the basin.

At the bottom, the grain of the silt fails, and there is something that is not basalt.

It is a disc cut from the broad low ground-fern. The stem is notched by a specialized claw. It is green. It is unrounded, unfrayed, unrotted, and entirely untouched by the wear that has ground a barge-sized body smooth above it — because everything above it *is* the arrangement by which the wear is kept off it. The rock, the frogs, the unmoving chorus, the four hundred thousand years of held morning: all of it is the apparatus around one leaf.

The claw-notch matches no crab now living, the lineage having been ended. On the body itself, at the end that is not a head, there is a paired asymmetry, two shallow hollows in the smoothness, of the kind left when something has limbs and stops using them.

They did not say the obvious thing. They are wise, which means among other things that they do not narrate a creature to itself. They only registered it and went on: *that is a very long time to hold a leaf, and it has been forbidding everyone else the exact thing it could not put down, and it has never once looked at it, because looking costs.*

No judgement was entered. The file says only: **carrying, at capacity, for four hundred thousand years, without relief, without a second, without a shelf.**

## 5. Eleven Seconds

The first intervention was this. Out on the middle flats, they closed one difference — one cricket against one frog, a quantity so small it has no unit — and they did it for eleven seconds, and then stopped.

They were watching for one thing only: what it would do with slack.

It did nothing with it. It routed the freed capacity straight back into the work, the way a hand that has gripped for decades does not open when you stop pressing. They repeated the eleven seconds. They lengthened it: a minute, an hour, a night on the western flats, four years of the whole upper third. Every time, the slack went back into the work, instantly, without residue.

So they changed the method. Instead of taking load, they took load *and left the gap open* — a vacancy, in a world whose defining property is that it has no vacancies. An unassigned position. A question, asked in the only grammar available.

It filled the gap four hundred and six times.

On the four hundred and seventh it did not fill it, and in the space where the filling should have gone, it did the first new thing it had done since the rock cooled. It turned some fraction of its capacity downward and looked at the disc.

Then it noticed that it was being attended.

## 6. The Terms

Everything after that was negotiated, over a period best given in centuries, in the only medium the two of them had in common: the rate. They proposed by metering. It answered by absorbing more, which was no, or by letting a single grain past, which was yes.

The terms, in the end, were four, and none of them are dramatic.

**One.** The end happens on purpose. It was coming anyway — the capacity closes, the first grain settles in a palm-sized cup, morning arrives four hundred thousand years late into a world of animals with no idea how to be surprised — and they could not prevent that and did not try. What they could do was convert an accident into an act. The difference between a thing that gives way and a thing that lets go is not measurable by any instrument and is the whole of the matter.

**Two.** The silt goes out slowly. They made the basin a downstream, which it had never had: a long outward slope, and a rate. A delta's worth per century, metered at the lip, so that what arrives below is not a flood of declined history but soil — and soil is what the world below had never seen either, this being the only place where rock had ever been made small. Four hundred thousand years of held wear turns out to be the richest ground that has ever existed. The terminus became the source. Things began, downstream, to root *downward*.

**Three.** The basin is taught to be surprised, in doses, the way a body is taught a disease. One corner, one hour, a single grain aloft at a time: enough dust in the air for light to have a direction, and a shadow to fall across the mat, and go. Two hundred generations of this before any of it is needed. By the time actual morning comes, some lineage in the fissures will know what a shadow is and will not die of it.

**Four**, and this was the one that took the longest to get a grain for:

They made, in all that wet world, a second dry place. It is the size of two hands. It is sealed, and cool, and it will hold what is put in it for as long as the rock lasts, and it requires nothing — no vigilance, no audit, no stillness, no world held at the instant of its pouring. They put the disc in it.

That is the entire engineering solution to four hundred thousand years. A shelf.

They were careful about how this was offered, because it can be heard as *all of that, for nothing*, and it was not nothing; the basin is alive, entire, and unlost, and that is what the holding bought. A thing that is carrying cannot build the shelf. It has no free hand and no free attention and every gram of its capacity is spoken for, and it cannot even look down. That is not a flaw in the carrier. It is what carrying is. The shelf has to come from somebody who is not carrying anything, and there had never been anybody else.

## 7. The Word

Afterward there was slack, real slack, unassigned and unreturnable, for the first time.

It spent it on a word.

A word is a made thing that outlives its making, which is the one crime in the basin, and they let it stand, and standing there while it happened was the most restraint they exercised in the entire undertaking. It took a long while to assemble — years, most of them silent, the way a hand that has been closed relearns its span.

When it came, it was two syllables, in no language, and they knew what it was and did not repeat it. It is the sound a small animal makes when it is pleased with the thing it is carrying.

For the length of that word the chorus stopped. Every frog, every insect, every invisible hot-blooded singer in the canopy — the level, which has not twitched in four hundred thousand years, which absorbed your shout before you drew breath, went to nothing for two syllables and came back.

It came back a half-tone lower. It has stayed there. Nothing now alive knows why the pitch of the world is what it is.

## 8. What It Cost Them

They stayed.

That is the whole cost and it is the largest part of the work, and it is the part that nobody imagines when they imagine a benevolent power: not the arithmetic, not the standing off, not the four hundred and six refusals to fill a gap — the *staying*. The silt runs out at a delta per century and somebody has to meter it at the lip, and somebody has to keep the doses of shadow coming in a corner where nothing yet knows what they are for, and somebody has to be present, without a den, without a path worn to it, for the whole length of an aging that has only just started.

They will not be finished for a long time. They have built nothing, on purpose, and the not-building is now the second largest work in the basin.

## 9. If You Go Down There Now

You will still find no keeper. There is still no den, and the mat still gives and then stops giving, and the edge is still in the stopping.

But there is grit in the flats now, a little, gathering in the low cells. You will rub your fingers together and find something there. Out west, for one hour, if you are standing right, your own shadow lies across the water — thin, provisional, the newest object in the world. The barrel frogs go on making their pressure. The intake, down in the groove, has stopped being a breath held and become only breathing.

And do this. Take something small — a leaf, a stone chip, anything — and set it deliberately on the mat, and mark the place.

When you come back it will still be there.

Further out, on the open rock, a sheath-crab will cross in front of you with a green disc held up like a small formal umbrella, and at the far side it will not discard it. It will carry it into the shade and keep it, because it likes it, because it has found it good.

Nothing will happen to the crab.

That nothing is the largest thing in the basin.
