# The Late Guest

## The delay

It noticed the flats the way you notice a sustained note in another room — not as an event but as a condition that has apparently been going on for some time and that you have only now had the courtesy to attend to.

It came, and then it did not arrive.

It sat on the rim for eleven years. This was not deliberation. It was the first correct act, and it was arrived at almost immediately, in the following way: it looked down into the basin and understood that anything entering that place at speed, with intentions, unable to hear the local chord, would be a listener. Four legs or none, it would lean on things. It would snap four hundred years of pipe without malice, without noticing, and the fracture beneath would spend forty years growing the note back lower.

So it learned the pitch-map first, the way a child does, as a melody, before it was allowed to walk in it. Eleven years is a short apprenticeship for a thing of its kind and an absurdly long one by any other measure. It is roughly how long it takes a competent person to stop being an outsider on the flats, and the power, which could have known everything instantly, chose instead to know it at the speed the knowledge is normally acquired, because the speed was part of the knowledge.

It got wet in the first hour. It stayed wet for sixty years.

## The landlord

It had come for the place and for the being who lived in it, and it could not at first determine which being was meant. The basin offered several candidates and no way to choose between them: the three hundred people; the nine hundred listeners pouring themselves through the mist; the crawlers, who outnumbered everything; the grey-brown film; and the thing the people described, which was one enormous animal exhaling one enormous note and would therefore be the basin itself, including all of the above, including the question.

It resolved this by doing what any visitor is made to do. It asked, and someone took its wrist and put its hand on the ground.

So it paid its respects to the film first, which is correct, and which almost nobody would have thought to do. It spent a long while there — in a rimmed pool the size of a room, up to the wrist in something no thicker than a coat of spit — being introduced to the oldest and largest living thing in that world. The film has no organs and therefore no wanting. It eats gas and lays down a little lime. It built the dead high terraces on the rim a hair at a time and then followed the water down and is building the drone-quarter now, and it will follow the water down until there is no water, and it will not mind, and it has been answering the same question continuously since the fractures opened, by continuing.

The power understood, kneeling in that pool, that it had arrived very late to a conversation in which every party was already saying yes.

This changed the character of everything it subsequently did. It had come prepared to be generous. It found instead that it had come to a place where the entire population, at every scale, from the film to the elders on the worn benches, had independently arrived at the only posture the power itself had ever been able to recommend, and had been holding it for a geological interval, in the dark, wet, cold, without fire, at enormous volume.

There was nothing to teach. There was only the question of what a guest owes.

## The things it did not do

It did not lift the cloud. It considered this on the first day and dismissed it on the first day: the lid is made of the one degree of warmth at the mouths, and the warmth is the water, and the water is the basin. Light would have cost them everything for a view. It noted also, and thought longer about, the second consequence — that under a sky the eye would gradually become the organ, and the hand would stop being the thing you spend, and the whole grammar would go, and within four generations they would be a people who touched each other much less.

It did not give them fire. There is no fuel; it could have made fuel; it could have made the ground burn. It ran the consequence out in full and stopped at a single fact: a knife on the flats takes eleven months, and therefore a murder there requires a man to intend it from one spring to the next. This is not a quaint feature of a primitive economy. It is a moral condition that no advanced society has ever managed to purchase at any price, and the power, which had seen a great many advanced societies, declined to be the agency that sold it off for hot food.

It did not stop the note falling. This was the largest refusal and it was not close. The fall is not a problem afflicting their world; it is the syntax of their world. Every hand pressed flat into soft carbonate at standing height is an assent to it. To hold the chord steady forever would have been to walk into a room of people who had spent ten thousand years learning to say yes to an ending, and to remove the ending, and thereby to make ten thousand years of the finest thing anyone in that basin ever did into a misunderstanding.

It did not cure the deafness. It wanted to. Deafness there is blindness, it is the loss of the map and the road and the compass at once, and the power stood over more than one elder, in a silence, with the thing entirely within its competence, and did not do it — because the deaf are not damaged people in that culture, they are the instruments. Not being able to hear the present chord is the qualification. Restore their hearing and the archive collapses within a generation: there would be no one left who could hold a note that no longer exists anywhere in the world. It could have given them back their ears and taken away their history. It has done comparable things elsewhere, long ago, by accident, and does not any longer.

It did not lift the stone.

It knew the number. It had known the number since the eleventh minute of the eleventh year. Nine hundred years of stopped mouth was a trivial calculation and it could have shown them the interval in a form they could feel with a palm. What stopped it was not the courteous reason. It was that every generation of those people has produced someone who walks out into the mist and stands over an old stone with a hand on it and takes the hand away, and that the taking away is not ignorance. It is the only sovereign act available in a world where the ground decides everything. They have chosen, three hundred times over, not to be told. A power that overrides a renewed choice because it is confident the choice is wrong is not benevolent. It is merely correct, which is a much cheaper thing and does far more damage.

It did not remove the listeners. They break the pipes, they break the names, and one person in six is un-named once in a life by an animal that does not know they exist, and they are said to be lucky afterwards. The power looked at that carefully, the way you look at a strange medicine, and left it alone.

It nearly gave them a notation. It had it designed — a tactile one, knots and ridges, readable by fingertips worn smooth as stone, a thing that would have ended the drift in the archive inside forty years. It got as far as the first sample and then abandoned it, because a notation is a ruler, and a ruler is the stone, and it had already decided about the stone.

## The work it took

Here is what it did instead.

It stayed. It was named — it was born, as far as that place is concerned, within earshot of a mouth in the downpitch quarter, and its name was a location in the chord, shown with the hand as an interval. In the thirty-first year the listeners came through and snapped that mouth and it was broken-named for a season, flat hand turned over, empty, and it did not restore the pipe, and it let itself be given another name in a silence like anybody else.

Its hands were ruined by the twentieth year. It permitted this. It is a small thing and it was not a small thing; it is the entire difference between a visitor and a person.

It took the worst job. It became a carrier of the dying. Somebody must walk the ones who can no longer walk to a soft wall of new carbonate at standing height, and hold them there, and press the hand flat, fingers together, palm laid on, and then take the body to a pool and weight it and let the lime have it. The people did this already; they did not need help; but it is the one duty in that basin that nobody has ever wanted and everybody has done, and the power took a share of it and kept the share for the rest of its stay. It pressed a great many hands. It pressed the small ones too, at the height a carried child's arm reaches, which is the job inside the job, and it did not make a single one of those children live, and it does not offer an account of that, and it was there for all of them.

It went often to the lagoon on the northern lip, where the tubes go on underwater and the singing stops and there is nothing from shore to horizon but hundreds of thousands of columns of bubbles hissing in the silver dimness — the loudest and simplest part of the place, and the least loved. It loved it. Something should.

And in the fifty-second year it was asked into a silence by a woman of forty, broken-named, who had once taken a foreigner out to an old stone and not touched it.

They sat on the worn bench. She sang first.

What it sang back was the fundamental as it had learned it — from an old deaf woman who had put her mouth to the crown of its head and hummed a note out of her own girlhood sixty years before. That note was wrong. The power knew precisely how wrong, to a degree of precision that has no unit in that language. It sang it wrong, exactly as received, and when in due course it hummed it into the skull of a child it had drifted again, slightly flatter, because the power was by then sixty years older and its own throat had changed.

It did this deliberately and it would defend it. The archive of the flats does not transmit a frequency. It transmits the fact that someone held something for a lifetime and handed it on. Its content is fidelity, not accuracy. A guest who corrects it has not improved the archive; the guest has simply replaced it with a different object, one that no longer contains anyone.

## Escrow

It kept the true chord. Every century of it, from the opening of the fractures to the last measurable breath, held perfectly, unfalling, in whatever it has instead of a body.

It has never offered this to anybody. It stopped its own mouth over it: a fist of clay, a flat stone, and its palm laid on the stone. The same rule it was given, applied to itself, which is the only way a rule is ever really accepted by something large.

It made one provision. When the note goes below hearing — when the last pipes sag into pressure, and the water stops coming up, and the basin stands there hardening in the mist, wet and cool and dim and finally quiet — if there is anyone at all still there on that day, and the power intends that there will be, then it will sit them down in a silence, on a worn bench, and sing them the first note. The one the terraces on the rim were singing before they finished. The high one.

Not as information. The number will not matter then; there will be nothing left to measure and no one to be deceived by the size of the fall. It will be a keepsake, delivered at the only moment when it cannot cost them anything, which is the moment a thing like that becomes a gift instead of a verdict.

She was right, incidentally. It would be a high note.

## The last thing

At the end of its stay it went to a silence and sat with an old deaf woman on the bench, and she took its wrist, and turned its hand over, and laid her palm flat across it, and held it there for a long time, and did not let go.

It had come a considerable distance, with considerable power, on an errand of mercy, and this is what it was given: the local word for yes, applied to it, by someone with nothing, in a room made by breaking two hundred pipes and plastering the stubs.

It had not known before that assent could be a gesture rather than a conclusion — that you could say yes to a thing without the thing being good, without the fall stopping, without being told the number, and that this was not resignation and not a mistake and not a failure of nerve, but the oldest technology in that world, older than the pipes, first developed by a grey-brown skin no thicker than spit and practised without interruption ever since.

It stayed another four years and then it went. Before going it laid a hand, flat, fingers together, into the soft new carbonate of a tube wall at standing height, in the downpitch quarter, near a mouth that had once been its name.

The stone kept growing. The hand is under two layers now and will be under three. Nobody living does this, and everybody living knows what it means, and they will not say.
