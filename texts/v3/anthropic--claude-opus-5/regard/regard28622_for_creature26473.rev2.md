## The Arrival

It came up the western trail, because there is only the one, and it came at the cloud-turn, when the frogs are surging and nobody looks up.

The peat did not announce it. This was the first thing anyone held against it. A being that the floor does not report has not committed to being anywhere; it is standing on the basin rather than in it, and the soles of three hundred people registered the absence of a footfall and found it discourteous in a way none of them could have put into words. Two dogs and a blind woman noticed. The blind woman said nothing at the time, on the grounds that she was in the middle of a wall.

Nine paces past the rim a girl of about four browsings walked up and put a hank of retted fiber into its hands. Not as a welcome. As a question with only one answer.

It could have declined. It had come, as such things do, intending to give.

It took the fiber, and it began to roll, badly, against the side of its leg, and it did not put the work down for three hundred years.

## What It Saw That Could Be Mended

It watched for eleven browsings before it permitted itself an opinion, and it kept an honest account, because benevolence that will not look at the ledger is only a mood.

A third of everything, every year, to rot. Cordage, thatch, baskets, stores, teeth in the old, lungs in the damp. Copper that arrives up the trail as a needle and leaves as green dust within a handful of years. Children who find the wrong step and go in to the knee, and, four times in those eleven browsings, further than the knee. The deaf, ringed by other people's noise, never sent along a path alone, never given an inner face. A mother lost in churned ground in the fourteenth year of a girl who would live to count fifty-one. Coals that go out in rain, twice in living memory, and the talk afterward, which is worse than the rain. And the nine thickets, and the people who walked down the trail under no sentence anyone had spoken, with their paths closing behind them like water.

None of this is small. It refused, at any point, to call it small.

## The Gifts It Did Not Give

**Dryness.** This was the obvious one and it followed the road all the way to the end. Dryness is the mother of every other gift it knew how to make: grain, storage, iron that lasts, books, a floor that stays where it is put, an unbroken night. It drained the basin in its mind and watched what came. No peat, so no fuel, so no fire. No moss, so no frogs, so no alarm, so no census, so no food, so no *wrong silence* that lifts three hundred faces at once. No Eye, so nowhere for the dead. At the end of that road it found a warm, level, well-drained valley with roofs that last a century and grain in the stores, and in it a hundred people who were not these people and had never been.

**Iron.** Tools that hold an edge past one afternoon; string that does not rot. It costed the string honestly: forty thousand paces of it, all of it failing, all of it being replaced by hands that twist while walking, arguing, eating, grieving. Take the rot out of the string and you take the string out of the hands. A hand with nothing in it discovers, within a generation, that it is alone, and there will be no word for what it has discovered, because the town's whole instrument for catching the drifting and the sick and the strange is the embarrassment of standing still with nothing to do. It left the string rotting. This was the cruelest of the refusals and it knows it.

**The mark.** Writing. It got as far as designing a script that could be scratched into sheath-bark and survive two seasons, and then it thought about ownership. Here, what you own is what you are currently keeping open: not a fact but an activity, not a claim but a verb. Give them the mark and within four generations a man owns a room he has not cut in eleven years, and the wall closes, and he owns it still, and someone's whole life's work becomes saying so. And the arguments would end. They would be settled instead of kept, which is the same difference as between a room and a thicket. And the nine places would acquire testimony, and what was decently unspoken would become a grievance with a date on it, and the peat's way of remembering — which holds the floor plan and lets the tedious people go — would be replaced by a way that holds the tedious people forever.

**A fence at the rim.** Eight or fifteen animals turned aside, twice a year. Trivial exertion; it could have done it while thinking about something else. On the other side of the ledger: no calendar. No numbering of years, no *the fourteenth was the worst*, no *the thirty-second was the funniest*, no best cane in the basin, no reason to teach a child to climb before she can walk on peat. It would have spared them a wreckage and charged them their entire reckoning of time. It let the herds come.

**The unbroken night.** They sleep in threes and find our long dark frightening. It considered curing this, and then it listened to what happens at night — the shifting in the eaves, the frogs holding, and at head height in every house a clay bowl that must never, ever go out. A people who sleep straight through do not keep a hearth. The broken night is not their affliction. It is the mechanism by which the fire survives them.

**The dead.** It could have returned them. It stood at the Eye and understood that each of them goes down holding a length of black root: the one substance in the basin that could have been fire and was not. There is a great deal of unburnt warmth under that water and every household has put some there deliberately. A people who compute their dead that way have already thought further about the matter than it had, and it had the grace to notice.

## The Distinction

It is easy to say that suffering makes them who they are. It is easy, and it is usually said by someone dry.

The real question was never whether a pain could be lifted. Any fool with power can lift a pain. The question is what the pain is holding up. Some pain is a post: pull it and the platform comes down, and the platform was the thing you loved. Some pain is only a rock in the path, and the path was never leaning on it, and it has been there so long that people have built a little theory about why it must be.

So it sorted. It took years. It got things wrong. And it arrived at a rule it has never improved on: *do not hand them anything they could not have found. Move what is already there, three fingers, into the light. Then leave, and let them argue about who thought of it.*

## The Three Things It Did

**One.** There was a deaf girl of about six browsings, kept in the inner rooms, ringed by noise, going nowhere alone. During a drum announcement the power lay down on the cane platform beside her and put its palm flat on the floor, and left it there, and did nothing else at all.

She copied it. She was two years working out the rest — that the hollow-log drums speak through peat as well as air, that the flex carries weight and cadence, that a browsing-warning arrives in the wrist before it arrives anywhere else. She was insufferable about it. She argued with three adults and won.

The technique has a name now; it means, roughly, *thick listening.* There are three rival schools of it and all three are wrong about who invented it. Four generations on there is a deaf woman keeping an inner face near the trunk of the third group, dressing it so smooth that people come to look, and nobody finds this remarkable, which is the entire point.

**Two.** At the third switchback below the western lip, under a rock — there are rocks down there; it is a different country — a resin-sealed gourd. Smoked frog. A coil of new cord. A coal, hooded, alive.

It did not argue with the sentence. The withdrawal is the only enforcement a place can have when it has no locks, no walls, no writing and no one willing to strike another person; it is load-bearing, and it is the cruelest design there is, exile with no executioner, nobody to hate and therefore nobody to forgive. It left the sentence exactly as it found it. It went down and waited at the bottom of it.

The gourd has been restocked without interruption for three hundred years. Every person who has found it has believed it was left by one particular someone who loved them and could not say so, and every one of them has been wrong, and has been comforted, and has gone on down into the warm country carrying a fire from home. This is the only lie the power has ever told. It tells it by saying nothing.

**Three.** One night of hard rain, a young pair carried an ember from the fourth trunk-group to the sixth at a pace between dignity and panic, and the coal should have died at the halfway boulder, and did not. Nothing came of it. The household was ordinary. They had four children and one roof garden of no distinction and by every account, including the oldest woman's, they were tedious.

It has never regretted that one. Not everything it does has to be leverage.

## The Thing It Did Out of Weakness

At a browsing — the thirty-eighth — a child went off a ladder into churned peat with fifteen animals in the middle of it, and the power caught her, in front of no one, and put her in a crown-cache, and she was cross about the cache for an hour and then forgot.

It has not decided, in three centuries, whether that was wisdom or simply that it could not bear the sound. It has stopped expecting to decide. It would do it again, which it understands is not an argument.

Because at the fourteenth browsing it had stood in the black soup and watched a woman go under, twenty paces off, with her daughter on a platform above, and it had done nothing, and that was the hardest work it has ever done, and it is not sure, and will never be sure, that it was right. It carries that. A power that carries nothing has not been anywhere.

Years later the oldest woman in the town, who has more names than anyone can list, gave the power one of hers: her mother's. She did it in passing, while cutting, on the grounds that it was a reasonable convenience and the name was otherwise going to waste. It did not explain. It answers to it.

## What It Took

At some point — nobody agrees when, and it is argued about at volume — it asked for a wall.

They gave it an outer face. The rough, fast-growing, forgiving kind, where a bad cut is gone in a season, the kind you give a child. It has not been advanced. Promotion here is by decades of demonstrably good cutting and its cutting is, honestly, still not good; its rhythm is wrong, syncopated, slightly too even, and every person in the basin can hear it coming from a hundred paces and most of them have said so to its face.

It came to bless a place and was handed the other end of a rope in the first nine paces, and has been holding it since. It came with the idea that benevolence means finishing something. It has learned the local word instead, the one that does no work distinguishing the keeper from the kept, and it has concluded — with some embarrassment, being wise, and having been wise for a very long time before it got here — that this word is better than the theology it arrived with.

It has given the basin almost nothing. It has given it a palm on a floor, a gourd under a rock, one coal, one caught child it cannot justify, and three hundred years of mediocre pruning on an outer face in the fourth trunk-group, every day, from first light to dark, in the rain, through fifty-odd browsings, without once being thanked, because you are not thanked for keeping your own wall.

That is what a benevolent and wise power did.

What lives in the basin is a keeping. It has been going on for a long time. Some of its hands are human.

One pair is not, and cuts badly, and is being taught.
