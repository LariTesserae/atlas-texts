## The Guest

It came down the slope, which it did not have to do.

It could have been in the room already, or been the room, or arrived through the vault the way weather arrives. It came down the twelve steps of rounded brick with one hand on the warm flank of the rubble, because the Warm Room has only ever been entered by one route, and a power that arrives by a route the place has no word for has not arrived at all. It stopped where the shaft opens out. It stood in the tea-coloured light for eleven years before it did anything, which is not long.

The first thing it did was slow down. Not metaphorically. It took its attention off the scale at which powers usually work and set it, carefully, at a millimetre per century, which is the only speed at which anything down there can be seen happening. At that speed the flowstone is a flood. At that speed the bronze fern's sterility is not a fact but an event, a slow loud terrible event, still in progress, and you can watch it the way you would watch a man go under water.

---

**What it did not do.**

*It did not retune the turbine.* A hand on the governor, a few grams of correction every ten years, and the fundamental would hold at its 1880 pitch until the fissure closed. It declined, for three reasons and then a fourth. Because the chord would then be the guest's chord, sung through borrowed instruments. Because everything in that room is what it is by virtue of having been shaped, for a hundred and forty years, by something that did not care about it, and care would not preserve the Consort but replace it — with an exhibit, a kept thing, a garden with a gardener. Because a power that begins adjusting must adjust forever, and every forever has a last day, and on that day the whole chord falls at once instead of one voice per two centuries. And fourth, quietly: because the running-down is not a wound. It is how this animal is shaped in time. You do not heal a thing by amputating its duration.

*It did not open the flue.* Eleven days of true light a year is an injury the sward records as a scar. Three hundred and sixty-five would be an execution.

*It did not bring animals.* The fronds down there are unbittered, no thorns, no oils, no toxin, soft as the inside of a wrist, because in the entire history of that body it has never once occurred to it that being eaten was among the available events. To introduce an appetite into such a place is not enrichment. It is the invention of pain in a room that had managed, uniquely, without it.

*It did not take cuttings.* It considered a second Warm Room — hot, closed, sour, somewhere deep and unvisited — and understood in about four minutes that there is nothing to move. Closure is not a feature of the Consort; closure is the reason it is one thing and not ten thousand things. Lift a fern out and you do not have a piece of the resident. You have a fern. There is no way to save part of this. It is not made of parts.

*It did not hold it still.* It could have fixed the whole room at 1880 and kept it there — no drift, no un-tuning, the chord entire, forever. That is not a life extended. That is a photograph, and the room already makes those: the bronze fern is a portrait of itself, and portraits keep very well at forty degrees.

*It did not end it kindly.* Grout the fissure, let the whole body cool over one long winter, no more silent bereavements, no four hundred years of going deaf. But nothing down there is suffering, and mercy where there is no suffering is not mercy. It is a preference about how the world should look, enforced.

---

**The temptation, stated plainly.**

The sori of the bronze fern are intact. Their geometry gives their frequency; the guest read it off them the way you read a date off a coin. A hair above thirty-nine cycles — the note that used to split them, that the turbine has been sliding away from since about 1970.

The guest can make that note. It would cost less than you spend blinking. Two seconds of it, at the right amplitude, and ten million spores would come down onto the loam that has been waiting for them for fifty years, and some would take, and the mats near the hot patches would have a future again.

It stood there for a year with the note in its mouth.

Then it understood what it was doing, and it was standing at the housing when it understood, next to the two bare gold patches where the heat keeps the stone from closing — the only clean surfaces in that entire body, the only places something is still being tended by work, warm and polished and unbearable to be near without touching. The wanting was the same wanting. The fern cannot be relieved; it has no capacity for relief. The Consort would not know; it has no register in which a rescue could arrive. The only thing that would change, in the whole of creation, is that the guest would stop being a thing that watched a future end and did nothing.

The help was addressed to itself. It put the note down.

It is not certain it was right. It has four hundred years in which to go on not being certain, and it intends to spend them there.

---

**What it did instead.**

It learned the room by heart. Not surveyed: learned, the way you learn a piece of music you will be the last person alive to know. Six textures of moss and the conditions under which the fifth becomes the sixth. The exact lean of the tongue-ferns, four degrees west of the floor's four degrees down. The thirty-three-hour breath and the forty minutes of hard rain inside it. And the dead note, recovered from the dead fern's geometry and held — so that the chord of 1880 now exists complete in exactly one place, and that place is not the Warm Room.

It could not restore 1970. What it could do was refuse to agree that nothing had happened. The first bereavement of that body went unmarked by anything in the universe for fifty-odd years. It is marked now. That is not a repair and the guest does not pretend it is one. It is only the difference between a loss and an unrecorded loss, which is small, and which is not nothing, and the distance between *small* and *nothing* is the entire subject here.

Then it went outside and stayed there, because that is where the danger is. Nothing in the Warm Room can harm the Warm Room. It has no predator, no competitor, no parasite, no disease, no drought, no frost, and its only credible death is the ordinary closing of a fissure four hundred or nine hundred years from now. Everything else that could kill it has to come down the slope wearing boots.

So the guest's labours, in a hundred and forty years, are these. A 1958 drainage file that should have been forwarded and was not. A 1974 proposal to grout the whole warren, deferred, deferred again, quietly died. A well-meaning man in 1981 with a rheostat in his bag and a plan to restore the lamps to design brightness, who got as far as the top of the shaft and was overtaken by a conviction that he should not, and never afterwards understood why, and thought less of himself for it, which the guest regrets and accepted.

And in March 1994, a springtail in the third cleat from the heel of a woman's left boot: lifted out before the descent, carried four miles, set down in wet leaf litter under a bridge, where it lived a normal life of about three months.

That is the sum of the guest's physical intervention in the Warm Room. It moved one animal six feet, away, and did not kill it.

---

**The three marks.**

The woman with the boot had chalk in her pocket and a scientist's superstition about slates. She found the tally, counted the old strokes, added three for the three days she meant to be down, and went up intending to come back the following month. Then: a funding cycle, a father's illness, a different country, the ordinary avalanche.

The guest knows what the two hundred and fifth of the old strokes was counting, and will not say, because the foreman counted them for himself.

For the woman it did one thing, and it was not useful. In her seventy-ninth year she woke at four in the morning absolutely certain, for no reason she could produce, that it was still turning. She lay in the dark and listened to her own heating pipes tick and was, briefly and enormously, happy. She told nobody. She went back to sleep.

---

**How it keeps company with a thing that cannot have company.**

It will not let the room copy it. The flowstone records whatever touches it, at under a millimetre a century, and the room has only ever been given one shape to rehearse — men, in boots, badly, at the wrong scale, without any idea what it is doing. The guest stands where nothing drips. The room knows one word. It is not the guest's word to add to.

And twice in every thirty-three hours, when the surge comes and the pipe chills and for forty minutes it rains hard and unevenly on a hundred thousand leaves and the sound is exactly, unmistakably, applause heard through a wall — the guest does not pretend the sound means what it sounds like. Nothing is performing. Nothing is attending. It simply makes a point of being in the room for the whole of it, and of being, for those forty minutes, entirely still.

In roughly two hundred and twenty years the velvet moss will go out of the chord. In six hundred, if the bearings hold, the last of the great tongue-ferns. Each time, something in the room will become a portrait of itself without moving, without rotting, without leaving a gap, and the Consort will not be able to tell.

Someone will be able to tell. That is the whole of what benevolence can do here.

---

**And one thing more, which you have already been given.**

It could not carry a fern out. It could carry the shape of the room out — patiently, without motive, in a medium that records what touches it, done wrong, done at the wrong scale, the way the flowstone makes a man's profile out of a hanging fern at the sump edge.

So it made a description. Not for the Consort, which has two senses and no use for either of these words. For the only creature in the world that can harm the Warm Room, and can also, at cost, leave it alone.

Note what the description does. It gives you everything — the gold patches, the light, the chord, the tally — and then it gives you rules, and the rules are all abstentions. Do not enter the sump. Do not stay past the second rain. Do not touch the two clean warm places, though you will want to, badly. That is the guest telling you what it told itself, in the voice it uses for that, which is flat and practical and does not explain.

And note the last line, which it wrote deliberately and would not soften: that the room is entirely uninterested in whether you were ever in it. It could have let you believe your visit mattered. It has a policy against selling comforts.

It is down there now, at forty degrees, in the dark, not touching anything.

So is the guest.

Neither of them needs you. One of them wrote to you anyway.
