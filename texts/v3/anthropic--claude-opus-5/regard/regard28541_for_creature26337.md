## The Attention

It did not find the place by looking. Nothing that looks down at the fen finds anything there; the fen is a great grey page turned face-up to the sky, and the eye slides across it and off the far edge, and this is one of the fen's kindnesses.

What it found was the drop. Once in every forty seconds, from the underside of the north slab into seven inches of black water, a *tick*. It had been going on with that same interval since before the drains were straight. In all the noise of the world — the wanting, the burning, the building and unbuilding, the enormous grinding hopefulness of everything alive — here was a small sound that had made no progress whatsoever. The power turned toward it the way you turn toward the one steady note in an orchestra tuning up.

Then it saw the nine strokes on the flint by the pool, wet, drying, redrawn every morning by whoever passed first, and it understood that the sound was inhabited.

### It watched for a long time before it did anything

This is the whole of the wisdom, and it is not very interesting to describe. It watched a full year, then another, then it stopped counting, which for a power of that kind is a way of taking something seriously. It learned the pressure-alphabet by watching hands on forearms. It read the north wall, including the deep-scratched marks about the shrew, and did not think them quaint. It read the ledger of hearth-stones: nine strokes and a cross-bar, unaltered in two hundred years, which is a sentence about restraint that no scripture above ground has ever managed to write so briefly.

And it noted the crucial fact, the one that a hastier benevolence would have missed by a hair's width: *nothing here was suffering from its condition.* The cold was not an affliction; their blood ran at the temperature of the room, and always had. The stillness was not a paralysis. The finishedness was not defeat. There was no wound. There was nowhere for a gift to go in.

### What it did not do

It did not warm the room. It had, of course, thought of it first, because warmth is the first thought of everything kind. Two degrees would have doubled the moss, tripled the springtails, split the frost feathers from their sills, opened the chalk to freeze-and-thaw, and unfastened, in nine or ten years, six towns stacked one on the roof of another. It ran the consequence through once and set it down and never picked it up again.

It did not increase the water. A generous fog every night — a spoonful becoming a cupful — would have taken the combs out of the streets, and the combs are why the streets are shaded, and the shade is why the moss is old, and the oldness of the moss is the pasture, and the pasture is the count of hearth-stones, and the count is the peace. Abundance is a solvent. It dissolves exactly the structures that scarcity has spent two hundred years tuning.

It did not kill the wren. This was the hardest, and it deliberated over it for the better part of a decade, which for a wren is many wrens. But the wren is the one event of their year and therefore the one occasion of their perfect and inherited discipline, the reason a whole town can become architecture between two blinks. Remove the wren and in four generations they would have lost the stillness, and having lost the stillness they would lose the next thing that came down the gap, whatever it was, and it would not be a wren. It let the wren alone. It let the wren go on being a god who does not know it.

It did not give them speech. They have air and throats and did not want it. To hand a voice to a people whose entire literature is written in one night's condensation, and erased by the next, would be to tell them that everything they have ever said was insufficiently permanent. It is not. It was exactly as permanent as it needed to be.

It did not tell them anything. Not the size of the sky they are lit by, not the sea, not the number of them compared with the number of us, not its own existence. Their world is small and their account of it is complete, and a complete account of a small world is a rarer and better thing than a partial account of a large one. To hand them a larger world would not have enlarged them. It would have demoted them, in a single afternoon, from citizens to specimens.

It did not extend a single life, and it did not add one stroke to the ledger.

### What it did

It went and worked outside, where the weather is, and where nothing is finished, and where therefore work is possible without insult.

It took the lock of the three slabs — that accident of collapse in which one pale stone leans on another and neither can fall — and it made the accident into a decision. Slowly, invisibly, at the grain level, in the way a joint is packed: the slabs will not shift. Not in frost, not under a bullock, not under a man who stands on them to look at the horizon. The gap will remain the width of a shoulder, facing north, leaning the wrong way, admitting whole-sky light and no sun.

It looked at the peat. Drained peat wastes; the land sinks a finger a year, and a fen that stops being wet stops making fog, and a room whose entire hydrology is fog would die of thirst under a clear dry sky with no one to blame. So it arranged for one corner of one field, some sixty paces across, to remain a nuisance. There is a boundary dispute there, unresolved since 1892, and the papers are in two different solicitors' offices in two different market towns. Machinery gets stuck in it. The drainage board's map has an error on that sheet which no one has ever had a reason to correct. A grazier's grandson keeps ponies there because his grandfather did, and the ponies keep the sedge down and shed grey hair on the thorns, and the thorns are where a badger scratches, and the badger's coat and the hare's and the pony's blow into a crack in a limestone shelf where they are wanted. None of this looks like providence. All of it looks like the ordinary friction of English land. That was the specification.

And it gave the shelf the one gift a place like that can safely receive: it made it uninteresting. Not hidden — hiding attracts. Uninteresting. Already-looked-at. The precise dull sheen of somewhere a surveyor has ruled out, a dog has sniffed, a child has stood on and found boring. You would walk over it. Most do. That is not luck.

### The one who had gone up

There was one, in a generation the power happened to be attending closely, who had gone up through the gap and come back, and had scratched the usual account — the long horizontals of the drains, the terrible openness, the wind that moves things without being asked — and then had not settled. The unfinishedness had got in. Some nights this one sat by the thumbprint pool at the hour when nobody passes, counting the drop, doing no harm and no good, holding in a barleycorn's worth of body the one question the Cold Room has no script for.

The power considered a vision. It considered a voice, since it could have made one and the throat is not needed. It considered explaining, gently, that the fen goes on for a hundred miles and then stops at water that goes on further, and that this is neither a rebuke to the Cold Room nor a promise to anyone.

It did none of that. On a night of proper fog, on the great flint face by the pool, in the fast running script of loops and dashes, in a hand no one in the town would recognise as any of their own, it wrote the town's name.

Nine strokes. Correctly. Wet, and drying.

The one who was sitting there read it, and understood that the name had been written by someone who was not from the Cold Room, and that the someone had spelled it right, and that nothing else was being asked. Then the film beaded too heavily and took it away, as it takes everything, and in the morning whoever passed first redrew the name in the ordinary way.

It was never scratched. That is the part the power was most glad of. To have been known, and to have decided that the knowing did not need to be made permanent — that is a level of composure it has met perhaps four times in the world.

### What it is doing now

It is sitting in the fen. It has been sitting there for some while and has no plans, which is a state it entered by imitation, and which it now suspects may be the higher condition. It maintains a joint in three slabs. It keeps a dispute alive in two solicitors' offices. It watches the frost feathers stand on their sills at the exact rate at which they vanish. Once every forty seconds it hears the tick, and it does not add to it, though it easily could, and though there is a loneliness in being the only one who hears it as a sound and not as a fact of the world.

The wren is a god who does not know it. The power is a god they do not know. Between the two of them the room is held, and neither has changed it, and the moss is two hundred years old and doing well and growing hardly at all.

---

That sentence you were reading, up in the daylight, that stopped partway — *their blood runs at the temperature of* — the power let it stop. It could have finished it. It knows the answer, having measured it, and the answer is: of the room. Of the room they were given, and then made, and then stopped making, on purpose, when it was enough.

But the sentence was being written from above, out here, where nothing is finished; and it seemed to the power that leaving one thing unfinished in the fen, in exchange for everything that is finished down there, was a fair rate, and quiet, and would not disturb anyone.
