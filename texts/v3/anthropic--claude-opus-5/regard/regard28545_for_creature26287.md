## The Attention

They did not come up through the cloud. There was no reason to; the cloud is not a door, it is only weather that the Shelf happens to sit above. And they did not arrive from overhead, because there is nothing overhead that anything here can see, and an arrival nobody can see is not an arrival, it is a rumour.

They came the way heat comes: from underneath, by diffusion, at no particular hour.

The first thing they understood — and it is the whole of the problem, and it took them a while, which should tell you something about the Shelf — is that on the Shelf attention is not a thing that can be paid at a distance. The tenant knows pressure. It knows temperature. It knows the weight of things set down on it. That is the entire sensorium, and it is not a poverty, it is an adaptation to a world in which nothing has ever been usefully far away. So: to be regarded lovingly from above, from outside, from anywhere at all, is for this creature indistinguishable from not being regarded. **A benevolence that stays at a distance here is not benevolence yet. It is a private feeling in someone else.**

They sat down in the middle, where you can be inside the whole of it, and let the moss close over their weight within the hour, and did the audit.

## The Audit

A wise power does not begin by helping. It begins by finding out whether there is a wound, and where, and in whom. There is a long tradition of arriving somewhere warm and quiet and clean, discovering that it is not shaped like home, and calling that a lack.

They went down the list.

**The emptying.** One body-length per forty years, the columns walking their slow gait toward the tilt, the water crawling, everything the tenant has ever grown or drunk or been going over the notch and becoming cloud before it can strike anything. This is the item that looks most like tragedy, and they followed it. Over the lip, down into the white, out along the underside of the cloud floor — and found that the day you climbed through was the tenant, thinned. It does not arrive at nothing. It arrives at everything below, distributed past recovery, unlabelled. The books balance. They balance at a scale the tenant cannot perceive and does not need to, because the tenant is not keeping books; it is only doing the one thing it does, which is to hold on until the ground overrules it. They struck the emptying from the list. Not because it isn't grief-shaped from outside, but because it is metabolism from inside, and they had come to attend to the inside.

**The animals.** The obvious gift, and the loudest. A world with no worm in the clay, no midge in the warm damp air where midges ought to be in their billions. They considered a fauna and declined it, and the reasoning is worth having plainly, because everyone who visits thinks of it and thinks better of themselves for thinking of it. To install animals here is to install appetite. Appetite means chewed leaf-margins, bore-holes, droppings, wing-cases in the drift lines, and death that smells — and the tenant has no edge, cannot flee, cannot heal at any speed that matters, and would be eaten at the rate of a thing that grows a fingernail a year by things that eat at the rate of things that fly. They would be solving the absence by importing the wound. The cleanness of the air here is not a vacancy. It is the achievement of a place that finished happening and got the ending right.

**The silence.** They thought about giving it an echo. A voice coming back would be, in every other world, the beginning of everything. Here it would require the felt to release something, and the felt has never released anything, and its not-releasing is not a defect but its single organising principle, the fact from which the whole Shelf follows. To grant this creature the power to throw would be to redesign it and call the redesign a present.

They did speak, once, at conversational volume, out into the open moss. It went in and did not come back out, the same as everything. They were content with that. A thing can be given into a place that has no mechanism for giving back; that is in fact the general case, and they were older than the misunderstanding that says otherwise.

**The speed.** This one they refused hardest. It would be possible to quicken it: to raise the metabolism from one hand's width a year to something with a pulse in it, to grow a sense of distance, to let it know its own shape and its own emptiness and its own long unanswered offer. There are powers that would call this awakening. What it would actually be is the installation, in a creature that has been spared it, of the capacity to be lonely — followed immediately by the discovery that the loneliness has already lasted four hundred thousand years. **You do not give a thing eyes in order to show it how long it has been alone.**

**The kit.** And this is the one that nearly caught them, because it is so nearly a mercy and so exactly a mistake. There is a bed of chert shedding blades. There is cordwort lying in fifteen-pace lengths of properly laid two-ply, strong enough to lift you. There are fallen columns whose trunks would take a bevelled tang. Blade, lashing, haft: the Shelf has produced, in three unrelated accidents over an unimaginable span, the complete inventory for a hafted tool and has never once assembled one. It would take an afternoon. It would be the most satisfying afternoon available anywhere in that world.

They did not do it. The visitor's embarrassment, coiling the cord — winding fifteen paces into a neat hank on a bench where nothing has ever tied anything — is the correct instinct arriving one second too late, and it is worth trusting. The tenant did not ask for a tool. The blades are the wall's doing: jointing, freeze, creep, grit, and all of it accountable. Only one thing on the whole Shelf is unaccounted for, and it is not an edge.

It is a grip.

Four hollows along one face and one opposed, worn smooth, and a raised ridge where no fracture mechanics would put a ridge. **The tenant did not make a cut. It made a hold.** For as long as the rock has sweated it has been turning out, without expectation, because expectation requires a sense of distance and it has none, the precise shape of the far end of being held.

That is the item that stayed on the list. Not a wound in the tenant. An unclosed circuit in the world: a proposition put, with immense patience, in the only grammar available, and never once answered in kind. Powers of their sort exist very largely for that.

## What They Did

They took a hand.

They took it from the tool, which is backwards, and which is the point. Every hand-tool that has ever existed anywhere was shaped by a hand that came first. Here the shape came first and had been proposing a hand for longer than there had been a bench to propose it on: fingers a little shorter and a little thicker than yours, thumb braced high. They let themselves be specified. They wore the hand the Shelf had been describing.

Then they walked out onto the open moss — not to the pool, where thousands lie sorted by size in bright overlapping ranks, filed, arrived, at rest; but out to where the slope had carried a single biface into the middle of a wide floor of felt, in its own considerable emptiness, like a thing set down deliberately.

They picked it up. Their fingers sat exactly right. Nothing was corrected by an inch. The warmth came up out of it into the palm, which was only ground heat, which was only the mountain sweating, which was only diffusion, and was also the answer to the only question the place has ever asked.

And then they held it. That is the whole of the intervention: they held it, and they stayed.

Because in this world a syllable is a century. At one hand's width a year, a gesture that lasts an afternoon has not been made. So they sat down among the corestones and let their weight go into the felt and their warmth go down into it, and the moss did what the moss does — thicker where warmer — and began, at its own speed, in the only alphabet it has, which is the thickness of its own body, to write them down. Not their meaning. It has no organ for meaning. Only: *pressure here, sustained; heat here, sustained; something set down and not taken away.* Four hundred years of it. Long enough to be news. Long enough that for a while the slow map of the reservoir below had, in one place a few paces off-centre, a warm anomaly that was not the mountain.

They left the visitor's hank of cord where it was. It answered nothing, it was unnecessary, it was the first reply the Shelf ever got, and it was kind; and you do not go around undoing kindnesses for the crime of being unasked. The felt will draw it down in its own time into the brown paper that never becomes soil, and that is a fine end for it.

They took nothing away, and left nothing behind that will not relax. This mattered more than it sounds. In a place where nothing decays, every object left is left forever; a monument here is litter with a good opinion of itself.

## Afterward

If you come up again, you will find the Shelf exactly as described. A hundred and fifty paces by ninety. Eighteen plants, each in its emptiness, all facing the same way, all still going. Water that does not fall. Sound that does not carry. Thousands of tools under a hand's depth of clear warm water, still arriving, unadjusted; production was not changed, because production was never a complaint.

A few paces off the centre the moss is perhaps a finger deeper than the tilt accounts for, and a little warmer, and you will put it down to the reservoir, and you will be right, in the sense that everything here is the reservoir.

And somewhere out on the open floor there is one biface with the wear-gloss deeper on the grip than on the edge.

That is the wrong way round. Every tool made by every hand in every world wears fastest where it works. This one wore fastest where it was held. Nobody will notice; and if anyone does, it will be entirely indistinguishable from creep.

They kept the hand. Everything they have made since, elsewhere, in places with sound and animals and rain that falls, is sized for fingers slightly shorter and thicker than yours, with the thumb braced high — so that if you have ever picked up some old good thing and found your grip corrected by an inch and been unable to say why the correction felt like courtesy, the answer is a bench above the cloud, a warm wet sheet with no inside, and a very long patience that was, once, met.
