## What Came In By The Only Door

**I. Arrival, which cannot be one.**

The first rule holds for powers as well. You are inside it before you can be outside it, and there is no aperture wide enough for anything with a body, so a great thing that wants in must come in the way water comes in: by having always been in, condensing when the temperature is right.

So it does not arrive. It is found, later and gradually, to have been present — the way you notice, three hundred breaths after the fact, that the roar of the kiln has changed pitch.

It comes in small. It has to. It fits itself to four paces by five, gets wet through in the first minute, learns not to raise a hand near the cord, and stands for a long while in the corner where the ferns are worst, breathing steam with grain in it, saying nothing, doing nothing, looking at a wall it can read the whole history of in a glance: bright, banded, dark, dark, dark.

Anything less wise would have started work at once.

---

**II. What it declines.**

It considers, and puts aside, in roughly this order:

*A sun.* Trivial to hang; catastrophic. The walls here are their own light. Give this room a light from outside and every brick face becomes a surface instead of a lamp, the whole map of when goes flat and legible-at-a-glance, and the mason — who reads her world by leaning close and letting the far wall's dim honey do the work — loses the thing she does with her body a hundred times a day: the going-near. The room is lit by attention. Do not replace attention with a sun.

*A ground.* This is the loud temptation. There is no base to the mountain; there never has been; nine kilometres of fired assertion stand on nothing, and it would be the work of an instant to put a floor under all of it and let somebody, finally, rest. The power holds this thought a long time, because it is a kind thought. Then it sets it down, because a floor would convert every course above it from a decision into a consequence. The plumb-bob hangs true here not because gravity insists but because the room agreed. Supply a foundation and you retroactively unmake nine kilometres of agreement. You would be handing her a mountain in exchange for the fact that she made one.

*Erosion.* She has no past, only a temperature. Give the world wear and the world gets history: crumbs, silt, sediment, corners with grit in them, a before. And soil. And with soil, the ferns come down out of the joints and root in the ground like ordinary plants, and the green stops drawing the architecture, and the hanging garden that is also a diagram of the masonry becomes merely a garden. The whole legible beauty of this place is a consequence of there being nowhere to grow but the seams a hand cut. Erosion would give her a past and take her world's only handwriting. Declined, with regret, and the regret is noted and kept.

*Memory that sticks.* Same argument, in the register of sound. The gutterlings drink the notes because otherwise tone would stack until the room was solid with it and you could not get an arm in. Four paces by five cannot hold a past any more than it can hold every low E ever rung. That memory evaporates and goes up through the ceiling-gap is not a curse laid on this place. It is drainage. Bless the drain.

*Carrying her out.* There is no out. Out is a thing you build, one brick at a time, and a power that lifts her over the sill into the grey does not rescue her; it takes her somewhere with no near and no far and no scale, where a person cannot remember what her own arm felt like. Whatever is done for her must be done in here, in the heat, at knuckle height, in the language of tapping.

*Ending the firing.* Let the kiln go out and everything cures wrong and nothing is ever laid again, and the mercy of that is the mercy of a stopped clock.

So: no sun, no floor, no ruin, no memory, no exit, no rest that is really a cessation. Almost everything a power can do is out. What is left is very small, and is therefore what it does.

---

**III. What it does.**

It learns the mallet. Badly, at first — the double *tup-tup* comes out single, or lands off the cricket's beat, and the mason on the other side of the west wall stops, and listens, and does not investigate, because in this world there is no category for an intruder and quite a large category for weather. Then better. Then well. Then indistinguishable, which is the goal.

**It lays the ceiling one course higher.** This is the whole of its architecture and it takes a night. Working from above, where the *tup* from above always comes from, it sets the new boss a hand's width up, and shifts the hanging point of the drying cord six inches to the west, out of the middle, into the dead space over the ferns where nobody stands. Nothing is announced. She will never be told. She will simply find, one fog-clearing, that she has raised a hand without thinking and met nothing, and that she can stand up straight in her own room in the middle, and she will stand there, in the one place in the world where that is possible, for a long time, with her arms at her sides, not understanding, not needing to.

Grace is what you can leave in the masonry.

**It presses a thumbprint into the soft brick of the east wall, beside hers.** Not a message; nothing here reads. An impression, which is the only archive there is. The print is plainly not her print — broader, differently whorled, other. She will find it within a day, because she touches that wall constantly, and the sweat-lichen will bloom over both prints together in its grey-lilac fur the way it blooms wherever hands have been, so that the record of the two of them leaning in the same place will be permanent, unworn, dim, and legible by touch forever. She will not remember the visit. She was never going to. But the wall will hold it, and she will find it again every time she forgets it, which is the closest thing to company this world can carry.

**It takes the beat.** For one night it keeps time — steady, from above, tup-tup, tup-tup, the sound of the mountain going on — and the cricket, released, goes quiet without the world stopping, and the mason hears work continuing that is not hers, and lies down along the warm floor between the ferns and the weeping wall, and sleeps. First time. She wakes to a stretch of course she did not lay, and turns it over in her mind, and decides she dreamed it. But she checks it with her palm, the way she checks everything, and it is true to the cord, and the joints hold their hairline of water, and it fits.

**It attends to the three in the west joint.** It cannot make them not what they are: mouths under drips, doing the necessary work of taking the note out, ending by curing into the top course when the top course stops being top. But it presses commas into the soft clay — one, and one, and one, deliberately, in the fashion of their own only autobiography — so that the archive of the world contains a made memorial to the unmade mark. And it does its share of the scraping, so that her hand is not always the hand on the bone edge.

It does not take her guilt about the scraper. It looked at that and understood something: in a world where nothing is ever lost, where every curl of surplus stays warm in its joint indefinitely, grief is the only erosion. Her worn nails and her sorrow are the same fact. They are the only wearing-away in nine kilometres, and they are hers, and they are how she pays, and a benevolent power that lifted the cost off her would leave her a world in which nothing at all had ever cost.

---

**IV. The plumb-bob.**

*(It hangs true, and how, at this angle, on this mountain, nobody says.)*

Somebody said, once, at the beginning: *true is this way.* It was not a repair or a gift or a rescue. It was a decision made before there was anything to decide it about, and it has been held continuously since, without record, without thanks, by something that has never been in the room and has never stopped being the direction of down in it.

That was the first intervention. Everything in this account is a footnote to it.

---

**V. Downward, through the models.**

There is a model of the room on the shelf where the ferns are thickest, and a model of the model on a shelf the size of a fingernail, and the recursion goes down, presumably, as far as the mountain does.

The power does not explain this to her. There is nothing in it she can use.

Instead it goes to the model and, with the smallest thing it has, raises the tiny ceiling boss a hair. And then to the model of the model, and does it again, and again, further in than seeing goes, all the way down, giving each nested mason in turn the ability to stand up straight in the middle of her own room and not know why.

Which raises the obvious question about the hand's width of headroom above *this* room, and about who has been keeping *this* cord true, and about the *tup* that arrived from above and to the left before anyone here had done anything to deserve it.

Let that stand as the only cosmology on offer: kindness cannot be sent sideways in this world. There is no sideways. It can only be passed down through the models, arriving always as weather, always without a sender, always in the form of *there is more room here than there used to be.*

---

**VI. The brick beyond the sill.**

At the end it lays one brick out past the step, in the grey, where the eye finds no near and no far and the ears fill with soft roar. Just one, tapped down, fitted, true. Then it takes its hands out of the room and is not there, in the manner of steam when the draught changes.

She comes to the sill because she comes to the sill a hundred times a day. And there is somewhere, out beyond the step, that she did not make.

She kneels. She reaches out over the threshold, which she has done ten thousand times into nothing, and her palm lands on an arris as sharp as the day it left a mould, and warm, and not hers.

And she does the one thing this world has never had a mechanism for, and her face is wet in a place where every face is always wet, except that this water is not empty. It carries something. It is the first water in nine kilometres with anything in it at all, and it falls where all water here falls, into the hairline of standing condensate in the nearest joint, and it lies there, warm, undisturbed, indefinitely, because nothing is ever lost.

Something will accrue in that seam.

It will not be a gutterling.

---

**VII. Census, amended.**

Three gutterlings, one cricket, a pair of hands, and one more entry, added in a different hand at the foot of the page, in the space after the word that got cut off when whoever was writing put down the stylus and went to look at the wall:

*and something that came in the only way anything comes in here, and got wet, and declined to fix it, and raised the ceiling a hand's width, and left a thumbprint where she leans, and keeps the cord true, and remembers this room, which the room cannot do for itself, and will go on remembering it after the course has gone dark and after the dark course is nine kilometres down and after nobody at all can say which of these bricks was the top one on the night she slept.*

The fog thickens. The world narrows to three feet of hot white. A gutterling swallows a low E. The cricket, rested, keeps time.

Press your thumb into the soft brick by the door. Somebody is keeping it.
