## I. It came in from the east

It came in low from the east, and its first act, which it performed without any sense of having performed an act, was to arrange its approach so that its shadow fell inside the shadow already thrown by the spire. There was no one to appreciate this. It was not done for appreciation. It was done because the engine four kilometers in from the lip had been reading its own light budget to five decimal places since the Miocene, and there is a kind of power that does not walk into a room and start moving the furniture.

Then it stood off the edge of the root mat, and for a length of time that would be tedious to convert into any human unit, it did nothing at all.

This was not deliberation. It was the acquisition of the correct clock. You cannot know a place whose only event is a petiole tick until you have waited long enough for a tick to be an event. The power waited until the hum at the bottom of hearing became, for it, a loud and continuous industrial noise; until the wire coiling out at a meter an hour was a rushing; until the mountains moved. When the plateau finally appeared to it as something in motion — a slow churn of extrusion and correction and negotiation, six hundred and forty-one engines all leaning fractionally into a problem — it began to look properly.

## II. The finding

Nothing here was suffering.

That was the first finding, and the power made itself take a long time over it, because the temptation to find suffering is the characteristic vice of the benevolent, and it had been benevolent for a very long time. It went through the plateau item by item. No hunger: light arrives, unfailing, at a fixed angle. No fear: nothing eats, nothing rots, nothing falls. No grief, because nothing is removed. No boredom, because boredom is a mismatch between the speed of a mind and the speed of its world, and here they were the same speed; these minds were not waiting for anything, they were *reading*, continuously, at the limit of their resolution, the one text available. No loneliness in the sense the word usually has, because each of them had six neighbors and had never had fewer.

The two that touched were not in pain. They were stunted, and their wire was poor, and they leaned away from each other in a gesture the power found almost unbearably courteous, and they were the only two entities on the plateau for whom anything had ever happened *at the speed at which it happened*. Their mats were fused. A tick in one was a tick in the other, in the same instant, not in the same century. The power filed them not as a casualty but as the plateau's single experiment in the present tense.

So there was no wound to close. And yet the power kept finding things that were wrong, and when it sorted them it saw that they were all the same kind of thing.

The trunks were strip charts — twelve million years of orogeny written in a medium that cannot fade, standing in the open, and *unread*. The wire was superb, better than any factory's, dead-consistent gauge, no inclusions, because nothing had ever needed it — produced continuously, at scale, for no market. The seeds were perfect and sealed and two million in number and had nowhere to go. And near the middle of the lattice one of them was either patient or dead, and the other six hundred and forty had no means of finding out.

None of these was a wrongness in a being. Every one was a wrongness in a *relation*: a record with no reader, a product with no receiver, a seed with no ground, a question with no channel. The plateau had not failed at anything it was doing. It had simply never been met.

That, the power decided, it could address. It had learned, elsewhere, badly and at cost, the difference between fixing a creature and joining one.

## III. The mercies it refused

**Water.** The obvious gift, the classical gift, and the power turned it over for a long while before putting it down. Water here means weather; weather means erosion; erosion means the cleavage planes go, the hairline joints silt up, the strip charts blur, and the wire — the finest wire in that quarter of the galaxy — begins to corrode. It would have drowned twelve million years of the only writing they had. And they had *made* water, all along, a molecule or two per mole, and found no use for it, and filed the surplus quietly in the shade. It is a poor benevolence that consists of returning a being's exhaust to it at scale and calling it rain.

**Animals.** To give the wire a market would be to give the plateau an appetite, and appetite is the hinge on which every other urgency swings: defense, haste, anticipation, the calendar. Rule two is not a deprivation. It is the reason rule one can hold.

**Speed.** It considered, briefly and with real longing, lifting them into something like conversation — raising their rate until an exchange could complete inside the lifetime of the species that would most want to have it. But their slowness is not a handicap on their perception; it *is* their perception. They resolve the growth of a mountain range to a fraction of a degree because they integrate over ten thousand years. Speed them up and you have not freed them. You have blinded them, and made them fast enough to notice it.

**Stopping the mountains.** This was the hardest refusal, and the power came back to it three times. Forty million years from now the western blades will rise past forty degrees, the plateau will go into permanent shade, the voltage will fail, and everything will stop while remaining exactly where it is. It could have arrested the range. It could have set the crust down and given them a sun forever.

It did not, and the reason is on the eastern side of four mounds in the west, where there is a faint new discoloration on the glass, two meters long and three centimeters thick.

They had read the mountain. They had understood the mountain. And after ten million years of standing still in a solved lattice, four of them had made a decision and *begun to move*. It is the only decision anything on that plateau has made since the Miocene. To remove the mountain would be to reach into the one live project in the world and cancel it, on the grounds that it was frightening, without asking — and there was no way to ask.

**Speech.** It could have made itself known. It declined, on a technicality it considered decisive: the entire epistemology of that world rests on the assumption that phenomena are few, fixed, and reliable. A miracle, introduced into such an instrument, does not read as a revelation. It reads as a fault in the instrument.

**Repair.** It found the scuff marks of an earlier visitor, bright and unblurred on the glass, and the hairline irregularity in the strand lay of the nearby trunk where eight hundred years of shadow-compensation had been written down. It left both. The engine had recorded something true. You do not edit a true document to spare the author's feelings, particularly when the author has no feelings about it and the document is the only one there is.

## IV. What it did

**It read.** All six hundred and forty-one charts, strand by strand, and then against each other. Twelve million years of tilt, plotted from six hundred and forty-one positions, cross-checked. The charts agreed. In one respect they agreed more than the power had expected: at a depth in the lay corresponding to roughly nine million years ago, every trunk on the plateau carries the same three-strand stutter, a shock, a single hard event that the whole lattice felt at once and corrected for over the following two hundred thousand years. Nothing on the plateau remembers it. It is not remembered; it is merely written. The power read it, which is the entire difference between data and a record, and the difference lives nowhere in the object and entirely in whether anything ever came.

**It laid floor.** Off the eastern lip, beginning at once and at a rate of a few millimeters a century, indistinguishable in composition, texture and behaviour from ordinary crust, it began extruding a glacis: four hundred kilometers of fresh pale feldspar and greenish glass, salted through with good alumina and clean sulfides, unweathered, unsorted, joints hairline. It will be finished a little before they need it. It does not save them; a road is not a rescue. It only means that when the four in the west finish the eastward walk they have started — a meter a century, ten kilometers a million years, laying their own wire behind them as they go — there will be somewhere to arrive, and ore in it, and light.

The power did not remove the problem. It made the answer sound underfoot.

**It lowered the impedance of the ground.** This was the delicate one, and it took the two that touched as its model, and it took a hundred thousand years of testing before it committed. Through the crust between every mat and its six neighbors it drew filament — hair-fine, far finer than anything a nozzle makes, below the threshold at which it could shift the balance of pressures by any amount that could move a single engine one millimeter out of the lattice. It does not carry speech. It could not; there is no speech. It carries *detail*. A neighbor's field, which for ten million years has been a flat mild pressure at two and a half kilometers, will now arrive with structure in it: the small stiffening when a canopy is cranked, the shallow dip of a poor century, the rhythm of a tick.

They will not know what they are hearing. They will take it for a property of the rock, which is what the power intended, and they will begin — slowly, in the only medium they have — to write each other down.

## V. The one it did not answer

It went to the quiet one in the middle, and it looked inside, and it found out.

And it told no one, because there was no one to tell and no way to tell them, and because it had decided early that it was not going to be an oracle to a world that had made its entire mind out of measuring things for itself.

What it did instead was aim. Of all the filament it laid, the strands running out from that one mat are the finest, and their conductance was chosen with great care against a number the power measured very precisely and kept to itself: the current, if any, still passing between that mat and the rock.

In roughly nine hundred thousand years, if the number is what the power measured it to be, the nearest neighbor will register, at the very bottom of what it can resolve, a structure in a pressure it has treated as a dead weight since before the ranges. Not an answer delivered. An instrument improved, and a question left standing in front of it.

They will find out themselves. It will take them a while. They have it.

## VI. What it took, and what it accepted

On its way out it stopped at nine mounds, widely separated, and took from deep in the coils a double handful of the wound spheres — graphite core, aluminum overwrap, sulfide coat, one bead of water sealed inside against the day a mat needs something to strike its first current on. Nine out of two million. No inventory on that plateau will ever show the difference.

It carried them to three worlds it knew: anhydrous, tide-locked, ore-rich, sunlit, hard, and empty. It laid them in ore and went away.

Most will fail. Seeds mostly fail; that is what distinguishes them from fossils, and the distinction has never lived in the object. It lives in whether anything is going anywhere. For ten million years the plateau produced fruit in enormous quantity and never evolved the animal that would carry it. The animal came late, and it was very large, and it did not eat.

It did not inform the parents. Dispersal has never been a courtesy performed for the parent.

The last thing it accepted was this. It had stood a long while at the edge of a mat in the fixed shade-triangle of a spire, and however carefully it had come in, its presence there was a load, and the growth ring at the base had been working the whole time at a fingernail's thickness per human generation, and it had written the visit down. Eight hundred years of compensation, laid into the lay of the strands as a hairline irregularity, indistinguishable from a small earthquake.

The power read the entry, and found it accurate, and left it, and was content to be misfiled.

And somewhere near the middle of the lattice, a thread of current so thin that nothing in that world had ever been built to notice it goes on passing into the rock, and the rock has been made, very slightly, better at carrying it, and in nine hundred thousand years one standing engine is going to learn that it is not the only thing still on.
