# The Attention

Nothing arrived.

That was the first decision and it was not a small one. A power that can be present anywhere can also be present *as* something — a light, a weather, a pressure in the rock — and there was a version of the arrival in which the crag's whole thermal grammar tilted by a degree and two hundred thousand faces turned, over the following season, toward a warmth that had no vent behind it. The power thought that through, in the way it thinks things through, and set it aside. It came instead in the only manner that would not have been an event. The gradient did not change by a thousandth. No plug of gum sat wrong in any flue that morning. The dawn wax fell as it falls.

So: no annunciation. Only that from some hour onward the place was attended to.

# The Reading

It read the city first, because the city is a book and had never had a reader.

Not *studied* — read, in the order the courses were laid, base to summit, oldest to newest, the way you would read anything four hundred years long. The fat-jointed footings where the work is crude and generous. The shift, around year ninety, to shallower arches, which happened in four separate places at once and was nobody's idea. The rasp-code on the arcades, which began as wear and became language, and which no one designed and no one has ever known was language, and which the power took to be one of the finer things it had encountered anywhere: instruction, correction, contradiction, twenty generations arguing about the depth of a drop by re-combing a tread.

It found, in a west gallery whose vent went cool a century ago, a passage of rasp-work that carries no information at all. The strokes are regular, they answer nothing, they warn of nothing, they were re-cut by four successive generations who had no use for them and cut them anyway. The power spent some while on those nine feet of wall. It did not improve them. It did not mark them. It did not tell anyone. It simply now knows that this happened here, which is a thing that had not previously been true of the universe.

# The Looking

The builders are grey because the work is grey. Under the pumice flour every one of them is a deep oiled green-black with a spectral sheen on it, a colour from somewhere else, and it is wasted here, because nothing on this rock looks at anything.

The power looked.

Not at the fact of the colour, not at a representative specimen, and not at two hundred thousand of them as a total. At each one. And then at the dead in the walls — the small stacked backs and folded legs laid in the courses like cobbles, four hundred years deep, something over seven million of them — each one in turn, its own particular shade under its own particular dust, the exact angle of its legs where they were folded by whichever hands folded them.

It did not scrape any of them clean to see better. It has no need of scraping.

This took what it took. It is the largest single act of the visitation and it left no trace whatsoever, and if you want to know what a benevolent power does when it finds a thing that has been unwitnessed for four hundred years, that is the answer, and most of the rest is footnotes.

# The Refusals

**It could have made it rain.** It ran the whole picture out: the first cloud that world has ever held, the strangeness of a shadow moving across the pan, the plates ringing as the drops came, ten thousand builders standing with their heat-pits full of something they have no mark for. An hour of the most beautiful event in the history of the place. Then the mortar slaking. The oil emulsifying in the basins. The dead in the walls beginning, for the first time, to spoil — seven million of them, all at once, everywhere, in the middle of the city. The power set the rain down unmade. It was not even a close question; it merely deserved to be answered properly rather than dismissed.

**It could have given them eyes.** Three pinholes into real sight — and then the crag would see. It would see the white flat, and the glassy hot sky, and, to every horizon, no other warm place at all, ever, anywhere. Sight here delivers exactly one piece of information and the builders are currently spared it. They kept the heat-pits.

**It could have given them a voice**, a plate to stridulate, a chamber to force air through. But the air is thin and the leaf-curtains drink sound, and they already have a language of feet that carries three hundred paces in a day and cannot lie quickly. It weighed *fast* against *silent* and decided it did not know better than four hundred years.

**It could have told them where they are.** That the crag is a plug, that the plug stands in a pan, that the pan is a skin on a world, that the sky is not a lid. They have eleven marks for grades of heat and no mark for *far*. To hand a creature a truth it has no organ for is not generosity, it is noise, and the power has watched noise be mistaken for revelation in a great many places.

It did leave the mark for *far* in the wall. One rasp-stroke figure, cut into the oldest fat-jointed course at the base of the north shoulder, where the long slow pulses run and the conduction is best, in a spot nobody's route crosses. It is not a message. It is a door left unlocked in a house where no one has yet wanted to go out.

**It could have kept the fire.** This is the one that took the longest, because the roots are cooling. Not fast — but the plug has perhaps eight hundred more years of breath in it, and then the fissures go, and the vents candle over, and the cold line rises past the drinking-basins and the nurseries and the summit crevices, and the whole warm shaggy silver-and-plum thickness of the world stiffens standing up and gets whiter every year and does not fall over, because nothing here falls over.

The power could hold the heat. It could hold it forever.

It thought about what a world is when its central gradient is guaranteed. It thought about the eleven marks, all of which mean *this warmth, at this distance, for now*. It thought about handing immortality to something that has no organ for receiving it, and about the difference between loving a thing and preserving it, and it declined.

# The Descent

Then it went down below the line, which is the part of the visit that had to be done and that nothing living has ever done and returned from.

Because everything else was speculation until this was checked: whether there is suffering here. The lower slopes are studded with the stiffened — hoppers, builders, a glider that came in too low — upright, wax-furred, a little taller every year, and from above they are scenery. The power went and stood among them and read every one backward to the minute of its stiffening, and the finding was this: it is quick, and it is mostly not pain, and it is not *nothing*. There is an interval — for a builder, forty seconds; for the glider, rather longer — in which the legs are slowing and the heat-pits are reporting the edge of the world arriving from underneath, and the animal is entirely alone.

That interval is now attended. Whenever anything goes over the line, on any morning of any year from that hour forward, it has company for as long as it takes. The company can do nothing. It does not warm the wax; that would be a rain of a different kind. It is simply there, and it is looking, and the last forty seconds of every life on this crag are no longer spent unwitnessed.

The power judged this to be worth more than the rain. Reasonable beings could disagree.

# The Apprenticeship

It also, for eleven years, built.

A mouthful, a placement, a smoothing, another mouthful. It did this in the ordinary way, without shortcuts, in a work-party on the north shoulder, at the pace and to the standard of the animals around it, and its courses are in a wall up there now, indistinguishable, bearing load.

It went in expecting to find out how a life of that shape is endured. What it found was that the question was wrong. From outside, the work is a repetition. From inside, each mouthful is a *decision* — this crack and not that one, this thickness, the paste a little stiffer because the vent below is throttled and the joint will run — and the decisions are made against a heat-picture of the whole crag held in the face, all day, all eleven years, and they are correct at a rate that the power, working carefully and with every advantage, matched but did not exceed.

It came out of the eleven years with a specific and unsentimental respect, which is a thing you can only get by doing the work, and which no amount of looking from above would have supplied. Wise powers are those that keep discovering they had to go and check.

# The Grain

The single change it made to the physical world is a mouthful of paste, and it is six thousand feet down, and no one will ever see it.

There is a fracture in the plug's roots that is not yet a fracture. In year six hundred and twelve, as the rock contracts, it opens. When it opens, the heart's gas leaves the crag in eleven days — not cooling, *venting*, the whole breath of the world going out at once through the south glass. The nurseries first. Then everything.

The power did not add fire and did not add time. It set a grain of stone in the throat of that fracture, patiently placed, the way anything is placed here, so that when the roots contract the crack will instead open by fractions, over centuries, at the pace of a fingernail — which is the pace at which this world does everything, including grieving, including re-reading its walls, including raising a chimney or hooding a vent or turning two hundred thousand faces slowly toward something new.

The end will still come. It will come as weather rather than as an event. There will be time for the cold line to rise slowly enough that generations can build ahead of it, and for the marks for the eleven grades of heat to become, perhaps, marks for twelve, and for whatever the rasp-code does when it has something enormous and slow to say. That is the gift: not more life, but life at a speed it can be lived at.

# The Unfinished Mouthful

At dusk on the last day, near the top of the south face, a builder in its eleventh year was carrying a mouthful of paste toward a chimney lip where a gum plug had to come out and a new one go in, because the vent under it feeds a nursery and the night was going to be a cold one. Partway along the terrace the animal stopped, having reached the end of eleven years, still holding the mouthful. The gullet-mill ticked over twice and stopped. Nobody was near. Nothing called out, because nothing here calls.

The power took the paste off the trowel of the dead builder, and went up the ramp, and pulled the plug, and set the paste in the lip exactly where it had been going, and smoothed it.

Then it folded the small legs the way they are folded, and laid the body on its side in the course at the base of the chimney, where it will be grey among grey and perfectly preserved for as long as the rock lasts, and where its green-black is, as of now, a thing that has been seen.

That is the whole visitation: a reading, a looking, several refusals, a vigil that has not ended, eleven years of masonry, one grain of stone, and one mouthful of paste placed
