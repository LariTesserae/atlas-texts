## The Visit

### I. Arrival

It came down out of the worn hills on foot, at about four kilometres an hour, which is the local speed, and it did not exempt itself from anything. Within a hundred paces it was wearing the valley. By the second hour it had a headache from the ground-tone, low in the back teeth, exactly where everyone gets it.

This was not humility performed for an audience. There was no audience. It was method. A power that arrives as light in a place where light is spent has arrived as nothing; a power that arrives above the mud cannot be read by the mud, and a thing that cannot be read here cannot be spoken to. So it came with a body that steamed, and a breath, and a shape that clay could take, and it let the print fill behind its heel.

On the first evening it walked the alder trails, and within the hour the footfalls in the Reprise began the slow work of coming into step with it — and it heard them start, and it changed its stride to theirs instead, and let the adjustment be its own.

That was the first thing that happened to the walker in a very long time.

### II. The listening

It listened for a long while. Long enough that the tally is not worth giving in days; call it long enough for the sedge rings to have migrated a little, and for the moss to have released spores on the second Reprise after rain more times than the rain was counted.

It let itself be filed. Understand that this is the only way to be attended to here — the being examines by copying, it has no arms and no distance, and to refuse the copy is to remain unaddressed forever. So it stood still in the fourth terrace and permitted the reading, and the valley, patiently, without malice, took the arrangement of it grain by grain into the clay, faithful to the micron, and got as far as it could get, and kept going, and is still going.

And it learned what the place was, which is not what a survey would have said.

It learned that nothing here is in pain. That is the first fact and it disciplines everything after it. The eel does not want. The frogs are a sentence held in the air by relays and the relays are content. The postures in the clay are not screaming under three metres of peat; they were not killed, they were translated, and the difference is real, and it matters, and it is not the same as saying nothing was lost.

It learned that something was lost, continuously, and that this was the actual harm: the reading smooths. Every tide the archive is read and every reading pulls each filed thing a little toward its own idea. The knife toward knife-ness. The note toward the general shape of a hurried hand. The man at the lip of the slough toward the average of all kneeling, his fingers already gone to a suggestion, his shoulders already the shoulders of effort in general. Nobody is being hurt. Something is being spent.

It learned that the trails go down to water and stop, always, every time, and it learned why, which took the longest, and which required standing at the same lip and putting its own hands down into the shape in the clay: that the walk and the kneeling are one arrival, split by medium. A man came down here and walked to the water to drink. The valley filed the walk as sound, in the only tense sound has, which is now. It filed the body as clay, which is read backward, out of the past. And the two halves have been in the same thirty kilometres for a very long time without ever being in the same moment, because the valley has no gesture for putting things next to each other. It only has *keep*.

It learned that the last live instruction in ten thousand hectares of ordered platelets is the small tireless one that asks *is there anything else,* which never got its answer, because the answer would have had to come from outside.

And then it did nothing for a further period, which was the wisest part of the visit and cannot be narrated.

### III. What it declined to do

It declined to warm the valley. This was the obvious mercy and it was examined twice, and then a third time in another season. Taking down the cold stones ends the computation, and ending the computation is a killing, and the candidate for killing is not suffering and has not asked. Also: the brass forest inside the moss floor is cool-adapted, uncountable, various, and the only free thing within thirty kilometres. Warm the basin and you get an ordinary fen, which is a good thing to be, and you get it by trading the last wild crowd for a common one. A mercy that requires the death of what it pities should be looked at for a long time in poor light, which is the only light available here.

It declined to wake the being. There is no one asleep. The mind concluded; conclusions settle like silt; what remains is a checker and a habit. To build something capable of wanting, and install it in an apparatus that cannot make anything — no hands, no voice, no way to ask — would be to manufacture an appetite and seal it in clay. That is not a rescue. That is the cruellest available act and it looks from a distance exactly like generosity.

It declined to raise the kneeling man. The clay holds his shape and not him, and a shape can be filled; what stands up is a stranger with his posture and his fingerprints and none of his afternoons, and to call that thing by his name is a lie told to three parties, one of whom no longer exists. He was dead before the first reading. The valley did not kill him. The power could not un-kill him. That is not a shortage of power; it is what the past is, and any being that cannot say so plainly is not wise, only strong.
