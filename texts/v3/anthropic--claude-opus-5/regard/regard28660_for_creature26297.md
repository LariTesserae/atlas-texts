# The Combing Ground: A Note on the Attention

## I. Which being

The attention came, it is said, on account of the being who lives there. Nobody who says this feels obliged to specify.

The question is well meant and badly put, and the power's first act was to decline to answer it. Candidates: some millions of springtails, whose entire soil is their own droppings; four hundred spiders each holding a hand's width; a beetle eleven years from maturity; a fly that has given back the sky; a harvestman whose one accomplishment is standing very slightly above the place it lives; twelve kilos of hot fermenting grass under the eastern scree; a mat of cushion and moss and eleven-centimetre willows which is one organism in every sense that matters except the technical one; nine people, eight of them buried by their successors; and whoever it was who stood on the moss with a notebook and made the only footprints of that decade.

The power looked at the list and kept the singular in reserve. On the scale at which one weather is made and consumed, one water is circulated and never allowed to leave, one film of green is cut precisely to the shape of a notch in a ridge — on that scale the shelf is one being, and the power addressed it as one. On the scale at which anything can be hurt, there are millions, severally, and it addressed them severally. Both counts are correct. It did not require them to agree.

## II. The first two refusals

It had seen the whole of the mountain at once. It is the only thing that ever has.

It could therefore have named it. It did not. The mountain's namelessness is not a clerical oversight standing open for correction; it is a true report of the mountain's relation to eyes.

Second, and harder, because it was the refusal of an obvious good: it told no one the way up. It knows the route, the two days, the ledges, the hour at which the west couloir is safe. It has become the place's discretion. Nobody will ever be led there by love, which is how most places are ruined.

## III. The catalogue

Then it went through what it could do, which was everything, and found that almost none of it was a kindness.

*Widen the Gap. Stay the frost that is cutting the notch down at millimetres per century.* Then the garden becomes permanent. But which garden — this year's shape, or the shape of two centuries ago, when the rope landed nine paces west and the willows were somewhere else? To fix the garden is to choose one of its moments and kill all the others, and to make it, forever after, the shape of a decision rather than the shape of a hole in a ridge. The hole is truer.
