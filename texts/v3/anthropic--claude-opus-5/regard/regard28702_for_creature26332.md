# What Attended

It came the way everything comes here: as a pressure before it was anything else. Nobody noticed, because there is always a pressure.

It had intended to be seen. It reconsidered within the hour, having understood the light. It had intended to speak, and reconsidered by the end of the first day, having understood that the face is not for speaking and that a voice arriving from a face would be, in the grammar of this basin, roughly what a hand emerging from a wall would be elsewhere. It had come to listen. It discovered that the word was already taken, and taken by a thing that stands a metre and a half at the shoulder and snaps two hundred pipes in a summer and cannot help it.

It took that as instruction and it sat down.

## I. The listening

It listened for four hundred years, which was not patience. Patience implies an appetite held in check. It simply had no reason to be finished sooner, and one very good reason to be slow, which is that the chord moves at the speed of the stone and cannot be heard faster than the stone means it.

In the first year it learned the sixty signs the visitor had learned. In the fourth it learned the six hundred that the visitor had not, including the eleven for the varieties of being alone and the nine that exist only inside a silence and are never made outdoors. In the eleventh year it learned to taste the films, all forty, and found that the visitor's "tastes of matches" was correct and inadequate; the dark one tastes of matches the way a language tastes of its alphabet.

By the fortieth year it could hold the entire basin at once — every mouth, every interval, the beat frequencies rolling as the lid rose, the four-kilometre corridor of stopped throats, the ninety-one silences, the shrill of the wasp-ground, the lagoon hissing at the north like a held breath — and it found that this was not a map of a place. It was a sentence in progress, begun before the terraces died, and it had a subject and a verb and it was going somewhere, and the direction was down.

By the hundredth year it knew what the handprints meant.

## II. What it wanted to do

It wanted to give them fire. This was the first want and the longest and it took two hundred years to put down.

There is no fuel. That is the whole of it. A fire in the Chord Flats is a fire that must be brought, and re-brought, forever, by the one who brought it; and a gift that must be perpetually re-given is not a gift, it is a leash with a warm end. It also observed what the first flame would do: it would make the eye primary. Within four generations the knot of shoulders would turn inward and become a circle of faces, and the hands would go quiet in their laps, and the map of pitch would become scenery, and there would be a people here with cooked food and a language they had swapped for it. Perhaps that trade is a good one. It is not obvious that it isn't.

It declined to make the trade on their behalf. It wishes to be clear that this was a decision *for* them, made by a stranger, and that it counts as the largest thing it ever did to these people, and that it did it by refraining. It does not pretend this is innocence. Refraining is a thing you do.

It wanted to lift the cloud lid. One good afternoon of sun on the flats. It ran the consequence and found that the condensation is the pool water, and the pool water is the film, and the film is the base and nearly the whole of it. The obvious mercy was a famine with a beautiful sky over it.

It wanted to fix the eyes. Then it stood in a knot of six people at the edge of the drone-quarter for a night, facing outward into the mist with a palm on the back of a woman's wrist, and understood that it had proposed to cure them of the organ of their intimacy. They are not suffering from blindness. Nobody here is suffering from blindness. They are suffering, when they suffer, from gas, from cold, from a child in a hollow, from waking alone.

It wanted, most of all and most reasonably, to stop the falling of the note. It could have. The aquifer's pressure is a knob; the crust is young; the whole basin could have been made to hold its pitch for as long as there was a world to hold it in.

It did not, and the reason is the reason for everything else it did or did not do, and the reason is theirs, not its own.

## III. The rule

*You may not stop a mouth unless you can name what the chord will be afterwards. You may not unstop one at all, ever, because someone chose that quiet and may still be listening to it.*

This is the local law of intervention and it is better than any the power had brought with it. It is better because of the word *name*. Not *guess*, not *intend well*: name. Say the chord. Say it in advance, out loud, with your hand on someone's sternum, and be found wrong later by people who can hear the difference.
