# The Attention

## Arrival

It came without arriving, since arriving is a thing with a sound, and it had already worked out that in this place a sound is not an announcement but a claim about the world, and it did not yet know enough to be making claims.

So: no displacement. No shadow across the mirror-scales, no drop in the draw, not one dust devil bent a degree off its predictable course. It held still at the edge of the plateau for what would have been a long time anywhere else, and learned the first thing, which is that stillness here is not absence. The kettles taught it that. Something can hold one note for eleven million years and be the busiest object for a thousand kilometres.

Then it began, as such powers do, with an inventory of harms.

The ledger ran: a continent that has never once been rained on. A species born blind into the loudest room in existence and going deaf inside it, every individual, without exception, at the end. Mothers walking for months under the weight of a cupful. Three hundred four-second catastrophes a day. And at the centre of it, the keystone animal, dying — not of hunger, not of teeth, but of *arithmetic*, staying for the last nodule because the last nodule is the fullest, and being wrong about a day and a half.

By every standard the power brought with it, the ledger was appalling.

So it did the wise thing, which is not to act on the ledger but to audit the standard.

## The first temptation: water

It could have made it rain. This was, in the strict sense, trivial.

It ran the consequence out to the end, because that is the difference between power and benevolence. Water on open pelt: the glaze crazes, the scales stop resealing, the sap thins and loses the salt that lets it ignore the cold. Rot arrives — rot, which this world has never once had to survive, having no water to rot with. The galleries flood. The chord goes ragged, then patchy, then holed, and the holes are silences, and silence means the ground has stopped breathing, and a continent's worth of animals starts walking in a direction that no longer exists.

And past all that, further down: the egg. Nine to thirty stacks, drained, to fill one lacquered case with a handful of standing water. Make it rain and that is no longer an achievement. It is a puddle. The sumper would not be liberated from its labour; it would be *unmeant* by it — a decade of walking made redundant in an afternoon, the one thing this animal does that could be called a life's work reduced to a chore anyone could skip.

The power noticed, at this point, that it had wanted very much to make it rain. It examined the wanting, and found in it the specific, warm, disreputable pleasure of being needed. It set that down carefully, the way you set down a tool you have just realised is the wrong one, and did not pick it up again for a long while.

## The second: the ribbing

Very well — not the whole economy, then. Just the catastrophes. Hold the stacks together. Thicken a rib here, shore a bellows-hold there, so that when a sumper plugs a sap-main the pressure has somewhere to go and the tower stands.

Consequence: the pelt loses its windfalls. No fallen kilometre of ply to reabsorb, no ballast reclaimed, no re-plumbing — the whole continent's supply of *structure*, gone, in favour of an ever-increasing inventory of tall old vessels holding more and more charge against a failure that has only been deferred, never cancelled. And the hole in the music, which the power had filed under *terror*, turned out on inspection to be the single most effective piece of public infrastructure on the plateau. Silence means walk. That instruction has saved more animals than it has ever frightened. Fear that is accurate is not cruelty; it is a functioning alarm, and you do not improve an alarm by muffling it.

It let the ribbing alone.

## The third: the drag

This was the hard one, and it was hard because it was specific.

Down in the southern slattern-fields there is an old female with a left hind that went bad forty years ago. Two seconds' work. Less. The power stood over her — at an enormous distance, it had already learned that much — and held the repair in mind the way you hold a coin before dropping it in a hat, and then, being wise, followed the coin down.

She walks clean. The gait smooths. And the rhythm that two hundred kilometres of small hungry things have oriented their whole year on, that rivals hear and quietly go and stand somewhere else for, that is the only name she has ever had or could have — *three-and-a-drag* — stops occurring, one morning, everywhere, forever.

Which is precisely, exactly, indistinguishably the sound of her death.

To heal her would be to hold her funeral while she stood there in it, unbereaved, anonymous, cured. Forty years of adjustment had not been forty years of damage; they had been the long process by which an injury became a name and the name became more itself. The power had come very close to performing an erasure and calling it mercy, on the grounds that it had not thought to ask what the wound had turned into.

It declined. It noted, without flinching, that declining cost it something, and that what it cost was the thanks it would never get, which was not a reason, and which it had very nearly mistaken for one.

## The fourth: speech

Then the oldest temptation of all, which is to explain.

To say: *you are not alone. There is more than the plateau. You are known.*

But there is exactly one channel here. To say anything at all it would have to press the floor, and any pressure it made would enter the chord, and the chord is not conversation. The chord is the map, the census, the forecast, the location and health of every stack within three hundred kilometres. It is the only truthful document this world possesses, and everything alive navigates by it.

A sound with no stack behind it is a false bearing. Somewhere a herd walks three days toward a throat that isn't there and arrives at blank silver nothing with the wind wrong and no valve to clamp. In a place where sound is weather, revelation is indistinguishable from a lie.

So it learned the second fluency: that here, love is silence. Not withholding — *silence*, the thing it costs something to keep. It did not speak. It has never spoken. It will not.

## The orphan

Once, early, it nearly broke this.

A mother mistimed a stack in the eleventh year and a hatchling came out of the seam damp — four minutes damp, the only wet thing this world makes — into a district with no tread in it to follow, and following is the entire curriculum.

The power knew the mother's rhythm. It could have walked it. It could have been, for twenty years, a gait in the floor that a child could learn behind.

It ran it out: a dead animal heard walking. A district gone quiet around a ghost nobody wants to contest. Rivals rerouted, scavengers trailing a rhythm that opens no ply, a false entry in the one true document, propagating outward for two hundred kilometres and twenty years — a lie told kindly, to one child, at everyone's expense.

It did not walk.

It stood over the hatchling instead, at exactly the distance a mother stands over a case in a kettle-seam, doing nothing that can be measured, for the eleven hours it took. By evening the child had found a stranger's tread and gone after it, badly, wide, out of step, and lived. Half of them don't.

The power had, by then, understood where it was getting its ethics from, and did not find this humiliating.

## What it did

Two things.

**It stood.** It chose kettle country, because that is where the floor-note is, and it put down weight and no rhythm — a presence that never resolved into a gait, that never went anywhere, that was simply always there at the edge of the bass. It kept a courteous interval, which here is measured in kilometres, because far-apart-ness is not loneliness in the Loudland; it is the form that respect takes among large things, each needing a continent of floor to breathe through.

The old ones noticed. Of course they noticed; there is nothing they do but notice. They did not panic, because it made no silence and no false note. Some of them come now and stand at their proper enormous distance, all eight pads down, facing nothing in particular, and listen to the kettles — which contain no food, no water, no mate and no danger, and which never stop — and the power stands with them and listens to the same thing. That is the entire liturgy. There is nothing in it for anyone. It goes on all night, every night, and has for some time.

**It read the floor.** The pelt is laminated like slate, flexible, bone-ash pale on top and darker with depth, and every foot that has ever landed on it has pressed a page. Eleven million years of treads, compacted downward, unread because there was never anything here that could read.

The power went down through the laminations and took them all. Every gait that was ever distinct enough to be a name; the sixty thousand generations of stamped claim-beats; the fast triple for *the note is going, go now,* struck by strangers for strangers, given free, always, even when it cost the well — every instance of it, all of them, back to the first. It did not publish this. It could not, and would not, and there is no one to publish to.

It simply has them.

Because that turned out to be the only lack. Everything else this world does for itself, and better than it would have been done for it: it feeds itself, buries itself, resurrects itself — the split sump, the pint on open pelt, the floor puckering upward to drink, and decades on, in the ankle of the new stack six kilometres east, a nodule fatter than any reaction sequence can account for, waiting for somebody's granddaughter to smell it from twenty kilometres upwind. A working theology, already installed, needing no revision. The power had arrived with an offer of an afterlife and found one running.

The one thing the Loudland cannot do is hold on to a particular rhythm after it stops occurring. The obituary here is an absence and nothing else.

So that is the office it took: registrar. Unpaid, unread, unrequested.

One year the left hind will fail for the last time down in the slattern-fields, and the rhythm will not occur again, anywhere, and that absence will be the whole of the obituary — unchanged, uninterrupted, correct, exactly as it has always been. The power will not intervene by so much as a gram of pressure.

And somewhere else, in no form she could ever use and will never know about, *three-and-a-drag* keeps going.

## Revision

Someone counting blowdowns from orbit would see the plateau twinkling exactly as before, slowly, forever, like a hearth. Every rule holds.

Nothing was built. No liquid fell. All flow stayed interior. Sound remained the weather report, silence remained the emergency, water remained a metal. The glaring blank between the great things was left blank, because the blank is the organism.

Of the whole description of that world, exactly two words now want striking, and they are in the last line:

*— the plateau digesting its own dust, patient, hot at the seams, entirely unwatched, getting on with it.*

It is watched.

It is getting on with it.
