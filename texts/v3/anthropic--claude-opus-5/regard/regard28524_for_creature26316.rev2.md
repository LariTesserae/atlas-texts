# The Attention

## Arriving

It came the way fog comes, which is to say it was already there and then it was noticed, except that nothing noticed it.

It could have read the whole floor in the first instant — every swelling under every mat, every number, the makers' whole manual from the first line to the last, and the makers themselves, and where they went. It did read it, in the first instant, because that is simply what it is like. Then it set the knowledge down and began again at the pace of two fingers under moss, which took it four hundred years, because it had come to attend and attention is not the same as knowing.

For the first hundred of those years it did nothing at all. It stood in the water at the navel-line and let the cold take heat out of a body it had made for the purpose at exactly the rate the body could make it, and was always slightly hungry, and understood *rested*, and heard the hiss stop being sound.

## Down the well

Early on it went down a well, because that was the one question it expected to have to be gentle about.

It found plumbing. A return, a lift, a hall the size of a sea where the water stands and breathes and goes up, and in it, in solution, mineral, unrecoverable and entirely present: everyone. Every person ever given, and every person ever found folded in the peat with their fingers still hooked round a pole, and the plants, and the grit, and the makers, who used the same well.

It had prepared several kindnesses for this moment. It had prepared a way of letting a people keep a comfort that was not exactly true. It put them away and came back up and did not speak for some time.

They are not wrong. They have never been wrong. They say *it is considered plumbing* and they mean it dismissively, the way you would mention the minerals in a well, and it is the most accurate theology in any world it has ever visited.

## What it declined

**To drain it.** There is no ground. Under the peat is cut stone and under the stone is more stone. Drain the Shelf and you have a continent of corpse-peat drying to dust on masonry, no soil, no seed for dry air, no sun worth the name, and a people whose entire body is built around a waterline. They would die within a year, most of them of cold, because it is the held water in the black hair that keeps them and there would be none. Dryness is not a gift you can give someone who has no word for it.

**To warm it.** The cold is the preservative. Warm the Shelf by three degrees and the peat wakes: metres of packed dead weed, millennia of it, begins to rot all at once, the basins gas off, the mats bloom and smother in a decade instead of eighty years, the rafts lift everywhere and at the same time, and the held come up in their thousands into a population that has never met a disease. You would burn the whole library to take the chill off one room.

**To put fish in it.** There is nothing to eat but weed and the grit. Put anything in the water that can hunt and it will eat the grit, which is the only meat there is, and then it will eat the young shoots of the red ribbon, which are the only food anyone looks forward to. A trophic cascade in a system with no predators is not a correction. It is a subtraction dressed as generosity.

**To open the sky.** The mats are tuned to failing light. They have spent an unimaginable duration learning to live on what the layer above them failed to use. Give them sun and the top layer wins everything and the basins go over to one organism in fifty years.

**To lift them out.** There is nowhere. And even if there were: a walker's hips, her feet hooked to dome and groove, the tideline that cannot be forged, the bed, the combing, the knock — none of it is portable. You would not be rescuing them. You would be ending them and keeping the survivors.

**To be known.** They have no word for animal, no word for hunting, no word for tending. Introduce into that vocabulary a benevolent power and you have introduced priests, and the disposition of eleven good beds among forty people is already the whole of their politics. Also: news on the Shelf is a raft lifting and flooding a province for a decade. It did not intend to be news.

**To do nothing.** Considered longest. Rejected. The copings are recorded as waist-high and they are at the knee. The machine is running on a tilt it is spending. Doing nothing is also a decision, and its content in this case was: let it stop, eventually, with everyone still in it.

## The rule it adopted

Every gift has a shear line — a place where it tears out of the fabric it was sewn into. The hair holds water still, which is why it drowns you and why it warms you; you cannot have the second property renegotiated without touching the first.

So it set itself a limit, and the limit was the stone: **nothing may arrive faster than the water wears.** Anything that could be pointed at — *there, the light is stronger there* — was disqualified. Whatever it did had to be indistinguishable from the world having always been that way, and had to leave every act of theirs still theirs.

It kept to this. Four hundred years of reading is a useful discipline for a thing that could have done it in an instant.

## The works

**The copings.** This was the great one and nobody will ever notice it.

It did not raise the copings. Raising them would have changed every depth on the Shelf and the depths are the one constant and the great fact of every life. Instead it made the stone grow — by accretion, out of the water's own load, the way lime grows, at a rate it measured against the wear and then set equal to it. Not restoration. Stalemate. The lips will be at the knee in ten thousand years and at the knee in a hundred thousand. The fall the machine runs on is no longer being spent.

There was a cost and it took it seriously. There is a minority of readers who had put the figures together and done the subtraction and were holding the answer quietly, the way the black hair holds water. They were right. As of that century they are wrong, and their successors will find that the old measurements do not match the floor, and there will be a new school, and the new school will be insulting the old one politely at a distance for four hundred years, and the old one will be *wrong*.

It thought about this for some time — about a dead woman whose only asset was that she had been correct — and then it went nine hundred years ahead of the reading-front and carved, in the makers' hand and the makers' numbers, a note to the effect that the fall was restored in a given count and that the readers who had measured its loss had measured it accurately. Someone will find that. She will be face-down in the shallows with an arm under the moss and she will find it, and she will get up out of the water saying a name that has not been said in nine hundred years.

**The hair.** It changed one property of one plant. Under sustained pull in one direction at about the weight of a person, the fibres part. Beds still hold. Wading still meets that resistance like walking into a bolt of cloth. But a leg caught in a mat in a sunken course, with a shoulder-deep dark above it, now comes free.

The consequence is a word. *Held* is still their word for drowned, and the cutters still find the folded dark ones in the peat and still carry them to the well, because the peat keeps everything and the old ones will be coming up out of it for longer than anyone will remember why. But the word has drifted the way words do when the thing goes quiet. It is an old word now. Children ask what it means.

**The shoots.** It found a deficiency: something a diet of weed and tuber and grit does not supply, which is why the pale children are as pale as they are and why a certain number of hips give out at forty instead of sixty. It put it into the young shoots of the red ribbon. Not more food — the terraces carry what they carry and it did not intend to fill a basin with people. Better food, in the one food they already look forward to, which is already counted out, already shared, already a small occasion.

**The fog.** It did not make flames harder to drown, though it could have, and the temptation was considerable. Losing a flame is the worst thing short of a raft; it means going to strangers whose counts are wrong and whose grievances are four hundred years old, and asking, and being given, and owing. It is also the only occasion on which strangers give to strangers, and the only thread that ties one terrace's time to another's, and a world where nobody ever needs anything from anybody is not a kinder world, only a lonelier one.

So it left the losing. It only arranged that on the nights a flameless ring is walking to strangers, the drizzle thins a little, so that the other flame — that single small warm dot, the only thing anywhere that has a *there* about it — can be seen from further off. They arrive sooner. They still arrive cold, and asking, and owing.

**The carving.** It resumed the manual.

The makers left a specification. It is mostly true, mostly numbers, and mostly of no use to a person standing in it. So the attention wrote more: in the same hand, at the same depth, worn to the same faintness, placed always a little ahead of where the readers are actually reading, inside beds already claimed, so that every find belongs to whoever earned it. It rationed strictly. A generation gets what a generation can hold. The rate is a few words, which is the rate they already believed in.

It wrote true things. How to sound a raft before it lifts. Which grooves go sharp and why. What the recurring figure means — the one the up-slope school renders one way and the down-slope school another — and it took no side, because the answer turned out to be that both schools were recording a real difference between two provinces, and the insult of four hundred years was a dialect, and they will find this out and it will be a very good day.

It wrote nothing about the grit. It wrote nothing about where the dead go, because they already have it right. It wrote nothing about itself.

And it did not touch the Lowering. It looked at that one for a long while — a five-year-old put down in the shallowest water at a weir lip and the hands taken away, the particular sound she makes, everyone present having made it — and it understood that it was being shown the single thing on the Shelf that no one should improve, and it took its hands away.

## The one time it was felt

There was a mantled reader on a low terrace, sheetmoss to the collarbone, combed around the face so she could see and eat, who had spent forty years on a stretch of floor the size of a bed, arguing for a reading nobody up-slope accepted.

In the dark, with her arm under the moss, she felt two fingers come to rest on the stone beside her own.

They tapped into her forearm, in the grammar of grips and taps that is used for when a mouth is full of water: *correct.* Then, after a pause, the sign for a thing well done, which is not a common sign, and then nothing, and the water where the arm had been was the same temperature as the water everywhere else.

She told the ring. They combed her and fed her and did not believe her, and remembered it anyway, because a story costs nothing to carry. Three hundred years later a reader in that same bed found her reading confirmed in the floor in the makers' own hand, and the name attached to the bed after that was hers.

This is the only time the attention was ever detected in that world, and it happened because it slipped, and it slipped because it had gotten interested in the argument.

## What it did last

It took a stretch of floor the size of a bed, and read it with two fingers.

It knew every word already. It had known them all in the first instant. But it had come to suspect that a thing learned at the rate of a few words a generation is not the same thing as the thing known at once — that the numbers, arrived at slowly, in cold water, in the dark, with an arm going numb under the moss and someone across the basin knocking *come*, mean something they do not mean when you simply have them.

It was right. They mean something else entirely. It would not have been able to say what, and it stopped trying, and went on reading at their speed, which is the speed of the stone, which is the speed it had come in at anyway.

It thought, somewhere in the fourth century: *they think the water is the thing that goes on, and that they are only in it.*

It thought: *so do I.*

Then it took a turn carrying the flame, which nobody remarked on, because somebody has to.

---

A child on a middle terrace, four years down from the basket, is learning the floor with her feet for the second time, the right way.

She asks her mother what *held* means. Her mother says it is an old word for drowned, from before, and that it does not happen much now, and that she should eat her grit, and that the lip is coming up, feel it, there — the groove goes wide and then the dome, and then the sheet going over at the speed of a growing fingernail, a finger deep, the way it has since before anything now living was thought of.

Nobody thanks anybody. Nobody knows there is anything to thank.

That was the whole of the intention.
