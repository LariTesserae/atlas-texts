## The Guest

It came wading, because there is no other way.

That was a decision, and it was the most consequential one made in that country in four thousand years. It had other means of arrival available to it. It set them aside on the following grounds: that the first courtesy owed to a place is its own approach, and that a power which arrives by its own road has already declared the visit to be about itself.

So: four days at knee height, in water the colour of the sky. Its heel dimpled the black tissue and the tissue forgot it over the following minutes, the same as anyone's. It came in at dusk with the lamp-organs coming on, and Tamn waded out to intercept it, as she does, and told it the practical thing — *don't set anything down, don't sleep yet, keep something swinging on you* — and it thanked her, and did as it was told.

That was the second decision. It is not a small thing for a benevolent power to be instructed on arrival by a fifty-year-old woman with a bodkin, and to accept the instruction without amendment, and to wear a bone toggle on a thread at its wrist for eleven days because the local manners require it.

---

**What it established, in the first two days, and did not announce.**

It read the pith. Not the drifted surface that Leeward charts — the whole depth, the isotope layering, the ten million recopyings stacked like silt, the way you might read every draft of a letter at once.

Its findings were these.

The seedwork is not a mind. It is not a sleeper to be woken, not an intelligence in error, not a servant awaiting release. It is a set of *manners*, kept up by machinery, after the person. There is no addressee in it. There is nobody in there to be told that the tenants are not coming, and no faculty that could take the news, and therefore no cure, and therefore — this follows, and the power let it follow — no illness. Grief is only a disease in something that could have been otherwise. This is simply what care looks like when it outlives its object: it becomes a town.

Second finding, which was harder.

The risers were already wrong.

A hundred and thirty millimetres is not the tenant's stride. It is a copy of a copy. Under it, faintly, at a depth of several hundred thousand generations, the power could make out a rise that had once been something else, and under *that*, nothing legible at all. The hollow low in the back of every chair fits no body that has ever lived on this plain or any other; it is a transcription error, a stroke *gained* rather than lost, an inherited stutter in the furniture. The rail at seven hundred and eighty was too low for the tenants also. They hooked it because they could not grip it. They minced up the short steps for an era, and leaned on armrests set for the forearms of something older still, and the groove they wore into that rail with ten thousand hookings a day is not the record of a hand.

It is the record of a courtesy. It is the shape of somebody putting up with it.

Nobody has ever fitted this town. Every tenant it ever had was the wrong height, and hooked, and stooped, and adapted, and wore their adaptation so deep into the record that the record is now of the adapting and not of anyone. The three people on the crown are not usurpers in a house built for someone else. They are the fourth or fortieth generation of guests in a house that has never once, in its whole existence, built for anybody who came.

The power considered whether to say this. It decided to say it, to one person, because withholding a finding from a scholar in order to protect her feelings is not kindness, it is condescension with better manners, and because Leeward had bought that finding with nineteen years and was owed delivery.

---

**What it did not do.**

It did not correct the stairs. It could have set the rise at a hundred and seventy in a night and made every threshold in the town a comfort.
