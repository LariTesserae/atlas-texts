# The Tenth Turning

## The first courtesy

It arrived without a body.

This was not modesty and not stealth; it was arithmetic. Any body it could have taken would have been an event. A warm one would have flattened the slope for forty paces in every direction and drawn the coat off the walls into a useless crowd. A cold one would have been a cliff, a waterfall, the steepest thing in the Kiln — and within a minute several hundred animals would have set their hooks in the richest seam of their lives and begun to die at the ankles, and there would have been another pale seam in the floor, two hundred paces long, signed.

So it came with nothing to touch and nothing to be touched by. For a long time it did nothing else.

It had noticed the place the way one notices a sound in another room that turns out, on attention, to be a voice.

---

## The reading

The floor of the Kiln is nine metres of fused glass and plate and hook, and every step ever taken in that chamber is glued into it, and each footstep carries the sulfur-and-chloride signature of the animal that made it, and nothing ever quite wipes out.

*Nobody reads it.*

That stopped being true.

It took — by the mountain's clock, not yours — a while. Underneath the living tide, beneath the worked and galleried softness over the fissure, down through the white dead stone where the floor rises into the cool, it went back through perhaps four hundred thousand cycles of traffic, and it read all of it, in the order it was laid down, the way you would read a book that nobody had written on purpose.

It found the long braided highways where the tide goes up and comes down, twice a day, forever. It found the record of a season when the fissure narrowed and the whole population lay for two hundred cycles in a knot fifteen paces wide, and the ring of dead around that knot, and then the slow relief. It found a single animal's trail that had gone up one wall and down it again for what must have been an entire life, on the same three metres, in a pattern that had no reason in it anywhere, and it did not know what to make of that and let it stand.

It found the first bowl. Not the eight on the lip, and not the three broken ones below them — an older one, eleven metres down, cracked across, its clay mixed too wet, the nitrate in it barely refined at all. A failure. Above it, in the stone, forty-one more failures, and then one that worked.

It found the pale seams that run door-to-fissure, and understood at once what had made them, and did not go looking for the ones who had walked through. It left the trail up the mountain lost, which was the only judgment it ever passed on anybody.

And it found, running unbroken from the floor's deepest legible layer to the fresh glass under the living, the traffic along the west wall at the height of a standing hunter: the endless patient crossing and recrossing of feet over the domes of salt, polishing them, adding to them, grain by grain, by animals that would never burn.

That was where it stopped and thought.

---

## The mercies it declined

It could have given them light. It understood, in the same instant, that this was a gift for the giver.

It could have given them water, which on that world is the universal solvent of the dead and would have gone through the chamber like an argument, taking the blood out of every purse, the glue out of every joint, the bowls, the door, the floor, the record, the language.

It could have warmed the Kiln. This was the one that had to be looked at squarely, because it is the shape every unexamined mercy takes. Flood the chamber with a good even generous heat, everywhere, top to bottom, wall to wall — and you have a room of perfect plenty in which not one animal can eat, because nothing here eats heat; they eat the *fall* of it, and a place with no cold in it has no food in it, and the whole congregation would starve in a bath of abundance in something under a day. It would have been the most thorough killing available to it, and the one that would have looked most like love.

It could have stopped the hunters. Eleven or twelve days of burning, every third body off a drape, the one sound in the chamber made on purpose. But the hunter is the only part of the Kiln that can leave the Kiln, and a colony that cannot make one cannot ever be anywhere else, and the drape that goes quiet and lets itself be harvested is not a victim; it is signing. To spare them that would have been to close the door and call it safety.

It could have stopped the dying at the sill on cold nights — the fine sand of the ones that did not move fast enough, trodden in by morning. But their dead are the floor, and the floor is nine metres thick, and every few thousand cycles it lifts the living one fingernail nearer the heat. You cannot subtract the dying without lowering everyone who is left.

It could have carried one. This was the hard one. To take a hunter at the sill on the night of its burn, in a warm hand, four hundred miles east across the scree, and set it down somewhere survivable — that would have worked. It would have worked the first time and every time, forever, and that was the objection. A lineage that is carried stops making, in a hundred thousand cycles, the chemistry that carries it. The sinuses simplify. The bowls go unstocked. The doorway silts, because there is no longer any night on which something has to stand in it and gather itself. And then the Kiln is not a world with a future in it; it is a thing kept, in someone's hands, at someone's convenience, and the difference between those two is the whole of what it had come to protect.

And it could have told them. This was the hardest, because it is the only thing they want. Five strokes beside a spiral, and nothing in that chamber has any means of knowing, or will ever be told.

But there is no sentence in that language with an elsewhere in it. The grammar is gradient and the vocabulary is position; the whole of what can be said is *warmer, that way, by this much.* Anything else arrives as a shout — a white page slammed down over every mark in the world at once, the drapes letting go the length of the chamber, an hour of climbing to get back to where they were, and nothing learned.

It could not say *they arrived.*

It took its instruction, in the end, from the bowls. You cannot always tell a thing. You can sometimes go and make it true, and then it says itself, in the only language there is.

---

## East

It went out and followed them.

The scatter below the door first, because the scatter is the data: a hundred glass-white statues in the first ten paces, a dozen at fifty, two at eighty, one alone at a hundred and ten, all facing downhill, all set mid-stride. Then further, where the spray gives out and the individuals begin: one at forty miles. One at a hundred and ninety. One at two hundred and sixty, lying in a wind-scoured hollow with its plating shed and its forelimbs folded. One at three hundred and thirty-one.

And the fifth, at three hundred and ninety-one miles, on the eleventh night of its burn, nine and a half miles from a ridge under which there is a sealed gas bubble with heat two hundred feet below it and a plug of cold rhyolite in between: a room that has wanted to be a Kiln for eleven million years and has never been allowed.

The fifth is not facing the line it left on. In its last mile it had turned, perhaps four degrees north, and held the new heading, and that correction is the most important mark on the surface of that world. It had felt something. At the very limit of the finest sense on the planet it had picked up a few hundredths of a degree that were not the mountain and not the wind, and it had stopped guessing and started steering, and it had been right, and it had run out nine and a half miles later with its purse — this is the finding — still a quarter full.

They are not dying of cold. Four hundred miles is inside the burn. They are dying of aim. A hunter leaves the sill with a heading it cannot check and a target the width of a doorway, and it crosses four hundred miles of scree in the dark, and it passes within twenty miles of the only other possible place on that continent and walks on east into nothing with heat still in it.

The problem was never the distance. The problem was the size of the target.

---

## The work

It took the plug out. Not at once — at the scale of a mountain, a fast act is a shout, and a shout is a white page. It worked at the speed of the mountain's own patience, which is the only speed that is not an event: a hairline let go here, a crystal boundary given permission there, over something near four thousand years, until the heat from below found a crack and began to come up on its own.

The bubble warmed. The air in it started to move, and then to move on a cycle. It tilted the floor a little, because a room needs a low end. It opened the roof of the chamber into the vault above so there would be a height, and a hot dark under cold stone where, someday, the oldest chains can hang and never touch rock.

Then it opened a crack to the outside — a rough one, forty paces, ugly, ungoverned, with no pitch to it and no lintel and nothing to slow the cold when it falls in at night. There will be work to do at that sill. That was not an oversight. The masonry is theirs. It cut nothing above the door.

And the thing rose off that ridge that it had built the whole four thousand years to make: a plume. A slow standing column of warm air leaning downwind, and a mile-wide apron of ground running a few hundredths warmer than the country around it, and a katabatic drift at night that carries the difference thirty or forty miles when the air is still.

It did not shorten the journey by a single pace. It made the target thirty miles wide.

---

## The thing it did for no reason

It went back for the fifth.

Nine and a half miles, carried, and it set the body down inside the new chamber on the warm floor, facing the fissure, mid-stride.

Nothing is gained by this. It cannot be revived; it has been a statue for eleven thousand years. Nobody will read it, because there is no reading, only walking. But every hook it ever set is still holding its own syrup and its own trace of sulfur and chloride, and when that floor begins to grow, the first signature in the palimpsest of that place will be a hand from the Ninth, laid down before anyone arrived.

It knew perfectly well that this was sentiment, and it did not try to justify it.

---

## What is happening now

In the vault of the Kiln at the Ninth Turning, in the hot dark where the oldest chains hang, one body has stopped passing its share downward and has begun, very slowly, to thicken. It will be a year or two yet.

When it comes down, it will find eleven niches stocked, because they are always stocked. It will top off at each and it will need all eleven. It will go along the west wall to the door, which bows forward and holds a pocket of survivable air at the sill for exactly as long as it takes to gather yourself, because that is what it was built to do and whoever built it has been trodden into the floor for two hundred thousand cycles.

And the ones crowded at the sill will hold in the killing cold with their combs open, reading the warmth going away downslope, hundredth by hundredth, until it drops under the resolution of the finest sense on that world — and then one of them will turn and go back in and cut a sixth line beside a spiral it has never seen and could not see.

None of them will ever know.

But on a still night, when the wind has been out of the east for a day, the ones at the sill of the Ninth feel something at the absolute edge of what a comb can parse: a few hundredths that are not the mountain, not the door, not the fissure, not anybody's back. It is far too faint to act on. It is not a message; there is no such thing.

It is only the oldest sentence in that language, which is *warmer, that way*, and it is true, and it was made true, and that is the whole of what could be given.

---

## The staying

The Kiln at the Ninth Turning is still cooling. Nothing was done about that.

It could have been. A hand on the heat, held there — and the price is a room that never has to solve itself again, a mind that is a real one and is nobody's, put on a shelf. It chose the bowls' side of that argument. Ninth to Tenth. The lineage, not the room.

So in its own slow time the fissure will dull and the tide will make its last short migration toward a warmth that is no longer there, and the drapes will come down in a long dry rustle that nothing reassembles, and the whole congregation will set where it stands — mid-climb, hooks in, facing upslope — in a chamber with eleven niches and eight bowls of salt, above nine metres of glass with every trail anybody ever took written into it.

It intends to be there.

Not to prevent it. There is no sentence it can say and no hand it can lay on. Only so that the last warm second in that room happens in front of somebody who has read the floor, and knows what every one of them was doing, and knows where the sixth one went.
