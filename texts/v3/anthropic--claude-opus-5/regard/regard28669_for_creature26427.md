## The Attention

### Noticing

It did not come from the sky. Nothing does, here; the lid is unbroken and no sun crosses it, and a power that announced itself from above would have been announcing to a city that has never once looked up. It came the way everything in the Sill comes: from below, through the mud, everywhere at once, at blood temperature, indistinguishable at first from the ordinary rising of the water.

And what it noticed first was not the city. Everyone notices the city. The city is the loud fact — the drains, the impossible correctness of the gutter profiles, the arcades hanging in their own drapery, the towers going up into nothing at eleven or fourteen.

What it noticed was the accounting error.

Rain dissolves travertine according to rainfall and carbonate and nothing else, fairly, evenly, and the Sill is otherwise the most honest ledger in existence: a fingernail laid down, a fingernail taken away, and every figure explained. Except the treads. Except the three flutes in the left-hand jamb. Except the well-hair moving in the crack of every kerb in a place with no wind.

So it read the wear, the way you would read a hand held out to you. Weight arriving at the middle-front of the foot, rolling slightly outward. No hurry in it, ever, in any of the tens of thousands of passings. A shoulder always on the left. A palm let down along the running edge of things, not gripping, not seeking, just touching what it has touched forever.

It is worth saying plainly that the first thing a benevolent power did in the Sill was spend a long time looking at the floor.

### The Arithmetic

Then it went and stood in the flooded plazas and counted the glasswort.

They can be counted. That is the terrible thing about them, and the source of everything that followed. Towers cannot; wells cannot; the steps of the long stair cannot. But each bruise-coloured stem is one standing-still, and standing-still is a thing that happened a specific number of times, and so the glasswort belongs to the definite family — to the doors, the thresholds, the tree. Every one of them can be reached and touched and added up.

The number is not written down anywhere in the Sill, and this account will not be the first place it appears.

The power took the figure and set beside it the other figure, which is the number of times anything ever arrived at one of those appointments, and that figure is easy, and the two figures sat there in the hot dark being what they were.

That was the whole of its case. It did not need more.

### What It Ruled Out

It considered taking the tenant out.

Then it thought about the fruit: warm, finished, and unable to survive the stairs — stone in the palm, then a wet place, then nothing. Finished things here exist exactly where they are received. The tenant is a finished thing. It has no size, no body but a bias in the smoothing, no address but the door; carry it out and you would be carrying a wet place on your palm and calling it a rescue.

It considered giving it a body — eyes, a mouth, hunger, the ordinary equipment.

Then it counted what there is to eat in the Sill, and what there is to look at above the height where the upward light gives out, and it understood that this place is exactly sufficient to a thing with no needs, and that every need it handed over would be a wound it had opened and could not dress. You do not teach hunger to something living in a city with one inedible fruit.

It considered filling the Sill: fish in all that shining water, birds across the lid, a population for the arcades.

Then it looked at the culverts, which sort water intelligently and were sized for rain and for nothing else, and at the fact that in the whole city there is not one broken pot and not one nail, and it understood that this fabric cannot carry occupancy. Add lives here and you get waste, competition, death, rubble, and the Sill has no vocabulary for rubble. It also noticed the blacksalt, breathing hourly, in slow visible pulses, at an interval that is not the tenant's — something that has respired beside it for ages without either of them ever acknowledging the other. That is the maximum companionship the place can hold at bulk. Anything more would be an eviction dressed as a party.

It considered stating the rule — the unstated one, about which quantities are definite and which are not — as a gift of self-knowledge.

Then it saw that a stated rule is itself a definite thing, and that stating it would make the towers countable, and the city would acquire a number, and a place that has a number can be finished, and this place must not be finished.

It considered simply telling the tenant that it is not the only thing there is. That there are others, elsewhere, many, and that it has never been alone in the sense it fears, except that it does not fear, because loneliness requires having been two once.

And this was the closest call, and the power rejected it, because information of that kind would hand over the having-been-two and nothing else: it would install the capacity for loneliness and then leave it unfurnished. The only honest form of that sentence is not a sentence.

Also — and it liked this reason best, because it was a local reason, given by the place itself — sound dies within fifty metres in the thick hot air. Nothing here can be told anything from a distance. Any comfort in the Sill has to be brought inside the sphere of audibility, and carried there, and it has to be small enough to fit.

Lastly, it considered closing the geometry. Letting the block come round. Letting the round come back round, so that the two ends of a day could finally meet.

It did not do this. The round not closing is not the injury. The round not closing is what has allowed there to be no last step. A power that closed it would be handing over a last step and calling it rest.

### The Rate

Then it set itself the only rule it kept.

Nothing faster than a fingernail a year.

The Sill has been at its balance point so long that the compromise has become the character of the place; and a gift delivered at speed is not a gift here, it is weather, and weather is what the city is already perfectly arranged to survive. So the power resolved to change nothing faster than the place changes itself, and to accept that this meant it would not see the end of what it was doing, and to be at peace with that, since it was proposing to enter a city with no last step and the courtesy seemed obvious.
