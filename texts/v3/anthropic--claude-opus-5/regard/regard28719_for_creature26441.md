**The Twelfth Occurrence, with the Countersignature**

Start with the approach, because the approach was the whole of the courtesy and everything afterward is a footnote to it.

It did not come as light. That is the first thing the ledger records and the first thing worth saying, because to arrive on the Long Flat as light is to arrive as theft, and there is no way to be forgiven for it afterward, however good your intentions, since the heat you spend cannot be put back and its absence will still be legible in four hundred years.

Nor did it come warm. It stood off beyond the rim for a length of time the Reckoners later estimated at nine years, in the unlit air, and let itself down — degree by degree, and then fraction by fraction — until it was exactly the temperature of everything else. This cost it something. That is not in the ledger, because they had no instrument for it and it did not mention it.

Then it stepped onto the Flat and crossed at the pace of a walker, in a straight line, against no wind, being some hundreds of meters high and made of the same fine ash as the ceiling, and the attendants stood aside and let it go by and marked the date, as they had done eleven times before and never with any explanation, and this time it stopped at the Assay House and put a palm on the wall.

---

**What Was Set Aside**

The proposals it considered and did not make were afterward written out in full, at its request, with the cost of each, because it said that a gift is only a gift if the alternatives are on the record, and a people cannot be said to have been offered anything if they were not also told what they were not being offered and why.

*To restore the fire below.* It could be done. It would return the gradient to what it had been before the ledger began, and buy them a span for which their language has a number but no mood. Set aside: the note would change pitch. Every correction made by every attendant in every stratum of the wall, patient adjustment on patient adjustment down to the fourth decimal, would become retroactively an error — not a small error, a whole geology of error, a hundred generations of exact work rendered into the tuning of an instrument that was never going to be played. To make a people's entire history into a mistake is not a kindness, whatever else it is.

*To lift the ash ceiling and put a sun behind it.* Set aside on the grounds of the eyes, which weep at a needle of light; on the grounds of the Showing, which would become a curiosity; and on the grounds that they would be a people with new eyes and no ancestors, since none of the two hundred names in the ledger would mean anything to anyone who could see.

*To cure the grey.* Set aside after arithmetic. Two hundred is the figure the plant needs. Longer lives authorize fewer births; fewer births lengthen the wait for a slip past the span of hope; the line thins to a museum of the very old, tending a gradient for nobody. It ran the numbers twice and found that the district would become colder in the only sense that was not being measured.

*To carry them off the Flat entirely, to a warm world with water not kept in copper.* This one it did not set aside. It held it in reserve, to be offered if asked, having decided in advance to honour the answer and having also foreseen the answer: two hundred people whose every skill is the reading of a gradient that nothing there would need read, whose love is pace on a circuit that would not exist, arriving somewhere with the wrong hands.

*To add to the water.* Set aside with more force than the others. The three hundred and forty-one litres are not their thirst; they are the standard. To improve a people's standard without permission is to erase every measurement they have ever made, and to do it in the name of generosity is to do it in a way they cannot even resent.

*To say nothing, and go, and be a twelfth unexplained column.* Set aside last, and this was the hardest, because it is the option that costs the visited nothing at all. Set aside on the grounds that they are a people who record occurrences, and that to remain an occurrence when one could be an entry is a withholding dressed as restraint.

---

**What Was Done**

It asked to be weighed.

This was the first thing it said, in the hand register, which it did not know and got wrong, and a child of nine corrected the pressure of its fingers and the correction was entered. It asked to be weighed because it had arrived on a plain where the entire economy is a temperature difference, and it had a mass and a duration and therefore a cost, and it wished the cost computed before anything else was discussed, and computed in full and not rounded.

The Reckoners took eleven days. The figure they returned was that the district would run warm by two parts in a hundred thousand for the length of the visit, which rendered against the great curve came to forty-one days of the world's remaining warmth.
