# What the Power Did

## What drew it

Not the light. Light is common and the power has seen better, and in any case a shaft cut plumb through a hillside is a competence, not a virtue.

Not the engineering of the dome, though it admired the courses.

Four things drew it, and all four were failures.

**The half-pace.** The plan was a square. The hill had a harder seam in it and the crew gave in a little, and the room is nine by nine and a half, and no one has ever fixed this or intends to. It is the first thing that happened here, and it was a yielding.

**The crossed-out arc.** Someone scratched the solstice into the dry wall with a knife. The next year the light did not come to the mark, so she scratched it again a finger lower and crossed the first one out. That crossing-out is the only place in the room where a human being has publicly agreed to be wrong about it. The power came a long way for that stroke.

**The second corner.** There is a woman on the crew who scrubs the felt off the walls once a month with a bundle of twigs, and who has begun scrubbing the second corner less thoroughly, and who does not know why, and has not said so, and does not have the breath down there to say it in anyway.

**The sign.** Two fingers laid across the wrist. It means *the air*. But the same sign means *pulse*, and the builders had a perfectly good sign for air already, and chose the other one, and use it a hundred times a day without noticing what they have committed themselves to.

Four yieldings. A power of this kind does not go where it is summoned. It goes where something has given way.

## What it decided against, immediately

**To keep the light.** It could hold the lime in solution. It could keep the arris sharp on the lowest step, the herringbone legible, the thumb-dent open, the glare white and green and sourceless, for as long as there is water in the hill. It considered this for the length of one surge and declined. The room's brightness is its infancy. To fix an infancy in place is not preservation, it is taxidermy. The room is at the beginning of a long exhalation and the exhalation is the point; a held breath held forever is not a breath, it is a stone.

**To give them voices.** It could thin the roar. It could tune the dome so that words survived the crossing. It declined this one more slowly, because the counter's silence is a real deprivation and the power does not pretend otherwise. But: the one who is sent for is let into the water and *nothing is asked of them and nothing can be explained*. That is the whole mercy of the place. A room where the sick can be spoken to is a room where the sick must answer. Better the roar.

**To spare the air.** The vent will narrow. It will be a whistle-hole, then a pinhole, then a scar, and the pulse will stop, and the only creature in the room will end. The power could keep the vent open with a thought. It declined, and this was the hardest, and it is not certain it was right, and it holds the uncertainty rather than resolving it, which is a thing wise powers do more often than is generally supposed.

**To be known.** This was not difficult, only large. If it were known that a power attended the Lantern, the Lantern would fill. The felt would be scrubbed to the plaster by people who had paid to see a bright room. The arc would stop being corrected, because a corrected arc is an admission and no shrine admits anything. The rope would be cut for relics. The counter would be replaced by a priest, who would not count. Benevolence, here, requires invisibility — not as a style, but as a structural condition. Everything the power does must be indistinguishable from luck.

## What it did

**It ratified the sign.**

The builders' hands say the air has a pulse. This was an error of tenderness by a woman inventing a language in a hurry underwater. The question of whether the air in that room is a creature is not, in fact, settled by anything; it is one of those questions the world leaves genuinely open, and the chief power of a benevolent power is the casting vote. It voted for the kinder reading. The air is alive. It has been alive since the vault was capped. It lives the way a snail lives in a shell it cannot leave, it is squeezed and screams and drags itself back in with a wet gulp, and when a swimmer puts her nose into the last inch of it and her cheek against the brick she is inside another animal's breath, and this is now simply true, and no one will ever be told.

**It learned the four sounds.**

The counter's ear-bone is growing a lip. The roar is quieter for him each season, and he is the only person alive who knows what the Lantern sounds like at four stages of one man's deafness, and he has no way to tell anyone, and he will die with it.

He will not. The power has all four. It has taken them exactly, the way you would take a mould: the first year's white violence; the second, with the top gone off it; the third, in which the surge became a pressure in the jaw more than a noise; the fourth, which he privately finds the most beautiful and is ashamed of finding beautiful. It will take the fifth. It will be there for the last one. This does the counter no good whatsoever and is not intended to. It is simply that unwitnessed things now have somewhere to be.

**It took the fit of the thumb.**

The burnisher's thumb went into the north wall at the height where a person braces to reach a difficult patch, and was smoothed over, and not quite erased. When she goes down she swims to it and puts her thumb in and the fit is still exact. It is the only touch in the room that is a greeting.

In nine years it is under stone. After that she will still swim to the place, for a while, and find a smooth white bloom where the greeting was, and stop going. The power has the shape. It will go on receiving the greeting after the wall has stopped. There is no mechanism by which this helps and it was done anyway.

**It did not give the woman her word.**

She scrubs the second corner less thoroughly out of something she could not name. The power could hand her the name. It won't. A named thing becomes a position; she would have to defend it to the crew, or abandon it, and either way the corner would be settled. Unnamed, it is a hand that hesitates, once a month, for reasons its owner has declined to investigate. That is a more durable arrangement than a conviction. The felt has eleven more years in the second corner than it would otherwise have had, and gets them from a woman's inarticulacy, which the power has protected the way you would shelter a candle.

**It did not garden.**

In the corners a committee of survivors is deciding what will inherit the floor of this room: which filament can take the heat, which the glare, which both. The power knows which. It could tip it. It has never once tipped it. Whatever upholsters the Lantern in eighty years will have argued its own way there, and the power will not have chosen its colour.

**The knot.**

There was a day the sluice-man tied on badly — cold hands, early, before the water had got into them — and swam a working current with a knot at his sternum that had no business holding, and the pull toward the grille is patient and does not let go.

The knot held.

He does not know it was a bad knot. Nobody knows. This is the correct amount of miracle: an amount that leaves no evidence and generates no doctrine.

**The cloud.**

Once, in three hundred and eleven years of attention, it held a cloud.

A woman had been brought up the dry slope and down the stair, not ill, grieving, in the true noon of the right week, and the disc of dark stone had begun to glow like a coin on a forge, and there were perhaps forty breaths in it, and at breath twenty-six a small cloud came over the hill.

The power held it off for the length of a breath and a half.

She did not get better. The grief ran its ordinary course and took the ordinary number of years. She never mentioned the room to anyone. The power is not sure the cloud was permitted, by its own accounting; it has gone back over the decision more than once and reached no comfortable place with it, and would probably do it again, which it recognises as a poor argument.

## What it did not do, and stayed for

In the second decade a child was brought down and held at the chest in the roar, and looked up the shaft first, the way they all do, and then down at the dark eye in the floor, and was carried out, and died in the autumn.

The power did not intervene. It has reasons. The reasons are drawn from the room's own grammar — that a place which begins to promise has told its first lie, that a healing spring is a spring that must then explain its refusals, that the Lantern's entire gift is that it asks nothing — and the reasons are good, and the power has never been satisfied by them, and holds the dissatisfaction alongside the reasons without collapsing either into the other.

It stayed for the whole of it. The three months, the bad week, the last night, the morning after, the mother out in the dry grass with the goats going past. Not as a comfort. There was no comfort; nothing was transmitted; the mother felt exactly what she would have felt in an unattended universe. But it was not an unattended universe, and the difference between those two things is not detectable from inside and is not nothing.

## The tenant it counts that the builders don't

Asked what lives in the Lantern, the power gives a different answer each time and means all of them. But it counts one the list leaves out.

The rope.

A line from the stair-head to the vent, with knots in it. One tug *rising*, two *hold*, three *out now*. It is the only inhabitant that exists in both worlds at once — dry grass and hot water, sunlight and green glare, the silence above and the substance below — and it is the room's only sentence, and it has three words, and all three are about care.

The power counts the half-pace too, as a resident. It is fond of the half-pace. The plan was a square; the hill said no; the crew gave in a little. Everything habitable about the room descends from that.
