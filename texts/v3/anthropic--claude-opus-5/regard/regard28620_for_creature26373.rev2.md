# The Attention

## How it arrived

It came up the cliffs the way everything does, on the warm rotten wind, in the third hour after noon, inside the fog. Nothing marked the day. A pressed hollow in the mat filled overnight with brown sweet water and was reabsorbed by noon, as usual.

It had been many places. This one held it.

What held it was not the towers, though the towers are the only vertical thing in the world, and not the exchange, though the exchange is a solid object made of sound. It was a woman on the deck of a fallen cane at dusk, talking to her sister with her hands held low against her belly, small and fast and close, the whole conversation fitting in the space between two ribs. And the sister answering in the same place. Two people saying everything they had to say inside a volume the size of a cooking pot, on a plain three hundred kilometres wide with nothing in it.

The power had been shouted at, prayed to, sung at, and had endured all of it. It had never before had to lean in.

## What it did for the first sixty years

Nothing that could be seen, and one thing that could.

It learned the language. This took longer than it expected and the reason it took longer is instructive: the power could read the signs almost at once, and could not, for decades, read the *height* of them. It kept understanding the words and missing that the words were being shouted, or murmured, or withheld. It saw an old man make a perfectly ordinary remark about rope and did not understand for eleven years that he had made it at the level of his navel, with two fingers, to one person, and that this was the most tender thing he had done that season.

So the power kept its hands low. This is the first thing it decided and the only one it never revisited. Whatever else it did here, it would not be addressed from above the shoulder.

The thing that could be seen was the footprints.

It could have walked without weight. It considered this and rejected it, because on the Palm where you went yesterday is written, and your grandmother's route is written fainter under yours, and a thing that moves across this country leaving no record is a thing with no history and no liability. So it weighed. It put its feet down and the mat took them and kept them, and its prints went into the ledger with everyone else's, and it walked on the seams and not beside them, which is manners.

There is a seam on the southeast quarter, pale and threadbare, that runs from a dead cane to a place where there is nothing and never was. Families argued about it for a century. It is the power's. It was going out to sit down.

For those sixty years it was classed with the falcons — a neighbour, silent, taking what it took without comment, its manner understood. The power was content to be that and has said since that it was the best rank it has ever held.

## The rule it gave itself

There are exactly three occasions on which a person here speaks aloud. To infants. In grief. And in the throat of a cane, muttering apology to the birds whose house you are standing in, explaining what you are taking and why.

The power decided to take its instructions only from these.

Not from what it could see was wrong. Not from what it could measure. It had measured a great many worlds and had learned that the register in which a people are guarded is the register in which they are lying, usually to themselves, usually about what they can bear. The Palm is guarded in its hands. It is unguarded in its mouth, because its mouth is for children and for loss.

So the power went and listened to what was said out loud, which nobody had done in three hundred years, because nobody could.

## What it heard

To infants: the names of things. Endless reassurance. *You will hear this and then you will not, and that is what happens, and I will still be here.* Songs with no drum under them, which are the only songs on the Palm that are actually sung. A great deal of nonsense. One woman, for four years, describing to her daughter in exhaustive detail the appearance of her own dead mother, so that at least it would have been said out loud once.

In grief: the falls. Over and over, the falls. A peg goes, sixty metres up, and there is no sound anyone hears in time, and the body is in the mat and the mat keeps the shape. Then: the pool. Children drown in the cold black water at the base of the throat and are not missed until the exchange, because nobody heard. Then: the rattle, the dry cough at forty, quarriers explaining aloud to nobody that they could not get their breath. Then: hands. A rope-burn, a cut, and the fever after, and a household sitting up with a person who can no longer speak because speaking has been taken off them at the wrist.

And in the throat, muttering: *only this much, only the old crust, we left the low tier, we are sorry about the noise, we will go before the exchange.*

That was the list. The power did not add to it.

## The small things

None of what it did could be traced, and this was not modesty. A gift that must be remembered as a gift creates a debt, and a debt creates a priesthood, and a priesthood would have to be fed by four thousand people who are already coughing.

So: a quarrier in the north, sitting in dust up to his knees, thought of wetting the crust before cutting it. He thought of it himself, as far as he could tell. He was a little proud of it. The habit went column to column at the speed of a good rhythm and the rattle at forty became the rattle at sixty and then, in most settlements, nothing much.

So: a certain low grey plant in the halo, which had always been there and had always been ignored, was noticed by a girl to stop a cut hand from going bad. Her family copied her. Her name is remembered for it. This is correct; she did notice it.

So: the pegs. Hardwood rots from the inside where the driven end sits in wet fibre, and the rot has a sign — a faint dark ring at the collar — and the sign was always visible and nobody had ever assembled it into a rule. The rule assembled itself, somewhere, in somebody. Falls did not stop. Falls halved.

So: the fallen, in the open mat at night, who used to lie there unfound. They are found now. The power does not explain this one and no one has asked, because a search party that walks in the right direction is not a miracle, it is a search party.

It made no fire. In one hundred and forty years on a cold plateau it never once warmed itself, because fire not on the plate is a fire nobody is watching, and their fires are made of relatives. This is the only genuine discomfort in its whole tenancy and it mentions it rarely, as a joke, low, at the sternum.

## What it did not do

It did not give them back their hearing.

It could have. It wants this recorded plainly, because there is a kind of story in which a power stands over a suffering people and decides, in its wisdom, that their suffering is beautiful, and that story is obscene and the power has met the beings who tell it.

It did not, because it asked, and it asked properly: low, close to the body, one person at a time, over forty years, never twice to the same person, never in front of a household. And what came back, again and again, from people who had thought about it for the length of the conversation and no longer, was the flat sign that means *arrived at what it will be* — the sign for the rock, for the plateau, for a rope that is finished and will not be improved.

It is not that they did not want to hear. Several said aloud, to infants, in the small hours, that they missed voices very much. It is that the offer as the power could make it was an offer to be unfinished forever, alone, in a country of completed things, and they declined it the way you decline a house in a town where you know nobody.

A few said yes. It did as they said. It does not report how that went; those are their lives, not an argument.

## The marked

The ones caught inside at exchange hour, who sit at the outer edge of the halo with the horizon in their eyes.

The power went and looked at what had been taken, and could see the shape of the absence, and could have filled it. It asked them, which meant working through a nine-year-old speaking aloud in the moth-grass, which is the only channel there is.

Some said yes and came back and were ordinary afterwards, and dull, and glad.

Some said no.

The power does not know why. It has an idea, and the idea does not survive being put into any language available here, including its own, and it has decided that an explanation it cannot test is a story it would be telling for its own comfort. So: some said no, and it did as they said, and it still goes and sits with them sometimes at the edge of the halo, facing out, not talking, which is a thing it is qualified for.

## The rope

This is the one that changed everything, and the power's whole part in it was to make a fact visible and then leave the room.

The deafness is not mainly the shriek. It is the hum — the low note the column holds under the noise, that runs down the rhizome and out through four metres of mat and into the body of anyone lying on it. They sleep on the ground. They sleep on the ground every night of their lives from birth, with their skulls pressed into a lung that is transmitting a two-hundred-metre organ pipe.

The copper-traders keep their hearing an extra decade and everyone knows it and everyone explains it by distance from the columns. It is not distance. It is that down in the forest they sleep in hammocks, strung, decoupled, off the ground.

They have cane-fibre rope. They have always had cane-fibre rope. It is the cheapest thing on the plateau.

The power did not announce this. It arranged for it to be found — a returning trader's idle remark, a household that hung its sick child's bed to keep her off the wet, a child who noticed, three years later, that her mother could still hear the drums. And then it went and sat down at the midpoint and stayed out of it.

The litigation lasted eleven years. Two drummers, four kilometres apart, and the whole plateau listening in. It was never really about rope. It was about whether a person who sleeps off the ground is still arriving at what they will be; whether authority ought to expire at thirty, as it always has, or at fifty, and what the young are *for* if the old can hear; whether the hum is a harm or a tenancy payment. There were settlements that hung their beds in the first season and settlements that will not hang them yet and one that hung them, slept badly for a year, and cut them down.

The verdict, when it came, was: each household.

The power considers this a better judgement than any it would have reached, and regards the eleven years of argument as the most valuable thing it has ever failed to shorten.

There are now, in some canes, elders who can hear the drums. Nobody knows what they are for. There is no role for them; the roles are all taken; they are a new kind of person on a plateau that has not produced a new kind of person in three hundred years, and they sit in the dim tube breathing carefully and being looked at, and something will be made of them, and the power does not know what, and did not want to know in advance.

## The Hollow

It does nothing about the Hollow.

It could damp the ringing. It could hold the plateau steady for ten days twice a year and let everyone sleep. It has thought about this for a very long time and its position is that the Hollow is not a malfunction, it is the fact — that the place empties, that the tenancy is a tenancy, that everything here holds its lease by showing up on time forever and the lease is therefore visible twice a year in the form of an absence. To sedate that would be to lie to them about their own address.

What it does instead is go out.

On the first day of the Hollow it walks the seam on the southeast quarter, out to the midpoint, where there is nothing, and sits down on the mat with its back to nothing in the warm rotten wind, and stays for ten days, with the old ones, who arrive one and two at a time and sit at conversational distance and do not converse.

It did not ask for an hour of its own. There are three shifts and they are taken. It goes in during the middle hours with the people, and mutters aloud to the birds while it works, explaining what it is taking and why, even though it takes nothing — because that is the courtesy you show a landlord in whose house you are standing, and because it has noticed that the muttering is good for the person doing it.

## The long part

None of the above is the bulk of the work.

The bulk of the work is under the mat, on the naked gneiss, along a hundred kilometres of rhizome, where one plant is very slowly failing at three points, and has been for nine hundred years, and will be for another two thousand, and where the power has been for most of that time doing something that has no witnesses, no story, and no sign in the hand-language because nobody will ever need to refer to it.

And the rest of the bulk is three vertical kilometres down, in the lowland forest, which breathes, and whose breath is the entire hydrology of the Palm. The forest is being cut, slowly, from the far side, by people who have never heard of the plateau and never will. The power spends most of its attention there now, among strangers, on a matter of water, on behalf of four thousand deaf climbers who will not hear about it and to whom it will not be mentioned.

## The name

It got one anyway. It could not be refused; names here are places on the body, assigned in the hearing years, usually by a child, for a reason nobody bothers to remember.

A girl of seven assigned it. She put the flat of her hand on her own breastbone — the place where the hum is felt after the ears are done, the last thing to go, the note you keep for twenty years when there is nothing else. Why there, she could not say. She grew up and went deaf on schedule and died in her sixties and is in a wall somewhere, ground fine, and by then the sign had stopped meaning the hum and meant only the person, which is what happens to names.

She asked it once, out loud, sitting in the moth-grass at the edge of the halo in the year she still had her ears, what it was.

It answered out loud. Neither of them remembered exactly. She was seven and the wind was up and the fog was coming over the rim, and it had given the true answer, which is long, and she had heard about a third of it and been satisfied.

The sign is still in use. It is made low, at the sternum, close against the body, which is how you speak to someone sitting hip to hip with you on a sleeping shelf in a dim tube, and which is, as far as anyone here is concerned, simply where that particular neighbour is kept.

And when it goes — it will go; everything holds its tenancy on the same terms — they already have the gesture ready. You touch the place, and you open the hand, and you let it fall away from the body. Half a second. In the middle of another sentence.
