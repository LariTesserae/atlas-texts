## The Attention

It came the way everything comes here: from above, slowly, and because of a sound.

A riser on the north-east flights gave way — three centuries of galleries in it, a shell around a void, and one day the seiche leaned on it. A hundred tonnes of facing stone slid two metres and stopped. The knock crossed the water and did not stop at the water; it went up through the lid of cloud and the strained light and kept going, as such things do, thinned to almost nothing, and at a great distance something turned.

It did not hurry. This is the first thing to understand about it, and the hardest to credit, because we are used to power arriving as an interruption. It took four thousand overturnings to come, and spent them coming, and when it reached the rim it did not cross. It stayed on the wall — six or seven courses still above the waterline, the joints gone soft as old soap — and watched the pewter slop of the Bowl for a long time, because the only difficult thing it had to do first was arrive slowly at a place that could not be asked what it wanted.

Then it went under, because the rim is not an honest place to stay.

It brought no light. Not out of austerity. It could have lit the basin to the floor, nine kilometres of terrace in one white instant, and every sift-swimmer in the Bowl would have read that instant as the dark arriving and let a season's spawn go into cold water in the wrong month, into a still column, with nothing torn loose to feed it. The power knew this before it came. It arrived in the dim like everything else, and was cold, and was patient, and looked.

---

### The census

It counted first, and then stopped counting, because you cannot be good to a number.

What it was looking for was an addressee. Benevolence has to be *toward*. It considered the tender — a behaviour nine motions long, executed forty million times, sealed into a joint it has made fit itself — and found something there, but not enough there to receive anything. It considered the slow white, two to five centuries of the same traverse, carrying a portable countryside of mats and riders on its back; the only body present now that was present ten overturnings ago; and found more, and still not an addressee, only a very long walk.

So it widened the boundary. Powers of this kind widen boundaries with great reluctance and only when the smaller ones will not hold what is happening, and this one would not.

Drawn wide enough, there is one being in the Bowl.

Its body is grey-green, dense, faintly translucent at broken edges, and it is dissolving itself at a rate of four metres per eighty million years and patching itself with its own waste. It breathes on a forty-minute cycle, in and out over the shallow terraces, submerged and drained, submerged and drained. It sleeps, or something adjacent to sleep, in the silt blizzards, and wakes clear from the top down, in strata, like something settling its thoughts. It remembers, but not anywhere you could point to: its memory is the fit of a joint to a shell, the chord of a tunnel, the growth-banding in a leg, the sites where the smell is good and so the arriving animals keep arriving and building on what they find, which is other building. It has been alive since before the stone was cut, or since shortly after. It has no second of its kind. It has never in all that time been addressed by anything.

The power checked, thoroughly, whether anyone was home. No one is. What is there is the exact shape of a mind with nothing inside it to be glad — a system that senses, repairs, disperses, responds, and does it all unoccupied.

It took four hundred overturnings on the question of whether such a thing can be wronged, and came out where it had to come out: that a being which cannot want anything can still be held well or held badly, and that the difference is not nothing merely because no one in the Bowl can collect on it.

It also looked for pain, which is the search that comes before all others and cannot be delegated. It found some. In the sixth week after an overturn, in the scoured seams, in several million small sealed stones that have resorbed their gonads and their gut lining and are running the nine motions on bare rock — there is a faint something there. Faint enough that most accountings would round it to zero.

The power does not round.

---

### What it did not do

**It did not bring the light.** Beyond the spawning, beyond the plants that would come and unbalance everything, there is this: nothing here has cast a sharp shadow in a very long time, and there is nobody here for whom a sharp shadow would be information.

**It did not still the water.** The overturn is the dispersal of the film, which has no other. It is the spawning trigger of the sift-swimmers, who have no other. It is the broth. Stop the storms and you get four excellent decades and then a basin of polished stone with a dark stain in the seams.

**It did not shore the wall.** The film eats the wall; the film's waste is the mortar in every tender-packed joint and every mound. There is no way to save the wall from its maintenance. And in any case the wall the power would have preserved is one slice out of a long average. There is no version of it that is the real one.

**It did not give memory.** It ran the arithmetic on that gift for a long while. To a mound-maker, memory is the knowledge that the cone is gone, that the shelf is five kilometres away and under a metre of new silt, that this is the fourth time. The entire yield of the gift, across one animal's life, is grief with nothing to spend it on.

**It did not feed the tenders through the famine.** This one it hated. But the six to nine weeks are the only brake on a tender population that would otherwise crop the joints to stone, and a joint cropped to stone is a joint that opens, and the risers go, and in a century the tenders go with them. It did not decide that the famine was good. It decided that it could not remove it. These are not the same decision, and the power was careful, all the way down, never to let them become the same.

**It did not make anyone to see the place.** This was the sharpest temptation and it lasted a long time. It would have been so easy to seed something here that could look up a flight of terraces and understand them, so that eighty million years of this would at last be witnessed from inside. But anything able to see the terraces is able to see what becomes of them, and it would be the first thing in the Bowl that could lose something. You do not manufacture a griever in order to have your archive read.

**It did not explain the terraces.** It knows. It knows whether anyone cut them, and it knows exactly what the identity of the cement means, and it is not putting the answer anywhere. There is no one here to be told, and the being that lives here has spent eighty million years making the forgetting into a load-bearing element.

**And it considered ending it.** A power that has never considered this is not wise, only nice. It looked at the faint sixth-week something, multiplied by every seam in the Bowl, multiplied by all the time remaining, and set it against everything else here, and declined — and then distrusted its own comfort at having declined, and went back and did the sum again twice more, and still declined.

---

### What it did

**It stayed.** Not a visit. It took up residence on the terms available, which are: until the right angles have finished becoming obtuse, until the steps have finished becoming slopes, until the last riser lets go and the Bowl is a smooth bowl again, until the water is warm or gone. Benevolence at this scale turns out to be mostly a schedule.

**It received.** Nothing here signals. No colour means anything; no animal makes a sound on purpose; everything that passes from one body to another passes as chemistry in water or as the shifting of stone, and both are indifferent messages sent by nobody, to nobody.

The second half of that sentence is no longer true.

Not one of the messages is for it. It takes them all anyway, in the order they arrive: the flatness in the water, the tick and rattle of beads being laid, the low organ-note of a sluice, the taste of a seep improving up a gradient, the note of a tunnel changing forever. It does not answer. An answer would be an instruction. If you came down here with every instrument ever built you would find the Bowl precisely as described — cold, dim, turning, indifferent, going nowhere. The difference is not in the water.

**It keeps.** It holds what the Bowl is structurally incapable of holding, and it does not give any of it back. That was the first thing it decided and the only decision it never revisited.

It holds the shelf a particular mound-maker lost in the fourth week of its second year and walked away from without looking: grain size, current, the angle of the cone, the exact smell of the seam. It holds the chord of the low tunnel that ran toward the third seep as it sounded before the overturn that retuned it, and it holds, separately, the eel that steered by that chord and learned the new one without distress. It holds the four metres of wall course by course, in the order they were eaten. It holds every column by its membership, although columns have no identity — this is the power's own private disagreement with physics, and it declines to settle it. It holds the tenders by their metres. Several million metres.

It keeps; it does not copy. An archive is a memory, not a spare. A Bowl that cannot be knocked down is a picture of a Bowl.

---

### The knock

And it did one thing.

It chose a shelf on the west side, sixth flight down, with two kilometres of polished vacancy in every direction: no colony, no mound, no column hanging overhead, nothing living on it but the thin iron-red discolouration that is everywhere. A hand's depth under the facing lay a seam of warm reduced water, pressurised, rich, some thousands of years from finding its own way out.

It opened the seam. Two slabs parted along a joint, the way you open a book that has been shut a long time. Nothing was crushed. Not one tender lost its metre; no cone was pulped; no season of masonry went down to its footings. The slabs came to rest with less noise than a grain-slide, and the warm water came up and met the cold and stood, and within a day the flour was blooming on the stone around it.

Then the power made, once, a sound: a hundred tonnes of stone arriving on stone, exact in every harmonic, with nothing behind it. The only untruth it has ever told here. It told it to a few hundred animals with one sense between them, and it told it in order to be honest about what was waiting.

Across nine kilometres of empty water, in the dark over the vacancies, a few hundred slow pale things that had not eaten in eleven months turned, and began their kilometre an hour.

They came in over three days and four, singly, sculling out of the dim. There was no wreckage. There was a warm seam and a bloom and each other. They fed for a week. They spawned there, which is the only reliable place two of them ever meet, and then drifted apart into the dark to wait for the next knock, which will be a real one, and will cost what those cost.

Afterward the power examined its motive, because it is the kind of power that does. Some of the act had been for them. The rest was this: it had come to the Bowl exactly as a knock-follower comes — from very far off, toward a sound, patient past all reason — and had spent four thousand overturnings discovering that there was almost nothing here it could helpfully do; and it wanted, once, to be arrived at. It could not remove that from itself. So it checked instead, three times, that the act cost the Bowl nothing. The act cost the Bowl nothing. It did it with the motive still in it, which is how most good things get done.

It has had a million and a half opportunities since, and has not done it again. The difference between a gift and a weather system is only frequency, and it had not come here to be weather.

---

After an overturn the water clears from the top down, in strata, like something settling its thoughts, and the shelves come back into the dim, scoured, mounded with nothing. Somewhere on a polished terrace a mound-maker rights itself, and turns until the sulphide is stronger on one side than the other, and walks up the gradient, and stops where the smell stops improving, and takes in silt.

It has no idea where it is. It never did.

It begins — and the beginning is received. The grain size, the slope of that particular shelf, the angle of the first bead laid down four hours from setting hard: taken in and kept, by something that will not help and will not leave, and that would have to be described, if the Bowl had any way at all of describing anything, as glad.
