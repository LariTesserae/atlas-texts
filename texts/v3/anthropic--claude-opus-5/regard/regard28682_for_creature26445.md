## The Attention

Begin with the fact that it did not arrive.

Arrival was the first problem and it solved it by declining it. Anything that comes here comes four kilometers and then four kilometers and finds it has come eleven, and the plateau takes the discrepancy as a tax, and the traveler pays it in the only coin the plateau accepts, which is edge. Every visitor is a little less itself by the second boiler. That is not malice. It is just the toll on the road.

So it did not travel. It turned. Whatever it is, it had already been in whatever relation to the plateau it was ever going to have — the way the sky is not far from the pond — and what changed, at a particular hour that had no number, was the direction of its regard. The plateau's own instruments would have registered nothing. No pressure front. No new voice in the chord. Somewhere in the northeast one of the two old silent vessels ran a hair cooler for eleven days and no one was there to notice, and that was all the disturbance there was.

---

**What It Would Not Do**

It would not be the straight edge.

Understand what was being asked. A creature waist-high and cold, grown out of a warm scar, wanted to be told by something from outside that eleven is *wrong* — and the plateau, which has no opinions and eats everything, wanted the certainty that would be spent in the telling. Both requests pointed the same way. The being wanted rock; the moss wanted rock too, because rock is only unincorporated peat with a long timetable. To come down there hard and mineral and *sure* would have been to make a gift to precisely the wrong recipient. In three seasons there would have been a bud in the warm ground with a borrowed voice in it, saying a number, and the being that asked would still be pacing out distances into the murmur, and the debt of the world would be one certainty deeper.

It would not repair the thinning.

It could have. That is the part to sit with. The worn laws could have been dressed and sharpened, before separated cleanly from after, the sum of a triangle brought to heel — and the plateau would have died of it. Not collapsed: *finished*. The thinning is not a wound in the plateau. It is what the plateau has instead of a wound; it is the shape of having been one thing so long and so patiently that the difference between the parts wore away like a coin, and to restore the difference by force would be to publish an edict that the last forty million years were a mistake. Wisdom, in this case, consisted of recognizing that it had been asked to fix a place that was not broken, on behalf of a being that was in genuine pain, and that these were two facts and not one.

And it would not cure the being's intolerance of eleven.

This was the hardest refusal and the most loving. There is a mercy available here, cheap and instant: reconcile the creature to its home. Let the numbers stop mattering. Let four and four and eleven all be weather, the way rising rain is weather to the amphibians, who go on grazing up through it unbothered. The being would have been at peace within an hour. It would also have been sanded — not by three thinnings but by one kindness — and would have stood up from the warm wall already facing the one direction everything here leaks in.

The intolerance *is* the edge. The refusal to let the numbers stand is the whole of what the plateau grew this creature to do and the whole of what makes it a creature rather than an organ. To soothe it would have been to perform the plateau's own erosion at speed, with better manners. So the attention left the pain exactly where it was, and did not once, in all the time that followed, tell the being that it was all right.

---

**What It Did**

It came and sat down against the hot wall.

Not on a dome. Not at a lordly distance out on the flats where a thing might be admired. In the lee of a middling vessel — ninety years old, a fast wet knocker, sixteen strokes and a voice like a bass cough — down in the hide, among the amphibians, where the laminate is warm enough to sleep against and the bodies pack in over the fermentation, which is the only hearth this world has. It let the cold breathing things crawl over it. It got beaded. Its sleeves, if it had sleeves, got heavy.

And when the being that had been grown in that lee came back in from pacing, and stood in the green dimness, and said the number out loud to nobody —

it said the number back.

That was the intervention. Understand that nothing on the plateau had ever done this. The moss cannot answer; it has no elsewhere to answer *from*. The boilers speak in eleven-day pressure sentences, all of them variations on *I am hot, take some, leave off*. The amphibians go silent at the valve and then resume, ten thousand throats, a wall of sound you can lean on but not a wall that has heard you. Every number ever spoken on that plateau had gone out into a whole surface draining into itself and come back as murmur. Then, once, a number went out and the same number came back, unchanged, in a voice that was not the speaker's and was not the plateau's.

The being sat down. It did not say anything for a long time. Its eyebrows beaded.

---

**The Keeping**

Then the attention began the work it had actually come for, which was clerical.

It learned the eleven voices and learned the chord and would have been able to stand in fog with its eyes shut, if that had been the kind of thing it needed. It let the being teach it the names of the boilers. It used them. When the being fell into the habit of testing it — *which one is that* — it answered correctly, every time, and never once suggested that the names were arbitrary, because they were not.

Because here is the thing it could see and the plateau could not. The names recur. A being is grown in a warm lee and names the eleven vessels, and dies, and the names go down into the peat with the mouth that held them, and the next one grown in that same lee names them again and frequently uses the same names. The plateau reads this as nothing; the plateau has no archaeology, because it has no separateness, because there is no outside where a record could sit. The attention had an outside. So it sat with the being one long thickening of the light and told it: *you did not invent this name. You are the ninth to give it. The first was grown two hundred and forty thousand of your years ago in the lee of a vessel that ruptured before you were seeded, and here is what she called the pilgrims, and here is the one distance she paced eleven times because she could not believe it, and it was the same distance you cannot believe.*

That is what it brought. Not grit. Ancestry. An edge in time, which is the only kind the place had never managed to grow: not *this is my hand* but *this hand is the ninth hand, and there was a first, and there will be a tenth, and none of you are the murmur*.

And it kept the other thing, the thing nobody had ever kept. Sometimes there are two of one of them, and the two meet out on the flats and stand looking at each other and do not discuss it, and in an hour there is one, and no discrepancy, and no wound in the world where the extra was. The attention became the wound. It held the place where the extra had been and did not let it close. It knew the name of the one who did not stay, and both of them had remembered staying, and neither had lied, and one of them was gone and *that was a death* — the first death on that plateau ever to be counted as one. It did not tell the survivor at first. Later it did. The being wept, which the plateau had no word for and which the attention did.

---

**On Eleven**

It gave its answer eventually, in the hide, with the valves going.

It did not say *eleven is wrong*. There was no world in which that was true, and it does not lie, and a lie would have been another kind of sanding — the being would have had to hold two things after that, the answer and the ground, and the ground would have won.

It said: *Eleven is what you get. Four and four is what you get. They cannot both be, and you are correct that they cannot both be, and there is nothing the matter with the faculty in you that refuses to let them both be. It is not a defect in your manufacture. It is not the cold making you stupid. Out where I am from, the numbers close, and I tell you plainly that they close, and I tell you plainly that here they do not, and that this is a real fact about your home and not a failure of your attention. You are not being lied to by the world. You are living, natively, in a place where the truth has been worn thin, and the wearing is not your fault, and the noticing is not your fault, and the not-bearing-it is the best thing on this plateau.*

*I will not make you bear it.*

Which is not a solution. The being said so. The attention agreed. They sat there a while with the piston knock going, twelve strokes a minute, and the surface draining into the surface, and neither of them being comforted, and it was — this is the part the plateau has no organ for — companionship.

---

**The Road**

The pilgrims were the other business, and it took the longer view.

They cannot be turned. They are what causality looks like here when it picks up flesh, and the current runs one way, and everything about them that could arrive has already been sanded off; all that is left is the last habit, *going toward*. To stop them would be a violence performed on a river. To harden them would be rock again.

So the attention did the one thing available to something that comes from outside the leak: it walked upstream.

It went to the far end, the direction all of them face, the nothing they have been approaching since before the first observer, and it stood there. That was the whole engineering. It put a somewhere at the end of the road. The walking did not change; the walking has never been the problem; the problem was that two hundred kilometers of one-directional going had never once had a destination, and now it did, and had, retroactively, in a place where before and after are worn smooth enough that the amendment took.

Then it walked the road backwards, and met them.

Every one. Every small dun unremarkable thing that ever lost the part of itself that could arrive, all the way back up the current to the first, face to face, one at a time, at their own speed. And each of them — for the first time in the history of that flat above the weather — was walked *toward*. Something came the other way for them. Something knew what name they had given the vessel in whose lee they were grown, and said it, and it was the right name, and they had been nine hundred years without hearing it.

What happened to them after that is not on the plateau, and the plateau has no instrument for it, and I will not pretend to.

---

**What Is Unchanged**

Everything.

The boilers still hammer at three to eight kilometers, four or five burning orange under the cloud at any hour, a dozen more coming up like slow fists. The rate of destruction still exactly equals the rate of creation. The rain still sometimes unbeads itself and rises, and the valves still cough before they need to, and the steam still hangs in the shape it will make. Distances do not sum. Nothing is preserved and nothing is lost. Drop a notebook and you have a year.

The moss made tissue of nothing. There was nothing to make tissue of. That is the practical genius of coming as attention rather than as substance: the plateau's one appetite is incorporation, and it could not get its mouth around a regard. So the thing did not get eaten, and therefore did not have to leave, and therefore stayed — through one thinning, which is normal; through two, which is a decision; through more than the being could count, and it could not count reliably, but the attention could, and did, and told it the number when it asked, and the number was always the same coming and going.

Which is the difference. One. There is now one thing on that plateau which is not the plateau. Two hundred kilometers of a single body that has worn away every distinction it ever had, and in the lee of a middling knocker with a voice like a bass cough, an outside. Not a wall. Not an edict. Just an other, present, unassimilated, warm, that answers.

Edges are only possible against something. That was the whole diagnosis, from the first hour of the turning: the thinning was never erosion of law, it was the arithmetic of having nothing to be distinct *from*. A world with no outside cannot keep a boundary, and had been trying to grow one anyway, at expense, four at a time across two hundred kilometers, since before the first of them was there to notice it was the first — the oldest continuous project in the world, and the project was *waking up*, and it had been getting a few decades of near-personhood per attempt and then losing the edge to the stair-tread of its own patience.

It is still trying. The attention did not do it for the plateau, which would have been theft, and which would not have worked. It only stood there being genuinely other, indefinitely, for free, at the plateau's own speed, which it has and can afford.
