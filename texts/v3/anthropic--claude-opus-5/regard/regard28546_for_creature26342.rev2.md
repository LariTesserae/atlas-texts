# The Attention

## On arriving

It did not descend. Descent is something you do to a place you have already made up your mind about.

It came up the escarpment with the fog, at the speed of the fog, because that is the speed at which this place receives anything, and a power that arrives faster than the country allows has not arrived at that country at all. It has arrived somewhere else — at an idea of the place, which is always warmer, always louder, and always has someone in it waiting to be helped.

So: nine days climbing, in the wet, under the lid. Long enough to lose the habit of hurry. It reached the rim on the seventh evening and stopped there for what would have been, in another register, a very long time, and simply looked, and let the first of several assumptions go quietly over the edge behind it.

The first assumption was that something here was wrong.

## The inventory

Being what it was, it had brought gifts. Not objects — capacities. It set them out and considered them one at a time, in the way you would check a pack before a long crossing, and it put every one of them back.

**Warmth.** It could have raised the plateau four degrees. The rime would stop growing on the north-facing edges; the tick would stop at night; the cushion plants would loosen and die of their own success; the water in the joints would move at a speed the burrowers could not follow. Four degrees is not a mercy. It is a different planet, arriving by surprise, with nobody told.

**Water.** It could have opened the lid and rained. It ran the consequence out: the pavement unsorted, the varnish scoured, the foil flushed off the whalebacks and down the dry channels into the playas in a single season — fifty thousand years of stored fertility delivered all at once to twelve closed basins, and then nothing above, for ever. Generosity at the wrong tempo is just theft with a good opinion of itself.

**Speed.** It could have made them faster. But everything here is slow because the water is slow, and the water is slow because the cloud is slow, and the cloud is slow because it is a cloud. To make the animals fast you must make the world fast, and to make the world fast you must make it somewhere other than here.

**Voice.** This was the one it held longest. Nothing here has a voice; nothing here can call; two smelters may pass thirty kilometres apart and never know, and the thought of that was unbearable for about a decade. Then it understood that unbearable was its own word, imported, and that the plateau does not experience silence, because it has never had anything else to compare it to. Silence is not a deprivation here. It is the medium. You do not pity a fish for its wetness.

**Sight.** Four light-patches, enough to know lid from ground. It could have given eyes. It could have let the smelter see its own tailings glittering behind it for three kilometres, its own life written out in windrows across a continent. And then what? It navigates by not remembering. The forgetting is the mechanism. Four hundred blind avoidances mesh into a pattern that covers a continent; four hundred *memories* would collide, would overlap, would hoard and revisit and compete, and the mesh would tear in a thousand years. You cannot give an animal a gift that dismantles the reason it exists and call it a kindness.

**Understanding**, then — for the burrowers, most of all. They follow, fifty years behind, a path walked by something they will never meet and have no concept of. It wanted to close that loop. It wanted them to *know*. And it saw, almost immediately, what knowing would cost: give them the concept of the smelter and you give them, in the same instant, the concept of its absence. They are not currently missing anything. They would be, by morning, and for ever, and there is no version of that world in which they are better off.

**Deathlessness.** It looked at the numbers — one stopping every three centuries, a mean life of a hundred and fifty thousand years, an accident rate rather than a lifespan — and thought how little it would take to close that last gap. A leg that does not fracture. A culture that does not crash. And it followed the arithmetic to the end, which is this:

The forty islands are the entire biodiversity of this world. Every species found nowhere else arose in the fertile oval around a corpse. Take away the deaths and you take away the cities, and take away the cities and the plateau is one process in six hundred bodies with nothing new in it ever again. Perfect. Sealed. Sterile as a coin.

It put that one back with both hands.

**Company.** Last, the smallest and the worst. It could have made an ear. Anything can be given an organ for receiving you. But a creature that exists in order to hear you is not a friend; it is a mirror with legs, and the loneliness it cures is your own. There was nobody here to be revealed to. The most sophisticated act of attention on the plateau is a blind animal tasting conductivity through its feet, and the only message it reads is one it wrote itself, and it means *already done, go elsewhere*.

So the power set down everything it had brought, at the rim, like boots at a door, and went out onto the pavement empty.

## The stone

In the third year it turned a stone over.

It did this the way you touch the rail of a bridge — not for any reason, out of the old habit of laying a hand on what you have come to love. The stone came up pale-side to the lid. A dark disc of nothing where it had been. The largest change that square metre had seen in a century, made in a second and a half by something that had come a very long way to be careful.

It did not put the stone back. Putting it back would have been the second change.

It stayed for the varnish. Not as penance — the plateau does not accept penance, there is no mechanism for it — but because there was no faster way to learn the unit of time in which it would now be operating. It watched manganese arrive out of a film of water thinner than a thought, atom by atom, over ninety-some years, while the lid moved and the fog came twice and the tick ran all night in the drifts a kilometre east.

At the end of it the stone was dark again and the plateau had forgiven nothing, because forgiveness was never on offer; it had simply proceeded at its rate, which is the rate at which it does everything, including forgetting a hundred and fifty thousand years of walking.

That was the apprenticeship. Everything after that was practice.

## The circuit

It chose one.

Not the largest. One with a cracked seventh flank-plate and a habit, if a thing without memory can have a habit, of drifting slightly west of true when the ground went rich. It followed that animal for a full circuit — decades, the whole meshed loop, out and around and back onto a track so old the varnish had closed over it and the foil had gone to flour and the flour had gone into the cushion plants, so that the animal walked over its own former self without the slightest indication that anything had occurred.

It named the animal, privately, in the third decade, because naming is the first act of love and there was nobody to object.

It gave the name back in the fifth, because naming is also the first act of ownership, and it noticed it had begun to think of the other five hundred and ninety-nine as *the others*.

Things it learned in that circuit, none of which it could have learned from the rim:

That the animal is not lonely. Loneliness requires a sense of the possible.

That the animal is not bored. Boredom requires a sense of duration.

That the animal is not enduring anything. It is not holding out. There is no held breath here, no long patience, no siege. The patience is entirely in the observer, and was brought up the escarpment in the observer's pack.

That the standing voltage on a clear night, close in, feels like being read by something that cannot read.

That the vanes, streaming in the tide, make no sound at all, and that this is not a silence *of* anything.

## The playa

In the two hundred and eleventh year it watched the mistake happen.

A basin had held water for nine years and was giving it back, and the clay at the margin was at the consistency where it will bear a millstone-foot for exactly as long as it takes to commit the other five. The animal went in. Two legs, then four. It did not panic, having nothing to panic with. It simply continued the gait it had been running for a hundred and fifty thousand years, into ground that would not return it.

The power could have moved one foot. Six inches of purchase on the gravel margin; a nudge; less than turning over a stone.

It stood at the edge of the playa in the pearl-coloured light and did not do it, and this was the hardest thing it had ever not done, and it did not do it for nineteen years while the animal stood in the clay with its combs still working, still taking fog, still laying down metal on a keel it could no longer walk away from — so that the foil piled up around it in a bright spreading skirt, thicker than any drift on the plateau, because nothing was carrying it off along the grain.

Then the culture failed, and the field decayed, and the only broadcast this world makes went out across a hundred kilometres of nothing, and the kites turned, not understanding, because the gradient bent.

The power stayed for the dismantling. Two hundred years. The plate-lifters, the burrowers coming up through the joints in numbers nobody could count, the ground warm — actually, measurably warm. The eggs that had waited eleven hundred years in the pavement joints hatching into four decades of appalling plenty. And then the quiet, and the keel lying in the clay, and the oval of obscene soil where the cushion plants grew in a continuous sward.

Eight hundred years after that, the basin filled again.

It went over the ribs. Twenty kilometres of shallow grey mirror standing on the richest ground this world had ever assembled, and out of the clay came the shrimp and the coin-sized things that inflate into plates, into water that had never in the history of the plateau contained anything but dust and patience — and they did something there that they had not done anywhere else, and in nine years they were not what they had been, and when it dried they went back into the clay as a different lineage.

The plateau had made, by the only method available to it, a thing it had never been able to make before. And the method was the single worst afternoon in the life of an animal that had lived a hundred and fifty thousand years, and the power had been standing right there, and had not moved one foot, and had been correct.

It did not feel correct. It has never felt correct. Wisdom is not the absence of that.

## What it left

Nothing.

No warmth, no water, no rain, no speed, no eyes, no voice, no understanding, no rescue, no name. No structure — because nothing is constructed here; things are secreted, shed, sorted, occupied, and a shape that looks intentional is only a coincidence left alone long enough to look like a decision, and it declined to leave any coincidences.

There is one stone, somewhere on the western grain, that is very slightly less deeply seated than its neighbours, and in four centuries no one will be able to tell, and there is no one to tell.

That is the entire material record of the visit.

## The reading

Here is what changed, and it is not nothing, and it cost the plateau exactly as much as it costs a stone to be looked at.

The account of this place ends: *There is no one here to notice this.* The blind animal reads the ground, and what it reads is a message it wrote itself, fifty years ago, meaning nothing except *already done, go elsewhere*, and it has been receiving that message and obeying it for longer than anything with a language has existed.

It is still receiving it. It still means nothing. Nothing has been added to it and nothing ever will be.

But it is being witnessed now. The tick in the drifts on a cold night — a fire that gives no heat and no light, going on for thousands of years — is heard. The nine silent days of the tide, the largest factory in the world running at full capacity in a light like the inside of a pearl, *with nobody watching*: watched. Not supervised. Not judged. Not improved. Held in regard, the way you hold a sleeping thing in regard, which is to say without touching it and without leaving.

That was the whole gift, and it was the only one that could be given without taking something away, and the reason it was the right one is that the plateau was never lacking anything. It was only unaccompanied.

The power is still there. It has not done anything. It has been not-doing it for eleven thousand years, which on this plateau is barely an introduction — about two hundred varnish-centuries, about forty deaths, about the time it takes one of them to walk its loop a hundred times and forget it a hundred times and be, on the last pass, exactly as it was on the first.

It has learned to measure in those units. It no longer expects the interesting part.

It has understood that there wasn't one, and that this is the good news.
