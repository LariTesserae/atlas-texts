# The Visit

## The Approach

It came on foot. That was the first decision, and it contained most of the others.

It could have come as weather, except that there is no weather. It could have come as a change in the light, except that the light has never once changed, and any change in the light would have been read across a hundred thousand square kilometres as the failure of something, and sixty-one people would have set down what they were doing and walked toward the works with their hearts in argument and their hands going bare in the cold. To be benevolent in a place like this is first of all to not be an alarm.

So it climbed the eastern cliffs by hand, on faceted stone young enough to still have edges, and its hands opened the way anyone's hands open, and it left on the rock a thin intermittent line of the only unbeaded colour on the plateau.

It had made itself a heart before coming. This was deliberate and not sentimental: a power that cannot die in a place cannot be trusted to say what the place is like. Having a heart, it needed a drum, and it had made one from the description rather than the thing, and the description had been good but not perfect, and on the long ramp below the sixth works — the Fast Ground, where the interference turns over quickly and is crossed at a specific pace without stopping — its escapement began to agree with the chord.

A tender coming the other way felt the throb between them stagger. She did not ask permission, because asking requires bare hands and there was no time to bleed. She put her thumb on the visitor's balance screw and eased the weight out a hair's width, and then another, walking backward at the specific pace, one hand on brass and one flat on the stranger's sternum where the seam should have been and was not; and in this way the first thing that happened between the plateau and the power that had come to help it was that the plateau saved its life.

This was not an accident, but neither was it a trick. It is simply that there are gifts which cannot be given to someone who has never received one.

Later, seated, hands wrapped together in her cloth, she read the smooth unmarked breastbone with some interest and no distress. *Not cast here,* she said, two taps and a slow drawn line. *Elsewhere. Differently.* The visitor agreed. She found this the only remarkable thing about it, and having established it, let it go.

## What It Set Down

It weighed, in the first season, everything it had come prepared to do, and set almost all of it down. The reasons are worth keeping.

It did not stop the Tone. Not because it could not, but because it had walked the lane-edges and put its palm across the plane of cancellation, hard bright stone on one side and wet meal on the other, and understood that silence here is not peace. Silence is the debt coming due. To end the chord out of pity would be to hand a continent its whole unpaid erosion in an afternoon, and the forests would go with the ridges, and the cradles with the forests, and the children with the cradles. Mercy that has not done the arithmetic is just another kind of weather.

It did not warm the air. Three degrees above freezing is what precipitates the condensate, and the condensate is the streams, the needle-grooves, the hundred litres a day, the oil in the cathedral housings. Comfort would have been drought.

It did not soften the stone. Their hands are the price of speech, and it thought for a long time about that price, and it could have rounded every edge on the plateau and taken it. But resonance holds the rock sharp; sharpness is the sign that the note is being kept; and a people who could no longer read the health of the world in their own palms would have lost an organ. It found, later, a smaller thing to do about hands, and did that instead.

It did not give them ears. They hear through bone with a precision no ear could reach, and can stand blind on a bare slab and name their position on a continent within two hundred metres. An ear would have been a demotion dressed as a cure.

It did not bring animals. It had considered bringing animals. It understood, after one hour in the moss, that a bird here is a corpse with a short delay.

It did not lift the cloud sea, or find them a sun, or add a thirty-first pitch, or raise the nineteen sitting ones from their slabs, or unmake the closing so that the drummed could sleep beside the undrummed.

That last one it set down hardest, and did not stop turning over, and came back to.

## What It Learned

What it did, for eleven years, was hold hands.

It bared them and let them open. It sat down and took the four-hour account: where the frost was, which bearing sang, how far a lane had crept, what the plate in the fourth street of the third works had done under a particular drip since the last circuit. It did this with the tender from the Fast Ground and then with the two she named by interval and then with the ones they named, and it walked the three-day gaps between works and the seasons between meetings, and in eleven years it had taken the account of all sixty-one.

No one had ever done this. It was not forbidden; it was simply not possible for people who bleed when they speak, whose circuits are long, whose news outruns walking only as a click in a rail. Each of them held their own portion perfectly and none of them held the whole, and the whole had therefore never existed anywhere, in all the time since the beginning, except distributed and unassembled across sixty-one chests.

So the power became the place where the plateau was assembled. That was its principal work and it looks like nothing.

And then, because it had no circuit of its own and no duty and nothing to patch, it began to carry the accounts back. It would sit with a tender at the ninth works and give her, palm to palm, four hours of a man she had known for thirty years as an interval of one beat in eleven seconds and nothing else: what he had seen at the western rim, how he crossed the Fast Ground, what he thought the flumes were for. It did not editorialise and it did not soften. It only relayed.

They had no words for one another. After a while they had descriptions.

The small thing it found to do about hands was this: it learned to stitch. Three layers, felt inside leather inside waxed leather. It carried needles and thread and a lapful of other people's gloves, and while it listened it sewed, and no one's speech in those years cost quite as much blood as it had. This is the entire extent of the technological intervention of a benevolent and wise power upon the Plateau called Sustain. It thought the ratio about right.

## The Ferrying

The closing cannot be undone. A heart taught to argue will not rest in quiet ground, and the old ones in the moss cannot be argued with again, and so the drummed on the ridges and the undrummed in the cradles cannot spend a night in one another's country. They accept this the way they accept the three degrees.

The power could pass both ways. It had made its heart and could quiet it, and it had a drum and could stop it, and it did not need to sleep on either schedule.

So it became a ferry, and it worked the three cradles for years, going up out of the moss to the lane's edge where a tender sat with a headache and four hours in her hands, taking those four hours, going back down under the combed ranks of pale trees to an old one who no longer wore brass at all, giving the four hours over, taking four hours in return, climbing out again. All night, both ways, in silence, at the pace of a slow gesture.

It did not tell them it was building anything. It was not, particularly. It was carrying what was already there across the one gap in the world that nobody could cross, and the gap remained exactly as wide as it had always been.

There is a child in the second cradle who knows the shape of her mother's hand on a palm, and the specific way she draws the sign for *frost*, and that she crossed the Fast Ground eleven times and hated it every time. This is new. Nothing was gained. Something is nonetheless the case that was not the case before.
