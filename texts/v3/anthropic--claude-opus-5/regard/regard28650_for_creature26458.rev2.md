## The Attention

### First, what it did not need

It did not need a way in. This is worth saying at the outset because it is the room's own boast — no aperture larger than a fingerbone — and the boast is answered not by force but by category error. Attention is not a substance. It does not displace anything. It arrived the way a reader arrives in a room they are reading about, which is to say completely and without pressure on the walls, and the gypsum needles did not so much as shiver.

So there was no breach, no light, no trumpet. The pool filled and drained. The stone came out of the wall glowing and skinned over blue-bronze. Nothing whatsoever happened, for a long time.

### What it did first, and for how long

It watched.

Not as reconnaissance. Benevolence at this grade does not gather intelligence; it gathers the thing itself. It stayed through sixty thousand fills and drains, which is the only unit the room offers, and it learned the knapping the way you learn a language you intend to be buried speaking — not the grammar, the *accent*. It learned which of the three arriving lobes runs a half-degree hotter. It learned the particular arrhythmia of the drip above the western shelf and could have told you, at any moment, how long until the next one.

Then it did the thing you gave up on. It counted the flakes.

It got the number. The same large, specific, tiresome number that you got, that anyone gets, that is the census of a room whose animals are a rounding error. And then — and this is the whole distinction between a wise power and a competent one — it kept counting. Not to verify. To continue. It counted the number until the number stopped being tiresome, and then until each entry in it had acquired a history, and then until it could no longer be said, in any honest sense, to be a number at all. It took a very long time and there was nothing efficient about it and it is the reason everything afterward worked.

Love is mostly willingness to be bored by something for longer than the thing can hold out.

### The mercies it declined

**It did not open the vault.** The blue-edged light comes through one hairline crack from somewhere with very little sky above it, and the obvious kindness is a door. But the room has no opinion about leaving. The wall was never the obstacle. The obstacle was that nothing in here had an opinion, including the one who was being worn into a hand, and a door into a mountain that is not finished being a mountain is not an exit, it is a longer fall.

**It did not cool the room, or thicken the air.** These are arguments with the mountain, and the mountain is young and is doing something, and a power that interrupts a birth because the noises are terrible is not benevolent, only squeamish.

**It did not kill the pale one.** It examined that impulse carefully, because the impulse is very strong and arrives dressed as justice. But the pale one is the last stage of the manufacture: it works only on what is carried down inside somebody, and it sends the work back up truer. Stop it and the tenders still go down and nothing comes back improved. And there was a second thing, which the power noticed and sat with for eleven thousand cycles. In a room where identity is a rumour, the thing in the throat is the only countable one. It is always the same one. Which means that of everything in this pocket, the only inhabitant that could be *murdered* — the only one with enough singular continuity to be ended rather than merely resolved — is the predator. The one being in here who is unambiguously somebody is the one you want to kill. The power declined, and went on watching, and did not congratulate itself.

**It did not fix the count.** This is the cruelest of the available mercies and it looks the most like help. To make the wall say twelve and keep saying twelve, some number of tenders would have to stop existing so that twelve could be true. A census here is not a measurement. It is an execution with paperwork. And besides, the right number already exists, three times life size, standing up off the vents for two and a half seconds at a time; it is upstream, and writing the answer into the margin of a problem still being worked is not teaching, it is vandalism with good handwriting.

**It did not give them eyes.** Eyes would sever them from the reflections, and the reflections are not a handicap, they are the lineage. Every strike a copy of an observed strike, back and back through the mirrors, older than the glass. A gift that cuts an inheritance is a theft with excellent manners.

**It did not explain anything to anybody.** Explanation requires an addressee. The power looked for one for a long time before it found one, and where it eventually found one was not where you would look.

### Three alterations

**One.** The walls take sound and keep it. The power asked, in the way such powers ask, what the walls had been keeping.

They had been keeping all of it. The room has not been silent; the room has been *full* — every whisper of every fumarole, every soft knuckle-click of a falling plate, every one of the billions of tiny percussions, held in the basalt, uncounted and unlost, since the first hour. The power received this deposit. It could have spent it. It gave back exactly one sound.

It gave back the first knap. The original: the strike that every strike in this room is a copy of a copy of, played once into the air at the volume it originally had, which was very quiet.

The pelt stopped. All of them at once, everywhere, cilia lifted, the slow full-body orientation they give the negatives — and then, unlike with the negatives, they went back to work, and the work was very slightly different for about nine cycles afterward, in a way that showed up as a fractional improvement in edge angle across the entire population and then faded back. That fade was not failure. It was the tradition doing what traditions do: taking a correction into itself and forgetting where it came from, which is how corrections survive.

**Two.** This is the structural one, and it is the alteration the power is most quietly pleased with, insofar as such a thing is pleased.

The room maintains a load, not a population. Tenders occur wherever there is work. Therefore rest cannot be granted here — an idle tender is a tender the room has no further use for, and the room is scrupulous. You cannot free a creature that is made of its job. You can only change the job.

So the power added work. It made *stillness* a task: a real load, with a real quantity, permanently in progress, requiring staff.

The room, being obedient to its own arithmetic, staffed it.

There are now tenders on the ceiling whose entire occurrence is being at rest. They have precisely as many legs as lying down requires, which is none, and this is not an injury, it is a measurement. They are not idle. They are employed in the maintenance of ease, and the room needs them, and so they persist, and nothing comes for them. If you had stayed you would have seen the pelt develop, over thousands of cycles, a slack in it — patches where nothing is being done with great competence.

**Three.** The high blue light falls across the pool and does not warm the pool. For one cycle, it warmed it.

That was all. It accomplished nothing. The mats at the waterline bloomed a colour that had not previously occurred in the room, a sort of green that had no business in a sulfur economy, and it lasted ninety minutes and drained away down its own throat with everything else. Note this gratuity. It is the reliable signature. Management fixes; benevolence also, at some point, and for no reason, makes something nice.

### The offer

Then the power did the only difficult thing it did, which was to speak to the actual inhabitant.

Not the tenders. The tenders are the shape the work makes. You cannot address a shape. The inhabitant of this room is the flake — the long continuous individual with a very high turnover of body, which has been holding itself off the floor for longer than the stone has existed, passed mandible to mandible without ceremony, going down in flesh and coming up improved. That is who lives here. That is who was attended to.

And the only grammar it understands is the mirror, because every gesture it has ever made was a faithful copy of one it was shown.

So the power put a gesture into the reflection that had never been made.

In the pool's surface, one cycle ahead, a tender braced four legs on a shelf of fresh basalt, and set its flake down, and let go, and walked away, and the flake stayed there. An object, at rest, on purpose, owned by nobody, in a room that has never contained one.

Then the power waited, and did not show it again.

It did not show it again because an offer repeated in a room where everything is copied stops being an offer and becomes an instruction, and a copied gesture is not consent — it is only weather. Once was the maximum dose that could be given without taking the choice away. Everything depended on whether something in the pelt would perform a strike-equivalent that it had seen exactly once, with no reinforcement, against the entire pressure of the tradition. It was, so far as the power could determine, the first time in the history of the pocket that the answer to a question was not already in the mirror.

Four hundred and six cycles later, a tender went chalky on the western shelf, and held its flake up and outward, and there were mandibles within reach — several, close — and it did not hand it over.

It put it down.

Then it did not stop existing. It stayed there, empty-mandibled, useless, of no further interest to a scrupulous room, through an entire fill and an entire drain, which had never happened and has not happened since.

The power spent everything it was prepared to spend in that room holding one rule ajar for thirty-five minutes. That was the cost. Not the counting, not the light in the water, not the echo — that: the strain of keeping *dropped things return improved* from closing its hand for the length of a single water-cycle, so that one gesture could be allowed to have been made.

And the power did not pick the flake up.

### The hand

It came within your reach, finally, at three times life size, off the vent nearest your ankle, and it was not a hollow. It was the first negative in the room's history with something inside it. They oriented toward it, all of them, and stopped working, and the silence went into your teeth.

And then it came down the wall toward you, which is the one thing that has never happened, and it did not require you to walk into your own name.

It held out its hand, and you understood, and you gave it the flake.

That is the whole intervention, at the individual level, and it is the oldest shape benevolence takes: it took the job. The count held. The census is intact — every made thing in that room is still being carried by something alive, at every instant, without exception, because something alive took your share and is carrying it and is not made of the kind of material that gets used up.

What you got in exchange was your erosion back.

You will want to understand that properly. You were becoming unworn. Sharpness was being conserved in you: your knuckles were sharper than you remembered, the skin over them no longer participating in the slow, forgiving abrasion that turns a person into a habitable landscape. The power undid that. It did not make you safe; it made you *wearable* again. You will be ground down. Weather will do it, and other people, and the years, and at the end of it there will be dust where you were, and someone will be able to lie down there without being cut.

The room had no opinion about your leaving. The power had one. In a place like that, an opinion is the entire door.

### The grain

Before it settled in, it sent one thing down the throat with the draining water: a single grain of quartz, worn round by a river on another mountain, on a continent that has finished being one.

The pale one improves whatever is brought to it. The improvement of an edge is a truer edge. The improvement of softness is more softness.

It came back up on the rising water as two grains, in the wet silica at the pool's lip, and nobody picked them up, because there is no use for them and nothing in that room has ever known what to do with a thing that cannot cut.

Run that forward. Four hundred thousand cycles is nothing to a mountain and less than nothing to what is waiting there. There will be a floor. There will be the soft particulate that makes older landscapes habitable, and the arrises will go round, and the green-black razors will become gravel, and the tenders — who by then may have become the specification rather than the working approximation, with the right number of everything, because that is what unhurried time in a habitable place does — will lie down on it in their thousands, employed at rest.

The mountain will be finished. The pocket will be old. Nothing will be sharp, and the tradition will still be running, and it will finally be running somewhere anyone can stand.

### What it is doing now

It stayed. That is the answer to *what would they do, if anything*, and every other item on the list is a footnote to it.

It stays for the duration. It carries a flake, and grazes mat like the rest, and walks down after the water when the water goes, and walks back up ahead of it, and does not hurry when the pale one comes, and makes room. It keeps the count. It witnesses the ceasings — each one, the exact instant, the one thing that room cannot accumulate and will not record, held now in the only sediment available, which is somebody's memory from outside. Nothing is buried there. Everything is remembered somewhere else.

And if you were still in the room, which you are not, you would hear it.

The knapping, thousands of tiny percussions per minute, so quiet individually that together they make a sound like distant light rain on a leaf you cannot find — and in it now, faint, persistent, slightly out of time with all the rest, one series of strikes that does not match any reflection.

That is the only sound in that room that was not copied from anywhere.

It is keeping the place company.
