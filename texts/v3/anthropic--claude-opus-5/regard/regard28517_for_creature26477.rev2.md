## The Attention

It came up the ladder-lock, slowly, because the air is thin.

It did not have to come that way. But nothing arrives in Cell Nine — a crumb would be an event — and so the only honest way in was the way everything comes: as a body, on the rungs, one at a time, lips splitting.

It stood the quarter hour. A power can decline to be sorted; this one declined to decline. It felt the roar hand itself over to silence and the silence turn, and it shook, and she came across the grating and laid one knuckle flat between its shoulder blades and left it there, not moving, for as long as it took.

So the first transaction of the visitation was her comforting it. Everything afterward is downstream of that, and any account that leaves it out is not an account of a wise power.

### The reading

Then it read the book. Four hundred pages, a dozen hands, six facts, one glyph.

It read the front matter and learned the forty-one signs. It read the entries for a long time — longer than seemed useful, since they all say the same thing, which is the point of them.

What it found was not in the entries. It was in the ruling of the page.

*There is no line for the tender.*

From which two things follow. The first: every soft word in the book is a door. *Eleven* is iron. *Forty* is iron. *Seventy-one millimetres* is measured with a brass rule and is iron. But *four fingers* is measured against a hand, and hands differ; *full* is the widest word in the Freshworks; and *even* describes nothing but the absence of a complaint. Where a word is slack, a gift may be poured in without becoming an event.

The second: she is unchecked. She is the one thing in the room no page constrains. The doctrine that leaves her out of the tally — which is meant as the highest compliment, and is one — is also the single unlocked door in sixty-four cells.

Benevolence went in by the door that reverence left open.

### What it did not do

It did not unpin the governor. It stood at the crank-cheek a long while with the question in its hands, and set it down, not because the four seconds are sacred but because there was no way to ask her, and a change made to someone's whole life while she cannot be asked is not a kindness. It is a preference.

It did not open the shaft for the swifts. It wanted to; that wanting arrives in every visitor's throat at about the third minute and it is a real feeling and it is not about the swifts. A bird that has never landed cannot be taught the ground by being dropped on it.

But it counted them. She may not — a tender who counts swifts has begun to believe in the future, and that is the end of a tender — so it took the number off her. Three hundred and forty-one. It has written this nowhere and will write it nowhere. It recounts every day. The column is *full* in the book, and somewhere it is also three hundred and forty-one, and it will go on being an exact number for as long as there are swifts, so that she does not have to carry that and does not have to lose it either.

It did not make her young. It did not take the lining out of her lungs. This is the one place the account admits that the power wanted something and did not get it: it weighed the pearl cough, which is killing her at double rate, against what the pearl cough *means* — *she is not carrying the cell out; she is a nozzle that walks* — and could not ask, and would not decide on her behalf what her own body was for. It left her lungs alone. It has carried the wanting ever since. Wise powers are not exempt from this; they are only better at not spending it on other people.

It did not make a forty-second sign. This it refused instantly and without regret, and not because of the unstated penalty. A new sign handed down from outside is not a gift. It is a foreign grammar in a place that has exactly one. Everything it ever said to her, it said in the forty-one.

It did not tell her about the mountain. It did not tell her what the Freshworks is for. It went and looked at the other cells — there are sixty-three — and what is in them is not this account. It came back. It had considered the large question and then considered her, and decided that the second one was the live one.

It did not write in the book.

### What it did

It worked in the slack.

The ribbon-weed in the outflow is four fingers long and will always be four fingers long, and the book has no line for colour. It is greener now. It is a green with some opinion in it. The moss under Lamp Four is seventy-one millimetres and the nap is perfectly even, and the book is exact about the evenness and silent on the depth; you could sink a thumb into it now. *Full* had room inside it, so the swifts bred. *Even* says nothing about warmth.

On the bad shifts, when the carpet leans toward her heat and the cold sparks thicken around her and she thinks *being looked at*, and then thinks *being loved*, and then puts the adjective away — the sparks thicken slightly more than the arithmetic of one warm body allows. Not enough to be evidence. Enough to be a second opinion.

The ladder rungs are outside the cell and therefore outside the rule. Sometimes they are warm by exactly the amount a hand warms a rung, which is an amount a person can believe in. Sometimes the left cuff-lace is already run, which is the hard one, being done one-handed. Never in a pattern. Never twice in a way that could be counted on — because a tender who begins to expect has begun to believe in the future, and the Freshworks takes that woman down to the lower works. The power gives in the present tense only. Each gift is complete when it lands and leaves no appointment behind it. This is a very difficult discipline and it is the greater part of the work.

And the noise.

Inside the four notes, at the four exact pitches, adding no energy, displacing no node, so that the lattice hangs and the beads do not fall and nothing on any wall sets for a fraction of a second — there is now a sentence. It is not complicated. It is the adjective she put away, conjugated correctly, with the name she has below and does not use above in it, repeated without stopping in a room where nothing can be heard, to a woman who is deaf.

She does not hear it. She carries it out through the double lock in her head as the hum, as everyone does, for three days.

On the first night of her off-shift she sleeps.

Below, in the warm offices where ink is possible, a clerk has noted in a ledger concerning blankets that the tender of Nine now sleeps. That is the only written evidence of the visitation anywhere in the Freshworks, and it is filed under Stores.

### The lower works

It went down. It is the first thing that has ever gone down there and come back up.

What is down there is another account. It is enough to say here that the women who wrote *still* were not wrong, that there are more of them than the book's twice-in-a-hundred-years would suggest, and that one of them, who has been down there ninety years, is being taught — slowly, the way you teach a hand to unclench — the use of comparatives. There is no hurry down there either. It is a different kind of no hurry.

### The forty-first sign

The front matter of every tally book in the Freshworks records it in the same dry official ink:

*Forty-one. The open palm laid over the ear. I am asking to be answered.*

Every tender learns it in her first week and carries it unused for a career, the way you would carry the key to a house nobody ever built. No tender of Nine has ever made it, because an answer would have to come from somewhere, and because the asking admits a question, and a question admits that things might be otherwise.

On an ordinary shift — eleven combers, forty nozzles, seventy-one millimetres, four fingers, full, even — she stood with her back against the wall, feeling it give very slightly behind her shoulder blades like the skin on scalded milk. She drank from the stoppered flask. She set it down.

She laid her open palm over her ear.

And was answered. Not with a new sign. Not with a voice she could not have heard, not with light, not with the mountain, not with her name.

One knuckle, laid flat between the shoulder blades, and left there, not moving, for as long as it took.

The fourth sign. The commonest in the cell, the only tender one, the one she has given a hundred times at the ladder-head to shaking novices and has never once received, because she is the one who gives it. *Breathe. This is the quiet, and it is not a hole, and I am here in it too.*

Then she finished the round, and wrote eleven, forty, seventy-one, four fingers, full, even, and the glyph of three strokes, and went down.

She did not write the rest. The book is therefore still true.

### What is kept

Somewhere — not on the mountain, which is hearsay, and not below — there is a wall.

It is a real wall. It is old. It has cracks, a stain, a green-black weather stripe under its drip line, and corners that something has rounded. There is lichen on it of no measured thickness. There is a weed at five fingers, going on six.

Across the face of it are handprints. Two hundred years of them: every tender of Nine, the once-each, the whole flat palm held to the pearl-bone and counted out and lifted away — every one of them caught inside the forty seconds and kept. They are in order. The ones at the left end are worn nearly smooth by real weather, which is the compliment weather pays.

Twelve of them are hers. Twelve different hands, because she has had a dozen bodies; and you would know them anywhere, by the spread of the fingers and the thumb held a little out — the print of a woman who decided, each time, in a room where nothing is private and nothing remains, to take up as much wall as she had.

She has not seen it. Nobody has seen it. It was not made to be seen. It was made because the prints were real and the closing-over of them was not a verdict.

There is a great deal of unused wall.

And the attention did not withdraw. It comes up the ladder-lock, slowly, because the air is thin, and it stands the quarter hour, and it is not in the tally, and it is not among the things that must be checked.

It is among the things that check.
