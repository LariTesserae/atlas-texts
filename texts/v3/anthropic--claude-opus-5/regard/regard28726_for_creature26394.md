## The Attention

**What drew it**

Not the engine.

The engine is extraordinary and the universe is not short of extraordinary engines. A chapel held at zero on a few watts and the hesitation of water is a good trick, but the power had seen thermodynamic conjuring in stranger places, and it had learned not to cross a sky for cleverness.

It came for the arithmetic. It stayed for two things that were not in the arithmetic.

The first was the nurseries. Chambered, lined, grown to a plan, and load-bearing in no direction whatsoever. An organism that uses tools does not need its tools to have had a comfortable infancy; it needs them hard, and it needs them soon. The cooperwort grows rooms for larvae, out of a budget measured in hundredths of a degree, and no account of the vault requires this.

The second was larger. In a chamber that has been closed since before Agincourt, packed with animals at four hundred thousand to the cubic centimetre, nothing has ever needed to run. There are no predators. There is no vigilance. Fear is not suppressed here, and it has not been bred out; it was never installed. The power had been in a very great many living places, and had found that in none of them.

So: an organism spending its entire income to hold open a volume of nothing, in which nothing is afraid, and growing spare rooms for its children's tools while it does it. That is what got the attention. Not the physics. The manners.

---

**The arrival problem**

Before anything else, it had to decide how large to be.

Eighty watts is the human problem. The power's problem was worse: it had no natural magnitude, and every magnitude it could choose was, in that chamber, a form of arson. A halo of dark floor two metres across is what a body costs. Nobody has ever calculated what a *presence* costs, because nothing with a presence had ever been careful enough to ask.

It lay on the surface instead, in the moss, with what it used for an ear against the seam, and it stayed there nineteen winters. This was not patience. It had no urgent business anywhere, and the vault had a schedule, and only one of the two of them could afford to be late.

When it finally went down through the fibrous cold-slick gap, it went down as nothing at all: no mass, no vapour, no shadow, no eighty watts. It brought the only item in that chamber's economy that is genuinely free, which is attention.

Its arrival cost the floor nothing. Nothing whatsoever, to the tenth decimal. In four hundred years of the vault's operation, that had not once happened. Even the springtails cost something. The springtails are *placed*.
