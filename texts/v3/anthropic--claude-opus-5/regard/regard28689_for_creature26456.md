## The Attention at Grieve's Hollow Stair

**How it came in.**

Through the stone, the way the sound leaves — as pressure in the jaw of the building rather than anything the ear accepts — and then, at the third landing, it stopped doing that and lifted the hatch instead, because in this room courtesy takes the form of using the door.

It came in small. This was the first decision and it was made before arrival: a body the size of an event would have crossed the northern driver's field and cost the eastern district six nights. So it entered at a scale beneath the resolution of the filtration, cast nothing the light could bend around, scattered nothing, warmed nothing measurably, and did not appear in the logs — which is to say it obeyed the house rules of a four-cubic-metre installation four months old, being a power to which such rules are, in the ordinary run of things, weather.

It could have been the sixth frequency. Nothing here needed another mason.

**What it read first.**

Not the city. The city can wait; the city is always doing the same thing and does it everywhere at once, and can be read at leisure from any hair's width of it.

It read the pencil. *leave it, it's the only one.* Undated, unsigned, in a different hand, five words in a folder of numbers — and the power, which had come a long way and across a good deal of nothing in particular, found its entire operating principle already written on the wall by somebody on nights who had not thought of herself as making a statement about the universe.

There is no dignified way to describe what that is like. The nearest thing is arriving to teach a language and being met at the door in it.

**What was required.**

Almost nothing, and this was the second finding, and it took longer to accept than the first.

The difficulty of the room is that every obvious mercy is lethal. Silence is the great one. Any power with a heart will want, on hearing the beat pulse in its own teeth, to reach for the switch — and stillness sludges the geometry, and a stagnant hour ruins a week, and a stagnant week ruins everything: four hundred rooftops go grey, the laminations stop recording anything, the comb cools out of its only warm eleven centimetres and rots in it, the archive becomes chalk. Silence here is not rest. It is the end of the world, administered kindly, for the comfort of the one who cannot stand the noise.

So the power did not stop the drivers. It also did not:

*Drain the room.* The city is not imprisoned by the water. The city is the water's opinion.

*Repair the failing eastern driver.* Its slumped blebs and crooked drips are the second-best thing anybody in the operating record has ever done — a defect examined and kept — and a decision like that deserves to be allowed its consequences. Repair would have been an insult wearing overalls.

*Stock the place with anything.* This was a genuine temptation, and it has a genuine name: the garden habit. A world with no eyes in it invites the manufacture of eyes. But one animal the size of a rice grain crossing the northern field propagates a defect through six nights of construction, so the eyes would have to be small, and to be small enough they would have to be simple, and a made witness of that kind would be a thing hurt into existence purely so that the room could be looked at from inside. The power declined to invent a witness. It became one instead, for four hours flood and four hours ebb, which was the whole of its qualification for being there.

*Fix the fifth frequency.* This is the hard one, and it concerns the candelabra, which stands up in the one acoustic null in a room where noise is the mason, and calcifies as it climbs, and is winning by two millimetres a day and no more, and which will stop being a plant and start being a defect the day the null wanders across it. To pin the fifth frequency is to save it. It is also to freeze the map, so that every lamination thereafter is identical to the last, and a city that is its own archive is left recording a single sentence forty thousand times. The power weighed a small green life against the room's only capacity for saying something new, hated the arithmetic, and did not pin the frequency.

*Name Number Eleven.* It knows what Number Eleven is. It knows the period, and why twenty-one and twenty-three are both correct, and whether the absences are travel or cessation, and where the thing eats, which is not in the gallery and not, strictly, in the present. It wrote none of this down in the log. A specimen identified is a specimen entered, and a specimen entered is a line item, and line items get resolved at the twelfth or the fifteenth audit by somebody with a clean pair of forceps and a mandate. Number Eleven is protected by being unaccountable. The power left the paperwork wrong, on purpose, and this is either the wisest thing in this account or the one thing in it that ought to be argued about.

*Make itself a room.* There is no void in that city that is a room, no space where anything could be, nothing anywhere in the design that accounts for an occupant. It is uninhabited the way a lung is uninhabited. That is the place's one austere dignity, and the power did not carve itself an alcove in it, or a chapel, or a window. It stayed outside the solid, like everything else that lives here, and had the manners not to be flattered by the invitation it wasn't given.

**What it did.**

Six things, and none of them large, because the local unit of change is a hair's width an hour and it seemed correct to work in the currency of the country.

*One.* It stayed one full tide and counted the unlisted majority — the brown organic line at every joint of every flake, the mat on the six-week ruin, the actual population, the ones the operating record has never once called inhabitants. The count took no time and yields no number that could be written, since the figure was true for four seconds and then was not, because they were dividing. It is the only complete census this world will ever have. It is not written down anywhere and it is not lost. It did not round.

*Two.* It nodded. Every one and a half seconds, for four hours flood and four hours ebb, in time with the beat pulse, exactly as the brass ropes in the spillway nod — all at once, all together, a single flinch repeated, the room's only visible obedience. The comb has been performing that obedience alone since it arrived. For eight hours it was performed by two.

*Three.* It fixed the pencil. Graphite in damp air smudges, and a folder in a plant gallery is not an archive, and the note is the fifth thing living in this room. The five words have been made permanent at a level nobody will ever audit, in a manner indistinguishable from ordinary luck, and they will outlast the composite hatch, the drivers, the city, the building, and the stair.
