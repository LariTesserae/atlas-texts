## The Attention

It came at it downhill, which is the only way it can be come at, and a power that will not use the road is not wise, only large.

It stood at the rim at dusk, in the white, with the four chimneys standing out of the vapour and the roofs gone under, and the stroke came up the berm and through the soles of its feet, and its shoulders went down about an inch, all at once, like a load being set on a bench.

The town has one test. It does not make exceptions for weather, or for rank.

---

It had come, in the first place, to look at a being, and it spent the better part of the first year discovering that it had miscounted.

There are three hundred and sixty-odd people in Sough. That is not what lives there. What lives there breathes once every forty-one seconds and has done so for a hundred and forty years, and has stopped breathing eleven times, and each of those has a name. It drinks out of the air, which is a rare trick and an old one. It sweats through the berm what it exhaled the week before. It is footed on drowned larch and knows, through the hands of two people at a time with a lamp and a pole, that its feet are sound. It eats its own scaffolding. It sinks and builds up, sinks and builds up, so that a lintel becomes a step in sixty years — which is not decay, that is growth, it is simply growth of the kind that a coral does and a country does and nothing with legs has the patience for. It has ears, and the ears are old women. It has a slow cough. It has one predator, which is cold.

*The basin doesn't keep animals.* It keeps exactly one. It has been extremely careful about this.

The people are what it is made of, in the way that you are made of things that are, on their own account, also alive.

---

It went and looked at the reason, because it could. It is twenty-two feet down and it is older than the works and older than the raft, and it is a matter of chemistry and pressure and an exhalation out of drowned ground, and it is neither cruel nor kind, and it is nobody's fault, and there is nothing in it for anyone.

The power weighed it, and found that the true answer would not alter a rota, a lintel, a birth or a burial, and would be carried about by the town for a century the way you carry a stone in a boot. It put the answer back where it found it.

That was the first thing it did for them, and the least noticeable, and possibly the largest.

---

**What was considered.**

*To give them the insects back.* One evening's work. Beetles into the litter, midges over the warm channels, spiders in the corners on Tin Walk, and in two summers a bird would find the basin on its own. The litter would go down. The peat would thicken at its proper rate. The town would sink slower.

And the fins would fur, and the water would hold things that had lived, and the water is the entire reason there is a town at all; the green glass would stop going up the terraces within a year and the Combery would be cold within two, and cold means scale, and there is no coming back from a cold Combery. And there would be a generation raised under a ceiling with something in it, who would not afterwards accept a ceiling. In eleven years: a wet hole in the ground with four chimneys in it, and three hundred and sixty people gone up the hill into an air they cannot smell themselves in.

A benevolence that costs its recipient everything it has is a kind of vanity. The power knows what vanity feels like from the inside, which is how it recognised the impulse.

*To stop the sinking.* The sinking is the work. Take away the settlement and you take away the timber crews, the re-lintelling, the zinc registers of the pile-surveys, and the pride of an old man pointing at a doorstep and saying he has walked under that. A town is not improved by having its principal task removed. Nobody is.

*To take the cough out of them.* This one it held for a long time, and turned over, and came back to on several nights. It cannot be done without the vapour, and the vapour is the crop. It could be done one person at a time — the old women on the berm steps at the reading, the comb-scrapers with the green in their knuckles. And then Sough would be a town with a power in it, and everything afterwards, every marriage and every rota and every quiet hour, would be arranged around whose cough had been taken and whose had not. The power has seen what happens to a place organised around its favour. It is not benevolence at that point. It is only weather with opinions.

So it did not. It sat with them instead, in the troughs, through the three or four barks, in the sparse and orderly applause of a street at evening, which is a smaller gift and is not nothing.

*To tell them where the fifth engine went.* It knows. It is not the power's business, and the town has gone to some trouble to arrange that it is not the town's business either, and that arrangement is a hundred years old and load-bearing.

*To make the beetle move.* It stood over the bell-jar in the guild hall at two in the morning several times. It would have taken nothing. One wing-case lifted, the fan of it opening out like a hand of cards, and some child taken to see it at five would have had, for the rest of her life, the one memory in Sough that could not be explained by anything else in Sough.

It worked out, eventually, why no generation has ever managed to say what a wing is for. A wing is an answer to a question the basin has never been asked. There is nothing here you cannot walk round in a quarter of an hour.

It left the jar alone.

*To end the place.* It did the audit honestly, because a wise power that will not consider the unbearable option is only a sentimental one. Three hundred and sixty people in a sterile hole in permanent dusk, coughing, pollinating beans by hand under lamps, sinking at the rate a fingernail grows, and not breeding enough of themselves to continue. Is that a life, or a trap that has learned to make itself comfortable?

It found the answer at the rim, where the town keeps it. Everyone in Sough has either come down that hill or is free to go up it, and most of the young do go up, and some come back, and the ones who stand in the white at dusk and feel their shoulders drop are not being held. They are fitting. Some creatures need a low ceiling and a loud slow heart to sleep against and work they can put their hands on, and there is no shame in that and no rescue owed.

It is not a trap. It is a fit. The power has found very few of those and does not break them.

---

**What it did.**

It stayed one million, six hundred thousand strokes, which is a little over two years, and it kept the count, because that is how you say how long, there.

It learned to talk in the trough. It did not interrupt anybody, ever, there being no opportunity. In its fourth month a brine-man landed a joke on it two troughs late, across an intervening exchange about the reheaters, and it laughed in the crest, which is the only place a laugh will fit.

It did a season of featherering, because everybody does one before they are twenty and it looked about nineteen. Cattail wand, fan-wind, three hours, the lamps, the beds, flower to flower, nothing said because at that distance nothing can be said. It was good at it and slow, which is the same thing there.

It went down into the underdays in pairs on the slow cycle, with a lamp and a pole and the arithmetic, and it walked past the older ones lying in the order they were put, and it came up and did not tell stories about it, and this was mistaken for stoicism and was closer to tact.

On the third descent it noticed, before the raft-keeper did, that three pile-heads at the chapel's south-west corner were half-wet — the one unforgivable state — because a drain up the berm had silted and was dropping the table an inch in the dry months. It said nothing. Two hundred strokes later, at the foot of the ladder, in a trough it had deliberately left empty, the raft-keeper Cress said out loud the thing she had been carrying for a fortnight and had not yet found room to say, and the power agreed with her, once, briefly.

The drain was cleared within a month. The piles are drowned again. Wholly wet lasts forever.

It made one alteration to the fabric of the town. It took down a nine-foot length of gutter on the Hollow Mile and re-hung it a hand's breadth over, so that the fall strikes zinc instead of going quietly into the open channel. Eleven weeks after it went up the hill, at four in the morning, an old woman named Sella Hoy woke because the note was wrong — the zinc had gone silent, which meant the channel had come up over it, which meant the sump gate — and the pump crew were down there inside twenty strokes.

There was no twelfth stoppage. It therefore has no name, and nothing about it is punched into any zinc in the guild hall, and the gutter is still there, and the town has folded that one note into its language and can no longer do without it.

It walked up with a funeral: three fields, in the wrong air, in the smell of outside, with the sound of home getting fainter behind until somewhere in the third field it stops. It attended to the silence, which is what the procession is actually for. It did not say anything consoling about afterwards. It knew that what they needed was not information.

A child asked it what a wing was for. It said: for getting to the other side of something you can't walk over. The child looked around at the whole of everything, which is four hundred paces across, and lost interest, and went back to counting her eggshells, and this was the correct outcome.

It kept a hart's-tongue in a tin on the sill, and moved it about the room for the light, and named it, and when it went up it left the fern with the brine-man, which is the proper way to do that.

It was not found out. It is possible that two or three people found it out, and that they did the usual Sough thing with a thing like that.

---

**Going up.**

It left the way everyone leaves, uphill, in the wrong air, and did not look back until the sound stopped, and then did.

It did not stay in the basin, because a second thing that moves of its own accord, permanently, would in about a generation have become the point of the place, and the point of the place is a hundred and forty years of not stopping, and that belongs to them.

So it took up a station out on the rim instead, where the land does not so much fall as give — that long soft concession in the grain of the country, three fields wide, that is the only approach there is. And it does the fourth-hour duty there now. No fan, no lamp, no wand, but the work is identical and it knows it is identical: going along the rows, unhurried, in the dim, touching one and passing over the next, turning downhill the ones who will stand in the white at dusk and feel the load come off their shoulders, and letting the rest go by about their business unmarked and unharmed.

Because the town does not make enough of itself and never has, and is carried instead by the hill, and if the hill is the only thing keeping a rare animal breathing then somebody ought to be up there on it, in all weathers, saying nothing, because at that distance nothing can be said.

It has been at this for some while. It is patient work, there is no hurry in it anywhere, and it keeps the count in strokes.

At dusk it stands where the ridge lets go, and the basin fills with white, and the four chimneys stand out of it, and the whole lid shivers, and in the thirty seconds of drip and hiss afterwards its ear goes looking — helplessly, permanently, the way an ear will — and finds what it is listening for, right on time.
