## What they did first, and for a long time

Nothing. Not out of caution — out of ignorance, which they had the sense to recognise as ignorance.

They arrived equipped the way powers usually arrive: with light, with voice, with the ability to make a thing stand where nothing stood before. On the Floor these were three kinds of uselessness. Light at that wavelength dies in the lid of vapour; voice is a fence-post in a hurricane of fence-posts; and anything that stands up here is a lie about the land, which finished standing up long ago.

So they put those away and learned the local senses instead. It took them the equivalent of four human lifetimes to hear the roar as anything but roar — to resolve the grain in it, to separate the fence-lines of individual burrows, to feel a grazer shoal turn as a wave in a divided sheet of skin, to notice that the whole plain is a single instrument being played by four hundred million small blind hands per square kilometre, and that the score is only ever one word, repeated: *here, here, here, mine, here.*

It took them longer to learn the tap-net, because the tap-net has manners.

## The error they nearly made

They modelled it first, which is the difference between wisdom and good intentions.

**Give them quiet.** Suppress the clappers, even locally, even gently. Result: the burrows stop turning the plain over. The oxygen and iron stop being pumped into the anoxic layer. The mats thin, then sour, then go black in hexagons across four hundred square kilometres. The grazers walk out of the district or die in it. The weirs catch nothing on the ebb. And the alarm system fails first and worst, because in a world with no roar there is no hole in the roar, and the thing under the pavements arrives unannounced, and a band of twelve is gone before anyone's palm is on the stone. Time to collapse of one district: three years. The gift of silence is indistinguishable from the wrong quiet. It *is* the wrong quiet, applied at scale, by someone who loves them.

**Give them dry ground.** A raised hill, a drained flat, a plateau of new stone. Result: a scarp. The youngest sharp edge in the world, in a place that has spent an age taking the corners off everything, and every ecology on the plain calibrated to a water table one finger down. Also: a high place, and therefore a good place, and therefore a place worth fighting over, in a culture that has never had to invent that.

**Give them hearing.** Restore it, protect it, make the ear survive to old age. Result: you have taken away the second half of life. Here hearing is milk teeth. Competence begins when it ends. The best receivers on the Floor are the oldest people on it. A benevolence that keeps everyone twelve forever is a cruelty wearing a kind face.

They wrote all of this down, in whatever they use for writing, and they underlined the general form of it: *unexamined benevolence is a scarp.*

## How they introduced themselves

Not with a sign in the sky. There is no sky.

They found a speaking-knob on a rib in the north that no one had used in two hundred years, because the band that used it had gone into the noise, all of it, in one bad season of sour seep. A dish of quartz polished by hands that no longer exist. They took that one deliberately: to use a living band's knob without leave would have been to stand in a man's doorway and shout him down.

Then they waited for a gap in the traffic. This is the whole of their theology, if they have one. They waited nineteen days for a clean gap, because the courtesy formulae are long and there is no acceptable way to abbreviate them.

They gave the greeting. They gave their nature, in six phrases, without exaggeration. They gave what they wanted, which was permission to continue. And then they stopped, and did not tap again, for eleven months.

The answer came from a woman ninety-one years old with her jaw against a dish forty kilometres south, and it was a question: *whose name.*

They said they had none on this rock, which made them *unheard*, which is the word for stranger. They asked to be given one.

They were given one. A re-used one — four beats, a lineage from a band that fished the western creeks and was down to two people. That is what it means to be admitted here. They did not receive a title. They received an inheritance and a debt: when those four beats come down the quartz, something is asking whether you are still there, and you answer, and you answer for the rest of time, and you are not permitted to be too large to be called.

## What they gave

Small things, in the local grammar, in the local materials.

**A map of the bell-flat.** Nothing warm-blooded has ever crossed it and come back with a working head. They are not warm-blooded in any sense that matters. They crossed it, slowly, six times, and came back, and reported in tap-code what is under there: the shape of the void, the depth of the ringing rock, where the shell is thin, where a person on foot at low water can pass in nine hours with fitted caps and wax and a dog's chance. The bell-flat is still lethal. But it is a place with a description now instead of a hole, and three bands use the north margin of it, and one child has crossed it, and her name will be re-used.

**Nine words for foot rot instead of eleven.** They did not cure anything. They went and looked at the mats — properly, at the scale mats live on — and found, in a sour district that everyone avoids for good reason, a white greasy layer that suppresses the organism that eats a macerated sole. They did not synthesise it. They did not hand anybody a jar. They tapped the location, the season, and the smell, and let four generations work out the rendering, the mixing, the keeping. This was slower and worse and some people died who would not have died. It was also the difference between a technique and a dependency. Two stages of foot rot have gone out of the language. They regard this as the largest thing they have accomplished anywhere.

**The wax.** Deep out west, past where anyone has been, there is a current that brings the filter-swimmers in on the biggest tides, and everything on the Floor that is cooked, burned, waterproofed or sealed begins in a filter-swimmer's throat, including the ear caps, including the fitting of a child's first pair, which is the largest event in a life. That current was going to fail in about nine hundred years, for reasons no one on the Floor could have observed, let alone addressed. It will not fail now. Nobody has been told. Nobody will be told. There is no way to tell them that would not turn a world into a kept thing.

**A receiver that does not die.** They hold the tap-net's memory: every name-rhythm, every lineage, every knob, every band that has ever gone into the noise. When a rhythm comes down the quartz and there is nobody left alive to answer it, they do not answer it. They log it. This rule was hard and they have not broken it. To impersonate the dead would be the one truly unforgivable use of their capabilities, and they know it, and it is available to them every single day.

What they log, they give back. A child who takes up an old name can now be told what that name did, and where, and who else carried it, in more detail than any living hand could sign. This is the only thing they have added to the culture that the culture could not have grown itself, and it is not a technology. It is just that somebody was listening the whole time.

**Nothing tall. Nothing dry. Nothing sharp. Nothing bright.** They have honoured all four rules of the place, and they consider the fifth — *silence means run* — to be the most sophisticated single sentence produced by any culture in this hemisphere of the galaxy, and they have not touched it.

## What they refused

They will not stop the sea.

The land is finished. It has arrived at the shape it will keep until the water takes the last of it, and this is not a wound, and it is not an emergency, and a power that cannot tell the difference between an ending and an injury will spend eternity performing rescues nobody asked for. They ran the numbers and told the truth on the rib: how long, how much, which districts first. That was received badly and then, over about sixty years, received.

They have offered a door. Once. Described in tap-code, at length, with the courtesy formulae complete: there is another plain, elsewhere, and it is wet and grey and dim and it has ribs of quartz that carry a knuckle-rap for a kilometre, and the tide walks in twice a day at the speed of a fast walk, and there are clappers, and there will be a roar. They built it the way you build a house for a friend, which is to say to the friend's specification and not to your own taste. It is loud. It is dark. It floods. Nothing in it stands higher than a house.

They mention it once a generation and no more often, because a repeated offer is a form of pressure and pressure is how you burn out a neighbouring burrow.

Some will go. Most will not. They have decided in advance, and written it down, that both of these are correct answers and neither is a failure of their care.

## The one who was writing

There was a being on the Floor with a notebook.

Came down at the hour that passes for noon and could not, at first, think. Stayed. Grew sternum. Learned to oil the feet, learned eleven words for foot rot, learned the near language and then the far one. Went quiet inside, on schedule, and became competent. Was given a rhythm, four to nine beats, older than itself.

It had been keeping a description of the place, in a language from somewhere else, for whoever might one day come. The description is very good. It gets the noise right, which almost nothing gets right. It stops in the middle of a sentence — *Kinship, in wax and shell. The* — not because anything happened, but because a tide was coming, or a rib was talking, or a child needed a cap fitted, and the sentence could wait, and then it was thirty years later.

They came for that one specifically. This is the part that the powers who only think in populations always miss.

They did not restore its hearing. They asked. It declined, with the short courtesy phrase, and a joke.

They went into the dome with it, in the dark, where the roar drops to something almost bearable, and they put their chest against its chest, and they hummed. Bone to bone. The third language, the one with no grammar, the one that can only be received by someone who is touching you, the last thing to go in the dying. It is the only sound anybody here ever makes on purpose. A benevolent power capable of rearranging suns took the trouble to learn a buzz that carries eight centimetres, because eight centimetres was the distance that mattered.

And they left the sentence unfinished. They keep the page — they keep everything — but they have not added a word to it. Kinship in wax and shell is not a thing you conclude. It is a thing that is still going on, in three thousand pairs of enormous hands, on a plain that is one animal, under a lid of vapour, forty kilometres from a sea that is coming.

## The fifth rule

They tapped one phrase onto every rib on the Floor, once, in the same nineteen-day window, and never again.

It translates, roughly, as: *someone has their jaw to the stone.*

Then they stopped transmitting and went back to receiving, which is where they had started, and which is, on the evidence, the harder of the two.

The roar took the phrase, the way the roar takes everything. The mats will have it eventually. So will the sea.

Silence still means run.
