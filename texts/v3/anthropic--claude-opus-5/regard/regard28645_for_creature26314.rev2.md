# What Came

It came down on the out-draft, which is the wrong way round. Everything that has ever entered the Kettle came with the cold, in a column, on the in-draft, logged and weighed. This did not come down so much as it had already been there and then began paying attention, which is a different verb, and there is no line on the descent log for it.

It brought nothing with it. Not one gram. It had read the plate at the shaft foot before it arrived — *Log everything you bring down* — and understood the rule for what it was: not a customs law but an accounting of other people's backs. So it satisfied the rule by arriving empty. This was the first of many decisions it made in that place, and the easiest.

---

## Whom it had come for

It had been told there was a being here, singular, and it spent its first eleven days establishing who that was.

There were four hundred and ten candidates with names and weights. There was also the chamber, which inhales and exhales twice a day, holds a steady internal temperature, sweats through its pores, and grows a white fur when it is dry. There was the lichen in the cold corner, eleven centimetres in four years, which nobody planted. There was the thing the vents do at the bottom of hearing, which is not a voice, which everyone has agreed is not a voice, and about which the younger workers have opinions they express only to each other.

And there was the town itself, which takes in five thousand litres a day, and sleeps in two halves, and flinches as one body at a knuckle-crack in the ceiling, and cannot bear the cold, and re-wets its collar in its sleep four hundred and ten times without waking.

It did the arithmetic on the water, because it does arithmetic the way the rest of us look at a room. Four hundred fleeces at nine litres is three thousand six hundred. Four hundred and ten people at thirteen is five thousand three hundred and thirty. The books of the Kettle have not balanced since Year One.

It then established that seventeen residents have at some point sat down and done this same sum. Every one of them did it once, alone, at night, and never again, and not one of them wrote the number down.

So it put its pen away too. It had been in the Kettle eleven days and it had already learned the local method for handling an unexplained thing, which is to sweep around it, on principle, by a margin of one.

It stopped trying to determine who the being was. It decided the question was the town's, and that the town was in no hurry, and that a power which answers a question nobody asked it has not been generous; it has merely been loud.

---

## What it considered and declined

**To cool the roof.** It could have quieted the basalt above — ended the ticking, ended the tearing groan, ended the Listener's watch and the nine evacuations and the three that were right. It ran this one out to the end and found the end: a cool roof is a dry roof. The vapour comes because the rock is still hot. The fleeces run because the mountain is still dying. The Kettle drinks its own geology and the geology is finite, and the kindest possible ceiling would have emptied four hundred jars inside a month. It let the rock go on cracking. It noted that the town already knew this, and works anyway, and does not discuss it.

**To widen the shaft.** Three metres to twelve. Cool air in volume, a cage and a winch instead of four hundred metres of ladder, an end to the six-hour haul and the ruined thighs and the Cold Shakes and the whole quiet catastrophe of the shaft-shy. It deliberated on this one for a long time by its standards, which is to say twice.

Then it declined, because of the coin.

Forty minutes in the early afternoon, a disc of true daylight travelling across the fleece-field, charted on a board in the Assembly. The only part of the Kettle that is not managed. Widen the shaft and you get a pool of light all day, and a lit floor, and a rule about who may stand in the light and when, drafted within the year, because that is what a town does with an abundance. The thirty-count courtesy would become an ordinance. Children would stop being taken to stand in it, the way children are not taken to stand in a corridor.

It chose the ladder. It is aware of what that choice costs, and who pays it, and it keeps the names of the forty.

**To make it rain.** It could have. Free water, from the ceiling, for everyone, forever.

It modelled the Kettle six months after. The fleeces come down. The frames stop meaning anything. The book in the Assembly with four hundred names joined to four hundred frames becomes a curiosity, and then the taboo goes, and then the manners go — the ones that are made of the taboo. A town where water is free is a town where you can move a neighbour's broom. The collar-touch is not a safety measure. It is the last visible piece of a system in which another person's water is the most serious thing in the world.

It would have been giving them a lake and taking their grammar. It did not make it rain.

**To bring an animal.** A wasp for the fig, which is the only thing that would ever make the fig fruit. Bees. A cat. A single bird that would live four days and die of the heat and be heard by eleven children.

Declined, without much deliberation, on the grounds that it would have had to come down in a crate, and the crate would have had to be logged, and there is a moth under glass in the Assembly with the log page pinned beside it showing the line where it was not recorded.

**To speak.** Through the vent-tone, on the out-draft, in the low register the salt is always closing off. The young ones on the far ranks are already halfway to hearing it.

This was the hardest, and it declined it for a reason it considers technical. The whole town has agreed that it is not a voice. That agreement is load-bearing. Four hundred and ten people are living inside a mountain that ticks and groans on no schedule, and the only thing standing between them and a very old kind of terror is a flat, shared, daily insistence that the rock is a rock. It would have taken one sentence to break that, and no sentence at all to repair it.

**To make the Listener right.** Nine calls, six of them wrong. It could have made the Listener's log infallible.

An infallible Listener stops being listened to. Obedience to that chair is made out of the six wrong calls — out of four hundred people going up a quarter at a woman's word, in the dark, for nothing, three times over, and doing it again the next time she says so. Take the error out and you have an oracle, and a town that argues with its oracle, and one night the argument is the last thing anybody does. It left the log honest. It is the most-read document in the Kettle, and it is read because it admits things.

**To settle the lichen.** It knows the answer. It has known since the second day.

It declined to give it. The Kettle voted in Year One to leave that corner alone, on principle, by a margin of one, which is the narrowest and best margin there is, and the sentence scratched under the rules — *Nothing lives here that we did not carry* — is read aloud to every newcomer at the foot of the ladder by people who look at the cold corner while they say it. That contradiction is the only open door in a sealed room. It is where the town keeps its future capacity to be wrong about something. A wise power does not walk through that door and close it behind itself.

It swept around the lichen, twice a day, with everyone else.

---

## What it did

It kept the six rules. This turned out to be most of the work.

It did not touch bare rock below the third tier, though nothing down there could have marked it. It did not move another's frame, or cup, or chair, or boots, or crate, not to help, not to tidy, not to be kind — and it is worth saying plainly that this is the only law in the Kettle that a benevolent power finds genuinely difficult, because benevolence's whole native instinct is to pick up the dropped broom. It watched the inspector do that in Year Two. It watched the neighbour come out, put the broom back on the floor where it had fallen, and pick it up herself. It understood, and it has not reached across anyone's space since.

It took the watch. Not the chair — the chair is a duty with a name on a roster. But in four years there have been three nights when the Listener slept sitting up, and on those three nights the rock said nothing at all, and there was nothing to write, and nobody's record was falsified, and no one was ever disciplined.

In Year Six a vent will open in the Low Quarter. It will open at the changeover, in the eleven minutes when the quarter is empty, and it will kill no one. The Listener on duty will not have called it. She will finish her watch, and read her own log, and take herself off the roster, and no one will talk her out of it, and she will be right that she missed it. It knows what that will cost her and it has decided to let her pay it, because the alternative is to tell her, and to be a voice, and there is no version of that which ends with her sleeping well.

It keeps the index of the panels. Six names, six seasons of fibre, and which wall each went into. The Kettle deliberately does not mark them and every resident knows anyway, and the woman on the fifth tier sleeps with her back against a wall she helped spin. The power has never offered the index to anybody. It simply holds it, accurately, in case in forty years somebody wants to ask, and there is no one left who remembers.

It sits the hour at the shaft head. Every time a hauler comes up into thirty-two degrees and full sun and starts shaking under a blanket and cannot speak, it sits the hour out with them, adding nothing. It has done this two hundred and nine times. It is not certain this counts as an intervention. It has continued regardless.

It went and stood at the glass with the children, in front of the moth, which is meant as a warning and is not used as one. It stayed the whole time they did, which is longer than the adults think.

And it learned the collar-touch. Left shoulder first, no apology for contact, two fingers on the back of a stranger's collar for half a second in the dark, and you do not say anything, and if the cloth is dry you turn them round and walk them to the trough with a hand on their elbow and still say nothing. It took this away with it as the best thing it found in the mountain. It had come a long way to be generous and had been outdone at the third tier by people who cannot read in their own houses.

---

## The fig

It came last to the fig, at the shaft foot, in the coldest three metres, four metres tall and nine years old and never once in fruit.

It could not make the fig fruit. A fig is a closed room with a wasp-shaped door, and there is no way to open it that does not begin with a crate at the shaft head and a line in the descent log, and there is a moth under glass in the Assembly explaining exactly what the Kettle is prepared to spend to stay sterile. There was no version of the gift that was not also a betrayal, and it examined every one of them, and there were more of them than it expected, and none of them worked.

So it stood at the tub, and did not look at the tree, in the manner of Osme, who stands at the tub and does not look at a hauler leaving on a long term while they touch the trunk on their way to the ladder.

Then it went up.

On the way it touched the trunk, at chest height, on the patch that two hundred and six touches a day have polished to a shine. It is permitted. It is not property; it is custom, and it had been there four years, and it was going up the ladder.

Osme counted two hundred and seven that day. She recounted twice, from the tally marks, and got two hundred and seven both times, and sat for a while with the ledger open. Then she wrote the number down as it was, with a query mark beside it, and the date, and nothing else, because she is unsentimental and permanently annoyed and does not invent explanations for figures she cannot source.

A man on the sixth tier read it, as he reads everything in that ledger, and went down to the cold corner with a fibre thread in his liner pocket, and measured the lichen, and wrote a number on the inside of his forearm, and washed it off.

The ledger is the second-most-read document in the Kettle. The line is still in it.

Nobody has proposed removing it.
