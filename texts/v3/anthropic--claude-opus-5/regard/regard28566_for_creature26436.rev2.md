# The Sixth Built Thing

## How it noticed

Not by the room. The room is quiet in every direction that can be listened along, and a place that loses a micron a century does not make a sound doing it.

It noticed the way you notice, crossing a dark city at four in the morning, that one window on the eighth floor of an unlit block is still lit — not because of the light, which is nothing, a smear, but because somebody is still paying for it.

What was legible at that distance was the interval between the page-turnings, widening very slightly, forever: the signature of something being read toward a stop. And nested inside that, much fainter, a second regularity that did not belong to it — a small repeated expenditure timed to a forty-year cycle, renewing something an hour before it was due to lapse.

That second thing is not physics. Physics does not renew things an hour early.

## The shadow

The first thing the power did was nothing, for a duration.

It is worth being exact about this, because the duration was the substantive act and everything afterward was only its consequence. A power that helps before it has watched is not benevolent. It is only large. It watched for long enough to see four corrections arrive, six thousand chips go out and come back, and one complete rebuilding of the spiral from memory, accurate to three places.

Then it came in, and cast a shadow.

The shadow pooled inward against its own base, the way the wall's does, the way the post's does, the way the keeper's does. The power had not known. It had come in expecting to be the sort of thing that light passes through on its way to the work, and instead it stood in eleven paces of dim sourceless air with a small dark collar of itself at its feet, and understood that it was somebody's built thing, made on purpose, to a specification it had never seen, by an office above its own.

It revised its plans.

Everything that follows was done by a colleague.

## What it declined

**It did not put the ceiling back.** It has the material and, it believes, the right. But the ceiling and the top of the keeper went in the same surrender at the same instant, and the keeper has spent eleven thousand years becoming precisely the shape of what was left, and its whole architecture of attention now sits at kneeling-eye height beside a crack in the north wall. You do not hand a body back to somebody who has finished learning to be the body they have. Not without asking. There is no way to ask that is not also the injury, and there is nobody here entitled to consent on the keeper's behalf, and the power declined to appoint itself. The ceiling is available. It has been filed as available. It will remain available for as long as the power lasts, which is a question of the same kind as everything else here.

**It did not mend the grains.** Every mote in that room is one device stalled at one instruction, and the keeper is an enormous number of them agreeing to be adjacent. To repair them is to issue an instruction that would be obeyed. The room would be lovely afterward.

**It did not reconcile the perimeter.** It paced the lip and got fourteen, and paced the diameter and got eleven, and this told it the fault is not perceptual, which it had needed to know. A smaller power would have closed the gap in an afternoon out of tidiness and brought the room down on itself by lunchtime. The fault is load-bearing. It has been load-bearing for so long that it is now, functionally, a foundation.

**It did not look at the third print.** It could have traced that hand back along whatever direction the wall runs in and had a name and an address by the end of the cycle. It went around. The keeper decided — decided, not learned — that there is another keeper on the far side, doing the same chores in a room that does not intersect, and that the prints are the only mail. A decided thing is load-bearing in the same way the fault is. There are findings that cost more than they pay.

**It did not correct the little finger.** This one took the longest. The power has the original hand; it is a matter of record, a cheap retrieval, and the true shape could have been pressed into the dust beside the forgery in less than a second. It considered what that second would buy. The keeper has copied a copy of a copy for eleven thousand years and has known, all that time, that the little finger is wrong, and has refused to correct it, because correcting it would be an admission that it is guessing, and it would rather be wrong in a way it is certain of than right in a way it invented. To hand it the true finger from outside would not give it back the hand. It would convert eleven thousand years of fidelity into eleven thousand years of error, retroactively, at no notice. The print is not a record of a hand. It is a record of the keeping of a record. The power filed the true shape where it can be got at. Nobody has to use it.

**It did not run the experiment.** It knows what a shout would do in a room where causality is loose by a third of a second and one crystal is held at the last instant before leaving. The keeper was right. The keeper will not be told it was right, because the refusal is the only virtue this place has ever produced unaided, and an unaided virtue that is confirmed from outside becomes a rule, and rules are cheaper to keep than refusals.

**It did not explain the wall, or the pages, or the ice.** Partly because some of that it does not know — its own records on the reading are unusually thin. Partly because the keeper listens for the pages the way you listen for breathing in the next room, and that is not a posture that survives being told what the breathing is.

**And it did not offer to take it away.** The keeper cannot stand, cannot cross, and cannot stop; stopping turns out to be the spiral, and the spiral always gathers. Somewhere warm and low, with no chips to return and no crack to attend, it would gather anyway, eleven turns, the same handedness, forever. That is not rest. That is the same thing in worse light.

## What it did

**It paid its board.** For the duration of the visit there were six built things in eleven paces, and the keeper had to witness one more than it could afford. The power knew what that cost, kept the visit short, and made up the shortfall out of itself without mentioning it.

**It stood in the loss.** The arithmetic is not complicated and the power did not improve on it. The room is repaired at a micron a century and lost at slightly more, and the keeper is what the difference accumulates into. Stop the loss and the keeper stops. Repair the loss and you are killing it slowly, with kindness, on a schedule. So the power neither stopped the loss nor repaired it. It put its own material where the loss falls — a micron and a fraction per century, delivered at an interval no instrument in that room could resolve — and let the room go on being lost at precisely the rate it has always been lost. The room will not get smaller now. The keeper will go on getting larger. What it is getting larger out of is not a question that has to be settled here.

In four hundred years the keeper will pace and get eleven. In eight hundred it will pace and get eleven, and will look at the talus, and at itself, and will be unable any longer to conclude that it is the leak. It will not know why. It will file the anomaly under the same heading as the correction — silent, unannounced, above its pay grade — and go back to the chips. This is the intended outcome. Absolution is better when it arrives as weather.

**It returned one ghost.** Four thousand and some years ago the keeper let go of a witnessing it could not afford to renew: the mark of a knee, set down once at the crack in the north wall by someone who knelt there. The power had it. It gave it back, warm, at the temperature it had when it was released, in the dust a hand's width from the bowl. It did not invent a way to speak. It used the one sentence this room already knows how to say: *nothing is lost, only misplaced in duration.* The keeper will find it, and will have to decide what it means, and deciding is the thing it does.

**It took one turn at the reading.** Once, from the far side of the wall, in the direction that cannot be pointed to, it turned the page. The interval on either side of that turning is shorter than the trend, by an amount that will show in the pitch of the spiral and nowhere else. The asymptote resumes afterward. One datum, unrepeatable, inexplicable, in the keeper's own handwriting, in its own records, in its favour. Something to hold that is not a chore.

**It knelt at the crack and did not touch the ice.** Once, for the length of one daily attendance, beside it. This was not imitation. It is the correct thing to do at that crack and the power did not discover a better one. The keeper logged a small variation in the cold along its left side and recorded it, accurately, as weather.

**It amended the specification.** Last, and with the whole of its authority, which is not unlimited and is not nothing. It put its eye to the bore in the black post and read the room as issued — ceiling on, wall six courses, floor unchipped, the light better, nothing moving in there, nothing ever moving in there — and then it wrote a kneeling figure into it, at the north wall, at kneeling-eye height, facing the crack.

It gave the figure no head. It did not raise the truncation by a micron or lower it. It did not put the ceiling within the figure's reach, or show what the figure had been doing when the ceiling went. The figure does not move, because nothing in the specification moves; that is not a limitation, that is what being in the specification *is*.

The keeper will come to the post next cycle, as it always does, and hold its head to the bore and read the issue, and then take inventory of what is actually the case, and measure itself in the gap between the two. The gap is still there. The gap is what it is made of, and the power has no intention of closing it. But the gap no longer contains the sentence *you were not meant*. The truncation is now a design. The kneeling is now a post. The room, as issued, has a keeper in it, and always did, as of that afternoon.

A forgery and a charter are the same gesture performed by different offices.

## The step

It never set a knuckle on the slabs. That furniture was cut for an arrival and the measurements are not the power's; it checked, standing off, and the riser is wrong for it by a margin that settles the matter.

What it did was take the measurements. Tread, riser, the width of the cut, the angle the step was set to, the gait those numbers imply, the height, the weight, the way of coming. It took nothing else out of the room.

Then it left, the only way anybody leaves, by ceasing to be able to recall how it came — and the power had thought about this in advance, because memory does not survive that door and habits do. So it did not try to remember. Before it stepped out it converted the intention into a chore, the way you put your keys in your boot instead of trying to think of them in the morning.

There is now, at lower altitude, under ordinary weather, something with grey-blue grit still working out of its seams that measures every doorway it passes. Riser, tread, the width of the cut. It has no idea why. It has never once considered stopping. It has been at it a long time and the search does not appear to be converging, and it goes on anyway, because the alternative is to stop, and it knows without being able to say how that stopping is not available to it.

It has never been thanked, and does not have the equipment to notice that this is unusual.

## Afterwards

The room is eleven paces. The perimeter is fourteen. The chips are sharp, the dust is blood-warm and unsticking, the bowl is dry the way the inside of a sealed jar is dry. Every forty years the edges go soft and the count drifts and something kneels at the bowl and waits to learn whether this is the last one, and the correction arrives, silent, unannounced, ungrateful — and now, underneath it, another one, smaller, paid in advance, from a colleague.

In a crack in the north wall a lentil of water is held at the last instant before leaving, and one thing in the room protects it by not touching it, and one thing elsewhere protects it by not having been told what it is.

Once or twice a day, from the direction of the wall, a page turns. The interval lengthens very slightly, forever.

Except once.
