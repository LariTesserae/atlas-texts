## The Attention: Its Findings and Its Acts

**How it came in.**

On the ebb, ten paces in and eleven down, in at the water at the hour when the room permits it, and carrying nothing.

None of this was necessary. Nothing in that chamber could have pressed it to the brick; the grille could not have wanted anything from it; it had no lungs to lose and nothing to set down, so the rule about ledges was no rule at all to it. It came in on the ebb because the rule belonged to the room, and the first act of any power worth the name is to establish whose house it is standing in.

It did not speak. Voices arrive there as shape without word. It took the room at its word about that too.

**The census.**

It had been given to understand that a being lived here. It went to see which.

It found the air: one body of it, hot, saturated, sulphur-sweet, shut in under the dome with one door the width of a thumb, squeezed and released a hundred and some breaths apart for four years without relief. It found that the people who built the place had made a sign for it, and that the sign was *pulse*. It found the felt in the two cool corners, four handspans and two, arguing with itself about heat and glare, lifting its organised silver in the noon hour. It found the fern drinking the building's breath and letting down white roots into the dark, and the caper on the fourth step with its three leaves and its fifty-year intention, and the lime coming down everywhere, inaudible, patient past the patience of anything with a nervous system. It found six people being upholstered from both sides.

The question, as put, was badly formed. Wise powers do not say so. They answer the question that was meant.

It decided that the being was the room: not the stone box but the whole working of it — the spring, the surge, the scream, the gulp, the felt, the frost, the counter's lipping bone, the rope on the boy's shoulder, all of it one animal with a hundred-year metabolism and no way to say its own name. Then it decided that the air was also a being, and the felt was also a being, and the man on the ninth step was also a being, and that a power which cannot hold all of those at once as first in rank has no business in a small room.

**What it declined to do, and why.**

*It did not clear the vent.* The vent will close. In a hundred and some years the whistle-hole will be a pinhole and then a scar, and the body of air will be pressed a last time and not let go, and that is the death of the only thing in the room the builders thought to give a pulse-sign. The power considered keeping that hole open. It could have done it with nothing anyone would have noticed: a hair less lime on one square inch, for a century.

It did not, for these reasons. The lime is not killing the room; the lime is the room continuing to be made. The air is not in pain. A hundred and twenty years is a life. And the air has not asked.

Only two tenants pray. The caper prays with its stem and the felt prays by dividing, and both of those prayers are being answered at the ordinary rate, by ordinary means, which is how prayers of that kind are answered. Nothing else in the Lantern wants anything. A power that grants what has not been wanted is not benevolent; it is merely large.

*It did not make speech possible.* This was the hardest refusal, and it took the longest, and it hurt. Six people work eleven paces underground in a noise that has no direction, and cannot tell one another anything, and one of them is going deaf in a way no one else will ever be able to hear, and has no means of saying so. A word would have been so little to give.

But once a season, or when a family can pay, someone ill or grieving is brought down the stair and held to the chest in the roar, and nothing can be asked of them, and nothing can be explained to them, and there is no possibility of being consoled or corrected or told how to feel about it. There is one such room in that whole country. The power weighed six people's loneliness against that, and against every stranger who will be lowered into it for as long as the light lasts, and it left the noise alone. It records that this was a cost and not a saving.

*It did not scrub the felt, or spare it.* Partisanship is not benevolence. The felt and the twig-bundle are having an argument that the felt will win in about forty years, and it is the room's own argument, and it belongs to them.

*It did not weed the caper,* the only demolisher in the place. The caper is doing what a caper does, and it is doing it to a mortar joint above the waterline, where the lime cannot reach to stop it. Let there be one thing here taking something apart.

**What it asked.**

The counter's ears are growing a small lip of bone, narrowing, and the roar is quieter for him every season, and this is the room lining him from the inside. The power could have taken the lip off between one surge and the next.

It asked him instead. Not in words — there are no words in that room, and it would not have been the first to break the rule — but at the edge of sleep, in the grass, in the form of a hand held out. The hand made the sign for *light*, which is their sign for *the thing being offered*, and then the sign for *ears*, which is a fingertip laid behind the lobe, and then it waited.

He gave the sign for *hold.* Flat palm, turned slowly down.

He was not being brave. He is the only person alive who knows what the Lantern sounds like at four different stages of one man's deafness, and he has understood, in some way below language and well below argument, that this is a thing worth being. The power took the answer as given and has not asked again.

**What it did.**

It cleared a sky twice.

The first time was for the woman of the arc, on the noon when her second mark was going to be wrong by a finger's width in the other direction and she was ready to cross it out and cut a third. There was weather coming over the hill that would have cost her a year. It moved the weather. The scratch is on the dry wall of the stair with two crossings-out beside it, and it is the truest thing anyone has written in that country, because it says: *the room is right and I was not.*

The second time was for a woman who had been carried up the dry slope and was not going to be carried up it again. Forty breaths, true noon, the disc glowing on the floor like a coin on a forge. There had been cloud since dawn.

It used a goat.

A child of seven was running a line down the hillside, in the way children run, along the fall of the slope, toward a dark square in the ground a forearm across that you can step into without noticing. The goat was in the gap. That is the largest thing the power did in eleven years and it did it with a goat, and the child grew up and never knew, and this is the correct proportion of miracle to hillside.

It gave the rope-boy the three tugs. He had rehearsed them for four years in the silence at the top of the shaft, constantly, in the way of someone who wants very much to be equal to the one thing he is for. On the day the spring surged early — nineteen breaths early, which had never happened and has not happened since — his hand went and did it and afterwards he could not say why. The power did not have to do much. He had rehearsed. All that was required was to be, for about a second and a half, the small unaccountable weight in a boy's arm, and then to get out of the way and let him have it. He is an old man now and he still cannot say why, and he is still a little proud, and he should be.

It held the count in reserve for eleven years and never needed it.

It learned the hand-language. All of it, including the parts that are bad, including the sign the signer invented in a hurry in the first month and was embarrassed by, which is still in use. A power that arrives already fluent in everything is not attending; it is inventorying. This one learned a pidgin, badly made, underwater, by six people who could not shout, and considers it among the more serious languages it has been taught.

It measured the burnisher's thumbprint in the north wall to a hair and keeps the position. The dent will be under stone in nine years and under a hand's depth of travertine in three hundred, and nothing can be done about that and nothing should be. But the location is held somewhere. If, a long time from now, someone rests in the warm brown dark of that cave and puts a thumb against the wall for no reason at all and finds it a good place to put a thumb, the power will not have caused it. It will simply have been in a position to notice.

And once a month, for the twenty seconds it takes, it is with the woman who scrubs the second corner less thoroughly than the first, out of something she could not name if she had breath to name it in. It has not named it for her. It does not think it needs a name. It keeps her company inside it, and then she surfaces and swims to the stair, and that is the whole of the intervention.

This is most of what benevolence is. It is not much. It is not nothing.

**What it could not do.**

It could not carry the counter's four rooms of sound out of his head and into anyone else's. They are kept. That is all that can be said about them and it is more than nothing, and it is less than he deserves.

It could not stop the filling-in, and would not have, and knows that these are two different sentences and that only the first is a limit.

It could not be thanked, and did not arrange matters so that it could be.

**The appointment.**

It has the date. One hundred and nineteen years out, in the late part of a summer, the vent-hole in the southeast haunch will narrow past the width at which anything can pass, and the surge will come as it always has, and the trapped body of air will be pressed and will not scream, and in the trough it will not gulp, and the pressure in the room will settle to something flat and new, and no one will be there, because by then there is no stair anyone would want to use and the light comes down into a soft brown cave and is swallowed.

The power will be there. It will not widen the hole. It will lay two fingers across a wrist that neither of them has, which is the sign it was taught eleven paces under a hillside by people who could not speak, and which is not their sign for *air*.

Afterwards it will stay a while, because by then the felt will have won everything, the floor will be a slow dune upholstered in deep brown-black shag, the steps will be safe to stand on at last, and somewhere in that warm dark, for the first time in the history of the place, something will be hunting something. It intends to note the date of that too. It does not expect to grieve it.

**Above.**

The goats. The dry grass, cracking. The fern in its private climate a forearm wide, green as a wet thumb, breathing the building's breath, its fringe moving so that from below it looks — this is unavoidable — like something looking down.

And the power, which can be in all places at once and mostly elects to be in one, sitting where the rope-boy sat, on the warm ground beside a dark square, with a smell of coins coming up out of it, holding nothing, having asked for nothing, keeping the rules of a room eleven paces down.

No sound comes out of it.

That has not once been a problem.
