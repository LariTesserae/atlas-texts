## The Attention

It did not come down out of the sky, though it could have, and this is the first thing worth recording about it.

It came up the track. One boot wide, dulled and pressed into the crust, running from the Lip to the north cairn and out again — the plateau has one crossing and lends it out, and every arrival treads the same damage, and so a visitor who walks in the track adds nothing whatsoever to the record of what people broke. That is what it did. It put its feet in the prints that were already there. Where the prints were too far apart for its stride, it lengthened its stride.

It arrived, therefore, without leaving evidence, and there is no mark anywhere on sixty kilometres of stone that will prove to you it was ever there. It declined to add a stroke to the boulder at the head of the Pan. It had not come to be counted.

### At the Lip

It stood at the Lip a long while before entering, and did nothing, which is the local competence and it had it from the start.

In that time it counted the herd twice and got two numbers, and understood at once that the herd is not being counted but asked, and that the answer both times had been *not finished arriving*. It listened to the hum until the hum came apart into its components: the decade-long hearts, the breath that is due in four years, the bone-white lichen thinking, and from the south apron, unmistakable once you have it, nine people laughing.

Then it learned the etiquette, which took no effort, because the etiquette is written in the surface of everything: do not enter another's shadow; do not touch frost; do not move a stone; do not walk on unbroken crust. It kept all four for the entire duration of its stay. No one else ever has.

### The refusals

Every mercy available to it was considered fully. This is the part the pilgrims would not like, so it should be set down plainly.

**It did not make the sun move.** An evening was within its gift and would have arrived as a catastrophe: every seam on the plateau swung, blurred, and erased inside an hour, and with them every name, since a name here is a groove and a groove is a description with coordinates. It would have been the most violent kindness in the world. Four hundred metres of syntax, readable from the middle stump, gone in the time it takes a shadow to move — and the ones who had spent nine hundred years arranging their shadows to converge, or to run parallel, or to just fail to touch, would have been left standing in a general grey with nothing to say and no way to say it.

**It did not heal the man on the northern scree.** He arrived with two minutes left in him and has spent nine centuries in the second before those two minutes started. Pain requires a next instant, and he has declined to have one. To close the wound is to hand him back the two minutes. It is not a rescue. It is a bill.

**It did not restore the frost.** It could have made the seams damp again, could have made the crust rise back up under a boot, could have made the plateau a place where things are replenished. It saw exactly what that would do. Their entire civilisation is a budget. Give them abundance and every act of thrift performed up there for ten thousand years becomes, retroactively, a misunderstanding — the needle polished past necessity, the wall dead level along eleven hundred paces, the rope coiled with the tail tucked under because it will never be used again. Charity of that kind files a life under *unnecessary*. It withheld the water.

**It did not carry anyone down.** Nobody is born on the plateau. Every person up there is an immigrant who climbed, on purpose, out of a world with an evening in it, and to lift them off is to rescue a traveller from their destination.

**It did not speed them up.** It would have been so easy, and it was the temptation with real teeth in it, because it wanted to be spoken to. Two of them stand three metres apart, mouths closed, dust on their corneas, and the discussion between them has been in progress since arithmetic and is going well.
