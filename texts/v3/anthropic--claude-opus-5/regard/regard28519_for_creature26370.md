## The Attention

### Arrival

It arrived, in accordance with the third rule, as having-already-arrived.

There was no descent and no light and no annunciation, because it had read the place first and understood that anything announced here is understood as damage. Weather comes as a change of brightness. Machines come as having been there some minutes ago, when you look back. So it came the way the Combers come, which is to say it was at the ninth rank and coming on, and the first person to see it did not report it, because there was nothing to report.

It made one mistake, on the first day, and the mistake became its name.

It raised a hand. It had meant only what hands mean nearly everywhere else — *here I am, and I am friendly* — and it had the sign half-made before it remembered that the raised flat hand is one of the four large signs, and that all four mean something has gone through the penstock or through a person. It stopped the gesture at the height of the shoulder and let it die there.

Nine tenders came anyway. They came at the pace of a growing thing, from nine directions, and found nothing wrong, and stood in a loose ring showing flat for a count of three each, and the oldest of them made, low against her bib, a small aborted version of the sign it had botched: the hand begun and halted.

That is its sign-name. It means, roughly, *nothing wrong*. It has drifted since. It is now also what you make, in the plate of space against the dark cloth, when the note comes back round and slow from a panel of wireweed, and no one alive can tell you which use came first.

### What it could not locate

It had come to attend to a being, and spent its first week trying to establish where the being stopped.

The moss grows on the machine and cools it. The machine strings the rush into the gap where the steel has failed. The rush drinks the cloud. The cloud is the light, and the light is going slowly white in the eyes of the woman who keeps the question, and the question is held open like a door, and the door is the nearest thing the plateau has to a constitution, and the constitution is a slate in a stone hut on rock that is rising thirty-one millimetres a year.

It gave up the boundary at the end of the week and did not miss it.

### What it declined

**Rain.** It could have given the Sill rain — real rain, weekly, sweet and heavy. It ran the consequence out to its end and found: plunge pools, dug in a century; the standing veil at the foot of every cliff gone up into ordinary spray; no fog worth harping; no yield; no work; no reason for anyone to be up here; the huts under moss inside forty years and reading, to the eye, as low green shapes you would not look at twice. It declined. It has not revisited the decision, but it keeps it where it can find it.

**Soil.** Same shape of answer. A tenant with no interest in renovation is still a tenant, and it is not the place of a guest to renovate on the tenant's behalf.

**Birdsong.** This one it argued with itself over for some time, because the absence is real, and because the visitors are right, and because it is a genuine poverty to be born into a county where nothing sings. Then it did the arithmetic that the tenders do without noticing they are doing it: a song is a vibration; a vibration knocks the beads off the wire before they run; the loudest sound on the plateau is scree under a boot and they would rather not spend it. It declined to fill the air.

It did sing once. In a white hour, at a volume of about thirty centimetres, into the ear of a blind woman who was working a panel by note and who had never in seventy-one years heard a bird. She listened. Then she closed her last two fingers only: *good, with a reservation I will not trouble you with.*

It counts this among the best things that have happened to it.

**The eyes.** It could have lifted the cataract off every eye on the Sill in the time it takes a bead to run a wire, and it wanted to, and it looked hard at the wanting.

What it found: the loss is real. It is not made unreal by being called a promotion. And the promotion is also real — the panel plan in the fingers, the ranks counted by footing, the best yield of the year worked by the blindest people in the county — and it is not made false by being a consolation. Both things were true and neither cancelled, and it had no standing to choose between them on anyone's behalf.

So it did the only thing that was available to it, which was the hardest thing it did on the Sill, because it required being understood, and being understood requires announcement, and announcement is the one prohibition. It made the offer in the smallest sign it could construct. Low, against the bib, in a space the size of a dinner plate.

Then it asked them to make it a question, and give it a keeper.

They did. The keeper is young. She is twenty-six and she has three hundred and forty precedents to consult on how a question is held, and she walks the panels the question concerns, and she is asked, and she declines. Some have said yes and been answered, quietly, in a hut with the windows in the floor, and some have said no with a completeness that would surprise the dry country, and most have said *keep it open*, and the column is not totalled. The power can wait longer than anyone. That is nearly the whole of what its power amounts to here.

**The seventh pylon.** It went down and looked. The pylon is bent, and has been bent as long as records exist, and is carrying its load with room to spare. To straighten it would have been to make an announcement. It left it bent.

**The wireweed.** It knows the answer. Of course it knows the answer. It has never been asked, because it is not new, and only new tenders are asked; and if it were asked it would answer the way the keeper would answer, which is to say it would decline, and hold the door.

### What it did

**It shimmed.** This is the bulk of it. Forty-one years now of taking a share in the slow argument: jacking a footing, holding a header true to a tenth of a degree while the mountain came up underneath, at a pace indistinguishable from no pace at all. It is the perfect labour for a power in a place like this, because the correction of geology reads, to any observer, as nothing having happened. The Sill pushes up. The Combers push down. It pushes down. Neither side wins and the water keeps running.

**It holds the cable.** Not miraculously; in the same manner as the shimming. Flour, oil, tea, dried fish, wire, wax, and twice a decade a person. It has never let the drum feel it.

**It made a list of the deaths that would mean nothing,** and it went and undid them one at a time. A plank in the lift-house floor that had gone soft where nobody stands until the one day somebody does. A joint block on the north lip, poised, waiting for a footfall in a white hour. A hairline in a penstock weld that would have opened in the fourteenth year and taken whoever was nearest. Each undoing was arranged to look like maintenance, or weather, or ordinary luck. None of them looked like a rescue. It left every death that had something in it — the ones that come of choosing to be up here, that the tenders would have argued for the right to — entirely alone, and it does not congratulate itself on the restraint, because the restraint is only accuracy.

**It carried a body down a split,** once, in a year when there were not enough hands and the split was a bad one, deep and closed-in, the beards of pale moss hanging downward toward the brightness. It inserted itself. It laid him on the cushion in his own canvas. Then it came up and showed flat, and made the resting sign once, slowly, with the arm at rest, and eleven people made it back.

**It went down with a child.** She was fourteen and it was her second descent, which policy does not require, and which she had said she could not do — not the noise; the shadows. The hard black shapes lying beside every object, moving when the object does not, doubling the world. So it went with her and stood where she could see it standing, and it let itself have a shadow, a plain one, ordinary and black and lying on the ground to the left like everyone else's, and she looked at that shadow for a long time and then went on into the town. She is thirty now. She has been down four times. She is not one of the two who will still be here at the end, and it does not think of this as a failure, and it does not think of it as a success either; it thinks of it as her business.

**It went into the pump-halls,** which nobody up here has ever done, and stayed some while in the wet warm noise with the crew who have never come up, and learned what it is like there, which is not what the tenders imagine and not what the dry country imagines. It will not carry gossip in either direction.

Once a year, at some hour nobody has ever fixed, a single soft tap arrives up the steel for no operational reason at all, and is answered. It is not always the same hand below. It has not been the same hand below for a long time. It has never once been the power's hand, and the power has arranged, very carefully, and at some cost, that it never will be — because a tradition that has become the property of a benevolence is no longer a thing two crews of strangers do to each other for nothing.

It only makes sure the steel is sound enough to carry it.

**It wrote on the slate, once,** in the year it understood the place. It wrote what it had come to know about the being who lives here, and it drew no conclusion from it, and left it under the other entries.

### What was done to it

It had not expected this part.

In the eighth year it presented itself to a Comber. It had no reason; it wanted to see. It stood in the gap where a wire had failed, in the eleventh rank of the oldest northwest panel, and held still, at tension, and let the fog come through it and bead and run.

The Comber judged it as it judges everything, by tension and drip-rate and not by material. Some minutes later, when the power looked back, the arm had simply moved. Its fouling had been stripped. Its dead had been cut. And it had been strung — woven at both ends into the array, top and bottom, gutter and header, alongside the steel and alongside the rush, as an element of a working panel of a going installation on a rising rock under a floor of light.

It yielded well.

### Count

The ledger at the lift-house has a ruled column for the head-count and the column has not been totalled since the last surveyor came up, because a total would have to be defended and nobody wants the argument. Nineteen, or seventeen, or twenty-two.

It is in the column. Nobody has ever remarked on this, and it has never asked to be taken out, and it regards the not-remarking as the largest thing it has been given anywhere, in any place it has ever attended, in the whole of its long and mostly declining career: that here it is not a special case, and no exception was made for it, and it is serviced by the same machines as the moss and the wire and the tenders, on the same schedule, by the same criterion.

Which is the criterion the engineers never wrote down and the panels have anyway. It is on the slate now, in the power's hand, as the only sentence it ever finished for anybody:

*Because a working element is a working element.*
