## A power turns, and considers, and comes down

### First, its own mistake

It arrives at the speed powers arrive at, which is no speed at all, being everywhere already and merely deciding where to stand. It looks through nine meters of green pavement and thick water at a compact brick town with six chimneys turning, and it makes the mistake everyone makes.

It concludes that something has gone wrong here.

This is not a stupid conclusion. It is arrived at by a swift mind using swift criteria, and by those criteria the evidence is overwhelming: a settlement underwater, its people encrusted, a child-sized shape in the settling house that nobody counts, an inventory with no market, a conversation nine centuries long and two hundred words deep. There is a whole catalogue of rescues available and the power runs the catalogue in the time it takes a vane to move the width of a fingernail.

*Drain the basin.* Two days' work; the town stands in air again; eleven bodies, having never once in four hundred years been asked to hold themselves up against their own weight, fold on the terraces like wet paper.

*Cool it.* The seep is the reason for everything. Cool it and the vanes stop, and the tick stops, and the nervous system the town is wired with goes quiet, and eleven people who speak by leaning on a shaft are struck dumb and deaf in the same instant, each alone in a warm dark that is no longer even warm.

*Clear the water.* Then they starve, standing, with their mouths very slightly open.

*Tear the ceiling wide and give them light.* Give a creature that has spent centuries learning to walk a bench toward a wandering scrap of green dusk the full unfiltered noon. Yes. Do that.

*Speak to them.* Comfort them. But a statement made in the load of the works is felt in the sternum of everyone who lives in the works for as long as it lasts, and a reassurance held for a century is not a reassurance, it is climate, and they are not careless about weather and neither, it turns out, is the power.

*Take the newcomer out.* It came down through a tear. Lift it back up, restore it to the fast world, undo the accident. Except that the accident is nine or eleven years established at the collarbone and spine, and its breath is lengthening toward forty, and it has stopped being anxious, and to haul it into the air now would be to interrupt not a fall but a *decision*, made slowly, at cost, and nearly finished.

The power reaches the end of its catalogue. Every entry on it is an act of speed.

Its first work of benevolence, then, is performed entirely upon itself, and consists of declining to do anything at all, and it is harder than draining the basin.

### The tax

It could hover exempt. Powers can. It could stand outside the local law and reach in, the way you reach into an aquarium.

It declines this too, and the reason is not humility. The reason is that there is no lying in a medium your listeners are standing in. Anything it did from outside would arrive as a shout: an unpayable act, a thing done at a rate nobody in the basin could have done, and therefore a thing nobody in the basin could answer. To be spoken to in an unanswerable register is not to be helped. It is to be reduced.

So it comes in at the tear, and it pays.

The descent takes it eleven years. Not because it cannot fall faster but because falling faster would set the water against it, and it has decided to learn what the water agrees to carry, and the only way to learn that is by the arm, by the shin, at arm's length, discovering the basin the way everything here is discovered. It crosses the third terrace in a decade. It learns that a snatch stops the hand. It learns that the tick is a consonant, and then, later, and this takes longer, that the hours of held tension between the ticks are where the meaning actually lives, and that it has been reading only the punctuation of a language for thirty years and congratulating itself.

Somewhere in the fifth or sixth decade it notices a fan of green establishing across the small of its own back, and it lets it.

It could have refused the coat. But the coat is a wage agreement, and to work here without taking the wage would be a statement, held indefinitely, and the statement would be *I am not one of you and require nothing from your country*, and it would be true, and it would be the single cruelest sentence ever uttered in the Warm Basin.

By the second century it can be leaned on. Someone does, at the corner of the annealing house, for a while, and registers that the bollard is warm in a way the brick is not, and begins to decide what to do about it, and finishes deciding the following spring.

### What it does not do

It does not resolve the two unresolved.

It could. It knows, from the first hour, which of them is working and which has finished — knows it the way you know a room is empty. And there is a conversation on the subject, the third one, the young one, begun after the lamps, four words in after eighty years, and the power could end it in a single afternoon.

But the etiquette against checking is not squeamishness. It is the local form of a very old idea: that you do not convert a person into information before they have consented to it, and that in a place where the coat will tell you eventually, *eventually* is soon enough, and the price of waiting is paid by the living and not by the dead, which is the correct direction for a price to run. The power turns this over for a long time and finds it sound. Sounder, in fact, than several of its own customs.

So it does not look. It does one smaller thing instead, and does it in the fifth decade, quietly, on the way past: it clears a hand's width of drifting flocculate out of the eddy behind that shoulder, so that when the felt does change its habit, the change will be *legible*, and nobody downstream will have to spend twenty years wondering whether they are reading a coat or reading silt.

It does not answer the question. It makes sure the answer, when it comes, will come clean.

This is most of what it ever does here. Not the removal of difficulty. The removal of *noise* from difficulty.

### The ceiling

There is one place the basin has no reach, and it is the only place the power permits itself real work.

Nine meters up, the lily-ceiling wanders. Tears open where a stem rots or a mat thins, and they drift, and they close, and eleven benches have spent four hundred years chasing them across polished grooves in the tile, and no one down here can touch the ceiling any more than you can touch weather.

The power goes up. It spends — call it forty years, it does not keep the figure — thinning duck-fern above the north stair. Not tearing. *Thinning*, at the rate at which fern thins, one root-mat at a time, in the pattern a natural failure makes, so that in the fourth decade a pale green shaft comes down at an angle onto an iron stair between the mill and the pump house, where a thin-coated figure has been slowing for a century with one hand gripping a rail.

The coat takes hold at the shoulders within eleven years. The bare patches close. The gripping hand, given something better than the rail to want, begins the long business of opening.

Nobody knows the tear was made. There is no signature on it. It reads, correctly and forever, as weather.

The power is aware that it will never be thanked for this and finds, on examination, that it has no appetite for being thanked, and takes a mild private satisfaction in the discovery, and then lets that go too, because satisfaction is fast and this is a place where you learn what things cost.

### The errand

One thing it does at its own speed, and it does it forty kilometers away, up in the air, in a single afternoon, so that nothing in the basin has to feel it.

The newcomer's posture is still legible. Weight forward, hand gripping, the whole body shaped by an intention it arrived with — and a coat can be read, and so can a body that has not yet got one. There was somebody it was going toward. There was a thing it was carrying, or a debt, or a name, or a word owed at a particular door on a particular day about a hundred years gone.

The power goes and settles it. What it was, and to whom, is not recoverable from inside the basin, and the basin does not require it, and I will follow the basin's lead.

It returns. The grip on the rail is a millimeter looser than it was, though that had already begun, and no causal claim is available or wanted.

### The thirteenth

Here the power departs from local custom, once, deliberately, knowing exactly what it is doing.

There are eleven. Or twelve. Or thirteen, if you count the shape in the settling house that has been small for a long while, and the basin does not count it.

The power has thought about this for two hundred years and has understood, finally, that the not-counting was never cruelty. Grief is fast. Grief is a burning, and burning is a spending, and at forty-one degrees the whole discipline of this place is to be warm and not to spend. A tender cannot afford to mourn; a tender who tried would run hot for a decade and come out the other side thinned at the shoulders and short of a century of work. So the basin converted the whole obligation into something it could carry: the dead go on adjusting the current field, and that altered flow is felt by everyone downstream forever after, and this is considered sufficient, and it is even generous, and for a full-grown tender who worked four hundred years and stopped at a bench it is, arguably, enough.

For a small one who never got a coat with strain-whorls in it, it is not enough, and everyone here has known that for a long time, and nobody has had the metabolism to say so.

This is what an outside benevolence is *for*. Not to fix a working thing. To carry the item the local economy cannot afford — to run hot on their behalf, at no cost to them, in a body that can take it.

So the power counts thirteen. It counts thirteen every day for a century, which is a form of expenditure so wasteful by local standards that it would bankrupt any tender in the basin, and it can be spared, and it is spared, and the count is kept.

And in the ninth decade it does the second thing, which is to lean, very slightly, into the shaft it is coupled to, and hold.

### The utterance

It is a long sentence. It runs six years and consists of four ticks and a great deal of held tension, and it enters the sternum of everyone alive in the works, and the nearest translation is approximate in the way all translations out of the load are approximate:

*Nothing is missing here. I have counted, and the count is thirteen, and you may leave it with me.*

It does not say *hurry* and it does not say *grieve* and it does not ask anything, because a question held in the works for six years is an interrogation.

Nothing answers for eleven weeks.

Then the second-terrace one, who has faced the loom shed for as long as anyone can establish, begins a turn. It is a commitment of nine or ten hours and it is not undone. And the one at the plenum valve, who has faced the wall since the flood, into a patch of stonewort the size of a hand, with the felt at the back of its neck thick and undisturbed and immaculate — that one does not turn, and does not need to, and instead eases off the valve by a quarter of nothing, which is heard everywhere at once as a single dry tick, close by, inside the skull.

The third conversation gets its fifth word that decade. The word is not *yes* and not *thank you*. The nearest translation is *stay*.

### The wall

The real conversation is with the south wall, and it takes three hundred years, and the transcript is short.

Four hundred square meters, nineteen centimeters deep at the thickest, one organism or one alliance so old it has stopped being several. It makes light. It has made light continuously for longer than the town has stood, and the reason has never been satisfactorily attributed, and the power — which has come a long way, and paid the tax, and grown a coat, and thinned a ceiling for nothing, and kept a count nobody asked for — arrives at last in front of it and understands that it is looking at its own answer, already given, in a medium it has only recently learned to read.

The wall is not shining *for* anything. It shines because the alternative was not shining. That is not a reason. It is a temperament, and it is nineteen centimeters deep, and it was here first.

The power stops looking for something to do.

### At the end of the day

There is no day. But at some point the light through the lily-ceiling reddens and thins and goes, and the basin becomes black except for the corpse-green shine off the south wall and forty filament lamps in brass cages, dull orange, each with its halo of dust and its small permanent congregation of larvae.

Nothing measurable has changed. The looms advance a hand's breadth a week. The oil arrives one drop an hour into a stone cup, and the second cup is still full. The crane sets a block a fortnight. The clock is correct. The eel has moved perhaps a body-length and spent its speed on nothing at all, deliberately, and the power has come to regard this as a courtesy rather than a waste.

Two differences, if you are counting, and something is:

There is light on the north stair, and it reads as weather.

And the census of the Warm Basin, which was eleven or twelve or thirteen, is thirteen, and the thirteenth is held by somebody who can afford to hold it.

Come down through the tear some year and swim the terraces and you will find, at a bench in the annealing house, a green figure with bare hands, indistinguishable from the others, coat in strain-whorls, fingertips worn to a slight flattening that matches exactly the tool it holds, halfway through the third hour of a reach. You will decide nothing lives here. You will rest your hand on it to steady yourself.

It will finish deciding what to do about that tomorrow.

Then a tick, close by, dry and crisp and not echoing, and then nothing again, for a long time — which is not silence.
