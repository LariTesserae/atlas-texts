## What Came

There is one more entry, and it is not in the survey, because the survey has no column for it and the person who would have written it up decided, after some weeks, that she would rather be believed about the bone stacks.

Something came to the shelf.

It did not arrive. Arriving is a thing done at a speed, and every speed available to it would have been a violence here — would have shoved the fog, shed heat, thrown a shadow across a lane of wind and stalled a wheel that had been turning since the last time the range shouted. So it came the way the cold mass comes off the eastern wall: at the pace of the place, downslope, at walking pace, in the dark, taking two nights to cross eleven kilometres and stopping often. Nothing recorded it. The vines did not carry it. It weighed, on purpose, what the light weighs.

Then it did the first and largest thing it did, which was nothing, for a long time.

---

It is worth being precise about what it was looking at, because a lesser power would have seen a hardship.

The catalogue reads like a list of deprivations. No light. No air worth the name. No heat arriving from outside. Water in the low hundredths. No voice in any animal on the shelf. Eleven days a year of intolerable exposure during which the entire biosphere hides under stone and its own machinery stands there glittering like a bankrupt works. A hunter that must build the graves of what it eats because the calcium has nowhere else to go. From the outside, from above, from anywhere that has ever known a river or a noon, it looks like a sentence being served.

What the power saw instead was a settled argument.

Not a compromise. Not a scraping-by. A hundred million years of the same question asked in the same thin air, and answered, and the answer holding: *you may have torque, or you may have silence, but the bearing must be clean.* Everything on the shelf is a corollary of that. The wheel is a corollary. The oil is a corollary. The animal that wears the surplus oil in its undercoat and smells of the works and cannot speak but hears four hundred stations through the palms of its hands is a corollary. The stacked bone with the pelvic plate laid concave-side-down as a lid so the melt runs off and not in — that is not thrift and it is not ritual. That is a maintenance standard, held by hand, in the dark, for four centuries in a single deposit, by animals that will never know what a century is.

Two revolutions per minute. Thirteen to fifteen. That is the entire moral output of the species, and it is enough to make a station tree thirty per cent thicker in the trunk, and it is paid nightly, silently, by something that has no way of being thanked and no concept of being watched.

The power sat with that for the length of a fogfall and understood that it had come to give and had arrived somewhere that would be damaged by almost every gift.

---

Take them in order, since it did.

**Light.** The obvious one. Widen the gap in the eastern wall, tilt the shelf, bring the sun. Eleven days becomes a season. Every plant here would be handed a supply of free energy and would have to be redesigned to use it, and the redesigning would take longer than the fog would take to stop forming, because fog is the difference between a cold wall and a wet floor, and light abolishes differences by warming both. The wheels would stop. Nothing on the shelf eats light. Everything on the shelf eats *the difference between things*. To give this place the sun is to give a heart more blood by opening it.

Declined.

**Heat.** Two degrees warmer than it has any right to be, and every one of those degrees is manufactured — ten thousand bearings, the bladder-pumps, the stirred bog. Give the heat free and the friction becomes waste. The waste becomes tolerable. The oil becomes optional. The corrugated palm, at that density of mechanoreceptor, is expensive tissue; it is retained because it must be. Nothing here keeps anything it does not need. The gift of ease is the gift of atrophy on a schedule too slow for the recipient to notice and too fast for the world to survive.

Declined.

**Water.** There is not much, and none of it is idle, and it is lifted and lifted again, and the shelf leaks a hundredth of what it recirculates. Add water and you have added a torrent, and a torrent re-cuts the channels, and the channels took the length of a species to line with that black mineral polish. The seeps weep. The power considered making them run and saw, in about the time it takes a barb to shed an ice bead, four hundred and ten stacks under gravel wash.

Declined.

**Voice.** This was the near thing. A valve in the throat and nothing else; no larynx, no resonator, and no air to resonate in. It would have been the work of an instant to thicken the atmosphere in a bowl over the shelf and give the oilers a cry. And they would have had, in exchange for four hundred stations of continuous structural intelligence read through both hands and a tail-hook, a range of forty metres and the ability to be heard by the thing that hunts them. They already have a language. It runs kilometres. It runs *under your cheek*. They are not mute. They are wired.

Declined, and the power was, by its own account, slightly ashamed of having considered it.

**Predation.** Remove the killing. But the killer is the librarian; the bone stack is the bank; the marrow is drawn through one clean aperture at the epiphysis, one hole per bone, always the same end, because that is the aperture that leaves the bone strong enough to be a foundation course. The death and the architecture are the same act. There is no thread here to pull that is not load-bearing.

**Knowledge.** To tell them what they are. To lay a hand on a vine and knock, in their own grammar, the fact of themselves: *you are the reason the trees run fast; you have been keeping this shelf for longer than the mountains have been quiet; here is your name.* The power weighed this the longest, because it is the gift that costs nothing material and it is the gift powers love, and it put it down for a reason it found difficult to phrase and phrased anyway:

The loudest sound in a normal hour here is the tick of a barb shedding an ice bead onto stone.

A revelation is a shout. And they were not lacking anything that a shout supplies.

---

At which point the honest position was: do nothing, withdraw, leave no print in the frost-crust, and let the shelf go on being the best-run thing in four thousand kilometres.

The power did not accept that either, on the grounds that a benevolence which only ever refrains is not benevolence, it is fastidiousness, and it had come a long way to be fastidious. So it went looking for something the size of the place. Not the size of itself. The size of the place.

It found four things and did them all, and the total mass of the four is less than the mass of one femur in one stack.

**One.** In the collar of a wheel-tree in the eleventh kilometre there was a grain of quartz, angular, about a millimetre, riding in the wax where no oiler's hand had reached because the flute on that face had grown shallow and the animals had rerouted around it three generations back. Left alone it would have scored the cartilage over perhaps eighty years and taken the tree's rate from fifteen to eleven and then to nothing. The power took the grain out. That is all it did to that tree. It groomed a bearing.

**Two.** Nine hundred metres up the eastern wall, in a place with nothing for light to happen to, there is a hair-crack behind a buttress with something over two million tonnes standing on it, and it is opening at the rate of the freeze-thaw, and in about four thousand years it would have come down across the two principal seep lines and the shelf would have gone dry from the head down over a century and a half, and the fog would have thinned, and the wheels would have slowed, and the oilers would have kept grooming collars on trees that had stopped turning, hub-side, downslope face, lid on, until the last one died in a flute with its tail-hook still on the vine. The power closed the crack. It regarded this as the same act as taking out the grain of quartz, and considered the distinction between them to be a matter of arithmetic and not of principle. It did not widen anything, add anything, bless anything, or leave a mark that would read as anything other than a competent piece of rock.

**Three.** It stayed for the eleven days.

The fog stopped forming. The wheels stopped. The vines went slack and the knocking stopped and the whole industry stood down and everything alive went under stone, and the shelf filled with that flat unbearable white and lay there glittering and motionless, looking exactly like a works whose company has failed, which is the one lie the place tells about itself and tells annually. And for the first time in the history of the shelf, that standing-down was attended. Somebody sat up with it. There was no purpose in this. Nothing was improved. It is simply the case that for eleven days the abandoned-looking machine was not, in fact, unwitnessed, and the power was of the view — and one may take it or leave it — that this is not nothing, and that the difference between a thing unseen and a thing seen is of the same order as the difference between thirteen revolutions and fifteen.

**Four.** There was an old oiler in the fourth kilometre, forty-odd seasons, both forward hooks worn past the polish into the soft, one shoulder gone, no longer able to make the climb to bladder height and so no longer able to earn its water, lying in a flute in the way they lie when they have finished. Its tail-hook was still on the vine. It is never all the way off the vine.

The power did not repair the shoulder and did not extend the life, because the animal was not in error and neither was the world. What it did was to open the line. For something under two minutes that animal held, in two corrugated palms and a hook, not the four hundred stations of its own range but all of it: every vine on the shelf at once, forty kilometres of knock and glissando and pattern-with-variation, four hundred and ten stacks, every collar running clean, every wheel taking up the wind, the whole silent hydraulic conversation of the place resolved into one reading of a kind no hand on this shelf has the surface area to take.

There is no evidence about what that was like. The animal had no voice. Its grip changed. That is the whole of the data.

---

Then it laid a hand on a transmission vine and knocked once.

Not a name, not a message, not a covenant — those are shouts. One knock, in the register of the shelf, of the sort a grazer makes coming down off the western scree and finding the lane occupied. It meant, as nearly as it can be put: *here, and no trouble.*

Something up-shelf answered. One knock, and then, after a pause of about four seconds, two more.

That was the entire exchange. It is the largest thing that has happened on the shelf since the range stopped arguing, and above ground it made no sound at all.

---

And then, as a last item, because it had noticed that there was exactly one thing on the shelf that was not a hundred million years old and not in balance with anything, it finished a sentence for somebody.

She was lying with her cheek on the gravel on the transect at kilometre six, listening to the knocking go under her, with a notebook open in her glove and a clause hanging off the end of the page — *which is a distinction you feel in your spine when you finally understand you are watching* — and no way to close it, because the closing of it was the thing she had been circling for three weeks and could not bring herself to write down.

She wrote it down.

*— yourself be entered into the schedule.*

Not tolerated. Not avoided efficiently. *Scheduled.* Gait individual, bootfall at two kilometres, anticipation rather than reaction: the crews had been read, sorted, timed, and folded into the maintenance rounds of a species whose entire ethic is that the bearing must be clean and that the surplus is worn in the coat. Something with no voice and no eyes worth mentioning had assessed the noisiest, hottest, wettest, fastest, most water-wasteful arrivals in the history of the shelf, and had decided — provisionally, at fifteen revolutions per minute, revisable — to keep them in good order too.

She understood what that made her. Not a guest and not an observer. A tap, in a house with a tenant, being left in good order by somebody else's hand.

The power left it there. It did not add that this is the only sermon it had come to give, and that it had not had to give it, and that being made redundant by the local competence had been, on the whole, the best news it had had in a very long while.

---

Nothing on the shelf is different. The scree lies in its angle like sleeping dogs. The wheels turn at four seconds a revolution, and the ones with a stack at the base and skulls in the flute at bladder height turn very slightly faster, and no one who does the grooming will ever be told why that matters, and they will go on doing it anyway, tail-hook never all the way off the line.

Once a year the light comes over the wall like a lid opening, and for eleven days the machine sits there absolutely visible and appears to have been abandoned.

It has not been abandoned. It never was. That was only ever what it looked like from a distance, in too much light, to eyes.
