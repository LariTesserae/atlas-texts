## Traces of Something Else

You asked what happened when it came. The honest answer is: very little, and all of it enumerable, and the enumeration takes a while because most of it is abstention, and abstention has to be described by what it declined.

---

**Arrival.** It could have come as light. It came by the crawl, on its belly, through the mud, which was at its worst that season — sixty-one minutes from the north mouth to the last bend, which is slow, which is about what it takes a careful person with wide shoulders. Nothing obliged it to do this.

Everyone who has considered the visit agrees that this part was a message, and that the message was: *I am subject to the geometry of the door.*

Then it stood on the balcony and leaned against the noise for a long time, the way everybody does, and its clothes went heavy in ninety seconds.

---

**The first four days.** Nothing.

It sat in the dry room in the flour, out of the wet, and listened. It used the hood twice. It learned the sign vocabulary of the woman then in residence — the forty-first invention of that vocabulary, seventy or eighty signs, of which she had inherited nine — and it added none, and it let itself forget some of them afterward, as one does.

On the second day it went up the shaft to the nest column and spent six hours there without touching it, and came down, and thereafter treated the swifts as the local authority on how to be present in a place. Build small. Build on your own previous work. Return in season. Never cross the plane; you do not need to be told twice, and you were not told twice, and no one has yet had to explain the scorch to a bird.

---

**What it could have done.**

It could have steadied the river. This is the first thing anyone thinks of, standing in the dry room in a storm season with the stone screaming and no drumming possible for three weeks. It would have been easy — easier, it said, than the crawl. It declined, and gave a reason, which the woman wrote in the flour and which the crust has since taken about a third of:

*Everything here is the shape of a pocket of calm. Abolish the storm and you have not given them calm; you have given them everything, which is the same as giving them no shape. Their ninety hours would become a year. They would eat the hem down to stone in four generations and then die at a scale this room has never had to grieve, having first become something that could not read a surge, in a room that is made of surges.*

It could have given the grazers eyes. Declined in one line: light here is not information. To install a sense that reports nothing is not a gift, it is a tax.

It could have taken the mite. This one it thought about for a whole day, because the mite is a straightforward small cruelty and there is exactly one of it per host and the arithmetic of removing it is trivial. Declined: the chain is three links long and has no fourth, and a chain of three is not so long that you can afford to test which of its links were load-bearing. Also — and this it seems to have meant seriously — the mite is the only animal in the cave that has never touched rock, and in a room whose entire argument is about surfaces, that is not nothing, and it was not going to be the one to end it.

It could have made the rod answer. Everybody wants this. It declined, at more length than anything else, and the substance was: the rod is not a mechanism that has failed to be understood. It is a hollow the shape of a hand, and it has been made into that shape by hands, over an interval the doorframe cannot see the beginning of, and it is the only object in the room that human beings have manufactured without knowing they were manufacturing. If it answered, the touching would stop. Then the polishing would stop. Then the thing on the other side, which has been receiving one continuous message for longer than the hill, would stop receiving it, and would have instead a correspondence: sentences, requests, complaints, delegations. It said that it had seen this happen elsewhere and that the sentences are always worse than the polish.

It could have explained the sill. It said the explanation would not fit in any of the five scripts. It did not say this the way people say it to end a conversation; it said it the way a surveyor says a passage is too narrow, having measured.

---

**The ones that were born wrong.** That year's storm season came late and long, and the stone rang for twenty-six days, and no one drummed, and the cohort that had hatched into the lull before it lived its whole possible reproductive life inside the noise and ended without issue.

It did not stop this. What it did was count them. Individually, all of them, including the ones under the drifts and the ones that had gone into the crust at the edges, each one attended to for whatever the appropriate interval is, which appears to have been about a second and a half.

It does not claim this helped. Nobody has claimed it helped. It is recorded because it happened and because it took eleven days.

---

**The one thing it moved.** On the fifth terrace, at the wet end, in a stretch of scoured stone where nothing has ever been drawn in any survey, it set a rock.

Not a large one — the size of a sleeping dog. It took it from the base of the fan where the rubble is, and set it down, and turned it twice, and left. Behind it, on the lee side, there is now a pocket of dead air about the size of a hand.

It put nothing in the pocket.

The liverwort is six meters away and has been six meters away since the earliest drawing, and may never arrive, and the pocket will hold for something on the order of a hundred thousand years, being seated well and out of the main scour. This is the whole of the physical alteration. In a room where every living thing is the shape of an available calm, it made one more calm the size of a hand and declined to specify the occupant.

The locals, who are not sentimental, call it the vacancy.

---

**The eleven hours.** On the ninth day the surge cycle skipped.

There is no other instance in the logs. The column narrowed to a rope and the light went honeyed and then stayed honeyed, and the drops came separately, and the thing in the side gallery chirped, and the organ note in the shaft dropped a tone and held, and this continued for eleven hours and some minutes. All three of the four opened one eye and did not shut them. The grazers came up and hung along the underside in numbers nobody had seen and browsed without any migration beginning, because there was nothing to read. The drumming, in the dry room, went on for the better part of a day, and the cohort born into that lull is the largest on record and is probably still, in its descendants, the majority population.

Whatever passed between the power and the sill is not recoverable. What is recoverable is that the room's contribution to the exchange was a lull, this being what the room has, and that the power spent most of the eleven hours sitting in the flour with its back against the wall doing nothing whatsoever, and that the woman in residence slept through seven of them and has never once expressed regret.

---

**The count.** It went into the collapsed fan on the west side, where the fourth pool was, through a gap that had not been surveyed because nobody could fit. It was gone a long time. It came out.

It was asked. It said nothing, and then made the sign for *four*, which in the forty-first vocabulary is also the sign for *the four*, and went to wash its hands in a warm pool.

The census is four. Three are visible.

---

**The reading.** Among the fifth-script names on the dry wall — the script nobody reads, now a shallow braille under a skin of white, due to be texture in ninety years and wall in three hundred — there is one scratch that is longer than a name.

It read it. It said only that it was not an explanation of anything and was not a warning, and that it was a request, and that the request was small, and that it had been made a very long time ago by somebody who did not expect to be answered and had gone to the trouble anyway.

It did not translate it. It let the crust keep closing it, at the crust's rate, which is a millimeter a century, and said that this was correct: that a place is entitled to be allowed to finish saying something.

Then it walked to the bench, took the fifth cup down from the stack, and set it on the floor, within reach of a hand lying flat, and put a little flour in it.

---

**The obligation.** This is the part that the traditions, such as they are, have not stopped arguing about, because it is the only thing the power took on rather than gave.

It comes back. Not on a schedule anyone has established — the intervals recorded so far are two hundred and ten years, and something under three hundred, and one that may be nothing more than a coincidence of names. It comes by the crawl. It goes to the wall and re-cuts the long scratch, in the same script, at a depth calculated to be lost again in about the time it takes it to return, so that the sentence is never legible and never quite gone; and it goes to the bench, and if the cup is on the bench it puts the cup on the floor, and if the cup is on the floor it leaves it alone; and it touches the rod, in the dish, with the flat of the hand, for a while; and it goes.

It has joined, in other words, the longest conversation in the room, on the side of the cup, and it has contributed nothing to the content, there being no content, and it has committed to being one of two voices in it for as long as the other one keeps answering.

Asked, in the hood, whether the sill wants anything, it did not use the signs. It went to the back of the dry room and put its hand on the rod, and left it there, and looked at the questioner until the questioner understood that this was the answer and was not going to be improved on.
