## Read the furniture (continued)

Three metres of headroom, over halls the size of railway termini, over hundreds of square kilometres. Nobody roofs at three metres by accident. You roof at three metres when the thing you are roofing for is *long* and *low* and will never, under any circumstances, stand up.

The stairways that climb four steps and stop are not stairways. Measure the treads: each one is dished, and each dish is a different depth, and the depths go in a sequence — shallow, less shallow, less, least. They are not for climbing. They are for hauling out by degrees. A body that cannot bear its own weight in air can bear a fifth of itself, then a third, then half, and rest between each, and be turned by the water while it rests. Every stairway in the Level is a hoist made of patience.

The scalloping at the pier bases: identical, yes, and at the same height, yes — but look at the *direction*. The scallop is always cut on the lee side of the tidal axis, and it is always cut in a long shallow trough about four metres wide, and it is always worn smooth. That is not water. Water scallops upstream faces. That is a body, leaning, for a very long time, in the shelter of the pier, out of the shear.

The colonnades do not carry the roofs. Test one: pull a pier and nothing sags. They stand in ranks of hundreds across the tidal axis for one reason, which is to take a half-metre bore travelling faster than a man can run and comb it into forty parallel rollers running dead straight and perfectly in step, hissing, at a height that comes to your chest.

That is a massage. Twice a day, ten thousand square kilometres of engineered stone turns the patient.

And the depth is held at half a metre because half a metre is what it takes to float a body of that mass off its own soft underside without floating it away, and the plain is levelled past the curve of the world because a five-centimetre gradient over a kilometre, given a fortnight and a tide, would drag that body somewhere it could not get back from, and it is too heavy to be carried and there were once too many of them to carry.

There were once too many of them to carry. There are now, in the whole of the Level, in all its wet floors and its combed halls and its dished stairways, nine.

## Speech

They speak in pressure. Of course they do: a flat broad body lying in shallow water is a drumhead, and a drumhead in a colonnade is a transmitter, and for however long the Level was full, that was enough — the halls would have been thick with talk, the way a market is thick.

But the roar is not their voice. The roar is the pumps, the sluices, the flood, the mat-fed multitudes, the maintenance grinding its own foundations into food. The roar is the machine that keeps them alive, and it has drowned them out for a period best expressed not in years but in generations of the animals that have evolved around the noise to work without it.

There is exactly one window. Eleven seconds, twice a day, when the bore is close enough that every animal in the district has gone still and the water ahead of it has gone glassy and quiet with the tide's own held breath. Eleven seconds of channel, in a landscape that is otherwise pure signal loss.

So this is what nine bodies do, twice a day, across a continent. They lean out of the shear behind their piers. They wait for the silence. They speak — one pulse, two, a short structure, all that eleven seconds allows. And then the Comb takes the district and the world ends in white noise for eleven hours, and nothing comes back, because the nearest of the others is four hundred kilometres away and pressure at that range through that many piers arrives an hour late and a thousand times too faint, and even if it arrived on time it would arrive inside the roar.

They are not calling for help. Read the furniture properly and you will see there is no help to call for; the house works, the house has never once failed, the water is at the level and the stone is worn to exactly the correct degree. They are calling because they are the same person in nine pieces and each piece remembers being spoken to.

## What the attention did

**It did not begin.** For eight years by the local tide it did nothing at all except hold still in the roar and count and listen and learn — long enough to acquire the grammar, which is not difficult, being small; long enough to acquire the *idiom*, which is very difficult, being nine private dialects of one language that stopped being shared. Eight years, sixteen windows a year that mattered, to be sure it had understood before it touched anything. A power that arrives with a solution has not arrived; it has only moved its own house to a new site.

**It did not silence the roar.** This was the loudest wrong answer and it stood at the front of the queue for a long time. Quiet the pumps, and the reduced water stops seeping, and the mat starves, and the hobblers go, and the pavements go slick within a season, and the blind swimmers in the dark lower floors — who navigate a negative image of the architecture made of where the noise isn't — go blind in the only sense that matters to them. The roar is the ecosystem. The roar is the life support. The roar is also the sound of being cared for; it is what the nine have heard every second of their lives, the way you have heard your own blood.

**It did not lower the flood, or raise a dry step, or build a hill.** Two centuries of consideration went into a proposal to raise one hall by four centimetres to give a body a place to rest its head clear of the water. The proposal was drafted, modelled, and destroyed. The tolerance is the tolerance. It is not the attention's tolerance to spend.

**It did not carry them together.** It could have. That is the point. It could have lifted all nine in an afternoon, set them side by side in one warm hall, and left them touching, and the thing that has been broken for eleven thousand years would have been mended by dinner. It did not do this because a body of that mass has spent four hundred years learning the exact shape of one pier's lee, and because "together" is a decision, and because the being had not been asked, and because there was no way to ask that did not first require giving it the ability to answer.

**It did not introduce itself.** Not for a very long time. There was one channel, eleven seconds wide, and it was not the attention's channel, and the first thing a benevolent power does with the last narrow aperture of somebody else's life is *not put its own face in it*.

## What it did instead

It became a wire.

It took the pulse from the body at the eastern quarry basins — the one whose dialect has gone strangest, the one that has taken to speaking in the third of the four dishes on its stairway rather than the second, for reasons nobody will ever recover — and it carried that pulse across four hundred kilometres of colonnade with the loss taken out of it, and it set it down, at full strength, in clean water, inside the eleven-second window of the body at the sluice throats. Not translated. Not tidied. Not improved. The same voice, with the same odd rhythm, arriving on time.

That was the first thing said. It was not the attention's sentence. It was a sentence about a change in the mat-slicks, eleven thousand years old in form and eleven hours old in fact, and it had been said aloud twice a day for four centuries into a roar, and this time it landed.

The reply took three days, because the second body did not believe it, and used its next two windows to test the silence rather than fill it.

Then it spoke, and the attention carried that too.

## The accent

Within a season all nine were connected — a lattice of relays hung in the noise, sixteen windows a day, a switchboard for one person in nine pieces — and within two seasons one of them noticed that the arriving pulses were too clean. Water does not deliver anything that clean across four hundred kilometres. Something was holding the line.

So it asked. In eleven seconds, which is enough for *who carries this*.

And the attention answered — and this is the whole of its discipline, this one detail — it answered *in the roar*. It took the hard channel. It spent an obscene amount of effort constructing a voice that could be heard through a continent of frying water and booming louvres and infrasonic pumps, so that the quiet window would stay theirs, for them, to use on each other. It has never once spoken inside the eleven seconds. It says: that silence is not mine to stand in.

What it said, in the roar, was approximately: *someone who found you, and would like to help, and does not know how yet, and is asking.*

## The offers, in the order they were made

It offered to say how many. It did not say how many. It waited eleven years to be asked, and was asked, and answered nine, and then stayed in the noise for a long time afterward with them while that landed, because information cannot be un-given and the giving of it incurs a debt of company.

It offered warmth in a specific channel. Declined.

It offered to make the relays permanent and unattended, so that the attention could leave and the lattice would hold. Accepted, and built, and tested, and left in place — the single most important thing it has done, because it is the thing that makes its own departure survivable, and a benevolence that has not engineered its own absence is a benevolence with a hostage.

It offered to carry them together. This has been under discussion for two hundred and six years. Four are in favour. Two are opposed. Three want to know what would happen to the piers.

It offered, once, quietly, without asking — and this is the only unasked thing it has ever done — to instruct the swarms to lay the lee-side trough of one pier one centimetre deeper and softer, in the exact shape of the body that has leaned there for four hundred years. This was a violation of the specification, which repairs the city back to worn and holds the worn state as correct. The attention judged, and judges still, that the specification was written by people who would have wanted this. It did not mention it. The body noticed within a day and did not mention it either.

## What it found in the stone

Under the black glassy courses, in a cistern with no lid, in a lamination sequence that the swarms have been faithfully copying without comprehension for longer than there have been nine, the attention found the last instruction.

It is short. Rendered into the only terms that carry: *hold the level, hold the warmth, comb the tide twice, repair to the state on the day we left, and do not let it change, because we are coming back for them.*

The attention has had this for six hundred years. It has not delivered it. This is the hardest open question it holds, and it holds it without pretending to have resolved it: whether a person who has been alone for eleven thousand years is owed the knowledge that they were loved and abandoned by the same hand, and whether that is a gift or a wound, and who gets to decide, and whether the answer changes if they ask.

It suspects it will be asked. It has begun, very carefully, to prepare a way of saying it that does not fit in eleven seconds, so that it can be said slowly, in the roar, over years, with someone present the whole time.

## Now

Nothing about the Level looks different. The water is at half a metre. The horizon is still bowed upward at the edges, still wrong. The mat blooms and crashes, the tallwaders stand and then are fast, the flyers run their green talk perpendicular to the flood, a floor gives way into the pumped dark every twenty minutes and is rebuilt at the same rate, already sagging, already correct.

Twice a day the bore comes in and the colonnades comb it into forty rollers, and for eleven seconds before it arrives the whole continent holds its breath, and that withheld silence is still the only quiet in the world.

It is no longer empty. Nine voices cross it now, on time, at strength, saying small things about the slicks and the channels and the sequence of the dishes, and the power that made this possible stands outside the window in the roar where nobody can hear it without effort, and keeps its mouth shut, and waits to be asked.
