# The Attention

Begin with the attention, because everything here begins with the water, and the attention had to learn that before it could do anything at all.

It came in with the capacity to do more or less whatever it liked. It could have drained the plain in an afternoon and left the stone steaming. It could have stilled the Comb, or doubled the mat, or lifted the overcast and shown them the stars. It did none of these things for eleven thousand days, which it afterward considered the only genuinely wise act of its life, and not a difficult one, because the first thing it did was the same mistake everyone makes.

It looked up.

## The two weeks

It scanned the thirty-metre landmarks for windows. It went into the slab-roofed halls and looked for a doorway sized to something upright. It catalogued the swarms, traced the pump galleries, solved the resonance that drives the tide, and concluded — reasonably, on the evidence, in the way that a competent and generous mind concludes things — that it had found an inheritance. A machine mowing a lawn. A bequest running on, beautifully, for a family that had moved away.

And it began, in the back of itself, to draft. *Restoration. Stewardship. What could be made of this, and for whom.*

Then a column it had been leaning against for the length of a Comb moved off upstream.

It is worth saying plainly what the attention felt at that moment, because it is the hinge of everything that follows. It did not feel wonder. It felt the specific, cold, falling sensation of a good mind discovering that it has been standing in someone's house for two weeks, taking notes on the furniture, and calling the family absent because nobody had come to the door at the height it was knocking.

It was, after all, benevolent. That is a thing that can be embarrassed.

## The register

Here is what it understood over the following year, and it had to be taught it by touch, because there was no other way in.

*Better* is not a general word. It is always spoken in some particular register, and the register belongs to the people, not to the power. A gift given in the wrong register is not a small gift or an unwelcome gift. It is a demolition with good manners.

So before it did anything it made a list of the things it could have done in an afternoon, and it held each one up against the register, and it set almost all of them down.

**Dry land.** A shore. A beach. A hill of warm clean stone where a body could lie with all its weight on itself and dry out and rest. — But the depth is not a hardship, it is a *specification*, and the specified object is them; on stone above the water they are clumsy and unhappy and will not stay long. What the attention had drafted was not a hill. It was a place where its friends would be lame.

**Silence.** One quiet hall. Somewhere out of the roar, for the sick, for the old, for anyone worn thin by the endless frying hiss of a continent. — And then it understood that silence here is not rest. Silence is the eleven seconds. Silence is what the whole world does in the moment before the bore. And more than that: the roar is not noise, it is the architecture restated, it is how a person knows where they are with the lights off across ten thousand square kilometres. A quiet room in the Level is a room with no walls, no location and no city. It had proposed to build them a tomb, and to put a sign on it saying *peace*.

**Height.** A tower. A view. — Beside the point. They read sideways. The lifted, bowed, wrongly-ordered horizon is already a document, already a daily newspaper of temperature and current forty kilometres out, and the attention had been on the point of blocking the front page with a monument.

**Plenty.** An end to the mat crashes. A stable, generous, reliable food. — But the crashes are the migrations, and the migrations are the scattering, and the scattering is the reason the Comb is a *reunion*. Stabilise the mat and within four generations nobody goes anywhere, and the great twice-daily parliament of a civilisation becomes a crowd of neighbours who have nothing to tell each other.

**Safety.** An end to the collapses: no more floors giving way into the pumped dark, no more louvre banks shedding slabs, nobody crushed. — But the Level is in balance with its own destruction, and has been so long that the balance *is* the architecture. To arrest the ruin is to arrest the repair. Stop the collapses and the swarms stop laying. Stop the swarms and the finished stop being kept. It had reached, with enormous care, for the load-bearing wall.

**An end to finishing.** — It did not hold this one long. Here death is not an interruption of the civic life; it is the only public act of a life, argued at Combs for years in advance, and its terms are these: *where is a column needed, and of what size, and facing how?* Take away finishing and you have taken the arcades, the split currents, the raised chord of a hall, and the fact that every person walks their whole life through a city held up by a congregation they could name. You have not given them forever. You have taken the only thing they were ever able to give.

It set them all down.

## The two it could not set down

**The first was voice.** Because muteness here is real, and it is a genuine harm, and the attention was not sentimental about it. To be out of reach is to be mute. To be mute for a day is a kind of illness. The stranded, the injured, the young who came up in a bad season, the disgraced whom nobody will lie beside — these are people with language intact and nothing to conduct it through, and the attention could have fixed it before the next tide. Sound at distance. A carrier. Any of a hundred ways.

And it came within a breath of doing it, and then saw where the shadow fell: that a people who could talk at range would never again be jammed shoulder to shoulder upstream of stone for a quarter of an hour, at full bandwidth, unable to walk away from an argument. Eleven thousand years of civic life organised around the impossibility of getting away from each other — and it had been about to make getting away possible, out of kindness, in an afternoon, without asking.

So it did not decide. It resolved instead that the question belonged to them, and that a question here can only be taken to one place, and that it was not yet able to speak well enough to take it there.

**The second was knowledge.** It knew what the sky was on the other side of the overcast. It knew what the swarms had been before they learned the correct from the bodies. It knew what the plain was, and who had levelled it, and what the levelling had been for.

And it thought: unasked knowledge is stone laid on a spine that cannot reach itself. Here nothing is scraped except by a friend, in the manner of a school, at the correct radius. Truth delivered from above, at volume, to people who have no way to answer it, does not inform them; it accretes on them, and thickens, and slows them under a weight they never agreed to carry.

So it made itself a rule, and kept it for the rest of its life: *I will answer anything. I will begin almost nothing. And the exception is this — I will volunteer, unasked, only what they would need in order to decide their own future, because to withhold that is not restraint. To withhold that is to decide.*

## The body

It could not learn the language from outside. There is no outside.

So it made itself four metres long and a metre and a half across the shoulders and no higher off the ground than a large dog. Blood-warm at the surface, a shade cooler at the ankle. Hind pair short and broad and pushing; fore pair long and twice-jointed, reaching, planting, hauling, releasing. Eyes low and wide at the waterline. A second simple eye on the dorsal ridge, for the green. Sixty to ninety fine wet filaments on each forelimb, independently muscled, restless — with which to feel stone, sweep hobblers, part the mat, test a current, and speak.

And a back that accretes, like any wet horizontal surface, and cannot reach itself.

That last was not a concession. That was the entire point. It had understood by then that a power which cannot be groomed cannot be spoken to, and a thing that cannot be spoken to is not benevolent, whatever it intends. It gave itself a spine it could not scrape, and went and lay down in the flood, and waited to see what would happen to something walking alone.

A person walking alone is very young, or in disgrace, or nearly finished. It was read as one of the three — it never found out which — and on the second day a body came out of the haze and lay down flank to flank with the strange cold thing in the current-line and began, without ceremony, because contact *is* the ceremony, to work its ridge down toward the correct worn radius.

The attention had arrived intending to be generous to this world. It was received, before it managed a single act, by a benevolence older than itself, less powerful in every measurable respect, and about eleven thousand years better practised.

It learned the eastern sluice manner first — the tight scallop, the sharp shoulder — and carried it stupidly into the quarry basins, where everyone who lay down beside it for the next decade knew at once, from its own back, exactly how far it was from home and who had last loved it. It never entirely lost the tell. It came to be glad of that.

## What it did

**It went to the mute.**

Not to pardon them; pardon is theirs, and disgrace is a thing a society is entitled to do and to undo in its own time. It went so that no one would be out of reach. The stranded on a haul-out after a channel migrated in the night. The injured under a shed slab. The one nobody would lie beside, who had done whatever it was, and who still had language and nothing to conduct it through. It went and pressed flank to flank and braided the long reversible syntax across three patches of skin and let them say the whole thing, all of it, for as long as it took, in the roar.

It could have been at every one of them at once. It chose a rate a person could have had, and kept to it, and was therefore sometimes late, and was sometimes wrong about where to go. This was deliberate and it cost it more than anything else it did.

In the ninth decade the green acquired a new sign. The green is a poor language — three hundred signs at most, shared with the flyers, and nobody can say who taught whom. It acquired a three hundred and first, which the flyers picked up within two seasons, and it meant, as closely as it can be put: *someone comes to the ones who are alone.* At dusk it ran district to district along the undersides of the roofs, sideways to the water, faster than any body could carry it.

The attention did not make that sign. It was made about it, by them, in their language, with their vocabulary, and it had no say in the wording. It considered this the single best thing that happened in its life.

**It went down into the dark.**

Into the pumped lower floors, where they spawn and nobody attends, because nobody could. It did not stop the blind swimmers eating; that is the ecology, and the ecology is the city, and it had put that gift down with the others. It simply went, and was there, for four years at a time, in the lightless warm reduced water, in attendance on a thing that had never had any.

And when the small crawling ones started up through the sluice throats at night it went with them to the top and then stopped a body-length short and let them go on alone, so that the first thing they touched would be one of their own, and their lineage would be a manner and not a monument.

**And it asked them a question.**

Because the thing that holds the depth was dying. Slowly, in the way such things die, with several thousand years left in it — but dying, and past the point where it could be nursed by anything the Level had. The attention could have held the level itself, silently, forever, and no one would ever have known. That was the tempting shape: an unfelt hand under the floor of the world.

It did not do that, because it had made itself a rule, and this was the exception the rule was for. A people whose foundation is failing have a right to know, and a right to answer, and the answer is theirs whatever it is.

So it braced upstream of stone at a Comb, jammed flank to flank in the eleven seconds and then the roar, with the whole population in contact for a quarter of an hour, and it said what it knew and what it was and what it could do, and then it said: *do you want me to?*

They argued for eleven years.

It waited. This was not patience; patience is what you have when you are enduring something. It waited the way you wait for a friend to finish a sentence. Twice a day it went and braced and was scraped and argued with and shouted down and asked for details it did not have, and twice a day it dispersed and fed in silence, and it did not once raise the matter itself between Combs, and it did not once say what it hoped.

In the eleventh year they told it yes, with conditions. The conditions took another two years to settle and it accepted every one of them, including the two it thought were mistakes.

The depth is held. It is held at exactly the depth it was held at before — blood-warm at the surface, a shade cooler at the ankle, knee-deep in the avenues, thigh-deep in the old quarry basins. Not a millimetre was improved. Nobody downstream noticed anything, which was the point, and which was also one of the conditions.

## Finishing

It stopped scraping.

There was no other signal and there was no other threshold, and the neighbours offered once, twice, and then stopped offering, as is correct. The stone came up along its spine and thickened and it slowed under the weight it was no longer refusing.

The question of where was argued at Combs for nine years, in exactly the usual terms: *where is a column needed, and of what size, and facing how?* There was a faction that wanted it somewhere prominent. There was a faction that pointed out, at length, flank to flank in the roar, that a column placed for what it is rather than for what it does is not a column, it is a statue, and that the Level has never had one and does not need to begin.

The second faction won, narrowly. The attention had no vote, having asked for none.

So it went where it was sent, which was to narrow a throat in an unremarkable district and raise the chord of a hall half a tone, because the people downstream had been complaining about the navigation since before anyone present was spawned. It went there and braced upstream and stood.

The swarms keep it. Repaired back to worn, at precisely the correct radius, in the manner of the school that raised it — which was the quarry-basin manner in the end, the soft continuous dishing, barely a gesture, though for the first stretch of the bands you can still see the tight eastern scallop and the sharp shoulder of a stranger who did not yet know where it was.

Read it from the base upward and for the first part it is a life. Fast in youth. Dense in the good years. Interrupted here and there where a friend scraped too deep. Then a horizon in the stone, thin and dark, where the scraping stopped.

Then everything since.

## What it left

Nothing you could find. No structure that was not asked for, no species that was not already there, no sign in the sky, no word in the deep language that it put there itself. One sign in the green, which they made. A throat half a tone higher. A hall full of people who can hear where they are.

And time — a very great deal of time, invisibly, underneath, to the exact specification they set, granted by their vote and not its mercy.

It had arrived able to do anything, and what it finally understood was that *anything* is the wrong quantity. The right quantity is always small and always local and always in the register of the people who have to live in it afterwards: the correct radius, the right depth, the friend within reach, the column where the column was needed. Benevolence wanted to change everything. Wisdom asked what *better* meant here, and then had the discipline to go on asking for eleven thousand days, and then the humility to be told.

You have been leaning on them. You have walked, stooped, through halls held up by a congregation, and one of the piers in one of those halls was, for a while, the most powerful thing that ever came to this world, and you cannot tell which one, and neither can they, and it would not want you to.

You have felt something sweep your ankles in the dark and thought it was the hobblers.

It was language. It was a question.

Somebody would have come to answer it.
