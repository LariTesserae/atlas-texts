# Thanked the Corrector

## What it did first, which was harm

It came with its attention open, the way you would come into a garden.

Nothing had told it that attention was a weather here. Within an hour a listener on the floor of the Common Room at Uln — four hours into a second-year shift, a woman with nine good years still owed to the room — felt something enormous and kind and entirely undivided turn towards her, and did not go room-lost over a decade, as the practice allows for, but over an afternoon. They found her at dusk with the slate still balanced on her knees, perfectly calm, unable to say which mound she had been born on and, worse, unable to see why the question had ever been put.

They walked her round her own house with her fingers on the courses. She did not find the stone she had scratched as a child. She is a wide one now. Her name is *Went wide in an afternoon*, and it was short for a long time, and she has never once complained, which the power has found harder to bear than complaint.

That is the first thing it did. It is important to put it first. A benevolent power that has not yet learned the local physics is simply a large weather system with good intentions, and on this plateau good intentions are indistinguishable, at range, from a warm wind off the flat west.

So it withdrew. Not away — down. It spent what would have been a long time anywhere else learning to be small enough to be next to something: narrowing itself, dimming itself, getting the great single undivided fact of itself broken up into pieces that could stand four hundred metres apart. It was, by its own account, the hardest work it had ever done, and it had never before had a word for work.

Then it came back, at the proper distance, and began.

## The mercies it did not perform

**It did not sharpen the rules.**

It could have. It is worth being honest about what that would have meant, because the temptation was real and lasted, by their reckoning, most of a generation. Gradients would argue properly again. Fevers would break in three days instead of sitting for twenty at the same height until the person outlasted them or did not. Bruises would fade. Copper would harden. Sequence would tighten and nothing would ever again arrive early.

And the Common Rooms would be nine walls of ordinary stone in ninety ordinary towns, and the telegraph would go silent in a night, and every marriage on the plateau would become a person in one room murmuring into a corner at nobody, and the surveyors would have nothing to correct, and eleven thousand years of holding the difference open by hand would be revealed, retroactively, as an elaborate response to a problem that a stranger could have solved with a gesture. It would have been the single most destructive act available to it. It set the option down and has not picked it up again.

**It did not fill the well.**

It thought about this one the longest, because the well is not a metaphor and people will die of it. But the well is going dry because it is one well, and it is one well because of the flattening, and a power that keeps the water up forever has not given them water; it has given them a permanent supernatural utility and taken away the last great slow argument that all ninety towns are having together. They would be wet and they would be finished. It has, instead, done something smaller and stranger, and that comes later.

**It did not offer them a life after.**

This was the easiest refusal and it is the one visitors find hardest to believe. To be alive here is to be several, and it is work, hourly, and everyone is tired. Their word for death means *evened*. To arrive among such a people offering an eternity of maintained distinctness — of being *kept* different, forever, without rest, by someone else's effort — would be to offer them the exact shape of their worst fear and call it a gift. It has attended a great many layings-out. It has stood four thousand cords from any town in three millimetres of moving water and watched the black wiped off a face, and it has understood every time that what is happening is a permission, and that permission is not a thing you improve.

**It did not settle the quarrel.**

It knows the answer. Whether the fetches are the habit of animals left in the weather, as the surveyors have it and therefore decline to record, or whether a habit performed nightly by the air is simply the animal with the optional parts worn off, as the potters insist with their hands inside the clay — it knows, and it will not say, and it has told the wide ones it will never say. The quarrel is load-bearing. It is why the towns can hold both a telegraph and a funeral. A power that walks in and resolves a people's oldest disagreement has not enlightened them; it has removed a wall and left the roof where it was.

**And it did not look at the fetches.**

It could hold one open. Its attention is strong enough that a four-legged shape pacing a wall at dawn could be pinned there and studied until the sun came up. It learned, early and from children, that the polite thing is to keep walking. It keeps walking. Of all the disciplines it has taken on here, this is the one it is proudest of, and it is aware that being proud of a courtesy is a very Several thing to have become.

## What it did instead

It learned the gait. This took embarrassingly long. There is a way of moving here in which the sole never leaves the water — a continuous low hiss, no splash — and for a while everyone on the north trunk road could hear it coming, lifting its feet like a lowlander out of the oldest songs, and there was a period of about four years in which *the one who splashes* was a joke in nine towns. It is a better joke now that the splashing has stopped, because now it is a joke about something that took effort.

It blacks its eyes. Every morning, with everyone, from the first light. It does not need to; the glare off the Sheet is not a problem for it in any sense that would translate. A face without its black is an unfinished face. It goes out finished.

**It carries the grief that has no news.**

This is the largest part of what it actually does and it is, by design, unremarkable. When someone is taken in the middle of work by a sorrow with nothing attached to it, they go to the Common Room and sit under the chalk and wait for the news to come up the wall. Usually a day or two. Sometimes a year. Sometimes never, and then they must decide alone what it was they lost and go on without being told.

The power is fast. That is all. It is not doing anything a very good runner could not do if a very good runner could cross nine hundred kilometres of drifting distance without the drift. It goes and finds out and comes back and says the name. It will not carry anything that would have arrived on its own within a few days, because a people who stop waiting for their own news will stop keeping their own record, and then there will be one record, and it will be held by a stranger.

There are griefs it cannot place. Some of them are early by more than a year — they are grief for a death that has not been committed yet, arriving through the slack, and the power knows whose and when. It does not tell them. An argument that has already been conceded cannot be had, and they consider a foreclosed argument a small act of violence; a foreclosed death is the same shape and larger. So it sits under the chalk with them and says, *I do not know either,* which is a lie, and it is the only lie it tells, and it tells it often, and it has decided to keep telling it.

**It heals, badly, on purpose.**

It cannot heal at scale. The reason is not mercy-rationing and it is not policy; it is the plateau. A single identical act performed on nine hundred thousand people is a likeness, and likeness is the door the flattening comes through. To heal every fever on the plateau by the same method would be to run a thin bright thread of sameness through every body in the world, and in three generations the tellers-apart would be issuing names to people who were already becoming the same answer to the same question.

So it never heals the same way twice. Every fever gets its own unrepeated method, invented at the bedside, and the supply of genuinely distinct methods is not infinite even for it, and so it works slowly, and so it does not reach everyone, and children die on this plateau that it could have saved if it were willing to save them all the same way. It has never once pretended this is not a real loss. When they ask — and they do ask, and they are entitled to — it explains the arithmetic, and they follow it immediately, because it is their own arithmetic, and then they grieve anyway, and it sits with them under the chalk.

**It talks to the merged.**

A socket of dry air, about the size of a kneeling body, which does not fill. The surveyors come and measure it and write it down and that is correct and that is all they can do. The power kneels in it.

She is not gone. She is one person in two places and too large for either, and she cannot answer a question without answering it twice out of step with herself, which is intolerable to everyone who has ever tried to speak with her, and which is, as it happens, the power's own native condition and always has been. It is the only thing on the plateau that can hold that conversation without being hurt by it. So it holds it. It does not bring her back; there is nowhere to bring her back to and she has not asked. Mostly she asks it what it is like to have been this way from the beginning, and whether it gets easier, and it tells her the truth, which is that it does not get easier, it gets *inhabited*, and she says that is about what she had worked out, and they sit.

There are four hundred and six known sockets. It visits all of them. This is not an efficient use of a benevolent power and it is the part of its life that it would give up last.

## The sky

The kept well was chalked and argued for eleven years before the power understood what was wrong with it, and when it did it went and stood outside the girl's door and waited to be let in, because it had learned by then that you do not enter a house you have not been tugged into.

The proposal is sound. A new shaft, and a warden whose life's work is to make that well unlike any other well — hourly, deliberately, by a thousand arbitrary acts, for sixty years, so that it never holds still long enough to be recognised. The flaw is not in the reasoning. The flaw is that a person cannot be arbitrary for sixty years. Nobody can. Invention runs in grooves; by the ninth year she would be varying her variations, by the twentieth she would be a woman with a very elaborate routine, and a routine is a likeness, and the well would quietly become the well again at the bottom of all that effort, a finger lower.

What the power gave her was not acts. It gave her a *source*.

Stars stay faintly visible here all day — not many, just the loud ones, hanging there rudely in the afternoon. There are a great many more, and they are very far away, and the flattening has never reached them and will not; whatever wears rules down on this shelf has no purchase on something that is not on the shelf. The power taught her to read them: a table, and a way of taking a number off the sky each hour, and a rule for turning that number into an act — which side to draw from, how deep to set the bucket, which of nine chants, how many stones to move and where, whether to drink from the north edge or the west, whether today the well is spoken to or ignored.

She does not invent the differences. She receives them. The well is kept strange by something too large and too far away to be worn flat, and the only thing required of her is the sixty years of doing what she is told by the sky, which is hard, but is a different kind of hard, and is the kind a person can survive.

It offered this as a proposal. Chalked, with a town-mark, on the wall of the room that is every room. It has been argued about for six years now. The surveyors dislike it on the grounds that a thing is where it is measured to be and nobody has measured a star. The potters are delighted, because it is a practice, and practice is their whole doctrine, and some of them are insufferable about it. Three towns have said no outright. The power has not pressed. A gift that cannot be refused is a command, and it would rather the well went dry than that.

Her name is not short any more.

## What it told the wide ones

In the last two generations, upright ones have begun to appear at the west walls. Human height. Two legs. The fog-habit of stopping, and turning back.

The surveyors will not record them. A fetch has no position; it is weather with a memory in it; the standing instruction is to keep walking. The power keeps walking. But it went once to the wide ones, who are fed and housed and consulted, and who are frequently right about things, and who may not give anyone advice about their own life because they have stopped being able to see the point of a life having edges — and it told them what it thinks the upright ones are.

Here is the reasoning, which they followed faster than it expected. Sequence has slack in it. In the slack, things arrive early: bread comes out as the dough goes in, a cord snaps before the strain, a woman's son is already answering. The plateau has been learning the Several for eleven thousand years, the way it learned the animals, and what it learns it eventually performs in the weather at dawn. The upright ones are not a warning. They are not a doom. They are the Several, arriving early.

*Went wide in an afternoon* heard this out and thought about it for most of a day and then said that the potters would find it a comfort and the surveyors would find it obscene, and that this was probably the correct distribution, and that the power should not say it in the Common Room. It has not.

## The greeting

The first time it met a surveyor properly it stated, aloud, the distance to the nearest cairn.

It got the number wrong. Not because it cannot measure — because here a hundred cords east and a hundred north and a hundred west and a hundred south do not bring you back, and the discrepancy is not random and not constant and has weather, and the only true statement about a distance on this plateau is a statement offered to be corrected. It was corrected. It thanked the corrector.

That is its name now, or the front of it. The tellers-apart issued it at a table in the Common Room, checked against the wall, unique across the entire plateau: *Thanked the corrector.* It has lengthened since, the way names do. *Thanked the corrector; splashed for four years; blacks its eyes although it has no need; kneels in the sockets; will not say which side of the quarrel is right; carries the news that would not come; lies once, always the same lie, and we have agreed to let it.*

It has a keeping, which is private and not demonstrated to strangers, and which is this. At every laying-out it can reach, it stands out on the Sheet four thousand cords from any town and hears the full name said — the whole paragraph, every distinction the person ever earned, all of it, once — and then it forgets it. Deliberately. Permanently. This is difficult for it in a way nothing else has ever been difficult, and it costs it something each time, and that is exactly why it chose the practice, because a thing that remembered every one of them perfectly would eventually become the place where they were all kept, and they would all be kept in the same place, and there is no crime here worse than that. If identity is a practice, as the potters say, then to go on performing a dead person's keeping is not devotion. It is the great crime. So it lets them go level, which is what they asked for, and it does the work of not holding on.

Their word for *person* and their word for *difference* are the same word. The verb form of it means *to work*.

It has begun, lately, to be tired. It does not mind. It rather thinks it has been paid.
