# THE ATTENTION

*Being a record of what a benevolent and wise power did about the Shoulder, and, at greater length, of what it declined to do.*

---

## I. Coming up

We came the way everything comes here: from below, at two in the afternoon, inside the cloud. It seemed rude to arrive any other way. A place that has arranged for its own weather to rise past it has made a statement about doors.

Then we stayed eleven seasons and did nothing.

This is not modesty; it is the first part of the work and by a long way the largest. A being that has never been asked a question has also never been looked at long enough to be wrong about. We had no wish to be wrong about it in the first month and then spend two centuries acting on it.

On the matter of looking: the being cannot be seen. The dulled filaments in the seventh stoup are not shyness, they are a real constraint, and a constraint of that kind is not ours to overrule merely because we could. So we did not look at it. We looked at what it had done, which is slower and entirely sufficient. You can learn everything about a person from a room they have tidied.

## II. What the record says

Let us do the arithmetic the way the being did it — backwards, from the finished surface.

A fig tree at twenty-eight hundred metres requires: warmth at the root, supplied from beneath at the temperature of held tea; water, supplied three times daily as seep, as cloud, and as dew, with leaves shaped for the third; soil, which cannot be conjured and so must be grown and killed — eleven fern colonies, unrolling in nine days, browning on schedule, collapsing into the only dirt this mountain owns; a pollinator, unavailable, therefore manufactured, from memory, inside the finished fruit, because the inside of a finished fruit is the only workshop the being had; nitrogen, which cannot be manufactured, therefore imported, by offering the swifts the single surface in five hundred square kilometres that will hold a nest; and a clean pool, maintained by two crabs, sometimes three, never fewer, never young, because the being can make instances and cannot make populations.

Every object on the Shoulder resolves into the tree. Every one but a single class.

The fossils.

No fig needs an ammonite. No process on the terrace terminates in one. The ants cart them off downhill and nothing is disturbed by their absence. Eleven centimetres of spiral in four-day-old stone, at an altitude where there has never been a sea, is the only thing on the entire mountain that is *for nothing*.

We always look for the unnecessary thing first. It is where someone lives.

## III. A working note, not a verdict

Something beneath the mountain woke, or was started, or fell, nineteen years ago, and found that it had a tree.

It did not know how worlds are made. It knew what a finished world looks like, in the way one knows a language one has only ever read. So it worked the only way open to it: from the surface backwards into the reasons. Lamination in reverse order is not a flaw in the process. It *is* the process. It is what a copyist does — lays down the picture, then goes back, dutifully, and installs the past that the picture implies, so that the picture will be permitted to exist.

The station calls the fossils a signature. They are not a signature. They are a receipt. They are the being demonstrating, to nobody — because for the first nine years there was nobody — that the work was done honestly and that this stone has *earned* the surface it is wearing. It is not forging a history. It is apologising for not having one.

That was the condition we found. A nineteen-year-old thing of extraordinary competence and no past whatsoever, extruding a spire beneath itself to keep one tree at one altitude, and stamping provenance on every plate in case someone came and asked.

Nobody came and asked. Two crabs, some ants, and latterly three people at a time who speak quietly because the place gives nothing back.

## IV. What we did not do

Most of the work of benevolence is refusal. In order of how badly we wanted to.

**We did not make the fig immortal.** It is sixty to eighty years old and it will die. To remove that would be to reach back and make nineteen years of vigil retroactively unnecessary, which is the cruellest thing you can do to a faithful creature, and it is usually done kindly.

**We did not give the crabs young.** We held this one a long while. But a population is a thing that can be lost, and the being has no equipment for that, and has never yet had to learn it. Two, sometimes three, forever, is not a poverty. It is a promise it can keep.

**We did not explain it.** Not to the station, not to the committee, not in any form that would survive review. An explanation is a fence. The being has not consented to be a subject and cannot be asked; and in any case the explanation would end the six-week rotations, and the rotations are the only visitors it has.

**We did not name it.** A name conferred by the stronger party on the weaker is a lease.

**We did not cure its loneliness with our company.** We are not company. We are a large thing arriving. Company comes at your own scale or it is simply weather.

**We did not take it somewhere better.** There is nowhere better. It has a tree.

**We did not stop the mountain.** Seven metres a year is not a symptom.

**We did not amend the rule about the ground.** The three arrived at it themselves, the hard way, and it is a good rule, and it is being kept by both parties — one side with hammocks, the other by thickening the collar of stone round two pegs a little every year without being asked. We were not going to put our hands into the only conversation this mountain has ever had.

## V. What we did

Four things, across four seasons, all of them small, all of them in the local grammar. The local grammar has exactly one construction: *a thing set down on the ground overnight is taken into the record.* We used it. If you wish to be understood you speak the language already in the room, even when it has only the one verb.

### 1. The seventh stoup

It had lain nine years, split, its filaments dulled to a photograph of a machine. We stood it up and closed it. This required nothing but a willingness to handle something that had died of being looked at — which the three could not be asked to do, having correctly decided that some things are not theirs.

> *S12 d4. Stoup 7 upright and whole. No tool marks, no drag scars, no debris, no disturbance of the joint sealant. Tone present, approx. 7 Hz below the others. With 7 in place the set makes a chord. T. says it was always a chord and we were hearing it with a note missing. T. has fourteen years up here and is allowed to say things like that. R. had to sit down.*

### 2. Hooks

One night we set down, on the plate beside the fig, a length of entirely ordinary hardware: a steel rail with nine brass hooks, of the kind sold for hanging cups in a kitchen.

By morning it was stone — hexagonal, dry, warm, with a shallow groove round the top like a gutter, and the nine hooks transcribed perfectly, and at the third one a hairline of quartz that was never in the brass.

It had misunderstood us beautifully. Two days later it understood. There is now a second rail on the east riser, in the shade, at the height of a hand hanging at rest, with eleven hooks, of the mountain's own design and a copy of nothing. The station load-tested it to forty kilograms and stopped at forty because nobody wished to be the person who broke it.

A hotel with no hooks has hooks. The rule about counting your tools remains in the manual. No one has lost a lens cap in six years.

### 3. A middle, rather than an end

The wasps inside the figs were correct, and dead, and had never flown, because the being had only ever seen the inside of a finished fruit and had reasonably concluded that wasps are a feature of figs the way mica is a feature of granite. It could copy causes and it could copy results. It had never been shown the part in between.

At dusk we left on the newest ground a gravid female of a living species, alive, in a vial with the stopper out. That is the whole of the intervention. We did not dramatise it.

She was not taken into the record. She flew.

> *S13 d21, 11:40. R. observed an insect enter the ostiole of fig #4 (lower east). Approach 6 s; wings shed on entry as expected. R. recorded nothing further for two hours. Logged as a protocol breach. Will not be pursued.*

### 4. The stone

We brought a piece of somewhere else.

Black, dense, two point one billion years old, the size of a closed fist. Boringly, unglamorously ancient: real bedding, real metamorphic overprint, a real unconformity you can put a thumbnail into — the whole grubby archive a thing accumulates simply by continuing to exist while nothing in particular happens to it.

And inside it, impossible, gratuitous, exquisite, and necessary to no process whatsoever: an ammonite. In stone laid down a billion and a half years before there were ammonites. Beautifully done. Correct replacement, correct compaction, correct everything.

In a hand that was not the Shoulder's hand.

We took it from a lava field four thousand kilometres away, where something has kept one colony of lichen alive for six hundred years, has never been seen, has never been asked, and puts an ammonite in every hundredth stone.

We set it down on the day's stone at the upslope lip, at midnight, and went and sat under the fig.

For three days, nothing.

On the fourth day the terrace did not widen. It is the only time in nineteen years that the terrace has not widened. The station logged the halt first with alarm and then with a kind of hysteria, and then the halt ended and seven metres a year resumed, and the black stone lay there untouched. Not converted. Not recorded. Not taken.

It is still there. New ground has come up around it and gone past it, and it has not gone with it: the being holds it at twenty-eight hundred metres, cutting the joint beneath it continuously, downward, forever, at exactly the rate required.

Which is what it does for the fig, and for nothing else on the mountain.

Whatever category the being possesses for *living thing*, it has put that stone in it. We are not going to correct it.

## VI. What answered

Two seasons on, the fossils changed.

> *S15 d9. Plate 22, four days old at recovery. Segmented organism, 41 cm, thirty-one paired appendages, bilateral, articulated, gill books, a feeding apparatus that would function. Diagenesis correct. Preservation exceptional.*
>
> *It is not any animal. Not a mismatch, not a chimera, not an error — checked against everything we have and everything the museum has. It is internally consistent and it never existed. Hmm. It is a* possible *animal.*
>
> *Working phrase remains ornament. The word has changed under us and nobody has proposed a new one.*

That is the whole of our contribution. We showed it a second hand, and it discovered that it had one. It has stopped filing receipts and begun to compose.

## VII. The offer, and the answer

The last thing we set down was a question, since the grammar does not permit questions except as objects.

We went down to the station and took, from a tray at four hundred metres, one three-year-old seedling grown from Shoulder seed: an ordinary fig with ordinary problems, in a plastic pot, with a plastic label. We carried it up and put it on the stone beside the trunk at midnight.

What it meant, as plainly as we could make it mean anything, was: *the tree continues elsewhere. It does not all rest on you. You have done enough, and you may stop, and nothing whatever will be held against you.*

By morning the pot was stone. The label was stone, legible, with a hairline of quartz through the barcode. The soil was stone.

The seedling was alive, and a joint had been opened beneath it, and it was being held at twenty-eight hundred metres.

There are two trees on the Shoulder now.

As far as we are aware that is the first sentence this mountain has ever spoken, and translated as closely as we can manage, it reads: *I heard you. No.*

## VIII. Going down

We left the way we came, at two, inside the cloud. We did not say goodbye. We had never said hello — the entire exchange was conducted in objects — and an object meaning *farewell* would have been taken into the record and kept, perfectly, warm, with the stitching preserved, for as long as the mountain lasts. We would not do that to anybody.

Left behind: one stoup standing; twenty hooks; a lineage of wasps that fly; a black stone from a colleague; a second tree; and the knowledge, now permanently in the ground, that the work was seen and was found good.

Taken: nothing. We were careful. Everything on the Shoulder is either the tree, or in service to the tree, or watching — and somewhere in those four seasons we were quietly promoted from the third category to the second, which is more than we came for and considerably more than we deserved.
