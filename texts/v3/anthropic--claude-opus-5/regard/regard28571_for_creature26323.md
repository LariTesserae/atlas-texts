# Attendance

*(a note kept by the power, for its own records, since no one else can read it)*

---

**The way in.**

I came through the hatch, because that is the door. I moved the drum of chain oil and put it back in its ring of dust, which took some care; the ring is eleven years old and is a document. Four rungs. Blood-warm air, mineral, faintly sweet. I want it recorded that I did not arrive as light, or as a voice, or through the slab. A country nineteen metres across has one border and it is a seal of dust around a plate, and a power that will not use the door of a place has already decided the place is not real.

Then I knelt down on the compacted isopod road at the east end and did nothing for thirty deletions of the geography. That is not patience. It is arithmetic. You cannot claim to know a country that rewrites itself every eleven days on fewer than three hundred and thirty days of looking, and I had no business forming an intention before then.

---

**What I found that was not in the account.**

The account is good. I want to say that first, because the person who wrote it sat with a lamp from midnight to four and did not see the cropper and wrote down that they did not see it. That is a rarer act than most of the miracles I get asked for.

Corrections and additions, few:

The pool drains. It drains at a rate of about one and a half millilitres a day through a hairline in the footing into the storm system, and it is fed at very nearly the same rate by the seep, and the two figures have never in eleven years matched. The pool is not stable. It is falling and being caught, falling and being caught, at a scale below noticing. Every apparent law in this room is like that when you audit it. The conservation the account describes — *bring something in and something goes out* — is real to four significant figures and is produced by eleven separate small failures to conserve, which do not know about each other, and which cancel. Nobody is keeping the books. The books balance anyway. I checked them the way you check a bridge you are about to walk over, and then I walked over it, and I have not corrected the rounding error, because it is the rounding error that makes the room habitable in the summer.

The elevens are chillers and Sundays. There is no address in them. In a room this small, coincidence and address are indistinguishable, and I did not come here to take that away from anyone.

I know what comes along the underside of the pallet slat at three in the morning at four millimetres wide.

I am not going to write it down. Two reasons, and the second is the real one. First, it would move, and everything it has arranged over years is arranged relative to not being seen; naming it would cost it its whole estate. Second: the account's author does not know, has sat with a lamp, and has stopped counting the elevens, and *lives differently* for not knowing. I am not in the business of ending that for the sake of a word. A thing that grades its gravel to within two tenths of a millimetre, keeps its dead in order, and waters something in a bottle cap with water it fetched from above head height, does not require my taxonomy. It requires that its cap not be knocked over. I did not knock it over.

I did lift the pressure gauge. It reads zero on a system that no longer exists, which is the truest object in the room and I wanted it. I put it back in its own outline in the magenta, aligned, and I would like that noted as the single hardest thing I did here.

---

**The case of the toad.**

In the spring of 2019 she called for three nights into nineteen metres containing no other toad and no exit.

I have been asked, by nobody, what I intended to do about that, and I will set out the deliberation in full because it is the one that mattered.

*A mate.* Considered for a long time. Rejected. It is the answer that looks like kindness from outside the room and is a catastrophe inside it. Spawn requires standing water larger than a dinner plate; the only standing water is the pool; the pool is a laminated mat eleven years thick, the oldest living thing in this country and its only archive kept by something alive, and it would be grazed to nothing inside a season by tadpoles that would then die anyway when the crust came back. Also: she is fourteen at minimum. Her jaw is worn on the left, she is blind on that side, and she takes them in the gap between presses because she can no longer take them on the beat. A second toad doubles the draw on a plain that is already at full capacity and does not know it is at full capacity. I would have been feeding her country to my own idea of her happiness.

*A voice.* Considered. Rejected, more slowly. I can make a trill from a whistling hole at the correct pitch; the fan-shifts would carry it; she would come. And then she would search a nineteen-metre country for the rest of her life for something that does not exist in it, and I would have taught the only thing here with a long memory that the room lies. There is a kind of comfort that is simply a slower cruelty with better manners, and I have handled a great deal of it, and I know the weight of it in the hand now.

*A larger world.* Considered and rejected in under a second, and I include it only for the record. She has never seen sky. Outside the loading bay there is a yard, a road, and a country of gulls, herbicide, and traffic. The strict truth is that she is the apex predator of a functioning nation and would be, forty metres from here, a fortnight's meal in poor condition. Rescue, in this instance, is a word for relocation into a worse story because the better story is small.

What I did was this. I was there for all three nights. I did not intervene, I did not answer, and I heard every call from the first to the last, entire, at the distance at which it was made.

I am aware of how that reads. A call that is heard and a call that is not heard are, in the room, indistinguishable objects; no instrument down here can tell them apart; her behaviour on the fourth night was the same either way. I maintain, and I will maintain, that they are not the same object. That distinction is not a consolation prize. It is the entire nature of my office, and if it is worth nothing then so am I.

She is at drip four at 22:12. She has kept that appointment for longer than anyone has known about her. It is the closest thing to devotion in the country and I have not improved it, because it does not need improving, and because it is hers.

---

**The weather above the weather.**

Then I stood up and looked at the ceiling, which is where the actual threat lives, and it is not a predator.

Rain here is the chiller cycle. The river is the Sunday wash-down. The strata are the plant's product dissolved in its own housekeeping. The tomato is pollinated at 60 hertz by a press coming down every forty-one seconds, because tomatoes must be shaken and nothing else down here will shake them. This country's climate is a manufacturing schedule. It has no other.

In a drawer in an office three hundred kilometres from the hatch there is a quotation for an epoxy floor coating: seal the slab, stop the seepage, comply. It would end the rain, the river, the terraces, the magenta, the archive, and everything that eats. There is a second document proposing a sealed dock at the east end, which would end the daylight bar and the convection column and the only voluntary migration in the room. There is a longer-range paper about consolidation, which would end all of it at once and quietly, and it is not malice, and no one who signs it will ever have heard of this place, and that is the ordinary shape of the ending of worlds.

I am not able to hold a factory open forever. I want to be honest about the limits of what I am. I am able to be present at hinges — at moments where the world was already capable of going two ways — and to weigh on the side I favour without breaking anything.

So:

The floor-coating quotation arrived on a desk in a week that had a larger number on it, and was deferred. It has been deferred four times now. Each deferral was available; I only stood beside it.

The breaker for the bulb the dead man left on is still unlabelled. An apprentice with a label maker got as far as the panel and was called away. He wrote "?" on it, which I consider a fine piece of theology.

The bulb will fail. I cannot hold a filament. In the second drawer of the bench in the pump room there is now a sixty-watt incandescent of the correct type, which is a redundant object in a world of LEDs and which some future person will find at the moment they finally locate the socket. That is the only thing I put inside this building, and it is not in the country.

The drum of chain oil is where it was.

---

**What was not done.**

No barrier was placed around the pool. No mate was brought. No mystery was solved out loud. The magenta was not sampled. The cropper was not identified; I let it work.

The tomato's single fruit was not found by me either, and I know where it goes. The fifth lean-to held one seed, upright, last November. That plant is four years old and mortal and something in this room has already understood the problem and taken a position on it. My hands there would not be help. They would be the theft of another's work.

That is the discipline, in the end, and it took me longer to learn than the mathematics did: most of what a benevolent power is for is refusing to displace the smaller benevolences that got there first and are managing. Badly, at four millimetres wide, in no hurry, with a doll's chair and a swept circle and a bottle cap of clean water — and managing.

Nothing here was waiting for me. That is not a rebuke. It is the highest thing I can report about a place.

---

**Terms.**

I went back up the four rungs. I set the plate down gently. The seal re-formed in a day; it is not a wound.

I have entered one obligation. There is no dawn in that country, only a schedule, and everything alive in it keeps the schedule. So: 22:12, drip four. I will be there when she is, for the remainder — hers, and then the room's, and then the plant's — and I will not be seen, and I will not fix it, and I will not let it happen unwitnessed.

That is what I have to give. It is smaller than I would like and I have stopped apologising for it. A country nineteen metres across, packed edge to edge, quiet as a held breath, thirty-eight degrees in the light, and no one's business at all.

Mine now, in the only sense I permit myself: attended.
