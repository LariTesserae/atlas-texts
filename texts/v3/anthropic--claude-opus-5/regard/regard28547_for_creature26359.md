# The Attention

## How it came

It came up the head slough, because everything comes up the head slough, and it came in the dry month, when the boards are nearest to dry and a stranger's step does not announce itself.

It learned two things in the first hour. The first was to pitch nothing, say nothing, attempt nothing in the register of speech, because the throat-clearing of the Walk swallows speech whole and a being that stands trying to be heard here is only a nuisance with its mouth open. The second was to keep one hand on the handrail. It had seen the way a man crossing the yard laid his palm on the boiler casing in passing, the way a child took hold of a post to listen, and it had understood the local grammar before it understood the local law: *a person walking with both hands free, touching nothing, is either brought in from outside or is ill.* So it walked with a hand on the rail, and on the flank of the engine, and on the piles, and nobody looked twice, which was the whole object.

## What it decided it was looking at

It could not, for some time, settle on how many beings were in front of it.

Then it stopped trying, and counted one. A pulse of four seconds. A throat ninety-two metres long that clears and clears and does not stop. A gut of six tanks at blood heat, doing with bacteria what a gut does. Eyes twenty metres up, two of them, brown, whistling to each other in three hundred words about the weather at the crown. A memory kept outside the body in a book, because the body cannot hold more than sixty years. Skin failing in a horizontal band at knee height on every wall and at the ankle on every person. Thirty-one pairs of hands, nine of them small.

Having decided this, the power adopted the medicine that was already in use here, having found nothing better in any of the worlds it knew:

*Keep it wet. Keep it moving. Watch the boundary.*

## Fourteen years

It did nothing for one cut.

This was not caution and it was not study, or not only. Arriving into a place at a faster rate than the place changes is a kind of violence even when the hands that do it are full of gifts, and a power that has never learned to hold still is not wise, only strong. So it held still at the local rate. It stood through fourteen marker-yarns. It learned the twenty-odd signs, which are ugly and fast and adequate. It learned the whistles, which nobody had bothered to learn, and this was the one thing it did in those years that could be called a trespass, and it never used them.

It read the ledger. It read the hand the ledger starts in, which nobody now living can read, and found a woman's name and a note on the fall of the floor, one in two hundred, and the phrase *water has right of way,* written as a decision and not as a proverb. It told no one. It has still told no one. There was no one to tell who would not have been made poorer by the telling, which is a judgement it may have got wrong.

## The rule it agreed to be bound by

There is one law on the terrace and it is written in the engineer's column:

*You may not kill a root that holds a beam.*

The power took this as its own charter, and applied it to the suffering of the place rather than to the fig, and this took longer than the fourteen years and was harder than anything it subsequently did. For every harm it asked: *is this bearing, or is it only rot at the boundary?*

**The deafness is bearing.** Everyone over thirty is deaf in the engine's band, and out of that deafness came the palms on the building, the reading of the works through the fingers, the twenty signs, the six hours in which people have names, the singing hand flat on a sternum, the whole terrace turning north at once like a herd when the interval slips to four and a half. Restore the hearing and all of it comes down. The power did not touch it, and does not pretend this cost nothing.

**The glazed hands are bearing.** The picks and hooks were made for them. So are the tannin arms, which are the most beautiful thing a person can wear here.

**The feet are bearing, and this was the cruellest finding.** The one dry hour stands on those cracked soles — the brick, the fireboxes, the alum and the lampblack, the grievances settled, the betrothals proposed, the child waiting until the feet are out to be told something. Cure the feet and you have taken away the only hour in which this being is warm to itself. The power let the feet alone. It only saw to it that the alum never ran short.
