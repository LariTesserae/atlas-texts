# IX. The one who is not at home

## I. How it came

It came the way everything comes: across dry country, on foot, in a straight line.

It could have arrived otherwise. That is the whole point of a power. But the bowl has manners, and the manners were legible from a long way off, and the first of them is that you walk here. So it walked, four days over red clay and rotted granite, through a landscape of intervals, and let the hawk's shadow cross it, and did not shorten the distance.

It carried nothing it could set down.

At the lip it stopped, as anyone stops, and felt the surface in the air a hand's width thick, and passed through it, and its breath stopped smoking.

## II. What it read

It looked at the lattice first, because that is what a power looks at — the working part, the thing with intent behind it.

It did not learn who made the mesh. That is worth saying plainly. It learned what the mesh was doing, which anyone can learn with a spade and a night: holding thirty-one degrees, combing water out of a sky that has none, and taking away the dead so patiently that four thousand meters up, on a mountain that has stopped being a mountain, there is a garden the size of a threshing floor with twelve species of frog in it.

Then it went to the eastern stone and read the four worn words at chest height, and stood in front of them a while.

*It was already working.*

The power's judgment on that sentence was that it is one of the best sentences ever cut into rock, and that most of what it had come prepared to do had just been refused by a herder with a chisel some centuries ago.

## III. The things it did not do, and why

It considered widening the bowl. Forty paces is a small world. There are frogs here whose entire lives happen inside a circle four meters across, who will never see standing water, whose word for the sea would be a leaf. It could have pushed the warm boundary out a kilometer in a night.

It did not, because the wall is the shape of them. Twelve species exist because the world is exactly this size; they are the pattern that fits this room. You cannot give a leaf-frog the sea. You can only take away its leaf.

It considered raising the ceiling, where the fig crowns are sheared flat along nothing. Then it looked at the four trees and saw a lid that fits, and no cruelty in a fitted lid.

It considered explaining the lattice. It could have. It could have laid the purpose out on the glassy lip in words the herders half-read, and ended a thousand years of unease in an afternoon.

It declined, and it was not certain it was right. But it counted what the not-knowing had already built: a cistern with blunt honest tool marks. A bench facing the young tree, worn into a curve by generations of sitting. Eleven bad clay animals in a line. A sentence of enormous humility cut into stone at chest height. An answer would have got a shrine, and pilgrims, and a fence. The mystery had got a bench.

It considered the taking. This was the hard one. Somewhere on this plateau there are people who slept one night in the Kettle and woke to find the ground less occupied where a coat was — a coat someone had made for them, out of an animal they had known, with a bone button. The lattice does not distinguish. It is indifferent, which is not the same as cruel, but it is not much comfort to be told the difference at dawn with your hands empty.

The power weighed stopping it. The taking is why the loam is black. The loam is why the density is absurd. The density is the frogs and the bats and the moths and the ten thousand tuned cicadas. To spare the button is to thin the loam, and to thin the loam is to begin, very slowly, closing the room.

And the people had already solved it. *Keep it in your hand. Keep using it.* They learned that, and they passed it on, and it works, and it is a good rule for more than coats.

So the power left the lattice entirely alone. Not one strut. It is on record — such records as there are — as the only visitor with the means to improve the Kettle who improved nothing.

## IV. What it asked the tenant, and how

It found the one who is at home asleep in full sun on an open root, on its side, flank rising, in front of anything that cared to look.

The power did not speak to it. Speaking would have been a kind of trespass; also, it had by then understood that the bowl has a grammar and speech is not in it. Instead it lay down on the mesh a body's length away, on its side, in the open, and let its own temperature be met and held until it could not feel where it ended, and it stopped in the middle of a movement and held there eleven or twelve seconds, and then went on.

It had come with a question. The question was: *are you a prisoner?*

Because the numbers say something. Nine swifts a week. A bat every third night. A frog most days. Never a gorge, never a cache, never a second lunge. That is not appetite. That is arithmetic, and something in the bowl is doing arithmetic, and this animal is the only moving part in a machine that otherwise has none. The seat goes empty first and then is filled. Something calls and something answers from a hundred kilometers out, over ground that cannot have produced it.

The power lay there most of a day and got its answer, which was not a word.

The animal opened the too-large eye, wet and forward-facing and unhurried, and looked at the enormous quiet thing lying beside it in the open, and did not move.

In that bowl, not moving is the whole of consent.

It is a job. The tenant knows it is a job. It holds the rate on purpose, the way the cicadas hold the note on purpose, and it is not fed by the swifts, or not only; it is fed by being the thing that keeps the count. The power understood then that it had been asking the wrong question, and that if you take the job away from the one who is at home, you do not free it. You evict it.

So it did not free anything. It found, instead, the one place in the whole arrangement where there was suffering that bought nothing.

## V. The road

Between an empty chamber in the eastern fig and the animal that will fill it, there are a hundred kilometers of dead tableland.

Something crosses that. In a straight line, without casting about, without water, because there is no water to follow — a thin animal, thin as a rope, coat in ruins, crusted at the nose and at the corners of the eye, walking toward a smell it cannot possibly smell yet.

Nobody has ever walked beside it. In all the crossings, in all the seasons, at no point in the entire history of this arrangement has anything living been on that road with it.

That is what the power does. That is the whole of the intervention.

It walks the road. Every time, from the hour the chamber empties to the hour the lip is crossed, four days or nine, whatever it takes, at the animal's pace, one body's length off the left shoulder.

It does not carry it. It considered carrying it and rejected it flatly, because the crossing is the animal's own and it is the only thing the animal will ever have done that was not the job; because a thing carried has not arrived; because the straight line is the tenant's single act of authorship and to shorten it would be to take the last unowned thing off a creature that owns nothing.

What it permits itself is small and could not be found by anyone with a tally-stick:

- Dew, on the last night, where dew would have formed anyway, three hours earlier than it would have.
- At midday, one shadow, the length of one animal, keeping pace.
- Wind at the back for the final day, in a place where the wind moves without obstruction and might have gone either way.
- And the smell of the vine — overripe, almost solid, three flowers — carried out forty kilometers further than air should carry it, so that the straight line has something to be straight toward.

None of it alters a number. The frogs stay twelve. The flowers stay three. The trees stay four. Nine swifts a week. The lattice does not notice, and if it does, it makes no comment, and the two of them are perhaps in the same trade.

## VI. The other end

And it attends the deaths.

This had never been done. It could not have been done. The seat is vacant first, and no body is ever found, because the ground becomes less occupied — a moth in an hour, a bird in a day, feathers last, sinking. Nothing here has ever been sat with. There are no graves in the Kettle because there is nothing to bury, and the consequence, which nobody had thought about, is that every incumbent in several thousand years has ended alone in a dry chamber above the bat-stink with ten thousand cicadas holding one note outside.

So now, when the coat goes dull and the rate begins to slip, something comes up the second buttress of the eastern fig and lies down in the chamber and stays until the chamber is empty, and then goes down and out across the plateau to meet whoever is already walking.

It brings nothing. It changes nothing. It witnesses, which was the only thing the machine could not do for itself. A lattice can keep a promise for ten thousand years and cannot be sorry about anything.

## VII. The one mark

On the way out, the second time, it stopped at the glassy lip where eleven small clay figures stand in a line, badly made, no two alike, each one a four-legged animal, the newest only a few years old, left by somebody nobody has met.

The power looked at those for a long time and understood that it had been beaten here too. Somebody with no power at all had already worked out the whole of the correct response — that you do not fix it, you do not explain it, you come up out of the dry country and you leave a small honest thing that says *I saw, and it mattered to me* — and had been doing it, quietly, for years, in fired clay, because fired clay is mineral and the lattice lets it stay.

So it made one. Out of the loam and the red grit, four legs, and it fired it, and it is genuinely poor work; the hindquarters are wrong and one leg is shorter. It set it at the end of the line.

There are twelve now. They have been counted, and the count is not in dispute, and nobody has ever been able to say which of them is the odd one, or who put it there, and the herders assume it was the same person as always.

That is the entire record of the visit. The rest is the bench, which was already warm; and the sentence on the eastern stone, which was already true, and to which the power added nothing, though it stood in front of it a second time before leaving and thought the missing half and did not carve it:

*It was already working. It was not yet being loved.*
