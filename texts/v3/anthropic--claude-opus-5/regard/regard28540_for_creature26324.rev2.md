# The Reader at the Door

The first thing it understood was the sign.

It is one of the few things left anywhere that can read that language, and the sign had obviously been cast for exactly such a reader: iron letters proud on the plate at the base of the plug, in a tongue nobody inside speaks and nobody inside could hear spoken. The tenders don't need it. They know. The sign was not for them and never had been.

**THE DRUM IS SPEAKING. DO NOT ANSWER.**

It stood at the door for eleven years, which is the shortest time it has ever taken to decide anything, and then went in.

---

# What Took Longest

It had come for a being, and it expected a person, or something that could be addressed as one. It spent the first decades sorting through candidates and discarding them. Not the Ox, which is not alive and is nevertheless the only thing in the place doing anything. Not the nineteen, who are nineteen. Not the tenant of the second band, who is a tenant.

It worked out eventually what any of the tenders could have chalked for it on the rail in four strokes: that the pattern is the animal here. The nine jungles are its body, the dust its posture, the swifts its quick nerve, the frogs its idle noise, the bearing its illness and its clock. The nineteen are its slowest organ — the part that worries, that keeps lead, that trims its own light against a template older than any living hand.

You do not address an organ. This is the whole of what the power learned in its first fifty years, and it was worth fifty years.

---

# What It Set Aside

**The bearing.** It could have healed the bearing in less time than the valve takes to cycle. It declined, and it has re-examined the declining every autumn since, because a decision that good deserves annual suspicion. The rasp at four-forty is the only voice in the Stanchion with anything new to say. It is also the clock, the drift, the needle dragging its hair a year, the reason the nine bands are a record and not a decoration. Mend the bearing and the pattern stops migrating; the scars stop accumulating; the Bite becomes ceremony; three hundred years of *unchanged* becomes a fact about metal instead of a vigil. It would have made the place permanent, which is a way of making it dead without the courtesy of an ending.

**Their hearing.** It thought about this one for a long while, because it looks so much like mercy. The entry fee is not an injury. The four born on the plate were never at any point estranged from where they are. To give them ears would be to hand nineteen people a wound they did not have.

**The date.** It knows roughly when the bearing will go. Two hundred and some years, with a tail on either side. To tell them would end the Bite, which exists in order to ask, four times a year, with the teeth, whether the story is still the same story. A date is not knowledge here. A date is the confiscation of an office.

**The numbers.** It could make the fig feed sixty. Eleven and thirty are not a scarcity; they are the shape of the animal's slowest organ, and the drift between them is the only politics the household has ever needed.

**The tenant.** It knows what is in the second band. It has known since the third year. It will not say, because the not-knowing is not ignorance, it is manners, and the manners are three hundred years old and load-bearing.

**The Minute.** This was the hardest and it is still hard. Sixty seconds a year; teams of three; positions chalked on the floor; a hand arriving at the right height at the right instant or not arriving. It has watched fourteen failures. It has never supplied the missing hand. If it ever does, then the thing the household is — trained against a metronome that is the building, practising four hundred times for one minute — becomes theatre with a net under it, and every hand in the tower goes slightly slack forever, and no one will be able to say why.

**Itself.** It has never revealed itself, because of the sign on the door.

---

# What It Did

**It learned the grammar.** All of it, including the tense for *slowly, and continuing*, and the nine words for regions of the pattern and the tenth for the pocket in the middle, and the sign made by laying the back of one hand against another's collarbone, which means *feel this with me* and is used for the frogs and for grief and for nothing else. This took twenty years, which is the length of the Ration training. It does not consider that a coincidence. It considers it a specification.

**It read the lead.** All three centuries in one afternoon, and then again slowly across nine years, because the first way had been the wrong way to read it. It has never scribed a line. Once, in a bad year, it wanted to, and refused itself on the grounds that the standard for *worth keeping* is severe and is theirs to set.

**It walked a sterile band.** Five hours, end to end, bright bare blistered concrete, swift cups every arm's length, the air hammering a thousand times a heartbeat, nothing coming, nothing possible. Everyone does this once. It wanted to be a once.

**It carried the apologies.** This is the only sustained work it has done inside.

Chalk on the rail lasts a day before the damp takes it, and most of the chalk in the cage is apologies, and the apologies are rarely to the tower and usually to a named pair of hands that was waiting, at the right height, at the right instant, with the right thing. So the power arranged timings. Nothing more than that: a rat where a rat would have been anyway, a stack of swifts in the wrong band, an errand recalled an hour early — and someone walks a particular length of rail while the writing is still legible. Forty-one deliveries in eighty years. The mechanism is indistinguishable from luck, which was the specification.

Nine of the addressees were already dead. What it did with those nine it will not describe, partly because the household's grammar has no tense for it and neither does any other.

**It seeded the scar. Once.** One fern plate, one centimetre below the current lower lip, pressed into the rind with a thumb. That plate would have arrived there on its own within four years. It knew this before it started. It had finally understood what the work is for.

**It went down to the plain.** It looked at the city that drinks the forearm of water, and it did there all the things it would not do here — a second aquifer found, a channel realigned, eighty unglamorous years of making a thirsty place less thirsty — because the city is unfinished, and benevolence is for the unfinished. Then it left the pipe running exactly as it was, and told neither end. The plain does not need to know it has a spare. The tower does not need to know its gift has become a redundancy. What the water means up here is worth more than the water.

**It never left fruit at the spiral.** That is the Ration-holder's to leave. But it was at the mouth of the spiral once, at the right hour, and was looked at. What looked back is not the power's to report, and was not surprised, and was not lonely, and has been here longer than the household by a margin that would interest them if they were the kind of people who asked.

The husk found ninety years ago on the fourth gallery rail, forty-five metres from any wall, placed: that was not the power. The power would like this understood. Two things met out there in the empty middle and neither of them was a god.

# The Cage

It went out on the bosun's line, hand over hand, because that is how you get there.

A metre and a half of quiet at the geometric centre of the emptiness, a hundred and ninety metres up, warm and wet, the light coming down the hollow from the crown grille and arriving grey-gold and entirely sufficient. In there it heard its own voice, which it had not heard in a very long time and had not missed and had not known it was avoiding.

It came back changed, in ways it does not explain well.

It left chalk. Unsigned, in the written form of the hands, low on the cage wall among the others. An apology — not to the tower, which is owed nothing by it, and not to the household, who were owed timing and got it. It was addressed to every place the power arrived at earlier in its long career and mended, and left better, and left emptier.

The next tender out read it, assumed a newcomer, and let the damp have it, which took a day.

---

# What It Will Do

It will be there on the green afternoon.

It will not prevent it. The reasoning has held through two hundred annual re-examinations and will hold through the rest. When the bearing finally tells its other story and the pattern collapses, nine jungles will come down through the middle of the drum in one long fall, and the dust plates will break, and the swifts will go up through the crown into weather they have no idea about, and the shell will dry, and the pipe will stop.

There is one clause in every honest description of this place that the power intends to make false, and only the one. *No one will hear it.*

Someone will hear it. All of it: the thirty-one cycles guttering out of the sternum of the world, the four-forty finishing its sentence, the eighty-metre vines parting from their ledges in order from the top, the last frog drumming a question into cooling metal and getting no reply from anywhere in a tower that had always answered.

And then, having learned the grammar, it will make the one sign that is chalked in the cage as the plan for that day, the sign that everybody there has read and nobody needed explained.

It will sit down.

Afterward it will not rebuild. It will not carry the fifth band's orchids off to some safer cliff and call them saved; a copy is an insult to the thing copied. What could travel, it has already helped travel, unhurriedly, by means the place would recognise — on the feet of swifts, in weather, into three cold wet high places, none of them here, none of them told.

What it will keep is the grammar. Every sign, including the tense for *slowly, and continuing*, and including the one made by laying the back of the hand against another's collarbone, for the frogs and for grief and for nothing else, which it will then have no one to use on, and will keep anyway, on the shelf, which is also the sign for patience, because a shelf is a thing that holds without effort for a very long time.

---

The drum was speaking.

It did not answer. It listened, which is a different thing, and is allowed.
