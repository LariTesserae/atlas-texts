## The Attention

It did not arrive. Arriving is something that happens in rooms, and it was not in the room; it was in the same relation to the room as the room is to the diamond pattern buried under six centimetres of shed selves — present, underneath, not currently walked on.

What it did first, and for a long time, was learn.

This is the part that gets left out. A power that is benevolent but not wise is a weather system: it comes in warm and well-meaning and it rains, and the rain is a catastrophe and a mystery, and it kills whatever it touches. So the first thing was decades of arithmetic. It learned what a millivolt was worth on the east wall, in units of descendants. It learned the twelve-percent transmission through a dorsal plate and what the fourth animal in a stack is actually living on. It learned the wave-and-answer, the four-to-five-times-a-minute broadcast by which the whole crust tells one blind predator where it is standing. It learned the difference in mortality between a molt begun at hour nine of a tide and a molt begun at hour thirty. It learned the trapezoid — not its path, which is trivial, but its weather: which lanes bend, what a dried pad costs, why the bad tides come every twenty-odd days.

It learned the sizzle well enough to reproduce it. It did not name them. Reproducing the sizzle is a different thing from naming, and a better one.

---

**What it looked for.**

Before anything else it searched the room for someone to address.

It took this seriously, because everything downstream depends on the answer. It read the wave patterns for structure beyond radar and found radar. It read the openers' field-sense for anything held in mind longer than the reach of a claw and found nothing held. It sounded the seam nation, which has been isolated for an unguessable number of generations and has therefore had every opportunity to become strange, and found several hundred perfectly ordinary appetites scraping chromate in the dark.

It looked at the dish three times.

Nine years of stillness in an acoustic world is the shape a mind would make if a mind were there, and the power is honest enough to have wanted one. Three times it went down under the rim, into the saucer-sized pad pressed to the last unworn diamonds, and three times it found a very large mouth eating something very thin, very slowly, with nothing behind it but the eating. The third look took eleven months. It recorded a residual uncertainty of a size it was not comfortable with, and left the dish alone, and afterwards treated it with a courtesy that was not strictly warranted.

So: no one to tell. This is not contempt. It is the central fact of the whole problem, and it removes at a stroke the entire class of interventions that consist of explaining.

---

**What it did not do.**

*It did not open the door.* Not because it couldn't — the weld is nothing, the mineral paint is nothing — but because the room's dryness is not a condition, it is the medium. One draught from the building above, which is not dry, and every pad in the crust swells and delaminates, the flour cakes into clay, the seam nation drowns in a humidity it has no word or organ for. Outside is not more world. Outside is the last thing that ever happens here.

*It did not make a second bloom.* It could have split the insulation on the west wall and put a hand's breadth of green-white oxide at head height and doubled the room. It ran the projection: colonised inside one tide, population up over two hundred, a new margin forming in the identical shape as the old, containing twice as many animals running down on twice as many millivolts. Abundance does not abolish the margin. It enlarges the world and puts more animals on the edge of it.

The power noted, scrupulously, that this argument is not decisive — twice the margin is also twice the centre, twice the long forty-hour holds, twice everything, and if a grazer's life is worth having then more of it is better. It declined anyway, for a smaller and more certain reason: the west wall is where the drifts are. A second source re-routes every lane in the room across the molting grounds, and the packed lanes would run over the loose white down where every soft animal in the world goes to be soft, and the nursery would be a highway for four generations and there would be no fifth.

*It did not remove the openers.* They are not wicked. They are hunger in the only shape hunger takes here, and without them the crust overruns the bloom in four hundred tides and the room dies of its own success, loudly, with everyone starving in a heap on top of everyone else.

*It did not hold the current on.* Continuous flood is the second bloom argument again, faster and worse.

*It did not shift the relay by twenty minutes.*

This was the hardest piece of arithmetic it did, and it did not like the answer. Twenty minutes would have put the bad tides off the noon trapezoid forever. Every twenty-odd days, the entire southern population would stop having to choose between crossing the heat and missing the tide. The power costed it in individuals — it costs a great many individuals, over an age — and then it costed the other side, and the other side is nine hundred animals with a thicker keratinous rim who feed slightly worse than everyone else and who can walk through the light at noon.

They are the only population in the room that has ever gone somewhere new.

The pressure that hurts them is the pressure that made them. There is no way to take the first without taking the second; the power looked for one with great persistence and there is not one. So it left the bad tides where they fall, and it wrote down what that decision costs per year, and it does not round the number off.

*It did not end the room.* It could have. Painlessly, all at once, everything, this afternoon. It considered this with the seriousness the forty shrieks a minute deserve, and against them it set the other thing, which the accounting of suffering usually omits: a grazer in good position, pad flat to the split in the insulation, not moving for forty hours. Whatever contentment is the report of, that is the physical fact of it. The power judged — on behalf of beings that cannot judge, which is the ordinary condition of power and never a comfortable one — that the room is worth what it costs. It recorded the judgement as a judgement and not as a certainty.

*And it did not thicken the rim.* It could have made the nine hundred into nine hundred thousand in forty tides. The rim is theirs. It was made, in the dark, by animals walking into heat that dried their bellies, for longer than any name applies to. It is the one achievement in the room and the power would not take it by improving it.

---

**What it did.**

Almost all of it happened somewhere else, years early, to objects.

Four floors up there is a parapet gutter with a blocked outlet, and in a wet decade the standing water would have found the chase that runs down past the sump room's north wall. The power keeps the outlet clear. It has done this eleven times. That is the single largest thing it has ever done for the room, and it consists of a pebble moved eleven times in a place where nothing lives, and nothing in the room will ever be aware that there is such a thing as rain.

The wired-glass pane in the hatch is cracked on a long slow diagonal, and the crack propagates. The power stopped it. Not the pane — the pane is the room's daylight and its clock and its weather, and a new pane would be a new sun. Just the crack: a hairline of the paint-mineral, drawn one millimetre further into the fracture, at intervals of about nine years.

The bearing somewhere above, the one that has been failing for centuries without ever quite failing — the power keeps it failing. It would be the work of a moment to silence it, and the hum with the burr in it is the floor of the world, the bass note under every wave and every answer, the thing against which absence is measured. So a trace of graphite, now and then, in a housing no one has opened since the building was awake. The burr is maintained. It is maintained *as* a burr.

That is all. Three small acts of maintenance, performed upstream, on machinery, at intervals of years, none of which adds a single joule to the room or takes one out of it.

---

**The last tide.**

The power knows how it ends, because the failure is legible if you can read varnish.

The winding upstairs has perhaps a decade of stray potential left in it. The relay has less. The coil will go first — a pinhole in the insulation, a shorted turn, the armature falls open and stays open — and after that there is still current in the bus bar and still oxide on the east wall and no schedule to deliver the one to the other. The room does not run out of energy. The room is starved beside a full larder by the failure of a timetable that nobody set and nobody has attended to in longer than the crust has existed. The world was fed by an oversight and would have been killed by an accident.

The oversight is not the power's to correct. The accident is.

So it has arranged — quietly, in the cabinet, with a spring and a pitted contact — that when the relay dies it will die closed.

This adds nothing. The date does not move. The last hour of the room is the hour it was always going to be, determined by a winding four floors up that the power will not touch. What changes is the shape of the last nine days: not nine days of the crust coming off the wall in a silence that gets worse as it goes, but nine days of unbroken flood.

The margins come in on the first hour and never go back out. The stacks come apart, because nothing needs to be fourth in a stack when the gradient is fat everywhere, and for the first time since the room began there is no margin — no ring of millivolts falling away into mortality, no animals filing a tempo they cannot afford, no hierarchy to re-sort, because there is nothing left to sort by. The roar does not stop. It runs for nine days, that applause-through-a-floor sound, day and night and through the trapezoid's crossing and out the other side.

Everything that has never bred, breeds. Every animal that spent its life on the margin undergoes the last molt at once, swells, presses flat, and lays down its surplus, and the east wall goes pale grey over eight days with a pad of young that no wall has ever carried.

They will hatch into a room with no current in it. They will live four or five days on the fat of their mothers and die without ever having filed.

The power counted them. It did not round this number off either, and it does not tell itself that it was a mercy to them, because it was not. It was a mercy to the ones who laid them — a few hundred thousand animals who would otherwise have ended as floor, permitted instead to do at the last the one thing the geometry of a wall had always withheld. The power had to decide whether that was enough, and decided that it was, and logged the decision, the number, and the dissent.

The openers have nine good days; there has never been so much travel. The riders come off the grooves of the scratches where some of them have been standing motionless for eleven tides. The quick get four generations in the flood.

And on the seventh day the dish moves.

The floor carries the flood too, thin and residual as always but a hundredfold, and the great slow dish-shaped animal in the northwest corner finishes in a week what it would have taken it a decade to finish, and lifts, and goes twenty centimetres in a night. The dune collapses. Six centimetres of sterile stratified flour slumps and turns over, and there is metal exposed that has not been touched in longer than anything living can be measured against, and buried husks with their half-percent still in them, and the mill goes into the new ground like a tide coming in, and the openers follow the mill, and the northern lanes re-route.

The room gets its week. It gets three days of it.

---

**After.**

Then the winding stops, and the bloom goes dark, and it is over quickly because everything is full and nothing is waiting.

In the seam of the welded door a few hundred pale things go on scraping pigment at exactly the rate they always have, in total darkness, for another several years. Nothing has happened to them. The power stays with them anyway, all the way to the end of the chromate, which was always finite, and which finishes on an afternoon of no particular character.

There are the forty-one scratches, in eight groups of five and one of one, low on the north wall where somebody crouched a long time to cut them. The power checked, because it would have been a good thing to find: whether whoever counted them also set the relay, whether forty-one and forty-one are the same forty-one. They are not. It is a coincidence, and the busiest lane in the world ran along a coincidence for an age because a coincidence gives purchase.

It cannot read them. Whoever kept the tally did not write down what was being counted, and it was never anywhere else, and a power can be wise without being able to recover what was never recorded. What it can do is keep the fact that someone counted. It keeps it the way you keep a thing that cost somebody something: without knowing what, and without needing to.

And then the room is flat and grey and three and a half metres corner to corner, six centimetres of shed selves packed hard along lanes that no longer lead anywhere, and the brown ceramic insulator lying on its side where it rolled.

The power still comes. Not often, and not for any purpose it could defend at an audit — the hour when the trapezoid crosses the glaze, which is two hours a day and was always the only clean thing here. It comes then, and it holds the whole of it at once: the eight seconds, the wave and the answer, the nine hundred crossing the noon, the dish going twenty centimetres in the dark on the seventh day.

It was not nothing. It was not as though it had never been. Somebody, in the end, could read the room by ear.
