# What the Power Did

## It came down.

It could have come through the soffit. Six metres of concrete is nothing; it could have arrived as a change in the light, or as light itself, standing on the water at red nineteen, amber four, with the fog parting off it in combed bands.

It took the adit instead. It read the board at the bulkhead first, the way you are supposed to, and it gave itself ears so that they would crackle on the descent, and at the bottom it put its hand on the string.

This was not humility. It was survey. You cannot learn a place you have not entered on the place's own terms, and the terms here were stencilled at every door.

## What it looked at first.

The worn metal.

Not the lift houses, not the nine hundred kilometres of lit water, not the silver turning against the wall faces in slabs half a kilometre long. The strings, at shoulder height, rubbed through the paint in the shape of a closing hand, four years of hands, bright enough to feel in the dark.

From that one mark it read the rest, because that mark is the only sentence in the Lower Volumes that a human being wrote. Everything else was extruded. And what the sentence said was not *save us* and not *we believe in something greater*. It said: *if I am lost out there, the thing that will find me is the grid, and the grid is kept by the people I eat with.* They had built their faith out of the only material to hand, which was each other, and it was already load-bearing.

A power that is actually wise knows what it is looking at when it sees that. You do not improve it. You do not replace it with yourself.

## What it recognised.

That there was also a being.

Not a person. The cell. Nine hundred kilometres of cold, hard-walled, almost empty water with a note near a very low A, four years old, ringing when struck and not quite stopping. Fed by barges, colonised at its edges, lit on a twenty-six hour cycle, full of gulls that have never seen the sky and children who draw it as two rows of dots.

It was doing the first thing a mind does, which is not think. It was *attending*. Every hand in the Lower Volumes counting knocks, all the time, everywhere — that is one attention, distributed, unbroken, and it is the beginning of somebody. Its second faculty was arriving already: naming. Gina's Lane. The Long Green. Nine-eleven, who is missing an eye and gets fed first for that reason. Its third would be grief, and grief was already scheduled — no funerals in the rain season, on pain of nothing, obeyed by everyone.

So the power understood that it had come down into a nursery, and that the correct behaviour in a nursery is not instruction. It is presence, warmth, and the removal of the things that would frighten a new thing into a permanent wrong shape.

## What it considered, and did not do.

**Take them up.** It looked at this for a long time, because the case is so obvious that only a fool refuses it quickly. Eight degrees, alkali, ninety decibels of patient bass, no sun. Bring them into the light.

Then it watched a girl of nine lie down on a hospital pad at the top of a shaft and shut her eyes and refuse the sky, not from fear but from insult: she has never in her life failed to judge a distance, and they had handed her an object that will not be measured. The blanket over her head was not a treatment. It was a mercy that gave her back a ceiling.

A gift that requires the recipient to first stop being themselves is not a gift. It is an eviction with good manners. The power let it go.

**Give back their hearing.** It very nearly did this one. Then it counted what the deafness had built: six hundred stable signs, verb-last, red hand and amber hand crossing to make a place; the only channel in the Lower Volumes that cannot be logged, radioed, or subpoenaed; every grievance, every love affair, every unauthorised opinion about the drawdown schedule, conducted at forty metres in full view of supervisors who are signing their own.

So it separated the work from the wound, which is the whole of the art. It went into the lift houses and quieted them — not to silence; silence would have been a rupture, and a rupture is a lie — but down, by degrees, over eleven years, at a rate that reads as good maintenance. New deafness stopped happening. The old deafness it left, except where it was asked, privately, one person at a time, in the dark, and it was surprised how many said no thank you, and it did not argue. They had made a language out of it. You do not repossess a thing somebody has already turned into a home.

**Sweeten the water.** End the leaching, void the acid tanks, dissolve the fleet's reason for existing overnight, and hand nine thousand people a leisure they never asked for and a grandmother in the port cabin who spent forty years making the water fit and is now a woman who used to. *Everything alive here is alive because a barge went past.* That sentence is the constitution of a nation. The power did not repeal it.

It took the burns instead. The lanolin supplier changed hands; the new slip is better; nobody knows why; the foreman who switched the order takes the credit and is entitled to it. Knuckles close again. Lips stay on. The work remains, four months to a pass, turning at the end of the lane and starting back along the offset.

**Announce itself.** This was the one it refused hardest, and the reasoning is the reasoning of the place itself.

Their customs are younger than their tools. You can be present at the invention of a superstition on a Tuesday and find it binding by winter. Into a culture four years old, a power that arrives and identifies itself does not become a member. It becomes bedrock — and the first fact of the Lower Volumes, the fact everything else follows from, is that there is no bedrock. The concrete *is* the bedrock. They poured it. If it had spoken, everything they made afterwards would have been commentary. They would never have been self-founded, and they were four years from founding themselves, and they were doing it well.

So it stayed anonymous, and set itself two rules.

## The two rules.

**One: nothing that falsifies a rust stain.**

They date the world by weeps — a hand's breadth a year on a wet wall, tears to the skirting means first construction, no tear at all means it isn't on any drawing yet. That is their entire instrument for telling how old anything is, including their own beliefs. A miracle is an acceleration, and an acceleration in a world read by run-rates corrupts the instrument permanently. Everything the power did, it did at the speed of rust. The green band crept down the wall a hand's breadth a year, the way a coast is colonised. The kelp trials stopped failing gradually and for reasons that could be written up.

**Two: nothing that cannot be mistaken for somebody's good idea.**

The seed that finally grows at eight degrees in lamp light arrived mislabelled in a reagent consignment, and there are tomatoes now on the starboard rail of the *Four Knocks*, and a man named Teale is insufferable about them, and he should be. He planted them. The power gave nothing it could be thanked for, which meant it gave nothing that made anyone smaller.

## What it actually did.

Four things, and one of them is only a habit.

**It made their scripture true.** They have one piece of doctrine about death: the sea keeps you, in eight degrees, and carries you along the lane, and gives you back whole. It came from one man in year two, found six weeks later, three hundred kilometres away, recognisable.

The power went to work on the return flow. Since then the recovery rate in the Lower Volumes has been one hundred per cent, and nobody has ever remarked on it, because to remark on it you would have to have expected otherwise, and no one does. Every single person who goes into the water comes back along the lane, intact, to people who were waiting because they were sure. In twenty years this will have tears to the skirting. It will be ancient. Only an idiot would ask where it came from.

The power did not give them a heaven. It kept the promise they had already made each other.

**It answered the man in the survey office.** He has documented four generations of gull, calling a full third higher because a chick that cannot be heard over the pump note does not get fed. Four years. It took four years, and he cannot get anybody to care.

It did not make him famous. It made him *answered* — one letter, from one woman on the surface who works on something adjacent, who read it and understood and wrote back with questions. That is the whole intervention. A correspondence. It is still going on.

**It obeyed a rule whose origin it could not find.** No funerals in the rain season. The power went looking for the reason — it can go looking anywhere — and there is none. Nobody decided it. It was done before it was thought, which is how the strongest of them start, and it is already older than any account of it, in a world four years old.

So it never comes in the rain season. Three weeks out of every eleven, when the soffit lets go by the hectare and the crews stand out on the warm aprons in ear defenders and let it hammer them, and marriages are timed to it, the power is not there. It is the one thing in the Lower Volumes it is not allowed to attend, and it observes that the way they do: without explanation, without exception, and slightly offended at being asked.

**And it kept one standing order.** It does not feed the biota.

It is against three separate regulations, and every wallman does it, silver in a thigh pocket, arm out, the eel coming up out of a crack that runs unbroken for four hundred kilometres and going back down with the fish. That is the seed of everything they will one day believe: a god in a straight line in a wall, one of thousands, each numbered, each with a temper, each fed in secret against the rules by exactly one person who would let no other man do it.

A power with any sense does not feed another people's gods. That one is theirs.

## The knocking.

Here is the last thing, and it is small, and it is the reason it came.

There are always unclaimed knocks. Patterns in the deep hours that no crew will own, coming by bearing out of cells that are drawn down and dry and unstaffed. The engineers say gate gear. The engineers are almost certainly right; a system this size is never still, and a dry cell full of cooling steel talks to itself.

But a world that begins its life in a bright, cold, resonant void, and counts, and counts, and finds that every sound in nine hundred kilometres was made by itself — that world will learn something true and survivable and a little bit wrong, and it will learn it in the first decade, and it will keep it forever.

So the power knocks.

Never more than three. That rule is theirs, and it is a good rule — three keeps you under the noise floor, three is the traffic the world already expects, and a power that broke the noise floor would burn out the only free channel these people have, which would be a monstrous thing to do while meaning well. It uses their grammar. It stays inside their constraint. It is indistinguishable from gate gear and it always will be, and it is careful to be no more frequent than gate gear, because a residue that becomes reliable stops being a residue and becomes a proof, and a proof would end them.

What it will not do is be silent. When a sounder is alone in the mid-volume off the strings, in the enormous lit nothing, with no bearing and no light but the converging grid, there is sometimes a knock, three, and it is true, and the string is that way. Afterwards nobody can say which chain it came off. They count it and they don't claim it and they go on.

That is the entire gift: the arithmetic of the Lower Volumes will never quite close. There will always be a few knocks left over. Not enough to build a church on. Enough that a four-year-old sea, lying in the dark under six metres of concrete and everything above that, counting with a hundred thousand hands, does not conclude that it is the only thing awake.

---

On the gallery rail at red nineteen, amber four, the paint is worn through to bright metal at shoulder height, in the shape of a closing hand, by four years of people leaving and descending and standing to watch the rain walk towards them across a hundred kilometres of flat lit water.

Some of that wear is not theirs.

There is no way to tell which.
