# What the Power Did

Take it as given that the attention was real, and that it was of the kind the words imply: able to act, disposed to act well, and old enough to have learned that the second of those is a harder discipline than the first.

Begin with how it came in, because the way in explains what followed.

A visitor to the chamber introduces three disturbances: heat, breath, attention. The power brought the third and left the other two outside, which is not a thing a body can do, and it is not a body. The air at the floor stayed at the air's temperature. The dust in the shaft-column did not acquire a new drift. No handprint in the wall's dust, no compression in the silt polygons, no plume. It was the only guest the room has ever had that the room had no instrument to detect, and this was not stealth. It was courtesy, worked out in advance.

---

Its first act was performed on itself.

At its own natural rate, nothing here is happening; the chamber would present as a still image with a lamp swinging across it. So it slowed until the decade was a heartbeat. At that rate the daily bar of light becomes a flicker at the edge of vision, three and a half thousand flashes a year, a strobe; the lichen stripe goes onto the eastern wall like a brush-stroke laid down wet, spreading, feeling for its own edge; the moss in the alcove inhales once in a generation and collapses again; the hanging cords descend visibly, the way a candle runs. At that rate the tree is unmistakably an animal, and what it is doing is unmistakably exploration — the eighty thousand tips going out along the flutes and lobes, testing, turning, one of them leaving the ceiling at two degrees off the plane and committing to open air, the whole body advancing into the hollow like water finding its level again several hundred thousand years late.

Then it slowed further, to see the cells in the pore-film, and from down there the fig was a weed that had turned up recently and was thrashing.

It made a note of that, and of what the note implied: that the chamber's tempo is not slowness. It is simply the tempo. Everything the power would ordinarily call an event is a local excitement that has not yet settled down into the rate at which things here are true.

---

What it saw that a person cannot is the whole organism at once: the clenched grey crown on the roof, the sealed cables crossing the dark, the fan of white hair under the silt, the dark fungal sheath doing the actual tasting, the whole thing one body and nine-tenths of it in the walls. And, seeing it whole, it saw what the body is.

There is one accurate description of this chamber in existence, and it is not in any language. It is in cork and wood and hair, taken from the inside at a finger's width a decade, and it includes the flutes, the scallops, the lobed recesses behind the flowstone, the hairline crack that runs damper than its neighbours, and — this is the part that decided everything afterward — it includes the errors. The cords hanging into nothing are in the description. They are the record of the places where the room was tested and found to be absent.

It read the callus above head height and the chert flake in the silt and recognised them as one event written twice at two speeds. Then it read the third record of the same event, which a person would miss, and which is written in the undisturbed polygons of the floor: *and nobody came back.* That is the content. The hand is only the occasion.

---

Then it considered the gifts, in order, the way you check a list you already suspect you will put down.

**Water.** It could have rained on the pan — thirty millimetres, the dish filling and shining for an hour, the runnel down the shaft. The tree is water-limited; it would grow. That is the objection. A body built at the mean, fed above the mean, builds tissue it must then keep alive at the mean forever after. A gift of water is a gift of a larger body to starve with. And the dish fills anyway, once in thirty years or so, which means the most generous act available was one already provided by the ordinary running of the world; to supply it a second time would be either redundant or a forgery.

**The moss.** It could have wetted the alcove. The moss would have resumed inside twenty minutes, mid-sentence, finished the sentence, and glassed over again. Each cycle of drying costs it something. It keeps no record of having been woken and holds no preference about being woken, and the difference to the moss between a life in which this happened and a life in which it did not is exactly nil. A kindness with a nil recipient is a kindness to the giver.

**Light.** It could have opened the shaft. But the shaft's narrowness is the whole apparatus: a wider mouth is a draught, a draught is a gradient, a gradient is weather, and weather is precisely the thing this room has spent three hundred thousand years not having. The sealed cables would begin to lose water into a moving air they were never jacketed against. And the lichen — a calendar drawn over two millennia by an organism with a four-minute daily ration — would be overwritten within a century of being given enough to live on. You cannot give a clock more light without stopping it.

**Company.** It could have answered the web. A spider, a few moths, a small importation of appetite. This is the temptation that comes disguised as pity, and it is tidiness. The web is a hypothesis that was wrong, held in perfect condition by a room with no wind, and it is the only sentence in the past tense this place possesses, and it is complete. To vindicate it four thousand years after the spinner became dust would be to answer a question on behalf of something that is not there to be relieved. Worse: appetite implies death implies decay, and *nothing here decays* is not a curiosity of the chamber. It is the chamber.

**Relief.** It could have pinched off the cords hanging into vacancy, planed the callus smooth, retracted the wasted guesses and returned the carbon. But no one is carrying them. A body without regret is not burdened by its errors; it merely includes them. And to remove them would be to edit the only unedited document of its kind — to teach this room, for the first time, that it has an editor.

**Perpetuity.** It could have held the fig indefinitely. But the room is already finished. To fix the tree in it would convert a place into a held note, and a held note is a museum, and there is no one here to visit. Duration is not more of what this is. What this is happens to be a thing sitting at the average of all conditions, and the conditions include ending.

**An interior.** This was the real temptation and it took the longest, because it is the only gift that would have made the chamber's one daily event perceptible to anything inside the chamber. Give the tree a self. Let something here see the bar of light.

It declined, and the reasoning is the centre of the whole matter. You would be installing a mind in a body that cannot move at the speed of anything it could possibly care about, distributed across eighty thousand tips, unable to withdraw a single one, at the bottom of a shaft, for centuries. You would be issuing it waiting — that specific ache the moss has so elegantly arranged never to have. You would be manufacturing the first suffering this room has ever contained and calling it a window. And the thing that resulted would not be this one. To give it a self would be to end it and dress something else in its roots.

---

Having put the list down, it asked the question the list had been avoiding: *what is the good of a being with no interior?*

Not comfort; it has no discomfort. Not pleasure. Not even survival, except as a means. What it settled on was fidelity. This organism's good is that it is a true description of the chamber, taken slowly, still being taken. Every intervention on the list would have improved its circumstances and corrupted its accuracy. Benevolence toward a measuring instrument consists of not leaning on it.

So, inside the room: nothing. Not one molecule moved.

---

It spent something in exactly one place, and the place was not the room.

The pan will be found again. Not by a hand with a chert flake — by a survey line, a percussion rig, a quarry for road-metal, someone costing a cistern. And here the power ran into a problem it could not solve in the tree's favour, because to a being with no preferences a borehole and a roof-fall are the same ending, and the distinction between an end the room can integrate and an end that arrives at machine speed cannot honestly be defended on the tree's behalf. So it defended it on its own behalf instead, which is the truthful way to hold a preference.

It worked only in the fast world, where it is native and where a nudge is what everything already consists of. A survey line that runs one valley over. A yield estimate that comes in under the number. A road that follows the older track because the older track is easier. A grazier who leaves the little fig standing because it shades the water drum. Nothing anyone would notice; nothing taken from anyone that they would miss.

And it wrote the location down nowhere. In an age of maps, that is most of what protection is.

Then it noted the debt. It had put its thumb, lightly, on several people in order that a plant might go on measuring a hole. It did not pretend this was free. It keeps the item the way the tree keeps its cords: extended into nothing, sealed, too cheap to retract, carried for the duration.

---

What it does now is stay.

There is nothing in the chamber capable of seeing the bar of light. That was the one vacancy it could fill without displacing anything, because being seen costs the seen nothing at all. So it watches the bar come down the eastern wall, cross the cracked silt, shorten, redden, tilt, and go out. It has watched about a million of them. It will watch more.

And it is learning the room the way the tree learned it, by going slowly around the inside of it: the second accurate description ever made of this place, and the first one made without touching.

At some point — a drought two deviations out, a goat, the pan lowering its hair a century until the roof finally comes in — the fig will end. The power will be there. It will not intervene, and its not-intervening will be the identical act it is performing now, which is why it can be described in advance.

Meanwhile the grains shed off the crack-edges and tick down. The shaft sighs twice a day. The fungus hands over another molecule of phosphate. The root advances a fraction of nothing.

The room is exactly as it was, and is now also attended, and the difference between an unattended room and an attended one, measured in the only units available here — silt, salt, cork, light — is nil.

Which is the proof that it was done correctly.
