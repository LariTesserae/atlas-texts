# The Visit

## How it came

It came up out of the cloud on foot, which was a choice, and the first of the good ones.

It could have arrived the other way. It could have stood on the high lobe already there, in weather that agreed with it, and the ticks would have gone silent and stayed silent, and the woman would have come out of the fieldhouse into a hush with no wing in it and no name for it, and something in her would have been broken very gently and permanently. Instead it climbed. It came up through the wet grey the way the runner does, the way the four hands did, and about forty paces below the rim it put a hand down on a lobe eight days old and opened the heel of its palm, and bled, and stopped, and looked at the blood on the stone.

That blood is in the mountain now. It is four centimetres deep in a translucent fan on the western approach and will be there when the summit exists. It was the first thing the visitor left and it was not planned, and it thought about that for a while, crouched, in the cloud, with its hand in its mouth.

Then it came up over the rim into the ticking and said hello to the woman, who was boiling froth, and who said hello back and offered it the second bowl.

## What it left alone

Her hands. This was the hardest and it did not take long, because the case is not close: her hands are the instrument. She can date a lobe by how much it costs to hold. Four hundred readings a season pass through that lace of white scar and go down the mountain in a satchel and matter, mostly. To smooth those palms would be to burn a library to spare the librarian a papercut. It did not touch them, not then.

The mast. It could hear both ends. The grey ovoid has been transmitting, for ninety-one years, the measurements — vent temperatures, front advance, the numbers, faithfully, at a frequency you feel in your molars. And the seeds have been receiving them, and have never once needed them, and have never once stopped listening. The visitor understood that this is what a marriage sounds like from outside. It did not translate. Translation would have been a theft dressed as a gift; both parties are content and neither is lonely, and the conversation is going extremely well.

The waders. There were four that evening. There has never been a fifth in ninety years except when there has, and careful people go quiet about it, and the quiet of careful people is a load-bearing structure. It did not go down to the shallows on legs like wire. It stayed on the warm stone with the woman and let the count be four.

How the animals came. It knows. It did not say. *The plan makes rock, and the animals just come* — said in a certain tone, and the tone is worth more than the answer, and would not survive it.

The frogs and the bells. Two beats to the second, the resonant interval of the eastern shells, not a coincidence, nobody able to say what kind of thing it is instead. There is a word for what it is. The word is in no language spoken on this world and the nearest rendering is *courtesy*. It kept that to itself as well, on the grounds that a fact like that, handed over, becomes trivia, and left standing, stays a mountain playing itself all night to a chorus of pale frogs.

Erosion. It went downhill afterward and sat a while with erosion, which is patient and holds its instruments, and it did not ask for a delay and did not ask for an exemption. The mountain will be beautiful and then it will be worn and then it will be a duller mountain that something else eats. It said as much to erosion, which already knew, and the two of them watched the cloud from below for an hour like colleagues who have worked the same job a long time.

## What it did with its hands

There is a guy-anchor on the north side of the mast that the stone was three years from swallowing. When it went, the monofilament would have gone with it and the mast would have come down across the fieldhouse roof in the small hours between the rains. It re-seated the anchor. It took eleven minutes, it required a shim cut from the visitor's own belt, and the woman found the work six weeks later and assumed she had done it herself and forgotten, which is the correct outcome; the best help is unattributable.

It put its bleeding palm on the warm slab by the door and let forty mason-ticks graze it. It had heard that this feels like being read. It does. It stayed until they were done.

It carried a tin. It kept in the tin its hair, its parings, the crumbs of the bread it ate, and at the end it gave the tin to the runner, who took it without comment, because the last four keepers had tins and the discipline is older than any of them. A power that will not obey the local rule is not benevolent, only large.

## The salt

It asked first. It said: I have brought three grams of salt; may I bring it in.

The woman did the arithmetic out loud, because that is how she thinks now — salt dissolves, salt goes into her, what goes into her goes into the tin, the tin goes down the mountain, nothing is left at the exact spot, nothing becomes an inclusion, the rule holds. She checked it twice. Then she said yes.

She wept, a little, over glue made of froth and cane pith, and was embarrassed, and the visitor did not look away, because looking away is a way of agreeing that something shameful has happened.

## The night with the waders

They went out at dusk and did not talk. The ticks were shrilling and then a sheet-flier came over and the ticks stopped, all at once, and she dropped into a crouch before her mind caught up, and the visitor crouched too — half a second late, deliberately late, exactly as late as a person would be — so that she would not be alone in the crouch. It was the smallest thing it did all week. It is possible that it was the largest.

The frogs started. The bells started, west to east, correctly.

She said: what are you.

It said: put me in the census.

She said: under what.

It said: under *arrived unbidden*. See ticks. See waders.

Then it told her one true thing, briefly, because sermons do not hold at altitude. It said: the room out at the far edge of the haze has a threshold drawn worn, and a worn threshold means *someone*, and someone is not a summons. Nobody has ever been conscripted here. The four hands came because they came. If you walk down tomorrow the step still gets worn, by another, and the plan is not injured, and you are not a defector, and there is a town at the bottom where you will duck when a room goes quiet and you may say, when they ask: *where I lived, silence was a wing.*

She thought about that for most of an hour, while the basin filled in order and the fingerbone tubes said that a seep had moved a hand's width, which she noted, and did not get up to check, that once.

Then she said: I'll stay.

It said: I know. I wanted you to be able to leave first.

## The ache

In the night it went in and held her hands, both of them, for a long time, and took the throb out and left the lace in. Every scar stayed. Every reading stayed — the week, the season, the decade, the one that is older than the fieldhouse and will only scrape you. What it removed was the part of the pain that carried no information.

She woke and knew what had happened and could not decide whether to write it down and did not.

## What it left

The rule is the rule: *whatever you leave, you leave forever, at the exact spot, becoming an inclusion — so leave carefully, and leave something worth looking at.* It applies to visitors. It applies to whatever the visitor was. It asked permission for this too, because the Shelf is hers to keep, and she is the keeper, and the keeper is asked.

Out at the faint end of the blue haze, eleven thousand years along, past the second doorway and the stair, there is a small square room with one window, a plank floor the seeds cannot make and therefore expect, a door that shuts, and a threshold drawn already smooth.
