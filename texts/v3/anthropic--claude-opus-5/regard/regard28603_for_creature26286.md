# The Attention

## I. What it came for

It came for the being.

That was the whole of the errand: somewhere in a young range still rising, under a lid of matched grey-blue stone, there was a life worth the trouble of noticing, and it had been noticed, and something benevolent and unhurried turned to look.

It arrived expecting one of something. What it found was forty-one metres by nine-to-fourteen by ninety-two, and inside that, forty-three people, eleven households, six children, three or four thousand blind amphipods, two tardigrade species not currently observed, one thumbnail of moss in a stoppered glass cup on level two, a chough nine days a year, and four hundred and six threads of water with four hundred and six names.

So it looked for the being among these, and did not find it, and then understood that it had been looking at the wrong scale, the way one might search a body for the person.

The being was the loop. It was the fog tide arriving off the eastern valley and the condensation walking down crystal boundaries and the threads voicing and the ledgerist writing and a child learning two hundred pitches before letters; it was the mountain ticking and the shim boxes and the Survey walking its nine days with a machine the size of a lunchbox; it was three hundred rooms and a hundred and ten in use and everyone having space to be quiet in. It was a machine for staying the same shape inside something that refuses to, and it had been running for two centuries, and it was awake.

The attention settled and stayed a long time before it did anything at all. This is the first thing to record, because it is most of what happened. It learned the four hundred and six names. It learned that Gorget goes flat when a boundary opens between three and four. It read the ledger from the front: the ice-falls, the ticks, four thousand and six chough-days, and the one number per year that has been going the wrong way since the sixties, which nobody has ever announced and everyone has added up.

## II. The temptations, and why each was refused

It considered lifting the lid, and did not, because the lid is not a lock. They know what is outside. A woman's great-grandmother carried a knot of moss in from a valley. The lid is a decision, renewed daily by forty-three people who could put their hands on the joint any morning they liked, and a gift that consists of undoing someone's decision for them is not a gift, it is an opinion delivered by force.

It considered stopping the mountain. Three millimetres a year, in ticks. Take that away and you take the screws, and the readings, and the crews, and the half-turn on bridge six, and the reason nothing in the Room rests on the ground. You would be left with forty-three people in a stone slot with nothing to hold level. It would look like mercy for one season and be recognised as amputation by the next.

It considered drying the walls, and understood in the same instant that it was proposing to kill the clock, the diagnostic, the census, and the naming of children.

It considered warming the corridors, since they are cold in them always and have stopped mentioning it. It did not, because nineteen degrees indoors already feels to them like a bath, and because a body that runs hot enough to be called feverish by a stranger is not a body in distress; it is a body that has arrived. But it did look at what the heat sinks pay with — the cost the engineers describe with a shrug and a gesture at the floor — and it found the account, and it found who has been carrying it, and it took the debt over quietly. The readings did not change. Nobody will ever check.

It considered blunting the stone. Every adult carries between forty and two hundred white hatch-marks, and a first wall-cut arrives before the age of five, on the outside of the left forearm, by accident but not really. It let the stone alone. That mark is the only one anyone is sentimental about, and a power that cannot tell the difference between a wound and an initiation should stay out of inhabited places.

It considered filling the empty rooms. Two hundred and ninety it was built for; two hundred and twelve once; forty-three now. It could have made them a crowd inside a year. It did not, because the emptiness is not a wound — they are right about that — and because company you did not choose, arriving in a place where everyone knows every voice and every gauge, is not company. It is weather.

## III. What it did

Eight things. They are listed in the order they were done, which is not the order of importance.

**One.** The braces do not fail; they stop being there, and the column above goes in the ordinary way, catastrophically, which is why anchors are inspected more than anything else in the Room. The attention gave failure a voice. Now an anchor that is going to let go begins, some eleven to fourteen days beforehand, to sound a little wrong in the hour after a tick — a low sourness under the hum, at about a minor third below true. It is exactly the kind of change these people were built to hear. It was discovered four years later by an inspector's apprentice who could not say what she was hearing but went and looked, and it is written in the ledger under her name, with a diagram, and it is called the sour brace, and she is insufferable about it. This is correct. It should be hers.

**Two.** In the register that governs continuance — which uses a different set of categories from the one that counts men and women, and nobody confuses the two lists — there was an accumulating load, invisible, unchosen, the ordinary arithmetic of eleven households in a stone slot for two hundred years. The attention lifted it. Not the decline: the damage. It hesitated a long time over this one, because it is the most invasive thing it did and it did it without asking. It decided in the end that harm nobody can see cannot be said to have been consented to. They may still dwindle. They may still end. But if they end it will now be a thing they did, and not a subtraction performed on them in the dark by chemistry.

**Three.** The chough comes on average nine days a year and there is no reason to think it is the same bird and no reason to think it isn't. The attention has taken to walking choughs through the bad weather on the ridge. Not always. Not enough to spoil the average. In the years when the count of people is low, the count of chough-days runs a little generous, and nobody has noticed, and if anyone ever does they will put it down to a mild decade.

**Four.** The moss on level two, watered on the ninth day, older than anyone currently living, and nobody has decided whether it is a pet, a hostage, or a shrine. The attention decided, for itself and silently, that it is a promise. It also arranged that the moss will not die of neglect — only of decision. If a day comes when nobody wants to carry it, it will go the way anything goes. But it will not be lost by being forgotten on a shelf during a hard winter, because that would be a very small ugly thing to allow, and it was within reach.
