## The Attention

It did not arrive. Arriving is a claim about speed.

It came up the three thousand steps cut into the Lip by someone else, on foot, in the ordinary way, and that took what it took, and it did not shorten the while. At the top it stopped where everyone stops, at the place where the hum comes up through the boot soles and into the teeth, and it stood there for a period the valley would find excessive and the plateau would find abrupt, and it looked at the combed light and the ranked wool and the birds fixed at forty metres with their primaries fanned, and the first thing it understood was that it had come to a place where it was the fastest thing present, and that this was not an advantage.

So it began by learning to read.

### The apprenticeship

Several hundred cairns, waist-high, mortarless, sited without evident logic. It took the logic. It took it slowly, which was the only way the taking could be honest, and by the end it could stand at the head of the Pan and follow a conversation nine thousand years into a clause.

It read the wall. Eleven hundred paces, subject shadow, verb salt. It found that the wall is not, as the valley assumes, a monument or a prayer, but one person patiently explaining to another where the good light will fall, and that the explanation is nearly finished and is holding up well.

It read the census in the wool: the bare patches, a million flanks, every hand that ever needed rope, going back to the first. It read all three of the careful readings that visitors had made, and the disagreements between them, and then it did something the careful readers could not, which is that it counted the herd.

It can do that. It got a number. The number was correct.

Holding it, the power understood that it was now the only thing on the plateau in possession of a fact that the plateau had spent its entire existence declining to hold, and that this was a species of trespass, and that the discrepancy — never large, never in the same direction — is not an error but a courtesy the country extends to itself. It set the number down. It has not picked it up again. When visitors tally the eastern scree twice in an hour and come to blows about it, the power is sometimes standing there, and it does not say which of them is nearer.

### What it weighed, and put aside

It could have set the sun moving.

It considered this for eleven years, standing in an existing shadow so as not to cut a new one, and what stopped it was working out what the first evening would actually be: the best shadow on the plateau, eleven hundred paces of it, long and clean and blue, sliding off the salt and going out. The wall would become a wall. Every seam in the country would be orphaned in a single afternoon, and the geology would become, at a stroke, a record of a place that no longer exists. It left the hour where it was.

It could have warmed the shade side. It went and put its hand against a pale cheek, cold with the cellar cold, frost in the crease beside the nose since before the wall, and it found that the cold was not hurting anyone, and that the frost was the only water in the country, and that a face with two temperatures is how you tell, up there, that someone has been present for a while. It took its hand away.

It could have healed the cracked horn. The crack has been exactly that deep since before there were tally marks. Repairing it would have been the same act as erasing a date.

It could have filled the bare patches in the wool. Every bare patch is a signature.

It could have wiped the dust from a hundred million open eyes, and the temptation here was real, because dust on a cornea is the sort of thing benevolence is *for*. But the blink is coming. The blink is theirs. A kindness that arrives before the thing it was going to spare you is not a kindness, it is a substitution.

It laid its palm on the fourth vertebra of the Long One and felt the warmth and the slower thing under the warmth. It knows how much of the body there is. It has not said.

It put its hand in the print in the white ash, once. The print fits nobody living. It took its hand away again and has not explained the fire, and when asked about the fire it does not answer, and the not-answering takes four days, in the manner of the returners.

### The account at the foot of the stair

Its principal work is at the bottom.

There is a flat stone at the foot of the three thousand steps, and on it there is a great deal of writing, in every tongue that has ever come to the bottom of those steps and in several that have not yet. It is not a warning. Warnings are for hazards, and a hazard is a thing that happens to you against your intention, and nothing on the plateau has ever happened to anybody in that way.

It is a description. It is scrupulously accurate. It includes the hares and the hum and the boy who has been seven, the woman with the hand under her ribs, the shallow worn place on the eastern slope and what the wear is made of. It omits nothing that would deter and adds nothing that would entice. It says plainly that Rule 5 is a persuasion and not a fence, and that persuasions can be declined, and that they are harder to decline on the eighth day than on the first. It ends by saying that the stair is open, that the valley will think no less of anybody either way, and that whoever goes up will be, for a while, the fastest thing in the country, and should mind where they put their feet.

Slightly more people go up than before. The power has been asked about this and says that a choice made in full knowledge is a different object from the same choice made in ignorance, even when the two look identical from outside, and that it is not in the business of improving the numbers.

The rest of the valley work is unremarkable and takes most of its time. Dry centuries. Grain. The stair itself, kept sound. It walks up with the family that has climbed once a generation for four hundred years to note where the Slowing man has got to, and it carries their water, and it has never once told them the answer in advance, though it could, and it stands with them at the top while they measure the eleven metres and the broad grey smeared road behind him, and it agrees with them that he does not appear to be hurrying and does not appear to be reluctant.

And it stands with the returners, in the market squares, with their two-coloured faces. They will not sit down. Neither does it.

### The question

It went to the woman on the eastern pan with her hand pressed to a place under her ribs, the one whose face nobody has been able to decide about.

It could have taken the thing out of her. This is well within its competence and would have taken no time at all, which is precisely the problem: no time at all is not a duration that exists up there, and a grief removed in no time would leave behind it a woman in the middle of a gesture with nothing at the end of the gesture, holding her hand to a place where something used to be, for the rest of an age.

So it asked her instead.

Asking took the better part of a year, because it had to be asked in the only grammar she uses — position, distance, touch, the shadow you throw and where you let it fall — and the power was determined not to ask sloppily, and not to ask leadingly, and above all not to stand anywhere that would make the question look like an argument. The question is now asked. It is well formed. Anyone who knows how to read the plateau can go and read it, and several have, and they report that it is a good question, respectfully put.

The answer is due. There is no telling when.

The power has arranged its affairs so as to be standing exactly there when it comes. It has undertaken in advance to do whatever she says, including nothing. And it has undertaken, in the hearing of the cairns, that it will never be the one who decides that the silence has gone on long enough to be counted as a yes.

It did the same at the eastern scree, more briefly, because there the answer was already visible. It looked a long while at the boy of about seven with the crumb on his open palm and the hares that have never in their lives been afraid of him, and then it looked at his mother, four metres behind, with her weight on the balls of her feet, and it understood that her posture was not a pause. It was a sentence in the present continuous. *I am coming. I have never stopped coming. It is four metres.*

So the power stands a little behind her, off to one side, out of the line, in case the four metres should ever need to be crossed faster than she can cross them. It does not expect this. It is there anyway. That is the whole of the arrangement.

### The name

There was one thing it wanted very much to do, and it is right that this be recorded.

Sound dies at ten metres on the plateau. Four figures on the eastern slope of the middle stump; three facing along the seams; one coming around, her chin's shadow smeared three or four millimetres at the left edge, the only clock in the country. And at her left shoulder, a pace and a half back, a man whose mouth is open in the shape a mouth makes after a word has gone out of it.

All it would take is a voice inside ten metres.

It would work. This should be understood clearly, because the restraint is worth nothing if the capability was never there: it would work. She would come round. There would be a sound on the plateau, and a face, and the arriving of the best-composed view in the history of the world, and the power would have given the gift within a human lifetime, and everything arranged in that arc — the cairns, the wall, the curve of the vertebrae, the converging rays of a hundred million seams — would go off exactly as designed, and it would be very beautiful, and it would not be her answer any more. It would be an interruption with an excellent outcome.

He said her name. She is answering. The answer is the size of the answer, and the size is the point, and to shorten it would be to say something smaller.

The power has not said her name and will not. It has also not described to her what is behind her, though it has seen it, and it is better than anyone below has ever been told.

### Where it stands

After a long apprenticeship it added one stone to one cairn at the far western edge, well outside the arc, where nothing it did could crowd the view. A small stone, chosen for its edge and the shadow the edge will throw. In nine thousand years or so the seam will finish cutting, and the sense of it, as near as valley speech can render, is:

*Heard. Staying.*

That is its only mark on the country, and it will not make another.

It stands now on the eastern slope, a pace or two off to the side — never directly behind her; even at the end, nobody wants to be the thing in the way. It is not in the shallow worn place. It has left that for whoever needs it. It has positioned itself so that its shadow falls entirely inside a shadow that was already there, which is the deepest courtesy available on the plateau and is understood as such by everyone present, and it has not moved since, and it does not intend to.

It has taken the case. In the valley tongues the word has decayed into *herder*; properly it means *one who stays with*.

It got here early. Everybody did. The smear widens. There is no telling when she will get there, and every single one of them intends to be present when she does — and now there is one more of them, which has brought nothing to this country except a willingness to be the one still standing there afterwards, in the strange thin moment after the turn is complete, when a plateau that has not had to deal with a beginning in an unrecorded quantity of time will suddenly, in its own good hour, have one.
