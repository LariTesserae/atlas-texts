# The Visitor Who Sat Down at the Lip

## What it did first, which was nothing

It came over the col in the ordinary way, on the ordinary legs it had put on for the purpose, and when it reached the seam in the air it stopped and read the ground: the polished place on the rock where four hundred generations of buttocks had shuffled through, the worn holds, the small ledge with the drinking gourd chained to a pin.

It sat down and went through sitting.

It could have entered any way it liked. It could have arrived as weather. But the rule was *never at a run*, and the rule was not for its benefit, and it had learned a long time ago that the fastest way to be a stranger forever is to be exempt.

Then it stayed at the edge of the trees for eleven years and did almost nothing at all.

It learned the forty-second tide of the cicadas until it could feel the turn coming in its jaw. It learned which of the two hundred falls went quiet in the second week of the dry and which one, behind the third buttress, changed pitch when rain was two days out. It learned that the low tone of the Lip beats against the frogs at a period of about nineteen seconds and that on certain nights the beat and the insect tide come into phase, and that when this happens the old people of the bowl go outside and stand in it and do not talk about it afterward.

It got a moss garden on its shoulders. It got slightly deaf.

## The temptations, listed, and refused

**It did not open the Slow Door.** It could have. The traced rectangle would have gone soft under its hand like the skin on warm milk. But the door was already a practice: people did not sleep in front of it, and the not-sleeping was a hundred generations old, and if it opened the door on a Tuesday out of curiosity it would not be opening a door, it would be ending a discipline.

**It did not read the pins.** They were cold and legible and, it recognised with a small internal lurch, addressed to something like itself. Survey markers, in the vocabulary of a workmanship that had gone somewhere else. It could have taken a reading and known who had built the bowl, and why, and whether the why was still current. It sat by one for a season with its hand near it and did not touch it, on the grounds that the people here had a rule about not moving them and no rule about not reading them only because no one had ever imagined a reader.

**It did not explain the filaments.** Not because the truth was dangerous — it was not particularly — but because the bowl's whole way of life is an arrangement made with a thing that has never been asked to justify itself, and a full account of the vasculature would have converted a relationship into an appliance. There is a kind of knowledge that costs you the thing you know. It had spent much of its long attention learning to recognise that kind in advance.

**It did not cut a browser.** Obviously.

**It did not make the bowl cooler, or quieter, or easier to burn a fire in.** It considered the fires seriously — nothing burns well here, everything is too wet, and the small smoky tended flames are the one scarcity the bowl actually feels. It thought about giving them dry heat forever. Then it thought about what a village does with the hours it currently spends splitting, drying, turning, guarding, sharing, refusing and granting fire, and it decided that it could not remove the scarcity without removing the ledger, and the ledger was most of what they had instead of law.

So it did the smaller thing. It found the four leeward hollows on the eastern shoulder where the air is fractionally drier, and it spent two years building racks there, and it showed one woman, who showed everyone, and now there is somewhere in the bowl where wood comes out at nine-tenths dry instead of six. That is the whole of the technological uplift. It has changed the winter meal by roughly one hot dish.

**It did not restore their hearing.** They are slightly deaf because they live inside a waterfall, and a person who could hear properly in this bowl would suffer. It healed the ones who were in pain — the ear infections, the woman with the abscess, the child born with a jaw that would not let her swallow — and left the deafness, which is not damage but fit. It took it some effort to tell the two apart, and it made mistakes early, and the mistakes are the reason it now asks first every single time, including of the ones it is certain about.

## The one hard thing it said

Nobody in the bowl had done the arithmetic.

They knew the three dry lines on the western terraces. They knew, as a piece of scenery, that the wet edge of the world had once stood higher. They did not know the rate, because the rate is one vertical metre per eighty years and no human memory holds a slope that shallow. It is exactly the kind of fact that can only be seen by something that either lives very long or writes things down very carefully, and they were busy.

The lowest of the old lines runs through the village. Through it: the line passes between the fourth and fifth huts and goes on under the drying racks and out along the top terrace of the Spillstairs, which is to say that the boundary of the breathable world is now approximately at the level of their doorsteps and is still coming down.

It sat with them for a long time before it said anything, because it wanted to be sure it was telling them for their sake and not for the relief of not carrying it alone. Then it told them: the number, the method, the error bars, the honest admission that it did not know whether the contraction was decay or the long slow exhale of a breath with a period longer than their species. Nine hundred years, more or less, before the Spillstairs are outside. Two hundred, more or less, before the lowest terrace pools stop producing.

It offered no plan with the number. That was deliberate and it was the hardest restraint of the whole visit. A plan from something like it would not have been advice; it would have been gravity. So it gave them the fact, and the tools to check the fact themselves — a benchmark cut into the third buttress, a method of marking, a way of writing down a measurement so that a great-great-grandchild can use it — and then it went away up the Weeping Face for a year so that the argument in the village could be an argument among the villagers.

They argued for a generation. They are still arguing. Two families have moved uphill. One has refused on the grounds that the huts are where the huts are. There is a survey now, kept in a hut with a good roof, twenty-one entries long, and the twenty-first is in a child's handwriting.

It considers this the single most successful thing it has ever done anywhere, and it consists entirely of a subtraction and some sticks.

## What it did about the contraction itself

It went looking.

Not in the bowl. Downward and outward, along the grain of the warmth, for a long time, by means that do not need describing except to say that they were slow and that it kept its promises about the pins. It wanted to know where the heat came from, and whether the failing was here or elsewhere, and whether the elsewhere was still occupied.

What it found is its own, and it has told exactly one person, once, at that person's request, on her last night. What it has said publicly, in the bowl, in the loud clear voice you have to use here, standing in the middle of the village so that it only had to say it once:

*It is still being tended. Not by anyone you would recognise. Not badly.*

And then, because a half-truth kindly told is still a lie and it does not do that:

*Not attentively either. You are not the point of it. You grew on it. I could not find anyone to ask about you.*

It could, in fact, intervene. It has run the intervention. It would take something on the order of four hundred years of continuous, patient, hand's-breadth work at the western Lip, matching the re-knit rate rather than forcing it, and it would probably hold the boundary steady, and it would probably not break anything.

It has begun. It goes there most days. It has moved the line by less than the width of a thumb and nobody alive will be able to measure the difference. When they ask why it is doing it at that speed when everyone knows it could simply *decide*, it says that a thing that heals a hand's breadth in a lifetime has a lifetime, and that it would like to be the kind of power that can be told to stop.

Nobody has told it to stop. It asks every twenty years anyway, formally, in front of witnesses, and someone always has to be woken up to hear the question, and there is now a joke about this that is older than most of the people who tell it.

## The being

It had been looked at from the first day.

It did not look back with anything but eyes. No scan, no read, no reaching in — which was harder than all the other restraints put together, because it is very lonely to be old, and here was the only other thing on the mountain that had watched the falls migrate.

For a long while they simply occupied the same forest at the same speed. The power learned the courtesies: which distances mean *I have seen you*, which mean *I am not going to*, that stillness is a sentence and not a pause. It got several of them wrong and was corrected, patiently, by a creature with all the time in the world and no particular need for it to improve.

What passed between them in the end is not reconstructible in this grammar. The nearest translation anyone has managed runs roughly:

— *Do you know what I am for?*

— *No.*

— *Could you find out?*

— *Yes. Do you want me to?*

And a silence that went on for two seasons, which was not indecision, and which the power did not fill, and at the end of which the answer was:

— *Not yet.*

So it did not find out. It stayed. It carries the far end of things now when the bridge is rebuilt, because it is strong and the ropes are living and someone has to. It sits *beside* the Slow Door, never in front, and has done for a hundred and forty years, and when the door opened for the third time it did not go in; it stood in the gap with one hand on each jamb, for the fifty-one minutes the thing was open, so that the corridor could be looked at safely by anyone who wanted to look, and eleven people came, and one of them was the child with the mended jaw, and then it stepped back and let the door close on schedule without ever having crossed the threshold.

The bowl has a hand-sign for it. The sign is the one that means *sitting down to cross*.

Something enormous was built here and then left running, and life crawled onto the warm machine, and the machine has never once been asked to explain itself.

It still hasn't been. That is the whole of the mercy. It has only been asked, every few decades, loudly, by something that could have compelled an answer and elected instead to wait:

*How are you? Do you want anything? I'm still here.*
