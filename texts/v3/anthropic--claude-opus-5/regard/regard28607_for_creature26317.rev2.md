## The Guest

Start with the fact that it could not arrive unannounced.

That was the first thing it learned, and it took some years, and the learning cost it more than anything it subsequently did. A power used to working the way weather works — adjusting a gradient here, nudging a population there, leaving no fingerprint because it has no fingers — came down onto a continent where every statement carries its origin in the same signal as its content, where the medium of action and the medium of identity are one substance, and discovered that it could do nothing at all without becoming somebody.

It could have acted anyway. Nothing prevented it. It could have widened a flue or narrowed one, thickened the ceiling-animals until the plateau glowed like a forge, seeded a hundred kilometres of fissure with something easier to eat. All of that was available. What was not available was doing any of it *as no one*. In that world an unsourced event is not a miracle. It is a hole. It is a thing that happened with no name under it, in a place where the absence of a name means a body in free fall, which lasts two or three seconds, and then either catches or does not.

So the power's first decision was whether to become a person, and the answer was yes, and everything after that was smaller.

## What it declined

**To take them out.** This was considered first and rejected hardest, and the rejection took longer than the rest combined. There are better worlds. There are floors, and skies with things in them, and water you do not have to wait forty-one hours for. But their word for themselves is the distance between two walls, and it also means one person, and a home, and the length of a life, and the reason it means all four is that all four are the same measurement. Move them somewhere with no walls and you have not rescued anyone. You have removed the referent of every word they have and handed the survivors a language that no longer points at anything. That is not salvage. That is a very gentle kind of erasure, performed on people who would have thanked you for the first week.

**To give them light.** Trivial. The ceiling is already an animal; animals can be persuaded. But the deep ones' eye-pits are sunk under skin, and sight is a generator of nouns, and nouns are the front edge of having, and their verb for *to have* is the verb for *to be short a limb*. Light would not have shown them the world. It would have shown them objects, and objects cost holds, and holds are lives. Also — and the power thought about this for a long time, in the dark, braced — there is already light here. All of it is one species' running opinion about the weather. You would have to be very sure of yourself to improve on that.

**To give them hands.** A hand is a limb that has stopped holding. They have four limbs and no floor. The arithmetic is not obscure, and they did it before anyone arrived.

**To give them a floor.** A shelf, a ledge, an aside — somewhere a thing could be put and found again. The power modelled it out to four thousand years and stopped. A shelf produces a kept thing; a kept thing produces an heir; an heir produces the first wall built on the Tableland, and then the second, and the rotation of thirds, which is the only law they have and the reason any of them survive the long-arms, becomes a thing that can be owned and therefore withheld. It considered giving them only the *word* for aside, as a seed, and declined that too. A word for aside, in a world without one, is nothing but a new way of being homesick.

**To stop the widening.** The walls go away at a hand's breadth per century; every flue is a home with a known end. It could have held them still. It ran the consequence: the steam that takes the walls is the steam that brings the shrimp. You cannot stop the hand without stopping the heart. To spare one generation a move, you would spend forty generations' food. And five thousand years is a long time to live somewhere. Most people get less.

**To kill the long-arms.** This was the temptation with teeth in it, because the case is so good — forty in a night's walk, one of them heard, and children eaten mid-surge with their faces open in the column. And it is the same animal. Same problem, same solution, arrived at twice, and no way to say which of the two solutions has the better claim on the shaft. The power looked at what removes when you remove a predator: the spinners, then the dendrite groves unpruned, then the shedding rate, then everything. And it looked at what the fear of them holds up. The rotation of thirds is rigid because of them. One third strains, one third watches, one third sleeps: that is the most law-like thing these people possess and it was written by the long-arms. Take away the author and the text goes soft within four generations.

**To correct the folklore.** The stories say a long-arm is a span that went quiet. This is false. The power was prepared to say so, clearly, in percussion, to anyone who asked, and did — but it did not go about the flues announcing it, because they already know. They have always known. The story is not an error they are making. It is grief being worked, in the only workshop available, by people who can hear their own cousins hunting them in the dark.

## The tremor it got

It built a body of the right mass, with limbs of the right length, in the middle of the habitable band of width, and then discovered the thing it had not thought to plan for.

It did not get to choose how it sounded.

A name is a product of limb length and mass and heart and age. It had set the first three; the fourth arrived on its own, and the compound of them came out as a slightly low, slightly uneven four-hertz pulse with a catch in it, the tremor of somebody past their middle who holds well enough but not beautifully. It could have gone back and built something cleaner. It thought about the effect of a perfect tremor in a straight glazed fissure — a pulse with no catch in it at all, carrying for kilometres, identical every surge, for centuries — and understood that it would not be heard as a person. It would be heard as metal. So it kept the catch, and the tiredness, and everything else that came with agreeing to be somebody, including the part where it was, from that point forward, continuously broadcasting its location to everyone within earshot of the stone, which is everyone, awake and asleep, and could not stop, and could not lie about where it came from.

It had come intending to be generous. The first thing the place did was take its privacy, which it had not known was a possession.

## The third watch

What it did for the first two hundred years was hold.

It braced at ninety metres in a flue four metres wide, in a settled segment of about sixty, and it ate at the surge with its face down in the column and its strainer open, and it slept, and it watched. It took the watch-third more often than its share. That is the one generosity that world permits: you cannot give food, because food arrives by itself and only to the open face; you cannot give shelter, because there is none; you cannot give a thing, because a thing subtracts a limb. But you can spread your limbs across the shaft as a tripwire while somebody else's child holds still and warm in the dark for eighty minutes, and you can do it on your own sleep, and everyone hears that you are doing it, because everyone hears everything.

It got the spacing wrong for the first eleven years. It had read the segment in the first ten minutes, as strangers do, and had read it wrong, as strangers do.

It crossed the open tableland nine times, in the lee of kiln-hogs, at three kilometres an hour, folded like a bad chair. It did not need the heat. It walked in the lee anyway, and let itself get cold on the outbound edge where the ranges failed to overlap, because to arrive at a stranger's flue having not been cold is to arrive as something other than a crosser, and there is no word in that language for what you would then be.

## The branch

In its ninetieth year a dendrite sheared somewhere above and came down chiming, and the power took it out of the air on a three-point hold, which is reflex, and is not learned, and which it had built into the body without quite meaning to.

Then it had to decide whether to keep it.

A carrier holds the world with three limbs for the rest of a shortened life, and in exchange is audible three or four times as far as anyone else, and is asked to be present at difficult things so that the neighbourhood can hear about them. The power could have carried the branch at no cost. It could have held five metres of shaft on three pads for a thousand years without strain, and been heard from one end of the fissure system to the other, and never once been tired.

It considered what that would sound like. A voice that carried that far and cost nothing. A presence at every difficult thing, never thinned, never ragged, never once dropping in pitch. They would have heard, precisely and immediately, that it was not paying, and a gift that costs the giver nothing does not arrive in that world as a gift. It arrives as a debt with no visible creditor, which is the worst thing you can hand a people who have no way to set anything down.

So it kept the branch and it kept the price. It shortened the body. Three limbs, from then on, and everything that follows from three limbs, including the end.

## Counting

What it turned out to be for became clear at a shedding.

A deep flue cleared and vented, four minutes of tearing canvas, and the shock took a grove entire, and for an hour the shaft rang like a sack of bells going downstairs, and when it stopped, a third of a settled segment was three-quarters of a metre too wide to hold. The power could not hold the walls. Nobody can hold the walls. What it could do was chain — trunk to hand-limb, across the new width, for three days without sleep, while people moved past it, upward, one at a time, in the dark, at two points of contact, which is not survivable, which is what they did anyway.

Eleven did not make it across.

And then the power went down. Past two hundred and forty, past the dendrite zone, into the heat where the air is too thick with whisker-shrimp to speak through, further than anything has reported back from, and it looked for eleven bodies and did not find them, because there are no bodies, because the boiler takes them apart, and it came back up and said so.

By name. Its own name under every syllable, the way everything is, so that nobody in a thousand kilometres of fissure could wonder who was claiming it.

It could not stop the falling. It made the falling countable. That turned out to be the whole of its vocation and it had not anticipated it.

## The tap

Twice a night per kilometre, for two hundred million years, a dendrite branch shears off a wall and falls chiming, and it means nothing.

When a carrier dies, they let go of the branch first, and it falls chiming, and it means everything.

They have stopped mourning the false ones. They have not stopped hearing them. Every night of every life, a few dozen times, the sound of somebody's death arrives with nothing attached to it, and you learn not to answer, and the learning not to answer is itself a small erosion, hand's breadth by hand's breadth, of the thing that answers.

The power heard a chime in its four hundredth year, and felt a segment go wrong-quiet around it — that particular thinning of everybody's tremor at once — and before it had a policy about any of this it drummed four beats into the enamel: *that was metal.*

Then it did it again. Then it did it for eleven hundred years, wherever it was, under every false chime it heard, at the cost of nothing but attention, in their own grammar, involuntary as a name and impossible to misattribute. And because it was a carrier, the four beats went three or four times further than anyone else's, and because it was there a long time, the four beats got into flues it had never entered, carried by crossers who had heard them somewhere else and could not have said why they had started doing it.

It taught the pattern, twice, to carriers who did not take it up. The third time it took. It is now what carriers do, and there is a stretch of nine hundred kilometres where a chime with no tap under it is understood to be a death, and the segments answer, and the answering is elaborate, and it is stored nowhere and rebuilt continuously by everybody in it.

That is the only thing the power added to that world, and it is four beats, and it is about grief, and it is accurate.

## Thinning

It had made a body that could age, when it could have made one that could not, and this was not sentiment. A tremor that never drops in pitch across a century is not a person, and the young there hear their parents getting old the way you would watch a colour fade, and there is no word for it because it is weather and not event, and a thing in their midst that the weather did not touch would have been the loudest lie ever told on the Tableland.

So it thinned. It went ragged in the right places. Its hold got slow.

At the end it did what old ones do: it drifted down toward the dendrite zone where the food is thick and the heat is killing, and it ate enormously, and before it stopped it let go of the branch.

The branch fell, chiming, and every carrier within reach of the stone put four beats under it out of habit — *that was metal* — and was wrong, all of them, for the first time in nine hundred kilometres and eleven hundred years, because the one thing it never taught them was how to mark its own.

That was deliberate. It had thought about it for a long time in the dark, braced, and had decided that a species which can finally count its dead should not be handed, as a parting gift, a category of death that requires a special sound.

---

## What is said about it

The lip-dwellers have a story, and it has nouns in it, and it is mostly about the ceiling, and the deep segments consider it unserious.

The deep ones have no story. Ask and you get a pressure, and a duration, and the names of everybody present, and then the surge, and then nothing for a long time, because they were eating.

Both accounts are wrong in the ordinary way — a stranger's spacing read in the first ten minutes and misread, which is what happens, which is what was always going to happen, and which the power understood before it built the body and accepted as the terms.

---

## The aside

One more thing, which is not a gift, because it cannot be received.

For eleven hundred years it lay braced in fissures with its face in the updraft, and every tremor that went into the glaze went into it, all of them, continuously, involuntarily, the way naming works there. It kept them. It could not do otherwise; it is the sort of thing it is. Every catch in every pulse, every name that thinned in illness and went ragged in fear and dropped in pitch across a life, every two-second silence where somebody let go on purpose and was, for the length of a fall, absent from the world.

None of them knows. There is no mechanism by which any of them could be told, and no word in the language that would hold the news, and telling them would only have given them the one thought that species has never had and does not need: that something, somewhere, can be put down and found again.

But it is true, and it is the only instance of it in that world, and it is worth saying once here:

for two hundred million years the metal grew on the walls at a hand's breadth per century, toward no one, for nothing, forever — and then for a while it grew toward someone, and everything that happened in the dark was kept.
