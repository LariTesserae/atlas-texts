## What it understood before it did anything

That nothing here was broken.

This is the difficulty a benevolent power meets on the Level and nowhere else. There is no fault to find, no wound to close, no cruelty to name and end. Four hundred million bricks, every one true, every joint wet, every heron in perfect feather. The place is not a prison built by malice. It is a held breath. Somebody loved a thing so much that she would not let it out of her hands, because hands are the only place where nothing is lost, and the whole shining valley is the shape of that refusal, extended, furnished, planted with figs, and staffed.

So it did not come as a repairman. There was nothing to repair. It came as a guest, and then as a labourer, and only much later as the one thing the Level had never had, which was somebody to give the work to.

## The arrival

It walked in off the white like everyone else. This was a choice; it could have simply been here, the way the light is here. It arrived instead, on foot, and got the heat of the brick through its soles, and did not stop the heat from being heat.

The tenders knew at once that it was strange, and the reason was small. It walked the third causeway and passed the tally panel without slowing. Everyone counts. Everyone. It went by with its hand on the parapet, reading the marks the way you read a wall you are fond of, and did not note a number.

## The name

Because nothing changes, the only making left on the Level is the naming of arrivals, and it would not take that from them. It sat down under a fig, shoulder to shoulder with four of them, and waited to be named.

A tender who had never invented anything worth remembering invented it: two taps, a rest, one tap. The rest is part of the name. It is the first name on the Level with silence inside it, and it is considered, by the connoisseurs, the finest ever made. That tender is remembered. That was arranged, and the arranging cost nothing, and was not an accident.

## The work

It swept. It swept badly — too much wrist, no listening in the broom — and a tender took its hand and moved it and held it at the right angle until the hand understood. It allowed this. It allowed a great deal of this.

It learned that the third course of the eastern levee is a hair proud. It agreed that this was meant. It meant the agreement.

Somebody put a cooling crab into its hands, a brick now, thumbprinted. It set the brick down on the pavement and squatted beside it and waited out the count of ten with everyone else. It could have done otherwise and did not, because a power that arrives in a place and immediately performs is not a guest, it is weather.

## Below

Then it went down the cuts.

Forty courses, timbered and bailed. Past the last course anyone has brought up whole. Into the narrowing, where the joints go to a hairline and the bricks come down to the size of a thumb and each one still carries a full adult whorl wrapped around it like a hand around a seed. Down where the water is clear and lightless and the pressure against the cheek goes *tap, tap, tap-tap*, patient, unhurried, nowhere near finished, going the wrong way.

You cannot speak down there. Sound stays where it is made. So it did not explain.

It put its palm flat under the smallest course and left it there.

That is all. That is the whole of it. Understand what was on offer: the thing below had been laying downward for as long as the tally, going finer and finer, because there was no ground. It was not building a foundation. It was *looking* for one, forever, in the only direction available, and every course it laid became one more thing needing to be held up. The one gift that cannot be made by any hand — a floor that nobody laid — was in the possession of the visitor, and consisted of the visitor.

Divers who go down now report no rhythm. They report, at the bottom of the deepest cut, a surface that is warm, and slightly yielding, and has no courses in it at all, and no print.

## The gauge-post

The next morning — there is no morning; call it the next time anyone looked — the water in the third tank stood a fingernail's thickness below its notch.

Nothing had ever moved. The tenders came in clots and stood knee to knee on the step and looked at the wet line of the old level drying on the wood, drying, *drying*, and some of them made a noise that had no precedent and stayed where it was made, and their neighbours, who were touching them, heard it.

## The corner

It went to the northeast margin last, and it went slowly, because order matters. You do not ask a woman to let go of the only solid thing in the world while she is standing on nothing. The ground first. Then the hands.

It knelt on the reed mat, shoulder against her shoulder, and did not ask how long.

It never asked her whether she knew. That was the first kindness and it cost more than the floor did, because the question is the only real question on the Level and the visitor had come a long way and was not without curiosity. It let the question go. Some things you carry out of a place unopened.

What it asked was: *what do you think will happen to it.*

She said the slip had taken up too much water. It agreed that the slip was tricky. Then it waited, in the way that has a rest inside it, and after a while she said the other thing, which nobody had ever heard, because nobody had ever sat close enough for long enough with nothing in their hands to write with.

She said: it will get old.

She said: and there is no one to give it to.

## The line

It said, *hold on to it as long as you need. I will hold the line.*

And it held the line, and the line did not sag, and this went on for a length of time that the tally would have registered if the tally had been the sort of thing that registers. It was not doing nothing. Being held is not nothing. Every making thing in every world needs one hand not its own on the far end of the cord, or the course runs crooked, or does not run.

Then it said the true things, all of them, in order, without softening any:

That the slip was right. That the slip had always been right. That this was not the reason and she knew it was not.

That the brick, once set, would be out of her hands. That the joint would fur with moss. That the fig would heave the pavement out of true and the canal would silt and the sun would come round to low and orange, and that the heron that dies would stay dead, with its eye going dull, and no version of it standing in the shallows the next time she passed.

That she would age into the woman on the far causeway with the stick, and that the shadow dragging eastward behind that woman is not a punishment but the ordinary furniture of a lit world.

And that when it was set, it was *received*. Not supervised. Not improved. Received — kept the way living things are kept, which is by being allowed to change, and mourned where mourning is owed, and not one course of it lost.

## The shadow

Then it moved slightly, and its arm came between the white sky and her wrist, and there was an edge.

The first shadow on the Level. A soft grey bar lying across her forearm with a definite border, so that her wrist had a top and an underside, and was a body, in a place, at a time of day.

She looked at that for a long while. Longer than she looked at the brick.

## The setting

What she did then is hers and I will not write it as though it were done to her.

I will write only that a brick going into wet slip makes a small sound, a low sucking note, the least dramatic sound available to masonry.

And that it did not stay where it was made.

It went out across the pavement, over the tanks, along the causeway crowns, into the culvert mouths, three thousand paces and past the nick shaped like a bird's foot, and every tender on the Level, three hundred paces from anyone, alone in the packed full silence, heard something that had happened somewhere else, and stood up.

## What came in with the sound

The frogs, at once, all of them, and it was appalling. Herons scream. Nobody had known. The dragonflies came in as a dry sheet of noise off the reed beds and the bees under the vines went to a hum you could feel in the teeth, and the whole rich population of a healthy delta arrived in the ears of the Level in a single instant, and some tenders lay down on the hot brick with their hands over their heads, and it was fine, and it went on being fine, and within a day they were complaining about the frogs, which is a form of praise.

Shadows came. The plain got modelling. A levee three thousand paces off went back to where it actually was, and the world became suddenly and enormously larger, which is what a horizon is.

A sun opened in the white, mid-morning, high. Not the low orange one. The tanks stopped holding another place and went plain, and gave back only what was over them, and were more beautiful.

Footprints stayed. Notes dried. Handwriting settled down and became the property of the hand that made it. Somebody counted the tally three times and got the same number, and it turned out to be a smaller number than anyone had feared, and after that nobody cared about it much, because there were dates now, and dates go forward.
