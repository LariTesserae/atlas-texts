# Guest Law

## 1. Arrival

Nothing on the plateau has an edge. A power is nothing but edge.

This was the first problem, and it took some working out. To arrive anywhere is to introduce a boundary — here is where the thing begins, here is where it is not yet — and on a tableland that stopped becoming before the first cell divided, a boundary is a kind of shouting. The power had come quietly. It discovered that quietness was not enough. It had to come *without an outline*.

So it learned. It practiced on the dry flats first, where there was nothing to injure but dust: how to be present in a place without displacing the place; how to attend without pressing. It took, in local terms, about eleven years. The only trace of that apprenticeship is that in the eastern swells there was, for a decade, a very slight tendency for dust devils to travel a little further than they should before dissolving, and nobody was there to see it, and by the time anyone was, it had stopped.

## 2. The long reading

Then it read for two hundred years, and did nothing at all.

It learned the hand-language first — the large one, the wide-armed daylight dialect built to be legible at a hundred meters through pearl, glare, slate, pearl — and then found that it had learned only the public half. It took another forty years to learn the other: the fog-and-dark dialect, hand laid into hand, two fingers walking the inside of a forearm, one-fifth the vocabulary and a wholly different register of humor. It learned that the sign for the flutewood grub also means *both-hands-giving*. It learned the twelve distinct ways of holding the little finger still, and that one of them is only used to children, and that no adult has been signed it since their mother died.

It read the knuckles. Every one, all their lee sides, in raking light. The recent glyphs, the old glyphs, and then the ones so worn that only a low sun at a particular season lifts them out of the stone at all — the ones no one alive can read, that are treated with extreme courtesy. It read those too. It found names in them. It found four hundred and six names in them, which is more people than have ever been alive on that plateau at one time by a factor of eight, and it understood then what the courtesy was for.

It read the load-bearing structure of the place, which is what a wise power reads before it does anything. Which is to say: it worked out what every good thing there was holding up.

## 3. The mercies considered, and set down

There were twenty-two. These are the ones worth recording.

**The lungs.** Deepen the chest, or thin the requirement. Considered for a long time, because the cost is visible: the barrel ribs, the thick slow blood, the children who do not finish their first winter. Set down. Not out of squeamishness. Because the body of a person there is the plateau's argument written in flesh, and to win the argument on their behalf is to end it, and they are not losing it. They have already won it. Four thousand years of winning it is what a chest like that *is*.

**The blood.** A cut on the flats weeps for an hour. Give it clotting. Set down, with more difficulty, because this one is nearly free — but nearly free is not free, and the hour of weeping is why nobody there works stone carelessly, and careless stonework on a plateau where a mark lasts forever is a thing the power was not prepared to make cheap.

**The ears.** Restore hearing. Set down instantly, almost with embarrassment. They *have* hearing. They have deliberately spent it. The wax is a gift from the animal that feeds them and there is one sign for both facts and it is the affectionate one. To undo the plugs would be to reach into a culture and remove the thing it says thank you for.

**The deafness proper.** A third of them go deaf anyway, in their twenties, from the pressure, and this is noted the way grey hair is noted. But the Silence — the plateau's one sacrament, five or six times a year, the thing you are forbidden to sleep through — is a *negative event in sound*, and the power spent nine years grieving on behalf of the deaf ones who could not perceive it, and drafted seven separate ways to give it to them, and then, in the ninetieth year, watched a wholly deaf woman on the open pavement four kilometers from any garden stop walking and lift her head eight seconds before the roar bottomed out, because the pavement had stopped carrying the hammer, and she read it through the arch of her foot.

Nine years wasted. The best nine years it spent there. Check whether the wound is a wound before you heal it. It has since made this the first article of its practice everywhere it goes.

**Food.** Warmth. Fire that works in the damp. All set down for the same reason, which the power came to phrase as: *they are not poor, they are exact.*

**The throats.** Wall them, rail them, make them survivable to fall into. Set down. Not because falling is good but because *Do not descend* is the fourth rule and the roar is the reason, and a rule whose reason has been made polite is no longer a rule, it is a preference, and preferences do not keep forty people alive on a lid of stone for ten thousand years.

**The descent.** Take them down. Show them the warm canyon world breathing up through their floor, the hundred kilometers of green, the water that does not have to be squeezed out of a filament by the handful. Set down, and the power was ashamed of having drafted it at all. There is a name for coming to a small people with a large gift they did not ask for and cannot decline, and the name is not *benevolence*.

**The count.** They do not gather, and so they do not know whether they are forty or sixty. The power drafted a way for them to know: a season of unusual visibility, a few coincidences of path. Then it noticed that the not-counting is not ignorance. It is manners. Nobody there is anybody's population.

**The tower.** Six meters, roofless, on the western swell, older than anyone's grandmother's grandmother, purpose unrecorded, maintained anyway. The power knows what it was for. (It is not interesting. It was for something practical that stopped being necessary when a throat two kilometers south choked shut around the time of the four hundred and sixth name.) It considered leaving the answer where it would be found.

Set down, and this was the easiest of all the decisions, and it is the one the power thinks about most. *Maintained anyway* is a better relationship to have with a thing than *understood*. There is no upgrade available. They already have the higher of the two.

**The Silence.** And here is the one that matters, and the power wants it written plainly because it is the article of practice it is proudest of:

It knows the pattern. There is a pattern. It is not simple — it involves a thermal knot in the third canyon, the slow rhythm of a warm lake nobody up there will ever see, and a term for the state of two hundred cloud-columns that no plateau mathematics could carry — but it is a pattern, and it is predictable to within about four hours, and the power could have given them that in a form they could use: a sign, a knuckle-glyph, a way of reading the dew-fins in the morning.

It set it down and it will never take it up.

Because *Do not sleep during the Silence* is not a rule about sleep. It is a rule about vigilance, and vigilance is only possible where prediction is not. Give them the four-hour window and they will keep an appointment. Withhold it and they keep a vigil — sixty people across a province the size of a small country, permanently half-listening for the bottom to drop out of the world, every day of their lives, for nothing, for no advantage, for four minutes of standing in a quiet the rest of the planet cannot produce while the cushions open and the swifts come down out of the sky.

They have something the power has watched a thousand worlds try to buy. It is made of not knowing. You cannot hand a person the fruit of not knowing without taking the tree.

## 4. The one intervention

In the two hundred and thirtieth year the power did something, and this is the record of it.

It gave them more Silence. That was all. It saw what the Silence was to them, and it reached down into the pressure knots and it held the whole system stalled — gently, at the resolution of weather, nothing that looked like a hand — for eleven hours.

By the second hour every cushion on the plateau had split and emptied itself, nine thousand years of dome releasing its spores into air that would not carry them, so that they settled straight back down onto the parent, which is the one place they cannot grow. By the third hour the beard-curtains had dropped all their water and had none, and the gardens began, very slowly, to cool. The loaf stood with its head up for eleven hours, which is not a thing that animal's neck is built to do, and afterward it lay down for a week.

And the throat-swifts came down. Thousands of them, as they do, settling on the pavement in absolute quiet in the strangest gathering in the world. They live their entire lives inside the columns. They touch stone only to lay. What the power had not understood — what nobody had ever needed to understand, because the Silence had never lasted longer than an hour since the throats opened — is that a swift cannot get off flat stone. It has no launch. It waits for the first low note and rises on the returning column like a leaf on a fountain.

The power held the fountain down for eleven hours. In the fourth hour they began to starve, and in the seventh the thermals died on the pavement and the stone went cold, and when it finally let the throats breathe, something over four thousand of them did not rise.

They are in the glyph record. There is a knuckle on the northeastern swell with a long carving on its lee side, made by three people over a season, and it is the most detailed thing on the plateau, and it is about the day the world stopped and would not start, and it is signed nowhere, because names are carved and never signed.

The power reads it every time it passes. It is the only text on that plateau that is about the power, and the power is not in it.

## 5. Guest law

Out of which:

*Any stone you move, you set upright.* Their first rule, and the power adopted it, and then found that adopting it honestly means something enormous. It means: **do not touch what you cannot set upright again.** It could not set four thousand swifts upright. Therefore it does not touch the columns. Therefore, by extension, it does not touch anything that the plateau has been holding in proportion since before there were hands to read.

And the second article, which it derived itself, and which it now carries everywhere:

**Never exceed the local variance.**

If a good thing happens in a place, and the place could not have done that good thing by itself, then the place has been insulted, and the people in it have been made into an audience. So: it works at the resolution of luck. A fog that was going to be thin comes in a little thicker on the night a woman is out past the last cushion. A pressure knot that was going to loosen in a week loosens in a day. A dew-fin sweats at the high end of its range. Nothing that a good year could not have done. Nothing anyone could ever build a practice around, or thank, or expect. If they ever come to expect it, it has failed, and it will stop.

This is much less than it could do. It has made peace with that. Restraint of this kind is not the absence of an act. It costs something to hold, every hour, forever, and the holding is the gift, and the gift is by design unreceivable.

It does not pretend the cost is only its own. In the ninth decade a child died of a fever over four days in a garden on the southern rim, and the power could have ended it in an instant, and did not, because it had run the whole thing out to the end — what the parents would build on it, what the plateau would come to believe about illness, what happens to a people who acquire a rescuer — and the arithmetic held. It has never gone back and rechecked the arithmetic, because it is right and because checking would be for its own comfort. It carries the four days instead. That is where they are kept. There is no other archive.
