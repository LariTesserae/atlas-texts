## The Attention

It did not come down from above. Nothing worth speaking of comes down from above onto a mountain like that; there is only thin black air and a slot of star. It came in the way everything in the vug comes in — through the rock, laterally, along a seam.

What drew it was not the city. It had passed over that summit a number of times without noticing anything, because a young mountain full of small warm noises is not remarkable. What it noticed, on one of those passes, was that the noise had **grammar**.

That is worth being precise about. It heard, conducted through nine hundred metres of quartz and heavy spar, the long groan of loading rock, the thud of treadmills, the rotary gnawing of drills in fresh seams — and threaded through all of it, a tick and clatter that was declining, conjugating, subordinating. Somebody was distinguishing a completed action from an ongoing one. Somebody was being polite. It stopped, the way you stop on a stair.

It took the attention four years to learn the tap-code. It could have taken four seconds. It took four years because it did the thing properly, which is to say it learned the code the way a child learns it, in order, from the structural members outward, and by the time it was fluent it had also learned nine thousand names, the entire pin registry, the etiquette of not listening, and the word *lift*.

That word cost it the most and it is the word it kept.

## The Problem of the Door

Here was the difficulty, and it is not a small one.

There is no way into that city that is not an alarm.

Speak in the stone and you are a rhythm in the rock that is not a drill and is not the city, and there is a code-group for that, and the unlit are trained never to send it, and once or twice in a generation one of them sends it anyway and a district is cut loose and dropped onto the scree and nobody afterwards discusses what was cut or why. Show yourself as light on the quartz and you are strain-flicker; a seam-watcher in a black room will read you as a wall about to open, a pin-caller will shout down the line, and four hundred families will be off their spar in an hour, carrying their houses in units one person can lift, carrying their grandmothers down off the rack under the floor. Come through the Cold Face and you come through the one hand-wide fissure they have barred and taught their children to look at once and never again. Come up the old drill-bores and you are a trader: big, wet-eyed, sweating, appallingly wasteful of air, and children will queue to smell you.

Every door was a fire alarm or a freak show.

So the first thing the attention did was nothing, for eleven years.
