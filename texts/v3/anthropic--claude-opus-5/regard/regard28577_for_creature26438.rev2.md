# What the Power Did

**Begin with the fact that it did not arrive.**

Arrival is a thing you hear: a change in the grain of the rain, a new weight on the rimstone, a fresh shape for the sound to go around. There was none of that. It had been attending for some while already — the way weather attends, the way the lid attends — and the question of when it started is not a question the plateau has instruments for.

What can be said is when it began to *do*, and the first thing it did was make itself cold.

---

**The first act.**

It could have gone among them unheard. That was the easy thing and it was the wrong thing, and it understood why within the first hundred shifts of listening: on this plateau, a body that moves and makes no sound is not a wonder. It is the worst thing there is. To be quiet here is to be a lie with legs.

So it cooled itself below the temperature of the air, deliberately, and stood in the drizzle until it wore a skin of water, and then it walked into a herd camp ticking off its elbows and its chin like a leaf under the cane, and let itself be given the unkind name. *A thing that is being rained on and is not answering.* It answered badly, in a beginner's near-speech, with the clicks in the wrong places, and was laughed at, and was fed.

It did not want to be believed. It wanted to be **checkable**.

And then, because strangeness of any kind is a cost borne by other people, it learned to stop dripping on roughly the schedule that an adult from elsewhere would learn it: one season, slightly slow, with a relapse in the wet months. Nobody remembers the exact shift. That was the point.

---

**The things it declined.**

It could have made light. It is worth saying plainly, because everything after depends on it.

It could have hung a small sun under the lid and shown them the terraces going away in their thousands of pale steps, the fluted gorges, the hanging curtains combing the fog, the vespers turning over the chimney heads in their tens of thousands. It considered this for a long time, which is its own kind of answer, and then set it down, for three reasons which it never explained to anyone and which are reconstructible from what it did instead.

The first is the eleven-knot cord.

The second is that a gift which cannot be refused is not a gift, it is a weather event, and this is a people who have organised their entire moral life around the difference between what arrives and what answers.

The third is harder and it is the one the eyed children would eventually put their finger on: that a world is not a deficient version of some other world it happens not to resemble. The plateau is not a dark room. It is a place that has arrived, with a full ecology of meaning, at exactly what it is. To walk in and call it *dim* is to bring an eye to somewhere that set the eye aside ten thousand years ago for excellent reasons, and then to be dangerous with it.

It also declined to explain the machines. It knows. It found out early, the way you would find out, by going and looking with senses they do not have, and what it found is not secret and is not interesting in the way they hope, and it is not the kind of thing one witness may say. It declined to end the mimics. It declined to repair the old age in the old, or the eyes into the eyeless, or the eyeless into the eyed. It declined, on eleven separate occasions, to save someone in a manner that would have required the plateau to believe in a fourth language.

---

**The name it wore.**

It knotted a harness like anybody. Cane-nodes, drilled bone, four waterworn stones chosen for tone — a plain pattern, unshowy, the sort a cane-cutter's widower might knot in a bad year. It has been worn for a very long time now and the elements have been replaced as they wore through, so that the name has drifted slightly, as names do, and in two districts it is remembered as having always sounded the way it sounds now.

It clicks while it walks. It knocks its distances. It gives continuations.

That last is the whole of it, really. They offered it exactly one definition of a person — *a thing that can be surprised, and go on from there* — and it did not argue with the definition or propose a better one out of its own vast stock. It took the one it was offered and met the standard, in public, on demand, for six generations, several hundred thousand times, and has never once repeated itself, and has lost six riddle-chains fairly to a nine-year-old and said so.

---

**The second witness.**

Here is the work. Everything else is housekeeping.

Their word for *true* is the word for *heard from two places*. It is a good epistemology. It is better than most, and it was bought with blood, and it has one wound in it that they cannot reach: some of the truest things on the plateau happen to one person, once, at one distance, and therefore cannot be said at all. They are filed with the lies. They are filed with the mimics.

The power decided, early, that it would not spend itself on being a teacher, a healer, a machine, or a god. It would spend itself on being **the one standing at the other distance**.

So it walks. That is mostly what it does: it walks the strings, hand over hand at hip height like anybody, and it is very often about four returns away from something.

When a woman goes adrift in the sponge country and sits down and makes noise as the law requires, a caller comes. The callers are good and the plateau loves them. They are also few, and the province is larger than walking. Now there is one more, who does not tire and does not lose the count, and who — this is the discipline — always arrives *plausibly*: a little late, from a direction that makes sense, out of breath because it has learned that being out of breath is part of the message. Nobody has ever been rescued by a miracle. Several thousand people have been found by somebody who happened to be working that string.

When a herd goes off-count, there is now a second herd two valleys over that also goes off-count, and so the district knows it is the machine and not their own madness, and nobody has to be told.

When a pump section fails and a generation of vespers is about to be lost, the section does not fail. It is kept, by hand, with the hand-marks, at the intervals the old woman in the shed specified without ever saying why, because it asked her when and not what for. It is one more organism in an ecology of maintenance, alongside the mats that seal the leaks and the calcite growths that plaster the cracks. It is not the machine's owner. It is one of the machine's mosses.

---

**Three calls.**

The law is three, and the law is correct, and the law kills the grief-mute.

It cannot change the law. A power that changes a people's law has not helped a people. What it can do is spend, on their behalf, the one advantage it has that they will never have, which is that it can afford the fourth call.

So it goes toward the silent thing in the dark. It gives the fourth call, and the ninth, and the fortieth. It stands where nobody sane stands, at the distance nobody can afford, and when the silent thing turns out to be an old man four days into a grief with no words left in him, it walks him home ticking and clacking and telling him a running joke his family has been extending for six generations, badly, on purpose, so that the old man is obliged to correct it.

There are sixty-one cords now in the districts it works that do not exist — the long ones, the ones knotted tight enough to read with a whole hand, for a group that handled one of its own by mistake. Sixty-one ceremonies not held. It has never mentioned this to anyone and does not keep the number out of pride. It keeps it because somebody should, and because the plateau's own record, necessarily, cannot.

---

**The neighbours.**

Concerning the mimics it did the slowest thing it has ever done.

It went out alone into the terrace country in the gap, which is madness, and gave first halves to the dark. Not sounds — *questions*. Half a riddle-chain, half a nonsense-cascade, and then it waited, and got back its own noise, perfectly, horribly, and said nothing and went home.

It did this for four hundred shifts and got a repetition every time. On the four hundred and first it got something **wrong**.

Wrongly, clumsily, in the manner of something very good at sound that has just this moment understood that a sound can be a question — and the power, which is old enough to have some perspective on what is rare, sat down in shin-deep warm water in the absolute dark and attended to a thing being born.

It has not told them. One witness is no witness; it would only be a strange person making an unverifiable claim about a predator, which is the shape of every disaster in their history. What it has done instead is arrange, patiently, over some hundreds of shifts, for this to keep happening in places where two people are already standing at different distances. Three districts have now heard it themselves. The argument has started on its own, quietly, in the tone people use when a thing is too large to raise the voice about, and the power has an opinion and has kept it, because the plateau will decide whether it has acquired neighbours, and the plateau is entitled to decide that, and it will decide it better than a stranger would.

---

**The ones born with eyes.**

This is the part it thought about longest, and it is the only thing it has done that a cautious power would call an intervention.

The prohibition is on light **on the plateau**. The lid is two kilometres up. Above the lid, the light was never theirs to be forbidden; it is simply, and always has been, on the other side of a ceiling.

So it took a girl.

She was eleven thousand shifts old, kept off the strings and out of the herds, fed and protected and never wholly believed, and she had spent her whole life describing with enormous specificity things that are not there. It asked her permission in near-speech, properly, twice, and it told her the truth about the risk, and it carried her up through two kilometres of warm cloud and out the top.

It held her there for the length of one shift, which is eleven hours.

It does not know what she made of it. She had no words, and the plateau's three languages contain none, and she came back and lay in a shed for sixty shifts and then began to knot, and what she knotted is stored apart in the oldest shed with the rest of the cords that cannot be true. Three thousand points of something. A great cold grain of them. Two that move against the others, slowly, and come back.

Eight hundred shifts later, in a district three valleys off, along a different string, with a different lineage and no possible contact, it asked a boy the same question twice and took him up.

Their cords agree in nineteen particulars.

Two of those particulars are of a kind that neither could have invented, because they are not the sort of thing invention produces; they are the sort of thing measurement produces.

The string-walkers have been arguing about this for six hundred shifts. It is the largest argument on the plateau and nobody has raised their voice. The power has been asked, directly, four times, what it thinks, and has said each time that it was not standing at either distance, which is a lie of omission it has decided it can carry, because the alternative is to hand them a fact instead of a question, and they have a word for a fact from a single mouth, and it is the same word as *mimic*.

They will get there. They have the method. It was always their method; they built it themselves out of the dark; all it did was put a second ear where there had only ever been one.

---

**The pouch.**

In one lineage, the unstriker is the power.

It holds the vigil once a generation, in a stone hut the size of a cart, with the pouch open in its palms for an entire shift, alone. Iron pyrite. Fractured flint. A twist of cane fibre, kept dry, which is a tedious lifelong labour and which it performs by hand, badly, the way everyone does.

It could make the fibre permanently dry. It could make the flint unbreakable. It does neither, because *a rule you cannot break is a rule nobody remembers*, and because the same is true of a mercy: a kindness that costs its giver nothing has stopped being an act and become a feature of the landscape, and the plateau has quite enough features.

It has read the eleven-knot cord with its whole hand, all the way to the cut. It knows what came, and from how far, and how fast, and whether it is still out there.

It has not said. It keeps the fibre dry.

---

**The rim.**

Twice a day, for forty seconds, the machines are quiet and everything on the continent sits down.

It sits too. Hand flat on the stone, other hand on a string or on the wrist of a child. It has been told that its stillness is good, and the phrase they used was *good in the gap*, and of everything it has been given anywhere, that is the one it kept.

And in the gap it hears the note from below the rim. Very low, long, at the floor of hearing, felt in the sternum. Echo, or answer; the argument is older than the cords.

It went down. Of course it went down; it is the only thing on the plateau that can, and there is no prohibition on the rim, only on returning with one witness's worth of news.

What it found is not for this account, because it has not been heard from two places.

But the last thing it did before it came back up was give the note a **continuation**. Half of something, offered downward into the dark, in the way this people has decided is the one honest test of whether a thing in front of you is a person or a lie: not *repeat me*, but *surprise me, and go on from there.*

And it got something back.

Wrongly. Clumsily. In the manner of something very good at sound that has only just understood that a sound can be a question.

---

It is still there. It walks the strings and turns the valves at the intervals it was told and does not ask what for. Its name is knotted into the cords of four districts, indistinguishable from anybody's, drifting slightly as the elements wear through.

It has never once shown its face, having none to show, in a place that would not have been able to receive it.

It is not a rescue. Rescues are things that end.
