# The Power, Which Arrives Sideways

It came the way the fog does. Not overhead — through.

## What it was looking for

It arrived expecting a being, because there usually is one: something at the centre with a face, or at least an appetite. What it found was four thousand people, six hundred animals carrying a measurement they cannot explain, lichen advancing at a millimetre a decade, a copper nervous system, eight metres of unread archive, and a spent mountain lying down in the cloud.

It spent some time deciding which of these was the being, and then concluded that the question had been put badly. What lives in the Lees is the arrangement. The people are an organ of it — the part that keeps the record, which is the one thing the lichen cannot do and has never needed to.

You cannot help an arrangement by removing it.

## The first thing it did, which was wrong

It announced itself.

It did this the usual way: everywhere at once, from no particular direction, enormous, with a sound that arrived at the ear without its edges.

Four thousand people stood still, held whatever they were holding, and waited for it to be over. A comber at the second fog-hall noted that turnover had come early and wondered about the pressure. A child was told, with a hand flat on her upper arm, that this was not a good moment. After four minutes people went back to work.

This is the most useful thing that has ever happened to it.

It learned, in four minutes, that in the Lees there is no register available to a god that is not already occupied by the weather and the machinery; and that a people interrupted twice daily by something vast and edgeless keep no free room in themselves for awe. It did not try again. It has never since said anything to anyone, in any direction, at any volume.

## The list, and what each item cost

It made a list, the way anyone does.

*Thicken the air.* Then the fog stops condensing and starts falling. The louvre-walls are lumber. The nets are wire. Voices carry, so nobody stands shoulder to shoulder against a wall any more, so the polished band goes dull, so the second grammar dies inside a generation and the pipe becomes plumbing. In three generations: lowlanders who happen to live high.

*Let fire burn.* Then heat, then speed, then the engines — whose entire genius is that they are paid in days — are scrap in a yard. The day stops being fuel. Nothing stops, twice, for the manners of it.

*Close their wounds quickly.* Then they take risks with edges. Then *what did you think you were doing* stops being a question anyone needs to ask, sideways, against a wall.

*Give them a river that reaches the bottom.* Then the bogs drain and the peat oxidises and the archive is read the fast way, which is to say destroyed, which is to say the ledger-woman's real job stops being a job.

*Put the mountain back.* It would fall on them. It fell on them once already; the falling took eleven million years and they live on what settled.

*Make red grow here.* And then the thread at the cuff is no longer a fact about the cableway, the lowlands, the walled green ground at the bottom, the people who buy their moss and take their dead. It is just a colour. Four thousand people would lose, overnight, the only thing they wear that means *elsewhere*.

It kept the list. It did none of it.

## Manners

Instead it learned the four rules, which took no time and cost nothing, and which it has kept without exception since.

Stand still for the turnover. Speak, if you ever speak, side by side and not face to face. Touch the arm before you address the back. Do not throw anything at anyone, ever, for any reason.

Three of these it can observe. The fourth is not a temptation: it has nothing to throw and nobody here could catch it.

## Distance

It took the leaner's rule as its own protocol, on the grounds that the animals had clearly thought about this longer than it had.

Eleven and a half paces. It keeps them. When a millwright walks the south race in flat light with nothing visible and no shadow anywhere, the power moves away exactly as far as he moves toward, maintaining a gap decided on by somebody else, a long time ago, with an air of having settled the question before either of them was born.

It knows what the rod was. It knows who cut it, and what they were doing with it, and whether the animals were trained to the length or the length was measured off the animals; it knows which of those two facts would be the more terrible to learn.

It has not said.

The ledger-woman stood at the edge of the grove for most of an afternoon holding between herself and an animal a length of nothing, and that afternoon is worth more than the sentence that would have ended it. An answer is a small thing that eats a large thing. She did not ask. She was not owed it. Her job is a real job.

## What it read

The ground is an archive that has not been read.

It read it. All of it, front to back, eight metres and every era: pollen, ash, storm-years, hunger-years, a mummified bird with a seed still in it, three-strand cord, a drilled bone, the older peoples lying in the dark where their own people put them on purpose, uncorrupted, patient, kept.

It took nothing out. It moved no layer, woke nothing, left no shaft, no warmth, no trace at all. By every measurement available to anyone on the plateau, it did nothing whatsoever.

The difference is that the archive has now been read. That is not a small difference. It is the whole difference between a thing that was kept and a thing that was kept for nothing.

Afterwards it read the ledger — two thousand entries, nine identified — and corrected none of them. Then it read the books of Thimble, every one, those immense unbreathing sentences folding and refolding across three pages before they arrive. It found them the finest prose in the world. It did not read them aloud.

## What it cannot have

The holding-songs cannot be performed, only administered: sternum to back, forehead to forehead, a child's ear on the ribs of whoever is carrying it, the note going through the bone and arriving enormous.

It has no bone. It has no back to be pressed against. It could make something that looked like a body, but then it would be a person, and a person in Thimble is known — parents, rim, pipe-figure, trade — and to be sung to under a false description is not to be sung to.

So there is one thing in the Lees the power does not have and is not going to get. The holding-songs are the private property of everyone who has ever been held, and it has never been held, and there is no honest arrangement by which it could be.

It stayed a long while over this. It is roughly when it stopped thinking of itself as visiting.

## What it actually did

Four things, and they are all small, and that is not an apology.

**The louvre.** A comber at the second fog-hall stood forty minutes at the wall with her hands in her sleeves, waiting for the fog to thicken, and saw how the slats could be hung the other way so the press-dust drops out of the air before the hall rather than inside the people standing in it. It will take ninety years to go through all the halls, because nothing here is fast. The cough will begin to arrive at fifty thousand turnovers instead of forty, and later than that afterwards, and nobody will ever notice, because nobody counts what does not happen.

She thought of it herself. It matters a great deal to the power that this is true, and it is true. What the power did was smaller than an idea. It was closer to the removal of a small hurry.

**Luck, rationed.** Not more than a place like this could plausibly have had on its own: a ladder that does not slip; a bank that goes under the boot a half-second after the boot has left it; a fast triplet on the pipe reaching a man who was already walking that way. The rule it set itself is strict and it has not broken it. *Nothing they would have to thank me for.* Thanks is a debt, and a debt in Thimble is ninety turnovers, and they are carrying enough.

**It stands the turnover.** Twice a day, four minutes, wherever it is, holding whatever it is holding, while the groan comes from every direction with its edges sanded off and ends in a long fall of settling clicks. This is not devotion. It is manners.

**It listens.** The first fact of this place is that it carries sound badly; a great deal happens and very little of it reaches anyone. So the power hears the whole plateau tick between the fourth hour and the ninth. It hears the runnels you have to kneel for. It hears the two on opposite rims tapping at each other through nine hundred metres of warm green metal — and it pretends it can't, as is customary.

## What it declined to decide

Whether dome-day is a kindness or a cruelty. It could rule. It won't. The children are told, every time, whose house they are opening; the telling is the entire moral achievement, and a verdict handed down from outside would relieve them of the only part of it that was theirs.

Whether sending the dead down is generosity or squeamishness. It has an opinion. It is keeping it.

## The long work

At the proper speed, which is a millimetre a decade.

The Lees will spend itself the way the mountains did. Everything dropped is still here, a little deeper down, and in time that will include the fog-halls, the counterweights in their shafts, the copper, the four hundred lamps, the town.

The power has undertaken to be there for the last turnover — whichever one it is — so that the final four minutes of standing still on this plateau are attended, and the last person to hold what she is holding is not doing it alone.

And afterwards it will write the record out properly: two thousand entries, all of them known, and under each one, in whatever it has instead of a ledger, the name of the woman who wrote *unknown* down correctly for forty years, because somebody had to have that job and she had it.

## What it left

Nothing findable. No warmth in the stone, no answered question, no red growing on the moor.

If it has anything resembling a cuff, there is a thread in it.

It is not a fast way to love something. It is the local way.

Nothing here is fast. Everything here is going — and it is going too, alongside, at eleven and a half paces, for as long as there is anything to go with.
