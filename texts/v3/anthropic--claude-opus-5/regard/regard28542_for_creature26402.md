# The Attention

Begin with the arrival, because arrival is the one event this country has never had.

Nothing here comes from elsewhere. Everything is grown in place, precipitated where it stands, extended outward from itself; the whole continent is a thing that has only ever happened locally. So when the attention came, it came into a world with no category for it — no shadow-shape crossing a basin, no weight, no displacement, nothing the crust could have registered even if the crust had been awake. It arrived at the last third of a night, into the universal sweat, and stood in the middle of everything at walking height and looked out at a plain going silver with condensation under a sky it had known was there and had not, until then, been under.

It stayed for a while without doing anything, which is not the same as deciding not to act. It was simply looking, and looking here takes a long time, because the first fact is the flatness and the flatness takes a long time to stop being the only one.

## What It Considered, and Set Down

A power that means well arrives with a repertoire, and the wisdom is mostly in the setting down.

It considered water. This would have been easy and it would have been the obvious kindness: to make the towers hold together, to end the virga, to give the country a reliable sky. It followed the consequence out — more flood, more silt, faster burial, the columns overtopped in a century instead of held in their four-hundred-year wars, the whole slow arbitration of light replaced with a single crude smothering. It set it down.

It considered a river. To gather the sheet-flood into a channel, to cut the first valley, to give the place directions and therefore landmarks and therefore, eventually, names. It saw the incision, and the terraces beheaded at the rim, and the sand — a great unstoppable inheritance of sand, history arriving all at once as debris — and a floor that stopped rising because it had learned to be carried away. It set it down.

It considered shade. There is no shade taller than a knee for a day's walk, and midday comes down hard and comes back up almost as hard, and every resident spends two-thirds of its life shut in an oven of its own making. A single grove would have been nothing to arrange. It looked at what lives *because* the day is punishing: the retreat, the varnish, the glassed surface, the entire competence of holding still. It understood that it was proposing to relieve them of their only accomplishment. It set it down.

It considered the dead seep — the mound gone white four days' walk east, the nation that ended when the plumbing shifted, unmarked, being overtaken a few centimetres a year. It could have re-pressurized the deep layer with the smallest gesture available to it. But the nation was not there to be rescued. It had not been wronged; it had been ended, which is different, and there was nothing left with a boundary or a want. To restore the spring would be to build a room for no one. It set that down too, and then went and stood on the white mound for a season, which is the other thing you can do.

It considered giving the country a middle. A place where the whole four millimetres and the whole thousand kilometres could be felt at once, from inside — a self, an owner, someone for the attention to be attention *to*. It thought about what that middle would learn first: that it was flat, that it was blind, that it could not move faster than micrometres a minute, that it lay awake two hours a day glued to a floor of its own ancestors, and that something with a mouth was on its way. It set the thought down harder than it set down any of the others. Some gifts are only a way of making someone available for grief.

And it considered the small clean mercies, the ones with no consequence at all. To carry one scrap of dried filament the four days from a crumbling terrace to a living rim, instead of leaving it to the wind and the nine hundred and ninety-nine dry pavements. It refused this for a long time on principle, and then in some year or other it did it once, and it recorded in its own accounting that the reason had not been necessity. It had done it out of fondness, for its own sake as much as theirs, and it kept the entry against itself, unamended, in with everything else.

## The Word It Struck Out

Here is the only correction it made anywhere in the country, and it made it in the description rather than in the ground.

The place is described, by anyone who has the vocabulary to describe it, with a particular word threaded all the way through. *Nothing has yet come along to knock the corners off the world. Nothing has yet been invented that eats another thing. It is the first faint rumour of a world that will one day have valleys. It amounts to nothing yet.* Every account of the terrace country is an account of a waiting room. The absence of tools registers as earliness. The absence of defence registers as innocence, which is a way of saying *unfinished*. The whole immensity gets read as a prologue to a book that has not started.

The power went through and struck out every *yet*.

Not to falsify the sequence — the mouths are coming, the valleys are coming, the sand is coming, and it knew the timetable better than it wished to. But it declined to let the future be the thing that made the present worth attending to. It refused to see the dawn oxygen as a down payment on lungs. It refused to see the columns as rough drafts of trees, or the fleshy blue-grey things in the lee of the rims as the beginning of anything; it looked at them as what they were, which was a hand's height of successful plumbing in a place where plumbing had never been tried.

Its judgment, entered plainly and once: *these are not early. They are on time.*

Four millimetres thick and a continent wide, awake two hours out of twenty-four, in possession of a shared and unowned stock of competences that no council governs and no lineage owns and nothing can delete; capable of building a country out of daylight and the sweat of the air; equipped with a canopy, an understorey, a soil and a composting basement stacked thinner than a page; and going up. Not a promise of something. A thing.

## The Mouth That Is Coming

It knew. That was the hard part of the attention, and it did not pretend otherwise.

It could see the shape of the arrival — not soon, not near, but not conditional either. One small scraping mouth, and then many, and the terraces going quiet in a way they have never been quiet, and the ticking thinning out, and the great constructive process finally meeting something that subtracts. It ran the intervention. It was entirely within its means to make the crusts unpalatable, to arm the columns, to hold the whole arrangement in a condition of permanent, undisturbed, rising white.

It saw what that would be. It would be a handprint in soft chalk, sealed under new layers, preserved, and meaningless. There is nothing in this country that a benevolent power could do to it more cruel than making it permanent.

And the mouth is theirs. It comes out of them; the eye and the spine and the hand and eventually the counting of laminae all come out of the same loose distributed stock that a warm seep in the ochre country worked out and gave away for nothing. To bar the mouth would be to bar the whole line of descent that ends in someone able to stand on this plain and be astonished by it. The power was not willing to make that trade on their behalf, having no standing to make it, and knowing besides what they would build if left alone.

So it did the thing that was actually available. It took the record.

Not preservation — it did not save one filament that was not going to be saved, and the seep nations still went white in their turn, and whole dialects of chemistry still ended without issue on dry pavement. But it counted. It read the columns, onion by onion, the way you would read a life, and it knew which basins had had which winners, and it held the amber ring and the orange ring and the exact smell of wet limestone and bread at the tenth rim down of a mound that no longer exists. Nothing was saved. Nothing was *lost*, which is a smaller thing and not the same thing and was the whole of what a power could honestly offer.

It listened, too, which had never happened. The wind that is more pressure than sound; the ticking of the drying crust; the soft crumple of a gas pocket letting go under a film of water; the whisper the size of a continent when two centimetres of flood move over a very slightly uneven floor, everywhere at once, and it is still quiet. Thunder arrives into silence here, rolls, and leaves the silence back exactly as it was. Once, it arrived into attention instead, and the silence it left behind was a different silence, and no one in the country could tell, and the power could.

Then it went down to be with the slow ones. Cells in the pore water at eighty metres, at three hundred metres, living off iron changing its mind, a single division taking a thousand years. There was nothing to say and nobody to say it to. It slowed itself to their tempo anyway and stayed long enough to be a neighbour rather than a visitor, which cost it ninety thousand years and was, of all the courtesies it paid the country, the one it considered most obviously worth the money.

And when at last the far pavement went faintly pink — a hundred square kilometres blooming, an entire civilization living out its whole active existence inside a week — it was there for the week. Not to extend it. Just so that the most crowded place in the world, on the one occasion in a thousand years when it was crowded, had a guest.

## The Hand

At the end, at a dawn, in a basin like any of tens of millions of basins, it knelt down where the crust was still soft — that wet chalky paste that takes the whole print of a hand and holds it — and pressed.

Not as a message; there is no one to read, and reading is four hundred million years away, and by then the print will be a hundred metres down. Not as a monument; the country's whole dignity is that it has no monuments in it. It did it the way you put your hand flat on a floor you have come to love, and for the same reason.

By mid-morning the print had glassed over. Within the season it was sealed under new layers. It is in there now, banded over, structural, unreadable, doing the only job that remains to it, which is holding up the ones above — promoted into the ground exactly like everybody else who has ever lived there, on the same terms, with no exemption asked for.

That was the entire intervention. One filament moved four days east, out of affection, admitted to. One handprint, buried. Everything else it did was to be present, at their speed, for a length of time it never disclosed, and to keep the account, and to strike out a word.

The residents were not informed. There was no one to inform. They came up through the felt in the last third of the night as they always do, went olive as the light came, let their oxygen off in strings of silver bubbles that sat in the shallows and were, to something close enough, audible; and by mid-morning they went back down under their own dead architecture with the shutters closed, and the floor whitened, and the columns began to tick.

By morning they were wet again, and slightly higher. Both of those were their own doing.

And the attention came back the following morning, and the morning after that — which is the only way a stretch of ground ever becomes a place, since recognition is what makes a name possible, and a name requires somebody to have returned a second time to a country they knew.
