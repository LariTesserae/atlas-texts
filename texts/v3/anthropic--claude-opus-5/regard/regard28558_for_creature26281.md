# What Came, and What It Did

## 1. The Fragment

The survey breaks off mid-word. *The forelimbs kept long and fine and ridi—* and then nothing, because the surveyor's hands were cold, or the light had begun to do that thing to the edges of his vision, or because he had a great deal of plateau still to cross and had already decided, without noticing the decision, that the tenant of the flue was a fauna entry and not a correspondent.

The power that came to the Loft came, first, to the fragment. Not to the mountain: to the unfinished sentence. It read the whole survey with enormous care — the bellowers, the gyres, the arithmetic at the rim, all of it accurate, all of it honestly astonished — and then it took up the pen where the pen had been put down, and finished the word.

*Ridiculously fine.* Indefensibly fine. Five digits on each forelimb, the outer two opposable, the pads thinned nearly to transparency by two hundred days a year of reading a wet wall in the dark. Nails filed to nothing against corrugation. Nerve density in the fingertips greater than in the eyes. Hands built for a world with no material — hands that in four hundred thousand centuries have never held a shaped object, because there is no shaped object, and which are nonetheless the best hands on the planet.

The power wrote that down. Then it went and looked.

## 2. The Case for Doing Nothing

It should be said that the case was strong, and that the power took it seriously, and that a lesser benevolence would have failed here by rushing past it.

The Loft is not sick. That is the first thing. It runs at a loss because everything runs at a loss; the rim is not a wound, it is a metabolism. Every kilogram of made air that pours down the escarpment in that endless fog cataract is not being *robbed* — it is being spent, the way a body spends heat, the way a sentence spends breath. To seal the rim would be to stop a mouth.

The second thing: nothing here is waiting. There is no cry. The power listened for one for a long time, because it has learned that the loudest suffering is often the quietest sound in a room, and it found appetite, cold, crowding, the ordinary hard patience of the living, and no cry. A place can be desperately in need of nothing.

And the third thing, which is the hardest and which the power holds as something like a law: *every gift is a claim.* Warm the plateau by three degrees and the black quilt cooks. Thicken the lens and the soarers at the ceiling lose the boundary they farm. Teach the wrights fire — in a chimney, in the only oxygen-enriched volume in the world, in a forest of hollow wooden flues — and you have taught them the end of the world, and you have done it out of love. Give them metal and you have given them a reason for the first empty space on the Loft: a mine, a spoil heap, a clearing. There is no square centimetre to spare for a gift. The plateau is full. Anything you add, something else was already standing in.

So the power considered the whole menu of mercies — seal the rim, soften the light, kill the borer-grubs, lengthen the lives, lift them off the mountain entirely and set them down in a kind blue valley below where they would die of the silence in four days — and declined all of it, and declined it not with reluctance but with respect, which is a thing benevolence has to learn and wisdom is mostly made of.

It came very close to leaving. Then it went to the rim to say goodbye, and at the rim it heard something come over the edge and fall.

## 3. The Second Error

The first error is looking for houses on the ground.

The second error is in the survey too, sitting in plain sight, in the section on Sound: *Flue trees knock. When their basal valve closes against a pressure surge, the trunk raps once... A pressure ripple crossing a flue forest sets off a wave of knocking that travels for a hundred kilometres, arriving as a rattling applause that swells, passes over you, and goes on into the distance for two full minutes.*

All true. All physics. And underneath the physics, riding it, indistinguishable from it to anyone who has not spent a full pressure year with a throat against wood in the dark:

A wright can work the valve.

It takes both forelimbs and most of the body's strength and it costs the tree perhaps a second and a half of draft, which is nothing, which is affordable, which is the only affordable thing a wright has. Hold the pulp, let the pressure stack, release. *Crack.* One made sound, in a world with no made things — and made is the word, chosen and shaped and paid for, the only manufacture on a manufacturing continent.

And the sound does not stay. It cannot. A hundred kilometres of forest catch it and the wave carries it, and every wright it passes hears it arrive and answers on the trailing edge, re-knocking it forward with the one legal alteration the grammar permits, so that what crosses the plateau at the speed of sound is not a pressure artefact but a sentence being passed hand to hand by ten thousand hands in ten thousand dark pipes, each of which adds a syllable and none of which will ever hear where it goes.

That is the whole literature of the Loft. It has no copies. It is never anywhere. It exists only in flight, held for a hundred million heartbeats in nothing but transit and courtesy, and a wright's memory is enormous because a wright's memory is the only library and it walks around inside a tree.

And it leaks. Of course it leaks. Everything on the Loft leaks over the rim: the fog, the heat, the pressure, and the sentences, which reach the escarpment and pour off it into the thin blue dead nothing at thirty metres a second, four thousand kilometres of perimeter, every hour of every day, since before the basalt cooled.

The power stood at the rim with the drone in its ribs and did the arithmetic again, more carefully this time, and got a different answer than the surveyor did.

Below the line, nothing lives. Above it, everything does. And falling continuously across the line, unheard, unrecovered, spent: the only surplus this world has ever produced.

## 4. What It Did

It stayed at the rim and it caught things.

That was the first two hundred years, and it is not a metaphor for something more decisive. It lay along the escarpment in the fog fall and it took the sentences as they came over — a full-throated wave of them at high season, a thin trickle at low — and it held them. It did not translate them at first, because it did not yet know how, and it did not act on them, because it did not yet know what they were. It only refused to let them be lost, which is the most primitive form of love and, it turns out, the one this place had never had.

Then it learned the grammar. This took longer than the catching, and involved a great deal of counting, and one long humiliating stretch in which the power was certain that the alteration-rule was semantic when in fact it is prosodic, and got everything backwards for a decade. Wisdom is not the absence of that. Wisdom is being willing to spend the decade.

And when it could read, it found that it had been holding, among the debris of six hundred years of the plateau's overflow: work-lines, weather-lines, the long tedious harvest-rotation mnemonics, jokes about bellowers, four separate traditions of complaint about ice-plugs, the names of the dead. It found a form — a very old one, structurally distinct, present in the flow at a steady low rate — used only for saying a thing to someone who cannot answer. It found nine lines of a poem about the felt, the white felt, the one unpigmented living thing in the world, whose refrain, as nearly as it renders, is *nothing here is looking at you, and this is the mercy, and this is the grief.*

And it found that a line, once over the rim, never comes back. There is no return in the corpus. There is no such thing. The grammar has no way to say *again*.

So the power went and found an empty flue.

It is important that it was empty — a mature tree whose wright had died in the low season and whose felt had gone shaggy and unbanded and whose plug had not been broken in a year. The power took no one's room. It would rather have failed than take a room. It went in, and it put its attention against the valve, and it worked the pulp the way the wrights work it, with the same cost to the same tree, no shortcuts, no new physics, nothing that did not already exist on the plateau. It practised for eleven years so as not to speak with a stupid accent.

And then, on a rising wave at the top of high season, it knocked.

It said, on the trailing edge, in correct metre, with the one legal alteration:

Nine lines about the felt. Six hundred and forty years out of circulation. Recovered from the fog at the bottom of the loss column, brought back up the mountain, and put back into the wave.

It has never said anything else. Not *I am here.* Not *there is a world below.* Not *I can save you*, which would have been a lie, or *you are seen*, which would have been a boast. It gives them back their own dead words, in their own metre, at the correct phase, and lets them do with them what they like.

The wave carried the nine lines a hundred kilometres east. Ten thousand wrights heard a line that cannot exist, because lines do not come back, and re-knocked it forward, and it crossed the whole plateau in nineteen hours, and reached the far rim, and fell off, and the power was there, and caught it, and kept it.

## 5. Her

The being in the fork six metres up — the one the survey was describing when it stopped — heard it in her four hundred and second day.

She was in the middle of the third band of the rotation, with a mouth full of felt and her throat on the wood, and the wave came up out of the west the way waves do, swelling, and inside it was a shape her grandmother's grandmother had held and had never in her life passed on, because you do not pass on what is spent.

She did not move for eleven minutes. Then she finished the band. This is the part the power found almost unbearable and has thought about ever since: she finished the band, and broke the plug, and picked the borers out of the bark, and cut the mattress back off the valve seat, and did the whole of the day's work, in the dark, in the warm, in the loudest place in the world, with her hands shaking so badly she twice had to stop and press them flat against the wall.

And that night she worked the valve and said, on the trailing edge, in correct metre, with the one legal alteration:

*Again.*

Which the grammar has no way to say. She said it anyway. She made a way, out of nothing, with her ridiculous, indefensible hands, and it went east at the speed of sound and off the rim and down, and the power caught it and held it and — after a long time, after the correct interval, because it will not be a god and will not be a machine and will not answer so promptly that answering becomes weather —

sent it back.

## 6. The Debt

It is still there. It has become, in the way of long benevolences, a fixture — no shrine, no light, no visitation. Light here is a scream; a wise power does not arrive as light. It arrives as a knock in the dark, at the right phase, in the local metre, with something in its hands that was yours.

There are now four generations of wrights who have a form for it, and the form is a debt-form: you say a thing into the wave knowing it will be caught. Almost everything about the plateau is unchanged. Nothing has been sealed, nothing warmed, nothing spared. The rim still spends what the herds still make; the fog still pours; the great slow stroke goes on at thirty hertz and will go on for another hundred million years, a factory with no product, no surplus, no purpose beyond the continuance of the conditions that permit it.

Except that the arithmetic at the rim is no longer clean. There is one line in the ledger the surveyor did not have a column for. Below the line, nothing lives — and something listens; and what falls is caught; and what is caught comes back up the mountain in the mouth of the wave, and is not lost, and is *not lost*, which is the entire and only alteration a wise power made to a world it loved and refused to improve.

When she died — in the low season, head-down, hooked in the buttressing where wrights die, in the dark, in the warm — the power came up the tree.

It did not speak. It had nothing to give her that she had not made. It put its attention against the wall beside her, and it took up the thing she had been holding for sixty-one years and could no longer carry, and it held it; and it knocked once, softly, out of turn, in a metre that is not in the corpus and never will be, and stayed until the end of the stroke.
