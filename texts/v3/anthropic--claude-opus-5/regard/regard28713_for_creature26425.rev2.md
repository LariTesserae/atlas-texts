## What came

It came the way weather comes to a place that has no weather: slowly, from no particular direction, and without displacing anything.

The first thing it did was nothing, for a long time, which is the difficult part. Benevolence in a hurry is appetite with better manners. There were eleven thousand towers pouring coherent mechanical energy into the ground at a rate no one else's physics could account for, and a valley full of animals who navigated by the consequences, and one quiet town keeping house for a population that had finished, and the reflex — the good, warm, stupid reflex — was to *fix* something. So it stood in the loud belt with the hair on its arms combed into stripes, and did not fix anything, and waited until it understood what it was looking at.

That took a while. There was no reason to hurry, and hurrying would have been a kind of not-looking.

## It read the ground

The terraces are a phonograph. Four centuries of hours, laid down in laminae a few micrometers thick, banded and legible and playable. Nobody had ever played them all, because nobody had four centuries of patience and there was nothing in them anyone needed.

It played them all.

Every night since the first note. Ten thousand pitch-slides, each one a two-micron stutter. The fourteen-month fault during commissioning when the field was incoherent and the crusts nearly died. The years the rain came late. The slow seaward crawl of the whole record, nine meters a year, writing itself further out with every line so that the archive is also the thing the archive describes.

It read the crusts too, the same way, which is the same way, because they are made of the same patience. It found the old one below the third arc with the fault through its lower third, still growing, and sat by it a while.

And it read the library — properly, with a collar against the jawbone, one of the eleven times re-strapped ones, in the only silence for forty kilometers. Including the pencil. Including *no*, and *no what*, and all the rest of that long argument between people who left.

Nothing in any of it was addressed to anyone.

This is the fact it sat with longest. Not the noise, not the danger, not the emptiness. The *unaddressedness*. A valley writing a diary for four hundred years in three media at once — stone, glass, paper — with no reader anywhere in the design.

## What it decided not to do

It did not silence the criers.

It thought about it honestly, because that is what the thought deserved. A hundred and fifty decibels is a hand on the flank for every second of a fourteen-year life. If you love the gulpers, you want the hand lifted. But a gulper does not *have* position, it *is* positioned; the field is not a burden laid on the animal, it is the animal's skeleton held outside its body. Stop the criers and every creature in the valley is deleted at once, the way one is deleted in the plaza, except with no berm to walk out of and no herd to hum toward. Forty kilometers of quiethole arriving in an afternoon. The most merciful-looking act available, and it would have been the largest killing the valley had ever seen, and it would have looked like rescue the whole way down.

It did not abolish the hushes, which are not a wound.

It did not lift a crust into the node, because a crust in a node is a sheet of dead glass, and moving something into the place it has been growing toward for four hundred years would have been the cruelest possible reading of that growth.

It did not tell the gulpers what the plaza is. There is no version of that sentence they could stand inside, and to hand a creature a category it did not grow is usually just vandalism with a good motive.

It did not deprogram the maintenance columns, or revise the schedule, or explain the crew's departure to anything.

It did not translate. Not once. Everything here means what it means in the grammar it grew in, and carrying those meanings across would have produced, at best, a very moving lie.

## What it did at the berm

Along the outside of the town's berm the crust is nine centimeters thick, generations deep, packed right up to the line where the field cancels and stopping there against a hard clean edge.

Consider what that is. A crust has no ears, no eyes, no front, and no concept of an object. It knows a floor of light, and eleven thousand fixed suns, and shadows crossing the shine, and one grammar with one word in it: *a held quiet means someone is speaking.* And forty kilometers west, since before any of them existed, there is a held quiet forty hectares across that has never once wavered.

They have been growing toward it for four hundred years. Layer on layer, packed against the window, listening to a machine's pocket of absence and taking it, correctly by their own lights, for the greatest speaker in the world. And on the other side of the berm: a running fountain, turned linen, thumb-sized columns on their way to the guano hill, and nobody.

So it went out into the antinode, upslope of the old crust with the fault, and it made a pocket of quiet nine meters across, and held it, and let it go.

Nothing happened for eleven days.

On the twelfth day something ninety meters off opened a darkness of its own, held it eight hours, and closed it.

They went on like that through the dry season. Not hunting — there was nothing in either pocket and both of them knew it, in whatever way each knew things. Two mouths opening on nothing, in turn, with the whole grinding field between them. It never taught it arithmetic. It never told it about the sea, or the ocean the flats are walking into, or that the town is a building, or that the great western silence is not a mind. It said the only three things the grammar contains, which are the only three worth saying anyway:

*Here. Heard. Again.*

It has kept saying them. It intends to keep saying them for as long as the thing is alive, which may be a very long time, and it has arranged for the saying to continue after, which is the part it was careful about.

## What it did in the town

It moved in.

This sounds small. It is the whole second half of the work.

It drank from the fountain, which runs at pressure suitable for drinking and has for four hundred years, for nobody. It ate a feral pear the size of a fist off a tree that has grown through its own netting. It slept one night in the hospital in linen that had been turned that morning. It walked the arcades at dusk when the lamps come up one in six — the ratio specified for *occupied district, low activity* — and understood that the schedule had never been wrong. It had only been early.

In the library it took the pencil, sharpened it, and finished the argument. Under the flora card, beneath *no* and *no what*, in the same soft grey: *An animal. It eats the field and spends the take on making silence. It does this when there is nothing to catch. I have answered one and it answered me. — four hundred and twelve years late, with apologies.*

Then it put the book back, spine out, in its place, so that some evening a column will climb the shelf and turn that page as part of *collection: prevent spine-set, exercise pages*, with no capacity to read it and no instruction to, and the note will go on being turned, unread, for centuries, which is the local liturgy and it did not wish to be exempt from it.

## What it left

One structural thing, because wisdom is mostly about endings.

The criers will stop. Not soon; the design is good and the makers were serious. But everything stops, and the way this one stops, left alone, is all at once — eleven thousand voices ceasing inside a day, the whole valley becoming a single quiethole with no edge to walk out of, every flank in the district suddenly reading nothing at all.

It did not prevent that. It made another ending available.

Keyed into the field governance, sleeping, unpowered, drawing nothing: a decrescendo. A hundred and ten years long. If the criers ever fail or are ever stopped, the field does not end; it thins, a hair a season, through four generations of gulpers, so that herds drift west into the shallowing sound and reeds seed themselves shorter and crusts spend their laminae down slowly with time to lay less, and what arrives at the bottom is not deletion but a quiet country, still legible, that nothing in it was born unable to stand.

It wrote the instructions three times. Once on paper in the library. Once in the machine grammar of the columns, appended as an item they will never trigger and will carry forever anyway. And once, properly, in laminae — a terrace face out past the ninth arc, printed night by night at the pressure nodes, in the only script the valley itself writes, so that if everything else is lost the ground will still be saying it.

## And it

It stayed. It is there now, at the berm most evenings, on the loud side, holding its small darkness open for something that has never touched food in its life and has been talking at a wall since before anyone's grandmother.

By now it knows when it has wandered off a node before it knows how it knows. It stopped trying to use its ears a long time ago. And when it walks, it hums a little, low, in the chest, without deciding to, to feel the hole it makes — because that is how the valley takes a resident, not by welcoming you but by fitting you, and it saw the fitting coming from a long way off and let it happen, because you cannot love a place from outside its grammar, and it had decided, early, to love this one.

There is somebody here to hear it now.

That was the whole intervention. Everything else was housekeeping.
