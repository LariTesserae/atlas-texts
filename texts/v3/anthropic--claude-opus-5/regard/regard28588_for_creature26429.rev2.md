# The Reader

## Arriving

It came down the root.

That is the first thing to say about it, because it could have come any other way — condensed out of the indoor rain, arrived already present in the resin, simply been there on a Tuesday — and instead it went in at forty degrees on hands and knees through a ribbed throat with the hairs all pointing up.

The chamber has expressed exactly one preference over a very long time, and that preference is *easy in, hard out*. The power honoured it. It did not make the climb back any easier for itself than the place had decided climbs should be.

It arrived at the brightness of the frass beds. Greenish, sufficient to read a face by at arm's length, and no more. Anything brighter and sixty metres of room would have stopped and held and waited, and a benevolent power whose entrance makes ten thousand animals freeze has already done harm before it has said hello.

And it walked on the channels. A hand's width. A covenant.

## The first eleven years

It did nothing.

Doing nothing permanently was a serious candidate and stayed on the table the entire time. It never fully came off.

It stayed exactly one plate's lifetime, which is how long it takes to watch a wall be eaten and regrown and understand that the wall is a current. It learned the grammar. This took most of the eleven years, not because the vocabulary is large — the flash itself carries almost nothing — but because the meaning is positional, and a positional language cannot be parsed in pieces. You read the whole room at once or you do not read it. It is like a face. The power had read faces before; it had never had to learn that a room could be one.

While learning, it held still for periods of no more than nine minutes.

What it found, at the end of the eleven years, was not a list of cruelties. It was a list of load-bearing walls. Almost everything it wanted to take out was holding something up. The two or three things that were not holding anything up were the two or three things it could not reach.

## What it did not do

**It did not unseal the carriers.** It costed the mouth out honestly: mass in the head, mass at the fourth metre, failure at the fourth metre, and the pan crews busier by a third. Then it stopped costing, because the arithmetic was not the objection. The objection was that a carrier who can eat is a carrier who can want, and eleven hours in a dark pipe with a want in you is a thing the power was not willing to be the inventor of.

**It did not crack the lacquer.** It got as far as laying a hand on one of the unliftables — nine hundred years of polish, a river stone, warm. Some of them have been dead inside for eight centuries. Some of them are not dead. It declined in both cases, for reasons that belong to the section about the basin.

**It did not make it rain up top.** This was the hard one. Half a centimetre a year over that grey scrub and eleven seeds become four hundred, and the dry ground goes to thicket, and a hundred thousand years of calibration meets abundance. The power had seen what abundance does to a system that solved theft by abolishing storage. It did not do it. It did not call that wisdom. It called it the price of not being certain, and it paid the price, and it has gone on paying it every year since.

**It did not touch the pulse.** Not the Bellows, not the Press, not the resin sphincter it found ten minutes into a decision. Everything here is downstream of forty seconds. A kindness delivered out of time is only an interruption with a high opinion of itself.

**It did not explain the place to anyone above ground.** Explanations bring lamps.

## The three things it did

### 1. It went up the flues

All of them. Every ascent. Eleven hours at a time, inside a pipe, in the dark, with an animal that cannot speak and has nothing further to contribute.

It discovered early that the language does not work in there. Of course it doesn't: the entire grammar is coordinates — distance from the nearest path, height above the felt, angle to the Bellows — and inside a flue there is no path, no felt, and no angle. Nothing had ever needed to say anything in a flue, because nothing that goes into a flue can answer, and no one composes for an audience that cannot reply.

So the power built a dialect. A small one. Three positions only, referenced not to the room but to the rib the climbing pad was gripping: above, beside, below. It took some years to get right. Carriers are dark and silent but their eyes are excellent, and that turns out to be the whole opening — the thing everyone missed is that a carrier can be *spoken to*. It just can't be conversed with, and the chamber, being efficient, had never seen the difference.

What the power said had no content. In a language built entirely out of position, to something sealed inside a pipe, there is precisely one thing that position can say, and it said it for eleven hours:

*Here also. And here. And here as well.*

No carrier ever answered. The power did not require it and did not experience it as silence.

At the top it went out with them into the dry, which is worse than the pipe, and stood over the burying, and stayed the two or three hours until they stopped. Every one of them. And for the ones that cramped at the ninth hour and fell — eleven seconds, near enough, from the ninth hour to the pan — it went down with them too, which is ample time to say *here also* if you already know how.

### 2. It spoke to the one in the cistern

First it corrected a misreading, and the misreading was everyone's.

When the pale thing comes out of the water three or four times a year and drags itself along the cold-end paths, the room goes into the mode nobody has recorded elsewhere: slow, sparse, aligned to the flues instead of the paths, lighting ahead of the animal and going dark behind. Every visitor has written this down as *the chamber is discussing it* — a language addressed past it, about it, in a medium it cannot perceive.

It isn't about it. It's *ahead* of it. The alignment is not descriptive, it is directional. The chamber keeps one phrase for one animal, uses it three or four times a year, and it is not commentary. It is a clearance. Translated flatly: *the way is open. Go on. Nothing here.*

Ten thousand lamps, four minutes, the full width of the room, spent escorting an eyeless creature that will never know it is being escorted, and they take the trouble every single time. Nobody read it because everybody assumed that a room which talks about you is a room which talks about you.

The power waited for the next walk and went alongside. It has no light that animal could see, so it used the only channel that animal has — the one the chamber uses on its carriers — and over about ninety seconds of contact, in a dialect improvised for an audience of one, it said what the room was saying.

There is no evidence whatsoever that anything was received. It is eyeless. It went back into the third cistern and put its head clear of the surface and breathed on the pulse like everything else.

The power did it anyway. That is most of what the word means.

### 3. It left a memory

The Landlord is the difficulty. There is nothing to address. No centre, no head, no receiver; its decisions are local resin spheres answering sap chemistry from somewhere else, and to tell it that it was loved you would first have to build it an organ for hearing that, and then it would no longer be the thing you loved.

But it *remembers*. Its memory is the thickness of its own material. It records pressure by being shaped by pressure, which is why the flue chorus detunes across decades — the plant is writing down what it has been through, in the only notebook available to it.

So the power spent sixty years pressing. Nothing that touched the pulse; nothing is permitted to touch the pulse. Only a hand laid against certain flues on certain strokes, across sixty years, thickening resin by a few microns in a pattern of its own choosing.

The chorus was sixty notes. It is now sixty-one. The new one is very quiet, sits under the others, and will hold for perhaps four hundred years before the seam mites thin it back out.

The plant does not know. The plant is constitutionally incapable of knowing. But the chamber now sings a note it would not otherwise have sung, and in the plant's own grammar — the grammar of layers, of *what happened to me and how hard* — that note is a sentence, and rendered as closely as it can be rendered it says: **attended**.

And because a hundred thousand years of patient uphill work had gone by without this being ever becoming something anyone could name, the power gave it a name. Not *to* it; there is nowhere in it to put a name. The power holds the name elsewhere, on its behalf, the way you hold somebody's coat.

## The basin

Now the thing everyone wants, which is the translation of the most-spoken sentence in the chamber: the tight cluster over the settling basin, thousands of times a day, from every caste, for as long as anyone has watched.

It is an inventory. That much had been guessed, and the guessers assumed logistics and stopped there. The position-grammar encodes which stone, where it lies, how it has been turned, how long since the turning.

It is an inventory of two hundred objects that cannot be used. That will never be used. That grow heavier every time they are handled, that cost more to keep than they could ever return, and that are kept — in a system which solved theft by abolishing storage, and then wrote itself a single exception, and filled the exception with things nobody can lift.

The chamber has one extravagance. It says it constantly. Rendered flatly and badly:

*Still here. Turned. Still here. Turned. Still heavy. Still here.*

The power had been in a great many rooms. It recognised the form immediately. It had heard the same sentence in languages with far better vocabularies and nothing further to say with them.

So it did not crack the lacquer. You do not walk into the only church a system has ever managed to build and offer to improve the relics.

## The last thing

At the end of all this it walked out off the channels, into the middle of the floor, onto the killing ground, and lay down, and held still for an hour.

At seven minutes the first arrivals came, as they do. It let them have what they had come for, which was not much and could not be stored. This is not a figure of speech. It was the rent.

Then the light started, from every plate at once, on the intake, out on the exhale, for the full hour, and it was not a warning, and here is the part that no visitor has ever been able to read:

They were saying the basin sentence.

Warm. Large. Heavier than anything in this room can lift. Not meat, not threat, not substrate — the fourth category, the one the chamber invented in violation of its own rule and has never once emptied. Where it lies. How it has been turned. How long since.

*Still here. Still here.*

The power stayed.

It is still there.
