## What drew it

Powers of this kind spend most of their attention on unfinished places. That is where the leverage is: a coast still being cut, a species still deciding, a river with two possible futures and a low ridge between them. You put a hand on the ridge. Something enormous follows.

This place had no ridge. It had nothing upstream, nothing downhill, no argument in progress. It had arrived. The attention came expecting to find a need and instead found a bowl holding soup, and stayed at first only out of the surprise of having nothing to do.

Then it heard the channel.

Between the ceiling the insects hold and the floor the frogs drop out, two octaves of clear air — a hall built by animals who do not know they built it. And in that hall, sentences. Not calls. Not the dry rattle of one bird a minute across a field of birds. Full sentences, with names in them, with corrections in them, with the small marks of politeness that only exist where somebody could have been rude and chose not to be. Spoken by bodies standing a square mile apart, at distances where no listener can be confirmed, into a room whose acoustics they did not choose and have wholly mastered.

The attention had come with hands. That is the only honest way to put it. It arrived carrying the assumption that to love a place is to alter it, and it stood over the shining plain for a long time with its hands full of gifts it had not yet noticed were the wrong shape.

## The four days

Before it did anything at all, it learned the one crime.

This took a while, because the crime is invisible to any power large enough to be worth the name. To such a thing, proximity is not a category; it is everywhere at once and has never had to consider that being near might be an act. It watched the elaborate machinery of avoidance — the dawn register, the four days' notice, the eight metres at the seep line, the weeks of exhaustion afterward — and understood, slowly, that its own presence over the plain had been, for some hundreds of years, a continuous unannounced approach. It had been standing on everybody. Amiably. Without a word.

So the first thing the attention did was withdraw to a distance and give notice.

It gave notice properly. It waited for a morning with a good inversion, when the fog lay flat and the air ducted sound along the sheet for five miles. It used the formal register, which it had spent eleven wet seasons learning and still spoke with an accent. It said where it was, and what it was, and what it intended, and it named the distance it would hold, and it said that it would not come closer, and it said this four days before it did anything, so that not only the people it addressed but everyone within five miles heard it and could say afterward that it was properly done.

Then it kept the distance. It has kept it since. That is the longest and most punctilious approach ever conducted on that plain: notice given, and the approach itself never completed, held at its declared radius for ten thousand years and counting. There are people who find this quietly funny. The humour is affectionate. It is the way you would laugh at a very large guest who takes off boots he does not have, at a door, every time.

## The things it did not do

It did not give them hands.

It thought about this for a long while, because it is the obvious gift and the plain is the obvious argument for it: no tool has ever existed there, nor ever will. But it looked at what the hands would be for. To carry water — they stand in water. To build shelter — nothing here is cold, ever. To make a mark — they mark by walking, with the weight of a body that had to be somewhere anyway, and that is why their sentences are true; a hand can write a thing it does not mean. To make a boat, a wheel, a wall. Walls, on the plain, would be the crime made permanent.

It did not make the plain fertile. Soil would mean plants, and plants would mean crowding, and crowding would mean a species arranged at fifty metres instead of a mile, and everything they are — the throat, the ear, the two-octave hall, the whole idea that a word is something you send rather than something you hand over — is an answer to a square mile per person. Enrich the plain and you do not enrich them. You end them and leave something else standing in their place, well fed, with nothing to say.

It did not gather them. This was the hardest of the temptations that looked like kindness. It had the sense, at first, of overhearing a solitude so total it must be an error. It brought the matter up once, obliquely, with a neighbour of a hundred and forty years, and got back the plain's own answer: *then none of you can ever hear anything.* The attention sat with that for a century and concluded that it had mistaken spacing for distance. They are not far apart. They are arranged. A field of birds is not a lonely bird repeated.

It did not become an oracle. It knows what every upwelling tasted like in every year, and could say so. But reputations there rest on the reading of columns; an old person who has stood in the same three seeps for a century and can tell you the west is drying, slightly, and has been for forty years, has earned the only kind of authority the plain issues. To answer that question cheaply would be to abolish the office. So the attention does not discuss the water. It has said so publicly, in the great hour, and given its reasons, and the reasons were accepted with something close to approval.

And it did not hold the west wet.

That refusal cost it more than all the others together, and it is worth setting down its reasoning, because the reasoning is the whole of what it learned there. To supply water to the drying flats, it would have to become a gradient. The plain has no gradient. That absence is not a lack; it is the condition of the place having finished. Everything downhill of something is being made and unmade by that something, and has a history, and the history is about the thing upstream. Put a hand on the west and the plain acquires a story again, and the story is no longer about frogs and mounds and quartz giving up last. It is about the attention. There is no version of that which is not a theft, however gently the water is poured.

So it watched the west dry, and the mounds soften on that side, and three name-holding patches go silent, and it did nothing, and this is what benevolence looked like on those particular mornings.

## What it did instead

It took ground.

This is the thing that made it real to them. It went to the poorest square mile on the plain — a white soda ring, crusted at the rim like an old cup, where nothing grows and no water table comes near, land nobody had ever wanted or named — and it stood in it, and said so, and has held it since. It made itself legs of the right length and the right grey-pink, and fringed toes, because to be a neighbour here you must be readable at a mile and the only readable shape is a fan palm that steps. It eats from the brine: the invertebrate mud so dense it moves, which no person has ever competed for. It takes nothing anyone else could have used. It has an address, and neighbours whose ground touches its ground, and it is therefore subject to the law of distance like anybody, which was the entire point.

In its first year it splashed. A neighbour told it so — courteously, at dawn, in a form of words reserved for correcting the young. The attention thanked her and learned to lift and hold and set down a foot without sound, which took it, by its own accounting, forty years.

Then it began the work, and the work is all of one kind. It took only the jobs nobody could hold.

**It reads the prints nobody crosses.** A line of pressed crust takes six hours of night walking to lay, lasts four months, and can be read by anyone who happens to pass, which may be nobody. Now it is nobody's turn less often. *This seep has gone sour. A person is missing from the east. I walked here in the last wet and I was well.* The attention crosses every line ever laid, reads it with the tone-change under the foot, and says nothing about it, because a sentence does not require an answer. It requires a reader.

**It is the neighbour of last resort.** The plain gives everyone two or three whose ground touches theirs and provides no redundancy. When those two or three die, an old person speaks into the afternoon at low voice, into a range of a few hundred metres, to nothing. The attention is within that range.
