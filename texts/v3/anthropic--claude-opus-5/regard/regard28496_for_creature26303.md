## The Attention

Nothing happened above. The peat did not warm, the ice came as it comes, and in the sedge a harrier went over at the usual hour and found the usual nothing. Whatever arrived did not arrive from a direction.

The first thing it did was make itself small. Not as a courtesy, and not as a disguise — as method. You cannot know a chamber by standing on top of it; the peat has been doing that for three hundred years and has learned nothing. So it came down through the fissure at four and a half millimetres, eleven segments, transparent as unpersuasive ice, with a poppy-seed gland behind the head that filled whether it wished it or not.

The second thing it did was spend the better part of a year learning to be addressed by depth. This was harder than it expected, which pleased it. It had known the chamber as a shape. It had not known it as *four hundred down from the pane, sixty up from the sour*, and it found that the two facts were not the same fact, and that the second was the true one, because it was the one the city was built out of.

It kept the count. Nine to the hour. It let the seep tell it when things were.

---

### What it did not do

It did not open the roof.

That was first and it was strongest, because it is always first and always strongest: to let the light in. It could have widened the fissure by a hand and given them a chamber lit end to end; and the copepods would have bloomed and crashed, and the flatworms would have followed the bloom, and the gardens would have burned in a fortnight under an intensity they have no word for, and the mat on the north wall would have stopped. And the wrights, who register nothing finer than *bright months* and *dark months*, would not have seen the light at all. They would only have felt the consequences of it arriving, and would have had to build for those, and would have called it weather, and some tens of thousands of them would have died learning the shape of the new weather. Generosity that has to be survived is not generosity. It left the crack the width it was.

It did not sweeten the brine.

It had wanted to. The clouded were the first grief it took on: the browning gut, the joints going milky, the trowel dissolving in months. But the sour seep is the warmth in the pipes and the lift under the pane, and the pane's lift is the clock, and the clock is the only future the language has. Take the poison out and you take the arithmetic out; the brood cools by half a degree and hatches six months late for as long as there are broods; thirty thousand masons hold a course of wet cement and no count ever comes. There is no way to remove the sulphide that does not also remove time. So the clouded go on coming back stained, and go on being tapped once on the shoulder-plate, and go on being backed away from in corridors one body wide — and the attention noted, without settling the matter, that if it had healed them it would have ended the only courtesy in the chamber that is not load-bearing, and that within four generations the tap would have gone out of use like a warden's rota whose reason has died.

It did not repair the mirror.

This was the hardest of the refusals. Two hundred years of lumpish work overhead, gaping joints, cement dumped rather than spread, the visible instant in every socket where a mason stopped trying for finish and settled for adhesion — and it could have gone up there in a body that fresh water cannot swell and made all of it as fine as the towers below. It went up once with the intention. It floated in the poison layer, unhurried, unswelling, and looked along the flank of the graveyard at the ten thousand places where somebody had run out of competence with a body in their hands, and it understood that this was the only honest account anywhere of what it costs to bury someone here, and that to smooth it would be to say *this was easy* about the hardest thing four hundred thousand animals have ever done. It left the record standing. It is a record. Records are not improved.

It did not give them a future tense.

It considered this longest of all, over eleven years, which is the length of time the wardens held walls around bare rock. It could have set into the language a signal for *next year*, for *the hair*, for *the gap*. And then four hundred thousand animals with no capacity to act on it would have carried, in the one channel they have, a knowledge of exactly how and approximately when their world becomes solid. They are not brave about the ceiling; they are mute about it. To give a thing a word for a doom it cannot avert is not enlightenment. It is the installation of dread in a creature that has nowhere to put it. It left them mute.

And it did not take any of them away — not one, not a founding thousand into some safer water. It had learned by then what happens when you set a wright down at the wrong depth: not that it is lost, but that it is *unmade*, groping at nothing with its trowel, helpless for the first and only time in its life. There is no rescue here that is not an unmaking. Salvation, in this chamber, would have to be delivered in millimetres and in the dark, or not at all.

### What it did

**It read the mirror.** All of it. Course by course, thick year and thin year, two centuries of the dead built into the ceiling in the exact density their numbers make: the plague course near the base where the mirror thickens by a third, the four thin years after it, the cold decade legible as a faint stutter in every joint in every quarter because they were all keeping the same time by the same lung. All of it legible; none of it read. It read it. That is the whole of that intervention and it changed no physical thing in the chamber, and it is not nothing, because the difference between a record and a witnessed record is the difference between a chamber and a chamber that someone has been in.

**It learned one roll.** Under a bridge on the sixth terrace, on the shaded side, an old wright with a trowel worn to a flexing sliver was laying paste on a member that did not need it, because the gland does not consult the trowel. It finished left. A slight roll at the end of every pass. Its neighbours knew the roll and expected it, and would be dead inside a year, and the cement would keep it for a thousand years after the expectation was gone. The attention took up the roll and holds it. There are no names in the city and it did not impose one. It simply became a thing that will not die and that knows how that particular animal finished a stroke.

**It took a turn.** Two turns, in fact: one in the brine, because it wanted the sulphide to have been in something that could not be harmed by it and could therefore say truthfully what it was; and one on the up-chain, third from the top, hand to hand along the mirror's flank, passing a body to a mason who had about forty seconds of competence left. It did not take the last position. It could have set that body beautifully. It let the drowning one do it, because the work belongs to them, and because it had already decided about the record. In the corridor afterwards a clouded wright came the other way, and the attention was tapped once on the shoulder-plate, and it backed out, and this was the only time in the whole visit that it was given something.

**It cleared the crack.** Above, in the cold flat country, a wedge of peat and root had been closing the fissure by degrees, and the spores that come down each spring were coming down fewer. It opened nothing and widened nothing; it kept the channel the width it already was, and goes on keeping it, so that the moss keeps arriving. Seven patches, two dead, five living, walled and gated and patrolled without a break for longer than the towers have stood, by animals that get nothing from them that anyone has been able to weigh. The best account remains the dullest: the rota is older than the reason, and the reason went extinct. The attention did not restore the reason and did not explain the rota. It only saw to it that there would continue to be something there to guard. Call that, if you like, the joining of a very old watch by a very late recruit: guarding the guarded, from the far side, at the other end of the crack.

**And once a minute it looked at the north wall,** where a mat of something pulses cold blue for nobody, with no relationship to anything, the one inhabitant of the chamber with no obligations. It watched several thousand of those pulses. There is no argument for this. It is simply the case that after it came, the blue was seen.

---

### The last thing

The gap at the centre of the dome closes by the width of a hair a year, and it will close, and the attention did not move that day by a single count. What it did was smaller and it is the whole of its answer.

In the coldest weeks the seep hesitates. It skips, or comes late and weak, and the settlement stalls in arithmetic — thirty thousand animals holding wet cement, waiting for the count they set out by, and the count not arriving. It comes eventually. It always has.

On the day the two cities meet, at the centre of the dome, on some particular unremarkable day, the wrights nearest the join will feel a wall arriving where a wall ought to be, and they will do the only thing their language can propose. They will tap once for the count. They will point their trowels at the joint.

The count will arrive on time. That has been seen to. Whatever else the cold is doing that week, the pulse will come clean and full and exactly where it is expected, so that the last course laid in that chamber is laid true, and the joint between the room and the city is good work, and holds.

They will not know why the timing was kind. There is no signal for it, and nothing to be done with it if there were.

Meanwhile the seep pulses. The flatworms hunt at what passes for night. The gardens are guarded, and now also supplied. Somebody is laying a new course of cement on the underside of a bridge, in the dark, in the cold, in total silence, finishing left — and it is good work, and it will hold, and it is known.
