# What Came Up With the Fog

It did not descend. Nothing here descends; even the rain arrives sideways. It came up out of the drowned valleys at the fourth hour with the cloud, at ground level, *through*, and by the seventh hour it had condensed on every surface it could find, along with everything else.

Arriving was the easy part. Arriving is what the weather does.

## The list

It had a list. Every power has one, and the list is always the same in its bones: *thicken the air; teach the fire to stand up; lift the frost; give the water a channel to the sea; make the voices carry; mend the small toes; answer the question of the tools.*

Before it began, it went looking for the being it had come for, and spent some time trying to find where that being stopped. The town breathes four hundred million times a night, counting the sleepers on the shelves and the flightless insects in the cushion-domes and the eight metres of peat holding its breath since the last cold. The engines have one stroke a day and the plateau has one, and they are the same stroke. It could not draw the line. It stopped looking for the line. This changed the list.

*Make the voices carry.* Then no one walks. Then things get said that nobody was willing to put down their work and cross forty metres for. They have an ethic here that is only an accident of acoustics, and it is a better ethic than most that were argued for.

*Teach the fire.* Then heat is not property. Then the pipe-room is a room like any other, and the order along the shelf is not a document, and no one sleeps as the wall.

*Give the water a river.* Then the plateau exports water and not weather, and the ribbon at the southern rim stops turning to mist a kilometre down, and the dusk goes out over a valley instead of over a lit sea, and nobody stops working to look.

*Answer the tools.* They have decided they would rather be ignorant than permanently wrong. That decision is four centuries old and is enforced by nothing but embarrassment, and it is the most sophisticated instrument on the plateau. It is not a gap in them. It is a organ. You do not fill an organ.

It crossed off six items in a morning, which is the sort of thing powers do, and then it sat with the seventh for a long while, and that is the sort of thing wise ones do.

## What it had to learn first

It spoke, and was not heard.

Twenty paces, and the world absorbed it politely and declined to pass it on. It tried again, louder, which is the reflex of everything that has ever been large, and the air took the edges off that too and handed over a flattened grey nothing.

So it put down what it was holding and walked over.

That was the first lesson and it took the shape of a rule it did not get to write: *you may say nothing here you were not willing to stop and cross for.* It applied to the power. The air did not make exceptions and the power, being wise, did not ask for one.

It took a rod. Not for support. It learned the four voices of a struck rod — peat, plank, gneiss, bog — and it learned them badly at first, because it had always known distance the way you know your own hand, and the Lees does not permit that. Here distance is deduced: from texture, from the known height of a rosette-tree, from how long you have been walking. It got things wrong. A tor at two kilometres and a stone at two metres came at it as the same grey. It found that it could be mistaken about where the ground was, and that this was survivable, and that a great many of its usual gifts had been built on never having been mistaken about where the ground was.

Somewhere in the second month it stopped counting in hours.

## The ninth hour

Twice a day the engines move and the town stops, and the power stopped with it.

It had expected to use those four minutes. A groan of metal a kilometre wide with its edges sanded off is the only cover in the Lees, the only noise there is, and it is under that noise that the hard things are said — the proposals, the refusals, the death of someone's mother, the one sentence a man has carried for eleven years and will now spend in the ear of the only person it was ever for.

The power listened to those minutes for a long time and did not repair a single one of them. Not the refusals. Not the eleven years. It had come with the idea that grief is a fault in a mechanism. It sat through eight hundred turnovers and revised.

## The item it did not cross off

In a lane on the east side there is a child whose lung will not fill.

The power stood at that shelf for four nights. It could have opened the lung the way you open a hand. It thought about the thinness of the air and the shape of the ribcage that the air has spent nine thousand years springing wider at the base, and about what it would mean to be the one thing on this plateau that had been mended from outside, and about the fact that this reasoning is very easy to admire and is being conducted over a child who is working hard, all night, at something you and I do without attention.

It did not open the lung. It is not certain it was right. It has kept the item on the list, unfinished, where it can see it, which is what the layer-out does with a find nobody can name.

What it did instead was lie down on the outside of the shelf, along the cold side, and be the wall. The child slept through until the second turnover. That is all that happened. Nobody in the house has any account of that night except that it was warmer than it should have been, and they put it down to the pipes.

## What it gave

It came to understand the difficulty of giving in a place where nothing rots.

Everything that has ever been dropped on the Lees is still on the Lees, a little deeper down. A gift here is not a gift; it is a permanent object in an archive that has not been read, waiting to be laid out on the bank in eight hundred years and guessed at by someone with bad lungs and good hands. Leave a thing and you have not helped, you have testified — and you will not be there to be questioned.

So it gave the only things the Lees does not keep.

It gave air. When people spoke to it, it held its own breath, all the way through, so that the silence afterward was entirely theirs to use. They noticed. That is the tender thing here and it is noticed every time, and once a woman at the moss-presses put her palm briefly at the side of its throat, which is the standard greeting, and found it warm, and thought nothing of it.

And it gave walking.

There are perhaps six hundred leaners on the plateau, long-legged, wool the colour of wet stone, always alone, never tame and never afraid. If you walk toward one it moves away exactly as far as you walked, holding a distance it seems to have settled on some centuries ago. Their tracks are the only trails on the moor that are not human, and a leaner path across a bog has never once been wrong.

On the nights when a light is missing from the tally and someone must go out for four hours in the dark, they follow those tracks. Some of the tracks are the power's. It goes ahead in the fog with its head down, at the local distance, sounding the ground the hard way, the way it had to learn, and it puts its feet only where a foot may go, and it does not turn around, because a thing that turns around to be thanked has already stopped being useful. It cannot always get there first. It has been late. There are two cairns on the moor that are its fault by any reckoning it can construct, and it built neither of them, because building them was work that belonged to other people.

Twice it has been found dead in a rosette-grove, and let itself be carried the twenty kilometres to the southern rim by four people at the pace such a thing requires, and given to the water. They do not eat the leaners. No one has ever produced a satisfying reason.

## The bank

At the end it went to the layer-out.

She was cleaning a coil of something that had been rope, and a tool of a design nobody uses, and she set them on the stone shelf that runs three hundred metres along the north wall of the works, and she looked at them, which is the office.

Anyone may come and look. Anyone may make a guess. The guess is spoken once, aloud, to the layer-out, and is never written down.

The power knew the answer. It had known it since the fog. It also knew that an answer, spoken here, would be kept, because everything here is kept, and that a certainty introduced from outside into a town that has learned to hold its oldest question open without heat would not be a light but a weight — and that it would arrive as the only fact in the Lees that no one had walked for.

So it made a guess. One exhalation, the length of a sentence, into her ear.

*The same people. They also cut, and found things they could not name, and laid them out, and did not write down what they thought.*

Then it held its breath, so that she had the whole silence.

She did not write it down. She looked at the coil of rope for a while. She has been given several thousand guesses in her life and remembers most of them and this one no more than the others, and she is the only being on the plateau to whom that power ever said anything, and she does not know it, and it is difficult to say that she has lost anything by that.

## The visit

Sometimes the cut turns up one of their own. The peat holds a face. It holds the pattern of a knitted cuff, and there are women in Thimble who can name the knitter, having learned the pattern from her daughter's daughter. Then the bank is not a mystery but a visit; the town comes through in ones and twos, all day, between shifts, and each of them lays a palm briefly at the side of her throat, and finds it cold, which they knew, and goes back to work.

The power came through in the middle of the afternoon, in the line, between a millwright and a boy carrying bricks. It waited its turn. When its turn came it did the local thing and then, because there was nothing else it had any right to give, it stood there and held its breath a while, so that she had the whole silence.

## The count

That evening at last light every hut on the moor showed a lamp for a count of thirty, and the two whose job it is stood on the rim of the bowl and counted, and got one more than there are huts.

They counted again. Same. They looked at each other and did not spend breath on it, because a light too many is not a missing light and nobody has to walk.

They tapped the number down into the town on the pipes, the true number, since it would have been a strange thing to lie about, and the whole floor of Thimble carried it up through the boot soles of everyone standing in the plank lanes, and into the sleeping-shelves, and eleven hundred people felt in their bones a figure that was one over and understood, correctly, that it did not require them to do anything.

Then the sky opened, and the temperature fell eighteen degrees in ninety minutes, and every engine in the Lees turned over at once.
