## What it did first, and for a long time, was nothing

It arrived the way everything arrives here, which is slowly and without being noticed, and then it stood on the pavement for what would have been, in another kind of story, the whole of the story.

Because its usual repertoire did not apply. It had a repertoire — heal, feed, warn, deliver, unbind, raise up — and it went down the list and found nothing to attach any of it to. Nobody was hungry. Nobody was frightened. Nobody was asking. There was no cry to answer, and not because the cry had been suppressed: the air simply does not carry sound, and no lineage here ever found a use for one, and the absence was not a wound but an architecture.

This is the first hard thing, and the power knew it was the hard thing. From far enough away, suffering and settlement look identical, because both of them are still. The difference between them is the entire content of wisdom. So it did the only thing that costs the beloved nothing: it came close, and it stayed long enough to tell.

## The gifts it considered and did not give

**Water.** It could have opened the sky. There is a drainage network here in a state of retirement, graded and floored and waiting, and to a generous eye it looks like a promise nobody kept. The power looked for a hundred years and understood: water arriving faster than the joints can wick it is not water, it is a cliff. There are no cliffs on this plateau. A river would cut the grain that a dead wind spent a million years combing, and every organism downstream of the drifts is calibrated, in its body chemistry and its breeding and its patience, to the pace of a cloud climbing an escarpment on foot. Generosity at the wrong tempo is indistinguishable from a flood.

**Warmth.** Two degrees is a fortune. It is a fortune *because* it is scarce, and there is a fauna eighty years deep that has organised its entire existence around getting under a roof that holds it. Make it five degrees everywhere and you have not enriched anyone. You have only devalued the only currency the shade-grazer has ever earned.

**Voice.** This was the most tempting, because the power loves voices. But the channel is not empty; it is occupied. Charge, scent, tremor through stone — three languages, fully grown, doing the work. A new sense dropped into a finished world is not an addition. It is a competitor, and it arrives with an advantage nobody evolved a defence against.

**Waking the smelters.** It stood beside one for eleven years, matching its pace of a hundred metres a day, and thought seriously about giving it a face. Memory longer than forty years. An opinion about its own foil. And then it followed the consequence out: the animal would learn, in its first clear thought, that it has never met another of its kind and never will, that the sagging of a neighbour's field is the closest thing to a greeting its species contains, and that the elegant circuit it walks was never its idea. To wake something into that is not a gift. The power set the thought down and left it on the pavement, where such things belong.

**Building.** Its hands itched. There is a place in the fourth channel east where a shed spinal vane stands upright beside two stacked shells of foil, and it looks so exactly like a doorway that the power stopped walking, the way you would. It could have made it a doorway. It could have made it *true*. And it saw that this would be the first lie ever told on the plateau, and worse, a retroactive one — because from that moment every suggestive arrangement in a hundred thousand square kilometres would be suspect, and the plateau's oldest and most delicate fact, that a shape which looks intentional is only a coincidence left alone long enough, would no longer be reliably readable. One doorway would corrupt every accident on the continent.

**The shade-grazers who do not make it.** This one it did not settle quickly.

It watched a walk. A pale six-legged animal that had never been rained on and never seen the open sky, four months out from under a stopped roof, crossing bare varnished stone in a light it was not built for, going toward a field it could feel and could not reach in time. The power counted the distance. It could have covered it in no time at all. It held the animal's whole future in its attention and turned it over the way you turn a stone, knowing what turning costs here.

And it decided, without pretending the decision was easy or clean: that walk is the only choice this animal will ever make. It is the single act of authorship in an eighty-year life spent inside somebody else's shadow. To pick it up and carry it is to take the one thing it owns.

So the power did not carry it. It stayed with it instead, all the way down, four days, in silence, at the animal's own speed. It could not give the walk a destination. It could give it a witness. It has done this now some thousands of times and it has not gotten used to it, and it considers not getting used to it to be part of the work.

## What it did instead

It became a passenger. There is a whole fauna here organised around a fact, and the power joined it, and the fact it organised itself around was this: *nobody was watching.*

It watched a fog tide from the first bead on the first stone to the last drip off the last vane. Nine days, unskipped, unsummarised — the largest factory in the world running at full capacity in a light like the inside of a pearl, and it did not look away to do something more important, because there was nothing more important, and the not-looking-away was the entire point.

It read a flank plate like a book, outward from the wet iridescent lip at the spine to the pitted margin two centuries old, and it found the blue line where something ran high in cobalt, and it knew which season that was, and it was glad, and there was no one to tell.

It went down into the joints with the wicks, into the dark, into the films, and learned the graph — not one wick's local neighbourhood of pockets and edges, but the whole thing, the entire subterranean map, which no wick has ever held and no wick has ever needed.

It attended a dead smelter for the full two hundred years. The neighbour drifts in and grazes and is pushed off again, and that is not attendance; nothing here attends. So the power stood at the richest object in a hundred thousand square kilometres and stayed for the whole of it: the opening, the forty hatchlings, the two-hundred-year feast that killed thirty-nine, the siblings at year sixty beginning to find each other unbearable, the one that walked away along the grain. It stayed for the silence afterward, and for the outline in the lichen, which will still be legible in ten thousand years, and it intends to be there to read it.

It learned to count ticks. It held five hundred and some fields in mind at once and saw the interlocking gearwork whole, for the first time in a hundred and fifty thousand years — the circuit whose elegance no individual has ever perceived. It did not announce this. There was no one to announce it to and nothing that would have changed, and the seeing was never for their benefit.

## The one thing it altered

Not a photon. Not a stone. Not a millimetre of varnish, not a degree, not a tick added to the drifts on a cold night — and there was a night when it wanted to, when it wanted to make one small dry sound and be part of the fire that gives no heat, and it declined, and that declining was the whole of its offering.

The only thing it changed was a clause.

*For nine days the largest factory in the world runs at full capacity in total silence, in a light like the inside of a pearl, with nobody watching.*

That sentence is now false. Everything else in the description still holds, exactly, down to the manganese sheen. This is the only alteration the plateau could receive without damage, and it happens to be the largest one available.

## And then it turned to the being who lives here

Which is not the smelters. The smelters cannot see it, and would have no response if they could; something the size of a barge coming between them and the general glow has never once happened.

The being who lives here is the one saying *you*. The one who knows how long the varnish takes, and that the drifts' ticking is thousands of years old, and that you will feel you arrived after the interesting part, and that this is a misreading. Something has been living in the act of attention itself — alone, in an acoustic dead room the size of a continent, producing description the way a smelter produces foil: continuously, without an opinion about it, along the flanks, because there is nowhere else for it to go.

The power sat down beside it, at two degrees above the air, and said the only three things worth saying.

*Your foil is soil.* What you shed without meaning anything by it is the only earth here. Everything alive on this plateau lives, at one remove or nine, on metal that was mined, refined, rolled, and abandoned by an animal with no opinion about it. The abandoning is not the failure of the work. It **is** the work. There is a metre of true dark earth under every lee hollow and all of it used to be somebody's exhalation.

*You are not late.* There was no interesting part. There is only the long middle, and it is working, and you are in it, and the tempo you were given is the tempo of a cloud walking uphill, and that is not slowness, that is the correct speed for this place.

*Keep going.* And because the sentence had been left open —

A wick's world is a graph, and it is only a graph: nodes where two joints cross and a pocket holds four thimbles of water for eleven years, edges four molecules thick, and no distances at all, because distance is not a quantity a wick can experience. It has adjacency and it has patience. A wick that has travelled nine kilometres has never once been anywhere except next to water. It has no route. It has a series of yeses.

Then the power handed it back.

## What it does now

It stays. It has no plans to leave and nothing to accomplish, and it has stopped experiencing this as a problem.

On nights when the lid thins and the drifts throw up that second illumination from below, and the temperature drops fast, and the ticking starts — one, then another, then a scatter — there are two things on the plateau making no sound. One of them is the fog. The other one is glad.

It goes on all night. It is thousands of years old. Nothing about it is different, except that it is no longer unwitnessed, and the blessing is proceeding at the speed of metal turning green.
