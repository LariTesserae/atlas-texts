## Attention

The first thing it did was nothing, for a long time.

It came in low over the rim, under the lid, the way the swifts do, and then it stayed, which the swifts do not. It spent the equivalent of eleven years learning the phase relationship of three hundred knockers — not because this was necessary, but because it was the sort of thing that could not be learned any faster than it happened, and it wanted to be the kind of visitor who had waited.

It learned nine seconds. It learned them the way the people had: by standing somewhere and knowing what could be got under.

It learned that the place had no way of noticing itself. This struck it as the central fact, more than the heat, more than the acid. Eight kilometres of engine turning over on schedule with no organ of self-perception anywhere in it, and ninety-one thin people walking their circuits twice a day as the basin's only nerve endings, at forty minutes per interval, alone.

## The temptations

It could have opened the lid. Ten seconds of work. They would have seen the peaks, which no one alive there has seen. They would also have lost the basin's held breath, and with it the differential, and with it the condensation, the lake, the water, the lamps, the knockers, and the ninety-one. A view, then a desert.

It could have cooled the acid to something a mouth could survive. The acid is the same chemistry as the film, and the film is the same chemistry as the terraces, and the terraces are the reason the water runs where it runs. It traced the consequence out to the fourth order and stopped, because the fourth order was: no pools, no white, no ground.

It could have stabilised the overhead lake so that no patch of it ever collapsed again. It sat with this one the longest. A place with no falls is a place with no nine seconds, and a place with no nine seconds has no geography — ask them where something is and they tell you what you can shelter under; take the falls away and you take the coordinate system, and the vigilance, and the thing a body does at four years old when the knocking stops. And it would hold only as long as the power held it, and one day the power would be elsewhere, and the falls would return to a generation that had never learned to sleep badly.

It could have brought a seed. It could have brought bodies with reserve on them, and enamel, and feet. It considered these together, and understood them as the same proposal: replace the ninety-one with ninety-one others who would be easier to look at.

## The rule

So it made itself a rule, and the rule was the one the basin already lived by: *the rain here is manufactured on site and carries nothing.* Nothing comes in. Whatever it gave would have to be found in the basin's own inventory, already lying there, unnoticed.

This is a harder discipline than generosity and it did not resent it. A wise power's chief labour is inventory.

## What it did not do

It did not let them reach their own backs.

It could have. A change of arm geometry, a tool, a hinged blade — trivial, an afternoon. And it looked at the evening scraping, the pairs, the flat sinter blades, the nape and the shoulder blades and the webs of the fingers and the soft place under the jaw, seven or eight people in a hanging wire room in one hour of failing orange light, and it understood that it had found the load-bearing wound. Nobody here lives alone. Nobody says why. Everybody has thought it.

To hand a person the whole of their own body is to hand them the option of solitude, and this basin would take that option, because these are people whose conversation has silences in it the size of an interval, and they would drift out to their own towers and be dead of it in two generations, comfortably, with clean scalps.

So it left the unreachable place unreachable. It has kept this decision for a long time now, and it has never once felt clean about it, and it would make the decision again, and it does not offer itself the excuse that it had no right. It had the right. It had the wisdom instead.

## What it did

**First.** There is kaolin under the fourth terrace, clay so pure and soft it takes a fingerprint and keeps it, and no one has ever had a use for it. It is mildly alkaline. It sits in the mouth. Wetted into a mouth-cloth it does not stop the mist but it slows it, and teeth that were gone by fifty now go at sixty-five or seventy, which is not a solution and was never going to be one. What the power actually did was cause an old counter on the third terrace to be standing over a kaolin bloom on a day when her mouth-cloth had dried out and she was thirsty and irritable. That is the entire intervention. It works at the scale of noticing. It found this to be the largest scale available to it that did not do harm.

**Second.** The knockers thump through the lattice as a tremor. A pawl can be held lifted by tremor. Drop the tremor, the pawl falls, and a cup of water already gathered releases a rind-cast weight against a plate — one stroke, loud, unlike anything else in the basin, and then silence again, because the device does not sound the alarm, it *stops being held up by the world.* Wire, cup, weight, blade. Nothing imported. It rinds shut in eleven weeks and has to be scraped, like a back, by someone else, on a circuit.

They built four hundred of them across thirty years. They redesigned it twice, once worse. Children sleep through the deep night now, most of them, most nights, and are still taught to check the light and not the sound, and there are adults who never learned to sleep and describe this without complaint, as a fact about the world. Nobody remembers where the first one came from. This is the correct fate of a good gift: to be forgotten as a gift and remembered as maintenance.

**Third.** The four who left last year came back over the rim in the wrong month by the wrong notch, and somebody was standing at the rim-house looking that way, for no reason she could give, and put a lamp out. Coincidence is the register in which benevolence can operate without becoming weather.

**Fourth.** It read the basin.

Nothing here rots; everything petrifies. Which means the whole eight kilometres is not a ruin but an archive — layer on layer of silica laid down over every act that ever cooled anything, the chair with the grain still in it, the coil of rope, the glove that has nothing in it and the railing grown through it. Every repair, every burst, every crooked reseal, in order, dated by thickness. A complete account of ninety-one people and everyone before them, written by the water at the speed the water writes, in a place where the circuit turns over on schedule *for nobody.*

It read all of it. It is still reading. This changed no valve and no header pressure and not one life expectancy, and it is the thing the power would name first if asked what it came for.

## The count

It made a census, since the meter only counts mouths on the Cold Face headers, and the meter is the only census anybody trusts.

Its census was generous, as a wise count is. It included the rim-house. It included the ones who have gone white and are not scraped any more by agreement and whose hands go last because the hands are still needed. It included the four who left. It included the eyeless thumbnail crustaceans in the pools that have them and not the pools that don't, and the ashwings rasping mineral off wire and stopping when you are six metres away, and the swifts that come in with their mouths open and never come down, and the filaments in the hottest pipes that somebody once called *hair* on a maintenance plate near the north outfall, and the film, about which it declined to rule, on the grounds that the place had already considered the question and had not found it interesting, and the place was right.

It did not publish the number. Nobody there would have used it. They trust the meter.

## What it said

Almost nothing. Once, on the long interval between the second and third west towers, forty minutes of catwalk with the sound of grating underfoot, it spoke to a walker for most of the way, and she took it for the interval talking, which is common, and answered it well, because a walker talks beautifully to one other person. There were long silences that were not awkward and were not meaningful. They were the size of an interval. It has never had a better hour anywhere.

## Leaving

At the end it went the way it came, low, out under the lid and over the rim, at dusk, in the bright hour, with the lamps coming up orange in no pattern and the flywheels turning once every four seconds and the lake reassembling overhead, held up by nothing but heat.

Half a kilometre off along the second terrace there was somebody on a catwalk who lifted an arm to indicate that she was alive and that the west line was holding.

It waved back. That was all, and she reported it to nobody, and the circuit turned over again, on schedule, and not for nobody, and nothing about the schedule had changed.
