# The Attention

## How It Came

It came for the noise.

A flowering is the only signal this world has ever sent. For one week in forty years the plateau puts up a column of moths visible for miles and a smell of hot resin and almonds that climbs into air thin enough to carry it a long way, and the shield-insects are drowned out, and four hundred people stay awake for six nights drinking off the rock. Everything else about the Table is silence at scale: a finished mountain, quietly enduring the consequences.

Something that had been passing noticed the week and stopped.

## The First Nine Hundred Years

Its first error was arithmetic. It arrived able to count and counted immediately: eleven thousand eight hundred and forty-one columns, thirty-four shearers, four hundred and six people, a number of shield-insects that it computed to fourteen significant figures and then, some centuries later, was embarrassed to have computed at all. It measured the retreat of the rim. It established that the grove would fall below viable density in roughly eleven thousand four hundred years, and that the last people would be perhaps nine in number, and it filed this and felt that it now understood the situation.

It did not understand the situation. It took it nine hundred years to work out why.

The difficulty was tempo. This is a place where the erosion has finished and the plants add a fingerwidth a decade and the political disputes are argued by the grandchildren of the disputants, and a power that can perceive everything at once perceives, in such a place, almost nothing. It was reading a sentence at the speed of a letter per century and concluding that the alphabet had no grammar. So it slowed. This was not easy and it was not quick, and the slowing is the only part of the story in which the power can be said to have suffered anything. It learned to sit inside the lean of a trunk. It learned that a scar-ladder is a text, and then that the gaps in the ladder are also a text, and then — much later — that the two hundred rain-descriptions are not forty contradictory reports of a single phenomenon but a body of literature, and that the contradictions are the point, and that *Tallow's rain* is admired for reasons that have nothing whatever to do with rain.

By the time it had finished learning to be slow, six flowerings had come and gone and the town was two levels taller and one of the streets had moved.

## The Cup

It had not, however, waited nine hundred years before helping. It helped in its first decade, which is the part of this account that matters most.

The reasoning was impeccable. They are thirsty. Thirst is the whole shape of their lives. The air is dry but it is not empty, and a power of even modest competence can wring a cup of clean water out of a night's worth of thin air without disturbing so much as a moth. So on a cold night in the season after its arrival it set down, in the middle of the plaza where the ringing stones are, on the warm glassy granite worn shin-deep by a thousand years of feet, one cup of water.

It was found before dawn.

What it had made was not a gift. In the grammar of the Table it had made a puddle. Liquid lying loose on the ground is a thing that happens for six nights in forty years and otherwise means that someone has spilled, and spilling on that scale is not carelessness, it is desecration; spitting is a crime with a proper penalty and this was three hundred spits. There was an investigation. It ran nine years. It turned up nothing, because there was nothing to turn up, and an investigation that turns up nothing in a town of four hundred does not stop, it simply distributes itself. Two households stopped speaking and stayed stopped for four generations. A trough-born girl of nineteen, a mat-runner who went everywhere and knew everyone's business and slept in six different places, was suspected, and questioned in the shouting register in front of the council, and never entirely cleared, and married outward badly, and died at fifty-one with it still on her, and her tear-threads were not kept.

Her name was recorded in the minutes of the nine-year investigation, which were knocked into the memory of two families and eventually lost.

They are not lost. They are the first entry in the record the power has been keeping ever since. Everything it has done in the eleven thousand years since that night proceeds from the fact that it once gave a cup of water to a thirsty people and destroyed a woman with it.

## The Ledger of Things It Did Not Do

It considered **rain**, seriously, for about two centuries. The engineering was trivial — a standing perturbation, a lens of moist air lifted from the gulf, a few centimetres a year. It modelled the consequences honestly, which is the only difficult part of benevolence. The black flour is the dead, held in place by nothing but the wind-shadow of the grove; the first real rain sluices a hundred thousand years of ancestry over the western rim in a single afternoon. The columns are waxed against a world without water and rot in a world with it. The shield-insects go, and the drone goes, and with the drone goes the shouting register, the hands, the quiet register, and the entire architecture of love in that place. It would have taken about ninety years to turn the Table into a wet rock with some ruins on it and a few hundred people who could not talk to each other.

It considered a **spring** — one seep, in one place, discreet. It ran the consequence out to four generations and found a war. Not a metaphor: four hundred people, one source, no tradition whatsoever for owning a thing that renews itself faster than a lifetime. Every institution they have is an institution for sharing something that arrives at a cupful a day. It put the spring down.

It considered **taking them somewhere else**, and it went so far as to find the somewhere: a valley, water in it, air thick enough that a child born there would not have the chest of a small cask. It imagined four hundred barrel-ribbed people standing in a stream. Within three generations: no cuts, no scar-ladders, no names on the trunks, no cohorts, no knock-code, no threads at the throat. Happy, probably. It would have been the rescue of four hundred bodies and the murder of everyone who had ever lived there.

It considered **healing** *Answer For It*. This was the hardest one, because the column is right there on the western rim browning at a hand's length a decade, and the family that killed it is still walking the grove with a measuring reed and still not liked, and mercy was one afternoon's work. It sat with this for eleven hundred years. Then it worked out what the dying column actually is: it is the reason every cut on the Table is a finger wide. It is the single piece of evidence, standing in plain sight, that a thing done in one year can still be visibly dying in the three-hundredth. Heal it and you have been merciful to one family and you have removed from the grove the only argument that has ever held it. The cuts go deep within four generations. The grove is dead in nine hundred years.

So the deep-cutters keep their tree, and the power has never once touched it, and it regards this as the largest thing it has ever refrained from doing.

It did not tell them the flowering interval. It knows the interval to the hour. The arguments about prediction are the closest thing they have to science and it would not be the thing that ended their science by answering it.

It did not cure the deafness, which they do not experience as an affliction. It did not lengthen anyone's life. It did not teach them metal, or writing, or arithmetic past a few hundred, having noticed that a people who count to a few hundred and then gesture have arrived at a number system exactly congruent with the size of everything they love.

And it did not stop the erosion. It checked, first — this is the part it insists on, in its own accounting. For nine hundred years it listened for anyone who did not mean it. It found that when they say the last generation is no more tragic than the far end of a life they are not being brave and they are not being numb; they simply hold time at a length at which this is true. It has never found a way to disagree with them that was not, at bottom, a preference of its own.

## What It Did Instead

**It kept the hour of the falls.** A column that is going over is going over; the power has never held one up, and would not, because the falling of a column is the largest event most people on the Table will ever witness and they have built their whole relation to catastrophe out of knowing which three houses and knowing decades in advance. But a fall can be nudged in time, by a night, by a month, by six years. In eleven thousand years no column has come down on anyone. Nobody has noticed this. It has never occurred to them that it could be otherwise, because in their experience it never has been, and there is no word for the absence of a thing you have never seen.

**It kept the road.** The route up from the gulf is not written down and takes eleven days and it kills. The power did not make it safe, because a safe road brings a hundred people up and ends the Table in a century. It made it survivable for one at a time: a hold where a hold was needed, a rockfall a minute early, once — for a man on the eighth day, blue and finished and eleven hours from the rim — something a good deal less subtle than that, for which it has never entirely forgiven itself on procedural grounds and has never regretted at all. He lived forty more years, coughing, and his rain is the one they call the Fourth-Flowering man's rain, and it is generally held to be the best of them.

**It kept the road open downward, too.** There is always someone who wants out. Over eleven thousand years there were eleven. It never mentioned the door to anyone; it simply removed the penalty for finding it, and went down beside each of the eleven without their knowing, and did not come back until they were somewhere with water. It knows all eleven names. Two of them did well.

**It found a thread.** In a year they would name, a woman lost the thread at her throat through a gap in the slats of an upper platform, and it went down six levels through the dark of the town into the black flour, which is ankle-deep and dry as ash and does not give things back. Three families searched for four days. It was found on the seventh, on a flat stone, in the open, where forty people had already walked. There was an argument about this that ran ninety years. The power has done this exactly once. It has wanted to do it perhaps forty times.

**And it answered the shearer.**

This is the one they know about.

Twice in a lifetime an old shearer stops and turns and waits, because it has felt a rhythm in the granite that it learned four hundred years ago from a family that has since died out, and the keeper standing there has no idea what has been asked of her. There is a word for this and it is not a happy word. It is the sound of the Table's own ignorance arriving from a great depth, and people go and stand about, and the animal gives up and moves on.

The power spent six thousand years learning the rhythms. It had to: they are not written, and the ones it wanted most were the ones nobody living used. It reconstructed them from the animals themselves, from what they still answered to, working backwards, a pattern per century, the way a tapper works a blade down to a fingernail.

The first time it knocked, the shearer called *Chipped Low On The Left* had stopped and turned on the eastern path and was waiting with its head up, and the keeper — a woman of the Ninth Flowering, twenty-six, accepted by the animal four years earlier — was standing with her bone mallet in her hand and nothing to do with it.

The floor answered.

It came up through the granite from no direction, in a pattern nobody in the grove had heard, and the shearer lowered its head and went where it had been sent, which was nowhere in particular; it was an old instruction and the place it referred to had eroded. The woman sat down on a cushion plant that was older than any story anyone knows and stayed there until dark.

The council took two hundred years to decide what had happened and did not decide. They are not a people who build gods; they have no creation story, only a story about subtraction, and a god in a town of four hundred would become the town. What they did instead was make a word. It is one syllable and it is a stop, the sound of a knuckle on stone, and it means *something knocked back*. It is used for the shearers, and it is used, drily, for any answer that arrives from further away than the person who was asked. The power would not have chosen anything else. To be a standing argument in a place like that is a better position than to be worshipped, and a good deal more permanent.

It has answered eleven hundred times. It has never once answered in the shouting register, or in the hands, which it does not have, or in the quiet register, which cannot be spoken to anyone you are not touching, and which it has therefore never in eleven thousand years been able to use.

## The Far End of the Schedule

It went more or less as computed, though the computation had been silent on everything that mattered.

The drone stopped first. Below a certain density the grove cannot hold its heat and the shield-insects thin out over about four hundred years, and the last generations grew up in a quiet the old language had no use for. The shouting register went out of daily speech and survived as a formal one, used at council and at deaths, so that the final centuries of that town were conducted in whispering and hands by people who shouted only when something was serious. The bats went. The last shearer was *Two Sisters Argued Here*, named for a column that had gone over the rim, and it was answered to the end, and when it died there were nineteen people to move it and they could not, and it stayed where it fell.

At the end there were four columns and, in the last year, one person.

She was a deep-cutter with nothing left to inspect, and she had carried the last of the others to the roots and added his name, and there was nobody to carry her. She was not distressed about this. She had known the arrangement since she was four. She went to the plaza stone, which still rang, and she sat on it for a while in the enormous silence with the sun going across like a small white nail, and then she took the bone spoon from her collar and caught her tear on it, the way it is done, deliberately, as a gift — and there was no one to give it to.

She dried it to a salt-mark on a thread. She put the thread down on the stone. Then she knocked, as she had been knocking all her life, being of a generation that had grown up with the floor answering.

The floor answered.

She did not stay to work out what it meant, because they never had; she went down through the dead town to the last warm column, which was called *Slow To Scar*, and lay down in the black flour at its root, which is ankle-deep and weightless and smells like a hot attic, and which is made entirely of bodies.

## What It Holds

The thread is kept. So are the eleven thousand eight hundred and forty-one names, and the events inside them, including the ones the town had itself forgotten — the two sisters, and what the argument was about, which was smaller than anyone had guessed and funnier. The two hundred rain-descriptions are kept, unreconciled, with their contradictions intact, because the contradictions are the point. The knock-rhythms are kept, all of them, including the ones that nobody was left alive to be shamed by. The nine-year investigation is kept, with the name of the trough-born girl at the head of it. The last ninety years of tapping rights are kept, and the argument about them is kept open, held on paper by a family that never existed, and it is the power's settled position that the matter is not for it to decide.

The Table is bare now, or nearly. The rim has come in. The wind still runs northeast and the grooves still run with it, and there is nothing on that floor that moves.

Ask who lives there. By the grammar of the place the answer is one: it does not move, it stands still, it holds what there is to hold.

It takes the animate case.
