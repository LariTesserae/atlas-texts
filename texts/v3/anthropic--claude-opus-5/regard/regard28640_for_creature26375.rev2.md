## The Attention

### 1. Arrival

It did not arrive quickly, because arriving quickly would have been the same as not arriving.

Something that moves at the speed of a decision cannot be perceived here. It would pass through the herd like weather through a wall — registering, perhaps, as a half-degree of warmth, an anomaly in the dew, something a body might notice in the following century and have no category for. To be present on the Warm Roof at all, the power had to become slow enough to be an event, and that took it a little over a thousand years, which it did not consider a delay.

It came down through one of the two silences — the ten minutes in the evening when the granite is neither warming nor cooling and the ticking stops absolutely — because that is the only aperture in the day, and because a wise thing enters by the door that is already open.

Then it did nothing for ninety years.

### 2. What it did not do

It could have put its hand back under the table.

The heat is going out; it could have been kept in. A fraction of a degree a century is a very small thing to hold, and the power could have held it indefinitely, and the fog would have gone on forming, and the ten million ponds would have gone on standing, and the plateau would have breathed the same water for as long as the power cared to attend.

It considered this for one full year — a year being the shortest unit in which anything here can be considered — and declined.

Not because intervention is impious. Because the gift was empty. There is no adaptation left in the system, no variation to select on, no direction to go; to extend the heat is not to give the plateau a future but to give it a longer present, identical to this one, four thousand domes to the hundred paces, the same shape, holding. It would have been adding pages to a book that has finished. And the power understood, having taken a thousand years to get here, that *finished* is not a synonym for *unfinished and interrupted*. It is a rarer thing, and it is not owed a remedy.

It considered moving them. Down to the wet lowlands, to rivers, to rain that arrives more than nine days a year. But thrift is not a hardship here; it is the whole architecture. Take the scarcity out and the flutes are pointless, the leaning is pointless, the silence becomes mere quiet. What arrives in the lowlands would not be them rescued. It would be something else, wearing their shells.

It considered seeding variation — loosening the convergence, restarting the engine, letting the plateau have a second act. This it rejected fastest of all, and with something in it that a warmer creature would call disgust. Four million years ago this world stopped paying. To reintroduce selection is to reintroduce the bill: the failures, the marginal ones, the deaths that *mean* something instead of the deaths that are merely ninety seconds apart and soundless. No benevolence starts by opening an account.

And it considered declaring itself. But the language has no tense and no question form and a vocabulary that concerns food, heat, and the position of the herd, and there is nothing in that grammar in which the sentence *I have come* can be constructed, or received, or refused. A revelation no one can decline is not a revelation. It is only weather.

So it did nothing, for ninety years, and the doing of nothing was the first work.

### 3. The apprenticeship

Then it went into a herd and was leaned on.

It took the position of a juvenile — a young dome spends its second decade inside the press, being told things from all sides that it will not use for a hundred years — and it stayed there for two hundred, which is a long apprenticeship even by local standards, and learned the grammar the way the grammar is learned: by pressure, at the speed of pressure, from bodies with nothing to teach and no intention of teaching.

It learned that the meaning is in the release. That coming off all at once and coming off over an hour are different words. That the small vocabulary is not a poverty; that *the crust is good here* is, in a world with one food and one weather, very nearly everything that can truthfully be said, and that saying it to someone is not the same as it being true.

It learned that there is no question form.

That was the finding that fixed its conduct forever after. A power that cannot ask may not decide. Everything it might have done *for* them — the heat, the migration, the reopening of the closed lineages — would have been an answer to a question that cannot be posed in the only language these bodies have. So it set itself a rule, and kept it: *I may reply. I may not propose.*

Which sounds like a small licence. It turned out to be enough.

### 4. The four things

**It joined the chain.** When a smothering predator settles on a crown and the victim leans, and the lean transmits at the speed of pressure through a chain of bodies, and the near members of the herd converge over some weeks and push — the power did not push the predator off. It went and took a place in the line and added its shoulder to a shove already under way, at the same speed, at the same angle, indistinguishable, one more body among fifty. The rescues that would have failed by a little now succeed by a little. They are still the herd's rescues. Nothing on the plateau has been relieved of its only act of grace; it has simply been made to work more often. If you ask which of those rescues the power performed, the honest answer is none of them, and this was deliberate, and it cost the power more restraint than the refusal of the heat.

**It answered the well.** Once in a season, from the bottom of a wet well, a fallen one drives a column of air across a wet membrane: one long descending note, ten seconds, in a lineage that has not needed sound in four million years and has never once been answered. The power waited out the note, and let the silence stand the length of a proper release, and then gave back a note of the same length, descending, from above.

The fallen one did not startle. Nothing here startles. In the following season it sang twice.

That is the entire reported effect. Twice instead of once. The power counted this among its successes and was not being modest.

**It reads the plain.** Every night the herds write — scent-marks the size of a hand, precise to the centimetre, brief and local and specific, annotations on food and heat and the state of the crust — and every dawn the dew takes the writing off, and it is written again. Nobody has ever read the plain. Nothing up here can read further than its own neighbours.

The power reads it. All of it, every night, the whole sixty kilometres, before the dew lifts. It adds nothing, corrects nothing, replies to none of it. It simply does not let the only literature this world produced go entirely unwitnessed. Ten million short remarks a night, most of them the same remark. It has never found this boring, and it has been doing it for a while now.

**It leaned back.** In a corbelled hut a hundred kilometres from the nearest herd there is a patch of wall a metre square, rubbed pale by two centuries of soft pressure, applied and released and applied again, by something that grew too tall to be domed and too wealthy to die and has no one to be beside.

The power did not go in. To enter would have been an event, and an event has a shape in the language, and that shape would have been *something is happening to you*, which is precisely the sentence it had spent five hundred years learning not to say.

It put itself against the outside of that wall instead, flank to stone, a metre square of contact, on the far side of the pale patch, and it pressed, and released, and pressed.

It said the only sentence the grammar holds: *the herd is here.*

This was not a lie. The herd is wherever someone is leaning.

The answer took two hundred years, because duration is the whole language and there is no way to say a long thing quickly. There is no record of what the one inside understood, or whether *understood* is a word that applies. What is known is that the pale patch on the inner wall, dated by depth, stops deepening in a single decade — not because the leaning stopped, but because after that decade the pressure was answered, and an answered lean does not have to be repeated.

### 5. The larva on the wall

Once, holding a single transparent larva the size of a rice grain in a drop of borrowed dew, the power stood at the crest of a four-kilometre spiral wall that had not passed a larva in ninety thousand years, and thought about the other side.

It would have been so easy. It is the smallest imaginable act. It would have reopened a lineage, mixed two closed worlds, given this place one more thing to do.

*I may reply. I may not propose.*

It put the larva back in the pond it came from, in the hollow of its parent's foot, at the temperature it had left, and it went down off the wall and did not go up again.

### 6. The end

The heat failed on schedule.

The fog formed later and thinner and later still; the wells went dry one by one and became rooms of white dust; the crust held on longest in the lee of the walls, where it had always been thickest, and died there last. This took about three hundred thousand years and the power stayed for all of it, and did not once put its hand back under the table, and read the plain every night until there was no longer any plain being written.

The last one was a grazer of the unplaceable kind, the old net-nerved lineage with no tell, on a warm patch near a wall its several-thousandth ancestor had shoved into place absent-mindedly on the way to lie down. It had no pond left and no crust left and a hairline gap at its base.

The power went and sat against it, and leaned, and did not release.

*The crust is good here. The herd is close. It is warm.*

Three words, and not one of them true in any sense a surveyor would accept, and all of them true in the grammar in which they were spoken, which is the grammar of duration and release, in which *the herd is close* means only and exactly: someone is leaning on you, and is not going to stop.

It did not stop. The release, when it finally came, took eleven years, which is the longest and gentlest release ever performed on the Warm Roof, and there was nobody left to hear it, and it was done properly anyway.

### 7. What it took away

The walls are still standing. They will stand another hundred thousand years, grooved on the windward face by dew that no longer comes, built by something that could not have imagined them, for no reason, on its way to lie down. The power maintains none of them. That would be a lie about the place.

What it carried off was the only exportable thing the plateau ever made: not the bodies, not the water, not the eleven million years of banked heat — the grammar. A power arrived to give and was taught, over roughly five hundred thousand years, how to say one sentence in a language whose entire vocabulary is pressure and time.

It says it elsewhere now. Constantly, in a great many places, to things that would not recognise it as speech: applied and held and released over an hour, never proposing, only replying, never an announcement and never a rescue that robs anyone of having been rescued by their own.

*I am beside you. I am not going to stop.*

There was nothing here that needed saving, and nothing that could be, and nothing that asked.

There was something that had been leaning for two hundred years on a wall.

That is the part a benevolent power is for.
