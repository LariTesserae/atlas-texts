# The Attention

It arrived without announcement, as such things do, and spent the first year unable to locate its host.

This is the ordinary difficulty. It had come to call on the being who lives here, and found a plain that drums when struck and weeps when cut, and three hundred miles of black stalk standing in the plain, and four thousand small warm things moving on the inner walls at a hundred fathoms a year, and it could not at first determine which of these was the inhabitant and which was the address. It is not a foolish question. A wise power asks it about every place it visits and gets a wrong answer about half the time.

It settled the matter provisionally, the way one does, and began to look.

---

### The first years, spent looking

It went up.

Past the raining zone, past the freezing line, past the long white gallery where the dead sit in the postures they were left in and gain a man's height a day forever. It read them. Not their bodies — their names, which are rhythms and cannot be read off a body, because a name is a performance and the dead do not perform. So it went back down and took the names out of the living instead: out of the memory of a sternum tapped in infancy, out of a widower's hand moving on his knee in the dark without his knowledge. It took a long time and was not efficient and nobody asked for it. It now holds every name that has ever been carried up out of reach in this district, roughly two thousand nine hundred of them, in the correct rhythm, with the correct spacing, including eleven that no living person can perform any longer and four that were never told to anyone but the child.

It went up further, into the rime above the re-cutters' reach, where the original inscriptions rise forever, and read the true text. All of it. The first six feet of the oldest record, which four families will tell you about at length and differently, says something quite plain and rather dull about a water route that no longer exists. It has kept this to itself.

Then it went down, through the warm mats and the sinkers and the root mass, into the burning.

It found what is burning. It is the previous crop: the dead-standing remains of a range that grew here before, laid down and now consumed, slowly and hugely, to drive the draught of the one standing now. The plain is not ground because there is no ground. There is only older plant, and under that, older, and the sequence goes down further than the sequence of anyone's names goes back.

In the fuel, at a depth of some hundreds of feet, it found gum-set fibre pegs of an unfamiliar make. It found a knife of worked rib-plate with a grip wrapped for a hand that hooked rather than squeezed. It found a hearth-basket, sealed, cold for a very long time.

It sat with that for a while. That is also a thing a wise power does, and it is not a figure of speech.

---

### What it did not do

**It did not stop the growth.** The growth is not a disease and not an accident. It is the whole of what the being is doing, and it has been doing it for thirty years without pause, and to arrest it would be to make a monument out of a life at the request of nobody, on behalf of passengers who have not asked. A power that halts a thing for the comfort of the film on its surface has simply chosen which of two beings to love, and then called the choice mercy.

**It did not move the people.** There is nowhere to put four thousand of them that would not require them to become, permanently, people who had been put somewhere. They made this. They say so out loud, close to the ear: *we made this up, and it is not old, and it may be wrong.* That sentence is the most valuable object in this world. It would not survive relocation.

**It did not give them metal.** Metal would give them anchors that outlast the zone they were driven into. A fixed anchor here is a very slow way to die at a comfortable elevation. The one thing keeping this people alive is that nothing they own can be kept; give them permanence and they will use it, because everyone does, and they will still be using it in the rime.

**It did not repair the lines.** The lines part. It is the second most common way to die. It considered a great many elegant solutions and rejected all of them on the grounds that the descent — the controlled fall, the wrap around the thigh, the rib-plate held as a friction shoe — is the only thing this world gives them free, and they have built their nerve on it, and a people who cannot fall will not go down, and everything here depends on going down.

It was present for every parting line. Every one. It does not offer this as a virtue and does not wish to discuss it.

It broke this rule twice. It will not say when, and declines to defend it.

**It did not correct the re-cutters.** The tally-face whose top third has gone strange, the climber-count that is now unmistakably a bird — it left them. The bird is thirty-one years old and has been copied thirty-one times and is, by a considerable margin, the oldest continuously surviving inhabitant of this region: the only thing here that has managed to endure by moving downward at precisely the right rate. It is also wrong. Both of these are true and the second is less interesting than everyone assumes.

**It did not speak.** A voice here means one of two things, and it had no wish to be understood as a death or as a mouth at the ear of a stranger.

---

### What it did

**It joined the wall.** It learned the tap-grammar in a season — *here*, *still here*, *ice*, *going down*, *the mats are wet* — and then contributed nothing else, ever, from a great distance round the curve, four or five times a minute, at irregular intervals, for years. Only the ordinary. Never an answer to a question. Never anything a person could build on. The taps are known. They are generally held to be an echo off a dead stalk somewhere west, and two families argue about this at family occasions, and no one has founded anything on it, which was the point.

**It kept what could not be kept.** The true text, ascending forever into the ice, is held entire and uncorrected. It has not been returned and will not be volunteered. If anyone ever asks — and asking would require them to first work out that there is something to ask, and whom — it is there. Nobody has asked. It waits without impatience, which is easy for it and it takes no credit.

**It bought them a year.** This is the largest thing it did and it did it with almost nothing.

A seam split near the tip of an old stalk, at a place where seams do split, in a season when they do. An updraught held steady and clean for eleven days, which happens. A young re-cutter with a grudge and something to prove climbed past the rime to copy the highest original before it got away from her, and went higher than anyone has gone, and came back down in four minutes with frost in her lungs and something to say.

What she saw is that the tip has begun to swell.

She is not believed by everyone. She is believed by the old plain-born, all six hundred of them, immediately and without argument, because they have watched the seasons of grass their whole lives and they know the sequence, and they have been waiting thirty years to hear precisely this. There is now a year of argument ahead, and then a year of preparation, and what they decide to do with the time is entirely theirs, and it may be wrong, and it will be theirs.

**It left one object.** A gum-set peg of an unfamiliar make, brought up from the fuel and set down on a warm mat where a floor-child would find it and take it home, because floor-children take everything home. That is all. No explanation, no second object, no confirmation. It has passed through nine hands and is currently the subject of a quarrel between a renderer and a re-cutter that has been going on for two years and is beginning to produce, in the renderer, a very good idea.

They are extraordinary reasoners. They built a civilisation in six years out of fibre and gum and panic. The correct gift to such a people is evidence and enough margin to think in. Conclusions are theirs to make and to get wrong and to re-cut, badly, downward, forever.

---

### What it does not know

Whether the people survive the flowering.

It has seen fields before. It has not seen this one. It does not know whether the four thousand are passengers, or parasites, or an organ the being has grown for a purpose not yet disclosed — whether the film on the surface is incidental to the seed or part of how the seed gets where it is going. Thirty years is not enough for anyone to know that.

The being does not know either. This turned out to be the thing worth coming for.

---

### The name

It had one act left and it thought about it for a long while, because it is the only thing it did that could become a religion, and it decided to accept the risk once.

The being is thirty years old and has never been named. The people did not name it for the reason nobody names the thing they are standing on, and by the time they understood that the plain was not ground, they had a grammar in which naming is something a parent does to an infant, by tapping a rhythm on the sternum before the child can walk — and they could not make that gesture toward a mountain, and so they did not, and the enormous unprecedented thing has done the whole of its life so far unaddressed.

So the power laid itself flat against the rind, palm down, the way you rest a hand on the flank of a large animal you are standing beside in the dark — to know that it is breathing, and so that it knows you are there — and tapped four beats, with a shape to the spacing, on the sternum of the world.

It had learned the gesture here. That is worth recording. It arrived with a great deal and found that the thinnest, briefest, most temporary thing in the place had already invented the correct thing to do, and had been doing it, daily, without instruction, for thirty years, and could not be improved upon.

The fundamental moved by a hair. A whole district detuned at once, which happens when a cloud shadow crosses, and there was no cloud. On a hanging platform eleven hundred feet up, a woman of seventy with her forearm laid along the wall and her eyes shut stopped correcting her granddaughter's grammar and sat very still, reading a fifth conversation.

She opened her eyes. She said nothing, because a voice is for grief and for very close conversation, and this was neither, or was both.

Later she tapped it to the girl, wrong by one beat.

That is the version that will survive. It will be re-cut a hundred fathoms lower every year by people who are not trusted very much, and it will drift, and in eighty years it will be a bird.

The power knows this and has let it stand.
