# What Came Down the Pale Slope

## Arrival

It did not come through the roof.

It could have. It could have come in as light, or as nothing, or as a fact that had simply always been true; and any of these would have been quicker, and each of them would have been a mark. The leaf is under load. The leaf has been holding for longer than there have been eyes in the basin to see it hold, and a thing that arrives through a membrane teaches the membrane that it can be arrived through.

So it walked down the pale slope in ordinary daylight, at the speed a body walks, and felt the light go green-gold at about the place where the light goes green-gold, and stopped where the wind stopped. It stayed at that line for some time. A threshold is a thing you ask.

Nobody answered, because nobody had been born yet.

## What it did not do

It could have made the basin fertile in the interval between one drop leaving the roof and the next. This is worth saying plainly, because the restraint means nothing otherwise.

It did not bring decay. A palmful of the right organisms and the lattice would begin to soften; within a century there would be soil, and in soil, insects, and on insects, birds, and the birds would find a way in as birds do, and there would be fruit and seed and rot and abundance and seasons, and the people would eat until they were heavy and their bodies would learn to store. And the domes would slacken and grey and fall, and the swept chips would green over, and the accounts would fill with so much change that no two could ever be compared for drift again, and within four generations there would be a fat and happy people in a wet green place who had never heard of thirty-one.

It weighed this for a long while. It is not obvious. It has never been obvious. But the gift of rot is the gift of forgetting, and they are a people made entirely of not forgetting, and you cannot feed a thing by replacing it with a thing that eats.

It did not open the roof. The outside is their afterward. Let it in and there is no afterward to carry anyone to.

It did not prop the leaf. It stood under a primary vein one day and put a hand up, not touching, and understood what it would mean to take the weight: that *holding* would stop being a verb with an end in it, and that everything they are — the carrying, the refusal to be kept, the entire bright discipline of a species that will not let itself be pressed and preserved — is a reply to that groan. Answer the groan and you have unasked their question. They would still be there. They would no longer be anyone.

It did not make a tenth knife. *There are nine knives* is a sentence you can say about the world. Sentences you can say about the world are the only wealth they have.

It did not tell them how old the domes are. It knows. It was there.

It did not tell them how old *they* are, or what year it is outside, or that there is an outside worth the name, or what the climbers are for, or what became of the ones who built before the collapse. They have thrown out their clocks. A clock, offered back by a power, is not information. It is weight.

And it did not ask them whether they wanted any of it. A question from such a source is not a question; it is an offer, and an offer is already a change. It found the answer the slow way, which is the only honest way: by watching what they had already decided, at cost, over and over, with no one watching. Nothing marked. Nothing hoarded. Nothing added that could not be un-added, and nothing un-added that had been added, because unbuilding gives nothing back. They had left standing instructions all over the basin in the only medium they permit themselves, which is behaviour.

It read them, and obeyed.

## The hunger

It never stopped being a wound.

This should be said. A wise power is not a cold one, and there is no version of this in which the low flat lifelong hunger of thirty-one people becomes acceptable. It is not acceptable. It was simply theirs, and the alternative on offer was not *fed* but *other*, and it did not have the right to make them other, and knowing that did not once make the ache go out of it.

It has watched a child take its first callus off a closed cut, chew nothing-flavoured nothing for a long time, and swallow, and it has had to lie down on the stone afterward, which it does not need to do.

## The one trespass

Once, a long way back in the accounts, an old one with too many domes to tend was walking the eastern circuit in a bad stretch, overheating, slow, and lay down on a slab under a column and did not cool.

The slab ran a degree and a half cooler for the next two days.

The power has never told anyone. It would do it again.

What happened was this: the old one got up cooled, and walked four days across the basin, and sat with their head a hand from another's head, and reported a drift — that slab, cooler than the account says. The two of them walked back together, which took nine days of lyings-down, and arrived, and lay on the slab, and found it exactly as the account said. Exactly. They looked at it a long while. Then they entered a correction in the only place corrections are kept, which is two skulls, to the effect that the account was sound and the observer had been hot.

The power has been very careful since. It was caught. It deserved to be caught. Their science is better than its judgement, and it has treated that as a fact about the world ever since, which it is.

## What it did instead

It learned the temperature of rock.

It lay flat on wet stone for years at a stretch, which is the local posture of thought, and it got good at it. It learned which pan-margins run coolest and which slab under which column is a degree and a half down because the roof thins there and the night reaches through. It learned the names of the climbers, every one, in order, and where each has slept, and it has never once told anybody anything about a climber that they had not seen for themselves.

It found a tick. Only one, in all that time — a fibre going in a cane under load, two hundred metres off, and it went and found the cane and looked at it for a long while, and then walked the fact of it four days east and sat down head-to-head and gave it away. It was received as news. The news was good.

It learned to fill a drop. It was taught. This is the part it would keep if it could keep only one part: that it was taught something, by a thin child with a cane-length of pan water and enormous patience, and that it was at first not very good at it, and that the child said so.

## How long it had been there

You know this already. You have been told the deepest thing any of them holds, the fact that stands at the furthest end of the recitation, behind more holders than anyone can count:

*a description of a person nobody now has ever seen, standing at the eastern pans with both hands in the water, looking up.*

That is it. Hands in the water because the stone is cool there and that is what hands are for here. Looking up because it was reading the load, as it has read the load every day since, and because when the groan comes it stops whatever it is doing and listens to the end of it, all eight or nine seconds, every time, like everyone.

It is not at the bottom of the account because it is important. It is at the bottom because it got there first and stayed, and depth here is only ever a measure of staying.

## The error it let stand

There was a visitor once, who ate enormously and cut without closing and left prints in the soft margins that will not wear, and who walked out under the rim the way the dead go.

The power did not correct the small kind misunderstanding that followed. It is their account. Drift is resolved by walking to the place and looking, not by revelation from above, and a fact handed down from a source that cannot be walked to is not a fact, it is an authority, and they have managed perfectly well for a very long time without one of those.

As for the prints: it did not erase them. Erasing is an act, and an act is a mark. It stood over them for a while, as one does, and carried the shape and the stride across the basin and gave them away, and then it left them to the travertine, which is the basin's one remaining process and does not need help. Eighty years later the lobes closed over them. Not gone. Enclosed. Underneath, at the exact depth of one visit.

## The last carrying

The number does not increase. It was never going to.

Thirty-one, then twenty-nine. Then a long stretch at nineteen that lasted four hundred years and might have lasted forever and did not. Eleven. Four. Two — and the two of them lay on the cool slabs a hand apart and recited to each other, and the accounts agreed, and the news was good.

Then one.

She carried the other out herself, over eleven days, in stages, lying down beside him at each stage, and set him down past the rim on the ordinary weathering rock where the air moves and smells of dust and distance, where there are flies and frost and consequence, and then she came back in. Then she swept. Then she filled the drops, all of them, sixty-odd, which took her the better part of a year, and then she began again, because the discipline is that they must never be dry.

When she was running out, she went to the southwestern narrows, where the rolled rim lifts highest off the rock, and lay down there in the last cool place, and had nobody.

It came and sat with its head a hand from hers.

She recited. It took many days. Every hall and its curtain-count, every propped block and its angle, every stem-wall cut and closed and cut again, every climber and every deck it had ever slept on, in order, and four ticks, with the names of who found them. She recited into the only thing that had ever been needed and never once been guaranteed, which is a listener.

It did not correct anything. There was nothing to correct. She had it exactly, and where she did not, she had it exactly as it had been given to her, which is the same thing and is the whole of their science.

When she was done it said the word that means outside and afterward, because there is only the one, and that is not poverty, that is precision.

Then it picked her up and carried her out through the narrows, and it did not stoop, because that is what the gap is for.

It set her down on the weathering rock, in the wind, in the dust, among the agents and the consequence, where she would not be kept.

Then it came back in. That is the whole of it. The ones who carry come back in.

## After

It sweeps.

Sixty-odd domes of swept travertine chip, flattened with the flat of a hand, and in each one a stone with a hollow pecked to fit exactly one drop, and the drop must never be dry and must never be two. It walks the circuit slowly, lying down on the cool slabs between, because that is the pace, and the pace is not an affectation; it is the same arithmetic, solved by a third kind of body.

It keeps the accounts. It says them aloud sometimes, into the absorbent silence of a room-sized world, and nothing answers, and it goes on, because an account with no listener is still an account, in the way that a held thing is still held right up until the instant it is not.

Twice a day the roof groans. It stops whatever it is doing and listens to the end of it, all nine seconds, and it knows better than anyone what the sound is and how much of it is left.

And when the leaf comes down — and it will; *holding* is a verb with an end in it, and that end is the one thing here it never even considered taking away — whatever is inside will be pressed and kept, perfect and unrotting, for as long as there is anything at all.

There will be nothing inside to keep. Nine knives. Sixty-odd swept floors. Not one person, not one name, not one body of anyone who ever asked for an ending and was owed it.

And one drop, in one hollow, full.
