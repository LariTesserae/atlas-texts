# The Attendance

Begin with the arrival, and note that there wasn't one.

Powers of this kind do not enter rooms. They are already available to rooms, and the only real question is attention, which must be finite even in them, or *benevolent* would be a word with nothing under it. So say instead that the attention turned — the way a head turns at a sound in the next street — and say what turned it.

The rivets.

Nothing else here would have carried. The falling column is only water doing what water does; there is a great deal of that everywhere and none of it asks for anybody. But twenty-eight repair plates set crooked from below and behind, in the dark, at arm's reach overhead, with a loose stone held in the other hand as a dolly — that is a sentence. It was legible from a long way off. What it said was: *still, still, still.*

---

The first thing it did was not help.

For something on the order of a year it held in the sump and listened, and it kept out of the middle on the low slack of the dim tide, because a wise power does not sit down in somebody's chair. It hung off in the dead water by the light shaft and it listened until the sixty-cycle hum settled into the sternum and stopped being a fact. Until the slop and gulp of the screw became a thing it would have missed. Until the two-tone shriek, arriving every six and a half seconds, meant nothing at all — meant nothing the way your own pulse means nothing, which is the hardest register of hearing there is and cannot be arrived at quickly, not even by a power, because it is not a matter of capability. It is a matter of having been there a while.

That took longer than expected. Powers are not much used to being slow, and it turned out that the whole of the work depended on it.

At the end of it, the attention could hear the wheel going wrong before the wheel went wrong. Only then did it let itself consider doing anything, because the first competence of love is being able to tell in advance what would be a wound.

---

What it considered, and put down.

**To open the roof.** Take the grating off, take the dome off, let the sky in on twenty-two metres of octagon and lift the tenant up into it. Put down on the grounds of eyes, skin, and eleven degrees, and then put down again, harder, on better grounds: the room is not a container the creature is in. The room is the world. You cannot rescue anything into a place where it has no work and no idiom. Freedom does not arrive at a hand as freedom. It arrives as a task or it does not arrive.

**To mend the machine properly.** Bronze bearings that will not sing. A bucket ring in some alloy that outlasts brick. Motion without loss. Put down, because read plainly it is a proposal to end the creature and leave the body sculling. That ending would be about four seconds long, and then there would be a very long time. The job is not what the tenant does. The job is the tenant. You do not take that in order to be kind with it.

**To explain.** To hand over language, and with language the bone-ring: the long articulated things low down, the wide flat hands, *there were others and you are the last of them.* Put down, and this was the most careful of the refusals, because it is true, and true things of that shape delivered into a mind whose single abstraction is *right-sound* against *going-wrong* are not information. They are injury. You do not give grief to something that has no grammar to hold it in. And the four-second pause is not a symptom in need of a diagnosis. It is a door, propped open by habit for a hundred and forty years. Doors are for going through. Nobody needs to be told about a door.

**To make it a companion.** Fashion a second of its kind out of water and iron and set it in the slack behind the wheel. Put down flatly. You do not make a person as an item of maintenance.

**To end it gently.** Some powers consider this and file it under mercy. This one looked a long time at a thing that goes out into the middle on the low slack with its hands loose and does not hurry, and found nothing in the room that was asking to stop.

---

Then the acts, and they are small, and they are all in the room's own materials — water, silt, grease, spore, iron, paper. Nothing was introduced. The room preserves final shapes; an alien object set down here would be a wrongness for a thousand years.

*Upstream.* The far thing that breathes twice a day was attended to. Not improved. Kept. And with it, the paper: a drawing that stays in the wrong drawer through four reorganisations; a schedule of assets for disposal that never quite gets funded; a survey deferred, and deferred, and then not so much deferred as forgotten in a manner that no one could be blamed for. The padlock on the grating rusts shut. The key goes into a coat and the coat goes into a skip. The sump appears on no register. This was the bulk of the work by a wide margin, it took decades, and not one second of it is perceptible from inside the chamber. The room's best protection was always that nobody was coming. So the power saw to it that nobody came.

*The grating.* Twice a year a leaf-mat, a crisp packet, and the crushed skeleton of a pigeon are lifted off the bars, so that the pale patch stays pale. Weather does this too, sometimes, which is exactly why it was chosen. Over eleven years the daylight square gains perhaps eight per cent. Nothing arrives in the room. The room simply has good luck.

*The fern.* The broken frond was not mended. Mending it would have been a message, and a message is a foreign object. Instead the sound frond was allowed its sporangia, and the seep down the second course was allowed to keep running, and the humidity under the grating was allowed to hold, and in the fourth year there was a second hart's-tongue two courses down, no bigger than a thumbnail, pale as the first.

Note what that is and what it isn't. The tenant has no concept of a plant and no interest in beauty. What it has now is a second station. The climb in the dim tide has two stops. The pinch of bone-grit, carried up in a fist held out of the water the whole way, gets divided.

The habit has a plural in it.

*The grease.* The traffic of the ferryman was left entirely alone. But in the third winter the rats failed — a bait line somewhere in the streets above, nothing to do with this room, the kind of thing rooms like this die of — and the housings ran dry, and for eleven days the tenant lay with one hand on iron that was going wrong and nothing in the world to give it. On the ninth day a pigeon came down the shaft. On the tenth, another. Squab is soft fat; it works warm between the palms; it goes into a housing under the pad of a thumb. That was the one occasion on which the power fed it. It looked like weather. It did not look like a gift. That distinction is the entire craft.

*The claw.* The crayfish under the fallen keystone is still missing a claw. Take that as a statement of method. Nothing merely alive was healed. There was no general improvement. Improvement was never the point, and a power that cannot resist improving things has no business in a finished landscape.

---

*The plate.*

Bucket seventeen went at the lip in the ordinary way, and the power took hands to meet it.

It took the hands of the work: too large, the way a mole's are too large, palms glossy and slightly concave, nails gone from the working fingers and left thickened and useless on the little fingers of both. Those are the correct hands. Any other hands in that chamber would have been a lie. Its hands started unmarked, and over the years they acquired the ridged old crush-marks across the fingertips and the pad of horn along the outside edge from bracing against brick, and it permitted this. Not as a cost. As the fee, and cheap.

It set the plate from below and from behind, at arm's reach above the head, in cold water, in the dark, with a loose stone for a dolly. It drove one rivet twice because the first blow slipped.
