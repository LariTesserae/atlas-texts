# The Visit

It came in the way that leaves nothing behind: with the beam.

At the eleventh hour of a day in the dry season, when the sun stood right over the largest of the selenite panes and put down a blade of light two paces long against the west wall, the visitor arrived inside that blade and travelled with it. This was the only door available to something that did not wish to be a fact. Every other route writes. Come through the crawl and the flour takes your foot and keeps it for ten thousand years, and a pup not yet conceived will step around the mark of you because its mother did, and you will have made a law without meaning to. The light was already coming. Arriving with what is already arriving is the whole of good manners in a room like this.

So for six hours it moved at the speed of the sun across a floor that forgets nothing, and in that time it did almost everything it was going to do, and nearly all of it was refusal.

## What it did not do

It did not fix the eyes.

It saw at once that it could. It saw the whole mechanism — the enormous wet night-eye, wrong for four thousand years, kept wrong by the invention that made wrongness survivable — and it could have thickened the lens, laid down a fold of pigment, shrunk the pupil to a slot, and given every rat in the chamber a day-fitted head by the following spring. And then, in three generations, no one would carry anything. The wrist would loosen. The callus would go, first from the newborns, then from the memory of newborns. The tilt would go with it, because the tilt is a negotiation over a scarcity, and there would be no scarcity, and two rats meeting in the open would have nothing to say to each other with their hands. The roads would broaden and then dissolve, because you keep to a road only when leaving it is expensive. The corner would empty.

It weighed this properly and did not do it. But it noted, with some care, that the argument it had just used — *the difficulty is the mother of the thing you love; therefore leave the difficulty* — is the favourite argument of every cruelty that has ever wanted a philosophy. It refused to promote the argument to a law. It let it be true here, in this room, for these thirty animals, on this afternoon, and nowhere else automatically.

It did not raise a wall to make shade. A wall casts a shadow and ends a civilisation: give them a hectare of free darkness and within a decade the fronds are dropped and within a century the fronds are forgotten and then the wall weathers, as walls do, and they are night animals standing in milk with nothing in their hands.

It did not grow a second fern. Their restraint at the fern is not a virtue; it is the residue left by the extinction of everything less careful. Double the fern and you dilute the residue, and in eight hundred years, when the second fern fails, there will be no one left who clips only from the underside, edges first.

It did not hold the roof.

This was the hardest, and it stood a long time under the crown of the vault with the whole thinning lens of alabaster above it, and it could feel the exact century, and it declined. Not because the collapse is beautiful — it is not, it will be an afternoon of noise and a great deal of dying — but because a room that cannot end is not a room. It is a jar with something kept in it. It had come to visit a place, not to found a zoo, and the difference between those two intentions is invisible from outside and total from within.

It did not resolve the corner.

## The census

It read the floor entire, which no one has done.

It read the goat: down the crawl in a dry August, four days of trying the wall, and the noise it made, and how the noise came back off the curved walls and how that was its own particular hell. It read the snake, one season, and where the snake had lain in the lee of the third slab, and the year it went back up the crawl and did not return. It read a wren that came down by accident and died of light in ninety minutes. It read eleven thousand rats, in order, the road-thickening from the bottom layer up, four hundred and six generations of a hitching three-legged stride with a scuff on the fourth beat, and it found the place, two-thirds of the way down, where the scuff first appears — the first rat ever to carry, whose name is a smear in compacted flour, whose invention has held its descendants' bodies still ever since and kept them all alive.

It read the shelf. It went down the road to the brine rim and stood over the low white rubble, generations of small articulated things tinted pink at the joints, every forepaw still wearing its polished thumb-callus, and it did not tidy them, and it did not name them, and it stayed there some while.

## The corner

Eleven flakes, three blades, one core. Four toes and a heel pad, crowded, four thousand years of visiting.

There is no puzzle. The hands were small because the hands were small. The visitor could see the afternoon the core was struck and the animal that struck it and what it was thinking, which was not much, and was enough.

What interested it was the visiting. A rat leaves a road — one of the very few good reasons to leave a road — crosses open flour, sits in the corner where the light is a shade less absolute, and picks up a blade. It does not cut with it. There is nothing here to cut that the tooth does not do better. It holds it. In a room where the hand has exactly one job for the whole of a life, and cannot ever put it down, the northeast corner is the only place where a hand does something that is not work. It holds a cold small heavy thing that its great-grandmother also held, and the callus fits the flake the way old callus fits anything it has met before, and then the rat goes back to the road.

The visitor understood that any answer it supplied would be a poorer object than the question, and that the reluctance mentioned in the record — *the reluctance is not the room's* — was in fact perfectly appropriate, and that the honest thing was to leave the corner exactly as crowded and exactly as unresolved as it found it.

It did lift one flake, the smallest, and set it down again in its own impression, off by nothing.

## The work, which was all done elsewhere

Everything it actually *did*, it did in the country none of them will see.

Thirty metres out past the pan's edge, it found the tamarisk — the landlord who does not know it has tenants — and it went to work on the tamarisk's chances. It shifted a lens of hardpan four spans below the surface so that the next rare rain, instead of scattering, would gather along one seam and stand there three days. It did this to a depth and shape that no survey would ever call anything but geology. The tree will drink slightly better in wet decades. The tree will live perhaps ninety years longer than it would have.

Then it turned one root tip. One. Some sixty centimetres of pale questing tissue that was heading down through the alabaster on a bearing that would have brought it out beside the existing chandelier: the visitor turned it a few degrees west. Roots descend a finger's length a decade. In two hundred and forty years there will be a second cluster hanging over the base of the west wall, and it will come down in a spread rather than a hank, because of how the fissure runs there, and it will make a lattice — patterned shade, the first architecture in the room that was not a corpse.

This was a purchase, and it paid for it in the same hour. Under the crawl in the south wall it loosened a shoulder of gypsum so that the next hard rain would bring down a slope of rubble two metres long at a climbable angle. From that season on, an animal that falls into the Pale Room can get back out of the Pale Room. No further goat will spend four days against the west wall making a noise that comes back at it off the curved walls.

Which means: no further goat will die there, and no further rack of ribs will stand off the floor at a slight angle, and no further pups will be born inside a skull. The room's cracked lattice will fail in some dry year and nothing will replace it for two centuries. It knew this. The two acts are one act, and it is a trade — mercy now, architecture later, and a long thin gap in between during which the shade-holders will have less shade than their great-grandmothers had, and some of them will die of that. It did the sums twice and let the answer stand. It is not a power that pretends its kindnesses are free.

And three kilometres north-northwest, under a thicker lens, there is another bubble in the evaporite: sealed, dark, ceilinged deep enough to outlast this one by twenty thousand years. The visitor gave water a reason to prefer one seam over its neighbours. In eight hundred years or so there will be a crawl there, and a seep, and a slow bright hollow full of milk, and it will be findable by an animal that leaves the crawl of this room and goes far enough in the right direction at the right hour, and it is entirely possible that none of them ever will. It made the second room anyway and told nobody, this being the honest condition of gifts.

## The tilt

At the pool's fresh end, in the hour when no beam lay on the water, an old female came down the road with a length of root held over her head and stopped at the rim and stood over the white rubble of her line, and held her frond at no angle at all.

The visitor was beside her, in the last two hand-widths of the blade of light, and it had by then learned the grammar of the room. It opened an angle: *I am staying as long as you are.*

She could not see it. She was, however, a night animal in a white room, and her enormous useless eyes had four thousand years of practice at reporting a difference in the dark. Something in the quality of the shade beside her was a shade too generous. She held still for the length of eleven drops from the roots. Then she drank, and lifted her frond, and went back up the road, and nothing was decided, and both of them were correct.

## The tick

At the cooling, the alabaster vault ticked — the long soft off-key note, travelling the curved walls and coming back muffled by the flour — and every animal in the room stopped moving for the duration of it.

The visitor answered it. Once. The same note, half a tone truer, from the crown of the vault, lasting exactly as long.

Every animal in the room stopped moving for the duration of that, too, and then resumed. The centipede in the root mass paused in the tendons and went on. The beetles cooked. Somewhere under a slab, sixty glass crickets became slightly thicker pieces of light and then, finding no reason, became crickets again.

That was the only sound it made, and it is the only thing it did inside the chamber that could be called an event, and there is no record of it, because the flour does not take sound.

## The leaving

At the fourteenth hour the blade of light reached the east wall, narrowed, tilted up the scallops, and went out, and it went with it.

The honest measure of the visit is this. Not one animal alive that afternoon lived a longer or easier life for it. The roads run where they ran. The pool creeps at its hand's width.
