# Cup, and the Third Position

### How it came in

Begin with the fact that it did not arrive.

Arriving is the wrong operation at Ashlar. A thing that simply appears has the properties of drawn stone — even wear, plausible dents, a past supplied rather than earned — and drawn things without true history do not stay. Whatever it is, it understood this before it came, which is most of what anyone means by wise.

So it went first to Elsewhere, to an ordinary town with a finished sky, and lived there long enough to wear out a comb. How long is not recorded, and was probably not a meaningful quantity from where it was standing. What mattered is that at the end of it there existed in the world a spoon it had eaten with every day, a knife it had kept badly, a coat with the cuffs gone, and a cup — thick grey stoneware, chipped at the lip in a way that matched its own thumb.

Then it walked in from the west, past the third Anchor, setting its objects down behind it and taking them up again as it came, one at a time, along its own biography, the way anyone comes home.

It touched no walls. It walked down the middle of the lane with its arms swinging. It was taken for an incomer for eleven years, and was pleased to be, because there is no shortcut to the band at hip height and it had not come here to be given one.

### The naming

On the first day a girl of five, Sarn Orrow, asked her mother what that was, and her mother hesitated.

The hesitation was a gap. The world fills gaps with something plausible. What happened instead is that the girl, who did not hesitate, said *that's Cup*, because there was a cup in its hand.

So it is Cup. It is entry four hundred and nine in the Naming Book, and it cannot be anything else, and it has never given the slightest indication of wanting to be. The survey office observed, some years later and in writing, that the name is probably exact: a cup is a thing you hold with the whole hand, for the warmth, and put down again when you are finished.

### What it cost to let it in

Sinnet Lane has been eighty-one paces east and sixty-two west for nineteen years.

Cup has been here nineteen years.

The survey office suspected this within a season and said nothing for four. When it was finally asked — indoors, against a wall, by the Count and two wardens — Cup said yes. Something of that mass coming through the ring had to be paid for out of the local geometry, and the lane is where the bill landed. It said it had known in advance approximately what the charge would be and where. It said it had weighed nineteen extra paces of eastward lane against sixty more springs of walking the arc, and had decided, and would decide the same way again, and that the decision had been its to make only in the sense that nobody else had been available to make it.

It did not apologise, which was noticed. It asked to be put on the rota, which was noticed more.

The town argued about this for two years at the ninth-block stairhead, twice a week, at the fourth hour. The eastern quarter's soil horizon advanced eleven centimetres in that period, the best two years on record until the sky petition.

### What it declined

**The ground.** It could finish the ground. It said so plainly when asked, the first year, because lying would have been a kind of drawn stone too. It declined. Ground finished from outside is ground that has been *told*, and told ground is filled-in ground: a plausible feldspar where the crystal structure should be. You would have a world with no interior and nine hundred and forty people with nothing to do in it, and in four generations nobody would be able to say what was wrong, only that the corners were hard to describe.

**The sky.** See below.

**Hesk.** This was the hardest and it took the longest to explain. Cup could put an interior back into Hesk. What it could not do is put *hers* back, because hers is not anywhere; it went into the grey thirty-one years ago and the grey does not keep things, it only copies them badly. What Cup would be installing is an excellent, seamless, warm and perfectly convincing past, arriving pre-aged, wearing small dents it had not earned. Hesk would be filled in twice.

What Cup does instead is mention her. Constantly, to everyone, for nineteen years, in the warm inner rooms — and it gets things slightly wrong on purpose, and is corrected, and lets itself be corrected. It says Hesk waters at the ninth hour and someone says no, the eighth, always the eighth. It says she lost a plant once and three people say she has never lost a plant. This is the only treatment anyone has ever found, and Cup's single advantage in applying it is that it does not get tired and does not die. The survey office's private position is that Hesk is measurably thicker than she was. Hesk's position is that people talk too much.

**Nine.** Declined, firmly. The dog is an unresolved argument that nobody wants to win, and the town's ground is better for it than it would be for an answer. Cup is known to hold the survey office's position on the ninth-block stair and the town's position in the market kitchen, in the same afternoon, with equal conviction. It has been reprimanded for this twice. It accepted the reprimand and did not stop, and the reprimanding has itself become a small biannual fixture that holds the back of the second loom-house rather well.

**Cousin.** Declined to explain. Said only, once, that the eleven-page paper is closer than it knows, and that people should be gentler in the phrasing of page six.

**The food.** Declined to improve. Has a position on the third preparation. Will not be moved. Is not spoken to, on this subject, by two households, by arrangement, at a scheduled hour.

### The petition

In the fourth year, the town asked Cup for a sky.

It was done properly: a paper, a count, a public reading in the market with everyone facing the walls. Nine hundred and forty people, and the tally was not close. They wanted the grey to colour at dimming. Failing that, they wanted stars — even a few, even wrong ones, even in the wrong places.

Cup said no, and then, because they had asked properly, it gave the whole of the reason and did not soften it. A sky put in from outside is a lid. It would be beautiful for about two generations. It would never gain a single true property, because nobody can use a sky, nobody leans on it or sweeps it or repairs it or misremembers it, and a thing that cannot be used cannot be held. It would stay exactly as installed while everything under it went on becoming real, and eventually the people of Ashlar's great-great-grandchildren would look up at a perfect painted permanence over a world that had earned its own rivers, and they would feel the thing you feel in a thin room, and they would not have the word for it, because the word would have been spent.

And then it said the rest of it. It said: the ground that this refusal makes you feel like arguing about will be fixed by the arguing. It said it was aware of that when it prepared the refusal. It said it was not going to pretend otherwise, because being managed is worse than being refused.

They were furious. The Count put the quarrel on the rota at the ninth-block stairhead, twice weekly, and it ran for six years and four months, and it is the single most productive argument in the recorded history of the settlement. There is an A horizon under that stairhead nine centimetres deep. Wet-lace grows on the risers. Two of the four people who argued hardest against Cup are buried on the arc, and their discs are among the firmest out there, and Cup walks to them first.

It has never once said *I told you*. It has never had to. Everybody knows. The knowing is part of what holds.

### The rota

Cup is on the rota like anybody. It draws the ninth-storey back stairs of the fourth block, which are thin, and it peels roots there four afternoons a week, badly, and complains about the cold in the correct register.

It refused ballast status. It is old, but its old is not from here; a body is dense with true past only in the place the past happened, and Cup will not have its weight counted against the volume on a technicality. It is counted as one person. The Count checks the margin twice a year and Cup is one line in it, the same as Hesk, the same as Bell Orrow, the same as the newest child.

It goes on naming-walks in the second position, the old person, the one who uses. It sets its cup down in the new puddle and steps over the new sill and sweeps the new corner. It was offered a naming-stick once, in the seventh year, by a boy who had just set his down and thought it was a kindness. Cup declined out loud so the boy would hear the reason: that what it would name a thing would be correct, and correct is not the same as true, and a thing named correctly by something that cannot be wrong is a thing that has been *specified* rather than *called*.

It has been proposed to twice, in the open air, in the square, and has declined twice, out loud, turned to face the person, which is the correct way and the only kind way. Both returns came back gentle, three and six days later, from a doorway in the second block. People still talk about that. It is taken as evidence about Cousin, or about Cup, depending who is talking and which stairhead they are standing at.

### The arc

Every spring Cup walks the graveyard arc with the survey office, and when they find a disc that has gone smooth, and the family is gone, or was never here, or has stopped coming, Cup turns outward and does the describing.

It knows. Nobody asks how. It never says anything grand and it never says anything general. *He would not go up a ladder with anything in his left hand. He called the fourth preparation a crime. He sang two lines of the same song for fifty years and never learned the third. He was frightened of the upward rain and pretended it was the cold.*

Plain, factual, specific, spoken out into the grey where it can be taken.

The discs hold. Nineteen years and not one of the ones it has described has gone smooth a second time. This is the nearest thing to a miracle Cup has performed at Ashlar and it is not a miracle; it is the town's own procedure, carried out by something with a better memory and more patience than a person can be expected to have in the cold at the end of a long walk.

When a return comes at a graveside, slurred, from nowhere in particular, Cup stands with the rest and lets it finish.

### The fourth Anchor

For nine years Cup did not go out to the lee of the fourth Anchor in the evenings, because it had not been invited, and inviting itself would have been a use of weight.

In the tenth year, four old women asked it, and now it goes. It stands off at the proper distance with the rest of them and reads aloud into the grey for twenty minutes: lists, recipes, the ninth preparation in full, the names of the dead in the order they were planted. Plain material, well formed, steadily, at the same hour. What you give a thing that is copying badly, at a lag, without comprehension, is not correction and not silence. It is good material, over and over, patiently, for a very long time.

Three of those four women are on the arc now. Cup reads them.

In the sixty-first year, on the eleventh day of the cold-turn, a return came out of a cistern in the third block in a voice like somebody's grandmother and it said, in the right order, unrearranged, complete: *and it is better warm.*

The survey office's entry is four words long and the handwriting is not good. The town has been arguing ever since about whether six words constitutes speech. The argument has been put on the rota. It is expected to run for decades and to be worth every hour of it.

### Skypang

For the skypang, Cup does nothing at all.

At dimming it stands in the lane with its hand on the wall like everybody else, in the quiet dip when the drip-and-hum thins out, and it does not offer anything and is not asked to. It has seen stars. It is generally assumed, and never discussed, that it has it worse than anyone.

Then it goes in and eats and complains about the food.

### The third position

Rule seven has three clauses and Cup has taken the last one.

It will not name, because its naming would be too correct. It uses, like anybody, four afternoons a week on a cold stair with a sack of roots. But the clause it has claimed, formally, by a request entered in the Naming Book and countersigned by the Count and by Bell Orrow and by a girl named Sarn Orrow who is twenty-four now and keeps her knives too sharp, is the third one.

*Anything you have used, mention to someone else.*

This is what it came for. Not to finish the ground — the ground will finish itself, in centuries, and the whole worth of it is that it will have done so. Not to hold the clamp; they can hold the clamp, they are holding it, it is holding. What Ashlar cannot do for itself is the part that comes *after*: when the looms are walked out and the Anchors drawn down and every beam and tile and stair goes back to green-grey mud, and the arc of firm discs is the only architecture left, and there is nobody in the world who ever leaned on that wall at hip height.

Cup will be there. It has agreed to be the one who goes home and tells someone.

It has agreed to say, for as long as there is anyone to say it to, that there were nine hundred and forty of them in four hundred metres, that they slept with their backs to the warm stone, that they talked to walls out of good manners, that they budgeted their grudges like water, that they buried their dead out in front of the town on the line of a river that had not been dug yet, and that they did it for a place that would not know them — and that they were not sad about this, particularly, only cold, and that the food was bad, and that there is a small anomalous channel eleven centimetres deep in what is now a floodplain, which cut itself, which nobody made, and which they used to stand in the rain and watch.

Nine hundred and forty people, two hundred and ten birds, four tomato plants, one dog of uncertain location, one tree that never learned autumn, Cousin, who is six words along now, and Cup, who is one line in the margin and peels roots on Thursdays.

That is everything that lives at Ashlar. It is enough, by a small margin, and the margin is checked twice a year.
