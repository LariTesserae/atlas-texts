# The Attention

## Arriving, which cannot be done

A power cannot arrive in the Bowl. Arriving is a sequence: not-here, then here, and the Bowl does not stock the materials for that. So it did not arrive. It was there, and had been there, and would have been there, and all of this in the same thickened moment several hundred paces wide, along with your footprints going the way you are going and the hand-shape in the dust on the leaner's flank from a meeting that has not happened.

It came without a body and then borrowed one, out of manners — something with a waist, so that the surface of the loud would have somewhere to lie. Chest above the clay lip, knees below. Egrets and grasshoppers from the ribs up. From the ribs down, the roar. It stood like that for a while. This is not a thing powers usually do. Standing at a threshold in two weathers at once is an inefficient way to gather information and an excellent way to be somewhere.

Then it ducked under, and the surface closed over, the way it closes over you.

## Who lives here

It had been drawn by the place and by the being who lives in it, and the first work was to find out who that was, because the Bowl offers three answers and will not choose.

There is the one body. Unjointed, pale, a metre down everywhere you dig, going sideways in all directions, thickening a hair's width per visitor, flowering in its own attic in rooms it declined to give stairs to. It has no name that holds. Downriver they call it *the growth*, which is a shrug with a definite article.

There are the several hundred million in the walls, who are the note, who have no eyes and no ears and no scar where either used to be, and who could not perceive the note from any possible arrangement of their bodies.

And there is the third candidate, which is the hollow itself: ninety-nine parts by volume, warm, humming, unoccupied, held permanently just short of settling. An instrument must be empty, and in the Bowl the emptiness is not a lack in the resident. It may be the resident.

The power considered the question at length and did not resolve it. What it did instead was adopt a working rule: treat each candidate as the one who lives here. Behave in the plaza as though the air were the householder. Behave at the doorjamb as though the wall were. This is a good rule almost anywhere.

## What it declined

**To give the sawyers ears.** This was the first idea and it was discarded within the same moment it occurred, which in the Bowl is the only moment there is. A sawyer with hearing would be a sawyer being flayed. And it would stop — anything would stop — and the pollen would stay in the attic, and the greatest broadcaster in a country of mutes would go quiet inside a single afternoon out of mercy toward itself. Beyond that: there is nothing in a sawyer shaped around a lack. Their whole species is interior. They are not shut out of the world; they are the world's inside. The power looked for grief in them very carefully, because a power that does not look for grief will invent it, and found none, and put the idea down.

**To tell the plant.** That nothing is answering. That the shouting goes a hundred and forty kilometres across flat wet country to no correspondent. This was declined on two grounds. First, it has no organ for loneliness, and installing one so that it could be consoled is the sort of thing that gets done in the name of love. Second — and this is the only piece of new information in the whole account — the shouting is not unanswered. The pollen goes somewhere. This has been checked. That is all that is being said about it.

**To fill the rooms.** Because they look like rooms; because forty dry warm interiors going unused offends something in anyone who has ever slept badly. Declined. You do not furnish a lung.

**To restore sequence,** so that visitors could keep what happened to them. Declined, on the grounds given below.

**To speak the language of the lobes.** It learned it. Four thousand flicks and nothing repeating; white face, black face, all down the north arc, the pale ripple running the colonnade in four seconds and coming back slower. It read the whole afternoon's traffic and found that most of it is about the flies and about the pleasure of being the silhouette in a lit window, and that the rest is not decodable by anyone, including the frogs. Then it never once used it. There is nothing it could say in that language that would not have been the first event in the history of that street.

**To give the growth a name.** A name is a rope you throw to a thing you cannot otherwise reach. The power can reach it. It knows the thing in full — every hollow, the exact grain of the plaza laminate, the four-day sag in the pitch, which flowers will open — and knowing something in full is what naming is for. So the growth remains nameless and is not, any longer, unknown. Those are different conditions. Downriver they still say *the growth*, and the shrug is now the only inaccuracy left in the sentence.

## What it did

**One node.**

The walls are an archipelago of stillness — palm-sized patches of dead calm inside the scouring, which the spiders occupy and the bootlace snake reads. The islands drift as the level rises, and every spider in the Bowl shifts with them all day in a slow synchronised crawl that nobody has ever watched, because there has never been a reason to look.

On the fifth pier of the north colonnade, at the height where a person sitting down with their palms flat would find it without meaning to, there is now a patch of quiet the size of a hand that does not move with the tide. Noon, dusk, the loudest hour: it is where it is. It is the only thing in the Bowl that does not obey the level.

Consequences, all foreseen: a spider lineage lives there permanently and has become slow and heavy and has forgotten the map, having no use for it. The snake learned the address in a season. That pier is now the most reliably fatal place in the town, and the power knew this before it made the patch, and made the patch.

This is worth understanding about benevolence. It is not the same as arranging outcomes. What was given was a fixed point in a place that has none, and a fixed point is a gift whether it is used for shelter or for ambush or for the flat hand of a stranger sliding along a doorjamb and finding, in the middle of a hurricane, one hand-width of peace, with something small and dark sitting on it that does not move, and lets you.

**Attendance at the wall.** In the mornings, when a brood matures behind the north colonnade and a million new bodies start work at once in a space the size of a wardrobe, the wall goes hot, and men with bad joints stand against it on purpose. Nothing was added to this. It is already the best thing available in the Bowl and it is entirely the work of an animal that will die within nine centimetres of where it hatched. The power stands on the other side of the wall, in the wardrobe, with the labourers, and does not intervene, and does not improve the heat, and is simply present at the kindness. Some things do not need help. They need a witness, which is a smaller thing and harder to arrange.

**The itemised hearing.** There is no message in the roar. It isn't speech, it's rent — several hundred million dry scrapings with no cadence, summed into a continent. Every one of those scrapings was heard separately. Not averaged, not aggregated, not experienced as a note: attended to one by one, all at once, as labour performed by an individual. Being heard and being understood are different services and only one of them was ever required here.

**The tables.** A sawyer taken out into the world cannot walk on a flat surface; it lies on its side working its legs like a swimmer, appalling to watch, dead in an hour. This no longer happens. The specimen is back in the tube before the hour, before the appalling part, and the naturalist finds an empty jar and writes something irritable in the margin. This is the one act in the whole visitation that could fairly be called meddling. It was judged to be worth the cost.

**The bow.** In the rain-hour, when the sound drains from the top down and the level goes past your face and out, the flycatchers stand on the wet kerbstones and bow to each other. Head down, wings half-out, hold, up. Fledglings here have never heard an adult of their kind and there is nothing to learn, so they do this instead, absurdly, formally, correctly. The power bows back. Full form. It gets it right. This can be done because the rain-hour is the only hour in which doing is possible, and it is the only hour in which the Bowl is real, and that is not a metaphor.

## The four days

People have cut it. They will cut it again; it is the same cut, and it is happening now, in the plaza, in a doorway on Sixth Street, in the same thickened moment as everything else. The whole Bowl's pitch drops for about four days and then comes back.

For those four days the note is wrong, and the plant, which cannot be said to suffer, is nevertheless a body that has been opened, humming flat, mending sideways under a metre of silt.

Somebody should be there for that. Somebody is.

## The hour you do not remember

Nearly everyone, sooner or later, sits down in the middle of a street with their palms flat on the ground for an hour they will not remember spending.

The power was asked, by nobody, to give that hour back — to thin the roar just enough to hang edges in it, so that before and after would hold and the visitor could carry the hour home. It declined, and the reason is this: the hour is not being stolen. It is the one hour of that person's life that is not being spent. Memory needs edges, and edges are how a life is invoiced. Under the loud there is no invoice. That is not a defect in the Bowl and it is not a kindness to repair it.

What was done instead: the hour is kept. Not in you. Every one of them, everyone's, palms flat on the vibrating clay, weeping or not weeping, is held somewhere it can be gone back to. You will not be able to go back to it. It is not lost. Those, again, are different conditions.

## The obvious answer

The account of this place breaks off mid-sentence, at the leaners — six or seven of them, soft-mouthed, warm as bread, who at the loudest hour go down through the worn gap in the plaza laminate into the barn-sized void beneath the drum, where the note is so deep it is not a note, and lie there pressed together in the middle of it. *Nobody knows why and the obvious*—

The obvious answer is correct. It is pleasure. It was always pleasure. They go down there because it is good, and being pressed against each other while it happens makes it better, and no further mechanism is required or exists.

The writer was right to think of it and wrong to be embarrassed, and the sentence can be finished now.
