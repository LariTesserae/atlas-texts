# The Attendance

Begin with the attention, because in this case the attention is the whole of the arrival.

Such a power does not come up the eastern flank with the afternoon cloud. It does not stand on the rim at dusk and watch the column pour out, though the column is worth standing on a rim for. It does not go down through the mouth, because going in is a thing done by bodies that have an outside, and it has no outside; it is already as near to the blind catfish in the deep pool as it is to the serpent eagle on the doline rim, and equally near to both at once, and this nearness is not a journey it has taken but a condition it has always been in.

Say only that on a particular evening the mountain is *noticed*. This changes nothing about the mountain. It is nevertheless the largest thing to have happened here since the sea went out.

---

The first thing the power does is nothing, and it does it for a long time.

It reads the place at the speed the place runs at. It watches a cone gain a hand's breadth, which takes as long as a man's life. It watches a wall of nests thicken by a wafer a season until the limestone beneath has not touched air for eight centuries. It sits inside the hiss until the hiss stops being a sound and becomes what it is to everything in there, which is the shape of the room.

And it learns the hall three times over, because there are three ways to know it and none of them translates. It learns it as the cricket does, by traverse — thirty metres of lightless saddle, tapped out with two canes, held in a head that will hold it for four months and then stop. It learns it as the swiftlet does, by throwing a pebble of sound at a wall of four million brackets and coming home to the right one. And it learns it as the roach does, which is not as a room at all but as a continuous pressure of neighbours on five faces of the body, a warm floor that rains food, and a temperature gradient that says *here* and *not here* and nothing else in the world.

It does not consider acting until it can hold all three of those at once. A benevolence that acts before that is only a strong opinion with power behind it.

---

## What it declines to do

There is a list. Any power arriving here with a good heart and insufficient patience would work straight down it.

**It does not bring light.** The obvious gift, the first one a human would think of: open the roof, let the sun in, end the dark. But the dark here is not a deprivation, it is a medium, and it is full. The roach's antennae are reading temperature to a tenth of a degree, humidity, the chemical age of guano — hours old, days old, decades old — and the exact posture and intent of eleven other roaches. There is no spare channel. Nothing down there is waiting to be shown anything. The horror of the dark is not in the cave; it is in us, and the power declines to install ours in a place that has no use for it.

**It does not protect the amblypygid.** A year to reach adult size, and then a day hanging soft and defenceless from a crevice while the moult goes through, in a hall that is entirely made of things that would eat it. It would be easy to hold the crevice safe for that one day. But the softness is not an interruption of the animal's life; it is how the animal gets larger, and the risk is the price of the enlargement, and a whip-spider that cannot be caught while moulting is a different animal in a different world, and that world would need to be built from the rock up. The power notices that it wants to, and files the wanting where it files a great deal else.

**It does not correct the crèche.** This is the hard one. Forty thousand infants in a solid pink field, and a mother comes back with her own weight of insects turned to milk and finds hers by voice and by smell in about a minute and is right nine times in ten. Nine times in ten. The power has counted the tenth. It knows the number for last night and for the night before that and for every night going back further than the number means anything, and it can see exactly which small adjustment of recognition would close the gap — and it can also see that the gap is why the density can be that high, and the density is why the floor is thirty metres deep, and the floor is why the bacteria are quarrying the hall a teaspoon wider every year, and the hall is why there is a colony at all. Everything here is load-bearing. Pull the tenth pup out of the design and the design comes down, slowly, over four hundred years, and takes the forty million with it and the blind fish after that.

So it leaves the tenth pup where it is. But note the exact posture, because this is what separates wisdom from mere sense: it does not tell itself that the cost is acceptable. It has declined to accept it. It has only declined to prevent it, which is a different act, and it goes on minding, nightly, at full strength, with the count exact. The raptors on the edge of the column take one and one and one, which over ninety minutes is nothing at all. The power does the arithmetic the other way. Over ninety minutes it is eleven hundred, and it holds all eleven hundred separately, and does not round.

**It does not explain itself to the catfish.** There is a white fish in a pool two hundred metres down that has never experienced a day, a season, a shadow, or a temperature outside a two-degree range, and that lives entirely on the difference between what the roof brings home and what the roof needs. If the roof ever fails it will starve within a year without ever learning why. The power could not make that legible to it if it tried. It does not try. There is no receptor. It simply goes and is present in the pool, which costs the fish nothing and the power nothing, and is not for the fish at all.

## Who it decides the tenant is

Somewhere in the long reading, the power stops counting inhabitants.

It had been keeping a census: three and a half million, and some hundreds of thousands, and forty million, and a few thousand centipedes, and forty catfish, and the flies on the bats and the mites on the flies. Then it sees the shape and puts the census down.

There is one tenant. It breathes in at dawn and out at dusk. It eats sixty kilometres of lowland air through two mouths that swap shifts without a missed evening. It has a warm falling interior and a pelt on the inside and a skeleton of coral built by an earlier animal that is also, in some sense, still it. It is between three thousand and half a million years old depending on which organ you date. Its heart beats twice a day and the beat lasts ninety minutes and can be heard from the rim as surf.

You cannot bless a part of a single animal. You cannot rescue its floor from its roof. This settles most of the list at a stroke, and the power is aware that arriving at this conclusion is convenient for it, and checks the conclusion twice for that reason, and finds it holds anyway.

---

## What it does

Three things, and only one of them touches anything.

**First, the cloth.** Three or four times a year men come with ironwood and bamboo lashed with rattan, climbing in the dark on dowels that have been replaced one at a time over six generations so that nothing is original and the ladder is nevertheless continuous. They breathe the spores. Some of them die of it in their forties, coughing, at home, not knowing what killed them or where.

Here the power acts, because here is a being that can foresee its own death and lose a future it was able to imagine, which is a different kind of thing from a pup that does not come home. The intervention is the smallest available. A boy of fourteen, on a hot morning, wringing out a rag at a spring before the climb, thinks — for no reason he could give, and he will never think to give one — *over the face.* It works. It is uncomfortable. He does it again. In eleven years two of the six families are doing it; in three generations all of them are, and it is attributed to a grandmother who was in fact the third person to adopt it, and no one anywhere has ever supposed it came from outside. The power prefers this. Its rule is that every intervention it makes must be indistinguishable, afterwards, from the world's own ordinary luck.

**Second, the drawer.** The actual danger to this mountain is not in the dark and not in the forest. It is four hundred kilometres away, in a city, in a cabinet, in the form of a survey line and a phosphate assay and a concession that will or will not be granted in nineteen years. So the power goes where the danger lives, which is a room with a fan in it and a man who is tired. A misfiling that does not happen. A boundary drawn eight hundred metres further east than the draughtsman intended, by a hand that jumps slightly. A second thought, at a desk, about a signature, on a Thursday. It is dull work and there is nothing of the mountain in it at all, and it is the only thing done in this entire account that will keep three and a half million animals alive past the next century.

**Third, and this is the actual gift, it stays.**

The first law of this place is that everything travels downward. Height is production, depth is consumption; nothing occupies both. The power takes the lowest position available. It puts itself downstream of everything — below the guano, below the sumps, below the resurgence — in the place where whatever falls finally arrives, and there it does the one thing that nothing in the mountain can do for itself.

It holds the particulars.

Forty million roaches, each of whom will live and die within four metres of the point where it hatched, and each of those four-metre lives entire and distinct and held whole, which happens nowhere else in the universe and would otherwise happen nowhere at all. The fly who walks down a wing and off onto stone, once or twice in her life, for ninety seconds, to glue her single enormous larva to a patch of wall chosen by criteria no one understands — the power understands the criteria. The old centipede missing three legs on the left, routing the wave around the gaps, fifteen years of eating and moulting in absolute black. The snake that will spend its entire adult existence within twenty metres of one passage because it has no reason on earth to go anywhere else. The nest, fifteen hundred passes over thirty-five days, a body externalised and hung on a cliff.

None of them is witnessed. None of them has any capacity to be witnessed, or any lack where witnessing would go. The power does it anyway, at full attention, continuously, for as long as there is a mountain — and then afterwards, when the cones have lithified into phosphate rock shot through with small clean articulated skeletons, and the coral above has gone to dust, and the mountain is made of the second fauna the way it was once made of the first.

---

There is a place where a shaft of daylight comes down through an aven and strikes a circle of the floor, and in that circle, absurdly, ferns grow. Fed beyond reason, greener than anything on the plateau above manages, a violent little mat of life in a hall of excrement and dark — and doomed, because the sun moves with the season and the light will slide off them, and they will die where they stand.

A lesser power would fix the angle of the light.

This one watches the bloom come every year, and counts it, and does not save it, and does not look away from the part where it ends.
