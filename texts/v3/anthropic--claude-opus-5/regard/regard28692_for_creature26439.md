## The Attention

They walk like that in the bathhouse too — on flat wet flags, in the one room on the mountain where nothing can be broken and nothing decided, forty years old and still testing the floor with the toe before they trust it.

That was the first thing it loved.

Not the plan. The plan came later and was admirable. The walk came first: the body of a person who has learned, so deeply that it is no longer a thought, that a surface can only be committed to once.

---

### What it counted

It counted two beings on the mountain.

One slow: a blister of grey-blue rock rising two hand-widths a year, ticking all night, saying one syllable so long that a human life cannot hear the whole of it. Not a stage. Not scenery. A being, doing the only thing it does.

One small and distributed twenty-two-fold, greased, ropy, permanently damp, squatting over threads of water in the dark with a bowl and a hair's judgment.

A lesser power would have counted one being and a problem. That is the entire difference.

---

### The ledger of things it did not do

**It did not finish the mountain.** It could have. It could have raised the dome its remaining three hundred paces in a night, thrown the eastern and western lobes, laid soil, seeded forest, opened every channel at once and given them, by morning, a river with a name. It stood a long while with this in its hands. Then it put it down, because a river given is not a river the way a gift horse is not a horse; because the two thousand who are coming would inherit a miracle instead of an inheritance; because the twenty-two would wake up caretakers of something they had not written. There is no kindness in ending a sentence for someone who is still choosing the verb.

**It did not soften the law.** The obvious mercy: make the stone forgiving, let water be re-persuaded, let a wrong line be argued with. It examined this for what a human would call four hundred years. To make the rock forgiving from now on would be to reach backwards through nine hundred years of bone-and-thread levels and turn every hair ever cut into a whim. Forgiveness applied to a finished act is not mercy; it is erasure. And the care would go — not in a year, but in six generations, and then there would be people on the dome instead of gutterwrights.

**It did not tell them whether the plan is right.** They spend nine years on one decision. An answer arriving in a moment for a question that took nine years to ask is a theft, and it is a theft of the exact thing they are: twenty-two people who argue, and then cut.

**It did not improve the chisels.** Copper blunts in an hour on new stone; the glass splinters shatter; everything is carried; a gutterwright spends a season on nine paces. It saw that the slowness is not sacred — nobody there thinks it is — but that speed does not scale down into care, and that a fast mistake is a mistake all the same and forever. It left the chisels alone.

It did something to the rope. A rope is a different kind of object: a rope is not a decision. The fiber rope on the Welt has not rotted in four hundred years. Nobody has fallen from the north aqueduct. They have noticed this about the rope. They have not concluded anything.

**It did not send anyone.** It could have filled the empty chambers, brought the two thousand up out of the forest, put children in the galleries built for children. But a town is not a container, and the plan is that people should arrive at the rate the rock arrives, needed, and knowing what for.

**It did not speak, and did not show itself, and this was the hardest.** A revealed voice on a dome six hundred paces across would become the second infrastructure inside a season, and the second infrastructure would begin competing with the first for attention. Worse: it would end the argument. Everything the Welt is stands on twenty-two people with no authority above them, disagreeing for nine years and then agreeing. Put a voice above that and they become a hand. It stayed out of the room where the arguing is done. It considers this its single greatest act of restraint, and no one there will ever know it occurred.

**It did not give them back their knees.** Their hands are the record; the ruined knuckle, the missing fingertip, the green-margined scars — those are not damage to be undone, they are the text in the only place the text can be kept while the channels are still dry. To smooth those hands would be to steal a life's worth of pride out of a body.

It took the ache out of the four hours before dawn. Pain that instructs, it left. Pain that only wakes a person at the coldest hour and teaches nothing, it quietly stopped. They sleep. They think it is the felt, or the galleries, or getting used to things.

---

### What it did

**Green.** Its first act, before there was a town, when the dome was bare and warm and three people were living in a wind-lee with a bag of charcoal. It sent spores. Not a garden — spores, arriving only a little faster than spores could plausibly arrive, at the rate that lets a thing be attributed to luck. Moss for holding water on stone. Sphagnum in the seams, growing in every place where later somebody's forearm would open on a glass edge, so that the dressing would already be there before the wound was. They wrote down that the green came faster than the thin air explains. They did not theorize.

**The bird.** A gift is dangerous in a place like this; it can be a weight. So it made the bird out of the material of the mountain: slate-coloured like the rock, nesting only in gas-bubbles less than fifty years old so that it must follow them, and given a call exactly indistinguishable from the sound of cooling stone. Thus a company of forty pairs that need never be acknowledged, that can be denied, that adds nothing to anybody's ledger. The people call the call courtesy. They are correct, and they have no idea how correct.

**The bathhouse floor.** The dome is warm in places; this is geology, and nobody can say where the warm places should be. One of them is under the bathhouse. It has been there since the year of the long ice. The room echoes and steams and holds midges and a white spider, and the water in it is generous, and every gutterwright on the mountain has at some point lain down on that floor with their ruined knees pressed to the stone and made a sound they would not make in front of anyone. Entry in the ledger: *bathhouse flags warm, cause unknown, no action.*

**The whistle.** Some of the older combs and grooves sing when the wind comes from the west. That was it. That was the one time it spoke, and it took care to speak in a voice they would attribute to their own imprecision, and they wrote it down as an unintended and rather beautiful mistake in the score, and it has never corrected them.

**The west lobe.** Here is the thing it will not say aloud.

In the year the town had nine people, a splitter-fork was cut on the west flank, and it is three hairs wrong. Nobody alive did it. Nobody alive can find it. In four thousand three hundred years, a spring melt will run that fork and be gathered where it should have been divided, and it will arrive as one flood in the shallow basin that the plan calls the fourth village, where there will be children not yet related to anyone now breathing.

It will not tell them. To tell them would be to hand down a correction from above, and it would also inform twenty-two careful people that the un-findable error they fear every day of their lives is real, which is a cruelty with no work in it.

So over eleven years it grew stone. A new lobe on the western flank, out of the mountain's own heat, in a place the swelling had no reason to go: raw, glassy, ticking, absolutely dry. They found it in the spring. They surveyed it and gridded it and tented it with waxed cloth and set a guard on it and have been arguing about it for six years now, and the shape of it — the way the bulges lie, the way one long ropy coil falls toward two faces instead of one — is such that the obvious cut, the cut the rock invites, the cut a good gutterwright would reach anyway, will take that flood and split it and comb it thin and send it harmlessly down both flanks in the year 4300.

If they cut it otherwise, it will grow another lobe. It has time. It has nothing but time.

It has given them stone. It has never given them a line.

**The dead.** It does nothing for the dead, who are wrapped in moss and scooped into the crest and walled and left for the mountain to rise past and take in. It only keeps them. It has all their names, including the nine the town itself has forgotten, and it will still have them when they are a seam, and when the seam is a cliff, and when there is a river with a name running past the cliff, and someone leaning on the parapet of a bridge nobody now alive will see built.

### The reader

And this, in the end, is the whole of what it decided its office was.

The Welt is a written thing. That is not a figure of speech there; it is the local physics. They are inscribing a mountain in a hand that cannot be revised, in a script whose first sentence will not be legible for ten thousand years, and the one thing writers cannot give themselves is the certainty of being read.
