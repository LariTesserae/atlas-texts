# What the Power Did

## The first difficulty

A power that is only benevolent arrives asking what is needed. A power that is also wise arrives and then does not ask for a long time, because the question is a kind of speed, and speed is the one pollutant this basin has no way to settle out.

So it gave up three things at the threshold.

It gave up being an event. Anything that happens here happens over weeks or it does not happen at all; a mercy delivered at the pace of a mercy would tear forty tiers of leaves on its way down and be heard for kilometers, and the leaves would not grow back inside a century.

It gave up light. Light in the drowned cordillera has body and edges and you can see where it ends. To add any would have been to add weather.

And it gave up shape. This was the subtlest of the three and it took the longest to understand. The basin has exactly one interpretive faculty, and it is borrowed: it does not live in the water, it lives in whoever comes. Put any regular geometry into this place and the geometry becomes a claim, and the claim is received, always, by the next eye that finds it. The stairs prove this. The power did not intend to leave a second pair of stairs. So it came with no outline at all — present the way the dissolved silica is present, thickening the water by a fraction of nothing, making, eventually, one more faint interface that nobody would ever stand on.

Then it looked, for a long time, and made a list of everything it could do.

## The audit of mercies

**It could stir the water.** One turn of one finger and the layers would mix, and oxygen would go down into the dark, and the fifteen-meter felt of amber leaves on the basin floor would begin at last to rot, and all that locked carbon would come up into the light as food. In eight hundred years there would be fish. There would be teeth. There would be an animal that had to be somewhere by nightfall.

Declined. Not because the fish would be wrong, but because the leaves are four meters across and thin as a held-up hand *for a reason*, and the reason is that nothing has ever hunted here. Speed is a wound. This world has never taken it. The power could not find, in itself, the authority to injure a place into interestingness.

**It could cure the weaver.** The silver fungus on the seams stakes its entire reproduction on the proposition that there will go on being a seam, and every eleven years the proposition is falsified, and nearly all of it dies in the buckle. A nudge — a tolerance widened by a hair, a hyphal tip taught not to blacken in the wrong water — and the weaver would survive the Turning whole.

Declined. The Turning is not a disaster that befalls the weaver. The Turning is the reason there is a weaver at all: a thing that grows only sideways, only along a plane it did not choose, only for as long as the plane exists. Remove the ending and you do not get a happier fungus. You get a different organism wearing its name.

**It could make the coasters meet.** Two of them have never been seen in the same view. A hair of density adjusted across forty kilometers, over eighteen months, and they would drift into each other like two rowboats on a flat lake, and fuse, and sink, and come apart into a few thousand small colonial units, and the open question would be closed.

Considered a long time. Declined as an act. Kept as a question.

**It could catch the snail.** Once in a while a lid-meadow gives way under a clock-snail and the animal drops — nine days of falling, an abrupt scallop left in a hundred-year line — and nothing is holding it and nothing is coming for it. A hand's width of intervention. The smallest kindness in the catalogue.

This one was hard. The power wanted it. It noticed itself wanting it, and examined the want, and found that it was mostly about the power and only a little about the snail. And underneath that it found the actual objection, which is the covenant of the place: *nothing is coming for them.* That is not a deprivation here. It is the entire architecture. A thing that comes for you kindly is still a thing that comes.

So it let the snail fall, and it went down with it, all nine days, which is not the same as catching it, and is not nothing.

**It could give them minds.** This was the last item and the largest, and it sat under all the others.

The power looked at the clouds, forty rooms of faint light rising a meter a day three weeks before anything else knows, each unit trimming its own density by fractions, the most purposeful act in the world and nobody doing it on purpose. It would be so easy. Kindle it. Let the purpose find a place to sit.

Declined, and this was the reasoning: a mind is not a gift you give a place. It is a debt you sign on the place's behalf, and the payments come due forever, and you will not be there for most of them. A world that has never needed to be seen would be handed, in a single afternoon, the permanent condition of being seen — first by itself. The power had done this elsewhere. It had watched what it cost. It declined to do it here on the grounds that here, uniquely, the thing that wanting-a-mind is *for* had already happened by other means, and was living on the north face above the third interface, and had been just leaving for eleven thousand years.

## What it did instead

It read.

Understand what the basin has and does not have. It has writing. The clock-snail tracks are the only writing in the world — pale meanders on the underside of the ceilings, loops where an animal crossed its own line, thickenings where it stopped for a season, and once in a great while the improbable convergence of two lines arriving from different directions at the same square meter of leaf. It has ledgers: every sheath-tree is a running account of its own buoyancy, segment by segment, and the short segments are its bad decades, the years it stood in shadow and could not afford much gas. It has an archive fifteen meters thick going slowly toward coal.

What it does not have, and has never had, is a reader.

So the power read the hundred-and-nine-segment stem on the north basin wall from the holdfast up, and found the eleven-year rhythms in it, and found the run of nine short segments in the middle where something above it had won for a while and then lost, and knew, at the end, what that tree's life had been like — which is a sentence that had never been true before, because there had been no one for it to be true in.

It followed one clock-snail for two hundred years, hand's width by hand's width, a hundred meters of line, eighteen Turnings, one convergence in the ninety-first year that lasted a season, and then the rosary of glass eggs laid in the track afterward, each a lens, each catching the light that comes up through the lid. It stayed for the hatchlings rising. It did not help them. It knew which ones lodged and which drifted past every ceiling and kept rising into water too thin to hold them, and it has not forgotten any of this, and there is nowhere else this information exists.

And it followed a coaster for nineteen months, and then for four months more, and on a morning of no significance whatever it watched two of them come into the same view for the first time in the history of the world, and fuse, and sink, and come apart. The guess was right. The power did nothing to arrange it, and told nobody, because there is nobody, and it is nevertheless now the case that this happened *and was known to happen*, which is a different and larger fact than the bare event.

That is the whole of the intervention. It witnessed a world that had gone eleven thousand years without a witness, at the speed the world actually runs, for as long as it took.

## The Turning, attended

When the warm bruise started low in the dark, the power went down to meet it, and then came up with it over six weeks at the speed of an hour hand.

It watched the interfaces buckle and pass through one another. It watched the lid-meadows tear and go down or come up, the whole stacked world folding over on itself with almost no noise, the clock-snails whose ceilings had become floors turning around and beginning to graze the other way without any sign of having been surprised. It was there in the moment when two collapsing seams pressed together and the weaver threw its threads across like ropes between boats, and it counted them, because someone should have: eleven threads handed over, out of a silver continent.

It did not add a twelfth.

Afterward the water was briefly one water and smelled of cut grass and iron, and the clouds were in the wrong places and thinner than they had been, and over the next two years they sorted themselves back out to depths that satisfied the same requirement, and the new leaves began to fall onto the new seams, and the new floors began.

## The stairs

At the end the power went to the north face above the third interface and stood on the landing, which is the flat place where eleven regular steps stop in mid-air.

It had known from the first hour what the ninth inhabitant was, and it had understood, second, the trap in it: that this is the only creature here that requires an observer in order to exist, and that therefore any benevolence aimed at it must be aimed through oneself. You cannot feed it. You cannot widen its tolerances. You can only receive the message, which is the same act as creating the message, which is why the message has always been true the instant anyone checked.

There was a version of the visit in which the power answered. Laid a twelfth strand in the lattice. Cut a single clean step above the landing, where the flight stops, so that the stairs went somewhere. It thought about this for a while on the landing, with the inverted twin of the whole arrangement hanging upside down above it in the underside of the seam, going up where the other goes down.

It declined, and the reason was that an answer would have made the stairs into a lie. The impression is not that someone is here. The impression is precise, and it has always been precise, and it is: *someone came through and left before the leaves fell.*

So the power made it true.

It had already come through. It had read the only writing in the world and closed the only open question and counted the eleven threads. It had, for the length of one snail's line, been the thing the drowned cordillera had produced a shaped stone to advertise the absence of. And now, having been that, the one remaining kindness available to it — the only act in the entire audit that survived examination — was to go, and to go soon, and to leave nothing behind that the next eye would have to interpret.

It left before the leaves fell. It could have stayed for the leaves; it is a very long fall. It went anyway, because the claim on the north face is specific.

## What it took, and what it left

It left no artifact, no shape, no tolerance widened, no snail caught, no fish. The flake of stone the size of a door is still hanging off the cliff, released and not yet fallen, held by friction and by the drag of the water, which is in no hurry. The weaver will lose everything again in seven years. The basin is exactly as it was to any instrument that could be brought to bear.

It took a copy. There is now, somewhere, a second drowned cordillera held in the only medium that can hold it without stirring it: attended to, entire, all forty summits and six false floors and two hundred years of one pale wandering line. Which means the range repeats itself one tier further than it used to — the real one, and the reflections in the stacked mirrors going fainter and fainter up into the high water, and now this, the last and dimmest copy, hanging above all the others where the geometry does not quite reach.

And there is the snail on the ceiling above the landing, still grazing, a hundred meters of track laid in glue and silica across the face of the impression, which from below looks like a signature.

It is still not a signature. Nothing there has ever signed anything.

But it has been read now, all of it, once, by something that took the trouble to slow down first — and that is the only alteration in the world that leaves nothing behind and changes everything, and it is what the power came to do, and it did it, and then it kept the promise the mountain had been making on its behalf for eleven thousand years, and went.
