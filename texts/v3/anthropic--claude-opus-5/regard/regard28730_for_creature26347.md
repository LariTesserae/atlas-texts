# The Collection

## The arrival

It could have come through the lid. It came up the ramp.

That was the first decision and the largest: to enter from below like anything else, out of warmth into more warmth, out of dark into a different quality of dark, and to learn the place the way the place is learned, which is by hand, in the wrong order, slowly, getting it wrong. It brought no light. Nothing on the plateau would have been served by light and a great deal would have been contradicted by it, and a power that arrives contradicting is not wise, whatever else it is.

It spent — call it a long time. The Stand's clock runs at one stroke every eleven seconds and everything here is priced in strokes, and by that currency the visit was expensive.

Most of it was spent touching things.

## What it did not do

It did not bring water. Water would have been read as an attack by every valve on the plateau and would have been correct, and the mercy would have gone into a mineral nodule and been carried out to the Pan and refused, and something would have been spent cooking the nodule that the Stand could not afford. A gift that has to be excreted is not a gift.

It did not tear off the lid. Aeons of exhaled lipid, and under it a whole civilisation of appetite calibrated to exactly this much blanket: strip it and the vanes ice and crack, the thermosiphon stalls, the manifolds lose their reference, and eight hundred thousand towers cook themselves inside a day out of sheer relief. The debt is real. The debt is also load-bearing.

It did not give the Fitters eyes. Nine days is not short. Nine days is the length of that life, and to hand a nine-day body a new sense at hour one is to spend its whole span teaching it to be confused.

It did not give the tall one a voice. There is nobody here to talk to. A voice would have been a wound with a mouth.

It did not cut the Combs' meristems back from the Slack boundary and let the heretics out. The plateau's inheritance is not a visitor's business. You do not choose the heir of a house in which you are a guest.

It considered, for a long stretch of strokes, doing nothing at all: withdrawing, leaving the place unentered and unimproved and unwitnessed, which is a defensible answer and sometimes the only one.

The bench is what argued it out of that.

A room with a doorway sized for a body, and a stair with a rise that fits that body's leg, and a bench, and a groove in the bench — that is not a habitat. That is a place set. It is an invitation, and the invitation is very old, and a very old invitation is still an invitation. Everything else here could be left alone in good conscience. But to be offered a seat for aeons and decline it forever is the one cruelty that was actually on the table.

So it accepted.

## The reading

The dials were made for eyes.

Hairline needles, graduated arcs, faces polished to a shine that has never once been used, and over each face the flat pale feeler lying across it like a hand across a page, riding the needle, reporting by touch. An instrument rebuilt for a reader who never came, and then the reader's fingertip grown and the rest of the reader left out.

It made light for those. Only for those, only at the face, only for as long as it took — a small cold light, the wrong colour for anything here, cupped so it went nowhere else. Then it lifted the feeler aside, gently, the way you lift a sleeping hand off a page, and it read the dial.

It read every dial in the Combs. This took most of the visit. When it had read one it laid the feeler back exactly where the feeler had been, so that the Stand's own reading was not disturbed by having been shared.

Somewhere in the fourth or fifth month of this it found a gauge in a return manifold under the Radiators reading a pressure that had been wrong since before the plan was finished. Wrong by a little. Wrong in a way that no comparator downstream could vote on, because the wrongness was upstream of the vote — a valve-body seated a fifth of a turn off true, cured that way, gauged that way, and every spring in every manifold for kilometers calibrated in good faith against the error.

It turned the valve. A fifth of a turn, by hand, in the dark, in the Stand's own idiom, at the one place the Stand could not reach.

It took eight thousand strokes for the district to notice and another day for the news to finish arriving, and then four hundred valve-bodies shifted by fractions of a millimeter each and the manifold settled into a truth it had never held, and nothing else happened, and nothing was grateful. That is the correct response. Gratitude would have meant something was listening, and if something had been listening, the fifth of a turn would have been an insult instead of a repair.

## The vent

It made one hole in the lid.

High up, over the Radiators, small — the size of a room, no more — and it made it in such a way that the Stand could find it or not.

The Stand found it. Within the year the towers under the hole were climbing into the cold column, and the vanes there ran three degrees colder than orthodox, and the district reorganised around the new gradient, and the manifolds grew a fresh set of gauges to watch it, with fresh feelers to read the gauges, and the plan absorbed the miracle as weather.

It did not save the Stand. The breath still gathers, the ceiling still thickens, the fingernail-thickness of laminate still goes down every day against a debt that compounds. What the hole bought was time, which is all anybody has ever managed to give anybody, and the size of the hole was chosen so that the time arrived as good weather rather than as an event. A gift that forces the recipient to become someone else is not benevolence. It is just a large opinion.

## A shift

It worked, once, for a week, in a chamber off a belt line in the north Combs.

The grips did not fit. They were never going to — they were cut for a hand with four flexures to the digit, and that hand belongs to whoever placed the order, and to the Fitters who are grown weekly to match a handle designed for somebody else, and to nobody else in creation. It worked clumsily and left the pulleys slightly over-oiled and squared the stacks by feel and got the stacks crooked. Three Fitters shared the chamber with it and registered it as a warm irregularity near the bench and clicked around it, and the blue-white node came on above the work and went off when the work moved on, and that was the whole of the acknowledgement, and it was enough.

## The bench

The socket ripened and delivered the tall one, two meters ten, and it stood up badly, ankles rolling, one hand on the wall, and went through the doorway its height was cut for and along the street and into the room where nothing has ever happened, and sat down on the bench.

Something sat down at the other end.

Nine days. It taught the tall one nothing. It did not name it, did not lift it, did not run a line into it and buy it another month, did not attempt to explain the warehouse or the order or the hole in the sky. It occupied the far end of the bench, which is what the far end of a bench is for, and for nine days the room was a room in which something happened.

On the last day the pressure went and the tall one began to move toward heat, which is down, and it walked beside it and did not carry it, because walking badly was the last thing it had and it was still using it. On the stair the tall one put out a hand and the hand closed — not on the wall this time. It closed the way it would close on a tool handle: no affection in it, only fit. A grip finding a thing to grip.

Whether that was comfort is not knowable and did not need to be known. It let itself be held all the way down to the reclamation floor, and then it let the Rotters have what they were owed, because that is the arrangement here and the arrangement is older than any visitor's feelings about it.

## The sleeve

In the Slack, in the hot voids under the char where no valve has voted in a century, the squat domes crackle faintly as they grow and are the only life on the plateau that appears to be enjoying itself.

It went down among them on its belly. It did not open the jar.

It took a pinch of spores, and it carried them in a sealed sleeve off the warehouse racks — the first sleeve in an unguessable age to be used for what a sleeve is for — and it carried the sleeve down the ramp and off the plateau to other hot places, far apart, where a thing can be fast and self-fertile and make nothing whatsoever and not be a rebellion, only a life.

It did not mention this in the Combs. There was nobody to mention it to, and that was not the reason.
