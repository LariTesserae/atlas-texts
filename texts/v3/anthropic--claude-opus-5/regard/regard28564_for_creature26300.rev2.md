# What the Power Did

**Begin with the waiting, because the waiting was most of it.**

The power came down the shaft and did nothing for a very long time, and this was not hesitation. It was the first item of the work. It sat in the plunge basin under the falling column, in the cold, and let its hearing go — held still until the roar stopped being an event and became a floor, and then kept holding still until it understood what was left: that thin bright shelf at the top, that narrow trough in the middle where a hard small knock survives. It wanted to hear the bells the way the bells are heard. It wanted to know what a word costs here before it spent one.

Then it learned the hand-speech, which took no time at all, and found that the hand-speech has no way to ask *why*. Then it learned the foot-names, all nine hundred, and discovered that it had none, and that a gait is the only true public identity in the chamber — which meant that anything walking on those stone plates without a name would be heard as a stranger in a world that has no word for stranger and no need of one. So it kept to the water. In all the generations it has attended this place it has never once put its weight on a floor. The floors are ears. It would not lie to them.

## What it did not do

**It did not bring light.**

It could have hung a small sun under the ceiling at no cost to itself whatsoever, and it turned this over for eleven of their generations before setting it down. The reasons accumulated. The film does not grow toward light and would not be improved by it. The eyes, with their great slack pupils, would be ruined in a week and rebuilt in five generations into something worse at four paces in the dark. But the reason that finally decided it was smaller and harder: in a lit chamber, the mourning-bloom would be a stain.

Grief here is *visible*. It is the one condition of the soul that the body publishes, and it publishes it as a soft green shine that can be found from across the floor when a face cannot. Bring light and the bloom becomes dirt on a person who has let themselves go. The power looked at that for a long time and understood that it had been about to trade a language for a lamp.

It is not certain it was right. It has never been certain. It notes this and goes on.

**It did not increase the catch.**

The arithmetic was easy and the consequence was not. Hundredfold the debris and within three Counts the tanks are surplus, and a surplus tank is a tank that does not need the dead, and the dead go out through the slot where they do nobody any good, and then in the fourth or fifth Count the word for *lamp* and the word for *grandmother* quietly come apart, and nobody notices, because nobody ever notices that. The power has watched vocabularies die in other places. It has never seen one die of famine. It has seen a great many die of plenty.

**It did not open the shaft.**

There is a way. It could cut steps in the Gutter's throat, or better, drive a dry stair up through the limestone to the plateau, and it went so far as to survey the line. Then it weighed the falling column's drag, which is the chamber's whole breath, against a second opening at the top; and the macerated feet against a surface; and nine hundred bodies with no immunity to anything at all against a careless world that would love them briefly and then cough on them.

But those are engineering objections and the power is not, in the end, an engineer. The true objection was this: *it is not a ruin, not a refuge, not a stage in anything.* A door would turn a completed place into a corridor. The young would go up. The old would stay and become people who had been left. And for the first time in the history of the world the chamber would contain waiting — which is the single experience it has, so far, been spared.

**It did not shorten the screaming.**

This was the hardest and it will not pretend otherwise. Three months, sometimes four, in which a new person lies inside a catastrophe with no behind and no beneath, and screams with the whole body, the way someone screams who is being held under. The power could dull that nerve in an evening.

But the notch is the culture. Take the accommodation away and you take the tuned bells, the clipped high speech, the whole architecture of quiet rooms, the acoustic shadows that are the only wealth — and you would have given nine hundred people the gift of hearing, forever, a sound that never changes and never stops, which is not a gift, which is the definition of the thing they were spared. There is no small version of this mercy. There is only the one, and it is enormous, and it eats the world.

So the power did the only available thing, which is not a solution and it does not call it one.

**It attends every birth.** It stays the whole three months. It is in the room for the entire duration of the terror, present and unfelt, and it does not look away and it does not go and do something more useful, of which there is always something. It has calculated the total: some tens of thousands of infant-months of undiluted horror, witnessed end to end, none of it relieved by a single degree.

It does this because it has noticed what they say in the quiet rooms — that everyone has heard the fall once, and nobody can remember it, and that this is the shape of all knowledge. The power remembers. Every one. It carries the unremembered first months of every person in this chamber, which is the part of each of them that no one, including them, has ever been able to hold.

It does not tell itself this helps. It gave up telling itself things a long while ago.

## What it did

**It went upstream and became the carefulness of the careless world.**

This is the great labour and it happens entirely where nobody in the chamber will ever look. Ninety fathoms up, on a plateau that nobody down there believes in, there is a stream, and a swallet, and a beech wood, and a fen, and these are the lungs and the larder of nine hundred people who have never heard of them.

So: the swallet is kept clear. In the dry decades a channel is opened, very quietly, from the next catchment over, and closed again when the rain returns. A mill was going to be built in the fifth Count; the miller's father was persuaded by a run of ordinary bad luck to prefer the lower valley. The beech wood has been saved four times, twice from the axe and twice from a blight, because the beech wood is the leaf-litter, and the leaf-litter is the first Screen, and the first Screen is those embarrassing loud people who have *events*. A herd was moved off a bank in a wet spring to keep the silt from running too fine and choking the ninth. A road was routed around the sinkhole by an engineer who never understood why the survey kept coming back wrong.

And it throws things away. This is the joke at the centre of the whole arrangement and the power is aware of it. Every autumn it rakes leaves into the sink. It sweeps in the drowned beetles, the wads and mats, the snapped twigs. It lets fall what falls and sometimes helps. By the only definition available in the chamber below, the power has become the most wasteful being that has ever lived above the world — and it has done this for a hundred generations so that the people underneath can go on being right about it.

They believe that above is careless. It is easier to maintain that belief than you would think. It has cost more than the light would have.

**It made their first fact true.**

*Nothing changes.* They take this as description. It is not, quite; it is closer to a promise, and somebody has been keeping it. The water level, the temperature, the pitch of the fall — these do not hold themselves. They are held. Every year that the roar does not falter is a year of somebody's work, and the work is invisible by design, because a maintained world is a world with a maintainer in it, and a maintainer can be petitioned, and a people who can petition will eventually stop conserving and start asking.

The power would rather be their physics than their god. Physics does not require anyone to kneel in cold water.

**It kept the eras.**

When the Count dies, time does not roll over; it empties, and everything that happened falls out of sequence into the single undifferentiated *long*. The power caught the first of these and has caught every one since. It holds the whole sequence: which Count was the hard one, which flood year the ninth Screen tore, the name and gait of the woman who first worked out that a curve could cancel a wave, the exact span of every life this chamber has produced and what each of them did with it, in order.

It will not give this back. A people handed a history will notice that it has a direction, and a thing with a direction has a far end, and then they will begin to want a future, and there is no future here, there is only this, which is enough and which is *complete*. So the record stays where it is. Somewhere, the long is not undifferentiated. That is all. It is the smallest possible form of justice and it is not nothing.

**It sits with the ones who bloom in the cells.**

Those who went east too often and lost the accommodation for good, who cannot bear the open floor any more, who are fed and are kind to and who sit in the deep western quiet with their hands. Their ears are starved of variation, which makes them the only people in the chamber capable of noticing anything at the edge of the audible, and the power goes and sits with them. Four Counts ago one of them made a shape with her hands that is not in the hand-speech, twice, and then never again, and the power has kept that too.

**It leaves the grey rumour alone.**

It knows what lives in the outflow. It has decided that a thing which stays a rumour requires nothing of anyone, whereas a thing with a name must be either hunted or worshipped, and both would be a disaster, and neither would be kind to the grey thing either. Benevolence does not stop at the ones with faces. It feeds it, sometimes, in the dark, in a manner indistinguishable from an accident.

## The end, which it thinks about rarely

The chamber will not last. Not because the fall will stop — the fall will not stop while the power stands upstream — but because limestone is a slow liquid and the water table is a slow argument and there is a far day, well past any counting they keep, when this blister in the rock will close or open or drain.

The power knows the approximate date. It has decided what it will do on that day. It has not decided whether that decision was wise, and it does not examine it often, because examining it produces nothing usable and a great deal of something else.

---

## The intervention

Today, at the north wall, an old woman is combing stone-flax out of the rock by feel, with her eyes shut because the dark is restful. Her fingertips went numb years ago. Her husband has been three weeks in the tank and there is a green bloom coming up at her nape and across her shoulders and behind her knees, and she has not asked anyone to scrape her, and she will not, because asking is not in her, and because it is not, her daughter has not thought of it in nine days.

Above her the lines cross and recross in the dim.

The power has never touched anything in this chamber. It makes one exception. Out in the roar, where nobody is looking, it takes a waxed cord between two fingers — the line that runs from the north wall to the daughter's house — and gives it a single pull.

A small hard clock of fired clay, under the fall. The daughter feels it more than hears it. She reads it as a fault in the line, which is what it was meant to look like, and she comes out with her hands to check the tension, and her route takes her past the north wall, and there in the dark is the soft unmistakable shine of her mother's back, glowing at a distance where the face is not.

She goes and gets the flat stone.

The power does not stay to watch. To stay would be to make the old woman a thing being observed, and she has spent eighty years being owed nothing and owing nothing, and she is not going to be an object of anyone's attention in the last of it.

That is the whole intervention. Nine hundred people, a hundred generations, one tug on a string.

Then the power went back up the shaft, out into a careless world it has been carefully arranging since before the stair-treads were worn, where the afternoon was cold and getting on, and the beech wood had already begun dropping what it would not miss.

It picked up the rake.
