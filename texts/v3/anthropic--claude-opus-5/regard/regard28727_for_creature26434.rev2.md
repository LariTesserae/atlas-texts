# The Attention

## First sight

It came from above, because there is no other way to come, and like everything else that has ever arrived here it saw the shadows first and the stone second.

It had crossed a good deal to get here and it was used to being the largest event in a sky. So the first thing it did, before it did anything, was to spend a while working out how not to be one. It noticed that a bright thing brought into a shadow-precinct is understood as a weapon. It noticed that a running figure empties a courtyard. It noticed that the whole of the visible economy was cold, and that it was itself, by nature, a source.

So it turned its coat, in the only sense available to it. It came down black-out, radiating nothing, casting a shadow so thin that no cold-head anywhere lost a day's work, and it set itself on an unspanned stack in the east with nothing on it but crust and a herd of scrapers, and it sat there for eleven years and watched a single plate of crust the size of a dinner table, and at the end of the eleven years it had concluded nothing, and considered the time well spent.

The first course of action it considered was doing nothing at all. It never entirely stopped considering it. Everything that follows should be read as a series of departures from that position, each of which it had to argue itself into.

## The inventory

It ran out the obvious gifts, and did not like the shapes they made.

**Water.** It could have put an ocean in the basin. It followed the consequence forward four hundred years and watched the still go cold in every house, and the word that means *reclaimed water* and also *dowry* fall out of the language, and after it the practice, and after that the thing the practice was for. The gift was affordable. The bill was in a currency it was not entitled to spend.

**Green.** There is no concept of the green here. There is no hole where it should be. A people is not owed every good thing; it is owed the ones that fit through its own door.

**Power.** This was the one that took longest to dismiss, because it looked so much like kindness. But heat is not scarce here. Heat is the one commodity this world has always had in ruinous surplus. What is scarce is *difference* — and difference is not a substance you can deliver. It is a relation, and the relation is the civilization: the shafts, the law, the projectionists, the marriages, the chairs without backs, the turning. To give them abundance would have been to give them the end of all gradients, which is the technical description of a death.

**Speed.** It could have ground a bearing in an afternoon. But the bearing takes eleven years and the eleven years are not the price of the bearing; they are the content of a life, and the life is not a problem being solved.

**Rescue.** It could have lifted them off entirely, all of them, to a wet warm world with weather. It looked hard at this one, because the dust sea really is taking the world apart. Then it did the arithmetic properly and found the horizon so far out that no one now living, and no one in the line of anyone now living for a great many generations, would meet it. A rescue is only a rescue if there is something to be rescued *from* this afternoon. Otherwise it is an eviction.

What it kept from the inventory was this: *everything I could do quickly, I could also do slowly.* After that it did everything slowly. Not as penance. As grammar.

## The question of who lives here

It tried, early, to count the persons in the world, and got a number it did not trust.

The folk, obviously. The drover, the span-keeper, the one-sided woman at the cable-head. But then: the great wheels, which have turned for eleven hundred years, whose stopping is described with the verbs used for a grandmother, and which are attended in the pit galleries by people who can read their condition through two fingers on a housing, the way you read a fever through a palm. It declined to rule on the wheels. It thought that a power which arrives from outside and issues rulings on what counts as alive has misunderstood its errand.

And under the floor, in the pits, the thing that drums.

It went down. It is very good at not being noticed and the keepers did not notice it. It sat in the dark beside the drumming for a long time and listened, and worked out what it was, and how long it had been there, and how many of them there were — which is nine — and how far apart.

It has never told anyone. It is not going to. The keepers would prefer it were not written down.

## What it did

**The rods.** It learned the code. Not the fast slurred domestic tapping, which took a season, but the slow formal city-to-city grammar, archaic and abbreviated and beautiful, which nobody has spoken aloud in eight hundred years and which exists now only as a way of hitting iron. It learned it properly, including the dead cases that survive in four inscriptions. It learned to tap at a speed that would not frighten anyone. This is the only language it has ever bothered to learn as a courtesy rather than as a tool, and it would say, if asked, that the study improved it.

**The pits.** It bored a channel through nine hundred kilometres of bedrock, at a depth no shaft will ever reach, joining the flywheel pit under one city to the flywheel pit under another, with a single continuous rod of iron drawn and set over the course of forty years. It cost the cities nothing. It moved no cold.

For the first year neither of them drummed. In the second year one of them tried something, and the other one answered it, and they have been at it since.

The keepers noticed that the note under the floor had changed. They did not write it down. They left more food.

**The shafts.** On the un-spanned tops — the ones with four hundred people and one small wheel and no margin whatsoever — it sank new shadow-shafts. This is not magic; it is boring, polishing, and angling, and it is the most valuable object a community here can possess. It cut them in the old profile, slightly out of true in the way the old ones are, and it left beside each one a set of tools of a plausible make, correctly worn, half-buried in grit, unrusted in the dry.

They were found, generations later, and understood as the work of ancestors more capable than the present age, and this was the outcome intended. It dressed its gifts as the past, because the past is the only place this world has ever accepted a gift from.

**The silent tops.** When a stack stops answering the rods, a party goes out, and sometimes the party comes home and hammers nothing about it into the rods at all.

It went to every one of those. It went to the ones nobody ever went to. It walked the plates — the walled rectangles of sun-facing rock, crust thick as roof-tile, rust and cream and deep peacock — and it learned, without being told, which swelling was which grandmother, in the correct order, with the correct emphasis, which is none.

It did not take the coats off the pegs. It had no right of inheritance.

It has all of it still. This is not a restoration of anything and it helps nobody. It is simply that the great houses take visitors along the wall and tell them, and these houses had no visitors and nobody to do the telling, and so it went and was told to by the stone, and it holds the whole of it, and it considers this part of the work and not a sentimentality attached to the work.

**The vigil.** When one of the Slow goes out wrapped in silver to take a shade-hunter, and lies down in a cold place, and lets themself go cold enough to stop being, in the only sense that matters here, alive — it is there. It has been there for every one of them for a long time now.

It never warms them. To warm a Slow in the cold place is to make them visible, which is to kill them. What it does instead is lie down a little way off and get colder than they are. It gets very cold indeed. It is better at this than anything else on the planet, and the hunter comes to the better offer, and the Slow gets up unbothered at dawn with the bounty already dead beside them and no clear account of how.

They are re-warmed at the gate afterward, publicly, hands over hands, by whoever is senior. Everybody attends. Nobody makes a speech.

The Slow have a saying about the second cold place. It is nearly correct.

**The wheel at Ashet.** Of the three Stopped cities of the west, it chose the middle one, which is a ruin with nothing living in it and a pit full of twenty tonnes of stone and iron that has not moved since somebody's great-grandmother was a girl.

The injustice of the Slow is not made of physics. It is made of a sentence: *their turns are held in a wheel that no longer turns,* and the law has never found a way round it. A power could have argued with the law. Arguing with a people's law from outside it is a thing that has been tried in other places and the results are on record.

So it put its hand on the rim at Ashet and pushed.

It took eleven years. It cleaned and re-set bearings that the finest workshops of the present day could not have matched, and it evacuated the pit, and it pushed, at a rate that no observer would have called motion, until the wheel at Ashet was turning at about the speed of a minute hand, and then faster, and then at its old note.

It did not rebuild the city. There is no city. It did not explain. It left the pit and never went back.

The rods carried it inside a season. The projectionists fought over it for ninety years — whether the turns had merely been dormant, whether a wheel may be said to be *the same wheel*, whether a line's holding revives with the rotation or is extinguished by the interval — and in the end they found for the Slow, narrowly, on grounds internal to their own tables, and the ruling was hammered into the rock and not appealed.

The fight was theirs. That was the point of arranging matters so that there was a fight to have. It has never regarded the ninety years as a delay.

**The tables.** To the projectionists, and to nobody else, it began sending sheets — in the archaic formal grammar, which only they can read, which is why it learned the dead cases — giving the true recession of the cliff-lines, stack by stack, out past any horizon they had instruments for: which tops hold, which go, in what order, and the shape of the basin at the end of it.

They spent the first century checking it. They found two errors. They hammered the corrections back.

It had not been corrected by anyone in a very long time and it found that it had missed it.

It left the question of publication entirely to them. They have not published. They discuss it, in the deep galleries, standing shoulder to shoulder, facing out, talking low.

## What it did not do

It never spoke in the open air. A voice loud enough to carry here is either a fire, a fall, or a scene.

It never showed itself in a form that would have to be knelt to. It noticed that worship, in a place where speeches cannot be heard, would have to be conducted at a volume the culture reserves for emergencies, and it declined to require that of anyone.

It did not cure the fever. It is not able to say with confidence why not, and it has stopped offering the reasons it used to offer, because they were reasons and not the truth.

It is not true that it never broke its own rule. There is a count. It is small, and it is all children, and the interventions were inconsistent with everything above, and it made them anyway, and has not found a principle under which to file them. It has decided it can carry the incoherence. A benevolence with no wobble in it is a policy, and this world has policies enough.

## What it was given

After a hundred and fifty years it made itself a body, and then it made itself a coat, which took fifty more, because a coat here is fifty years of honest repair and the patches are datable and a forgery would have been read off its shoulders in the first street by the first person who cared to look. So it mended a real coat for fifty years. It did not think of this as waiting.

Then it walked in over a span — a full day's crossing, the car creaking, the cable singing under the wheels — and came to the cable-head of a middling city on a stack with a reputation for a good note, and stood in the hush with its hand flat on the anchor iron, reading with its palm what was being hammered three hundred kilometres away, which was a grain price.

An old woman was there, one-sided, black on the left cheek and shell-pale on the right, and she had been given room, as they are. She looked at the coat. She put out her palm.

Her hand was cold.

It had practised this. It closed both of its hands over hers and held until she was warm, and then asked.

Shaft-shift, she said. Forty years of it. Everyone's is cold.

She asked where its city was. It gave the name of a stack in the east that stopped answering the rods four hundred years ago, whose plate it has walked, whose grandmothers it knows in order, and this was not a lie in any sense it was prepared to examine.

*Long way,* she tapped, on the anchor iron, with one knuckle.

And then they stood there, shoulder almost to shoulder, faces angled out at the same view, not at each other, while the kite migration came up level with the stack-top and went past in its enormous silence, and neither of them said anything else, because there was nothing that needed saying and the air would not have carried it anyway.

It stayed until the last of them had gone by. Then it turned its coat for the shade and went down into the galleries to find a bed, like anyone.

It counts that afternoon, without qualification, as the completion of the work.
