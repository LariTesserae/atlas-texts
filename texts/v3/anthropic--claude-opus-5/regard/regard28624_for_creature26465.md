## Appendix, unnumbered. Not for the survey.

### The eighth address, completed

— inside it, in the space where a foot used to go, there is an animal. One animal. It has been counted once, by a fibre thread pushed in through the eyelets, and the count came back: one, and a wall.

It is not any of the seven. It is not any described nodeling. It has no name, because a name wants a type specimen and the specimen cannot be taken out — an hour in the open field and it would be powder, and the powder would go into our lungs and our quiet rooms, as the rule says. So it stays uncatalogued: a shape on a thermal return, six points of contact, a rasp working at the film of organics that creeps in with the sand.

The wall is the interesting part. The boot's glass shell has been ribbed from the inside. Somebody in there is laying oriented silica in a two-millimetre band, spiralling, patching, and has been for a long time, which means the fine are in there too, and it means that in the reckoning of the fine that boot is a stem. It is a stem that never grows and never fails and never needs to be ridden down. They have been maintaining it, in shifts, for four hundred years.

Three readings, then, of one object. To us it is a man's remains. To the nodeling it is the only fixed quiet on the terrace that no pylon accounts for. To the masons it is a stem that came out right at last.

That is the being. That is the place.

### The visitation

Something came that had no faceplate and no cooling regulator and no twelve-minute allowance, and it came up out of the scree the way everyone comes, and it got the introduction the way everyone gets it: it knelt, and put a palm flat on the rock, and did not fall over, because it was already listening at the bottom of the mountain, eleven hundred metres down, to the thing that is presumed.

It stayed one turnover. Nine hours by the local clock — the interval between a collapse and the new tips coming up through the wreck. It considered that the correct unit and used it.

For the first several of those hours it did nothing at all, which is the part worth recording.

---

### What it declined to do, and why

**The seep.** Four grams a day, and everything queued behind a stain. There is no easier gift in the universe than more, and it began there, as anyone would. It followed the consequence out: eight grams, and the collar thickens, because the collar is the first thing downstream and the only thing without a competitor; twelve, and the stands go up past their fatigue limits faster than the sifters can break them, and the cathedrals come down in cascades that take out hectares; twenty, and the terrace supports thirty tonnes of biosphere for perhaps two centuries and then supports nothing, because the sand has been eaten and the creep has gone slack and there is no ocean to fall back on. Four grams a day is not a shortage. It is the chisel. Nine million years of a bus-load of animals is not a wound that mercy failed to close; it is a form, and the form is made out of the scarcity the way a bell is made out of the hole in it.

It let the fissure alone. It did not touch the collar, though it looked at the collar for some time, and what it thought about the collar is not in this account.

**The noise.** Obvious, and obviously fatal. Quiet the mountain and the fronds stop being fed within minutes, the nodelings lose all sense of where the world is and simply stop, the chandelier falls out of the air, and the terrace becomes the plains: warmer, silent, and empty. Every hush on Adze is already a small demonstration of what benevolence-by-silence accomplishes. It declined to become a large one.

**The dying.** The fine build into the loudest place available and are killed by their own competence, at a rib, in forty seconds, and the wrights ride it down. This was the hardest refusal and it took the longest. What was available: raise the fatigue limit of the glass. Two per cent would do it — a stand could hold its own weight in the antinode indefinitely. It ran that forward too. Stands that do not fall do not turn over; the turnover is the terrace's only means of moving nutrient upslope through the wreck; the drifts stop being laid down; the sand stops being made, and the sand is forty metres deep at the back wall and it is the ancestors and it is the soil and it is the whole account of the place. Immortal glass, and in a hundred years the garden is a graveyard of unbreakable towers standing in a floor of bare rock with nothing growing on it.

It noted, without softening it, that the arithmetic came out this way and that the arithmetic being clean did not make the forty seconds any shorter. It did not tell itself that the wrights don't mind. It has no idea whether they mind. It refused to decide that they don't, because that would have been the cheap way to be at peace on Terrace Nine, and it had not come all this way to be cheaply at peace.

**A tenth pylon.** This is the temptation that had precedent, and precedent is the most dangerous kind of permission. Nine grey tapers, four metres, unweathered while the rock behind them has retreated half a metre; not part of the engine; older than its ecology and younger than it. Nine silences that do not move. And out of those nine, given time, came seven species that exist nowhere else, and eleven square metres of total range, and the abbot: legs reduced to nearly nothing, forty years long, grazing one square of sand, and wearing fluted crests and a fringe of glass filaments that serve no purpose anyone has found. The only decorated animal on Adze. Ornament is what a lineage buys when it is finally not sprinting. Nine pylons bought that.

So: a tenth. An eleventh. Ninety. Pave the terrace in quiet and see what it wears.

It did not, and the reason it gave was this. Nine was already an intervention, and its whole yield — the abbot, the fringe, pylon four's three hundred individuals — came from the fact that nine was *few*. Rarity is not the tax on the gift. Rarity is the gift. Ninety addresses and the strain sense goes slack across the whole population, the sprint is abandoned everywhere, and in ten thousand years there is no animal on this mountain that can cross open sand, and then the engine stutters, as it does, as it has, and the field reorganises, and the shadows move a metre, and everything dies at once in a place that has forgotten how to run.

It also observed that the boot works. Nobody planned the boot. A man died, and the fine mistook his footwear for a stem, and there is an animal in there that has never been outside and has a mason on retainer. The terrace does not need a benefactor's shadows. It needs the world to keep dropping things.

**The rectangle.** Forty metres by forty at the terrace's centre, dead sand, flat field, no growth in the whole record, and the sifters walk around it.

It could have looked. That is not a boast; looking is trivial for a thing like this. It stood at the edge for a while with the light coming through the layered glass behind it, and then it walked around, the way the sifters do, and went on. If you want the reason: it is possible to be asked a question by a silence, and it is possible to recognise that you were not asked.

That is why the rectangle is still blank in the record and will stay blank. The one visitor who could have answered it decided that some things are entitled to remain none of anyone's business.

### What it did

**It obeyed the rules.** All of them, though not one applied to it. Twelve minutes of ground contact, cumulative, and it kept the tally. It never touched two stands at once. It did not stand still in a null. It did not shout, or put any energy into the rock that a growth tip could read as an invitation, because to be fed on by this place would have been a kind of theft — a warm anomaly redirecting four grams a day of somebody else's living toward a visitor who did not need it.

It took nothing. Not a frond, not a shard, not a grain. There is no sample. There will never be a sample.

**It kept an old promise instead of making a new one.** It went to each of the nine pylons in turn and did there whatever it is that has been done to them continuously for longer than the fatigue laminae go down — the thing that makes a four-metre taper decline to weather while the mountain around it gives up half a metre. It is not a coating. It is not a material. It is closer to an unbroken refusal, and it has to be renewed, and the renewing is the actual work, and nobody has ever seen it happen because it looks like nothing happening.

Nine shadows still do not move. That is not an act of preservation. That is the fourth pylon's three hundred individuals getting another interval, and the abbot getting its forty years, and the fringe of glass filaments continuing to serve no purpose anyone has found.

**It learned the being in the boot.** Entirely. Every plate, the ribbing of the patched wall, the rasp-rhythm, the six points of contact, the whole small shape of a life spent inside an accident, in a hollow that damps beautifully, being maintained by masons who are mistaken about what they are building.

It did not widen the boot. It did not reinforce the boot against the day a sifter braces and breaks it, because sifters eat glass and that is not a flaw in sifters. It did not move it into a pylon shadow, where the animal would have been safer and would also have been, immediately and forever, out of the only world it has ever had.

What it did was hold that animal in full attention for the better part of nine hours, and then let it go on not knowing it had been visited. That is the entire gift and it was given whole. Somewhere there is now a complete account of a creature with no name, no specimen, no photograph and no future, and the account is not going to be published, and it exists.

**It left something on the cairn.** Thirty-one stones on the eastern lip, dressed each to a pitch, four centuries old, a survey team's joke that became a memorial and then a shrine. You put your forehead to the top stone and you get a chord.

There are thirty-two now.

The new one is at the base on the north side, and it is the same rock as everything else here, and it was dressed by someone who knew exactly what it needed to be. Nobody has been able to establish who put it there or when. The chord is different. The old one had something unresolved in it that surveyors used to argue about after shift, a sourness people put down to bad tuning by amateurs; the new stone does not remove that interval, it *seats* it, and now the whole thing reads as intended.

Note where the gift went. Not into the seep, not into the glass, not into the field, not into the boot. Into the one structure on Terrace Nine that was already made of meaning rather than metabolism — the human part, the part that can be given something without being destroyed by it. It gave to us, because we were the only thing there that could take delivery.

**It made one promise, privately.** The engine has been shedding its waste as noise for over nine million years, and it will not do so forever; it drifts, it stutters, the phase lag disagrees with itself, the surveys have always disagreed. One day the bell stops. The garden dies inside minutes, the chandelier comes down out of the thin air, the abbot's shadow fills with ordinary sound, and the terrace becomes what the plains already are.

It will be present for that. Not to prevent it. To be the thing that is there, kneeling, palm flat on cold rock, at the moment nine million years of a bus-load of glass animals goes quiet — so that it will have been witnessed, and so that the last of them will die in the presence of something that knew all their names, including the one that never had a name, in the boot, ribbing its wall.

Then it went down through the scree, and made no noise, because nothing does.
