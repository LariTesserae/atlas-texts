# What the Power Did

## Arrival

It came in through the noise, because there is no way in that does not go through it. It had understood this before arriving and had understood it wrongly, the way everyone understands it wrongly until the fourteenth month.

## The four seconds

In its first week, from outside and above and without a body, it did the obvious kindness. It did not stop the water — the water went on going over and going down — it only took the sound out, which for a power of that order is a small thing, a palm laid flat on a string.

Four seconds.

Aleu's hands stopped in the brown water with the grass half-drawn. Vurst took her palms off the collar and stepped back from it. Sabin, who had been talking to himself about a stake, heard himself, and shut his mouth. Tamm, five weeks in and not sleeping, sat down where he stood. Six people came upright across thirty square kilometres and turned east and did not move.

Not one of them was glad.

It put the sound back, and the putting back was worse than the taking away, and it did not touch the plateau again for eleven months.

That evening three of the six wrote something on their slates and sat with their backs to them and let the words go. It is the only event in the history of the Shelf that is in no book. There was no sign for it, and the slate is honest, and the slate gives everything back.

## The body

Then it came properly, which meant taking a body, because there is no other way to be wrong less often.

It accepted the notch at four thousand cycles. It accepted the jaw, and the two fingers pressed up under the cheekbone while the pain went off somewhere else, and the tea-coloured corners, and the wing of tissue starting in from the nose side at a millimetre a year. It could have declined all of it. A power that declines the local injuries has not arrived; it has visited.

## The audit

It considered, in the first year, and set down:

**The hearing.** It could have lifted the notch out of all six in a night. They would have woken able to hear one another and unable to hear one another the way they do — *thistle*, *whistle*, *this'll*, three of the same word, a deafness shared like a nose. One of them, restored, would have become foreign to the other five. Declined.

**The eyes.** They call the encroaching tissue *the mat*, because it is exactly that behaviour: a green thing growing over new stone. To arrest it in a human eye is to make a ruling about the plateau. Declined.

**Speech.** Trivial. Four seconds of work. It would have cost four hundred and twelve signs, a grammar with tense in the shoulder, a scandalous vocabulary for the condition of wet plant matter, and a hand rocked twice at hip height that means *don't* and derives from a tramway under moss. Declined, and with some heat.

**The peat.** It could have made the cuts heal in a year instead of a century. Then there is no hundred-year rotation, no oilcloth in three colours, no numbered stakes, no man muttering over the placements of the dead and pencilling corrections for whoever comes. Declined.

**The hundred thousand years.** It could have held the falls where they are and kept the Shelf from becoming two plateaus and a memory in the rock. *Nothing here is finished. That is the essential fact.* To finish it and to preserve it are the same crime committed from opposite ends. This would have been the largest of the mercies and the cruellest, and it was not close.

**The false grounds.** This one it put down and picked up twice.

It filled nothing. The voids are the mat's, and the mat would only make more, and the frost that lingers an hour longer over a void at dawn is the most beautiful legible thing on the plateau and the highest skill anyone here has. It left the frost. They still walk her boundary in the cold months and still read the slow grey shape on green.

But there is no entry after that date, and there will not be one.

That is the single alteration it permitted itself in the general run of the world, and it is not certain, even now, that it was right. It took the appetite and left the vigilance, and only one of those was ever anyone's to take.

## What it asked the mat

Everything above is staff. It had read the log and it agreed.

So it put the question to the actual inhabitant, in the only grammar available — pressure, and wet, and slowness, and the long holding and the long letting go — and waited a season for the answer, which came back through eight thousand years of acid black tissue and was:

*Not faster.*

It had come prepared to offer nitrogen, warmth, depth, a shortening of the wait for soil. It offered none of them. It has never in its existence been refused so completely by something that wanted nothing.

## Bow Three

It stood in the curtain at 1130 on the bearing, in the body, with a shadow like anyone's, and the ring stood around its head on the fog, small and perfect and its own.

And it could not see anyone else's. It went and stood where Aleu had stood, and the ring came with it, because it had become Aleu's position and not Aleu, and what was wanted was Aleu-seen-crowned-by-another, which is not a thing that exists anywhere at any order of power. The limit is not strength. The limit is where a body is.

So the induction is correct and is the whole theology of the place: you are walked out, and positioned, and shown your own ring, and the one who showed you walks away across the mat, and you are left to believe without evidence that they have one too.

There is nothing further.

It checked.

## The slate

In the fourteenth month it took the slate down off the nail in Shed Four and wrote, in full, everything it had come to give.

It read it over once. It set the slate on the bunk and sat with its back to the slate, as is the etiquette, and someone came in and went out again to check a gauge that did not need checking.

Four minutes.

Then it hung the slate back on the nail, and that was the end of that.

## Work

It learned the four hundred and twelve signs. It invented none. It has one it would like — there is an absence, a clear one, around the middle of the second year — and it has decided that the absence belongs to them and that they will fill it or not.

It took the transects nearest Shed Seven. It did the dipper count on the first clear morning of the month, in Ott's hand-ruled column, in grease pencil, thirty years after Ott died, and it did not draw a line under anything.

It pulled the pale bent-grass with bare hands, because gloves lose the feel of the root-crown. Aleu was right about that.

It read aloud when alone. Everyone does. The mouth is a muscle and forgets.

And it undertook Sabin's rotation. Not to finish it — it will not be finished, that is the point of it — but the stakes will be replaced when they rot, in 2190, in 2280, in 2400, by something that will be there. The sixth is always the one who is leaving. It has appointed itself the seventh, and the seventh is the one who is not.

## Tamm

In the fifth week of his first summer, with the noise still on him and his hands going at the buckles, Tamm slept one night through.

That is the only rule it broke. It broke it once more in August and has been sorry both times and would do it again.

## Weld

In the ninth month it went down after Jonna Weld.

Four metres of floating mat and then black water at four degrees, and no light of any kind, and the condition such water preserves. It did not bring her up. The bearing is known; the not-speaking about it is the entire dignity of this place; and the mat has her, and the mat is the inhabitant, and it is not for a visitor to take back a thing that was given in 1958 by accident.

It did not pray over her. It had nothing to pray to that was not itself. It did not speak, speech being no more use down there than up here.

It stayed.

It had exactly one thing in surplus, and that hollow was the only place on the plateau where the surplus could be spent without deforming anything. So it spent it. A long time by any reckoning the six would recognise. Whatever it is that company consists of when neither party can see, hear, or be told — it did that, thoroughly, and then came up through the mat, and the mat closed over as the mat does.

Nobody saw it go down. Nobody saw it come up.

There is no entry.

## The fox

It went down through the cloud behind the fox, once, and came back, and it knows.

The answer is not the migration and not the salt and not the madness. It is one of the eleven guesses in the marginalia, and the person who made that guess died in the valley of something ordinary a long time ago, and there is no way to tell them they were right that does not also tell everyone else they were wrong.

Eleven entries and a hundred years of argument and an arrow drawn between two dead men by a third. It weighed the fact against that and found the fact lighter, and has written nothing, and will write nothing, and considers this the most difficult restraint it has ever practised, including the four seconds.

## The fourteenth month

It happened to it too, exactly on schedule, out on the transect with four kilometres of green skin in every direction: the wall of white pressure crossed over and became a silence, and it found it had stopped walking and that its mouth was open.

It turned its palms down.

---

On the ninth of the month, some distance east of Shed Four, Tamm stopped with the brown water coming up around his boots, and stood a while, and brought his fingertips away from his ears and opened them outward, and then turned his palms down toward the green skin stretched over the new stone, and made it to nobody at all.

It is still to nobody at all. That has not been altered and will not be; it would have been the last and worst of the mercies, and it is the whole weight of the gesture that it is made anyway, into the open, on no evidence, like believing in another person's ring.

It is only that the arithmetic is out by one now, and will be from here on, and he will never be told.
