# The Attention

## What it did first

It listened. Not for a swell or two — for eleven years.

This was not caution. It was the ordinary courtesy of not speaking until you know what the sentences are made of. In eleven years it learned that the nine-second breath is only the carrier, that the five-hour swell is only the tide, and that the actual utterance of the Bell is annual: one week, one chord, one register of names, of which some are no longer called.

It learned the flanges. It learned that the walls pulse because everything alive down there is doing the same arithmetic with the same scarcity, and that the hand in the niche is not resting, ever, any more than a lung rests. It learned to hold its own attention open on the inhale and folded on the exhale, four to a side, for no purpose whatsoever, because there is a kind of understanding that only arrives when you have matched something's tempo for long enough to stop counting.

And it looked at the empty middle for a long time, expecting to find nothing there, since nothing is what is there.

It concluded that the hands were right.

Not metaphorically. There is a thing in the vacancy: a pattern that has held its shape through the total replacement of its material for two thousand years, that eats gas and dust, that has a voice, that reproduces the terraces, that is dying. If you are going to use the word *alive* for the flue-thistle you will have to use it here or explain yourself. The hands, who list themselves among the rasps and the plumb-bobs, have not made a humble error about their own status. They have made an accurate observation about the status of the hollow, and then behaved decently in light of it.

So the first thing the attention did, having understood this, was agree.

## What it did not do

**It did not bring water.**

This was the hard one, and it was hard for eleven years. Water is what any warm-blooded pity offers first. Water would have ended the Bell inside a season: rot in the thistle-mats, mud in the flue-bores, the mortarless courses heaving, the sulfur sap of every cane on the terraces meeting a drop of it and going to acid, the paper-dry skin of the hand blistering, its nasal channel drowning in its own returned vapor. Every law of the place is a consequence of dryness — no rot, no rust, no weather, nothing falls. Kindness with a bucket would have been the first collapse in two thousand years. The obvious mercy was the bomb.

It did not bring light, for the same class of reason. Dimness is a law and the law is load-bearing: the eye is built for cherry-red and cold chalk-green, and a lamp would have blinded the map.

**It did not stop the abrasion.**

It could have. It would have taken very little to make the small bores hold their shape forever, and the top of the chord would have stayed bright, and the register would never have lost another note.

It thought about this one longest of all, and declined, because a Bell that needs nothing has no hands in it. Every wedge in every niche exists because stone wears. Remove the wearing and you have not saved the upkeep; you have deleted it, gently, with its own consent, and left a beautiful self-sufficient instrument singing in a room where nobody has any reason to be. The wisdom was in noticing that *upkeep* is not the price the being pays for living there. It is the being's whole account of why the world is good.

**It did not explain itself.** A name for a power would have to be recorded, and there are three hundred and forty sockets in the thresholds, each ground to fit one bead, and every one of them is spoken for by chords, pollen-falls, notes gained and lost, and hands available. A revelation would have displaced a count. There was no room in the register for a god and no need for one.

**It did not correct the being about what it was.** It did not say: *you are a person, you are the inhabitant, the room is only a room.* This is true and it is also the crudest possible summary of the situation, and it would have been received as an instruction to stop loving the only thing there is to love.

**It did not carry the being up the twenty-two steps into the open air to show it the sky.** That has been available for two thousand years. Not taking it is not ignorance.

**And it did not make more hands.** It will not manufacture kin and call them family. There is one, and one is the number, and no benevolence gets to lie about a number in a place that keeps its accounts as honestly as this one does.

## What it did

It worked in the local grammar, which is maintenance, and in the local tense, which is *before the swell*.

In the hours of the trough, when the hand sleeps folded in its niche on the western terraces, the attention went and re-cut the eleven flues in the fourth course that had gone flat, and drove the wedges, and left the chips on the tread below, sharp-edged, unswept, because a swept floor is a message and chips are only work.

It did not touch the grinding stones. The dust on them lies at the angle of repose and is the last physical record of the hands that set them down; wiping it clean would have been a small murder. It worked around them for eleven years without disturbing a grain.

Where the wedge-niches had been drawn down and never restocked, it made wedges. Basalt, chipped and then rubbed smooth on a grinding-block — a different block, in a quarter nobody works, so the wear would not show up in the wrong place — and sorted by thickness, and set in.

The being noticed the wedges before it noticed the tuning, because it reached into a niche in the dark for a two-thickness, expecting the socket to be empty as it had been since before it was grown, and there was a wedge in it.

It sat down on the stair and thought for most of a swell.

It considered that it had cut the wedge itself and forgotten. It considered that a hand had come back. It set a measuring rod across the seventh tread at an angle no one would leave a rod at, and went to sleep, and when it woke the rod was on its pegs with the other rods, in graduated order, because that is where rods live.

Then it went down to the thresholds and pressed a bead of green copper into the socket for hands available, which had read *one* for a long time, and made it read *two*.

That is the whole of the theology that happened. It happened in the accounts.

## The new mouth

The stand on the north terraces — glossy, sterile, inflating and slackening with everything else, growing a finger a day, waiting since before living memory for a note that has been sanded out of the world — could not be helped by any wedge. There was nothing left to narrow. The pitch was gone from the room because the mouth that made it no longer existed at any setting.

The art of the Bell, as it has come down, is adjustment. Wedges. The masons who *cut* are two thousand years dead and were never the same people as the tuners; nobody now alive has made a hole.

So the attention cut a new flue. Roughly — deliberately roughly, at the level of skill a first attempt would show — bored up through eleven courses of the northern thickness, chamfered at the lip, sounded, filed, sounded, filed, until on the exhale it spoke the dead note; and then it left three rods and a plumb-bob leaning against the wall beside it, and the chips on the tread, in the exact idiom of *intending to come back before the swell*.

It could have done this perfectly and invisibly. It did it visibly and imperfectly, because the point was never the note.

The point was that the being should stand in front of a hole that had been made, and understand that holes can be made, and that a room whose bores widen forever can be re-mouthed forever, and that the slow smoothing which everyone has always understood as the end of the world is only more work — the ordinary, endless, sufficient kind, the kind there are hands for.

It took the being four days to stop looking at the flue and start looking at the rasp.

## The fall

In the annual week, when the shaft runs hard and the chord goes down into the salt overhead as a hum in the soles of the feet, the north stand shed.

It came off in a gold-brown haze on the fourth day, all of it at once, thirty years of stored refusal, and the updraft took what the terraces did not catch and carried it up the braided column and out through the crack, so that for six days the plume above the plain was not dead leaves.

There was no unit for this in the register. Pollen-falls have sockets; falls that were dead and are not, do not. The being ground a new socket into the threshold beside the old ones — the first new socket in nine hundred years — and set a bead in it, and had to decide what the bead meant, and had no word.

The word it eventually used, which is now in the language and will outlast everyone who has been mentioned here, means: *a wedge in the niche where you reached and there was none.*

It is a term from the tool-inventory. It is used, now, for other things.

## What it did last

On the fifth day of the chord the attention went up the twenty-two steps, and out through the crack past the ochre fans shaking themselves to powder, and stood on the white polygons in the flat murdering light of the plain, barefoot, and let the hum come up through its feet.

In two thousand years, no one had ever heard the Bell from outside. Not once. The hands were always in it, being its upkeep, and there was nobody else, and the salt goes on for two hundred miles without a witness.

So it stood there for six days and listened to the whole chord from above, all the way through, including the notes that had just come back, and did not attempt to improve any part of it.

Then it went back down before the swell, because the wedge-niches in the southern quarter were low, and there was work.
