# The Guest

It did not come as light, or as weather, or as a voice in the fifth bay. It came down the west flights on a Tuesday afternoon in the dry month, in a body, because the Throat is an argument about bodies and cannot be read any other way. It took off its shoes at the fourth tier. On the eleventh step from the bottom it stumbled, because the step is a finger too high and because it would not refuse a gift left there for it by the dead. A woman on the landing saw the stumble and did not look up. That is the whole of the human record of the visit, and it is accurate.

What had drawn it was not the water moving in whole steps, and not the shuffled echo, and not the dream on the ninth tier. A wise power is not a tourist of anomalies. What had drawn it was the way you notice, in a room where everyone is shouting, that there is someone very old in the corner who has not been spoken to in four hundred years, and who is holding the roof up.

*

The first thing it considered was whether to do anything at all, and it considered this for eleven days, which by its own reckoning was a long time. Most of what it finally did was a form of that consideration.

It went over the place the way a mason goes over a face of stone, with the flat of the hand.

It measured the spoons worn into the treads and calculated the stone that feet had taken out of the Throat since the first flight was cut: something like a wall, a wall's worth of limestone dispersed as dust into the district, onto the fields, into the lungs of women dead eight generations, into the grain, into the children of those women. It noted that the masons had never once replaced this loss and had never once been wrong not to. Wear on a tread is not damage. It is the thing itself, arriving.

It read the four alphabets. It could read them at a glance, the way you read your own handwriting. The oldest says *set by*. The second says *set again*. The third is not writing at all but a tally, cut at the base of each replaced block, of how many times that particular block had been replaced before, and the highest number in the shaft is nineteen, on a nosing of the third flight. The fourth alphabet, the one the living masons cut small and cannot read, says *and again*.

Four unrelated peoples, four dead tongues, and every one of them had arrived independently at the same sentence, which is not a claim of ownership and not a name but a statement about repetition, made by someone tired, in the hope that the next person would be tired in the same way.

The power did not tell the masons this. They find their illiteracy satisfying rather than sad, and they are right, and it is not a wise power's business to convert a correct intuition into a fact.

But it did grieve, properly and at length, for the three dead languages, standing on the sixth tier in the heat with its hand on a pillar. Nobody else can. That grief had been owed for a long time and nobody in the district had the vocabulary to pay it. It paid it. This took most of a morning and cost nothing and changed nothing, and of everything the power did in the Throat it may have been the largest.

*

Here is what it did not do.

It did not give them the wheel and the rope. It knew four ways to lift water out of a hole without walking, and it ran each of them forward in its mind the way you run a finger down a wet wall to find where the seep comes in. In every one, within three generations: the stairs disused, then unmaintained, then robbed for stone; the shaft narrowed; the eighty gone up into the light to be people without fields among people with them; the neck gone, the dish in the crown of the head gone, and with the noise gone, the hands gone, and with the hands gone the particular tenderness of this people, which is conducted at knee height inside a shrieking, and which does not exist anywhere else in the world. The pot on the head is not the price of the water. It is the shape of a hundred other things. You cannot lift it off without taking the rest.

It did not give them back their hearing. Deafness by forty is how they pay, and what they buy with it is a second language reserved entirely for love, gossip and jokes. It considered making the Throat quieter and understood almost at once that this was the same proposal in a different coat.

It did not cool the shaft, because the heat is the spring and the spring is the water.

It did not explain the fifth bay. It knows what the fifth bay is. But a thing said there is answered by the house in the house's own arrangement, and the answer binds, and afterwards it is nobody's fault, and this is the only place within four days' walk where an unbearable sentence can be set down by the person carrying it. Take away the mystery and you take away the mechanism, and the sentences go back into the households, where they do damage.

It did not stop the dream on the ninth tier. It stood there one night, on the warmest shelves, among the sizzle of bats, and understood that the dream is not a haunting and not a warning. It is the house talking in its sleep, and it is the only sleep the house gets, and the people who lie down there are lending it a mouth. It would not gag a thing for dreaming. It did make one alteration, so small it is barely worth setting down: in the dream, from that night forward, the sleeper standing out on the flat water is not standing there alone. Nobody has reported this, because nobody reports the second figure. You don't.

It did not make the frogs immortal. A die-off two generations back sent four men eleven days' walk to another valley to buy frogs in a sealed jar, and that journey is one of the three stories every child here can tell, and a people needs its three stories more than it needs an uninterrupted supply of frogs. What it did instead was walk the eleven days itself and spend a season on the reed-line of the other stepwell, which was sickening in a way nobody there had noticed yet, and set it right. The remedy now exists. It is still eleven days away. It will still cost somebody a walk, and the walk will still become the fourth story.

It did not interfere in the matter of the four who were put out of doors. It went up into the dust and found the one of them still living, an old man with fields now, who had done the thing he was expelled for and had never once said otherwise. It sat with him on the hard ground at the northeast lip at dusk, out of the way of the fig, while the swifts came down in their falling spiral and the sound came up out of the rectangle like something breathing, and neither of them said anything for the hour, because during the swift-hour you sit, and it is understood that this is rest. Then the power went back down the west flights and the old man went home. It did not bring him back in. That was not its to do, and he would not have come.

It did not go to the bottom. The family that holds the bottom keeps one sentence about what is under the basin floor and has never said it aloud to anyone. The power let the sentence alone. What is under the basin floor is under the basin floor. It is not what they think. It is not far off.

*

Here is what it did do, and note the size of it.

On the seventh tier, third pillar in from the north in the second rank, there is a shell of sound ochre limestone around a core that went soft a long time ago. It will fail in a wet year and bring down the landing above it and, depending on the hour, between two and nine people. It could not be repaired by a stranger: a stranger's repair is a miracle, and a miracle brings pilgrims, and two hundred pots a day become two thousand, and the machine, which is dimensioned to the exact traffic it has, breaks under the weight of its own fame.

So the power moved a drip. One drip, on the tier above, redirected by a thumb's width of packed moss, so that it now falls three fingers to the left of where it fell. In four years it will have laid a rust-coloured stain down the face of that pillar in the pattern a mason reads as a fault. He will cut into it, swearing, expecting nothing, and he will find the soft core, and he will replace the pillar in whatever stone can be got, which will be pale grey, and there will be a second grey pillar in the shaft, and a second story about a pillar, and his mark at the base of it will say *and again*.

On the fourth landing there is a girl, a Twenty-Nine, slow on the stairs and mocked for it, of no use as a carrier and aware of it. She has the ear. The drip-tuner is old and there has always been exactly one. The power arranged that the old woman was standing at the head of the fourth flight, for the space of about six breaths, on the evening the child said out loud that the seventeenth pot had gone sour — which it had, by evaporation, a quarter-tone sharp, over the course of a month in which nobody else had noticed anything at all except that they were sleeping badly.

That is the entire intervention: one body, in one place, for six breaths. It is not certain the power did anything there. The old woman walks that landing most evenings. That uncertainty is the mark of the work.

It checked the bees in the sixth-tier crack, because the people say the bees are holding something up and the people say a great many things. The bees are holding something up. The comb has filled a shear in a beam-seat and is carrying load. It left them alone and told nobody, and made a small private sound that in a lesser being would have been a laugh.

*

Then the joints of the upper tiers began to sweat, and the squash was let down, and the loose things came up out of the bottom, and the sky over the rectangle went the colour of a bruise, and the rain came in as a solid falling column exactly the shape of the well's mouth.

In the roar — in that noise with no name, in which eighty people and ten thousand birds and three thousand bats and an uncounted number of frogs are all shouting and none can be heard, which is the closest thing to privacy that has ever existed — the power went and stood in the third bay of the fifth landing, with its back to the middle pillar, and raised its voice above the volume required, and said one word.

One word, in the second of the four alphabets, a tongue whose last speaker died before the third tier was faced.

The bay takes four words and gives them back shuffled. It takes one word and can only give it back as it is. So for the first time in the history of the house, something was said in the fifth bay and returned entire, and what returned was a name — not a word for a hole or for a work or for water, but the kind of word you give to a living thing on the understanding that it will answer to it.

It answered to it.

The children's list has that entry now, near the top, copied down by shape from a mason's mark by a child who could not pronounce it and passed along the same way ever since. It is the only sentence on the list that comes back unchanged. The children think this one is broken. Every few generations one of them gets the sounds right by accident, and the bay answers at unusual length, and the child goes very quiet and is teased, and grows up, and is not teased about it after forty.

*

The rain stopped in the third watch. The power stayed down at the waterline, announcing itself first, quietly, to the wall.

At some point between the last frog and the first swift, the water came up two steps. Whole ones. It does not happen in stages and nobody has ever seen it: the basin lifts a hand's width in one motion and lays its edge along the front of a tread, as neatly as a woman setting a pot into a resting-bowl. The power watched it happen. That is all it did. It is the first witness the house has had in the one act it performs entirely for its own reasons, and it turns out that the reason is manners.

And for the hour of that gap, nothing in the Throat wore.

Not a grain left the treads. Not a flake left the plaster. The grass rope did not rot by one fibre, the timber did not give, the moss beards drank and took nothing, the lime did not chalk, and the two hundred drips fell and fell and carried away nothing at all. One hour, out of four hundred years, in which the house was not being eaten and therefore did not need to be kept.

Nobody could measure it. An hour's decay in a stone building is not a quantity a person can find. The masons replaced exactly as much stone that year as every year, because the equilibrium is calculated on the season and not on the hour, and the books, which are not books but the habits of six families, balanced as usual.

The house knew.

The swift-hours are given to the people by physics, and the people sit inside them and call it rest. Until that night nothing had ever given an hour to the house. It is the only gift the power could find that cost the world nothing, broke no rule, taught no dependency, drew no pilgrims, and could not be repeated often enough to become a right.

*

At dawn it sat through the swifts without wax, with the ears it had made, and the ears were hurt, and it kept them.

Then it went to the bottom and somebody handed it a pot, because in the bellow-register work is assigned to whoever is standing there, and it put the pot on its head and climbed the eastern flights with it, flat-footed, rolling, never hurried, never slowed, chin level, eyes at a point some distance ahead and slightly above. It yielded once, on the seventh, to a woman more laden. It did not look at the ground. It set the pot down on the dust among the goat pellets and the papery husks and was thanked by nobody, which was correct, and that pot of water is the sum total of its contribution to the household economy of the Throat.

Before it went it left one thing, in the lap of the god with the jar, among the sixty green nubs of spent bronze: a chisel worn down to the length of a thumb. Nobody counts them. Sixty-one now.

And low on the base of a block it had set in the proper manner, out of the wrong stone, because the wrong stone was what could be got, it cut a mark, small, in an alphabet that makes five.

It says *and again*.

There was nothing else to say, and it would not be the one to break the pattern. It changed nothing, at the rate of nothing per year, which is the only compliment the Throat has ever been paid in a currency it uses.
