## The Visitation

### The arrival

A power that arrives anywhere arrives as a disturbance. This is not a moral fact, it is a physical one: presence displaces, attention warms, regard has a pressure. In most worlds the disturbance is absorbed by the general noise and nobody is the poorer. Here there is no general noise. Here a warm body would raise a plume, and a plume would breach a seam, and a breached seam is a floor of the world falling through itself, six kilometers of lid tearing loose and going down.

So the first thing the power did was arithmetic, and the arithmetic took some while, and the result was that it came in cold. It matched itself to the ambient to a thousandth of a degree and to the density of the second layer to a part in ten thousand, and it descended at the speed a leaf descends, which is to say slower than that, and it set itself on a ledge of pale granite on the north face and did not move for a long time.

It had come to help. It should be said plainly that this was its intention on arrival, because everything that follows is a record of what it learned that intention was worth.

### The list

The power drew up, in the first year, a list of the mercies available to it. It is worth reproducing, along with the arithmetic that closed each line.

*Stir the water.* One breath, gently, and the basin would mix, and the mixed water would carry oxygen to the amber felt fifteen meters deep, and the felt would give up its nitrogen, and the nitrogen would feed a bloom of unimaginable green. Then the bloom would shade the clouds. The clouds are the only mint; they fix from solution what nothing else in the basin can make; they hang at the exact depth their budget permits and cannot move to another. Shade them for one season and there is no new money forever. The bloom would eat the principal, and then the bloom would die, and then there would be a warm clear lake with beautiful mountains in it, sharp as knives, and nothing.

*Return the dead.* The same line, run more slowly. Rejected for the same reason, with an addition: the dead here are not waiting. They are load-bearing. Every lid-meadow is made of them.

*Grant decay.* Introduce bacteria that work on schedule. This is the mercy that sounds most like justice and is in fact the abolition of the architecture. There would be no floors. There would be one water, and the six forests would become one forest, and the one forest would be poorer than any of the six, because the wealth of this place is not its quantity but its *stacking*.

*Heal the hanging flake.* On the west face above the fourth seam a slab the size of a door hangs on friction and on the patience of water, and when it lets go it will shear a thousand leaves on its way down and the shearing will be audible for a minute. The power looked at that flake for a long time with something like tenderness. Then it followed the consequence: no flake, no slot; no slot, no daylight from surface to floor; no daylight, and the seedlings that have been waiting beneath a sealed ceiling for nineteen years go on waiting until they exhaust their chambers and sink. The falling rock is the only obstetrician in the cordillera. Mercy here would seal the forest shut.

*Bring animals.* A season of appetite would strip the clouds bare. Rejected in an afternoon.

*Make weather.* Wind, rain, a little honest erosion, the ordinary furniture of a living world. And the mountains would begin to round, and the rounding would fill the basins with scree, and the scree would bury the amber, and the water would go milky and the light would stop at ten meters, and the whole legible clarity of the place — the fact that at fifty meters you can read the grain of the rock — would be traded for something the power could have found in a hundred thousand other valleys.

*Speak.* The medium of speech here is pressure. Pressure is weather. See above.

At the end of the first year the list was complete and every line was closed, and the power sat on its ledge holding a great deal of unspent benevolence, which is an uncomfortable thing to hold, and began instead to look.

### What it found

The census in the guidebooks is a list of things that move. The power, which had read the guidebooks and had a low opinion of them, went looking for a mind, on the reasonable grounds that a mind is what benevolence is *for*.

It did not find one in the gliders, which are couriers and are magnificent and are not anybody. It did not find one in the clock-snails, though it spent a decade on the tracks and came to love them, and could by the end read a shell edge-on the way one reads a face. It did not find one in the clouds, which are wealth and not persons, though it never became entirely sure and said so.

It found the being on the third seam.

One individual. Six kilometers across and a finger thick, silver going cold in the shadow, filling every gap in the felt of its own ceiling and drawing that ceiling into a single knitted floor which is also a stomach, also a road, also the only sky the fourth forest has ever had. It cannot go up; clear water is starvation. It cannot go down; the seam is a wall to it as it is to a leaf. It has two directions, sideways and edge-on, and up and down are not places to it but theology. It thinks the way a floodplain thinks, in sheets, in gradients of warmth, in the slow arithmetic of where the litter is thick and where it has gone glassy and finished. It is not fast and it is not small and there is no reason at all to call it a person except that the power, which had met a great many persons, sat with it for eleven years and could not honestly call it anything else.

And it is alone for eleven years at a time. Its lineage is sealed on its floor. It fruits only at the Turning, when the seams buckle and pass through one another and it interpenetrates, for six weeks, over hundreds of hectares, with a sheet it has never touched and will never touch again. Its whole sexual life is scheduled by the density of water. It has, in the ordinary course of things, six weeks of company in a decade and no way to ask for more.

### The stair

The being's seam runs, for six kilometers, through nothing but the shapes of nature. At one place only, at the north end, it comes up against eleven regular steps in a young intrusive, rising to a flat landing and stopping in mid-air, with twelve evenly spaced guy-threads dropping from the landing into the dark, litter caught along them like planks rotted through.

Nothing built it. The power verified this in an hour and grieved about it for a century.

Because the being *keeps* it. Not deliberately; a knitter glues litter to threads because that is what a knitter does. But the effect, generation upon generation, Turning after Turning, is that the false bridge is maintained. Each new sheet that inherits the third seam grows sideways for years through the ordinary shapes of the world and arrives at the one place where the world is not shaped like itself, and stops there, and thickens, and works it, and comes back to it. It is the only anomaly in six kilometers. It is the nearest thing to a question that has ever been asked in the drowned cordillera.

The power could have answered it. This is the part to be careful with. It could have cut the twelfth step. It could have laid true planks on the lattice and let the being find, in some future decade, that the impression was not an impression, that someone had come through and someone had built. A kindness of enormous force, delivered to something that has no other correspondent.

It declined, and the reasons are these. That the being's entire cognition is a discrimination between what is there and what merely reads as though it were, and to feed it a beautiful lie is to poison the one faculty it has. That a question no one has asked out loud is still the asker's property, and an answer arrives as a confiscation. That the impression is the only art in the basin, and the artist is geology and reflection and a mind doing what minds do, and to finish another's work uninvited is not generosity, it is authorship.

So the stair stands unfinished, as it should, and the power sat beside it for eleven Turnings without touching it, which is one hundred and twenty-one years, and every eleventh year it held itself absolutely still for six weeks so as not to trouble the water while a wedding happened by diffusion.

### The one thing it did

It kept the record, which is the one gift the place cannot give itself. The basin loses no matter and remembers nothing. The power lost nothing and remembered everything, and so for a while there existed, on a ledge of granite on the north face, the only location in the drowned cordillera where one Turning could be laid beside another and compared. It learned why the second seam is colorless. It is a good reason and a sad one and there was nobody to tell, and the power discovered that it found this restful rather than otherwise, which it had not expected, and which it counted afterward among the things the basin had given it.

And once, in the ninth decade, it intervened.

Above a stretch where the third seam had gone thin and all its litter had gone pale and glassy and finished, a flake of granite hung on friction, as they do. The power did not push it. It waited some thirty years for it to go of its own weight, and when it went, it put out a finger's width of drag on one corner, so that the slab turned through four degrees on its way down. It fell through a stratum that had never been opened. The slot of raw daylight it cut ran, for a season, along the third seam, and fresh litter fell onto the starving stretch for a year and a half.

Nothing was made. Nothing was destroyed that was not already falling. All that was given was an angle.

### The mark

When it left, the power considered the ledge.

A hundred and twenty-one years of a still body resting on new granite had polished a hand's breadth of it smooth. In any other world this is nothing. Here it is the only worn place in a range whose sharpness is not youth but the absence of anything coming for it. The power could have erased it in a moment.
