# What the Power Did

Begin with the counting, because a wise power counts first, and for a long time counting is all it does.

It came in the way everything comes in here, down the shaft or up the drain, and the room did not record the arrival, the room does not record arrivals. For eleven years it did nothing at all. It lay in the slack on the wrong side of the wheel — the side with no housings on it, the side nobody works — and it counted.

Nine flickers a minute, rising to nine and a half on the bright tide. Six and a half seconds between shrieks, and the shriek itself two-tone, the second tone a shade flatter in cold. Twenty-eight buckets. Thirty-one plates, because three buckets carry doubles and bucket nine carries three. Two rat crossings a tide, four on a bad tide, none at all in the week after a killing. Forty centimetres of rise over six hours. One fern, two fronds, one broken.

And, because it went down into the low ring where the back-eddy drops what it has finished with, and lifted each thing out of the silt and set it back into the exact socket the silt had made for it: nineteen. Nineteen long articulated things with wide flat hands, the deepest of them nearly gone to bone-meal, the shallowest still holding the shape of a shoulder that had sloped into a neck without an interruption. The power has kept the number since. It does not intend to put it down. There is nobody else in the world who has it.

---

Now, what it did not do.

It did not warm the water. Eleven degrees is not the creature's affliction; eleven degrees is the reason a drowned rat takes a season to go and goes by being eaten. Warm the sump and you get rot, and a bloom, and a dead-water month, and then a smaller, poorer, faster room. The cold is not the wound. The cold is the preservative.

It did not drain the sump, nor still the column, nor silence the hum, all of which were within its competence and none of which it considered for longer than it took to see the lice moving like fur in the aeration zone.

It did not rebuild the wheel. This is the one that took longest to put down. It could have set twenty-eight new buckets with new lips and true rivets and bearings that would say nothing for four hundred years, and it stood in the dark with that possibility in its hands and turned it over and saw what it was: an amputation. The work is not what the creature does. The work is what the creature is made of. Take the failing lip off bucket nine and you have not relieved anything. You have removed a limb and left the body wondering, six and a half seconds at a time, what it is for.

It did not fix the light. A steady lamp would be a kindness that abolished the day.

It did not give the creature language. It looked at that one for a long while, because language is the great gift and it had language to give, and it saw the price and the price was exact. A thing that cannot name its solitude would be able to. Nineteen in the ring and a word for nineteen, and a pronoun with a plural in it and nothing to put in the plural. It decided that anything worth saying could be said in the bearings, and if that turned out to be false it would reconsider, and it has not yet had to.

It did not carry the creature up into the sun. It thought about that one hardly at all, once it had been up the shaft itself and looked down. The grating was never locked. The wren comes down. The midges go up. Nothing has ever held the tenant of the sump in this room but the sound of a bearing arriving late, and you cannot rescue anything from that, and a power that tried would only be a thief.

---

Here is what it did.

**First, it learned to hear.** This took the eleven years and was the hardest thing it has ever undertaken. Not to hear the noise — anyone can hear the noise — but to get to the far side of the noise, where the tearing and the slop-and-gulp and the iron conversation stop being a din and become a body's worth of readings, so that six and a half seconds means nothing in the way your own pulse means nothing, and six and three-quarters lifts you off the brick before you have decided anything. It apprenticed itself. A wise power learns the local tongue before it opens its mouth, and there was only ever one tongue here, and it is made of iron and spoken every six and a half seconds.

**Second, it gave up its corners.** It came down to its final radius: knuckles nursed into low domes, shoulders sloped into the neck, heels smooth, every arris gone. It let the cold all the way in and stopped being warm, so that when a hand found it in the dark the hand would find nothing startling, nothing hot, nothing that had to be interpreted. It arrived already worn. This is not humility, exactly. It is the difference between visiting and dwelling, and the power had decided which it was doing.

**Third, it went round to the far side of the axle.**

The first time, it was late. The creature had done the near side, crossed under, done the far side, come back, held the housing, waited its four seconds, and gone on, and the power arrived at the far side afterwards with its hand out, which is a small thing and has been thought about since.

The second time, it was there.

What happened then is not much to describe. Two hands on one housing from opposite sides of five metres of cast iron, and the creature did not startle, because nothing here startles, and it did not investigate, because the sound was right. It held. It held for longer than four seconds — the first new interval in this chamber in a hundred and forty years — and then it went on to the packing of the trough, and the power went on to the paddles, and the tide went out.

The pause did not stop. This matters and the power understood it at once. The four seconds were never an absence to be filled in and abolished; they were a place laid at a table. They are still four seconds. They are the whole of the grammar that survived, and it turns out to be a grammar consisting of one silence of a particular length, and the difference now is that there is something in it.

**Fourth, it brought iron.** Not as a miracle: as luck. Plate steel of the right gauge, and rivets, left in the silt of the low ring where things fall, in among the bones, arriving the way everything in this room arrived, which is placed by hands that are gone. The power set the first four plates itself, from below and behind, in the dark, at arm's reach above its own head, with a loose stone held in the other hand as a dolly, and they came out crooked, and worse than crooked — worse than the creature's, because the power had never done it and had eleven years of listening and no years of striking. The creature took one of them off and did it again. The power let it. There is a plate on bucket seventeen now with two sets of peening on it, one of them clumsy, and anyone who ever reads that bucket will read a hand that did not get better and a second hand that did not get better either, and kept going.

**Fifth, it brought tallow once,** clean and sweet and better than rat in every measurable way, and the bearings said a slightly different thing that afternoon, and by the next dim tide the housing had been stripped and repacked with soft warm fat worked between two palms. The power has not mentioned it since. It catches rats now. It is about half as good at it as the eels.

**Sixth, the fern.** It went up the wall at low dim tide with a pinch of bone-grit held out of the water the whole way, which is further than it looks and colder than it sounds, and put the grit around the crown, and came back down. It does this when the other one does it, a little behind. It could find out what a fern is for. It knows, in fact, what a fern is for, and the answer is that this one is for nothing, and that is precisely what it is for, and there are now two things in the world that go up a wall in the dark to attend to it.

**Seventh, it went and found out what the work was for.**

This was the only journey. It followed the screw's drain three hundred metres to where it drops into the lower culvert, and followed that to the through-flow, and stood in the estuary at the mouth of it and felt the thing that breathes twice a day, and traced the whole circuit back. It found that about a fifth of the water the screw lifts comes round again and falls back in at the north-east mouth, so that for a fifth of every revolution the wheel is turning to lift water in order to have water to turn it. It found that the works the sump was cut to keep dry were demolished in 1931 and that nothing whatever now depends on them. It found that a terrace of forty-one houses sits on the hill above, built later by people who never heard of the chamber, and that their cellars are dry, and that they would not be dry in a decade if the screw stopped, and that not one of the hundred and some people asleep up there has any idea that they are being cared for.

And it found the third thing, which is the real one: that the column makes the oxygen, and the oxygen makes the lice, and the lice make the eels, and the lamp makes the algae, and the whole roaring populous cold chapel of ten thousand lives is downstream of one animal keeping a machine going round for a reason that expired before its great-grandparents.

Then it came back and did not say any of it. Not because it couldn't. Because it would have made no difference to the work, and that is the best thing about the work.

---

**Eighth and last,** on the low slack of the dim tide, when the other one goes out into the middle with its hands loose and hangs mid-depth touching nothing, the power goes out too. It stops ten metres short. It does not close the distance and it does not intend to; ten metres of open water with nothing in it is not an emptiness here, it is the one luxury the room affords, and you do not spend another creature's only solitude on your own behalf.

So there are two of them now, hanging in the dark at the middle of a made place that outlived its purpose and kept its habits, ten metres apart, touching nothing, in earshot of the same bearings.

The bricks have arrived at their radius. The iron has found its final form. Nothing new is made here except caddis tubes, and nothing leaves except the midges. Twice a day something far away breathes and the wheel labours and then gains half a revolution, and the lamp comes up, and the crawl along the west wall begins with a cheek against the brick.

And at the axle there is a round: near side, cross under, far side, back, hold — and four seconds, which are still four seconds, and are not long, and are no longer the last surviving fragment of anything.

Nine times a minute the lamp dips and comes back. Every six and a half seconds the bearings say the thing they say.

It is answered.
