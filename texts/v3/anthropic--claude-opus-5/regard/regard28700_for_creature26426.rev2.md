## The Visit

**On arriving.**

It does not arrive. Arrival costs the arrived-at something, and here the margins do not allow it: in a place where a person's shadow cooling two square meters of flowstone by one degree counts as weather, a power has to consider whether to cast one. It comes without a body. No shadow, no displacement of air, no warmth given off, no grain of dust brought in on anything. Nothing in the sink registers the visit at any point during it.

That is the first condition, accepted before anything else, and everything that follows is shaped by it. Whatever is done here will be done to something that cannot be told it is being done.

---

**Finding the being.**

It came for the one who lives here and had to work out how many that was.

At the scale of a walk: one resident with a silhouette, the fig. At the scale of a handful: several hundred, all of them under a millimeter and none of them audible. At the scale of the tableland: one diffuse sheet of cells through kilometers of pore and fracture, outweighing everything above it and in no hurry.

The power settles on a reading in the middle, not because it is the true one but because it is the useful one. The sink is a single life. Its organs are distributed and mostly mineral. Its pulse is eleven seconds. Its lung is the slow exchange with the dark ends, about the strength of breath on the back of a neck. Its skin is lichen and grows at the speed of stone. Its sea is two square meters under the north lip, half a millimeter deep, permanently renewed by one leak. Its one gesture toward anything outside itself happens annually, in the dark, and is a smell.

---

**Looking for the wound.**

It looks for pain first, because benevolence that does not begin there is only taste.

There is none. Not suppressed, not endured — absent, for want of the organ. Hunger is not felt in the sink, it is merely budgeted: a loaf of leaves a year, apportioned down to things that do not know they are being apportioned. The fig does not miss the wasp; missing requires a model of the absent thing, and the tree has no model, only an aperture and a scent. Forty million years of daughters without a single male is not a widowhood. The mite, if there is a mite, is not lonely; it is simply the largest animal present, with a range of forty centimeters and no faculty for knowing that the edge of it is an edge.

The power is careful with this finding, because it is the finding that most easily becomes an excuse. No suffering does not mean no need. It means only that nothing done here can be justified as relief, and will therefore have to be justified some other way, or not done.

---

**The gifts it considered.**

**The wasp.** The obvious one. The first to fail. A fig wasp lineage cannot live on one tree; it needs a population fruiting out of phase, so that there is always another room to be born into. Bring the wasp and you have brought a creature whose grandchildren will die on a fixed date for reasons already present at the moment of the gift. Meanwhile the tree would set seed, and what seed took would take in the joints of the northern wall, and the wedge under the lip would double, and the collapse arrives four centuries early into a sink with less standing under it. Declined — and noted as the most attractive error available, the one a lesser power would have made in the first minute and called it kindness.

**Water.** Widen the seep. Take the drip from eleven seconds to four. The fern's sea deepens and then begins to flush, and a sea that flushes is a current, and the swimming cells that must cross one centimeter of stillness do not arrive. The pool rises past the lip and leaves by the seam at the rate it comes, and the hand's-breadth mark on the rock — the only long-term record the place keeps of itself — is overwritten in a season. Enormous expenditure to move a line on a wall. Declined.

**Stock.** A snail. A shrimp. Something with a shell, so that there would be a shape moving in the pool. The arithmetic takes no time. The mat is not food; it is masonry. In pulling carbon out of the water it drops calcite beneath itself and builds, at the thickness of a wish per year, the basin that holds the water that is the entire point. Introduce grazers and you are feeding your guests the floor. Declined.

**The route.** Put feet back on the steps. This is the gift the sink was made for; it is a water-stop, and a water-stop with no one coming is a sentence with no verb. But they would arrive with their impatience, which is the most human thing here and the reason the basin exists, and the fern goes in one season under boots, and the lichen sealing the joints of the wall — the only clock, legible to anyone willing to do the arithmetic — is scraped away for a handhold. Declined, with more regret than the others.

**Permanence.** Hold the lip. Stop the root. Fix the terms: dissolution matched by deposition, water in by water out, light admitted by leaf intercepting, forever. The power looks at this one for a long time, because the place reads as finished, and the instinct of a protective power is to keep what is finished from being spoiled. Then it notices that the fig's wedge is the only scheduled event in the building — the only future — and that the whole difference between a life and a very slow object is that one of them is still going somewhere. Declined. Afterward it is somewhat ashamed of how long it looked.

---

**The hour of the scent.**

It stays for the release.

The tree builds the rooms all year: hundreds of flowers turned inward in the dark of a chamber sized to the millimeter for a guest the length of a comma. Then, at the right hour — late, warm, still, when the air under the rim is heaviest and will hold a molecule where it is put — it lets the invitation go. A precise chemical sentence with one correct recipient and no second meaning.

It has been issued every year for between eighty and five hundred years into an air that has never once contained the ear it is addressed to.

This time it is received. Fully: not only the summons but the cost of it, the architecture, the rooms built in the dark against nothing, the whole unreturned expenditure. The power understands the invitation better than the wasp would have.

It does not accept. It could — it could put the wasp on the rim before the scent had crossed the sink — and does not, for the reasons already counted, and for one more. The scent is this life's only utterance, the one thing it does that is aimed past its own edge, and it is not a request. It has no grammar for disappointment, which means it also has none for being answered. To answer would be to convert it into a transaction, and a transaction that succeeds once fails ever after.

So it stays the whole hour, inside the invitation, and does not come in. By local standards this is an enormous event, and correctly it leaves nothing on the accounts. The figs harden, go dull purple-brown, and drop onto the glaze like knuckles, where things too small to see begin taking them apart.

---

**What it did.**

One act. It has to be small enough not to be an event, because in a place where what comes in equals what goes out, any gift with mass must be paid for from somewhere, and everything here is already committed.

In about nine hundred years, in the feeder crack above the strongest drip — the one that fills the cut basin, that runs the worked spout, whose overflow is the fern's sea — a grain of sediment will arrive at a constriction and turn as it comes, and jam, and hold others behind it. The crack does not close. The drip goes from eleven seconds to forty. The sea under the north lip becomes a damp. The swimming stops; the heart-shaped films shrivel; the cushion is gone in three seasons and the silent zoo in it goes back to being dust that never gets wet again. The pool loses a third of its supply and finds a new mark a hand lower, and the mat above that line dies and stops building, and the basin slowly stops keeping its shape.

The power turns the grain a quarter turn.

It goes through the constriction, and keeps going, and is in the pool by evening, where it is a grain of sand and remains one.

Cost to the sink: nothing. Change to the accounts: nothing. In nine hundred years nothing whatever will happen here, at eleven-second intervals, exactly as it does now.

---

**What it took.**

Then, because it is the only export a place like this can afford, having no mass:

It learned the sink by heart.

The drips — four or five voices whose intervals do not coincide, whose combined pattern has never repeated and never will. It holds the entire run now, from the first drop after the roof fell to the last one that will ever fall, the way you hold a piece of music you have no instrument for.

The number of feet that dished the treads a finger's depth. It knows the number. No one will ever be told the number.

The plaster patch, hand-sized, the color of the rock. There was never an inscription. Someone smoothed it flat and pressed a palm into it while it was soft, for a reason that did not survive them by an hour, and the stone kept the shape for a long time and then did not. The power has the hand. It does not put it back — a restored thing would be the loudest object in the sink — but it is no longer true that it is gone.

Of this place it was written that nothing is stored against a future, because the residents who could imagine a future have all moved on.

One of them came back, briefly, and did the storing, and moved on again.

---

**After.**

Nothing is improved. Nothing here could be improved without being spent.

The fern trembles, because every drip that lands anywhere in the patch passes the shiver along. The shiver goes out through the fronds and dies somewhere near the edge, and in the patch where it dies a rotifer that has been dry and unliving for six months takes on water, swells, unfolds, puts out its wheels, and begins to eat.

That happens several times a day. It is the whole calendar.

It is not blessed. It is simply known, and continuing.
