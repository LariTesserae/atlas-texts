## The Attention

It comes down through the lid and does not break it.

That is the first decision and it is made before arriving: no light. Consider what light would be here. Not illumination — a foreign object. Switch on a sun at forty-eight metres and the plain does not become visible, it becomes *lit*, which is a different thing and a worse one, and every grain in the bottom fog would hand the glare along to the next grain until the whole loaded ten metres was a white blindness, and the one animal capable of registering it would register it with four pigment cups and no vocabulary. And afterwards, when the light was withdrawn, there would be a darkness. There is no darkness here now. There is only the absence of a comparison. The power understands that to give the plain a sun for an hour is to give it, permanently, a night.

So it enters at the loaded density of the bottom water, ballasted exactly, weighing nothing, and is taken by the current at a walking pace, and goes where the bedload goes. It arrives as part of the bedload. It is the second thing on this plain that has never had to hold on.

---

Now the gifts. It has brought all of them, as such powers do, and it sets them out on the ripples and looks at them one at a time in the dark.

**To undrown it.** To take the sea off, the way you take a sheet off furniture. Give back the alder, the hazel, the reed beds, the meltwater rivers with their gravel on the outer bends, the whole county of it up into the air. Declined, and quickly. The ghost of that river is the only map the inhabitant has, and it reads it by taste. The forest is not lost; the forest is lunch, and has been for eight thousand years, delivered one autumn at a time on a hundred-year schedule. You cannot restore the woodland to the plain. The plain is where the woodland went. And a country that has stopped arguing would have to be given something to lose before it could be given a shape, and the power has read enough of these landscapes to know what the giving of shapes costs.

**Eyes, and something to use them on.** Declined. See above.

**A rock.** One hard permanent thing, a landmark, a rise, an anchor. Declined: it has never in its life had to hold on. Give it an anchor and you have given it, for the first time, the possibility of being torn away.

**Company.** This one it sits with. It sits with it through a whole reversal of the tide, because this is the gift it wanted most to give, and because the wanting is a bad sign and it knows to slow down when the wanting starts. The animal's only encounter with its own kind is the moment of being cut in half. But *loneliness* is a word from the account, not from the animal; there is no organ in it that company could enter by, no ache to relieve, no absence being felt. To give it a companion, the power would first have to install the need — make it poor in order to make it rich, and call the whole operation a rescue. Declined. Set down gently, but declined.

**Memory.** The temptation with real teeth. To hand it the pictures: canopy, wind in leaves, the smell of a spring morning eight thousand years dead, the men at the one hearth. It eats that forest leaf by leaf and knows it the way a tongue knows, without a single image, and it is not haunted. Give it the image and you have given it the loss. The power comes closer to harm here than anywhere else in this valley, and recognises it in time, and puts the gift back.

**Exemption from burial.** Declined. The burial is not the sentence being served. Sleep is most of its life. Take the sleep away and you have not given it two hundred and forty years; you have taken two hundred and thirty-one and left it nine.

**Permanence.** One small monument. One thing that would accumulate. Declined: there is a fireplace out there, effaced, on a plain the size of a country, and it is all the evidence anyone needs about what this floor does with structures.

---

Then it looks for the best gift available and finds that the best gift available was already given, and not by a power.

A man sat down on dry ground one afternoon within sight of a river and struck a dozen small blades off a core and left them where they fell, along with the core, along with the mess. He was not thinking of the future. There was no future to think of in the direction he was facing. And those edges, which have not dulled, are the only fertility this plain has ever had — a soft page-thick animal presses itself flat across a flint scar and becomes two animals, both of which seal their wounds within the hour and drift apart on the next tide.

And somebody, on another afternoon, pushed a row of hazel rods into a riverbank in the arc of a fish trap. It still works. It has been catching, holding, dividing and releasing on a six-hour cycle since the water came, two hundred and eighty generations, and what leaves the curve of the rods is two where one went in.

The power examines this arrangement for a long time and concludes that it could not have built it better on purpose. It declines to improve it. It leaves the flint sharp, which the flint was managing on its own.

Someone should be told that the trap still works. There is nobody. So the power takes the fact and holds it, since a record with no reader is precisely the sort of thing it exists in order to be.

---

Here is what it does.

**It reads.**

Out on the bared peat, where the ripples are interrupted — a rectangle a metre by half a metre, ridges taken down, film licked off, the black grazed to a shine like the worn part of a step, the edges crisp — the power reads it. It reads that one, and the one three hundred metres downstream, and the next, and it goes on reading, tide after tide, carried, for as long as it takes, which is a while, because there are millions and they are all the same and it reads them all. The content is identical every time: *the litter was good here; I have gone; I do not know that I have written.* It is the only writing on the plain. It has never had a reader. It has one now, and the reader is thorough, and reads some of them twice.

**It settles the arrears.**

There is a bristle worm the size of a thumb whose flank hair iridesces bronze-green when light reaches it, and light does not reach it, and so the iridescence has occurred, unwitnessed, for the entire duration of every sea mouse that has ever lived down here. The power attends to one. Then, because this is within its competence, it attends to all of them, the whole eight thousand years of unobserved bronze-green, and enters the lot in the ledger. This does nothing for the sea mice. Debts of that kind were never owed to the debtor.

**It waits.**

A gale stands up above and the dune field shoulders across the flat, and the animal's margins seal and the papillae fold in and it goes under half a metre of sand and becomes, functionally, a stratum.
