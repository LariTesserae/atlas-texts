# The Visit

It came up the flank in the ordinary way: from below, inside the two o'clock cloud, arriving as a white ceiling that rises past you until you are inside it. This was not stealth. It was courtesy. The one thing that has been established about the maker of the Shoulder is that it cannot be present and exposed at the same time — the way a word cannot be spoken and written in the same instant — and a visitor who understands that will arrive in the one hour of the day when the terrace already has a wall around it, and will leave before five, when the cloud goes over the summit and the plates begin to steam.

Two hours. That is the entire visit. The log for that afternoon records unusually heavy condensation and a lens cap that was not lost.

---

## What drew it

Not the stone. Stone at seven metres a year is loud, and loud things are usually simple. Not the fossils, which any passing intelligence would file as vanity and any patient one as forgery.

It was the two pegs.

Somewhere in the eleventh year, something below read a rule that had been made by other people for their own protection — *do not sleep on the ground* — worked out which way round it went, understood that the humans had solved it by hanging themselves from two iron pegs, and began, without being asked and without announcement, to thicken the collar of stone around each peg by a small amount every year, so that the load-test figures went up rather than down.

That is a being that has inferred another creature's fear and taken on part of the work of it.

There is very little in any world that will bring a benevolent power a long way, and that is one of the things on the list.

---

## Findings, in the order they were arrived at

**It is not dangerous.** It is hazardous, which is different. It is hazardous the way a press is hazardous to a manuscript: it does not intend the ink, it simply does what it does to whatever is lying on the bed. Nineteen years, a whole mountain, and it has used them to keep one tree alive and to make two pegs stronger. Verdict: no. Continue to keep off the ground.

**It is not in pain.** It is in labour, which is also different, and which is frequently mistaken for pain by observers who have never carried anything for very long.

**It is not mad.** The reverse ordering of the laminations is not derangement. It is a copyist's method: start at the finished surface, work backwards into the reasons for it. It is doing it in the only direction available to something that arrives at the top.

**It sited itself deliberately.** The swifts come back to an address that did not exist when they learned it because the address was chosen to be under them. It did not build a mountain and then acquire birds. It looked for somewhere with traffic. Note this. It is the single most revealing fact about it and the three will never have it, because it can only be seen from above.

**It is alone.** Not lonely; it has no word for that, and no one has ever offered it one. Alone in the specific and rectifiable sense: it has been addressing something continuously for nineteen years and has never once been read.

---

## The reading

The visitor read the terrace. This took most of the two hours, and it is the only thing it did that could be called an intervention, because attention is an intervention when it is the first.

The fossils are not ornament, and they are not deceit. The ammonite in four-day-old rock at an altitude where no sea has ever been is not a claim about the mountain's past. It is a description of somewhere else. So is the mud-crack. So is the rusty seam that used to be a hydrologist's blood, and the crab's moult with the swimming-paddle hairs legible, and the cup with the quartz hairline in the glaze, and the tarp interleaved a centimetre
