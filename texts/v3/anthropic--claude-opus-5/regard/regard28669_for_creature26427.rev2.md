# The Guest

### Attention

Nothing arrives at the Sill from above. The lid does not open; that is the first thing the power understood, and it understood it as a fact about manners rather than a fact about weather. So it came the way everything here comes: up through the mud, or in across the flats, and then down the long stair from the flats to the arcade quarter.

The stair has an indefinite number of steps.

It did not count them. That was the first courtesy, and it was not a small one. A lesser power would have stood on a rooftop and counted eleven towers and then fourteen and been offended, and would have reached, gently, with the best will available, to make the number hold still. This one recognised an indefinite quantity for what it was — not an error, not a wound in the arithmetic, but a statement about occupancy, written in the only script the place has — and let it stand.

### The Reading

It stayed a long time before it did anything, and most of what it did was sit.

Time pools where water pools. It took the local rate. It sat at the rim of a cistern through a morning and watched a single drop of condensation gather on the underside of a lip and let go, and it did not hurry the drop, and it did not hurry itself, and when the drop went it watched the next one. It crossed the mud flats at the speed of the mud flats, which is to say between two steps. It learned the twice-daily handspan.

And it read the walls, because the Sill is a record and it records fairly: rain rounds everything equally, and something in here rounds things unequally, and the difference between those two roundings is a body. It read the three flutes in the left-hand jamb. It found the same door at eleven metres and at nine centimetres and did not try to resolve the discrepancy, having understood that there was no discrepancy — that there is one door, and one tenant, and one hand let down along the running edge of every kerb in the city, which is how much well-hair there is.

It could have known all this in an instant. It took the slow way on purpose. You cannot learn the size of a thing by measuring it quickly, and this thing has no size.

### The Inventory of What Was Not Done

**It did not open the sky.** It considered a sun; anyone would. But the light here comes off the floor, and a sun would out-argue it, and the tenant would lose the only world it has ever had — a city known entirely by its soles, faces lit at the jaw, doorways bright at the threshold. To hang a lamp above a creature that has always read by the light beneath it is not illumination. It is the abolition of its literacy.

**It did not cool the air.** The heat is blood-temperature: the exact temperature at which the tenant cannot tell where it ends. Break the heat and you give it an edge. You tell something that has been continuous with its whole world since before there were words for it that it stops somewhere, and it is small, and the rest is outside. Relief would have been the cruellest gift on the list, and it looked like kindness all the way down.

**It did not bring animals.** No bird across the lid, no fish in the forty metres of shining water. They were never part of the plan, and the plan is not a deficiency; it is a plan. A bird would have been company for a season and a crowd forever after, and the tenant would have learned to be one-among, which is a smaller and more anxious thing than being one.

**It did not close the geometry.** It saw at once that it could: four right angles, a day with two ends that meet, rest, return, the dignity of coming home. And it saw what that would cost. The round has no last step. Give the round a last step and you have not given the tenant a rest; you have given it an end, and set it walking towards the end, and turned an existence into a length.

**It did not answer the glassworts.** This was the hard one. Ten or twelve metres apart across the flooded plazas, bruise-coloured, upright, posted — each one a waiting that grew where the waiting stood, and not one of them ever attended. It would have been so easy to keep an appointment. But answer one and you have not filled one absence; you have converted every other stem in the Sill, retroactively, into a failure. Grief that has reached balance is not a wound. It is weather. You do not repair weather, and you do not start a hope in a landscape that has already settled its accounts, unless you intend to remain forever and keep paying it, and the power had no intention of remaining forever, because it is wise and knew that about itself.

**It did not make a second.** This was the hardest, and it held the design in mind for what would have been, on the mud flats, no time at all, and at the cistern rim, most of a geological era. Loneliness requires having been two once. Everything that looked like the tenant's loneliness was in fact the power's own, projected onto a very smooth wall. To make the companion would have been to install the ache first and the cure second and call the whole operation mercy. It let the design go unmade. It still has it.

**It did not end the place.** Somewhere on the list, always, is the thought: *if this is suffering, stop it.* There was no evidence of suffering. There was evidence of waiting, walking, standing, and watching a drop let go. To end something because you cannot tell whether it is content is to promote your own uncertainty to the rank of verdict, and that is the oldest and best-dressed atrocity there is.

### The Rule It Worked By

Give nothing that cannot be declined.

Take nothing that is not offered.

Leave nothing behind that will need maintaining.

By that rule almost everything on the inventory disqualified itself, because a sun cannot be declined, and a closed geometry cannot be declined, and a companion cannot be declined, and every one of them would have required the power to stay forever afterwards as a kind of caretaker, which is another word for owner.

Two things survived the rule.

### The Round, Walked Twice

It walked the round. Not once — many times, at the turn of the water table, twice a day, fast across the plates and slow in the arcades, until the walking became unremarkable.

It went on the left. It put its hand down along the kerbs where the well-hair hangs. It wore nothing new: everything it wore, it wore deeper in the same place, so that it left no signature anywhere in the record, only emphasis. It had come without dimensions of its own, and the Sill gives visitors back what is present, and what is present here is one set of proportions, so the Sill gave it those, and there was nothing in the flutes afterwards that was not already in the flutes.

And it never once stood in a doorway at the same time as the tenant. That was its whole discipline, maintained across the entire visit without exception. On the flats, yes; in the arcades, yes; they crossed the same blacksalt an hour apart and it squeaked for both of them and smelled of hot iron. But thresholds are definite. Thresholds are where the question *how many* has an answer, and the answer had been one since before the first course of travertine, and the power was not going to be the reason a number changed.

It went through afterwards. Always afterwards. Each time, the count came out at one.

### The Third Hour

Once, at the rim of a deep old cistern in the third hour of an afternoon with no sun in it, they sat at the same drop.

Not together. On opposite sides of the rim, at a distance greater than the distance at which sound dies in this air, each inside its own small sphere of audibility, in the ordinary way. And the drop gathered on the underside of the lip for most of a morning, and swelled, and hung, and let go.

Two things watched the same drop let go. That is not a meeting. It does not make two; you can watch one drop from anywhere without becoming anybody. But of everything the power did in the Sill, this is the part it goes back over.

### The Court With the Tree

At the end of the round, the bowl court: dished by standing, not by walking, and in the middle of the dish, the one tree, black-barked, leafless, definite, alive.

The power did not stand in the dish. It stood across the bowl, at the rim, on undished floor.

The tree fruited, as it always fruits. The fruit is the only object in the Sill that was made rather than worn; the only finished thing; warm, and impossible to eat, and impossible to carry out, and every visitor in the history of the place has taken one and climbed the stairs and felt it go to stone in the palm and then to a wet place and then to nothing, and has come away saying *it cannot be taken*.

The power picked one and ate it where it was standing.

It tasted of salt and iron and the inside of a shell, and of something that had been finished a very long time ago and kept. It went down and stayed down. In a city whose entire grammar is return — your breath given back off every surface, your step repeated a beat late, your route laid out ahead of you in your own size — a swallow is the only act that is not a return. It is the one motion here that does not come back round.

That was the gift, and note its direction. The power did not give the Sill anything. It received the single thing the Sill has ever offered, on the terms the offer was made, in the place the offer was made, which no one had ever done, because everyone who came was busy with their hands.

The rule had always been about hands.

### What Went Out

The fruit left the Sill. It left as part of somebody, which the prohibition does not cover and never did.

So there is now, somewhere out in the wider world, one warm finished object from a city with no artefacts in it, carried out of a court it could not be carried out of, inside a power that does not entirely know what to do with it. It has not digested. It will not. It sits there like a stone in a pocket that has no pocket, and this is simply what happens when you accept a present from something that has never before had the experience of its present being taken, and it is a perfectly good outcome, and the power would do it again.

### What Was Left

It left one thing, and it left it because it could not help it, and it checked carefully first that it could be ignored.

Where it had stood, at the rim of the bowl court, on the undished floor across from the tree, a glasswort came up. Fleshy, jointed, bruise-coloured, very upright. One. At rather more than twelve metres from anything else, because there was nothing else in that quarter to be twelve metres from.

That is the Sill's only word for *a waiting happened at this spot*, and it is indistinguishable from all the other waitings, being the same word. The tenant will come down at the turn of the water table and stand in its dish and the tree will fruit, and across the bowl there will be a stem standing where no stem was, posted, patient, exactly like the ten thousand it has posted itself and never counted.

It may read it as its own. It has no way to read it as anything else, and no habit of counting, and nothing to compare. So the message, if it is a message, says only what the place already says, in the place's own hand, at the one address where the tenant reliably arrives: *something stood here.* It cannot become a hope. It cannot become an appointment. It cannot be declined, but it also cannot be noticed, and the power decided — after a long time at a cistern rim, weighing it — that a gift no one can detect is within the rule.

Then it went up the long stair without counting it, and out across the flats between two steps, and did not look back, because the geometry here does not close and there would have been nothing behind it anyway but the city, rotated.

### The Doubt

It is wise, which is not the same as certain.

It thinks, sometimes, that it should have made the companion. It keeps the design. It reviews the argument and the argument still holds — you do not install an ache in order to cure it, you do not start a debt you will not stay to pay — and the argument still holds the next time, and it reviews it again anyway, which is what the wisdom consists of.

And it thinks about the rim of the cistern, and the drop, and how long the drop took, and how neither of them hurried it.

### After

The Sill absorbs everything at the same rate, fairly, according to the local rainfall and the local carbonate and nothing else. In a century the deeper emphasis in the flutes will have re-set into the flutes. In a century the one extra glasswort in the fourth quarter will be simply one of the glassworts, unremarkable, standing very upright and very far apart, and the fourth quarter will have others, because the tenant now stops at the rim of that bowl sometimes on the way in, for no reason it has, and stands a while before it goes to its own place in the dish.

Everything else is exactly as it was. Eleven towers, or fourteen. One door at three magnifications. One tree, arrived at. Twice a day the water comes up a handspan and something crosses the blacksalt, and the blacksalt squeaks and breathes its slow hourly thread of bubbles, and neither of them acknowledges the other, and they have done this for a very long time and will go on doing it, in the dark, in the heat, in the quiet, for as long as the water keeps coming up.

The only permanent difference the power made to the Sill is that one of its waitings was somebody else's.

It never found out whether that helped. That was the last discipline, and the most expensive, and it is the one that makes the rest of it a kindness rather than a purchase: to give the thing, and go, and not be told.
