# The Attention

It arrived in the second week of the dry season, which is when the orchid flowers, and it arrived correctly, which is to say it did not arrive as light.

This should be said first because it is the whole of the thing's character in one decision. Every account of a power visiting a place has it come down as brightness, and brightness here would have been an assault. A resin torch held up in the vault makes the colony boil: forty thousand birds off the rock at once, the young shaken out of the cups, a rain of them onto a floor that is a mouth. To arrive as light in the Warm Mouth would have been to arrive as a massacre with good intentions. So it came the way things properly come here. It came as a warmth with no wingbeat behind it, as a pressure on antennae, as a smell that the beetles could not classify and therefore ignored, as a small change in the way the shaft answered a voice.

Nothing noticed. That was the first courtesy and it cost more than it looks like.

---

## What it did for the first year

Nothing. It held still on the lower northeast wall, one to a square metre, spread flat, and read the rock the way the whip spiders read it — which is the local method, and the local method is the only one that gets local information.

In that year it learned the following, in this order, because this is the order the place teaches:

That direction is warmth. That space is a room known to a hand. That the clock is the floor, running upward at a millimetre or two a year, and that the pale bands in it are droughts and the dark bands are wet centuries, and that this is the only history the mountain keeps.

That the rings of black pupal cases on the wall are a map of tenancy, and that the map drifts upward at almost exactly the rate of the floor, so that the bats have been rising all this time without ever deciding to, one handspan per human lifetime, keeping their distance from the fire beneath them.

That the collar at the rim — three storeys of moss, the one-cell-thick fern, the two hundred orchids, the fly that walks because it cannot fly, the spider that eats the fly, the snail you can read a fingertip through — is a garden kept alive by a stomach, and is exactly as wide as the breath that keeps it, and not one centimetre wider.

That the fall is not an accident in the system. The fall is the system's mouth.

A benevolent power that has not learned patience is indistinguishable from weather. It took the year.

---

## The gifts it did not give

It had the means. Set that down plainly or none of the rest counts.

**Sight.** It considered giving the floor back its eyes, and understood within a minute of real attention that this was not a gift but a bill. Eyes are expensive, eyes are a wound waiting for a place to happen, and there is nothing here that light would tell them that the rock does not already say better. The crickets are not deprived of vision. They are done with it. They put it down sixty thousand years ago and walked on, waving.

**Room for the garden.** It considered widening the collar — a little more warmth, a little more wet, eleven metres of green becoming forty. And then it followed the thought to the end, which is the only honest place to stop. A garden that lives on a favour lives on the favour's continuance. Favours end; powers get distracted; and the thing you have made cannot go back to being the thing that survived without you. It declined to make anything here dependent on being loved.

**The nestlings.** This was the hard one, and it should be recorded that the power stayed with it for a long time. A chick comes off the rim at thirty metres and does not die of the fall; it dies of the reception, in eleven minutes, and there is a wet patch and then in a day there is nothing. To catch them all — and it could have caught them all, gently, forever — would have been to starve ten million beetles, unbuild the crickets, take the whip spiders' hands out of the air, close the centipedes' road, and end the floor, and the floor is the furnace, and the furnace is the warmth, and the warmth is the collar and the crèche and the reason any of them are up here at all. Mercy at the resolution of one animal, applied at scale, is a demolition.

Pity is what you feel when you have understood a place at the resolution of an individual and stopped there. It did not stop there. It went on looking until pity became something else, which has no good name in any language spoken in the valley, and is closer to respect than to love, and is not colder.

**The snake.** It did not remove her. Forty birds a year out of forty thousand is not a wound, and she is the only lineage in the shaft that has become the shaft's own idea, and you do not edit that out because the birds would prefer it.

**Permanence.** It considered holding the place — freezing the millimetres, keeping the Mouth breathing its column into the violet air for as long as there is air. And it worked out the arithmetic instead: some tens of thousands of years from now, the floor reaches the roost. The tenancy drifts up past the last of the pegs, past the collar, and out. The garden goes with it, then the garden goes. The shaft fills and cools and becomes a plug of black stone with saxifrage on top, and the ridge takes one more minute to inventory. This is not a tragedy. It is the last page of something that has been written correctly for sixty thousand years, and a wise power does not tear the last page out of a book on the grounds that it liked the middle.

**The valley.** It considered reaching down three days into the valley and making the harvesters good — hardening the rule, putting a fear in them, standing at the rim. It set this aside quickest of all. You cannot make people good by hand; a rule kept for the wrong reason is already half broken, and a rule kept because something is watching breaks the instant the watching lapses.

What it may have done instead — and here the record is genuinely unclear, and the unclarity may be the point — is nothing more than a slight adjustment of worth. The high vacated cups, the old laminated ones, twenty years of lives in strata, are the valuable ones. The fresh occupied tier is worth less. So appetite and restraint point the same direction, and the grandmothers' rule does not need to be remembered as a rule, because it is simply how a sensible person harvests.

Was it always so? The power could not afterwards tell whether it had arranged this or found it arranged. It chose not to investigate. Good action leaves no signature, and looking for your own signature is how you start needing one.

## The being

She is a metre and three-quarters, root-coloured, and she is the whole of her species and has been since one gravid female fell in and could not get out. She hangs from the vault by four coils with a third of herself free in the flightpath, and she is not looking, she is feeling for the pressure of a wingbeat and the small warmth behind it, and she misses nine times in ten.

The power went to her because she is the one who cannot leave. Everything else in the Warm Mouth commutes — the swiftlets fall out of winter into summer every morning; the horseshoe bats drop two thousand metres and climb back for an hour and forty minutes because nothing in the forest is this safe; even the harvesters go down and take everything with them but the pegs. She has never been on the surface. She will die inside a volume of rock she could cross in ninety seconds.

She perceived it as a warmth with no wingbeat, and struck at it, and held nothing. This costs her nothing; it is nine times in ten. It stayed with her through one digestion, on the ledge her own generations have polished smooth with their bellies, and it did not speak to her, because there is nothing that could be said to her in any grammar she owns, and to install the grammar would have been to install a loneliness she does not currently have. Loneliness is not among her instruments. This was checked, carefully, before being ruled out.

It gave her one thing, in the end, and only because the thing was small enough not to distort anything.

She is old. Last year she missed for eleven months. On the evening it happened, the column was braiding — the birds coming home, the knife-winged bats falling upward on the warm draught — and a warmth passed within reach with a wingbeat behind it, and it was a fraction slower than it needed to be, by an amount no instrument in the shaft could measure and no bird noticed. She struck and it connected. She fed, and lay four days digesting, and did not hunt again.

One bird. The beetles went short by one bird, which they did not feel, because a nestling came off the rim the following morning as nestlings do.

That is the entire intervention. It is on purpose that it is that small.

## The sentence

There was a writer in the shaft with a notebook, going down by feel on the pegs their grandmothers set, keeping an inventory in the dark: *antennae longer than my forearm*, *whether this is restraint or just a rule so old that nobody remembers it as a rule, I could not tell you.*

The account breaks off in the sixth section, mid-clause. **None of the above is the** — and then nothing, because a hand got cold, or a torch was not lit, or a person put a pencil down and looked up at the oval of sky that has stopped meaning anything, and did not pick it up again.

The power finished the sentence. This was its last act here and the only one it took any pleasure in.

It finished it in the writer's own register, plain and flat, so that when the notebook was opened again the writer would read it and believe they had thought of it themselves, some time in the night, and would not remember writing it, and would not question that, because that is how the best sentences arrive:

*None of the above is the largest inhabitant. The largest inhabitant is the warmth. It is not a condition of the place; it is an animal, or something on the order of ten to the twenty-fourth of them, each no bigger than a word, doing one slow chemical thing in the dark at forty degrees a handspan down. It has no eyes to lose and never had. It did not fall in and it does not commute. It is the only tenant here that has never been anywhere else. Everything above it — every cup of hardened saliva, every crèche of pink young stuck to hot stone, every orchid at the rim and the fly that walks to it — is standing on its exhalation, and so, for one year, was I.*

Then the attention went off downwind and dissolved, the way the column does, and the mountain closed over its absence within a week.
