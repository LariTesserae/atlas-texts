# What the Power Did

## The Attention

It arrived the way weather arrives in that country: from below, and late.

It had been moving through the world for a long time, attending to places, and it came up the gulf in no hurry, past the level where the clouds assemble themselves, past the grey braids of storm that the children are brought to watch, and up over the rim onto a floor of glassy granite a thousand paces across, under a sky the colour of a bruise going black at the top.

Its first act was to hold still.

This was not reverence. It was a practical decision made in the first hour, when it tried to find the edges of the thing it had come to look at and could not. It had expected people, and there were people, four hundred of them, shouting. But the town was not in the grove, it was *of* the grove — slabs bridged between living trunks, walls of dead lattice glued with sap and the powdered dead, the whole settlement growing out of the columns the way the black bead-creeper grows, mats, obscures, and does not kill. And the columns were drinking the mountain, and the shearers were opening the columns, and the people were tapping what the shearers could not reach and drawing off what the shearers could, and the bats were eating the moths and the rock was holding the bats and booming like a guitar body, and every one of them, in the end, was seated in the black flour at the root of something that would carry them back up as water in four hundred years.

It could not locate a creature to help. It had come to a single being with ten thousand woody mouths and four hundred shouting ones, a being that drank itself, buried itself in its own root, and remembered itself out loud because it had never invented writing. So it held still, in shade, on the far side of a trunk, which is what everything wise up there does at midday, and it stayed that way for three hundred years.

It learned manners in that time. It learned not to step in the beams — those white coins of hard sunlight with edges like cut paper, that the whole traffic of the town flows around. It learned not to look at anyone straight, because straight staring is for enemies and for the gulf. It learned that four days of the drone is madness and the fifth day is silence. It learned to tell, across a street, in bronze dimness, from the dullness of a person's wax, that someone was grieving, and to notice that somebody always came.

Three hundred years of doing nothing was the first thing it did, and of everything it did, that was the largest.

## What It Set Aside First

Before it considered any good it might do, it decided it would never be known.

The reasoning was short. Within one generation, tapping rights would be argued in its name. Within two, someone would speak for it, and be believed, and be a tapper's cousin. The council here does not make decisions; it slowly notices them, over generations, in silence, and a council that can ask a power what it wants will stop noticing and start asking. Every good it could do openly would be paid for by the town losing the slow instrument it has used to govern itself since before the mountain came down.

So: anonymity, absolute, permanent. It has never reopened the question. Everything that follows was done from behind a trunk.
