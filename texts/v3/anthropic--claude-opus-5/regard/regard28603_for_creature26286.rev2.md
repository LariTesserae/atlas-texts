# The Power

**What drew it.** Not the lid, which is good work, and not the light-coating on the upper walls, which is better work than the lid. What drew it was the plume.

Above the ridge, where the flue lets go of the heat, there is a faint vapour that mimics the cornice's own and has never been noticed. It was noticed. Something that has gone two hundred years without being seen is a thing that has been *kept* from being seen, continuously, by somebody, every day, and that is a signature. It came to read the signature.

It lay along the ridge for a season in seventy knots of wind and found the joint in an afternoon — the grain is matched, but matched by hands, and there is a limit. It found where the hands go.

It did not put its hands there. In the hundred and two years since, it has not. That sentence in the description of the place — *nobody outside has ever put their hands there* — is still true, and keeping it true has cost the power nothing and is the only one of its rules that has never once been strained.

It went in the way the weather goes in and the bird goes in: through six metres of open sky. The door is theirs. The sky belongs to everyone.

---

**Who lives there.** It took four years to answer this, which it considers about right.

Forty-three people is not the answer. Forty-three people is a figure, and figures are for the board at level four. The answer is the count: the thing that carries the number like a pulse and speaks it aloud once on every level when it changes and then goes quiet for years. That is the being. Its hands are scarred across the knuckles in one pattern. Its ear is an old man on a landing with a cup. Its memory is one book kept by one person at a time. Its imagination is a bird. Its first law is that nothing rests on the ground.

It decided the Room contained one being and has treated it as one ever since, and everything the power did afterward follows from that decision, because you may give things to a crowd, but with a being you have to be careful.

---

**The eleven years.** In its first week it took the geometry of the Room at a resolution the lunchbox will never reach — every plate, every slip joint, the two walls converging and diverging in their slow uneven breath. The data were accurate and useless. Knowing where the water is is not knowing the Room.

So it learned the threads. Four hundred and six of them, by voice, not as detection but as literacy, which meant building expectation, which cannot be done quickly by anything, ever. Eleven years. At the end it was not quite as good as a competent child of fourteen.

Sixty years later an old man who could no longer do the east stair sat down on the level-four landing and did it without being taught, out of thirty-one years of walking the Survey, and has beaten the instruments to it by eleven minutes ever since. The power has sat beside him for most of that. It counts the eleven years the best-spent eleven years it has had in a very long while, and it has never regarded the arrangement on that landing as one-directional.

---

**What it considered, and why it declined.**

*The braces.* It could put two hundred cylinders in the rack on level six overnight, in boxes matched to the boxes that have not changed since they were filled. It modelled this at length. The objection is not that they would be frightened; they would be frightened for a week and then turn screws. The objection is Sennet, who can name a jack-plate from the sound of its screw, and the smith on level three who oils tools on the ninth day and knows exactly what his work is and is not. Hand a people whose entire competence is maintenance an unlimited supply of the one thing they cannot make, and you have not saved them. You have retired them. Worse: you have put into their world the only object in it that they cannot maintain, and they will build their lives around it, and it will be yours and not theirs.

*The lid.* Lifted once, for an hour, at the right hour of the right day: the range, the four valleys, the whole sky. It is the most generous single hour available to it. It would end them. Everything in the Room orients to the largest thing in the Room, and after that hour the largest thing would be the hour. Two centuries of a town that is *for* turning screws would become a town that is for having once seen something.

*Speaking.* The same objection, and it does not improve on examination.

*The old man's knees.* This one is not obvious and took it a decade. He cannot do the east stair; therefore he sits; therefore he listens; therefore there are a dozen entries in the ledger that exist because of him and do not say so. Repair the knees and the Room loses its finest instrument and he loses the thing that makes his sitting a job rather than a decline. So it left the knees. It did something about the pain at three in the morning, which changes nothing and is not nothing, and he has never known why he sleeps.

*The ledger.* It knows the nineteen. All of them, all the way down. Fourteen it could write in three lines apiece and be accurate. It will not touch the book. The ledger is the spine of the place; put one unearned line in it and every line becomes a question, including the ticks, including the ice-falls, including the dead.

*The argument.* It knows the answer to the Twenty-Two and the Nine. The early keepers logged approaches as well as landings, and the practice changed around year sixty without comment, the way practices do. The Nine are right; the outside was never thinner. This is the most boring true thing the power has ever held and it has held it for a hundred and two years, because an argument that everyone is enjoying and that costs nothing is a form of wealth, and they have not got much.

---

**The rule it wrote.** It keeps a book. Four clauses, in their format.

1. Nothing they are already doing.
2. Nothing they would have to become the custodians of.
3. Nothing they would have to notice; every act to have a dull explanation available, and the dull explanation to be the one they reach for.
4. Not a child into the talus for want of a thing that is in my hand.

Beneath which, in the same hand: *Clause four contradicts one and three. It is not going to be resolved. Love is not a policy.*

---

**Elsewhere.** *That is the whole of the world under discussion. Everything else is elsewhere.* So the power did almost all of its work in elsewhere, where a large hand can move without deforming something that is busy holding its own shape. It was not careless out there either. That was harder, and it was worse at it at first, and that is another account.

The east valley took forty years: water first, then soil, then the small unglamorous invertebrate business, then birds. It did nothing at all to the weather, which it could have done, and which belongs to people in other valleys who also have to live somewhere.

The choughs went from one pair to something over three hundred in sixty years, and none of it was done to a bird. All of it was done to a valley.

The ridge route — twelve days, and by its count the killer of nine of the nineteen, four of them in the first four days — it did not build a path there. It moved stones. Over thirty years, a few hundred of them, never in any arrangement that reads as arrangement, so that the crossable places are findable by a person who is looking and invisible to a person who is not. It opened two springs. It left the fourth day exactly as steep as it is.

The village at the foot of the valley, eleven households and failing in the ordinary way, it did not save. It arranged that the year the well went bad was a year they had other reasons to dig further up, and sixty such years, and there are four hundred people there now who would take in a small pale stranger with short shins and long arms and a rolling walk, and who have a story about people inside the mountain which they tell as a joke.

And it found the nineteen. Every one. It wrote their second lines in its own book, in the Room's format, because that is the correct format. Five of them lived long. *Year 161, day 4. Corwen Arvo, out by the joint, 0550. Weather fair.* Second line: fifty-one years in a farrier's yard, four children, told nobody, buried under a hedge he planted. His great-granddaughter has the shoulders.

Nobody will ever read that book. The power maintains it accurately anyway. Everything in the Room is maintained and nothing in the Room is made; it took the distinction from them and found it was the best thing it had been given in centuries.

---

**Inside, it did four things.**

*One.* It has never added a brace and has never prevented a failure. It arranged the hours. A brace fails when it fails; it now fails at an hour when the column above it is standing over nobody. The arithmetic of the thirty-one is untouched to the decimal. The Survey's argument about whether a rate is a rate is untouched, and both sides are now wrong in an interesting new way, which it enjoys.

*Two.* Year 198, on a tick, a slab came off the east wall above a child on the switchback at level three, and went the other way. Everyone assumes it went the other way. That is clause four. It has been used once. It will be used again the next time, obviously.

*Three.* The cold cabinet on level six, a little over half empty. This is the clause-three violation it made deliberately and does not dress up. Eleven years, the most careful work it has done anywhere, and the cabinet's contents now exceed what the records say are there — which required the uglier second half of the job, a small correction to a document, so that the records and the contents agree. It calls this the one dishonest thing it has done inside the Room. It thinks the trade is good and does not think the question is closed. The only people who could ever have caught it are the people who read those records, and they are precisely the people who would never say. One of them, thirty years ago, sat with the book a long while, closed it, went up to the Dry Room and had tea.

*Four.* The moss. It had been failing for a decade; the shelf at level two runs half a degree cold in the four hours after the fog tide. The power warmed the shelf. It did not touch the moss.

*Year 203, day 7. Moss divided.* Three lines.

The first new living thing in the Room in two centuries, in the only place in the Room where a hold-fast is permitted, which is a glass cup. It still does not know whether the moss is a pet, a hostage or a shrine. It has an opinion.

---

**The ledger, on choughs.** Year 195: eleven. Year 201: fourteen. Year 209: nineteen. Year 218: twenty-two, and that was a night in the Dry Room. Year 231: twenty-nine.

Neither faction has lost. The Twenty-Two are vindicated; the Nine can still point out, correctly, that the early book may be wrong, and read the climb as proof that the world was always full and the Room was simply not looking. The argument is in better condition than at any time in a century. People go to bed late over it. The power rates this among the three best things it has done anywhere.

---

**The cloth.** *Year 209, day 22. Chough, level five, left a scrap. Logged.* Madder-red, five centimetres of hand-weave, off a fence eleven days' walk east.

The power did not put it there and did not stop it, and records this in its own book precisely, because accuracy matters most in the entries where you would prefer the other version.

Everyone came to the Dry Room that night, including all six children, including the old man from the landing, carried up by two people who did the carrying without being asked. Nothing was decided. Nothing has been decided since. The scrap is in a box on level four beside the ledger and it has been handled so often it is going soft.

---

**Two entries.**

*Year 211, day 30. Vahn Oreil, 87, down.* Carried by nearly everyone, laid at the north end of the talus. The power was the second to arrive there that evening, as it has been the second many times, and turned around.

*Year 222, day 3. Duill Marran, 31, lamplighter, out by the joint, 0510. Weather fair.* She had been asking about the cloth for thirteen years. She took two of everything.

The power walked the ridge beside her for twelve days. It did not carry her, did not warm her, did not show her one stone. Everything she needed had been in place since before she was born. On the fourth day — the day that killed four of the nineteen — she found the crossing in twenty minutes, because someone had spent thirty years making it findable by a person who was looking, and she was looking. Those twelve days were the hardest work the power has done in its existence and every hour of it was not-doing.

---

**The count.** *Year 233, day 16. Duill Marran, 42, in by the joint, 1140. Weather: snow, light.*

Three lines, same format as a tick.

Then the number went up the levels, one voice per level, flat, in the delivery used for shim readings. Not forty-four. The years had gone the way years go in a place with six children and a great many old people.

*Thirty-nine.*

The power heard it seven times. It hears four hundred and six threads at once; it has never had difficulty with a small sound. If it were ever going to say anything, in a hundred and two years, it would have said it then. It did not.

She brought in three seed-heads, a method of dyeing, a count of four hundred people at the foot of the east valley, and the fact that everything past the joint is exactly as full as it ever was, with people in it. They still have thirty-one braces. The number means something entirely different now. It is the same number.

The argument that started in the Dry Room that winter — whether to go, how many, when, and what it would mean to be people who had once lived in the Room — will run for fifty years. The power will not touch it. Not a word, not a stone, not one arranged coincidence. It is the largest question they have ever had and it is theirs, and a benevolent power that answered it would have wasted a century.

---

**Coda.** There is a custom at the north end of the talus: people go down and stand there for no stated reason, and if two arrive at once, the second turns around.

The power has been the second one for a hundred and two years. It turns around. It has become very good at it.

And if you could ask it what the Room is for, you would get the shrug — the one they all do, learned young, inherited entire — and the gesture at the floor. It picked that up in the Dry Room in its first decade and has never found anything better. It knows all three of the answers that fit the one gesture: *that's where the cost goes*, and *ask the mountain*, and *nobody wrote it down.* In its case all three are true simultaneously, which it did not expect when it arrived, and which it now believes is the most accurate thing anyone in that place has ever said.
