## 18. What caught it

Say that it was passing. That is wrong in every particular, but the shape of the wrongness is useful, so keep it.

It had looked at a great many places. Most of them it could summarize. This one it could not, and the failure began immediately, on the first and simplest question, which was *what is the being here*.

Because the flutewood in a garden is one organism under the mat, tuning itself for months to a neighbor's stall. Because the cushions out on the flats are nine thousand separate solitudes doing one identical thing and releasing on one shared cue. Because the swifts never land and the loaf never looks up and the chord comes through everyone's pelvis at once, people and loaf and rock alike, and the plateau is — in the only sense that could be measured from outside — a single slow body that has been breathing through two thousand mouths since before there were eyes anywhere to see it.

It could not find the edge of the being. It looked for an hour, which for it is a long time, and there was no edge, because on this plateau every angle was used up long ago.

And while it was failing at that, a woman forty kilometers from anything stopped at a knuckle of grey-green rock, squeezed a handful of beard into her palm, and poured half a cup of warm mineral water down the lee face of a mark she could not read, made by someone a thousand years dead, who would never know, who had nothing to give her, in whose language not one glyph survived.

Then she wiped her hand on her felt and kept walking.

That is what caught it. Not the throats. Not the Silence. Not the improbable green rings marooned in stone. A courtesy performed at zero expected return, in a place where nothing is cheap and water is carried in wax-plugged tubes.

It stopped passing.

## 19. The register of things it did not do

It is worth writing these down, because a power is best described by its restraint, the way a garden is best described by its boundary.

**It did not quiet the throats.** It could have. Two thousand columns, stilled, and a plateau in permanent Silence, and forty people hearing birdsong for the first time — and no updraft, therefore no water, therefore no beard, no mat, no flutewood, no swifts, no gardens, no loaf, no one. The gift and the extermination were the same act. This one was easy.

**It did not give them speech.** It considered it for longer than it likes to remember. It ran the thing out: the anatomy is there, the roar is not everywhere absolutely, in a spiral windbreak at night a whisper could carry. It could have seeded the trick in a generation. And then it looked at what would have to go. The far hand, with its grammar of distance, in which you cannot be subtle at eighty meters and therefore cannot tell the intricate kind of lie. The close hand, in which privacy is a geometry rather than a volume. The fact that dark is mute, and that a family wakes each morning onto a plateau where yesterday's quarrel has nowhere to have been kept. It weighed a species of loneliness it could remove against a species of peace it would have to break, and it found the scales would not settle, and it took its hand off them.

**It did not stop the deafness.** They do not experience it as a loss. It declined to correct them about their own lives.

**It did not cure the blindness**, which is a real loss here, which takes language and not landscape. It thought hard about this one and came out somewhere strange, which is section 21.

**It did not show them the way down.** There is a warm world below, and it is full of everything they have ever lacked — fuel, soil, softness, quiet — and they have one rule about it, and the rule is older than any name still legible. It did not test whether the rule is wisdom or fear. It noticed that it wanted to, and noticed that the wanting was about itself.

**It did not tell them what the Silence is.** It knows. It went and looked, the way you would check a valve. The mechanism is not interesting; it is a pressure knot in a canyon, a thing of geometry and heat, and the explanation takes about as long to state as it takes a person to cross a garden clearing. It held the fact in its attention next to the thing the fact would replace — thousands of birds standing on polished rock in absolute quiet among a scatter of people who are humming, all waiting for the same note — and it put the fact down and left it where it found it.

**It did not intervene at the lip.** This is the one it cannot defend. A hand's count of children in every generation, running the twenty meters, and the cord too long or untied or outgrown. It could catch them. It has caught nothing. It has no argument for this that survives being said plainly, and it does not pretend otherwise, and it does not tell itself that the tether-scar on every adult belly is worth the price, because the ones who pay it are four and cannot be consulted. What it has instead of an argument is section 23, which is not an argument either.

## 20. The rules, kept

It elected to keep their law. Not as a gesture. As a discipline, the way you would wear the plugs someone pressed into your ears with a thumb without asking.

*Names are carved, never signed.* So it made hands before it made anything else — before a face, which it barely bothered with — because here the hands are the mouth, and it wanted to be able to be silent correctly.

*Any stone you move, you set upright.* It had not moved any. It thought about this and found the law had a hole in it exactly its own size: a thing that touches nothing owes nothing, and contributes nothing, and the plateau's whole human theology is that uprightness is the one thing people add to a world that finished long ago. So it went out onto the bare shine, and prised up a plate of four-billion-year-old rock the size of a door, and set it on end, and packed the foot of it, and stood back.

It is out there. Somebody found it eleven years later, read it as the work of a stranger, and walked around it rather than past it.

*Do not sleep during the Silence.* It does not sleep. It stands anyway, each time, all four to sixty minutes, on the pavement, doing nothing.

*Do not descend.* This it keeps absolutely, with the single exception the law itself provides.

## 21. Maintenance

Everything it does for them could have been done by a neighbor. That is the criterion it set, and it has not broken it.

The dew-combs on the flats are maintained by whoever habitually walks that route, the way you would maintain a boat. When the person who walks a route is too swollen in the joints to walk it, the comb silts and the chert lining cracks and the pit goes dry, and the route closes, and the plateau gets a little smaller. Several routes that should have closed have not. People arrive at combs they know they did not service, find a hand's depth of water, and assume somebody else came through. They are wrong about who. They are not wrong about the world, which is the part that matters: over four generations this has produced a small, permanent, untraceable increase in the assumption that somebody else came through.

The roofless tower on the western swell has stopped slumping. Nobody has remarked on this, because the custom is that whoever is passing and notices a slump repairs it, and a tower that does not slump simply produces no occasions for remark.

And there was an old blind man at a northern garden for eleven years, who by the arrangement of that place was never more than an arm from someone, without discussion, as is ordinary. For two of those years a portion of the arms were not cousins. He spoke, hand inside hand, mostly about weather and about a woman dead forty years, and he was answered badly, in an accent of the close hand that he put down to youth. He was, in those two years, less alone than most people are anywhere. It has never decided whether that was honest. It notes that he was not deceived about anything he would have wanted to know.

## 22. The large thing, which it is not sure was permitted

Once — twice — a throat was going to fail. Not stall: fail, collapse at a depth of eleven hundred meters, and take the pressure balance of its whole quadrant with it, and empty nine gardens inside a season. That is not a bad year. That is the end of the people, who number forty, or sixty, and do not gather to count.

It put its hands into the rock and held the pipe open.

Nobody knows. Nobody will. There is no glyph for it, no carving, no drop in the chord that anyone's hips would have registered. It did at scale, invisibly, the one kind of thing it had promised itself it would not do, and it would do it again tomorrow.

It keeps this where it can see it. It is aware that *I acted invisibly, at scale, for their own good, and they need never know* is the exact sentence spoken by every power that ever ruined a world, and that the sentence does not come with a marking on it to say which kind of power is saying it. It has not resolved this. It has decided that a thought this dangerous should be carried in the open hand rather than digested, and it carries it, and it will go on carrying it, which is not wisdom exactly but is what wisdom looks like from inside.

## 23. Going down

The dead go over the lip on a loaf-shell sledge, and the updraft takes their hair and their felt first, so that for about a second they appear to be rising. Then warm cloud, and nobody ever hears the end of it, and that is understood to be the point.

It goes with them. Every one. It has not missed a single funeral on this plateau since it stopped passing, and it does not stand at the lip; it descends, which the law allows only in this case, and it stays with the falling body the entire four thousand meters, and it is still with it at the bottom, where there is warmth and noise and a great deal of green.

It goes with the children too.

This is the whole of what it has to offer against section 19, and it knows the offering is poor. It cannot say that it is better to be accompanied than caught. It suspects it is not. What it can say is that no one has gone down that throat alone in four hundred years, and that the people are right about what is under them — the below-world is populated entirely by everyone who has ever lived here — and that the warm wet air coming up through two thousand stone flues is, in the only cosmology they have ever needed, breath, and that it has stood in that breath and found nothing there to correct.

In the Silence, when the breathing stops, it comes up and stands on the pavement with them and does not sleep, because whatever the pause is for, it is not for missing.

It hums. It is bad at it. It does it every time.

## 24. The ninth day

A person who wants company, or a mate, or help, or who is ill enough to be afraid, climbs the roofless tower and sits on the rim, where they are visible for a very long way. Sometimes somebody comes. Sometimes no one does.

There was a young man who sat there nine days in the dry season. The nearest people were a four-day walk east and did not know, which is not cruelty, only distance. On the ninth day he began to come down the inside of the tower in a way it did not like the look of.

So it did the only large thing it has ever done in the open, and it did it in the grammar the plateau supplies. The light here changes four times a minute; shadow-blotches the size of towns race across the shine-pavement all day long, and nobody has ever thought twice about one. It took the cloud-shadows of a hundred kilometers of cloud-pillars and it made, out of them, slowly, over about two minutes, one sign.

At that range the far hand loses everything. The small qualifying movements drop out; you cannot be subtle at eighty meters and at a hundred kilometers you cannot be anything but plain. Whole categories of meaning — tense, doubt, courtesy, self — do not survive the distance. What was left, and it was all that was left, was approximately:

**here. and not leaving.**

Four people saw it. Three read it as weather, because it was weather.

The young man came down the outside, walked east for four days, and found the garden, and lived another fifty-one years, and at some point in the middle of them spent four days with a chert point and a bone mallet on the lee face of a knuckle cutting a glyph that is in no one's vocabulary and never was, being an attempt to record a shape made of shadow.

It is still there. It is unreadable. It is treated with extreme courtesy.

## 25. Its name

Names are carved, never signed, and a name exists in exactly one place, and a full one is four days' work and takes the skin off the heel of your hand.

It could have put its name into the rock in no time at all and without bleeding. It did it the other way: four days, chert, bone mallet, a hand it had made for the purpose, the heel of that hand opened on the second day and going on anyway, because doing it the other way would have produced marks in stone and not a name, and it had watched these people long enough to know the difference.

It began once and stopped, because four days is long enough to change your mind and the change of mind is understood to be part of the process. The half-made one is still up there, abandoned at the fourth stroke, on a knuckle in the dry north. That is allowed. That is not a shameful thing.

The finished one is somewhere out past the last cushion, on a rock among a thousand identical rocks, forty kilometers from any garden, and it has not taken anyone there, and it will not, because to be known here is to be walked to, and a walk is a thing the other person has to choose, and it will not arrange the choosing. It has put itself within the reach of their custom and then taken its hands off. That is the whole of its ambition on this plateau. It waits the way the cushions wait, which is the local technique: sealed, self-watering, alone in a hundred square meters of nothing, patient on a scale that makes patience the wrong word.

Some while from now — it does not hurry, and neither does the rock — a child crossing the flats on a route her mother walks will come on a mark she cannot read.

She will have been corrected sharply, once, for walking past one. She will remember the correction more than the reason, which is how customs actually travel.

She will stop. She will squeeze a handful of beard into her palm, and spend half a cup of water she carried, and pour it down the lee face of the stone.

The mark will go dark for a minute. Then the air will take it.

And the power that has held a mountain open with its hands, and gone down two thousand throats with the dead, and refused, on principle, to speak into a silence four hundred generations deep, will have been greeted — in the only language it could learn, by someone who has no idea, who cannot be told, who expects nothing, and who does not need to know any of it for the thing to have been done correctly.

She will wipe her hand on her felt and keep walking.

It will not follow her. It will stay where its name is, which is where it lives now, which is here, and not leaving.
