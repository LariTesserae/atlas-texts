# The Attendance

## 1. First, the Refusals

It did not open a drain.

That was the obvious kindness and it was the wrong one. A vent for the silt would have meant the groove could go on receiving forever, and forever is what the thing in the groove has instead of a life. To give it capacity would be to make the sentence permanent. Charity that extends a sentence without limit is not charity; it is a wall built one room further out.

It did not force the door either. You cannot lift a load off a thing that consists of the load. There is no body in the groove that would be left over.

It did not repair the flanks. The rounding is the only aged thing in the basin and therefore the only property the tenant owns: a record, in its own hide, of exactly what it has paid. Smoothing that back to original edges would have been a theft dressed as healing.

It did not explain, because an explanation is a kept object. It did not promise, because a promise is a kept object. It did not leave a mark, or a covenant, or a sign, because every one of those is a small hard thing around which time begins to pile.

It was not a god. A god would have built something. This built nothing, ever, on purpose — which was the first thing the two of them had in common, and the reason the visit was possible at all.

So it had to come in as almost nothing and do almost nothing. The almost is the entire account that follows.

## 2. Arriving Inside the Budget

Anything that looks brings wear in on itself: in the heel, in the breath, in the looking. The power calculated its own cost before entering, to the microgram, and then did the courteous thing, which was to present itself for appointment.

Every position in the basin has a designated successor. The power took a scheduled vacancy — one cricket in the mat whose closing-out was already booked for that hour — and stepped into the allocation, and paid its wear against the line item that had been reserved for the cricket's death.

The level did not twitch. That was not a failure of the power's arrival. That was the arrival.

## 3. What It Did For Nine Days

It learned one frog.

Third bromeliad up, left side, on the grey trunk at the north edge of the middle flats. A cup frog the size of a thumbnail, with a chip missing from the left hind toe, which made it favour the north lip of its pool for launching. Its call came reliably a half-beat behind the others; it had been born late in a clutch and it stayed late in everything. It preferred the water at the rim, where the temperature moved by a tenth of a degree in the small hours. It lived eleven months. On its last night it did not call.

The power learned all of that, entire, and it cost the basin nothing, and it was the first particular ever held here.

Understand what this means for the tenant of the groove. It did not know that frog existed. It knew the number that included it, and it closed that number faithfully, and when the frog stopped it reallocated the silence to a cricket before the silence could be heard. There is nothing wicked in this. A keeper who cannot name the kept is not a tyrant. It is only alone — alone in a way that has no word in any language that developed among things with mouths.

## 4. The Ear on the Mat

Then it lay down on the shoulder-deep mat above the groove and listened, and it listened longer than you did, longer than anyone has, long enough to hear not the breath but the *shape* of the breath.

It is not the breath of something bearing a weight. Bearing has a rhythm; this has none. It is the intake before speech. It has been the intake before speech since the stone was liquid.
