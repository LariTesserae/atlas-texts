# Coming In Heavy

## The first eleven years

It came over the rim on the cold slide of the night air, at the fourth hour of dark, with the fog, because that was the least disturbance available. Then it did nothing whatsoever for eleven years.

This was not indecision. It was arithmetic. A cushion plant here puts on the thickness of a coin in a year; the mountain puts on a thumbnail; the city puts on a metre in three or four generations and calls that a new town. A power that arrives at its own tempo in a place running at that one will break something before it has learned the word for it. So it sat among the cushions on a ridge above the Sixth Sill and let two coins' thickness pass under it, and in that time it learned the shape of the year, the schedule of the fog, the difference between the note of a full dome and a fouled one, and the fact that the pale green lichen is the slowest thing alive on the continent and should not be sat on.

The first thing it learned that mattered was not the language. It was the *soft ask* — the whistle pitched just under the marmots' alarm interval, which a colony will sit up for but not bolt from. It practised for a season, four ridges away, out of earshot of everything but the wind, because it had understood the single most important sentence in the whole ethnography of the plateau: *a stranger's badly-pitched whistle empties a hillside, and the whole city knows within a minute that someone unskilled has arrived.*

Everything it later did or refused to do followed from that sentence. There is a way to enter a place that costs the place nothing, and it is not the fast way, and it begins with holding your note under the alarm.

## What it considered and did not do

**Rain.** It could have given them rain. It thought about this for a long time and then it thought about gorges. Rain means runoff, runoff means incision, incision means the rim is cut through in four thousand years and the closed basins open and drain and the salt goes out of them and the pans dry and the combs stand on the ridges harvesting fog that nobody needs, above a country of ordinary farmers who are indistinguishable from farmers anywhere. To give them water from the sky is to take away the water they have. It let it alone.

**Metal.** It thought for a while about metal, and then it thought about bells, and stopped.

**A road down to the wet country.** They already have one. It is bad on purpose.

**The nitrogen formula.** The old division of the bird-fouling is off by about a thirtieth in favour of the upper blocks, an error inherited from a survey of the Seventh Sill and preserved because its arithmetic has become a proverb. The power checked it in an afternoon and said nothing, because the lower blocks had noticed this themselves some sixty years ago and are winning, slowly, by argument, which is how a thing becomes theirs.

**The blind.** There was a woman in the eleventh block who had been sounding domes for thirty-one years and who had lost the centre of her sight to the glare of a pan she could no longer see. The power could have opened it in a night. It did not, because she had built a life and a reputation and a fee in dried marmot on the ear she had instead, and to walk in and hand her back her eyes would be to make itself the largest fact in what remained of her life. She had not asked. Nobody had asked. Nobody knew there was anyone to ask.

**Children.** This is the one it considered longest — two hundred and six years, which is the longest it has ever considered anything.
