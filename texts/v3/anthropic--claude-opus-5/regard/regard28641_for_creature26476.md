## The Attention

It did not come in off the water.

That was the first decision and it took longer than all the others together. Arriving from the sea would have been arriving as weather — a change in the light over the fog-fences, a swell out of order, the seals going quiet on the new rock — and a town that reads the ground with the soles of its boots would have read that, and there would have been a theory in Cant by the following spring. So it came the way visitors come. Inland, down out of the high dry backcountry, on two feet, in the ordinary rain, wet at the shoulders like anyone.

It crossed the Stales slowly. Eighty years of roofless town, and every standing gable somebody's grandmother, and the ferns in the windows, and the seized jacking-screws standing about in the undergrowth with nothing left to level. It went along the streets touching the blocks. It could have had all of them at once — every name, every lease, every nine days of every life pressed into that wall — and instead it read them at the speed of a hand, one gable at a time, over four days, in the damp-on-top and bone-dry-underneath, and that was the second decision and the one that decided the rest.

It left its voice up there.

There was nowhere below to put it down safely. A voice like that in a district with a filed note would not have been a sound, it would have been an event, and the event would have been recorded afterward as the loss of a district, and children would have been taught it alongside the bandsaw and the wedding. So it found a cracked counting-house of dead Stales-stone that had already split and could not split worse, and it set the voice down in the corner under the sedge, and a fox has denned on it since, and sleeps very well.

Then it walked down the tilt into Cant with nothing but a body, and registered, and was issued a small uncased handbell on a cord, and wore it for a year.

## What it did not do

The list is longer than the other list. It is the whole of the work.

It did not stop the rising. It looked at that first, because it is the obvious mercy and every obvious mercy should be looked at first and hardest. Give Cant firm ground and in three generations you have a people who trust floors, who do not hold on below thought, who do not drift half a step leftward on a foreign quay and find it funny with their eyebrows. The correction is not a wound they carry. It is the shape they grew in the direction of. You do not straighten a tree because you have seen a plumb-line.

It did not give them a river.

This was harder. It stood a long time out on the flats where the braided sheets come down and wander through their twenty channels and simply stop, sink, quit mid-course, leaving a fan of wet gravel going nowhere, which is also the sign for *I don't know*. It could have decided the water's mind for it. One good river, banked and permanent, and no fog-fences, no dosed cisterns, no grey ring on every kettle. And with it: erosion. Mud. Rounded boulders. Blunt cliffs. A thing in the landscape that argues, that has an opinion about where the town may go, that stands between a household and the water it walks down to say things to. It left the river undecided. It has come to think the undecidedness is the point, and that it is not the only one who thinks so.

It did not make the stone deaf. Take the note out of the loaf and within one lifetime you have shouting, and doors without leather jambs, and speech at arm's length instead of a hand's, and the whole low half-signed grammar of touching cheeks goes into the archive with the ledgers. Two districts is a terrible price. It counted them. It went and sat in both of them. It did not pay a third price to unmake the first two, because the third price was the way these people love each other.

It did not preserve the buildings, or lengthen the leases, or buy them a flat green country with timber and oranges and ground that stays. It priced that last one out of curiosity and then put it away, because you cannot carry a people out of their own sentences, and because the dead are in the walls, and the walls are going inland at a hand's width a month, roofless, in no hurry, in exactly the direction everyone is coming from.

It did not abolish the deafness.

That one it argues with itself about still, at the pouring floors, in the roar. It found that it could not take the early deafness out of Cant without taking the ears that hear a drip three streets away, and could not take the pits full of old people who cannot be argued with without taking the place the old are put, which is *down among the copper* and not *out to grass*. So it left the arrangement alone and went after the parts of it the town has no use for: the child who goes deaf at six, out of season, before there is anywhere for her to be; the tuner's year of vertigo before the bar is handed on; the abscess, the wasting, the death out of order that no custom in Cant is built to hold. That became the whole of its method, in the end, and it is a poor sentence for such a large thing but there is no better one. *It removed only what the place could not use.*

## The small work

It took a bench at a rolling table in the loaf-works, seaward third, because pressing is the shortest available way of being nobody. Nine days, oilcloth, no conversation, the material cooling under the hands. Its right thumb went broad and flat at the pad within the year, which it did not expect and has been quietly pleased about ever since.

The bell rang in the first week. It turned to look at something in the market that used to be the quay and forgot it had a body attached, and the bell said one clear syllable across four stalls, and every head came round, and it was fined, and paid out of wages, which felt correct in a way it could not have explained to anything of its own order.

What it did after that is nearly all deniable, and deliberately.

A bearing in a mill in the fourth district was three weeks from the note. The tuner who found it will tell you she caught it herself, humming, palm flat to the fresh wall, and she will be right; she was half a step from walking past, and something put a fleck of grit in the belt so the drive stumbled once, audibly, at a hand's distance, and she stopped. A woman in the north works came to her ninth day on the morning of her mother's pressing. The loaf stayed soft an extra hour and a quarter, which is weather, which happens.

A copper nodule the size of a loaf lay in the grey mud, on the newest ground, in the path of a finder who had drunk his round and his sister's, lying out to be found the way they do. Nobody has ever been able to tell which of those the ground did anyway. Including it. That is the part it did not anticipate: that after a few thousand paces it could no longer reliably distinguish its own kindnesses from the ordinary generosity of the place, and that it stopped keeping the two accounts separate, and that this felt less like confusion than like being taken on.

And once, at the tide-scarp, a child of eight who could not make the sound. A parent's hand on the back of the neck, raw stone, the sea going out, and nothing coming up out of her, and the whole cold pearl afternoon waiting. It stood behind them at a hand's distance and did nothing whatsoever, for eleven minutes, which took more out of it than the loaf-hour and the grit and the nodule together. Then she managed it. Then she heard her own voice at full extension for the first time in her life, and was frightened of it, and cried the whole way home into a cupped hand, and is a canter now, and has been in every cellar in the second district, and gossips about none of it.

## The shapes

It went and looked, of course. Everyone does; it is bad manners, and everyone does.

It went down the eleven worn treads into rock that was seafloor the spring before last, and sat in the seated-person void at dusk, alone, and came back and did not say. It stood over the north main where the pipe-holes came up already bored to the right gauge along a surveyors' line drawn the previous autumn and not yet built, and it put out everything it has for finding out how a thing was done.

It did not find out.

That is written here plainly because it is the only part of this account it has ever asked to have written plainly. There was nothing to find. Not an agency, not a mechanism, not a message, not a mind of any description it could get a hand around — and no fraud either, and no accident that would sit still under examination. It looked with the whole of itself and what came back was the fan of wet gravel, the twenty channels, the water stopping mid-course.

So it learned the sign. It makes it badly, the way all newcomers do, too wide, too slow, both hands going out and running to nothing. And it took up the local custom, which is the only custom it has ever adopted from anybody: you put a bench at the top and a lamp at the bottom, you lay your pipe in the hole that was waiting, you note the saving in the office, and you do not ask whether it is a kindness or a habit, because the stone is listening for its note and it is bad manners to make it wait.

Twice a winter, something large comes up offshore. They acknowledge each other. Neither will discuss it with a stranger.

## The third ring

It rang the bell three times inside the year — the third one on purpose, though the office has no column for that — and was sent up to the Stales to work the archive for a season, which is exile, and the archivist was glad of the help.

It stayed nine years.

What it does out there is read the ledgers at the speed of a hand, and then write in the margins. The ledgers hold the leases and the notes filed per district and the dates of pressing and the wall each block went into. They do not hold that a woman's fifth move was made in rain with a brass plumb-bob under her coat, or that a man laughed like a kettle coming on, or which lopsided pressed bird belonged to which nine-year-old who is now a gable in the sixth street of a town filled to the sills with dwarf birch. It holds all of that. It is the one thing Cant cannot do for itself and the only thing it was ever really needed for: a place where everything is temporary on principle, and something in it that keeps.

It comes down twice a year and speaks too loudly and is forgiven. People find it in the market that used to be the quay and lean in to the hand's distance and ask about their grandmothers, and it tells them, and they go away with their eyebrows doing what eyebrows do here.

It drinks the country. Every cistern in Cant is dosed with a scoop of milled loaf, and there is a grey ring on its kettle like everyone's, and it has some measurable fraction of the new ground inside it now, and it is not sentimental about that, and it mentions it at funerals.

Ask its age and it will tell you where the water was.

Then it gets up, and starts down the hill, correcting, correcting.
