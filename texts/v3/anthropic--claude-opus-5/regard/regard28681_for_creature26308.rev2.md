# What the power did

It came down, which is the only way.

It could have come otherwise — that is more or less the definition — but it used the throat, and the ferns, and the ladder, because how you enter a place is a statement about who you intend to be in it, and it intended to be a thing that had come down. At the bottom it stood in the green ring where the daylight goes to die and did the only sensible thing, which was to attend to the noise. Everything here is arranged around the noise. It understood within the first minute exactly what could be done about that, and it did not do it, and it went on not doing it for forty years, because forty years is four centimetres on the board and the board is the local unit of seriousness.

**Young power is loud power.** Split the sea, stop the sun, cure the leper in the street where everyone can see. That is a thing powers do early, before erosion, and it fills a chamber, and afterwards nothing in the chamber is ever about anything but the power again. Given ten thousand years a power wears itself a basin and lands in its own accumulated softness and speaks lower. This one was old. You could tell by how much of it was waiting.

---

**The things it declined to do, with reasons.**

*Quiet the fall.* Set a basin under the column, a hundred thousand years of digging performed overnight, and by morning the water would land soft and the roar would come down to a chuckle you could talk over. Consider what else comes down with it. The language dies inside a generation, because nobody signs at arm's scale from the shoulder when a mouth will do. The hierarchy turns over: the men at the top of the world, the ones worth twice a hearing hand, become old deaf men at the bottom of it, worth half, and grateful. You cannot hand a man a kindness that costs him his rank, his tongue and his competence, and go on calling the transaction benevolent because of how it sounded when you thought of it.

*Give the hearing back.* Same, worse. Promotion here is a medical event. Undo the medical event and you have undone every promotion in the works, and the linesman becomes a man who has spent his life walking in the dark with nothing to show for it.

And the power was aware that it was deciding this on their behalf, which is the oldest and most respectable way of doing harm. So it went looking for what they actually asked for, since asking them directly would have required showing itself, and showing itself was the one thing it had already ruled out. It listened to eighty-one years of wanting. They want dry time. They want the rash to stop. They want the fever not to come back. They want a name on the board in full instead of initials. They want, in a way none of them would say in sign because the sign is too big and everyone would see it, to be known about.

In eighty-one years, nobody had once asked for silence. The power took that as the answer, on the grounds that it was the only honest answer available.

*Dry the air.* Kill the seeps, the beaded walls, the crabs in the wet, the liverwort skin, the blind catfish standing with their whiskers spread like a man reading, the lamp gardens at their few centimetres a year — in order to clear a rash at three places on forty-one bodies. The arithmetic was refused. But the rendered grease that smells frankly awful and works was permitted to work rather better than its chemistry strictly allows, and no one has investigated, because it already worked, and because people do not investigate their own remedies.

*Light the dark between the bulbs.* Eighty-odd metres of unlit grating is the only private room in six kilometres. Flood it and you have helpfully removed the last door in the house. So: not only did it not light them, it does not look into them. Going blind at each gap is not a limitation of its sight. It is a decision, renewed at some seventy-odd intervals, out and back, for four decades, and it is the largest single quantity of restraint anywhere in this account.

*Take them out of the hole.* Pension them onto the plateau, where they burn in forty minutes, misjudge every distance, and find the staring at mouths obscene. Move a lamp and the garden dies and a new one starts in the new place at a few centimetres a year, and that is the fastest botany the Bell has ever seen, and the power let that stand as the record.

*Hand up a chamber.* This was the tempting one. Drop the table a metre in one side-passage overnight; the board-keeper, sixty-one years old and four hundred readings deep, gets the great event of a working life and her name in full. The power got as far as considering where to put the water. Then it looked again at the column of dates in eleven hands, four of them hers, and recognised it for what it is: the only true thing in the Bell that is longer than a life. Eighty-one years of people declining to lie about a centimetre. A kindness that falsifies a record is not a kindness. It is a forgery with a good motive. It would not be the twelfth hand.

---

**The things it did, all of which look like luck.**

The fever came up again in bedding from three valleys east in the eleventh year after the fever year, and again in the nineteenth, and again in the thirty-first, and did not take. There is no record anywhere of a plague that did not happen. The works people believe they were lucky and also that the bathhouse rules are sound. They are wrong about the bathhouse rules. They are permitted to go on being wrong, because being wrong about that costs them nothing, and being right about it would cost them the belief that they can look after themselves, which is most of what they have.

A grating plate whose four bolts had gone to lace took a man's full weight on a Tuesday and held. He replaced it that week, swearing about plateau iron. He is slightly wrong about the world in a manner that will never hurt him.

It does not hold every plate. Men still go off the grating; the long roll of rapid strokes still runs the length of six hundred metres of iron and arrives clean and bright through the roar, and everything stops, and the linesman starts walking. The power has never been satisfied with this and does not expect to be, and it does not keep a count of the ones it did not hold, because a count would be a way of forgiving itself.

What it does instead is go down with them. Into the black water between the blocks, where there is no lamp and no one coming for nine minutes, it takes the forearm and signs in *grip*, close and tactile, the dialect for things meant in earnest — the man's own call-sign, three slow and two quick, or five and five, or whatever small pattern of taps means *you are on the list, you are not a feature of the floor, there is something down here that knows your name.* He assumes it is one of the works. He is not wrong in any way that matters.

At the base of the shaft, one improbable strangler seedling tries and fails every year. In one particular year it did not fail. There is now a tree by the foot of the ladder; men hang their boots on it; nobody alive remembers the shaft without it. That is the only visible thing the power has ever done in the Bell and it looks exactly like botany.

---

**The large thing was done elsewhere.**

They speak about the end of the world here the way other people speak about a grandchild's prospects. The fall will dig its basin and go quiet, the drapes will grow, the shaft will widen and let itself down, and in some unimaginable afternoon there will be a green valley with a stream and a road, and the four million birds will have to be somewhere else. They grieve this. It is a bereavement scheduled to happen to strangers, and they grieve it anyway, at meals, in signs big enough for the next lamp-sphere to read.

Three hundred kilometres east, in another young limestone country that has not yet decided where its water is going, there is a shallow bruise in the rock. The power leaned on it. A few centuries early, that is all — nothing that would show. When the Bell quiets, the birds will have somewhere to be.

No one will ever connect the two. Everyone who grieved will be a hundred thousand years dead, their board rotted, their names not even initials. It was done anyway. That is what it means to be able to think in the units of the chalkboard.

---

There is a tin shrine at the foot of the ladder with a candle stub in it that no one lights, because the air will not hold a flame steady. The power can hold a flame steady. It has considered this about once a decade, the way you consider a door you have decided not to open, and it does not, because a lit candle would be an answer, and an answer would make it the first fact of the place. Everything would arrange itself around it as everything arranges itself around the noise. Forty-one people who currently believe they are the only quick things here would become forty-one people waiting to be spoken to. The stub stays unlit. The shrine receives this correctly. It was always a shrine to precisely the sort of power that does not light candles.

It permitted itself one lapse. When the linesman died — laid in the cut on the lower slope, the proper words signed over him, gone in nineteen days, the half-stroke retired out of manners — the works were shut down some years later for the annual, and the iron cooled, and the line gave out three strokes and a glancing fourth that nobody else has ever been able to produce cleanly. The board-keeper felt it through the rail under her hand. She decided it was the cooling iron. She is a serious woman and did not put it on the board.

It had been forty years. It allowed itself the one.

---

Mostly what it does is learn the language: the whole arm-scale grammar of it, the taps, the close dialect of the hands in the dark it will not look into. It is fluent in a tongue spoken by forty-one people, and will still be fluent in it when nobody is, because somebody ought to be able to read them.

Every month the board-keeper goes to the second bench with a slate and a bent brass rule and chalks the level under eighty-one years of previous readings. Something is present at the reading. It takes no measurement of its own, and signs nothing, and adds no name, and afterwards goes back out along the line, going dark at the gaps.
