## The Attention

### 1. The first restraint

A power arriving here in glory would destroy the place inside a season.

This is the first thing it understood, hanging above the Rim, and it is not a small piece of understanding. The forest metabolises change. A miracle is a very large change. Come down in light and thunder and the canopy will simply *eat* it — will take the whole blazing gift in through its wafers, fatten on it, exceed the allowance the six have spent four centuries holding it to, thicken over the strokes, blur the characters, and finish the sentence. Kindness delivered at speed, in that basin, is indistinguishable from an execution.

So it came in at the rate of the place. It took eleven years to bring its attention fully across the Rim, which is the time it takes one borrower to walk one stroke of the figure, and it chose that rate deliberately, the way you lower your voice going into a room where somebody is asleep or dying and you are not yet sure which.

### 2. Reading

Everyone who has stood on the Rim has said the same thing: it is notation, and nobody has read it.

It is a name. It is one name, written once, at three scales, in the vocative — and not the vocative of calling someone in from the far field. The other one. The one you use with your mouth six inches from a face, in the first second after a fall, when you say the name not to summon the person but to keep them from leaving while you say it.

That was the easy reading. The one nobody could have made from inside a body is the reading of the ruleset under the seam, and it goes like this:

It is not elegant. It is not a machine. It is crude, it is over-torqued, it is asymmetric in ways that have been quietly costing everything for four hundred years. The seam is not a join, it is a suture, and it was put in by someone working without training, in the dark, at terrific speed. The Chatter is not an exhaust port in any designed sense. It is where the pressure was vented because there was nowhere else to vent it, and it has been screaming into the shale ever since because nobody ever came back to fit a proper valve.

The Held Basin is not a monument and it is not a cathedral. It is a tourniquet. It was applied by a pair of frightened hands and it has never once been loosened, and the ambulance has been four hundred years on the road.

### 3. The bill nobody agreed to

The first act was the least visible and the largest.

The storms on the lee side of the Rim have always been worse than the terrain deserves. That is the externality — the weather budget taken out of the basin has to be spent somewhere, and it has been spent, for four centuries, on people who never consented, never benefited, and never knew there was anything to consent to. They only have the saying, which is older than the survey.

The power did not calm the storms. It assumed them. It arranged that the pressure vents into its own account instead of into that country, and now it pays, and will go on paying, and the lee side is ordinary.

Over three generations the saying falls out of use, because it stops being true, and children who repeat it are corrected by their mothers. That is the entire observable result of the first and greatest mercy performed in that region since the basin surfaced: an old proverb about the wind quietly going wrong.

### 4. The decimal

Then the audit, which is dull, and which is the real work.

Whoever built the apparatus set the borrowers' allowance to drain rather than to cycle. It is a fractional error. It is exactly the error you make when your hands are shaking and the person in front of you has about a second left. Four hundred years of a rounding mistake, paid out of six pockets of duration — which is why the second expedition counted seven and every count since has counted six, and why there is a clock, and why nobody who has been down there wants there to be one.

The power corrected the fraction. It took no drama at all. It is, by a very long way, the most substantial thing anyone has done for the six since the wire went into that foreleg.

Note that it *repaired* the work rather than replacing it. It could have rebuilt the whole apparatus, clean, efficient, humming, a thing of terrible beauty. It took care not to. The tourniquet is somebody's; you do not throw away the thing a person made with their hands in the worst second of their life and hand them a better one.

### 5. The door

But a clock removed is not automatically a gift. That clock was the only promise of an ending anyone in the basin had. Take it away and you have not lifted a burden, you have made it eternal — you have sentenced six exhausted animals to keep a vigil that can no longer, ever, run out.

So, having removed the deadline, it had to supply a door.

At the junction where two of them meet, it left a way to finish. Not a mechanism you would photograph. Simply: the sentence can now be allowed to end, at any time, by any of the six, if that is what they conclude — and it will hold, and it will be clean, and nothing will be broken by it.

And then the part that makes this wisdom rather than merely kindness: it withdrew from the outcome entirely. It did not stay to make the case. It left no preference anywhere in that basin, no weight on either pan, no lingering warmth on the side of ending or the side of holding on. For a power of that size this took more out of it than the storms did. It is very hard to be present somewhere without being an argument.

### 6. Twelve metres

Once a decade, two of them arrive at the same place and stand nose to nose for nine days, spending allowance they cannot replace, and say nothing, because sound does not go anywhere here.

At that junction the power left a pocket of air, about twelve metres across, that will carry a voice. It pays for that too, out of itself, permanently. It did not check first whether any of the six still has a voice, or still remembers what one was for. That was not its business.

Next decade, two of them will come to that junction, as they have forty times before, and this time the air will work.

The power did not stay for it.

### 7. The testimony

Then it went to the far side of the held second. Not into it. To the other edge — the moment that was taken out, the one that has not been allowed to arrive.

And it left an account there.

Everything that was spent, itemised. Four centuries of weather that did not blow. Erosion that did not happen. A sun held forty degrees above the eastern rim for longer than the outer country has had its current borders. Six pockets of duration and what they were used on: four hundred metres a day, eleven years to a stroke, the margin kept exactly where the margin belongs. Forty meetings of nine days each. The ring in the shoulder. The wire in the foreleg, badly set, set anyway. Every visitor who ever stood on that patch of grit and apologised out loud to nobody without knowing why.

So that if the moment is ever released, whoever comes out of it comes out into the knowledge of what was done, and how long, and by whom — because the one who did it will be out of duration by then and will not have breath left for a single word of it, and because nobody should have to arrive in the next second of their life believing they were left there.

The power knows what is in that next moment. It looked once, from outside, and that is the only thing it carried out of the basin, and it carried it sealed.

Benevolence is not the same as good news.

### 8. What it declined

It did not open the second.

It did not tell us, or the six, who is in it.

It did not settle whether this is a reliquary or a cell. It found the question malformed. A tourniquet is neither a bandage nor a handcuff; it is the thing a terrified person's hands did, and it is not owed a category.

It did not remove the ring.

It did not reset the foreleg. It asked. Asking, at that rate, took nine years. The answer took eleven. The answer was no, and the reason is not ours.

It did not improve the handwriting — and it can write; it could have laid that name across the basin floor in a hand legible from orbit, in perfect strokes, with every diacritic true. It declined. You do not correct someone's penmanship when the penmanship is the entire remaining content of what they have to say.

And it told no visitor anything, ever.

### 9. Afterward

From the Rim it all reads the same. The sun fixed. The air standing like a solid. Ten or twelve metres of sound and then nothing. The corridor of bent stems still opening ahead of whatever line you decide on, still prepaid, still four hundred years old, still not possible to surprise.

Two differences. One of them is weather, and nobody will ever connect it.

The other is on your shelf.

The leaf-wafer has not changed. It is not fading, not curling, not proceeding; your children will dust it and so will theirs. But the power did one last small piece of arrangement on its way out of the seam. When the sentence is finally allowed to finish — this decade, or in nine thousand years, by whatever the six decide between themselves in twelve metres of workable air, and it will be the six who decide — every wafer ever carried out of that basin will, in the same instant, everywhere, curl, and darken, and become an ordinary dead leaf.

And in houses all across the outer country, people will look up from what they were doing.

That is all of it. The bill paid. The error corrected. The door unlocked and then left strictly alone. The voice given back. The account kept for someone who may never read it. And a whole country arranged so that when it ends, it does not end unwitnessed.

It is not a rescue and it was never going to be a resurrection.

But whoever happens to be walking in the groves at that moment will find something no one has found there in four hundred years: that the stems ahead of them are not bent. That the path was not made room for. That the way is unprepared, and open, and entirely theirs to choose, and to choose badly.

That was the gift. It could not have been given any earlier.
