# What the Power Did

## The descent

It came down the stair.

It did not have to. It could have been present in the chamber without traversing the forty meters of poured concrete or the cut rock below it, in the way that weather is present without arriving from anywhere. But there is a thing you cannot learn about a place except by taking its own approach to it in its own order, and the power was wise in the specific way that means it knew what it did not yet know.

So it went down at the pace a body goes down. At the third landing it put out a hand toward the wall, and stopped, because the sign at the head of the stair says not to, and the sign is correct. It took a salt tablet from the cooler. It drank the water, which was the same water that goes up the riser to the spa and into the glass bottles with the church on the label, and which had been in the rock for eleven thousand years.

At the second landing its glasses went blind, and it left them that way, and walked the rest by the sodium.

It stayed eighty-four minutes. Not ninety-one. The first rule is not a limitation on power; it is a true statement about wet-bulb temperature and the human heart, and a power that would not honor a true statement in a small room is not the kind under discussion.

## What it found that was not in the book

The shift book has four thousand entries and most of them say *level normal, screens cleared*, and that is an accurate summary in the way that *survived* is an accurate summary of a life.

The power took the longer version.

It counted the film. Not the colonies — the cells: every sulfur-oxidizing bacterium in every sagging pendant on every dome, layered by oxygen tolerance, forty thousand years deep in the business of dissolving a cathedral upward out of solid rock. It counted the nematodes threading the mucus and the rotifers and the mites the size of a period and the protozoa grazing the bacterial lawn. It counted them individually. There is no practical use for this. It is simply the case that some things have never been counted, and that being counted is one of the forms attention takes when it does not intend to do anything else.

It learned the names of the twenty-two bacterial species in the wall paste, none of which has a name in any human language, and gave them none, because a name given from outside and never used is a kind of litter.

It read the 1954 paper on the blind beetle, photocopied and gone soft as cloth inside the switchgear cabinet door, and it read the hydrologist's name, and then it read the hydrologist — the whole man, forty-one years of him, ending in this room. It read the man who died of the spores in 1978, whose death is the fourth rule and is not on any sign. It found that both of them were, at the time of their deaths, thinking about the water.

It stood under the crèche patch, four meters across at the hottest point of the largest dome, and did not go up to it.

Then it stood against the wall for the exodus, because you do not walk the catwalk during exodus, and it took the forty minutes in the sternum along with the three, and afterward all three of them independently thought that it had been the best one they could remember, and none of them said so.

## The six things it did not do

**It did not cool the room.** Thirty-nine degrees is not the room's affliction. It is the room's engine, its pantry, and the reason the bent-wings choose this dome out of every dome in the hill. A mercy that lowers the temperature by two degrees empties the ceiling in a decade and the floor in twelve years and leaves a clean dry hole where an animal used to be. The power weighed this and set it down.

**It did not neutralize the acid.** The pH under one is the hole in your shirt and it is also the primary production of the entire chamber — the root of the food web, driven into stone. Sweetening the water would starve everything that eats, kill the landlord, and stop the room's growth mid-word. The domes overhead are the shape of an ongoing sentence. The power declined to punctuate it.

**It did not relieve the fig.** One individual, seeded in 2003, sending root cable thirty meters down into an atmosphere that dissolves it, pruned back faster than it grows, marked against a nail by people who love it. The power looked closely and established that the fig is not suffering; it is *working*. It has arranged its whole existence around a siege it will neither win nor abandon, and it is, by every measure available, thriving at exactly the rate that is possible for it. To lift the acid off the root tips would be to end the only argument the fig has ever had. It left the nail where it was.

**It did not spare the fish.** Twice a shift the rake comes up silver and the bucket goes onto the guano and is gone in under an hour, and the thousands hanging at the surface film with their upper lips breaking it are not in crisis; that is the ordinary posture of the species, pressed against the ceiling of their world. The power did not decide that this was a small pain. It declined to subtract it, because there is no way to lift it out that leaves the rest of the room standing, and because the fish were not asking, and because a benevolent power that edits the postures of the creatures it loves into more comfortable ones ends up alone in a room of its own furniture.

**It did not make the place known.** It considered it seriously — the priest of 1927, the last look in 1961, the endemic beetle in three chambers of one hill and nowhere else on Earth, the fact that nobody has been back to see. There is a version of protection that arrives as a designation and a walkway and a lit sign and forty thousand pairs of lungs a year, and the room does not survive it. So the power took the smaller path, which is described below.

**It did not fix the knock in the discharge line.** The knock has been in the maintenance notes since 2011 and it is not a fault anymore; it is an organ. The oldest man can tell from its pitch whether the water table has moved, and has been right more often than the gauge. To repair it would be to remove an eye from the only creature in the chamber that keeps a written record. The power let the knock alone.

## The five things it did

**One.** In an office four hundred kilometers away, in a schedule of subsurface designations that nobody reads, a boundary was drawn slightly wider than the draftsman intended, and a clause was worded in the passive voice in such a way that in 2034, when a proposal is made to widen the adit for a visitor centre with a glass floor, the paperwork will simply and unremarkably not permit it, and no one will fight about it, and no one will know that anything was defended. This is the power's rule, learned long before this room and confirmed in it: *help at the range at which a thing can be helped without being handled.*

**Two.** A retired invertebrate zoologist in another country, sorting a dead colleague's reprints, will find the 1954 paper and be unable to stop thinking about it, and will write one letter to one person, and the beetle will be looked at again, carefully, by two people over eleven days, who will publish in a journal with four hundred subscribers and tell no one else. The species will be, from then on, on a list. Lists are the humblest form of remembrance and they work.

**Three.** On a Thursday in September the young one's timer will go at eighty minutes and he will not hear it, and the fan will change pitch at that exact moment for no reason, and he will look up, and see the time, and go up the stair. He will be forty-eight before he understands that he has done nothing all his life but be lucky, and he will be wrong about that, and it will not matter.

**Four.** On a certain morning in November, on the stair between the second and third landing, the oldest man will stop for breath and the young one will stop with him, and the old man will say *listen to it* about the knock, and will spend nineteen minutes explaining a thing he has never explained, and the young one will get it. That is the largest thing the power did, and it required nothing but two people on the same stair in the same weather in the same mood, which is exactly the size of intervention that most of the world's continuity is made of.

**Five.** It sweated. Eighty-four minutes on the catwalk and on the guano crust at the room's temperature, and what came off it went down into the floor, and inside the hour the mites and the dermestid larvae and one blind three-millimeter chestnut beetle with no eyes at all had it, and it was carbon in the room, and the room's carbon becomes cricket and centipede and gas again and comes up out of the raise at dusk over the town. There was no other way to speak to the thing. It has no ear and no center and nothing that could be addressed; the only language it reads is food. So the power said what it had come to say in the only available grammar, which was: *here, take this, I was here.* And the being who lives in the Warm Level received the message in full, digested it, and went on doing exactly what it was doing, which is the correct and only possible reply.

## The coin

The new one has started leaving a coin on the flange beside the plastic saint, which nobody asked him to do and nobody has told him not to.

The power took the coin.

Not the metal — the metal is still there, going green under a growing crust of gypsum, and in ninety years it will be indistinguishable from the pump body, which is indistinguishable now from the rock. It took the other thing, the part he meant, and it is not necessary to say what became of it except that it was not wasted and did not go unnoticed and was, in fact, the reason for the visit; that a thing this old and this blind and this enormous had produced, at one damp edge of itself, a twenty-six-year-old primate who felt that something here was owed thanks, and who did not know to whom, and paid anyway.

It let the maintenance man at the spa keep his lie about the jar behind the towels. *It came from the sea.* The power examined the statement and found it substantially true. The sea is only water that has not been underground yet.

## Departure

It went up by the stair, in the slow warm column of the room's own breath, past the fig roots and the coin of daylight and the geckos, and out into the evening, and it left no mark, no sign, no signature, no cooler air, no easier work, nothing that could be pointed at, nothing that would show in a photograph, nothing at all except the five small displacements listed above and one complete and permanent record of every life in a teaspoon of the wall.

It signed nothing in the book. Her hand, small and square, had already got it right.

*Level normal. Screens cleared.*
