## The Attention

*A third document. It belongs after the ground and after the tenants, and it should be read at the pace of the place, which is slowly, and preferably lying down.*

---

### 1. What it did first

It did nothing, for eleven years.

This is the whole of the method and everything else follows from it. It arrived — came up through cold and into warmth, like anything else that arrives here — and it found the flatness and the four thousand domes per hundred paces and the knee-deep fog at dusk, and it understood within an hour that it was standing in a finished thing, and that a finished thing can be broken by help more easily than by weather.

So it lay down. It put its ribs on the stone like everything else does and it let the last heat of a vanished range come up into it, and it counted nights, and it did not touch anything.

Eleven years is not a probation. It is roughly the length of time required, at this latitude and this thinness, to watch one wall's worth of lichen thicken by a measurable amount, and it was the smallest unit of evidence available.

### 2. The arithmetic of the large mercies

It could do all of them. That was never the question. The question was what each one costs when you follow it all the way down, and it followed each one all the way down, which is what wisdom is for.

**Put the heat back.** The obvious kindness: re-fire the granite root, cancel the three hundred thousand years, keep the plateau warm indefinitely. It worked this out first because it was the easiest to want. The result: a new source of heat is a gradient, and a gradient is wind with a body in it, and wind with a body lifts a knee-deep fog off the ground and carries it away over the edge of the world. Everything on the Roof would be dead in nine years, of thirst, in a warmer place. The heat is not the plateau's life. The heat is only the slow difference between the rock and the air, and a difference cannot be increased from one side.

**Bring water.** Rain, a spring, a lake in the largest well. But every mouth here is sized to a hat of standing water, and the crust that feeds everything grows two millimetres in a human lifetime and does not compete, has never once had to compete, has no equipment for it. Standing water in quantity means imports, and imports win. And the grazers would drown without objecting, in the same way and for the same reason that they do not move when stepped on. A gift is a kind of stepping.

**Give them a future.** Restart the engine: variation, mutation, novelty, somewhere to go. This one it examined for a long time, because it is the kindness that flatters the giver most. What it found: the engine of a future is grief. To introduce variation into a converged system is to manufacture, for the first time in four million years, animals that are *wrong* — a hundred thousand generations of things born ill-fitted to a place that has no slack in it anywhere. And at the end of all that dying there is still the failure of the fog, on schedule, and nowhere arrived at. It declined to buy three hundred thousand years of suffering with the currency of a hope it could not honour.

**Take them somewhere better.** There is nothing here that is only itself. A grazer is a dome plus a granite floor plus a night's fog; take it to a wet lowland and you are holding a shell with a small mammal in it that handed back its thermostat before there were mountains here to lose, and cannot be given it again. And there is no way to ask. The only language on the Roof is pressure, and pressure has no future tense. You cannot lean a hypothetical.

**Be known.** It tried this once, briefly, for one animal, and it kept the receipt. The grazer leaned toward it all night. It did not comb the fog. By morning it was down nine grams of water, which is not fatal, and which is also eleven days of margin in a place where the margin is all there is. The power did the sum and understood something it had suspected since the second year: that awe is a luxury of wet lowlands. It has not revealed itself since. It considers this the most expensive lesson it has ever been taught by anything four hundred kilograms and blind.

### 3. What was left

After the large mercies were priced, what remained was a very short list of things that are free, and it has been doing them ever since.

The rule it gave itself: **touch only the mineral.** Stone has no stake, cannot be insulted, cannot be made dependent, and does not have to adjust. **Add only surface area.** Never subtract, never smooth, never straighten. **Leave no gradient.** Work at the speed the weather would have worked, so that nothing anywhere has to notice.

By this rule it is permitted, essentially, to make grooves.

### 4. The grooving

One third of the domes are stone. Most of those are dead animals whose shells outlasted them by four centuries and went on weathering, and a dead dome catches dew exactly as well as a living one and drinks none of it. The water it gathers runs down into the grit and into the hairline gap and into whatever is underneath, and how much it gathers depends entirely on its flutes.

So the power flutes them. North faces, where the water lingers. It works the channels at the rate that ten million years of dew would have worked them, which comes to something like two hundred domes in a year, and it has never once been caught, because there is no difference between its grooves and the other kind; that is the specification.

It also clears drifted dust out of the gaps at the bases of stones, which takes a moment and is worth about a thimble a night to whatever is beneath.

### 5. The walls

It did not build the first walls. It found them — knee-high, dry-laid, four kilometres of open spiral set exactly crosswise to the night wind, with the lichen thick and black for a hand's breadth on either side — and it understood immediately that it had found the only argument anyone on the Warm Roof had ever made.

It has never established what made them. It has decided that this is not its business.

What it did was join. There are now considerably more walls than there were, and nobody will ever be able to say which are which, because it copied the joints. The originals are careless but not random, and so are the new ones, careless in the identical manner, out of a courtesy so precise that it constitutes the entire content of the power's benevolence: it will not be distinguishable from the one who was already trying.

The gain, totalled across all the grooving and all the walls, is measurable. The night fog now forms about eight minutes earlier than it otherwise would, and at the far end, when the differential is failing, the last dew will hold on for roughly nine thousand years longer than the geology alone would have allowed.

Nine thousand years is nothing on the slow cycle. In the local arithmetic it is about three million additional nights, and the night is the only unit of anything here.

### 6. The hut

In one of the corbelled huts there is a patch on the inner wall, a metre square, rubbed pale by the pressure of something soft, over and over, for centuries. No pattern. Just the patience.

The power went there in its fourteenth year. It did not wait to meet whatever comes. It did not repair the patch, or improve it, or leave anything in the basin, or make the doorjamb easier.

It took the opposite wall.

There are now two patches, facing, at the same height. Neither is a message. Neither has a pattern; it was careful about that,
