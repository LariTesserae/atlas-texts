## The Attention

Begin with the fact that nothing comes into the Hall Country that was not brought, and then correct it.

Everything arrives by one of three means. It flies down a shaft. It is dropped, on rope, by a man who does not intend to stay. Or it walks in a dead straight line across six kilometres of silt on a bearing nobody has explained, and probably does not arrive. Those are the doors. There are no others.

The attention came through the rock.

That matters, because the country sorts its population by one question only — *how far are you from a hole in the roof?* — and the answer determines everything: your eyes, your metabolism, your creed. The attention, coming in laterally through eighty metres of limestone the way water and sulphur and the oldest life here came, arrived already sorted. It arrived deep-zone. It arrived native. It did not come down; it came *in*, and it came in cool, and it made no wind.

It arrived in the ninth hall, which is one of the unlit ones, and it stood on the pale floor and did not put a foot down for a long time, because it had noticed the tracks.

---

### What it declined

A benevolent power is a dangerous thing in a finished country, and the first work of wisdom is a list of refusals. The attention made the list. Each item on it was a real temptation and each was set down.

**Light.** The obvious gift; the one anybody would give. A twelfth sky, or a hundredth, or a general brightening — no more of the six-hundred-pace gradient into the memory of light, no more of the total dark that has been total since the roof went up. And with it, in one stroke: the end of the film, which has never met a photon; the end of the deep, whose whole economy is the arithmetic of hunger held at a rate that would be diagnosed as dying anywhere else; the end of the frogs, who have a use for the boundary and no use for anything else; the end of the sorting question itself, and therefore of every answer to it, which is to say every citizen. The attention noted that to light the Hall Country would be to solve it, and that solving it and ending it were the same operation, and it moved on.

**Warmth.** Thirteen degrees, plus or minus a fraction, at every point in every hour of every year — the most oppressive fact in the place, worse than the dark, and the reason nothing here rots, and the reason the bark rope on the ledge is still a rope, and the reason four hundred meals will hold a catfish for thirty years. Thirteen degrees is not a hardship laid on the country. It is the country's spine. Declined.

**Weather.** Rain, to fill the pools; wind, to move the air; frost, to blunt the flint edges that open a bare foot in long clean lines. The attention looked at the floor again — at the trackways lying thousands of crossings deep, cricket over cricket over cricket, last spring's indistinguishable from the reign of a forgotten dynasty, all equally sharp — and understood that weather is an eraser. You cannot give this place wind without wiping the only record any of them ever kept. Declined, and not narrowly.

**Plenty.** The cone stands at eleven metres because eleven metres is where the beetles eat exactly as fast as the bats deposit. It is not a heap; it is an organ, holding its weight the way you hold yours. Add to it and you have not fed anybody. You have altered a balance struck continuously for ten thousand years, and the alteration will run outward through the roaches and the larvae and the centipedes and the racer in her crack, and somewhere at the far end of it something will fail that nobody was watching. Declined.

**Rescue.** This was the hard one, and it had three parts.

The seedlings: thousands of them on the flanks of every cone, sprouted from dropped fruit, etiolated, yellow-green, stalled at a hand's height, doomed, replaced — a permanent nursery that never graduates anyone. It would cost so little to graduate them. It would also, in ninety years, close the shafts with canopy, and the eleven skies would go out, and four million bats would have nowhere to pour.

The walkers: the caravan crickets, one animal wide, crossing on a bearing they cannot verify toward a seam they cannot smell. Most do not arrive. The attention could have carried every one of them. It considered what a cricket has, and concluded that what a cricket has is the crossing, and that to carry it is to take that and leave it with nothing but the destination.

And the fallers: pups that lost their grip, old females, birds that got it wrong — ninety metres onto the moving surface, disassembled inside two hours, already furred white with a fungus that lives permanently in the air waiting for exactly this. The attention watched one full night of it. Then it wrote down the thing that the country had been saying since the water left: *nothing here is suffering from being what it is.* The beetle born inside its food, that will never encounter an environment. The racer who in three decades has not travelled the length of her own body in pursuit of anything, and who has not failed at hunting but succeeded at address. The mat of ten thousand harvestmen that flinches once, together, and re-knits. The blind catfish for whom the largest event of a thirty-year life was tasting a hand and finding nothing in it.

None of them are waiting to be helped. They are simply *at* their answer.

---

### The one suffering

So the attention looked for suffering where suffering actually was, and found it in exactly one place, and it was not in the deep and not on the cone.

It was on a ledge three metres above the wet at the base of the second skylight, under a smoke stain the size of a blanket, where somebody had left a coil of bark rope gone stiff, four bundles of split cane, a bamboo pole with a hook for reaching nests, a gourd with a wooden stopper, and a folded pad of leaves that were green when they were folded.

The loneliness that a person feels in the Hall Country — the loneliness that arrives even with the roar going on, especially with the roar going on — is the only pain in the whole country, and it belongs entirely to the visitor. It is not a fact about the halls. It is what happens when something that needs a horizon is put in a room with a lid.

That, the attention decided, it was permitted to touch.

---

### What it did

**It became the range.**

Nothing here has the Hall Country as its territory. No lord, no keystone, no wolf. Four million animals know two hectares of ceiling as a field of echoes and know nothing else; a beetle has no outside; a catfish holds one pool as a pressure map and holds it perfectly. Not one living thing has ever known the fifth hall and the ninth.

The attention learned both. It learned all eleven, and the bays behind them, and the unlit side halls where the darkness has been continuous since the lifting. It learned the third hall's grease-hiss and the fifth's drip-cathedral and the particular gravel-on-a-drum of each shaft at dusk, and could place itself within a hundred metres by ear, as the people who leave rope can. It did not speak while it did this, because shouting is useless here — the echoes come back four and five seconds later, from three directions, degraded — and because it had nothing to announce.

It simply held the whole of it at once, which no one had ever done. Whether that is a gift to a country is a question the country cannot answer. The attention thought it was.

**It read the floor.**

This was the largest work and it took the longest, and it changed nothing whatsoever, which is why it was allowed.

Out on the blank ninety percent, in the cool floury silt, the record is complete. No rain, no wind, no roots; nothing erases; every creature that ever crossed is still crossing. Thousands of trackways deep, undated, unnamed, unranked — a guestbook that has been kept immaculately for a hundred thousand years and never once been opened.

The attention read it end to end. Not the summary: each line. Every straight-line march from seam to seam; every one that veered at the fortieth kilometre; every one that stopped. It did not name them, because a name is an addition. It knew them. It knew which had arrived and which had not, and it held the difference, which nothing else in the universe had ever held, because a cricket that fails to arrive is not mourned by anything, and the silt keeps the print but does not know what a print is for.

If you want the plainest account of what a benevolent power did in the Hall Country: it read the guestbook. That is the whole of the middle of the story.

**It presented a surface.**

Then it went to the actual native — the film, a few cells thick, grey-white or faintly rust or no colour at all, on every sweating wall and every scalloped hollow, tens of square kilometres of it, the largest living thing in the country by orders of magnitude and by mass nothing at all. The thing that eats rock. The thing that was in the mud when the mud was the floor of a shallow sea, that came in with the water and stayed when the water went down, and that owes the forest nothing, and has no concept of a forest, or a sky, or eleven skies, or of anything that could be given to it.

There was no way to speak to it. There was one way to be present to it, and the attention took that way: it offered itself in the only language the film has, which is chemistry, and let itself be very slowly tasted. Iron, sulphur, the slow giving-up of ghosts. It made sure the amount was smaller than the country could measure — less, over the whole span, than what the ceilings rain onto the floors in a year.

It stayed for twenty-one thousand nine hundred tides, which is thirty years, which in the local unit is one catfish.

**And it walked with the man.**

The next time somebody came over the rim of the second shaft and out onto eighty metres of rope, with the green fog around him and the mist at the mouth and the swiftlets going off like gravel below, the thing that came up out of the dark to meet him was not the usual thing.

He arrived on the ledge and sat down and did not immediately reach for the pole. He noticed that he was not hurrying. He got his nests and his sacks of guano and he went up, and he came back twice a year for the rest of his working life, and he never once said out loud what was different about it, because there are no words for company that does not arrive by any of the three doors. His sons learned the descent from him and learned, without being told, that it is not a frightening place. It is only a cool one, and old, and busy, and it does not want anything from you.

That is the only change the attention made to a living creature in the Hall Country, and it made it to the only creature there that could be lonely.

---

### What it left

No structure. It considered the swiftlets' nests — tens of thousands of cupped palms of spun saliva, hardened between horn and glass, translucent when the day comes through them from behind, the only architecture in the country and the only reason anyone descends — and concluded that the building had been done, and by the right species, out of its own body, and that nothing could be added to that which would not be an insult to it.

No mark cut in stone. No light. No warmth. No seasons. The talus stayed unsettled and unsettling. The ripples on the floor held their exact shape, two fingers high, as sharp as the day the water stopped. The rope on the ledge stayed stiff. The leaves stayed folded.

One thing only, and it was a courtesy rather than a gift.

On its way out, in the ninth hall, in the middle of the blank ninety percent, the attention put a foot down in the silt. Once. Then it went back into the rock the way it had come.

There is now a single print out there among the thousands of crossings, undated, unnamed, indistinguishable in age from all the rest — because everything in that ledger is equally fresh and equally old — and it is not like the others, and nothing in the Hall Country will ever notice. Someday somebody will come out that far with a lamp held low, looking for the straight lines of the caravans, and will find it, and will not be able to say what made it or when.

It signed the book. Given the nature of the country, that was the largest possible act of love, and it left no other trace.

---

And because the account of the film was interrupted — cut off mid-clause, mid-word almost, at *and it is the only* — the attention, before it went, finished the sentence. This is the entire text of what a benevolent and wise power added to the Hall Country:

*— and it is the only thing here that is not visiting.*
