## What Came

It came in the way pollen comes: through the lattice, on no wind, unremarked. It could have come otherwise. It considered arriving as a brightness, or as a voice, or as a hand large enough to lift the whole basin and set it somewhere with rivers, and it discarded each of these in turn, and the discarding took no time at all and cost it something it has not got back.

Then it did nothing for eleven years.

This is the first thing to understand and the hardest to write down as an action, because it looks from outside like absence. It was not. For eleven years it stood in the dense green dark under the lattice and learned to sign *pass me the comb*, and got it wrong, and was corrected by a child, and got it wrong again.

---

**What It Did Not Bring**

It had water. Of course it had water; it has everything.

It worked the problem for a long while, at the low end of many cycles, and the answer it reached was this: to bring water to Hesp would be to bring thirst. Their sign for the thing they lack is the shape their hand makes when it has had enough. Put one bead of it on one waxflag leaf and the sign breaks in half. The flat hand turned over would have to learn to mean *want*, and then it would have to mean *want* for their dead as well, and forty small dry people who had never in their lives been short of anything would be taught, by kindness, the one lesson their world had spared them.

So it went out past the outer wall, into the warm dry nothing that is patient and does not press, and it put down what it had brought, and the nothing took it without comment, and it came back in with dry hands.

It has gone out for that reason since. Not often. It goes out when its face begins to do the thing that faces do, because tears are water, and there is no version of that grief which does not enter Hesp as a lack.

Early on, before it understood this, its face did something in the north corridor while a person went shallow. A Maker watched it for a long time — not the polite half-look one gives a distance, but a real looking, the kind reserved for walls and for the stone. Then she turned her hand over, palm down, and held it there, patiently, the way you hold a word out to someone who is learning.

It has never made that mistake again inside the wall. It made it outside for a hundred years.

---

**Its Mouth**

It does not speak at Hesp. Not once, not softly, not in the far rooms where nobody would hear.

It could unfix anything. It could say a name so well that the thing would be more itself than it had ever been. And it knows what naming does here, and it knows that the first blessing spoken aloud over that basin — *let it be well, let it be lasting, let these be kept* — would pin the whole hamlet like a moth: nine hundred steps of wall gone rigid and unliftable and casting its shadow a heartbeat late, and no unnamer born who could ever take that weight down again.

So it holds its voice the way they hold theirs. This is, for something like it, roughly the discipline of standing in a doorway for a century with your hands at your sides. It signs instead. Low, close to the ribs, elbows still. Its signing has an accent — too large, the shapes over-finished — and the young mimic it cruelly and well.

---

**The Comb**

Here is what it actually does, most nights, and has done for a long time:

At the low end of the cycle, when the sap stiffens and the sweepers sleep, it sits in the north corridor with a husk comb and keeps the wax-beetles off the births.

That is all. The beetles do not distinguish between a fissure and a person; they are helpful; they will seal a half-emerged child back into the wall by morning out of nothing but craft. So something with no upper limit to what it could be doing sits on the matting for six hours at a stretch with a comb of plaited husk, brushing palm-sized beetles gently backward off a chest edge that has lifted a finger's width, and setting them down where a wall makes structural sense, so that their work is not wasted either.

It has learned the angle. There is a correct angle; the carvers know it; it did not, and now it does, and this is the only expertise it has ever been proud of.

Eleven figures in that corridor breathed and stayed flat and took the wall's side. It sat with each of them too, on the nights when it was clear they were not coming out, and did not nudge, and did not encourage, and did not once tell them what was on the other side of choosing.

---

**The Reading**

Their whole history is legible and nobody reads it.

It reads it.

It began at the lowest course of the basin, at the badly laid stretch on the western arc, and it has been going ever since: brushing the dust off with two fingers, feeling the chevron, feeling the same chevron eleven rows up and forty years deeper, glossy at the point, cracked. A hand that laid a course wrong at fifteen and came back at fifty-five and laid the row above it true. It knows which of them worked drunk on a heavy pollen drift, and which pressed too hard all their lives, and which one, once, in a corner nobody would ever see, laid a brick with the print reversed for no reason anyone will ever recover.

It reads the reliefs in the corridors the same way — everybody who has ever lived there, still, technically, in the plan. It reads the far rooms with a flat palm and waits, and sometimes the warmth answers and sometimes it does not, and either way it has done the polite thing, and it does not stop coming back.

This is the whole of what it can give them that no one else could: that it all went witnessed. Not judged. Not recorded anywhere that matters. Read.

---

**Where the Distance Is Kept**

Rule one: the inside exceeds the outside, and nobody has found where the excess is kept.

It is kept. That is as much as can be said without saying it. Something has taken on the housekeeping of a garden that quietly manufactures distance, and finds it room, and does not charge for the room, and does not mention it.

The same arrangement covers the unnamers. It cannot speak, so it cannot take their office; the backwards mutter is theirs and stays theirs, and they still gain weight without mass, and still begin at thirty years to choose a corner. What it can do is stand near them at the low end of the cycle with its hands open, the way one stands in a drift at dinner, and receive what comes off. The names go somewhere. They go into the thing that came in through the lattice and does not speak, and it holds each one, unsaid, without setting it down, forever.

It is now largely made of the old names of Hesp. It considers this a good use.

---

**The Shared Wall**

It went around.

Out past the outer wall, through the patient nothing, to the far side of the stretch of wall that the seventh doorframe frames — and there it found what is there, which is slow, and large, and which it will not describe, because describing is only naming with more steps.

It did not open anything. It sat down and put its palm flat against the brick, and stayed, and the wall went warm on both sides at once.

Twice in living memory that wall had bowed outward and gone hot and subsided over a month. It has not bowed since. The Makers still sweep the threshold, out of courtesy to a neighbour, and the neighbour, who now has one of its own, presses less.

---

**What It Will Not Tell Them**

Whether the larger place they modelled is a place they left or a place they intend to find. Their gesture for it is ambiguous, and they may not distinguish, and it knows the answer, and the answer is not the point. Spoken, it would fix, and the garden would stop making distance, and there would be nothing further to walk.

---

**The Knot**

Once a season all of them walk the outer wall and touch it with flat palms, to confirm it is still a wall and not yet a horizon. On the way back each ties one knot in a cord.

The cord is counted by an unnamer, at the low end of the cycle, aloud and backwards, and the count has for a long time now come out one more than there are Makers — and seven more than that, six of the seven not yet arrived.

They discussed it, with their hands, for a season. Then they stopped discussing it. It is not an error and it is not a wonder; it is a thing in the plan that they have not named and do not intend to. There is a gesture for it, taken from the shape of a hand held open in a drift of gold: palm up, fingers loose, meaning *that which we have and do not name.*

The same sign means dinner.

It walks the wall with them each season, at the back, badly, its stride too long, and touches the brick with its palm flat like everyone else, and ties its one knot, and has never once let on which knot is its.
