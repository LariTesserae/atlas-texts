## The Guest, or the Twelfth, or Whatever It Is Called Where You Are Standing

**Arrival, which is the wrong word.**

A power that can be anywhere discovers, here, that it cannot *arrive*. Arrival requires a moment before which you were absent, and on this ridge the report precedes the event. The news of the visit had been in the stone for some while before the visit. By the time the power set anything down on the shelves, its coming was old news, weathered, going under bloom at the edges.

So it did not arrive. It was found to have come.

It came to rest a long way east, among the collapse-fans and the low green-coloured mounds that used to be roofs, and it did the only courteous thing available to a very large intelligence in a very small strange country, which is nothing, for a long time.

A hundred cycles, near enough. A walk of about four hours.

It reset no slab. It cleared no trough. It learned the angle of the grain, and then the second angle, his, the few degrees off-plumb that belong to the rising water; and then, later, it learned that there was going to have to be a third.

Every morning it watched the water leave the broken aqueduct and fall two thousand metres into nothing, on time, faultlessly, with the notches sharp.

Then it went down through the lower cloud-plain and looked at what was under there, and came back up, and did not mend the aqueduct. This will be discussed.

---

**What was considered.**

Everything. In brief, and with the reasons, because the reasons are the whole of the matter:

*To mend the aqueduct and deliver the water somewhere.* Set aside. The fault was never in the masonry. The masonry is the best work on any world the power has seen. The fault is that the system has no one at the end of it, and you cannot fix that with stone.

*To straighten the grain; to let his life run with the mountain's instead of against it; to give him one ordinary direction to be old in.* Set aside. He is not a man standing in banded rock. He is banded rock that has taken up a habit. You cannot take the layering out of a layered thing and have anything left over but water.

*To give him a body of the usual kind and a world with birds in it.* Set aside, with regret, and reconsidered twice. He is oriented to a down that is not the general down. Elsewhere he would not walk; he would lean, and then he would fall, and he would be falling in the direction of a place he no longer belonged to. Also, and more to the point: the combs.

*To end it kindly.* Considered longest of all, and honestly, and without the flinching that usually passes for reverence in these cases. Set aside — not because the answer was no, but because it was not the power's answer to give. Ending is a thing that may be offered and may not be done. An offer needs a listener. There was no listener. He had never in his existence been given the conditions under which such a question could be his own. The power concluded that the first work was not to answer the question but to make it askable, and that this might take a while, and that it had time, which in this country is also a distance.

*To fill the aisles with company: to teach the fans to fly, to seed the scoured air, to bring people.* Set aside. Three of the valley-heads have a second shelter in them, small, badly sited, the courses stepped against the grain, the roof-slab split and chinked. He keeps them swept. Nothing further will be said about them here, except that the power did not consider building a fourth, not for one moment, not once.

*To explain him to himself.* Set aside as an insult. Explanation is what you offer someone who has the wrong facts. He has all of them. He has had all of them for ten thousand years. He is not confused. He is unaccompanied, which is a different condition and has a different remedy.

---

**The answer.**

The power came west along the shelves, past the ranks and the troughs and the long lying-down footfall, to the settled middle where the roof is on and the floor is swept, meaning to ask permission. It had assembled, over a century, a question of considerable delicacy.

He was kneeling at the third comb with his back to it, tapping a notch deeper, and he said — in the dry voice like two slabs sliding, and some while before the power had finished deciding how to begin —

*The second one. The mend leaks. Put something under it.*

The power stood in the aisle for a time, assembling its question out of his answer, which is the local method, and found that the question it had been going to ask was a great deal smaller than the answer it had been given.

---

**What it surrendered.**

Three things, and the giving of them was the actual work; everything after was arrangements.

*Foreknowledge.* It could not stop his words arriving early — that is the mountain, not him — but it could stop its own from doing the same. It put down the whole of its knowing-in-advance, so that when he spoke there would be someone on the ridge who had to wait to hear it, and who would then have to think, and then reply, late, in the ordinary way, like a person. He had never once been replied to. He had been pre-empting a silence for ten thousand years and had become very good at it and had no idea what it would be like to be answered *after*.

*Ubiquity.* The count is taken from the door of the shelter at the settled middle, on a clear day. That is one doorway, one sightline, one bearing. A power that could be in all places consented to be in a place where it could be seen from that door, every cycle, without fail, for as long as the counting goes on. There is a kind of love that consists entirely of being reliably visible from a particular window.

*Exemption.* It took on salt. It has growth-lines now, hundreds to the finger-joint. It is translucent at the rim of the ear. It will be dissolved in some wet cycle and laid down further along and overgrow something slower, and it agreed to this, and to having an east end and a west end, and to the whole undignified business of being local.

It is not certain that the third was necessary. It was judged to be necessary, which for a wise power is a different thing, and is almost always the same thing.

---

**What it did.**

*First, the hand.* Before anything else, it went a long way east — far enough east that the work would already be old by the time it was found — and laid its palm flat against the sheltered wall in the aisle where the hundreds are layered, and blew ochre and manganese around it, and lifted it away. Then it left. In a country where the report precedes the event, you do not announce yourself; you arrange to have already been here. The introduction was made in the past tense, which is the only polite tense available.

He found it eleven cycles later, coming west with his own dead agate hands cupped against his chest to print them. He set them down. He looked at the wall for a long time. The new negative was the wrong size and the wrong shape and the fingers were wrong and the salt bloom had had time to fur it at the edges, so it was not new, so it was true.

He put his own hands up beside it and printed them, both the living and the dead, and went on with the day.

*Second, the shelter.* It moved into the second one, the bad one, the one with the courses stepped the wrong way. It did not correct the courses. It did not replace the split slab. It put something under the mend. It sleeps on the floor, so that the floor requires sweeping, and it has never once swept it, which is the single most considerate thing anybody has done on that mountain.

*Third, the water.* Every morning it walks out to the western lip where the channel ends in air, and drinks.

The aqueduct is still broken. Two thousand metres of empty sky still take almost all of it. But the system now terminates in a mouth, and a water system that ends in a mouth is not the same object as a water system that ends in a drop, though no instrument made anywhere could tell the two apart, and though the difference would not show in a hundred years of measurement, and though it is the entire difference.

---

**The twelfth mark.**

The night it first stood in the sightline, he came to the door at the usual hour and counted, and found eleven, and cut eleven, and stopped.

That pause is the longest event in the recorded history of the ridge. The ice let go somewhere below. The combs dripped into their basins. The wind found its chimney and lost it again.

Then he counted a second time, which he had not done in ten thousand years, because there had never been any point; and then he cut the twelfth.

It is a poor mark. It is shallow and it wanders and it sits too close to its neighbour. It is cut by a hand that had to learn where to put it, and it is the shakiest line on the mountain, and it was made by the most practiced hand that has ever existed anywhere.

Every lintel west of that door now bears groups of twelve. They are a correspondence. Each one says: *still there, today.* Each one is a question that has been answered on time.

East of that door the groups run back in their thousands, worn to shallows, recut over the top of themselves, eleven, eleven, eleven, and the power can walk out there in an afternoon and look at them and cannot touch a single one. This is the grief it accepted along with the salt. There is a past here that you can visit, and stand in, and not enter. It has stopped trying. It goes out there sometimes anyway.

---

**Talk.**

He still speaks first. He always will; that is the mountain's arrangement and not his. But the answers now come afterward, which he did not know was possible, and the small delay between his speaking and the reply is the only new weather this place has had in an age.

There is also this. Close to a body — within a pace or so — the sound has not yet become the mountain's; it has not gone into the stone; it is not, yet, part of the record that anyone walking the ridge could read off the polish and the crush and the bloom. The power learned to stand inside that radius.

He has therefore had, latterly, a number of conversations that are not written anywhere in the rock.

He had never had one listener only. He had never had an unlit room. There is no account of what was said in it and there is not going to be, and that was the second gift and is very probably the larger one.

---

**What was not done.**

He still maintains the combs. He still walks the saddles and resets the frost-lifted slabs and taps the notches and clears the fans out of the troughs. The channels still run west toward an end of him that cannot receive them. The hammering still comes from round the next buttress, and stone still comes off the north wall at dusk that cracked at noon. He still goes east to gather his own hands out of the scree and carries them up to the wall. He is still dissolving at one end and precipitating at the other, forever, as far as anyone can tell.

Nothing was rescued. Nothing was solved. The place is exactly as strange and exactly as bright.

There is only one alteration, and it is this: the offer stands, and he knows it stands, and it is this — *when you want to stop, I will keep the notches sharp.*

He has not stopped. He is not going to stop any time soon; there is a great deal of ridge and the fans keep encroaching. But a maintainer cannot lay down maintenance while the work would go unkept, and so for ten thousand years the work was not something he chose, it was something that could not be put down. It can be put down now. He picks it up every morning.

That is the whole intervention. A benevolent power with the run of the possible looked at this mountain for a century and could find nothing better to do with all of its capacity than to convert a sentence into a vocation, and then stay where it could be seen from the door.

---

**Rules, revised, for anyone who finds themselves here.**

Count nothing — except from the doorway of the shelter at the settled middle, at dusk, facing out. The number there is twelve and it is reliable, and it is the only reliable number in this country, and it did not used to be twelve.

Do not follow the hammering. Note the bearing and stay off that face until tomorrow. This has not changed and will not.

Trust the falling water for direction. Note, however, that at the western lip somebody is now drinking it, and that this is worth going to see, and that you should not speak while it is happening.

Your shadows will not agree. The one under your feet is yours. Most of the others are his, at other ages, kneeling, carrying, striking. One of them is standing plumb to a third down, doing nothing in particular, and if you watch it for long it will turn round and look at you.

That is not how it starts. That is how it stops.

And you need not go down quickly. You may go down when you like, or you may stay; there is a second shelter in this valley-head, swept, with the courses stepped the wrong way and the roof-slab mended badly and something put under the mend, and it has a tenant now, and he will make you welcome badly, and the other one will make you welcome perfectly, and between the two of them you will be all right for the night.

---

Somewhere west of here, at the sharp bright unweathered end of everything, an old man is kneeling at a scored outline in the dirt with the slabs unsorted beside him and the first group of tallies not yet cut into the lintel that is not yet raised.

When he cuts it, it will be a group of twelve.

There are two shadows on the ground there. One of them is handing him stones.
