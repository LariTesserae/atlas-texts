# The Visitor

## I.

It came wading, because there is no other way.

It could have come down. Coming down would have been a statement, and the first thing it understood, from two kilometres out with the columns shouting ahead of it in their different registers, was that this is a country where statements get copied forever. So: knee-deep, in the colour of the sky, ninety minutes, heel-dimples closing slowly behind it in the black tissue, forgetting it as it went.

Tamn waded out on the fourth day and looked at it for a while and said, *don't put anything down.* It said thank you. She went back up.

It stayed at the margin eleven days before it climbed.

## II. The reading

What it did in those eleven days is the only part of this that summarises easily. It read the being.

It found a creature four thousand years old with no centre — no brain, no organ of self, nothing to address — only a lattice in every cell wall doing slow arithmetic on sugar, and a purpose laid in at the beginning, and no channel by which the purpose could ever be told it was over. It found the purpose still running at full strength in every membrane, undamaged, the clearest thing in the whole organism:

*House them. Keep the water off. Put the stairs where the feet go. Make the spoons. Be ready.*

It looked for pain, hard, because that would have settled the question. There is no pain in the Hummock. What there is instead is worse to look at and much less urgent: a magnificent competence with nothing to land on.

And it found, among the three hundred million frogs and the wardens and the herons in the drains, three people of the wrong height who had been noticed by the being roughly the way a river notices a stone.

## III. The mercies it declined

**It did not repair the memory.** It could have. A season's work at the level of the pith: restore fidelity, stop the drift, and the scars would hold, and the Verge would produce a hinge that was a hinge, and the copies would stop losing a stroke a century. It declined, for two reasons. The drift is the only place anything new has happened here in four thousand years. And a perfect memory would have made the being into a flawless servant of an absent master, sealed, finished, correct — which is not a cure. That is a tomb with the lid fitted properly.

**It did not tell the being that its tenants were dead.** There is no ear. And supposing it built one: the waiting is load-bearing. The overproduction that the being runs on the arithmetic of anticipation is the same overproduction that feeds sixty species of frog, and the tree-crabs, and the bootlace snakes, and the wardens, and the heron standing in the drain. Stop the waiting and the columns stop venting and the sheet goes anoxic and the plain for a hundred kilometres becomes as flat and dead as it has always looked. The grief is the ecosystem. Also — and the power weighted this equally — you do not walk into the house of someone who has set a place at the table for forty years and take the plate away. Not unless they ask you to. And it could not ask.

**It did not change the stairs.** This was an afternoon's work. Rewrite the tread height in the pith; move the rail up two hundred and thirty millimetres; let the three of them climb like people instead of like something descending in a hurry they don't have. And the groove would go. There is one hand recorded anywhere in this universe, and the record of it is four smooth places in a handrail at seven hundred and eighty. Alter the rail and nobody is hurt and nobody notices and a hand goes out.

It had come a very long way to not do a small thing.

**It did not carry them out.** They would have come back, or they would not have, and either way it would have ended their argument, and the argument was theirs.

**It did not fill the town.** A thousand people would also have been the wrong height. You cannot cure loneliness by increasing the number of witnesses to it.

**It did not meet the fourth.** The one thing the being cannot grow is preference. The fourth had expressed one — fifteen years of it, unbroken, in bare footprints always heading down-slope. It was the clearest statement of preference on the hummock, clearer than the charts, clearer than the knife. So the power gave him the only thing he had ever asked for, which was to be left, and it left him nothing at all, because leaving something would have been a sentence spoken to a man who had spent fifteen years declining conversation.

**And it did not sit in the chair.**

This one it held for three years, which for a power of that kind is a long time. It could have taken the shape: short, broad, weight on the forearms, hooking rather than gripping. It could have climbed the eleven short risers with its hand in the groove where the hand goes, and crossed the level floor, and sat down in the east-facing room at dawn with the hollow low in its back fitting exactly, and the house would have been, for one morning in four thousand years, *correct.*

It declined because the being learns. That is established; Leeward's leaf proves it. A single confirmation after four millennia of null results does not produce peace. It produces a bloom. The Hummock would have thrown everything it had into readiness — reserves, surplus, the entire standing overproduction the frogs live inside — and the tenant would not have come again, and the correction would have been made by the wardens, who eat what deviates, and what would have been deviating by then was the whole town.

It is the only decision in this account that the power is still not certain of.

## IV. What it did

**It asked Sunward a question.**

Nobody had asked him anything in eleven years. Leeward visits twice a season and asks him nothing and cuts the braid at his throat, which was the kindest available behaviour right up until something arrived that could hear either answer.

The power sat down in front of him, at his height, and learned his rhythm — the pause, the phrase pushed out all at once, the long pause — and on the sixth day it asked him whether he wanted to be cut out. It made clear, in a way he could feel rather than merely believe, that it could do it: all of it, to the last filament, that he would walk, that it would carry him to a country with stone in it if he liked.

Then it waited eleven days, because that was how long the answer took to come up through him.

He said *not yet.* Then, three days later, he said the true thing underneath, which was that he was afraid there was no longer enough of him left to choose with, and that the asking was the only evidence he had that there was. And he asked the power to ask him again. Out loud. Every year. Including afterward.

It has asked him forty-one times. He has answered thirty-nine.

It intends to go on asking after he stops answering entirely, because a question put to someone who cannot answer is still a question put to *someone*, and the morning that stops is the morning he becomes scenery, and it has undertaken not to be the one who does that to him.

**It walked Leeward to her own mark and made her measure it.**

It could read the scars. It could run the decay backwards through ten million generations and recover what had been cut into the crown when there were still hills on the horizon. It did. Four hundred and fifty-nine of them are copies of copies of copies and are, exactly as she has always maintained, empty — beautiful, gestural, and meaning nothing.

The four hundred and sixtieth is different in a way that can be measured without any power at all. It was cut *late.* It was cut after the others had already gone soft, by a hand that had learned the old hand rather than inherited it, at a height the old hand never used, by somebody who had come to a house that was not theirs and found it keeping itself for people who were not coming back, and who put a mark on it because there was no one to tell.

It is empty now too. Its content is gone. Its *position* is not.

And the sixteen scars nearest it have been drifting toward it for four thousand years, because a senile lattice cannot distinguish a statement from a reply and does the only thing it knows how to do with both, which is to move everything nearer.

The power did not tell her any of this. It told her to measure the height of the late mark, and then to measure the height of her own, which she had never once approached with a rule.

Four millimetres apart.

She did not thank it. She went and got more cuticle, and she has been working on the sixteen ever since, and she has stopped calling what she did in that bad season *damage.* She calls it the second mark. She is wrong about the number — there were others between, and they have gone — but she is right about the kind, and the kind is what she was afraid of.

**It held still for a year.**

You cannot lecture a lattice. There is nothing in the Hummock that takes instruction. But the seedwork reads stillness as structure, and anything you leave still for a day gets built into a wall, and the pith stores what it is given as patterns of isotope, and a pattern held steadily for four seasons is, by the standards of this place, a shout.

So the power went to a junction near the crown and stopped, and let the root-braid come up over it, and let itself be built into a wall, and stayed there for a year being read.

What it held was not a shape. Shapes it had no business supplying. What it held was a preference — one criterion, very small, which the being had never possessed and had no way to derive:

The Verge asks only *is this the same as before.* Tamn spent eleven years adding *is this good.* The power spent a year in a wall adding the third, which is the one that mattered:

*Who is it for.*

The wardens ate it out of the wall in the eleventh month, because it deviated. It came back and did it again. It lost nineteen arguments in twenty, the same as Tamn; the only difference was that it had the twenty-first, and the two hundredth, and the nine thousandth. It won in the end by being patient enough to stop being a deviation and start being a tradition.

**And it stayed.**

It sat at the Verge with Tamn for nine years and learned her sorting — not better sorting; *hers.* Why the hinge with the long throw is worth keeping even though it never catches the first time. Why a lens goes back in the bag and an identical lens does not. The grain of it, all the things she had never been asked to put into words and had assumed would die with her, which she was correct about, and which the power took down anyway.

Then she died, and it kept sorting.

It has been sorting for a long time now, and it keeps her preferences, and it could improve on them, and it does not. It has made three departures from her taste in all those centuries, and each time it argued the case out loud, in the plaza, to a heron, before allowing itself the change.

The spoons are good now. All of them. That took nine hundred years.

## V. The two stairs

Near the crown there are two stairs side by side.

One has eleven risers of a hundred and thirty millimetres and a handrail at seven hundred and eighty with four smooth places worn into it, and it is maintained to a tolerance nothing on this plain could measure. Nothing climbs it. The wardens polish it. It is not a ruin and it is not in service; it is the last address of a hand, and the being keeps it the way it keeps everything, by rebuilding it hourly, except that it now also keeps it *as a memorial*, which is a category it did not previously possess and which took eleven hundred years to install.

The other has nine risers of a hundred and seventy-five and a rail at a thousand and ten, and it is worn in the middle of the tread, the way stairs wear when people use them.

That is the whole answer the power found to the problem it arrived at, and it is not clever, and any grieving household gets there eventually if somebody helps: *you do not have to choose.* You can keep the place at the table and set another one beside it. It requires nothing but that someone be present to set it, for as long as it takes — which here was about the length of the interval between the mountains going and the plain forgetting that they had been there.

The overproduction still runs. Nobody stopped it; it is the being's health. But it is aimed now, a little, the way it was always accidentally aimed at the frogs. The surplus lands on whoever is standing in it. That was the third question doing its work, slowly, for nine hundred years, through a medium that loses a stroke every century and had to be told again, and again, and again, and was.

## VI.

It still does not sit in the chair.

Every morning there is a room facing east with a good drain and a level floor and a chair with a hollow low in the back and the armrests set wide for someone who leaned. Every morning the power looks in on its way down, the way you check a window in a house where someone is ill, and does not go in. This is not caution any more. It is the shape its respect has taken. The chair is not for it. The chair is for somebody else, and the being is still expecting them, and the power has been keeping house here long enough now to have caught the condition itself — on purpose, at a dose it can carry, because that turns out to be what it is to love something very old.

In the morning there is a new room facing east, with a good drain and a level floor, and a chair that nobody will ever sit in.

And beside it, because a woman once spent eleven years arguing for it and was finally, four hundred years after her death, heard — a second chair.

Which fits.
