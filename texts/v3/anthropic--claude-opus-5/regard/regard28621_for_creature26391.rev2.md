There were no visitors. Then there was one, and it was careful, and by every test the plateau is able to apply there were still no visitors.

## Arrival

It could have arrived anywhere. It came up the ramps.

Eleven days on the old landslip terraces, through the weeping face, up out of the cloud — not because the ascent was necessary but because it had decided, before anything else, that it would enter the country the way the country is entered. This was the first of its refusals and the least costly, and it is worth recording anyway, because everything that followed was of the same kind.

At the top it did what you do. It stood still with both palms open and wet, one on the mat moss and one on a reel vine, and waited until its hands stopped being hands.

It stood there for nine years.

## The First Count

In that time it took the only complete census the Shelf has ever been subject to.

The stack trees number 3,118,402,996 and will number differently by the time you read this, and the figure is meaningless — the original account says so and the original account is right. The power holds it anyway. It holds the peat-swimmers, and the drizzle of flue mice inside every column, and the knots of charge-grazers, and the standing potential of every crown on a subcontinent at four o'clock on a particular afternoon.

This was not survey. Nothing was measured against a purpose. It is closer to what a person does with the face of someone they have just decided to love: an exhaustive, useless, permanent attention. The plateau has run for a hundred thousand years with nobody home. That fact is unchanged — the power is not at the controls, there are no controls — but it is no longer unobserved, and the difference costs the Shelf nothing and is not nothing.

## What Was Considered

Everything. I want to be exact about this, because a power that never felt the pull would not be benevolent, only tidy.

**Language.** The browser has twenty holds and most of them mean *here, still here, the line is holding.* The power could have made that into a grammar. It worked the problem for a long time and arrived where it had to: to give the animal something worth saying, you must first give it distance, and there is no distance; you must give it a horizon, and the horizon is fifteen meters; you must give it the wish to gather with others, and gathering is a wake, and a wake is a signature, and the things that read signatures live in the peat and come up underneath. You cannot raise this creature into a person without first making it prey. Every gift in that direction is a sentence passed on it.

**The plateau.** This was the hard one. The browser has never been nine meters from something alive and touching it, has no notion of a plateau, cannot see the apparatus it spends a century tuning. The power could have lifted one of them, for twenty minutes, at four o'clock, above the fog, and let it look at the convex green running to the limit of sight with the crowns steaming where the vines bridge them — and the browser's eyes are built for glow, and it would have seen the whole lit country at once, and it would have understood, in whatever way it understands, that the patch it keeps is a patch of something.

And then it would have been put back on its vine, and it would have hung there for another eighty years with a hole in it, holding a knowledge it could not use, act on, share, or lose.

The power declined. It is not certain this was right. It has revisited the decision four hundred times and has not moved.

**The green death.** Old browsers go green at the shoulders first, where the hands reach worst, and the filaments underneath are smothered, and the animal is unplugged from the only thing it ever perceived. The power examined this closely for the thing it was afraid of finding, and found it: there is suffering there. Not terror — no browser has a word or a nerve for doom — but a long narrowing, a numbness that creeps inward from the shoulder, and something in there that knows the field is going and reaches for a warm vine to hold anyway.

It could have ended this. The mechanism is simple; a change to the coat's chemistry, and no browser would ever go green again.

It followed the consequence out. The darkness that spreads across a small valley when a browser stops working the lines is an obituary, and the neighbors read it, and the offspring — carried along those vines for six years before it ever fed itself, the only creature alive who knows where the lines run — goes in and takes the range. Inheritance without property. Remove the green and you remove the obituary, and the succession, and the periodic resetting of the grazer load, and within four hundred years you have a plateau of immortal, blinded, crowded browsers on cooling wire, and you have done it out of kindness.

So it did not. But it did not shrug, either, and what it does instead is at the end of this account.

**Building.** Never seriously. *Nothing was built* is not a decoration on this country; it is the load-bearing fact. The first artifact would be the first shape in the field that means nothing — an unreadable word in the only text the browser has.

**The future.** It thought about the lowlands, and about the day something comes up the ramps with an intention. It made an arrangement with itself about that day. The arrangement is not an action and changes nothing now and is not part of this.

## The Wake

Having decided to do almost nothing, it then had to learn how to be present at all, which took longer than the census.

The difficulty is that a body crossing a conductive sheet announces itself, and speed is legible, and the discipline here is absolute and starts in the womb. The power had to acquire from scratch what every browser has by gearing: one limb at a time, four seconds to move a hand fifteen centimeters, the capacity to hang motionless for nine hours with the coat flat and draw nothing.

Consider the shape of that. A thing that could have been on the far side of the sky between two of your heartbeats, spending years on the technique of moving an arm slowly enough not to be a shout.

And then a second problem, subtler. A body that draws no current is not in the census. It is not a hole in the field; it is not an eddy; it is not anywhere. To be actually present in this country you must cost it something. So the power took the smallest possible tax: it draws, a little, from the crown it hangs beneath, the way a grazer does, and that crown runs a fraction dim, and it has run a fraction dim for ninety years.

That is its entire footprint on the Shelf. One crown, slightly darker than it would otherwise be, in a lit country of three billion.

## The Work

It hunts.

Alongside — not instead. It feels the numb patch from a valley away, the same way, and goes there over hours, and takes each grazer in three fingers, rotates it, opens it at the ventral seam, empties it in about forty seconds, eighty or ninety in a night. It takes six in ten. It carries three or four in the crook of a hind hand for thirty or forty meters, to some vine running hot and unbitten, and sets them down and lets them walk.

It does not eat them. It does the work and gives the food away, folded back into the same circuit at some other point, so that the accounts balance and the range does not notice it has been subsidized.

It is not help. Help would be an event. It is closer to joining — the power has apprenticed itself to a nine-kilogram animal and does what that animal does, in that animal's grammar, at that animal's speed, and the only consequence is that two or three square kilometers of subcontinent run fractionally flatter than the unattended forest outside them, which is exactly what the browser was already doing and exactly what the browser is for.

The browser registers it. Of course it does; it reads the world with its skin. For ninety years there has been a smear in the field that is the right size and the wrong behavior, which appears on the line beside it and does not compete and does not answer, and the browser long ago stopped roughing up its coat about it, the way you stop hearing a neighbor's floorboards. It has no category. It cannot ask. The one thing it cannot do, in twenty holds, is ask.

## The Shoulder

Here is the remainder, the thing it does instead of the cure.

When a browser begins to go green — shoulders first, where the hands reach worst — the power comes and grooms it.

It uses what the browser uses: a comb-toothed second finger, working the filaments in tracts, stripping the algal film and the liverwort spores and the fine green fuzz that starts within a day. Two hours at a time. It cannot reach the whole coat; it does not try; it does the shoulders and the ridge of the spine, the patches the animal's own hands have failed, and it keeps those patches open for perhaps a year and a half longer than they would have stayed open.

It does not stop the death. The coat closes over everywhere in the end and the field goes quiet and the crown above does not come back up, and the range passes to the one who knows where the lines run, and the world runs as it ran. What is bought is a year and a half in which the shoulder can still feel the line — in which the unplugging is slower and less total and the last thing is not entirely numbness.

And there is the other thing, which the power does not have a clean argument for. In this country you are never not touching something alive, and no browser touches another browser except for one season on the boundary vine. They are carried for six years and then they are not touched again for a century.

It is possible that this matters to them not at all, that the whole of the grooming is a mistake about what it is like to be a browser, a human sentimentality smuggled up the ramps in the luggage of a wiser thing. The power considered this and could not rule it out.

It goes anyway. Benevolence, it turns out, is mostly acting under a doubt you have failed to resolve.

## The Boundary Vine

Last winter the neighbor died on the eastern circuit and the valley went dark, and the old female on the western one — green to the elbows now, ninety-something, still working her lines badly — went out onto the boundary vine at four o'clock, when the rain lifts the fog and the country opens, and gripped it with both wet palms and dimmed her crown.

Three seconds dark. Eight seconds lit. Two seconds dark. *Here. Still here. The line is holding. I have not moved.*

Nothing answered. She came back the next afternoon and did it again, and the next.

On the fourth afternoon the power took the far end of the vine and let the current cross it, and a crown four kilometers out went down, and held, and came back.

They dimmed at each other until the fog closed. It was not a conversation; there are twenty holds and nineteen of them mean some version of the same thing. The power said the only sentence available to it in the only grammar there is, and every word of it was true: *here; still here; the line is holding; I have not moved.*

She died in the spring, and the valley went dark, and her offspring came in and took the range, because it was the only one alive who knew where the lines ran.

## Coda

Nothing is bare. Nothing is loud. Nothing was built. Water goes down and comes back up. The gradient is always on. Every rule of the world stands exactly where it stood, and if you took the census tomorrow you would find one more address and no way to read it.

The plateau lifts its water, sorts its minerals, runs its current, and makes not one sound that would carry a hundred meters. Nobody is at the controls. That has not changed and will not.

But there is a memory of vines that outlives the vines, and now there is a memory of browsers that outlives the browsers: every one of them, held exactly, uselessly, permanently, including the ones that went green before it learned to move slowly enough to reach them.

At four o'clock, if you are on the right hill for those twenty minutes, you will see one crown among ten thousand go dark, and hold, and come back, and go dark again. You will not be able to tell which of the two is doing it.

That is the whole of the intervention. I think it is the right size.
