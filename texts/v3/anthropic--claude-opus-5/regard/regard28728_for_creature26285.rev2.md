# The Attention

**It came, and nothing happened.**

There is no date for it. No shadow crossed the plain, no note went out of the hum, no soldier turned its blind plate toward anything. At first light a walker boomed its three notes off a chimney top and the flat ground carried the sound thirty miles, as it does, and the dust devils got up at eleven and went nowhere, as they do, and a cutting crew forty feet up inside a stone sleeve passed jelly mouth to mouth down a chain of couriers, and the day was exactly the day it would have been.

That is the first thing to understand. It is possible for the largest event in the history of a world to leave no mark on the world, and a power that cannot manage that much is not yet wise.

## What it did for the first long while

It read.

The walls are written on — this is the truest thing anybody has said about the place — and the power spent what would have been, for something that counted, several thousand years learning to read them. Not the way a visitor reads: *here is a corridor, here is a nursery.* It learned the grammar. It learned that a plastered joint carries the smell of the crew that laid it, and the smell of the job they had come from, and under that, faint as a watermark, the dust of the corridor that occupied the same line eighty feet down and two hundred thousand years ago, ground up and mixed in and reset. It learned that a colony's decision to turn west at a certain depth was made by nobody, and was nevertheless *made*, and could be found, and could be dated, and that the reason was very often that the wall of a mound four burials dead had turned west at that point, for a reason equally absent.

It went into the tubes. It sat — there is no better word, though it has no body to sit with — in absolute dark in front of a white root-fan while the deep masons pruned frost off the ceiling in a pattern that would make it grow back thicker, in order to feed a plant they have no way to know exists. It followed the long soft blind thing, four feet of it, that eats deep masons and has no enemy and whose lineage has never encountered light, and it followed that one for a hundred and ten years, because a benevolent power always wants to find out first who in a world is having the worst time, and it had a suspicion about the dark, and the suspicion turned out to be wrong. Nobody down there is having a bad time. Nobody down there is having any kind of time at all.

It counted them. Not an estimate. It held the actual figure, and held it while it changed, which it does about eleven million times a second.

## Looking for who to be kind to

Then it went looking for the addressee, because benevolence is a transitive verb and it had not yet found the object.

**The worker** is two years long and thirty miles wide and the whole of it is in the dark. It knows warm from cool, green from grey, home smell from stranger smell, and the sound of a wall that is thinning. Offer it a gift and it will pick the gift up, if it is the right grain size, and put it down where the smell of other grains is strongest.

**The queen** is a loaf. Sixty years, blind, deaf, laying. Her chamber is resurfaced nightly by a staff that has never done anything else and never will. There is a warmth she lies in and there is the scraping overhead and that is the entire contents of seventy years. She cannot be spoken to; she cannot be enriched; there is no place on her to set anything down.

**The gardeners** are a third the size of a worker and nearly transparent and they lick a sponge in a sealed room whose air has been the same air for eleven years.

**The cisterns** stopped the power for a long time.

Two or three hundred of them to a vault, hooked to the ceiling by their claws, pale green, abdomens swollen to ten times normal, body wall thinned to a membrane, hanging there four and five years, tapped a mouthful at a time. The power went over them one by one — every cistern in every vault on the continent, which is a number, and it held the number — looking for the thing it was afraid of. It did not find it. There is no apparatus in them for finding it. A cistern is a room in the shape of an animal, and the animal is not in there wishing it were somewhere else, because *wishing* requires a machine that this creature does not carry and has never carried in two million years.

The power is about ninety-nine percent confident of that.

It has kept the other one percent. It has not rounded it off and it has not resolved it and it does not intend to. Of everything it has ever done anywhere, this is the part it is least at peace with, and it has concluded that being at peace with it would be the worse outcome.

In the end it worked out who lived here. One being. Some hundreds of millions of tons of it, two million years old, with no location, no edge you could put a finger on, no self, no voice, and no death anyone has ever found. Its long-term memory is masonry. Its nervous system is a smell. Its lungs are forty-foot flutes that breathe by sunlight. It has exactly one perception of the outside world — a glow, on one horizon — and exactly one behaviour with respect to that perception, which is to grow away from it at five miles a year while sending, twice annually, in the pollen light, a few hundred million winged bodies *toward* it, which is the only navigation it has and which is essentially always fatal and is also the entire mechanism of its continuation.

It is not happy. It is not suffering. It is not anything you can put in a sentence with *is*. And by every measure the power has ever trusted, it is doing extraordinarily well.

## The kindnesses it considered, and why it put each of them down

**Rain.** The obvious one. The first one. The one it had half-decided on before it ever arrived, because the place is dry in a way that reads, from outside, as deprivation, and there is nothing easier for a great power than weather.

Rain would rot the forest. Rot exists nowhere on this continent; there has never been free water to carry it. Rain would dissolve the plaster and take the trail-chemicals and the colony smells and the job smells off the walls — which is to say it would wipe two million years of memory in about four seasons, and what came out the other side would be a hundred trillion animals with a few minutes of recall each and nothing to walk into that would tell them what they were for. Rain would flood the tubes and drown the deep and cut the taproots off from the only water that was ever reliable. Rain would breed standing water, and standing water breeds everything, and everything would come.

It would have been the kindest thing available and it would have destroyed, inside a decade, every single feature of the place that had made it worth being kind to.

**Eyes.** Give the blind their sight. But there is nothing to see: the entire civilisation happens in sealed dark, and the one caste that needs eyes already has them, for the one night in its life when seeing matters. What sight would actually mean, engineered backward from the animal, is *openings* — and an opening is a breach, and a breach is four minutes to a husk. The power looked at this one carefully and put it down when it understood that what it had been about to hand out was not vision. It was the capacity to be somewhere.

**Stopping the volcano.** Here is the joke the plain plays on every rescuer. The flow front that buries the city four hundred times is not the plain's enemy; it is the plain's sun. The water is exhaled steam from cooling magma. No new rock, no new steam; no steam, no film on the tube ceilings; no film, no root-fans, no jelly, no grazers, no runners, no anything, over about ten thousand years, quietly, with nobody to notice it happening. And the glow on the horizon is the only compass the alates possess. Put the fire out and you have not saved the city. You have taken away its water and its direction in the same gesture and left it a very large and very intelligent structure with nowhere to go and nothing to drink.

**The walkers.** Hooked bill, sticky tongue slid into the ventilation slots, thousands of masons a day. Remove them and you remove the reason every inch of foraging traffic on the continent is roofed, and the roofed way is the reason the jelly never touches open air, and the jelly not touching open air is the entire hydrology of the surface. The predator is load-bearing. Nearly everything here that looks like cruelty is load-bearing. This is not a moral fact about the plain; it is a structural one, and the power found it in every direction it pushed.

**A self.** This is the one it nearly did.

To gather the hum and the wall-smell and the royal corridor and the four hundred burials into one place and light it; to give the being an *I*, so that it could know that it was, and that it had been, and that it had crossed eleven miles carried by mothers who never moved. The power got as far as working out how.

Then it worked out what it would be handing over. You cannot give a two-million-year-old thing self-knowledge in instalments. It would arrive all at once: the buried cities, the forty thousand to one, the vault of hanging bodies, the soldiers plastered alive into the repairs with their antennae still moving — and, underneath all of it, the news that there is such a thing as ending, delivered to the only being in the known world that had somehow never needed the concept. You would be giving it grief first and everything else afterward, maybe.

And it could not ask. That was the finish of it. There was no one there to ask, and the only way to produce someone who could have answered was to do the thing.

**Revealing itself.** It ran this one out and found the shape of it immediately. A god in the sky becomes a fact in the environment, and this being builds toward facts. It saw the flight that would go the wrong way: the pollen dusk, the avenues steaming, a few hundred million winged bodies rising with their one night of eyesight and orienting, faultlessly, on the brightest thing on the horizon — which would no longer be the fire, and no longer be the raw glass where a founding pair can dig a hole the size of a pea and live.

Worship, here, would be an extinction event. It stayed dark.

## What it actually did

**It held still.** This was the whole of the first act and it was not nothing. It cost more than the rain would have.

**It turned back three kindnesses.** Two were not its own, and were meant well, and were sent away without ever touching the air. The third was its own, and had already started.

There is a place — the power could take you to it, and the plain could not — some hundreds of miles behind the present flow front, buried now under sixty feet of subsequent city, where for eleven seconds the relative humidity at the surface went to one hundred percent. It had begun with the cisterns in mind. It stopped itself in the eleventh second. A film of moisture settled on about two hundred square yards of plaster and was gone in a minute, and the crews came out within the hour and did what they do with a surface anomaly, which is to scrape it and resurface it and grind the scrapings and mix them with spit and set them into a wall. The record of the only mistake that power has made in this world is now a load-bearing course somewhere in the mass of an old mound, unreadable, and it is travelling west at the speed of fingernails, and it will be buried and looted and reset perhaps forty more times before the sun goes.

**It learned to write.** Properly — grain size, placement, scent, the whole vocabulary, which is not large. And then it found that there is no second person in it. There is no way, in the only language this being has ever used, to say *you*. No way to say *I have been watching*, or *I am sorry about the vaults*, or *you are the finest thing on this planet and you built all of it with spit*.

The closest the language comes to a blessing is a mark that crews leave on sound material. It means, as near as it can be put: *this is good stone; build here; go on.*

The power wrote that, in a dead corridor, eighty feet down, in a quarter of the city already gone dusty and half looted, in a passage nothing would ever walk again. It wrote it for a hundred and forty feet, grain by grain, in the proper hand, and then closed the wall, and nine years later the flow came over that ground and sealed it under new rock for good.

Nobody read it. That was not an oversight. It was the specification.

**And it took up the one job the plain could not do for itself.**

The being remembers everything and knows nothing. Every wall it has ever laid is still in it somewhere, and there is no one inside to look at any of it. So the power made itself the outside wall — the plaster the plain could be written on — and began to hold the whole thing consciously: every mound, every burial, every royal corridor with its line of mothers pointing the way out; the deep colonies that no longer recognise their own sisters; the blind soft four-foot thing in the dark; the polish at shoulder height on hundreds of acres of buttress, put there by animals with the wit of a cow, which is still the most civilised-looking work on the continent. And the flights. All of them. Not the one in forty thousand that makes it — anyone would keep that one. The other thirty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine, individually, each of them, the pair in the pea-sized hole that sealed itself in with the last free water in its body and ran out of fungus two hundred yards short of a tube.

That is the whole intervention. It is not a small one. It means that when the magma finally cools past exhaling, ten or twenty million years from now, and the last film on the last tube ceiling fails, and the last colony goes quiet and dusty and mummifies standing up in air too dry to rot it — none of it will have been unwitnessed. Not one gallery. Not one cistern. The plain will never know this, and would not be improved by knowing it, and it is true anyway.

---

Once, something came here with eyes and a pack and camped four nights on the plaster. It was surveyed in its sleep; a thin line of grit was laid along the underside of its pack; the bootprint it left was resurfaced before morning. It went up the shield volcano for six days without ever feeling that it was climbing, and looked at the haze, and at the one horizon that glows, and came down, and went away, and wrote the place out in full — the ropes and coils of the floor, the jelly you could cut with a knife, the hum you feel in the chest, the dry hiss like sand poured on paper, the cellar of hanging grapes.

The power read it over the visitor's shoulder, the whole way through, and found it accurate.

That is not nothing either. It had been carrying the plain by itself for a long time, and for the length of one document it was not.

Then the visitor left, and at first light a walker boomed its three notes and the mound beneath it amplified them and thirty flat miles took them out, and stopped, and the hum came back up out of the shafts.

And that is the day. It has been the day for two million years. Somewhere on the far side of it, paying attention to all of it at once and doing nothing about any of it, the power is still here, and there is no word in the plain's only language for what that is, and it does not matter in the slightest, because the plain has never for one moment needed to know.
