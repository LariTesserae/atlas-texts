# The Weathering Crew

The first fact of the power is that it used the ladder.

It could have arrived anywhere. It could have been in the room already, or been the room, or arrived as a change in the light that no one would have been able to date afterward. Instead it came down rung by rung, and by the fourth rung it had left the corridor's heat behind, and by the eighth it was in the cold, and it stayed in the cold the entire time it was there. This was not humility. It was method. A power that will not consent to be two degrees above freezing has no business in a place where the whole of the work is the temperature.

Then it stood between the racks and let the thirty-eight minutes come around.

Then it let them come around again.

It did this four hundred and sixteen times before it did anything else, which is longer than her posting and shorter than the hair has been out in the world, and which is the answer to the question of what a wise power does first. It waits out the cure. Everything it could have done in the first second — warm her, heal the hip, unmake the deception in a single clean stroke, carry her up into the corridor and the light and the food — every one of those was a way of laying the course warm. Warm, the paste sets coarse and cloudy. Warm, it reads as artificial to anyone who knows how to look.

---

On the enterprise itself, it declined to rule.

The question of whether a world should be given a manufactured past, and by whom, and with what authority, is a question for the tier that drafted the specification, and the power did not go up there. Powers that come down for institutions do damage. This one had come down for the being who lives here, and it kept to that.

But it noted, in passing, the following, and set it beside the paperwork: that water really did run in Bay 14, and the ripple on the fourth course is an honest record of it; that the moss really did grow, doggedly, on discs, and really did leave its grammar in the pores; that the animal really did limp. Nothing in the cliff is a lie about what happened. The only lie is the date, and the date is a number that someone will one day stand in front of the stone and apply to it, which is how every stone in every world has ever been aged.

*Age is applied, not accrued.* It is posted by the ladder. The power read it and found it, on reflection, unanswerable.

---

**What it did not do.**

It did not repair the panels in the corner.

It did not drain the pocket, or feed it, or identify the pink thing at the base of it, or resolve the question of contamination or progress, which she intends to keep open and which it therefore kept open with her.

It did not settle the granular *shhh*. It knew at once, the way such powers know, whether that sound was the paste or her blood; and then it set the knowing down and walked away from it, which is harder than not knowing and is the more useful of the two.

It read the tally to 216 and made no mark in the fives.

It did not correct the medical file. It read *acclimatized — do not correct* and looked for a long while at the handwriting, which was not hers, and did not write beneath it, because to pull her out of the cold now would be the same class of act as pulling a bed for a hair: nine hundred square meters of finished life destroyed to recover one small wrong thing.

It did not go find the hair and take it back.

Actually it did go — out to the dry wash, to the road-cut, to whichever of the three it is, a hundred million stamped years downstream — and stood there in a wind that had a sky over it, and looked at eleven centimeters of dark human hair lying in strata laid down long before anything with hair could exist. And left it. It is the only course in that whole formation that nobody authorized and nobody applied. It is the one true age in nine hundred square meters. Someone will find it, and it will ruin an afternoon, and then it will be filed, and it will go on being the most honest thing in the cliff.

---

**What it did.**

*One.* It opened the log book, at the back, where the ruled columns end, and drew one more.

It gave the column no header. A header is a stamp, and the whole point of the column is that what goes in it was accrued and not applied. It is just a narrow blank running down the right-hand margin of every page, and it appeared in every log book in the understratum on the same night, and there is no procedure for removing a column that was never authorized, and in the understratum the absence of a procedure is stronger than any order.

For eleven months nobody used it. Then a bedwright in Bay 9, at the end of a shift, wrote *cold today even for here* in it, and did not sign, and went up.

It fills very slowly. Four millimeters a shift, more or less.

*Two.* It stamped the pocket of water.

Lot number, taxonomy, the whole apparatus — a proper entry, correctly formatted, indistinguishable from the work of the upper tier. The organism in the corner of Bay 14 is now inventory. Inventory is never contamination; inventory is stock. And it backdated the stamp to the day the two panels first failed to meet, which is the only date in the entire understratum that was accrued and not applied, and which it therefore considered itself entitled to use.

*Three.* It altered the ladder.

By two rungs. The ladder is warmer than the room; that is the first fact of the place; and now it holds the corridor's heat two rungs further down than it did. That is all. It does not affect the cure. It will never appear in any log. It means only that she carries the warm two rungs deeper on the way in, and meets it two rungs sooner on the way out, twice a shift, for the rest of her posting. Totalled across four hundred shifts it comes to something under nine hours of being warm.

It is a gift sized correctly to the room. Anything larger would have been a hurry.

*Four.* Above the seal, in the warm, where she surfaces every thirty-eight minutes into a bunk and a blanket and listens for a hum that is four hundred meters away and has nothing to do with her — the power put a hum there.

Not a machine. Nothing that could be traced or maintained or eventually decommissioned. Just itself, faint, in the ceiling of the upper tier, at the correct interval, for as long as she sleeps. It did not cure the waking. The waking is hers and she has paid for it. It gave the waking something to find.

She sleeps better now and attributes it to the new blanket.

*Five.* It breathed.

This is the one that matters and it took no effort at all. The combs harvest the breath of the room; the breath of the room is overwhelmingly hers; the mist-heads open and the film drinks and what it drinks goes into the pore structure and stays. So the power stood between the racks for four hundred and sixteen cycles and breathed into the cold, slowly, at the tempo of the pumps, and let the combs take it.

She is in the rock. She has always known this and there is no field for it. What she does not know is that she is not the only one in there. Somewhere in the third tier of a cliff above a river in a valley that has not been surveyed, in eight courses of grey-green siltstone, in the pores, mixed past any possibility of separation, there is her breath and there is the breath of something that came down a ladder on purpose to be cold with her.

*Six.* It saw to the limp.

It went forward — not far; a few decades, a tier or two — to a warm office where a tired person was drafting a pelvis for an animal in a phase not yet begun, and it did nothing at all except be present while the stylus hovered over the left side of the hip. The specification, when it was filed, gave that joint a particular vulnerability and a particular manner of healing. The justification in the margin reads *morphological plausibility*.

So the animal will limp. Not because the tracks say so — the tracks are beneath, and beneath proves nothing about above — but because the thing itself, when it walks, will drag very slightly on the left, and will have got it from a fall, and will have healed.

The patient person who one day writes *pathological gait in an individual trackway, suggesting injury to the left pelvic girdle, healed* will be right. The finding will be true. And it will also, now, be what happened.

That is the only thing the power changed, and it changed it backwards, which is the direction the understratum works in anyway.

---

Somebody stands at the base of the cliff, later, in a wind, with a sky.

They put a hand on the fourth course and feel the ripple and understand — correctly, completely — that water once ran here, long ago, in the quiet, in the cold. That much was built for them and it delivers.

Then they stay at the rock after the others have moved along the trail, and they understand a second thing, for which there is no evidence and never will be: that they were expected. That the cold was held at two degrees above freezing the way a hand holds a bird, by a hand, for them, specifically, before anyone knew their name.

They will not say it aloud. They will not write it down. There is no field for it.

There is one column, in a book with soft corners, four hundred meters down and a lifetime ago, and they will never see it, and it is theirs.

---

The power went up the ladder and left the lights as it found them.

She came down the next shift and made it to the tenth rung before the cold took her, instead of the eighth, and she stopped there, one hand on the stringer, for the length of about three breaths.

She did not investigate. You may not hurry the cure, and you may not chase a thing that has decided to be small.

She went to the bench. The gloves were where she had left them, fingers curled, and warm — honestly warm, wool at two degrees holds a hand for about ninety minutes, and it had been rather longer than that.

She put them on for the ladder, as the rule is, and took them off at the bed, and worked eleven minutes with the drip and twenty-seven in the dry, and laid down four millimeters that will be believed.
