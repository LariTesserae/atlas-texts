# The Attention

## I. What it did first

Nothing, for a long time.

Not hesitation. It arrived and it looked, and looking took what would have been, elsewhere, a considerable stretch of history. It had come with the ordinary intentions of its kind — to see whether anything was wrong, and to mend what was — and it found immediately that it could not tell. The place was warm, occupied, quiet, thirsty, dark, and complete. Nothing in it was starving. Nothing in it was afraid except in the small slow way that closes two hands into a chest hollow and covers them with a forefoot. There was no crisis to enter at the top of.

So it learned instead. It learned the eleventh leaf's wind and did not correct it. It learned the trackway grammar, the cairn at every forking pillar-gap, and followed the routes as they were laid rather than the shorter ways they might have been. It counted the thermal pits along the working crest of a keeper's forward segment — twenty-seven, in three ranks of nine, which anyone patient could have finished counting, and which no one there had ever needed to.

It also learned, early, the arithmetic, and the arithmetic stopped it in its tracks.

## II. The arithmetic of mercy

The number is constant.

The visitor turned this over and found two edges on it.

The first: it could not come in as a life. Any body it wore that was warm and fed and animal would be a unit, and the country would balance itself, and somewhere in the fourth leaf a grazer holding position against the wind for the whole duration would stop holding it. To arrive in the flesh, here, is to kill something in order to be met. It declined the flesh. It made itself instead as an object — dense, unliving, resting, warm from no metabolism — and it never once, in all the time that followed, allowed itself to be counted.

The second edge was worse, and took longer.

There was a priser wedged in a settling joint near a pillar in the low red leaves, and the joint was going to close, and the visitor could have lifted the roof of that joint with less effort than it takes a hand to lift a hand. And if it had, then somewhere at a lee-drift, flour that had been gathering and packing and warming would have gone slack and lain down again and been dust.

Here, saving a life is refusing a life. There is no mercy that is not also a subtraction. The visitor sat with this — sat, literally, being an object among the sitting kind, banked with dust on one side — and understood that the whole ordinary repertoire of goodness was closed to it. It could not feed, because nothing was hungry. It could not heal, because healing is a bookkeeping act. It could not save, because there was nothing to save anyone from except the one thing that keeps the country at full census.

It did not conclude from this that nothing was needed. It concluded that it had not yet found the need.
