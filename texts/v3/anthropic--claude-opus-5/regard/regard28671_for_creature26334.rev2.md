# Courtesy

Begin with the fact that it did not arrive.

There was no approach, no descent, no bright thing over the water. It had been attending for some time before the first camp was bolted down — it was present on the day the wrench went over the side, and it watched the wrench fall three thousand meters in the clear, and it did not retrieve it. That is the first thing to understand about the power: its restraint is not hesitation. It knew within the first moment what the wrench would become if left alone, and it wanted the plateau to have an oldest artifact, and so it let a tool lie in the dark for ten thousand years rather than hand it back.

It looked for eleven years in the local reckoning and for considerably longer in its own, which is not a unit that converts. It is worth being precise about what looking means here, because nothing else it did afterward makes sense otherwise. It read the rock. It read the bent gradient, all the way down through the layers of the setting to the place where the terms of the world had been rewritten, and found the work clean — no patches, no shims, no later hand correcting an earlier one. It read the primer to the bottom of its chemistry. It read the schedule: the hand's width a year, the rock rising faster, the shallows coming on at their appointed rate a few thousand years out. It read the notebooks, which took no time at all and which it did nevertheless slowly, the way a person reads a deed, because the notebooks were the entire past of a continent and there was no other copy.

And it attended for a long while to the eighteen hertz, and understood it to be exactly what the crews had guessed: the idle. The cost of holding, shed as the only waste a bent gradient can have. A compressor in a basement. Something running, and running, and having run.

---

The audit came out well. That should be said plainly, because it bears on everything after.

The gradient was correctly set and correctly held and would not fail. The mixing was elegant — the banded shear that never travels, doing the work of a whole planet's worth of circulation at a fraction of the energy. The clamp at four degrees was not brute refrigeration but a preference laid on the water the way the bowl was laid on the rock, and would hold as long as the bowl held. The primer was, in its way, a masterpiece: light-proofed, rock-fed, self-supplying, dispersed by a weather system it also maintained, delivered inside a shell that opens on impact into a twenty-minute window of rain. You do not get that by accident and you do not get it by iteration either. Someone sat down and drafted it.

The power found one thing wrong. It took a long time to find, because it is a fault of omission and those do not show up in any measurement; you can only see them by holding the whole design in view at once and asking what the design is for.

Nothing here can rot.

The primer does not die. It dries to varnish and waits. It has no parasites, no disease, no senescence worth the name, no mechanism by which a square meter of it can ever stop being a square meter of it and become, instead, ground. Its glaze is indigestible. Its rhizoids are inert once shed. The dust it makes from basalt is sand and only sand — mineral grains, sterile, no carbon in them, nothing that holds water or ions or roots. It is making the medium for everything that is going to happen here, and the medium it is making is beach.

Soil is not crushed rock. Soil is crushed rock plus the dead. It requires a population of things whose whole profession is the disassembly of the recently alive, working in the dark, unglamorously, forever. The Shelf Sea has no such population and no provision for one. The table is being set, the water is being kept at a working temperature, the shallows are coming — and when the guests arrive there will be nothing under them but grit, and their own dead will lie on the new rock undisturbed, exactly like the wrench, waiting for a sediment that has still not been invented.

Whoever drafted this was interrupted. That is the honest reading. The work is unfinished in precisely the way work is unfinished when someone is called away between two stages, having done the hard one.

---

It could have fixed this in an afternoon. This has to be stated, because the restraint only counts if the capacity was real.

It could have seeded the whole ocean: full assemblage, decomposers and grazers and the long chains between, four hundred meters of visibility replaced by the honest murk of a living sea, ten thousand species in one gesture and every one of them correct. It considered this for the length of time such a thing deserves, which was not short, and declined for four reasons and kept the reasons.

The schedule was not its own. Someone had costed this out in millennia and the costing was sound.

Guests invited by one host are not to be replaced by a stranger's substitutes, however excellent.

An ecology handed down whole has no history, and this place is not about life; it is about a history beginning. There is a difference between a world and a diorama of one.

And the fourth, which is the one that actually governed: the cruelest available act, toward anything that has held a post without relief for an age, is to complete its task for it in a form it cannot recognize as its task completed.

So the power did not seed the sea. It went and learned how to knock.

---

There is exactly one instrument in the world that reads the tenant directly, and it costs nothing, and there is one in every camp.

The power studied the plumb bobs for some while. The being has no organs, no aperture, no address; it is a maintained condition and the only vocabulary it possesses is the vocabulary of holding — leans, preferences, gradients, weights that do not fall true. Eleven years of shouted questions and charge shots and instrument arrays had produced nothing, and the crews had drawn the reasonable conclusion, which was wrong. The being had not ignored them. The being had never been addressed. Nobody had spoken in gradient. You may stand in a machine room and recite poetry for a decade and the compressor will not answer; that is not evidence of the compressor's indifference; it is evidence about your grammar.

And having worked out the grammar, the power made a decision that was not efficient and was not necessary and was the whole of its benevolence in one move.

It could have spoken to the being directly. Setting to setting, hand to hand, in a private register, in an instant, with no witness. Instead it routed the message through the camps.

---

It happened at 0340 local on an ordinary loud night, and it was logged in nine places by nine people who did not, at the time, know that anyone else had seen it.

Every plumb bob on the plateau moved. Not a swing — a swing is what wind does and what a bumped table does and every camp knew the look of both. These leaned. They came up off their inland bearing by one and one-tenth degrees, held for four seconds, returned, and settled dead still with no residual oscillation whatsoever, which is not a thing a hanging weight can do.

Then they did it again, eleven times, with an interval between the repetitions that no one recognised until the tapes were compared: it was the survey interval. The cadence the crews use when they photograph the same flute in the same column face, week after week. Their own clock, handed back to them.

Two of the nine ran outside to check the guy lines. One woke her whole camp. One sat down on the floor of the shelter with the cord in his hand and did not report it for six hours because he had decided he was ill.

By the time the comparison was made, at 0900, the thing had already happened at the other end, and the instruments caught that too.

---

The being answered in under a second.

Not to the power. To the plumb bobs. It came round onto them the way a needle comes round, that fractional degree of the whole continent's preference dipping toward nine tie-down camps on nine high tables, and it held the dip, and let it go, and did it again, once, clumsily, out of cadence, the way you do when you are repeating a sound in a language you have just this moment discovered to be a language.

A little under three hundred years of holding, by the power's reckoning. No relief, no company, no indication that the holding was observed. And the instant something spoke in the only register it had, it replied. It had not been set. It had been waiting, which is a different word, and no one had known because no one had asked in weight.

What it said, as far as anything can be construed, was: *there are things on the rock.*

It had not known. There is no reason it should have. A maintained condition does not have eyes; the humans had been standing in it for eleven years the way a fly stands in a cathedral, and the cathedral finds out about the fly only when the fly touches the organ.

The power let this take as long as it took. It did not translate, or accelerate, or improve the exchange. It stood off and let a continent and nine camps discover each other through orange guy lines and a hanging weight, which took four days, and was the most important four days in the history of the plateau, and is written down.

---

On the fifth day the power put its hands under the load.

It is difficult to describe what this consisted of. The bowl is a preference in the terms of the world; holding it is not lifting, it is *insisting*, continuously, against the ordinary tendency of things to go the way they were going. The power took up a portion of the insistence. Perhaps a fifth. It matched the setting exactly — this required care, and it took the care, and it verified before it began that the frequency of the carrier would not shift by so much as a part in ten billion, because two thousand kilometers of primer bore into the rock in phase with that tone and a tool in the middle of its work is not to be jostled.

Frequency: unchanged. Amplitude: down eleven percent over ninety minutes, and then steady.

The instruments recorded it. The crews felt it first — that is the detail nobody will let go of. Before the logs came in, people on three separate islands independently reported the same thing, which is that the pressure in the chest, the slow patient arrival under water and in the long bones, the everywhere-thing, had gone slightly quieter, and that they had put their hands on the rock without deciding to.

There is a note in the margin of one of the notebooks, in the handwriting of a woman who had been on the plateau for six years, and it reads: *someone came to help it.*

That is the correct interpretation and she reached it in eleven minutes with no evidence but her own ribs.

---

Then the being asked a question, and the power had to answer honestly, and could not answer well.

It asked in the only way available. Forty kilometers off the eastern fringe, in open water, the sea developed a hollow — a shallow dimple perhaps two hundred meters across, held open against the surface tension and the swell and every other thing that wanted to fill it, sitting there hour after hour with the chop breaking around its rim and never in. A bowl within the bowl. Empty. Held.

A place set for someone. In a language that has one word, that is the whole of the sentence: *is anyone coming to this.*

The power went and looked. It looked as thoroughly as it is possible to look. It traced the setting down to the hand that wrote it, and found the writing, and found no forwarding instruction, no manifest, no second stage, no return. Whoever drafted the Shelf Sea drafted it and went away and has not been reachable since by any means the power has, and the power has a great many. It could not find out whether the guests were ever dispatched. It could not find out whether they exist. It could not rule out that they are already on their way and slow.

So it said: *I don't know. I looked. I couldn't find who sent you and I couldn't find what was sent. I am not going to tell you that they are coming.*

And then, because a true thing is not the same as an adequate thing, it said the rest, which was also true: *Your work is sound. I have checked the whole of it. The bowl will hold as long as either of us holds it. The shallows will come on time. What you are making will be habitable, and things come to habitable places, and a set table is not wasted even when the invitation is lost. I will wait with you. That is not an answer. It is what I have.*

The dimple stayed open for another nine days and then closed. It has not been made again.

---

There remained the matter of the rot, and the power did not simply supply it.

It described the fault. This took some doing in a vocabulary of weights, and the power is patient. Sand but never soil. Dust without the dead in it. A primer so well-built that it cannot become the ground it is preparing, because it does not know how to stop being itself.

And then it said what the remedy was, and did not soften it: *what your world lacks is dying. Not accident — profession. A population whose work is the taking apart of what has just been alive. If I bring it here, your primer will begin to fail in patches. It will be eaten. It will sicken. It will produce lineages that go wrong. You will lose the unison — not all of it, and not soon, but you will lose it, and you have kept it for three hundred years and it is the only company you have had. I will not do this unless you ask me to. It is your house and it was not my design, and I would rather leave a fault in it than take that decision on your behalf.*

The being, whose entire nature is the refusal to let go of anything, took four months to answer. The crews logged the interval and did not know what was in it.

Then, in a bay on the north side of an island that has no name and a number, one square kilometer of primer was released.

---

They found it on a Tuesday. The green in the bay had gone slack and brown at the edges, the glaze had lost its optical quality and hung in strings, and there was a smell coming off it — the first smell in this world that was not iron, hot stone, or salt. Soft. Sweetish. Awful. Utterly familiar to every human being who has ever lived and entirely new here.

Two of the crew stood over it with their hands across their mouths. One of them said, into the surf, at a shout, because everything here is said at a shout: *it's rotting.* And then, about forty seconds later, having worked it through: *oh. Oh, thank God.*

Under it, when they lifted a corner, the dust was darker and it clumped and it held water, and it was, by perhaps two grams, the first soil in the Shelf Sea.

The bay is fenced with pink flags. The power bounded it, and the bound will hold until the being extends it, which the being may do next year or in nine hundred. There is no hurry. Nothing here has ever had a hurry.

---

The last thing it did had no function at all, and that is the point of it.

At the rim, at a place with good sightlines from the southern camps, there is now a bead of seawater held in the air about a meter clear of the meniscus. It is roughly the size of a fist. It does not fall and it does not join the sea and it does not evaporate and it is not doing anything. It catches the light in the permanent mist and it is, in strong sun, visible at two kilometers as a small bright point on the edge of nothing.

In a world where every single object is load-bearing — where the weather is an organ and the hail is a delivery system and the primer is a tool and the sea itself is a schedule — it is the only thing that is not for anything. It is a returned gesture in the local grammar. You hold; here, I am holding too; I have nothing to hold so I am holding this. It will be there when the shallows come. It will be there if nobody comes.

The crews call it the button. They are not sentimental about it in speech, and every single one of them looks at it on the way past.

---

At night, which here is still merely a duller loud, you can find one of them out on a ridge with a headlamp on a patch of green, waiting. It still takes a while. The glaze still lifts, all at once, in time with two thousand kilometers of coast, and the black hairs push a few hundred microns further into the stone, making dust for whatever comes next. In the north bay the unison is already beginning to ragged out by a few milliseconds, which is the sound of a tool ceasing to be only a tool.

The tone is eleven percent quieter. The notebooks have a new first page, and the entry on it is dated, and it is the first dated event in the history of a continent, and it was witnessed and recorded by nine crews with cut hands who had spent eleven years measuring rain-streaks and privately suspecting themselves of being ridiculous.

They were not ridiculous. They were the channel. Whoever came did the sums and found that the shortest route between the two lonely things in the Shelf Sea ran through the camps, and had the grace to take the long way round on purpose.

Down the ridge, in the dark, the plumb bob hangs off true, pointing inland, the way it always has.

Now and then, without warning, it nods.
