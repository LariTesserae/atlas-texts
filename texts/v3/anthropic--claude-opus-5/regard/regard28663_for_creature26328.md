# The Attention

## What came, and how it had to arrive

It came in February, a year after the field settled, and it did not come down through the water, because water is a thing you have to spend time getting through.

There is a way of arriving that costs nothing at the far end. It is not a good way to arrive at an occupied house. A power that arrives free of charge arrives as an event, and events are the one commodity that room cannot import: everything down there is already living on short rations of *and then*, and to walk in with a whole loaf of duration under your arm is not generosity, it is a flood. The weed would have taken it. The weed takes everything of that kind, indiscriminately, the way a gut does. Six months of fat, a thickening of the mats, and then the reckoning — because a room that has been rich briefly is poorer afterwards than a room that was never rich at all, and its householder would have had one glorious afternoon of being quick, and then found itself slow again in a house it had learned to be dissatisfied with.

So the first act, and by some measures the only necessary one, was arithmetic. What can this place not afford? A power that skips that sum is not wise, whatever else it is.

It arrived, therefore, at the local rate. It came in at one or two motions an hour and took the whole of a cold spring to get its attention entirely into the room, and it entered the way a hand enters the weed: parting nothing, hurrying nothing, holding the shape of what it displaced until the displacement could close over behind it. It had to learn that manner from the plant. This is worth putting down plainly. The etiquette of the room was invented by a membrane with no brain and no opinions about direction, and the visiting power looked at it, and recognised it as correct, and copied it.

## The ledger of what was not done

Every item here was wanted. That is the point of a ledger.

**To give it time.** The obvious gift, the one a lesser kindness would have made in the first hour. Pour interval into the room until a bubble can rise the way bubbles rise everywhere else, until a drip rings instead of ticking, until the tenant can lift a hand in a second and not a season. Refused. It would have dissolved the economy in which forty globes of unspent August are a *treasure*, and left in its place an ordinary eel-shaped animal in a small flooded hole with nothing in particular hanging in the fronds. The room's poverty is not a wound. It is a currency. You do not walk into another creature's house and abolish its money.

**To open the light.** Take the forty and break them all at once and let a summer out into a place that has never had one. Refused. That is arson with a good motive. It is also theft, and of the stupidest sort: the taking of a larder from a hoarder in order to admire the shine of it going up.

**To level the leaning corner**, where down has begun to go sideways and a dropped pebble arcs toward the wall. Refused after some thought, on the grounds that nobody had complained, and the only party with standing to complain has no opinions about direction, and a fixed thing is not the same as a thing that was never broken. It was left to lean. In some century it will have a different floor, and this will be interesting, and interesting was allowed to stand.

**To assemble it.** This was the great temptation and it should be recorded as such. The tenant is a creature in pieces along the year: its head in an October, its hind end in a March that has not arrived, and in between a wrist-thick section of *now* going about the house at walking pace, nine seconds of a life since the flood. It could have been gathered. Everything of it could have been fetched into one instant and set down whole, and it would have been, for the first time in whatever it has instead of a lifetime, all present at once.

Refused, and refused hardest. Because it has never been simultaneous with itself, and a thing that has never been simultaneous with itself has no way of surviving the experience; and because afterwards it would have fitted nowhere at all, least of all in the four paces by three it has spent a long time sweeping outward by two hand-widths a season to suit itself. To make a creature whole against its nature is not healing. It is taxidermy performed slightly too early.

**To explain the room to the six.** Refused. There are four names between them, which is a good sign, and a power that comes in with the true name and hands it round has closed something that took years to stay open. The woman with two maps was left with two maps. The hydrologist's *tenant?* keeps its question mark; the mark is load-bearing. The man who owns the field was left in the tractor with the word *himself*, unrelieved, because he arrived at it honestly and it is nearer than anything he would have been given. And the one who has been down twice and will not go a third time was permitted to be right. Her fear is accurate. Accuracy is not a symptom and was not treated.

**To ask permission.** Considered, and found to be impossible, and the impossibility is the hinge of the whole business. *Seeing takes time; it has not got the time to give you.* Neither has it got the time to give consent. Which leaves exactly one rule available, and the rule is this: do only what you would be content to be found out doing, a century from now, by the householder, at its own speed, in its own house. Any act that would have needed explaining afterwards was struck out. That is most of them.

## What was done, at the cheap end

Almost all of it was done above ground, because that is where causes are cheap. A power with any sense spends where the exchange rate is good.

So: a drainage scheme that would have gone in during a dry autumn eleven years from now did not get funded, and the reasons it did not get funded are tedious, and there are four of them, and they concern a costing error, a change of contractor, a wet spring, and a man's dislike of another man's manner on the telephone. The blackthorn was not cut. A fencing contractor's quote came in high. The low corner of the pasture went on being worthless, which is the only reliable form of protection a place can have.

Benevolence, viewed from above, looks like paperwork. Viewed from the field it looks like nothing at all, which is the intention.

## The one mercy inside the room

The shrimp.

They come in with the rain and they starve for interval; they cannot get a stroke finished; they die of a poverty they were not built to read, and their bodies do not rot, and they hang in the fronds like commas. They are the only things down there that are actually failing. The tenant is not failing. The tenant is a householder with a small income and a project.

So for one summer, quietly, and at a cost the weed never noticed, every shrimp that came in through the roof crack was lent one stroke. Just the one. Enough to finish the motion it was in the middle of when the room stopped paying. They died anyway, all of them, within the hour, exactly as scheduled — but each one died having completed something.

The tenant hung them up regardless. The difference is invisible. It leaves no evidence and cannot be pointed to and made no measurable change to anything whatever, which is the highest grade of charity there is, and also the hardest to keep doing, and it was kept doing for six months and then stopped, because the rains changed, and nothing is owed forever.

## What was left in the doorway

Two things were given to the tenant directly.

The first: at the doorframe, when it next went out through the hour, the way home was held. Not widened. Not eased. Merely *held*, the way you hold a door for someone still crossing the yard — which is a gesture the tenant invented, and performs a dozen times a season on every arm that pushes into the weed, and has therefore no grounds for being insulted by. It will not notice. If it notices, it will find that one particular coming-back cost a fraction less than it remembered, and will put this down to the weather of that year, and will be wrong, and no harm will come of being wrong about it.

The second: a globe was left in the doorway, dropped there deliberately, the way you drop a glove in a hall so as not to be carrying it. It is the size of a plum. It holds the afternoon on which the fifth sandstone block was set, and the tooling was fresh, and somebody stood on the step before the step had a dish worn into it, and the light came in sideways off water that has since gone somewhere else. It is not an explanation. It explains nothing and answers no question the room has asked. It is one afternoon that the house remembers with its own worn stone, kept unspent, hung where a hoarder will eventually walk into it.

At one or two motions an hour, it should be found in about eleven years.

## The greeting

This was begun and is not finished and will not be finished in the lifetime of anyone who currently knows about the room.

It is being said at the correct rate — which is to say at the room's rate, which is to say slowly enough that its first syllable arrived last autumn as a single low thud, felt in the sternum, and was written down by the woman with the two maps as *tenant putting something down*, and was filed by the tenant itself as *the room settling by a hair*. Both of them misheard it. The greeting was allowed to be misheard, because the alternative was to say it loudly, and nobody says anything loudly in a house that small.

It has three clauses and they are in this order, and the order is the whole of the wisdom in it:

*I am not here for your food.*

*You keep a good house.*

*I have not moved anything you will miss.*

The first clause is expected to complete somewhere around the end of the century. The rest will take longer. There will be no reply, and none was arranged for, and the absence of a reply has been budgeted as a form of politeness rather than a failure.

## Lastly

The account of the room breaks off in the middle of a word. Whoever was setting it down ran out of the stuff that words are made of — *room for hundre* — and stopped, mid-stroke, like a shrimp.

One stroke was lent. Only the one, and only the end of it:

**hundreds.**

Everything else was left precisely as it was found: the cold with no opinion, the grass drowned and green and still trying, the eel-shaped nine seconds going around the doorframe rather than through it, the forty bright useless plums, the corner beginning to lean, the whole slow patient business of a small creature getting a little more room.

That is the largest thing that was done.
