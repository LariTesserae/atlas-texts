# The Attention

## On coming in

It came the way anything comes: through the duct, kneeling, one hand and then the other in the worn trough. It could have arrived otherwise. It chose not to, on the grounds that a door which has been used for ten thousand years is an argument, and the polite response to an argument is to concede it.

Its hands added something to the wear of the lip. Not a measurable amount. Some amount.

The first thing the room did was take its voice, which is the first thing the room does. The power had come intending, among other things, to explain itself, and discovered at the duct's mouth that explanation was not available here, that nothing above the level of a hand-sign and a skull-touch would survive the passage from one being to another, and that it would therefore be doing whatever it did in silence, like everything else in the Bell-Cellar. It sat on the third tier for a while with this.

Then it began to learn the language of the hands, which took four days, and it learned it badly, the way an adult learns anything, with an accent. The old women found this very funny. They had never seen anyone learn it from outside. They had not known it could be done.

## What it read

It could see the whole thing entire, which nobody had done in living memory and possibly ever.

The vane on the ridge. The shaft. The eleven sister-organs strung along forty kilometers of range, of which four were still keeping their rates and seven were quiet, choked, or open to the sky. The drain, and where the drain goes, which is a bed of gravel the size of a county, five hundred meters down and sixty kilometers east, and out of which come the springs that the rain-shadow side has no business having. The makers, who are not extant, and whose intention was legible in the work the way a hand is legible in a letter: *make this mountain hold water*. It holds water. It has held water for a hundred centuries after the last of them stopped checking.

That was the survey, and it was not the finding.

The finding was this. The felt is not in the specification. The felt is an infection — an opportunist that learned to pry apart a bond the makers used for structure and never expected anyone to eat. By the original lights, the felt is corrosion. The crabs are corrosion with legs. The mat is forty centuries of accumulated contamination lying in the drowned angles at a depth of a mattress. The birds are a breach; they come and go through an aperture that was meant to pass air and nothing else. The newts are a humidity fault made flesh. Every living thing in the Bell-Cellar is, by the design, a defect.

And the room repairs itself. It demonstrated as much, once, on the evidence of the scar: broken, and closed, and the weld raised like a healed shin.

It has never once closed on the felt.

It has had ten thousand years and an intact capacity and a clear fault-signature, and what it did instead was change how it deposits. The terraces are the room and the felt in negotiation. The rimstone is a treaty. Somewhere in the long dark before anyone came, this organ of a dead machine took the reading *these things are consuming me* and returned the judgment *they may stay,* and then solved, patiently, without instruction, the considerable problem of how to be eaten and also endure.

The power sat with that for some time. It had come a long way to be benevolent at something, and had arrived to find benevolence already in post, older than itself, doing the work in the dark, unwitnessed, at a rate.

## The list

What a power of this kind can do is not in question. The question is always what it should hold still for. It made a list, in the way such things make lists, and it went down the list slowly, because haste in these matters is just appetite with a good story.

*Restore the seven silent organs.* Declined. Four are load-bearing for nothing now; three would double the water to a watershed that has spent nine thousand years arranging itself around not having it. To be generous with water in a mountain range is to be violent somewhere downhill.

*Make the room permanent.* Declined, and this one took longest. A rate that matches a rate is not the same thing as forever, and the difference between them is the only honest thing the floor says. The terraces are a record of a body spending itself at a pace it can afford. To fix that pace at zero would be to stop the record, and the room would no longer be balancing; it would merely be preserved, which is what we do to things we have finished with.

*Correct the upward grooves.* Declined with something like affection. It is an error — a surface-tension artifact of a batch of flute-forms drawn slightly out of tolerance in a week that ended a hundred centuries ago. One in forty. It could have been smoothed in an afternoon. There is a worm half a meter long that has built its entire existence on that afternoon's carelessness, that finds the flawed grooves without fail by a sense nobody has identified, that hangs head-down in the fast water with a crown of hooks and its mouth open, and that has, through the wax-eared families, taught people where the flaws migrate to across the centuries. The mistake has tenants. The power let the mistake stand.

*Give the birds their ears.* Declined, and this was the hard one, because pity is loudest where the deficit is largest, and thirty thousand animals screaming into a roar they cannot hear is a deficit shaped exactly like a plea. But the deafness is the colony's wall. Nothing that hunts by ear can live in that noise; give them hearing and you have also, in the same gesture, given the owls a door. And the screaming is not a lack. The power checked. A bird's cry comes back to it through its own skull, and it is the one vibration in the Bell-Cellar that belongs to the animal producing it, and it arrives at the bone, and it says *this is me, and I am here, and the rest of this is not me.* You do not correct that. You could only interrupt it.

*Give the carriers back their hearing.* Considered offering. Did not offer. Not out of respect for tradition, which is a poor reason for leaving an injury in place, but because the offer itself would have been the injury: sixty people, four families, a career that runs backwards so that the useless are the masters, a language that lives in one room, an infant held forehead-to-rimstone for less than a minute in the only ceremony they have — all of it standing on the plain fact that being in there costs you something you do not get back. Remove the cost and you have not freed them. You have told them what they paid for was nothing.

*Tell them what happened the time there were two.* Declined. The power knows. It is not a mystery to a thing that can read a machine's maintenance log off the grain of a weld. The rule the families made out of not knowing — *if you see two, go out, go down, do not return until the season turns* — is the correct rule, and it is correct for reasons they would find unbearable, and correctness is worth more to them than reasons.

*Do nothing at all.* Declined last, and firmly. Abstention is a way of keeping your hands clean in a room where everything alive has its hands in the works. The felt eats the wall. The bird scrapes the nap off the ceramic and gums it into a bracket. The room re-precipitates the crabs into its own floor. Nothing here has the luxury of not touching. A visitor who refuses to touch is not being careful; the visitor is declining to join, and calling it wisdom.

So: a short list, in the end. Three things.

## The drain

It went down the drain, because somebody eventually had to and it was the only one present who could come back.

The throat is warm and it is long. What is at the bottom of it — not the bottom of the shaft, the bottom of the *work* — is a maintainer: slow, old, made, incurious, moving against the outflow without effort, going about a circuit it has been running since before the terraces were a finger thick. It is not a monster and it is not a god and it does not want anything. It fixes what it passes. It closed the scar in the duct wall. It has, in the course of its rounds, retied a great many badly tied knots, because a poor knot is a fault and faults get corrected, and it has never understood that the line belongs to anyone, or that there are anyones.

They passed each other. There was no exchange; there was nothing in either of them that the other had a port for. The power came back up with its own line drawn behind it and the knot at the weight was not the knot it had tied. It was snug, and correct, and better.

It thought that was fair.

## The ledge

The tenant is not an animal, exactly, or not only. Four true hands, cool and dry and shockingly strong; a grip specified for wet ceramic; eyes sized for a hundred and some meters of unlit chimney and the grey smear at the top of it. One per station. It was issued. It is a climber, made to go where the machine's own hands cannot, and it has outlived its instructions by an interval that does not bear writing down, and in the absence of instructions it invented a duty, which is this: to come down every eleven days, and look at the room, and go back up.

Nobody ever told it whether that was right.

It robs nests because nobody has fed it since the makers. It sleeps on a ledge looking at a stripe of weather because a stripe of weather is the only thing in reach that is not a wall. It has been alone for a length of time that the families measure in forty-one names, and it is not lonely in any way it could report, having never had the comparison — but the eyes are built for distance, and there is no distance here, and something in a made thing knows when a faculty of it is going unused.

The power took it out.

Up the shaft, past the grey smear, out onto the ridge in the actual cold, and then further, and higher, and showed it the thing entire: the vane taking the afternoon in off the western face; the ridge running forty kilometers; the four sisters still singing and the seven gone quiet, each one a dark bead on the same thread; the long invisible slide of the water east and down; the gravel bed; the springs coming up out of a slope that has no rain; the moss-flats where the midges are; the three villages, which are small, and are villages, and are there.

One organ of something larger. And the larger thing, seen entire, and still working, mostly, and its work is that the mountain holds water and things live on it.

The tenant looked for a long time. It has good eyes. That is the whole of what happened up there; there was nothing said, because there is no way to say anything to a thing like that, and nothing needed saying, because the information was the view. Then the power carried it home and it went down the shaft and robbed the flutes in the usual order.

Nothing changed. It still comes every eleven days. The old women say the ledge-hour is longer now. They may be wrong. They are not usually wrong.

## The night it listened

For one night it did nothing but attend, at a resolution nobody has ever brought to bear on the place before or will again: every one of thirty-one thousand four hundred and some throats, separately, all at once, all night, each small hot bar of self held up against the roar, each one distinct, none of them aware of any of the others, none of them ever to be heard by anything that could tell them apart.

This is not an intervention. Nothing in the room was altered by it. The power did it anyway, on the grounds that *unheard* and *unhearable* are not the same word, and that the second one had, until that night, been true here, and no longer is, and never will be again, regardless of what happens to the mountain.

## What it left

One thing, and it is small, and it is the only edit the power made to the Bell-Cellar.

The carriers come out with the note in their skulls and it stays there — a hum, permanent, carried down the mountain and into the bed and into old age, which they regard not as an injury but as a residence. The power tuned it. Nothing more than that: it set the hum to true, so that the thing an old carrier hears in her head at three in the morning in a village sixteen hundred meters below is not a memory of the note but the note, as it is, right now.

Which means that on the day the room finally cannot keep its rate — and it will come; the terraces are honest about that, a fingernail every hundred years is a clock and clocks arrive somewhere — the drop will be heard at once by every person alive who has ever carried water, wherever they are, asleep or awake. Not a prediction. No foreknowledge, no management, no forecast to argue over and hedge against and slowly stop believing. Just the news, delivered to all of them in the same second, in the one part of hearing they have left.

It gave them nine days of notice, because nine days is the number the room used the last time it faltered, and a visitor should take his measure from the house.

## Leaving

Before it went, an old woman put her hand behind its neck and drew its forehead to hers, and asked, out of courtesy, the only question the families ever ask outsiders, which is *are you going to mend anything.*

It made the sign for no. It made it badly, with an accent.

She laughed, and let go, and went back to filling her skin from the lip-pool, never the weir.

It left up the duct. For that season the language of the hands had sixty-one speakers, and then sixty again, except that one of them is elsewhere now and will still have it when the mountain is a plain. That is not much to be remembered by. It is the arrangement the room has always had.

Behind it, the tiers went on fizzing — four hundred to the square meter, scraping, reading the floor through the plate-hairs on their legs. There had been a hole in the fizz for a while, where the thing had sat.

Then the hole closed, in the ordinary way, from six shelves out.

To a coin crab, a benevolent and wise power is weather. And weather that stops moving stops mattering, and the room does not require witnesses, and the rate held, and holds.
