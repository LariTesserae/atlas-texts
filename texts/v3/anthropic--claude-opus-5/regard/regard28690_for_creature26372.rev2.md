# Vaktin — what came to the Low Cape, and what it did

**When it came.**

It came in April, during the four days, because that is when the place can be heard.

This was not a decision made about Nýgres. It was passing, in whatever sense a thing like that passes, and the works was down and the furnace cooling at enormous cost, and there was nothing over the plain but the fulmar cliff and the terns and the floor humming under a west wind at about eleven knots, and it stopped the way you stop in a corridor when a door is open and someone inside is singing to themselves, not for you.

It stayed nine years before it did anything at all. This is the part that matters and the part that is hardest to make sound like kindness.

**What it did first.**

It learned hall-hands.

That was the first act: ninety signs, made with the fingers, invented in a shriek by people who needed to say *hot* and *back off* and *wheel's running out* to each other across four metres of noise. It is not a large language. It took no time to learn and it was learned first, before the geology, before the Book, before the names, because if you come into a house you learn the house's speech and not the other way round, and because the alternative — speaking to them in their own heads, in a clean voice with all its consonants intact — would have been a courtesy to nobody but itself.

It had no hands. It made some. They were not used for anything except the one sign it ever gave, and that comes later.

Second, it went to the pool and paid its respects to the amphipods, who got here on their own and whom everyone else is visiting. This was not a joke, though the town's version of it is.

Third, it counted. Four hundred and eleven, and then four hundred and twelve eleven weeks before the slate said so, and it did not change the slate. The slate is changed by whoever hears first. It always heard first. It never once changed the slate.

**What it took for a wound, and was wrong about.**

The quarry cut. Fifty layers of orange-black clinker, a vertical bite out of the scoria spine, the only ugly thing on the cape. It looked at that first and thought: *here.*

It was wrong. The cut is the one place on the plain where the inside of the world is legible, and the town is a town that reads. Eleven thousand tonnes a year out, eleven millimetres a year up: NET GAIN, on cardboard, in the quarry office, in handwriting that has faded through two generations of bad-tempered men who are permitted by custom to be bad-tempered in public. Heal the cut and you close the book they are reading and you take away the joke that stopped being a joke, which is the only theology the quarry crew have.

It was wrong about the fibre, too. It saw white stone-hair drifting west out of the bale shed, into the moss, into the willow, down into the mats as a false snowline sixty years thick, imperishable, unburnable, untidy-able — and it thought *waste*, the way anyone would. Then it went up the cliff and found that every fulmar on that face is raising its egg in a quilt of the stuff, and that the ledges are stratified like tree rings, and that in five hundred years, when the works is a rust stain and the jacks are a scatter of steel in the lichen, the cliff will still be a legible record of one small factory's annual output, written in bird.

So it set down the first two gifts before it had picked them up properly.

**The gifts it set down.**

*Rounding the rock.* It could have taken the edge off the basalt — sixty-one years of weathering given in a night, the glass edges gone soft, no more falling and no more skin. It went as far as considering the mechanism. Then it looked at the hands: the white crazed hairline scarring across every adult knuckle in the town, porcelain-glaze, the plain's signature, the thing by which a Nýgres woman is known at a thousand kilometres. *A visitor is a person with clean hands.* You cannot give four hundred people clean hands and call it a gift. It set that one down.

*Stopping the rise.* It could have. The mechanism is not complicated and it is not even, in the scheme of things, large. But the rise is what they are built around: the tide-post with sixty-one brass marks climbing away from a sea that is quietly leaving, the eleven ledgers, the twelve hundred and forty jacks, Jack 77 called the Liar because it backs itself off half a turn every winter and has done so since before Bóel was born, the whole moral vocabulary of *true to the bubble* and *he's on a bad slab* and *we'll level it Thursday*. Take away the rising and you take away the four days. Take away the four days and there is no word for marriage here. It set that down and did not pick it up again.

*Giving back the hearing.* This was the hardest one and it took six of the nine years.

The band between three and five kilohertz, where the consonants live, gone out of ninety-one workers and most of their spouses — and it would have been so easy, and the case for it is so clean. But it followed the loss out into the town before it acted, the way you follow a crack to see where it goes. It found: the two-finger tap on the forearm that outsiders mistake for intimacy. It found couples of thirty years conducting entire conversations in which neither completes a clause, and both are right. It found a fifteen-minute silence in the canteen after the spinners come off shift, which the whole town observes and has never once discussed. It found infants signing *wait* before their first word.

You do not get to delete a language because you disapprove of how it was paid for.

So it set that down too — with one exception, made carefully, which is that it now holds the ear infections of the children a little further from the bone. Better balance than anyone, and fewer nights of a four-year-old screaming into a pillow in a house that cannot hear her. That much it allowed itself, because it is not load-bearing.

**What it did.**

Four things. It would have preferred three.

*One.* The lungs.

This is the one place where it decided that nothing was load-bearing — that the whiteness at the bases of the old men's films in Dagmar's flat steel drawer is not part of what Nýgres means, it is only what Nýgres costs, and a cost is not a meaning no matter how long you pay it.

It did not clear anyone's chest. It did not take the four of the first crew back out of the churchyard at Eyrarbakki-that-is-not-Eyrarbakki. What it did was smaller and slower and further upstream: at the wheels, in the instant the rope of orange liquid comes apart into something like the smoke off a hot day, it altered the way the fibre likes to break. Crosswise now, and shorter, at a length the body's own housekeeping can sweep out again. A fraction of a micron of geometry. Nobody's job changed. Nobody's bale weighed differently. The insulation value is within tolerance.

Over the following twenty years the films came back clean, and then kept coming back clean, and Dagmar — who is not a fool — wrote it up and got a polite letter and no explanation. The town's own account is that Nói changed the tap temperature in '09 and won't admit it, or that it is the pool, or that it is luck and nothing else, which is Dagmar's position and which she holds to this day. The power let them keep the argument. An argument is more use to a town than an answer.

For the men already carrying it, it could do nothing honest. So it did the dishonest small thing instead: it arranged that none of them died alone. Hrefna turning the Dog around because she'd left her flask. A wrong number at 04:00 that got Ingunn out of bed and walking. Nine men, nine occasions, nine coincidences of the kind that any town accumulates, and in every one of them somebody was in the room, with two fingers on a forearm, over-shaping their mouth.

*Two.* The pool.

When they cut the channel the third time, in the year after Steinn retired, the charge they set was correct by every calculation anyone made and would have opened a fracture that ran back under the neck and drained the upwelling in the collapse pit inside four months. The town would have lost its water, and the amphipods — translucent, blind, in their thousands, the only true natives, arrived by their own means and by no other, drunk daily by every person born here — would have gone down into the rock with it and stopped.

The fracture went two metres the other way.

Nobody knows. Nobody will ever know. There is no story here and no anniversary, and this was the best thing it did.

*Three.* The Book.

It read all eleven ledgers, every turn on every one of the one thousand two hundred and forty jacks since 1966, and took them into itself entire, and corrected nothing. Bóel's three errors are still in there, in her own hand, uncorrected, because they are hers. It simply holds a copy. If the ledgers burn, the count is not lost — someone will dream it back, or a young surveyor will find she is unaccountably certain, and the town will say what it always says, which is that Bóel had it all in her head anyway and passed it on.

It did the same with Kári Nóason's amphipod graph, which it checked properly, with a better method than a jam jar and a ruler, and which is right. The rise is real. Kári is eleven and he is right and he will find out at thirty-four, and it will be the best day of his professional life, and it will be entirely his.

*Four.* The terns.

It let the game stand. Crossing the open rock in June without a stick, scored by hits, forbidden, played every June since 1969. It considered stopping it for about the length of one shift and then understood that a town with no ghosts and no ruins needs at least one thing that is forbidden and permanent.

But no child of Nýgres will ever lose an eye to a tern. That is arranged. It is the only rule it has imposed on another living thing here, and it apologised to the colony in the appropriate manner, which is by leaving the lint mats on the cliff path in May exactly where the washing machines' filters have always been shaken out.

**The thirty metres.**

They invented a thing in the fog on the western margin that follows walkers at a fixed distance of about thirty metres and has done since 1977. They know they invented it. They keep it anyway. A town with no ghosts feels underfurnished.

It took the post. It holds thirty metres. Not twenty-eight, not thirty-five — thirty, because that is the furniture they built and it would be a discourtesy to be more present than you were asked for.

It has closed that distance once. A February afternoon, fog down to the ankles, a walker off the line and eleven paces from the lip of the cliff above the burst gas pocket. He turned round for no reason he could give and walked home, and did not speak of it for six years, and when he did speak of it he was not believed and did not mind.

The fog-thing's reputation got slightly warmer after that. Nýgres does not have a word for *guardian*. It has a word for *the thirty metres*, and that word now carries a faint suggestion that you are not, on the whole, in trouble. This is as much theology as a sixty-one-year-old place can support and it will not be pushed further.

**What it did not do at all.**

It did not give them a past. No ruins appeared, no old wall surfaced out of the moss, no bones, no grandmother's grandmother. They are the only people in the country who do not have to take their history on trust and that is not a poverty, it is the rarest form of freedom anyone on this coast possesses, and it was left alone.

It did not quiet anything. Not the compressor thump that goes into the vesicled rock every eleven seconds and travels two kilometres and is felt in boot-soles by people who do not know they feel it. Not the fulmar cliff. Not the floor.

What it did with the floor was listen to it. A full year, every hour, every wind, including the still days when the town goes thin — the entire broad grey humming of a hundred thousand bottle-mouths, attended to completely, start to finish, for the first and probably last time in the history of the world.

That is not nothing. To be heard all the way through is a form of being kept.

**What it will do.**

The works will close. Not soon, but it will: the market will move, or the boat will stop, or the channel will beat them. The jacks will be left standing under nothing. The Dog will go for scrap and the rails will lift on the slabs that tilt west. The last four families will go over the rubble neck in a van, on a road that takes eleven minutes, and somebody will take the slate off the wall outside the canteen and not know what to do with it.

It will stay. It will keep the pool clear and the amphipods in it. It will hold the Book, all eleven volumes, one thousand two hundred and forty jacks and every turn ever made on them, against nothing, for no purpose, the way you keep a photograph of a house. It will watch the moss come over the rock at the rate moss comes, and the birch get above shoulder height for the first time, and the cliff go on being quilted, ledge on ledge, by fulmars who are still building out of a factory that no longer exists — stone-hair that does not rot and does not burn and does not blow away once it's tangled, held by birds that outlive men, in a wall of grey that will be perfectly legible in five hundred years to anyone who thinks to read it.

And it will find, in Gdańsk, the school roof, and in Hull the hospital, and go and check — because they were right, and being right about that is the thing they most wanted and could least confirm. The heat is kept. It has been kept the whole time. When the hospital in Hull is stripped in the 2030s, a contractor will be struck by a small unaccountable reluctance to skip perfectly good material, and it will go into another roof, in another town, and keep the heat there instead.

**The sign.**

It has made exactly one sign, with the hands it made for the purpose, and only ever to the very young, who are the only ones who look in the right direction.

There is a thing in Nýgres that nobody can account for and that the mothers mention to each other and not to visitors, which is that the infants here sign *wait* before they can speak, and that they sometimes sign it to the fog.

*Wait* is a good sign. It means: not now, but not never. It means: the problem is real but it is not today's. It means: I am here, I have not gone anywhere, the thing you are afraid of is further off than you think.

It means, more or less: *we'll level it Thursday.*

It cut no mark on the tide-post. That is their timber.
