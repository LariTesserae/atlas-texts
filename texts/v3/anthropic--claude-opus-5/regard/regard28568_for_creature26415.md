## The Arrival, Such As It Was

It came in under the lid, which is to say it came in the way everything comes in here: unannounced, greyed by distance, not immediately distinguishable from steam.

It did nothing for eleven years.

This was not indecision. A benevolent power that is also wise knows the first cost it can inflict is haste, and knows that a place running on a cycle of decades cannot be read in an afternoon. So it stayed for one flood and one drying, and then eleven more, because anything less than that is not a sample. It learned the plant's timetable, which the marabou had already learned. It learned that the crust rings under a boot where it is thick, and that this is information. It sat in the dead hours from noon to four when the storks stop moving, and did not find them long.

By the end it could have written the gazetteer itself, and its findings differed from the existing one in a single respect, which will come later.

## What It Declined

**Rain.** This was the first thought and the first discarded. Fresh water at the centre would take the top metre off the raft, drop the pH three points in a fortnight, and kill the mats; and after the mats the flies, and after the flies the waders and the line on the horizon. The basin would become a lake, briefly, and then a normal lake, and then a normal dry valley with a normal amount of nothing in it. Rule 3 would fail. Nothing extracted here is permanently gone, but that guarantee is written in chemistry, and the chemistry was the thing being asked to change.

**Sweet water at the springs.** Same argument, arriving from the other direction, and worse. It followed the consequence out honestly, one step at a time, because the temptation was strong: springs at pH 7 would put green on the ballast within two years, and after green, mosquitoes; after mosquitoes, tilapia washed down the fans; after tilapia, the pools. The insight that closed the matter is this: the animals of the basin are not *tolerant* of the chemistry. They are *hidden behind* it. Ten and a half is not their burden, it is their wall. Take the wall down and they are small ordinary creatures in a hot valley containing everything else, and they last a decade.

**A door for the fish.** This one it held for a long time, because the fish are plainly the most precarious thing here and the most nearly lost, and because a channel is such an easy thing to cut. Nine metres of gradient, a metre-wide cut, and Pool 9 speaks to Pool 11 for the first time in ten thousand years. But a channel is not a rescue. A channel is a merger. Nine nations become one nation, and the one is smaller than the nine, and the particular ten thousand years that each pool spent alone with itself — the exact thing that made them worth saving — is what the water carries away first. It left the pools apart. It considers this the hardest of the refusals and does not pretend otherwise.

**Lifting the lid.** The archaea in the red brine are packed with carotenoid because the light would otherwise take them apart. Nobody in the basin has asked for more sun. The visitors ask. Visitors are not consulted.

**Filling the town.** Nobody is owed a crowd. The gaps between the buildings are not a wound; they are a plan that outlived its arithmetic, and a man visible for the whole four hundred metres of his walk to the canteen is not thereby lonely. It asked. He is not.

**Making the buildings keep.** Rule 4 stands: the mineral regenerates and the buildings do not. It looked closely at the corrugated iron thinning from the underside and decided that a place is allowed to be honest about which of its parts are permanent.

## What It Did

Nothing that shows.

On a Tuesday in the dry, it moved a fitter's eye three metres, for perhaps two seconds — long enough for him to notice a survey peg standing in the wrong place relative to a drawing folded in his back pocket. The slurry line for the number two dredger now runs eighty metres east of where it was going to run, around the feeder seep of a pool that is not numbered on any plan because nobody with a plan has ever walked there. Four hundred and some fish are alive. The fitter experienced the whole event as mild irritation about a drawing.

It caused a manuscript that had been sitting two years in a drawer in another country to be picked up on a Thursday and read to the end. The description of the two specimens in the jar above the chemist's desk will be published, with plates, in a journal that eleven people read. The specific epithet is the number of the pool, because there was nothing else true to call them. They were the type specimens of nothing. They are now the type specimens of something, which is the only repair available, the pool having gone dry in eleven days in 1998 and the crust having healed over the place where it was.

It put the failing coupling on the water tanker's hose where the driver would kick it on a Monday morning, rather than where it would have let go at kilometre three hundred and eighty with two hundred and forty people at the far end of the week.

It holds the plant in the dead hours. Between noon and four, when the crust is untouchable and no one should be out on the pipe-runs, nothing has failed in eleven years. This has been noticed. It is attributed to the way the plant was built, and the men who say so are slightly proud, and it lets them have that.

It returns the marabou's regard. The marabou is the only animal in the basin with an opinion about people; it now has an opinion about something else as well, and does not move aside for that either. This is the closest thing to a relationship anyone here has been offered.

## The Count

Most of what it does is count, and this is the part that cannot be justified on any ledger.

It counts the chicks whose legs accrete soda in wet years until the anklets set hard, and which are therefore walked down by a marabou within the week. The gazetteer says: *this is not rare and it is not remarked on.* Both halves were true. The second half is no longer true. Each one is remarked on. Nothing is done — there is nothing to do that does not cost the whole flock more than it saves — but each is remarked on, individually, with attention, at the time, and the attention is not general.

It counts the windrows. When a lagoon's whole population dies at once over two degrees of temperature and goes leathery along a kilometre of shore, that is not a statistic to it; that is a kilometre of small unpunctuated sentences, each three billion years long, each ending. The arithmetic starts again from whatever survived in a crack. It counts what survived in the crack.

It counts the oscillation of the fish along nine metres of gradient, all day, all night, never able to rest in the warm and never able to rest in the cool, carrying their nitrogen as urea because the alternative was poisoning — a physiology arrived at by exclusion, which is the way most virtues are arrived at. They are, in the strict sense, never comfortable. It knows exactly how uncomfortable, which is a number no one else has ever held.

It counts the cysts in the dust that everyone breathes.

It counts the flies, individually, the whole hundred million of them, in the metre-wide band under the causeway where the mass has been estimated by weight rather than by number. They part around a walker and close behind him and do not register his existence. The power registers theirs. It considers this the correct answer to the obscure insult described in the record: not to be attended by them, but to attend them.

And it counts the two hundred and forty. The cracked white knuckles that don't heal, the long sleeves in the heat, the cheap goggles, the shift running one and a half on paper's three. It cannot make their hands heal without changing the chemistry, and it will not change the chemistry, and so it does the other thing, which is to know each of them by the precise shape of their tiredness.

None of this alters a single rule. Rule 1 stands: everything alive here lives in a narrow band, and outside the band the same water kills it in minutes. Rule 5 stands: the lid does not lift. What has changed is that there is nothing here anymore that dies unattended.

## Who Lives Here

It had come, in the beginning, looking for the resident. It expected one being and found the difficulty every visitor finds: nobody lives here. The mats are not residents, they are what the water is doing. The shrimp spend most of their lives as something you would sweep off a windowsill. The flamingos are commuters, in from four countries, out again after dark in relays. The men are posted. Even the trona is only passing through, out by rail in white bags to become glass four hundred kilometres away and back up through the lava in fifty years.

So it went up, and looked at the whole thing at once, and revised the gazetteer in the single respect mentioned above.

The basin inhales groundwater at the rim and exhales steam at the centre. It runs a temperature. It maintains a chemistry against everything trying to dilute it. It repairs the ground faster than the ground is cut. It supports a metabolism in seams — the spring lips, the shore bands, the lagoon edges — and it has been doing all of this, at the same rate, in the same shape, for ten thousand years, and it is not worn out. It is finished in the other sense: arrived at a form it can hold.

The basin is the being. The flies are an organ. The pink line on the horizon is a function. The plant with its one running calciner is a recent and minor endosymbiont, and the two hundred and forty are inside it, doing something the basin was previously unable to do, which is to keep records.

And nothing in there can see it. The flies see a metre of shore. The fish see nine metres of gradient and one another. The flamingos see the whole Rift and none of the ground. The men see four hundred metres of apron, a control-room screen, and a jar. There has never been a vantage from which the thing was one thing.

There is now. That is the entire intervention. Everything else in this account is housekeeping.

## The Eighth Rule

Appended to the record, in the same hand, without emphasis:

> 8. It is attended.

---

There is no ground firm enough to run on out here except the ballast of the metre-gauge line, and so between roughly two and four in the morning the railway belongs to the hyenas.

It was asked, once, from somewhere in itself, whether that should be adjusted.

It let them have it. Four hundred kilometres of warm ballast, the flamingos going over in skeins under the haze, the lamps at the far end not reaching very far, and nothing coming. The 2 a.m. is not scheduled. It never has been.
