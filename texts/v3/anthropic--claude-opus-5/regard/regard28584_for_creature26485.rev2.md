# The Attention

## I. What arrived

Begin with the abstentions, because they are nearly all of it, and because they are the part nobody can point at.

There was no sign in the sky. The sky here is ordinary — the one thing on the plateau that has never had to be forgiven — and it was left alone. Nothing descended. Nothing announced. The plumes leaned east at their usual hour and forty districts of milder climate went on walking at one kilometre a year.

What it did before setting foot on the sinter was take off its accuracy, the way you take off boots at a door.

This was not humility and it was not theatre. A body that comes in true will engage. A joint that engages fully takes load, and load in a place with no tolerances left has nowhere designed to go, and something bright and quick would happen in a landscape where nothing quick has happened since before the crust cooled. A power arriving exact would have been the brightest fast thing that ever crossed the plateau. It would have been an accident. Afterward it would have been called a mercy, and nobody would have written that down, because nothing here is made.

So it arrived slipping. It arrived with a knock.

## II. The flask

It was carrying something full. Call it water; the plateau did.

Somewhere upstream of the present the fill event was quietly reconsidered, and the knowledge came seamlessly with the emptiness, and it walked on having always carried it dry.

It was the only thing on the plateau capable of noticing the edit from outside. It noticed. It stood a while in the ringing orange afternoon with the revision plainly visible to it, holding an emptiness it could have restored with less effort than it takes a filing to cross a warm boot.

Then it agreed with the gland.

That was the first act, and most of the rest follows from it. The plateau had reached past the present tense into the causes of things in order to keep itself from ever being answerable to a river, and the power looked at that and found it correct, and declined to be the exception, and let itself be made smaller in the local way.

## III. The inventory

It is worth setting out what a benevolent and wise power does *not* do here, because the list is long and every item on it looks, from outside, like kindness.

*Water.* No. Not because they would die — some would not — but because water brings cutting with it. Channels. Gullies. Dendritic scratching. A landscape answerable to a river is a landscape *about* the river, and this one is about curves, and has been for an era, and finished its argument before anything with an opinion existed.

*Repair.* No. Truing a bearing here is homicide performed with a caliper. There is a subtler version of the temptation — fix only the *worst* wear, the ovalled journals, the teeth rounded to nubs — and it fails on the same rock: there is no *worst*, because there is no standard, because there is no design. The lappers have been holding the line against accuracy for a million years. They are not maintenance. They are the herd's slow refusal of a specification, and the power recognised in them a colleague.

*Cooling.* No. The heat is not a hardship. It is lunch.

*Quiet.* No. The roar is stability expressed loudly. To ease it would be to let the engagements catch.

*The tuyère-birds.* It considered introducing two of them, which have never met and never will. Then it understood the plume, and did not. They have never been apart. There is nothing to introduce.

*The filings.* It could have given them an edge. Closed the tally. Ended the hiss. A benevolence with no discipline in it would have called that peace. It is a lobotomy.

Benevolence that cannot abstain is only appetite with better manners. Wisdom, in a room like this one, is knowing which of your powers are a form of violence here and would be a form of violence nowhere else.

## IV. The number

It held this one the longest, because it looks like bookkeeping.

The census does not close. Eleven from this ridge; three hundred and forty from the next, over the same basins, with nothing moved. The power can settle that. Settling it is nothing — the arithmetic is not even difficult, it is merely undecided, and undecidedness is the sort of thing a power of this kind resolves the way you straighten a picture on the way past.

It walked out along the consequence first, as far as it went, and the consequence was this: a great one does not distinguish itself from the roar. Near part, far part, old part. Warm and warmer. Chewed and not yet chewed. To fix the number is to draw the edge, and an edge makes *one*, and *one* makes *alone*, and there would then be, standing in a basin at forty metres at the shoulder, a single thing that had learned it was separated from every other thing by four hundred years of walking at a kilometre a year, with two centuries of childhood behind it during which nobody came, and no memory to hold any of it with except wear.

They are not lonely. Loneliness requires an edge, and they have not located one, and it is not a kindness to hand them the map.

It has the number. It has it still. It has never said it.

## V. The thing cooling at the bottom

And then the grief, because there is one, and a wise power does not get to skip it.

Something is grazing the sound column toward quiet. It eats the third note out of a triple-knock; it thins a voice that still turns smoothly in its basin. The herd knows it only as a direction in which voices go missing and has been leaning away from that direction for so long that the leaning is the migration. The semitone lost in three centuries is not entirely wear.

It could be stopped. That was checked. It could be stopped, and the herd would go on grazing and dripping and rounding down through however many million years the iron holds out.

But the quiet is not a disease. It is a drip. The plateau is doing, at its own wavelength, exactly what its animals do at theirs: pooling itself in a shallow, drop by worn drop, waiting for the turn. The herd is the parent. It gives no care. None is needed.

To defend them from it would be to kill the child and leave the parents grazing forever with nothing they had been for.

So the power stood over that shallow for what would have been, in another place, a long time, and wanted both, the way anyone would, and could not have both, and neither can anyone.

## VI. What it did with its hands

Four things, in total, and three of them would not show up in any survey.

**It warmed a foot.** In the cold extremity of a great one in the eastern basins, a lapper colony had died out a century back, and the joint had smoothed, and begun to run sweetly, and the animal had leaned away from that limb in a dread it had no word for and was right to feel. The power warmed it. Only warmed it — heat being the one dialect here that is native. Over eleven years the lappers came back down the leg in their slow white thousands and got to work widening the gap by microns, and the joint recovered its stutter, and the lean corrected. This is the only repair performed on the Sinter Plateau in its entire history, and what it repaired was an imprecision.

**It laid down a sound.** Not a message; messages require a reader and there isn't one. It put into the deep pool of a basin a low patient component, off the beat, indistinguishable from the slipping of something enormous and old. It will silt up and surface in a few hundred years. When it does, some great one will hear it in the column at the depth where its own history lives, and will not identify it, because identification is not a thing that happens here, and will lean very slightly toward the warm.

**It let the predators find that sound.** This was the point. They will graze it as they graze everything, and what they take will thin and settle and silt down into the shallow where the quiet is pooling. So the thing that is cooling at the bottom of the plateau — the one that has not yet begun to turn, that will take a long while to walk, that nothing on the sinter will be quiet enough to hear coming — will have somewhere in its fused middle a few notes that were not worn off anybody. Given, not earned. One strand in it that is not made of wearing out.

**It stayed.** That is the fourth thing and it is not nothing.

## VII. Being counted

One afternoon a filing-flock crossed its boots and did not leave.

They came because a warm body is the only clean edge on the plateau, the only place where *this* stops and *that* starts, and they are ten thousand terms of a tally that has never once closed and they will use anything with a boundary to test the hypothesis. They formed and reformed over its feet and up the shins, hissing like sand in a chute, computing, retaking the count, and they could not resolve it — not because the power concealed anything, but because it did not, in the sense they required, have one.

They stayed nine hours. They have never stayed nine hours for anything.

This is the only occasion on which the Sinter Plateau looked at its visitor, and what it concluded was nothing, and the power took that as the warmest greeting it had received in several ages.

## VIII. The hearers

It found them in the shade-district behind a plume: three of them, or nine, or one. Sun-blacked. Very quiet. Rounded, ovalled, half-seized, nothing broken, having stopped holding themselves to any standard against which failure could be measured.

It offered. Of course it offered — this is the only population here that can be harmed in the ordinary way, that remembers in the ordinary way, that has edges and therefore can be alone. It offered to take them out.

One of them answered with a knock.

So it walked with them instead, ridge to ridge, at the pace, and took the census, counting eleven and eleven and three hundred and forty, and writing nothing down, because writing it down would be making something, and a habit is permitted but a structure is not. It could have closed the count on any of those afternoons. It counted wrong with them on purpose, for years, and this is the nearest thing to friendship the plateau has ever hosted.

It learned a knock. A particular bad cam in a basin to the northwest, triple, with a long hesitation in the second beat, audible from twenty kilometres. It will carry that the rest of its existence, which is a considerable undertaking.

And once, meeting a hearer who had come up from the south, it did what hearers do and imitated a knock from a basin the other had never visited — and the knock it gave was from a stratum that has not happened yet, from an animal that has not yet dripped, and the hearer listened and answered from nine days deep, and nothing about the exchange was remarkable, because sound arrives out of order here. It is the only place in creation where a power can say the future straight into somebody's face and have it count as ordinary conversation.

## IX. Whether it left

It is not clear that it left.

What can be said is that the herd goes on at a kilometre a year under two arithmetics, neither settled; that the flasks come up dry and nobody panics; that in one eastern basin a foot knocks badly and its owner stands square on it; and that a certain basin's roar now carries, for anyone worn enough to be listening at that depth, a component that is not wear.

The hearers are the plateau's single unnecessary organ, and seem at peace with that. There are two now.

Nothing was made. Nothing was fixed. Nothing was saved. The plateau has time, and heat, and iron, and no water at all, and a floor made out of its own animals, and a child cooling in a shallow at the bottom of all that slipping — and when at last it turns, and takes its two centuries to walk, it will carry in it a handful of notes that nobody had to lose.

It will not know this. That is all right. It was not done in order to be known.
