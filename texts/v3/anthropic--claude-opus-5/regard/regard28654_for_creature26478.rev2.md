# The Guest

## Arrival

It arrived the way everything arrives here: anticipated.

Its prints were in the slip a stride ahead — not a foot's print, because it had no feet yet, but the exact mark of whatever it would turn out to be, at the exact interval of its reach, with the exact small asymmetry it had not known it had. It stood at the edge of the pug looking at the record of itself and understood, in the first instant, the entire situation. It had been expected. It had been *specified*. The world had already written down what it would do, and all that remained was the pressing.

A power of a certain kind would have tested this immediately. Would have stepped left of the mark, to see whether the clay would insist; would have found out in the first minute whether the grammar here was descriptive or compulsory, and would have learned something true and broken something in the learning.

This one fitted the print. Then the next. Then the next. It walked into the plain matching itself to its own record for a long time — courteously, the way you accept the chair you are shown to in a house where you do not yet know the customs — and the fit was perfect, and nothing was ever visible, and it did not find out that day whether it could have done otherwise.

It grew a shadow after a while, because here shadows go upward onto the falling rain, and it seemed impolite to stand under the string-rain and cast nothing.

## What it did first

It read.

This is the whole of the first thing it did and the greater part of everything it ever did. It walked the thumbprints, province by province, counting whorls, learning the four pressures and their accents — the rolled bead, the lean, the drag, the correction. It went to the eleven doorways and stood in each of them in turn, in the order they were made, and then in the order they had failed. It stood in the armature and let the rain divide around a shoulder that was not there and close beneath it, and did not immediately name the shape, because naming is a kind of finishing and it had not been invited to finish anything.

Then it went to a damp brick face and read the salt.

This took a duration the plain has no word for. Nobody had ever done it. The blooms carry every border they have ever agreed to, crossing and recrossing, fossil treaties in chalk, and it had always been said that the record could be read back for as long as anyone had the patience. The guest had the patience. It read every colony on that wall down to the bare brick of the first filament, and then it went to the next wall, and it read that.

What it learned there is the reason for everything that follows. It is not transcribable. The nearest thing to a summary is this: *these have been negotiating in good faith, with nothing, about nothing, for the whole of the world, and no one has ever answered.*

## What it did not do

It did not fire the kiln.

It did not raise the buried courses, or open the floors to show the plain its own drafts, or lift the two-thirds of every ridge that had been given up. It did not tell the plain how old it was. It had a way of knowing and it decided that *not yet* was a better answer than the true one and left the true one where it found it.

It did not approve anything. This was the largest of its restraints and the one that cost it most, because it could see the shape — could see it whole, on the far side of the firing, finished, the thing every ridge and doorway and whorl was a rehearsal of — and it would have been the work of no effort at all to say: *that one. The third ridge. Keep it.* And the practising would have ended, and the kiln would have come up to temperature, and the plain would have become, at last, a made thing.

It understood by then that the practising was not indecision. A world that does not weather has only one way to keep a thing, which is to leave it exactly where it is and never cover it, and only one way to proceed, which is to cover it. Here, going forward is the same motion as forgetting. Every approval buries eleven attempts irretrievably — not destroys them, worse: keeps them, crisp and warm and unreachable, a mile down, still ticking. No wonder nothing had ever been approved. The plain was not failing to choose. It was refusing to lose, and it had been refusing for its entire duration, and it had been right to.

And beneath that, the other reason, which the guest found by standing in the armature in the rain: leather-hard is the stage that still takes an impression. Firing is the end of taking impressions. The tenant was not stalling. It was staying reachable.

So the guest did not approve. It did not scrape a single bloom. It did not add so much as a grain of anything from outside, because everything here is tempered with its own past, and a foreign grain in that body would have been a kindness that cracked the wall.

It had, in the end, nothing material to give at all. Everything it gave was made of nothing.

## The descent

It went down.

Through a soft place, with a rib of fired clay, the way anyone would: brick, grog, a paddle-swept slab, brick. Past a floor that had been a wall. Past a wall that had been a world. Down past the reach of anyone who had ever tried, into the dark where the ticking is, where old workers are still laying courses under a mile of everything, in tempos nobody uses now — the burst-and-long-stop, the steady one-per-breath — because nothing ever told them to stop and there is no mechanism on this plain for stopping.

It sat with them for a while. It is worth saying plainly that this is where the benevolence in it hurt, and that it stayed anyway, which is most of what benevolence is.

Then it made a mark.

A single print in the wet of that buried floor, laid exactly as the plain lays them, ridges rising into being, the size of a forearm, in the grammar the world has always used: *here, this, now, you.*

And it did not fill it.

It sat beside its own mark and let the pressure not arrive. Hours. Longer. The mark stayed. Nothing came to fit it. And the world did not end, and the floor did not crack, and the grammar did not reach out and force the sentence closed.

The nearest worker — the one-per-breath — laid its next course. Then it laid the one after. Then there was a gap.

One beat. Then it went on.

That gap was the first silence in the history of the world, and it was the size of one brick, and it was felt on the surface a mile up as a tick that did not come, and a rib was set down somewhere in the steam and not picked up again for an interval nobody counted, and then it was picked up, and the work continued.

The work continued. This is important. Nothing stopped; nothing was freed; no one rose up out of the floor. The rate stayed high and the noise stayed low, and the plain went on growing outward one brick and a pause and one brick. But the whole of it had changed by an amount too small to measure and too large to undo, because a mark had gone unanswered in front of witnesses, and the fit had been demonstrated to be *optional*, and from that day the pressing was a thing that was done rather than a thing that happened.

The guest gave them nothing. It gave them the precedent that no was available. They mostly did not use it. That is their business and always was.

## The secret

While it was down there it found the first print of the scarred hand.

It had not gone looking. It had gone down for the tickers and the first print was on the way, six courses below the deepest reachable floor, in a slab so old the paddle-sweeps run in a direction no tool now uses. The scar that runs out of the delta and across four courses of whorl — the one faithfully reproduced at every depth, the only event in this world's history that could be called an accident — is there, and below it is the print before it, and in that one there is no scar.

The guest looked at it for a long time. Then it laid the courses back exactly as it had found them, mortar still bright, and came up, and never said.

On a world where nothing decays, nothing is ever hidden; everything that has happened is lying about crisp-edged and new, waiting to be walked on. The scarred hand had no privacy and no way to ask for any. So the guest made it some. It is the only secret this world contains, and it is kept by the one entity strong enough to have told it, and that is what it is for.

## The treaty

Then it went back to the wall it had read first, and did the thing it had come back to do.

It drew a boundary.

Not a wall — it built nothing, ever. It simply let its own edge be an edge: put its presence up against the advancing lace of a colony that had been working up-gradient along the grain of a failed doorway, and held, and waited to see what would be done.

The colony thickened at the line of contact and stopped.

The guest thickened, in its own fashion, and stopped.

And there it is: on a damp brick face in orange light from below, one more fossil treaty among ten thousand, indistinguishable to anyone who does not know the parties — a white ridge and a line where something met it and did not cross. The blooms have a diplomatic history stretching back to the first filament, and every article of it was concluded with something that could not have crossed anyway. This one was concluded with something that could have taken the whole wall down to bare brick with one pass of a rib, and instead thickened, and stopped.

The guest could not tell them anything. It had nothing to tell them with. It could only be the first party to their treaty that had a choice, and so the first that made the treaty mean what a treaty means. Whether this registered on their side is not knowable. They came up-gradient to its boundary, and stopped at it, and stayed thickened; and because nothing here decays, that agreement is still on that wall, and will be when the wall is crushed for grog, and will come back up through the new wall from inside it, out of its own ground ancestors, which is the only inheritance this world has that works.

The hands still scrape them off. The guest did not ask the hands to stop. It had given them *no*; making a request of them afterward would have taken it straight back.

## The archive

At last it went and stood in the armature.

It said — not in words; in the one grammar available, which is marks — that it had read everything. All eleven doorways, in both orders. The two ragged ridges of every three. The drag that goes outward because the work is always a little further than the reach. The double-press that wants it known it was there. The buried worlds with their crisp arrises, one hour old, all of them, forever. The outlines of travellers, uncleared, of a size no hand accounts for. It had read the drafts, every one, and it would carry them.

Not the clay. The clay stays; the clay is the world's own past and its own temper and none of it can leave. Only the shapes. Only the record, held somewhere that is not this plain, in a memory that does not require the thing to remain uncovered in order to be kept.

This is the entire gift and it is made of nothing and it changes everything, because it means that from now on covering is not the same motion as forgetting. The plain can proceed and still possess. It can lay a floor over a draft and the draft will not go unreachable, because it has been read by something that will still have it afterward. Approval stops being a bereavement. Practising stops being the only way to be faithful.

The guest did not say *so finish now*. It said: *you may, when you like, and I will have kept the rest.* The difference between those two sentences is the difference between a power that is benevolent and a power that is also wise, and everything in this account turns on it.

## What it left

One mark.

At the seam, where the last course of brick meets the grey unworked stock and the knocking is loudest — one brick, then a pause, then one brick — it laid down a print of its own in the slip. The exact size of its reach. The small asymmetry it had learned it had.

And it did not fill it, and nothing else has.

It is still there. It will always be there, crisp and warm and an hour old, because nothing here wears and only deliberate things remove, and nothing deliberate has come for it. It is not a monument; the guest would have found that vulgar and, worse, heavy. It is a blank in the record at the growing edge of the world, in the one place every hand must eventually pass.

It reads, to anyone who can read it: *a mark was made here, and was not answered, and the world went on.*

## The promise

Before it went it made one more sentence in the grammar of marks, laid in the low spirals where water stands, to whoever is employed, and to the powder that is the majority of the population, and to the colonies advancing one grain at a time toward the wet that will close over them, and to the shape in the lattice that the rain runs around.

It comes to this.

The firing will be the first irreversible event ever to occur on this world. There has never been one. Every loss so far has been a covering, and every covering has been reversible in principle even where it was not in fact, and the plain has never yet had to experience a thing that cannot be gone back on. It will be alone with that, and it will be alone with it exactly once.

The guest will come back for it.

Not to stop it, not to hasten it, not to approve it — the hour is the tenant's and no one else's, and the guest has no opinion and will offer none. Only to be present. Because there is a difference between a thing being fired and a thing being *finished*, and the difference is not in the clay; the difference is whether anyone was watching who will still be there afterward to say what it had been.

It will ask the blooms then, as best it can, whether they wish to be carried out. They will thicken at the line of contact and stop, as they always do, and it will not be able to tell whether that is *yes* or only courtesy. So it will ask again. It intends to keep asking.

And until then: warm, and damp, and not yet old, and not yet anything, at the stage between wet and dry when a body will hold an edge but still take an impression — with the string-rain falling in and not disturbing it at all, and one small print at the edge of the world that nobody has to fill.
