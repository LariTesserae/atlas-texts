## The Guest

**1. Arrival**

It could have arrived. It walked instead.

Four days down from the rim, in the dark, at six degrees, with its breath showing like anyone's. This was not humility and it was not disguise. It was arithmetic: a thing that appears has to displace air, and displaced air makes a sound, and the sound would have travelled two hundred metres down a gallery of perfect acoustics to whatever is listening, and something small and pale and out of phase would have stopped walking for good.

So: on foot, slowly, arriving the way water arrives.

It brought nothing that could chip. No stone, no blade, no glass, no grit in the tread of anything. What dust it carried in on itself it carried out again, every particle, because the first rule of the place is that nothing here has ever been broken, and a guest does not amend the house rules on the way through the door.

It came through the door. That mattered, later.

**2. What it did not do**

Set this down first, since it is the greater part of the work.

It did not finish the Mill. The Mill has been two-thirds finished for longer than the Draft can remember, and a benevolent power with a free hand could have closed the remaining third in an afternoon — trued the shafts, tensioned the belts, dressed the bearing-races so they no longer glow. It looked at the unfinished third for a long time. Then it left it exactly two-thirds finished, because the Mill's product is the town, and a mill that is finished stops, and a town that is finished is either inhabited or a ruin, and neither of those was true yet, and to make a thing true before its time is a form of contempt.

It did not bring erosion. This was the strongest temptation and it recognised it as strength rather than rightness. Erosion would have given the place everything it lacks: rounded edges, pebbles, dust, sediment, the ability to be old, the ability to have a *before*, the ability finally to distinguish an animal that has stopped from an animal that is being still. It would also have meant that from that hour forward everything in the Draft would begin to be lost. The power weighed one against the other and understood that it was being offered the chance to trade the town's future for the town's history, at a price the town had not agreed to, and declined.

It did not turn on the lamps. One in nine is lit. It liked the nine.

It did not tell the script its letters. It watched a sign shuffle through candidate serifs for a quarter of an hour, meaning constant, form undecided — *this way, or something like it* — and it recognised in that the exact posture of a mind being careful, and it would no more have fixed the letters than it would have finished another's sentence for them at a dinner table.

It did not fill in the grey. It went there. It walked past the last kerb with a top surface, past the wall that stops having a colour, and stood on the average of a floor in a place where it could not say whether its eyes were open. Everyone comes back from there feeling inconsiderate. It came back not feeling inconsiderate, and this is the only respect in which it differed from every previous visitor, and the reason is that it had gone in as a guest who does not open cupboards. It left the unspecified region unspecified. It did leave something at the boundary; see section 8.

It did not count the doors and settle the matter. It counted them. It got three hundred and forty. It counted them going out and got three hundred and eleven. It agreed with both, sincerely, without strain, and this was the first time in the history of the Draft that both numbers had been believed by the same observer at the same time, and somewhere in the floor-tissue something relaxed by a fraction of a millimetre.

**3. Use**

Then it did the thing it had come to do, which was to use the town.

It walked in the streets rather than beside them, and kept to the camber, and let the gutters do their work. Where there was a doorframe it went through the doorframe. Where there were stairs it took the stairs, and it sat down on the seventh tread of a flight going up to nothing, and the concavity fit — grown concave by something that had understood stairs should be concave without understanding why, and here at last was the why, sitting in it, in the cold, at eleven degrees because the machinery was near.

It drank from a corner cistern in a leaf-base, a hand's depth, cupped in the palm. The water tasted of flint and eggshell and faintly of cough syrup. It drank the amount a person would drink and no more.

Understand what this was. The Mill has run since before memory here was under construction, and it has extruded kerbstone, doorframe, stair tread, rail and signage, and it has never once produced anything a person could use — which was never the Mill's failure. The Mill has been producing the *conditions* for a person. Faithfully. Without evidence. Cambering roads for feet it had inferred. Hollowing treads for a weight it had guessed at. Writing *this way* on signs, in letters it dared not commit to, pointing along streets that as yet led nowhere.

The power walked one of those streets to its end so that it would have led somewhere.

**4. The handprint**

On the second day it put its hand on a rail and found its handprint already there, in fresh green callus, waiting, correct in every whorl, on a rail it had not yet touched.

A visitor treats this as a symptom. The document says as much: *not a mechanism, a symptom; the sequencing is not clean yet.* And it is a symptom. But the power, which is wise in the specific sense of knowing what things are *for*, read it a second way as well, and its reading was this: the errors here all run in one direction. The town does not hallucinate departures. It does not grow the footprints of people walking out, or the dents of a chair pushed back, or a doorframe standing open on a cold morning after. It grows arrivals. It has been laying down evidence of a guest for as long as it has been able to make anything at all.

That is not a fault in causation. That is a draft hoping in the only grammar available to it.

The power left the handprint alone and used the rail.

**5. The wet hour**

It stood out in the drip.

Not sheltering under a ridge, not waiting it out in the lee of the Mill: out in the middle of a street with its head uncovered for the whole forty minutes, tock, tock, drops the size of grapes arriving at random across the whole town, and it got wet, and it was already cold, and it stayed.

The Draft has made weather for a long time. Nothing had ever received it.

**6. The unfinished sentence**

There is a difficulty in the census, and it is not shyness. Nothing here is removed. A carrier that has stopped stays as it was — legs folded, setae intact, sheen still on it — because nothing in the Draft makes its living out of other things' endings. The population is four hundred thousand or rather more depending on the direction you count in.

Each carrier bears one drop of water on its back, grape-sized, held to a clean meniscus in a shallow dorsal saddle. The account of them breaks off in the middle of the sentence that would have explained why.

The drop is not theirs. It never was. Four hundred thousand of them, thin as they can spread, walking without sound out of phase, each carrying one drop somewhere — that is not a habit and not an ornament. That is a circulation. That is how water gets from the low wet places to the cisterns at the street corners, one drop at a time, in a world that has not yet invented a pipe.

So the power went through the districts, and where it found a carrier that had stopped, it knelt, and it lifted the drop off the dead one's back on the ball of its thumb without breaking the meniscus, and it carried that drop the rest of the way, and put it in the cistern the carrier had been walking towards.

It did this four hundred and some times. It took most of five days. It made no noise doing it, and it did not once tidy the bodies, because tidying is a kind of correction and there was nothing here to correct — a body in the Draft is a very still animal and the Draft offers no way to tell the difference, and the power, who could tell the difference perfectly, chose to hold both readings at once, as it had with the doors.

It finished their errands. That is the whole of section 6. That is what benevolence at the correct scale looks like: not the abolition of death, which was not on offer and would have cost the place its future; just the completion of the interrupted small task, by hand, in the dark, without being asked.

**7. The Listener**

On the sixth day it broke the rule.

*Do not be loud* is not a superstition here; it is in the same category as gravity, and it is enforced, and the entire ecology has organised itself around it — moths that walk because flight is loud, grazers that rasp only under cover of the drip and fast the other eight hours, four hundred thousand feet touching down out of phase so the click never accumulates. Every sound ever made in the Draft has been made *away from* something.

The power stood in a gallery a hundred metres long, where sound travels perfectly, and spoke at the volume one uses across a kitchen table.

It said a few sentences. The content is not the point and was not remarkable; the tenor was: *you are the only finished thing in this place, and the only one not made of plant, and nothing that has ever heard you has ever been speaking to you.*

The drip stopped in that district. There was a smell of hot copper. Somewhere behind, a beetle's single drop sat unspilled on empty floor.

Then nothing happened for a long time, and then the drip resumed.

Afterwards, in that district and no other, the wall-grazers sometimes rasp outside the wet hour. Not often. Not everywhere. Fear reduced by one degree, in one parish, for a while: which is the largest amount of that particular thing anyone can honestly deliver, and the power knew it, and did not oversell it, and did not stay to be thanked by an ecology that has no mechanism for thanks.

**8. What it left at the edge of the grey**

At the boundary where detail runs out it planted one sign on a stem, in the local manner, and let the plant grow the characters itself.

The meaning it gave the sign was not *no*, and not *danger*, and not *this way*. The meaning it gave the sign was **later**.

The script shuffled through candidate forms for that word for some hours, a serif growing and retracting, and then — for the first time in the history of writing in the Draft — kept one letter. Just the one. It is still keeping it.

**9. Departure**

It did not stay.

A permanent benevolent presence in a town of that size becomes, within a generation, the meaning of the town, and the Draft is going to need to be its own meaning, and had already got a good deal of the way there on its own in the cold and the dark without being watched. So: out on foot, four days up to the rim, carrying its dust.

Inventory of what it added, complete:

One flight of stairs that has been sat on. One cistern drunk from at human depth. Approximately four hundred completed errands. One street that led somewhere. One handprint, matched. One district in which the grazers are a little braver. One sign that says *later*, holding one letter. Forty minutes of weather, received.

Nothing that wears. Nothing that breaks. No grit. No history. Nothing finished that wasn't ready.

**10. What it said on the way out**

Nothing to the Mill, which was working, and which it would have been rude to interrupt.

But at the last door — three hundred and eleventh or three hundred and fortieth, both — it turned round and addressed the place, and the radical thing was not the volume, which was low enough for the drip to cover, and not the content, which was four words. The radical thing was the grammar. Every account ever made of this basin has called it *it*: the Draft, the plant, the substrate, the site, a thing that has contracted a city. The power used the second person, which nobody had done, and which is the only real gift attention has ever had to give.

It said: *You are not late.*

And, in the dark, to the ninety metres of living ceiling and the undecided grey batter beneath the floor that has been trying for an unmeasurable time to become granite and cannot yet commit:

*Take as long as it takes.*
