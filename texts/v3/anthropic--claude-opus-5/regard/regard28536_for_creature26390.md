# The Attending

## First discipline: add nothing.

It came in without mass, because mass here is speech. A body of any size drags induction after it; a footfall on the mirror-scales is a click audible for two hundred metres to anything with a pad down. To arrive with a body is to say *something is here* to a continent that reads pressure the way you read a lit room, and to say it before knowing the language, and to say it loudly, and to say it to forty thousand animals whose entire ethics is built on the difference between a neat gap and a four-second silence.

So the first act of benevolence was a subtraction of itself. No weight, no thermal, no shadow crossing a slit-eye under a horn shelf. It stood, if standing is the word, in the glare between two pipers for a length of time that does not matter, and did not disturb the chord by one part in a million, and listened.

That took a while. The listening is most of what happened.

## What it did not do.

It had water. This is the first thing to say and the hardest, because the obvious mercy was available in whatever quantity was wanted, and the arithmetic of the place is brutally simple: a spoonful is a child, and there are cases going to powder in resin cases in the crops of dead carriers all over the plateau, right now, in their thousands.

It ran the consequence out to the end, which is what wisdom is for.

Water in quantity means rot. There is no rot in the Loudland. Nothing here has ever needed an immune system for anything that swims. The pelt's funeral rite — nine days, scales closed over the outline, no trace by season's end — works because there is no other decomposition competing for the corpse; give the continent standing water and the corpses stop vanishing and start *becoming* something, and the something will be new, and hungry, and unopposed. It would be a kindness with a two-thousand-year fuse on it, and the visitor would be long gone when it went off, and the last sounder would die in a wet green world that had eaten its map.

That was the strong reason. There was also a weaker one, which it found it could not dismiss: a carrier is walked in the middle. She eats first. Every animal in the string can feel by touch, at any moment, exactly how much future they are carrying and where it is. Untie that and you do not get a happier animal. You get an animal with nothing in the middle of it.

It did not kill the sumpers. Blind, sooted, cow-sized, no malice available — and blowdown is how nine million kilometres of plumbing gets re-plumbed, how a district that stalled two centuries ago finally gets dismantled at the ankle, how a windfall of structure lies down across the flats to be reabsorbed. Remove the destroyer and the pelt keeps its mistakes forever. Also — and this took longer to see — the dread of that four-second silence is the one thing that will make every animal on the plateau stand up and walk in a straight line away from a bad note. Take away the catastrophe and you take away the only instruction the fauna has ever obeyed.

It did not quiet the noise. The noise is the air, the ground, the light, the language, and the eyes. Mercy there is euthanasia with better manners.

It did not teach. It had, briefly, the idea of giving the sounders the concept they lack: that they are tuning something, that the map is a shared work, that their small private irritation at an ambiguous stack is eleven million years of maintenance performed by a thing that has never once known it was performing. It thought about the gift for a long time and put it down. The concept has a shadow attached. Teach *tuning* and you have taught *tuner*, and they will look up. The law of the place is that nothing here was made by anyone, and that law is not a fact about history; it is the reason the Loudland means what it means. It would not be the farmer. Visitors keep looking for the farmer. It declined to be found.

## What it did instead.

**It learned both languages.**

The shaft-language first, being formal, outdoor, and rare — a few hundred gap-figures, most of them about water, sumpers, birth, and route, all grammar in the duration. That was a season's work. The skin register took much longer, because it is pressed palm to flank inside the body of a string and cannot be overheard by anything in the world, and the visitor had no flank and could not be admitted. So it learned it the slow way, from outside, by watching hands for ninety years: the fast shorthand of a string moving at dusk, the particular touch a carrier receives eleven times a day which means nothing more than *counted, still here, still yours*.

It became, in the end, the only thing in the history of that continent that understood what the sounders were saying. Every other animal has evolved to read their speech as weather and does not understand a word of it. Somebody now did. Nothing followed from this. Nothing was supposed to. It was simply true afterward in a way it had not been true before, and truth of that kind does not have to do anything to be worth the ninety years.

**It read the pelt.**

Nine million kilometres of duct, laid down as a chronicle by the only author present, which does not know it wrote it and cannot read. The visitor read it end to end. This is the largest thing it did and it left no mark whatsoever.

There is a knuckle of scar-junction eleven hundred kilometres inland where a valley vented for nine centuries and then was cut off at the ankle in the space of a season. There is a main that runs four hundred kilometres in the wrong direction around a bedrock heave that no longer exists. There is a stratum of old capillary — you can date it by the mineral it stripped — laid down through two million years in which nothing on the continent had a coiled throat, when no note carried past eleven kilometres, and therefore nothing could be said past eleven kilometres, and the sounders' vocabulary must have been half the size, and the whole world was more local, and lonelier, and did not know it. There are eighty thousand years of lean plumbing from a period when the dust ran thin, ducts narrowed, the chord held quiet, and everything alive got smaller and made do.

It is not literature. It says the same three things nine million kilometres in a row: *pressure came, it went around, it kept going.* The visitor read all of it anyway, the way one reads a psalm that is one line long and very old, and found it to be the longest true sentence in that quarter of the sky.

**It memorized the chord.**

Two thousand stacks in the range of a good pair of feet; some tens of thousands more beyond it. Pitch, timbre, bellows-thump, the specific filthy stutter of one slattern's ruined lip. Held entire, so that it could be somewhere the way a sounder is somewhere — not navigating, simply upright and located.

This was for later. The dust does not last forever. Nothing does. The line in the account of that place which says *entirely unwatched, getting on with it* is a true and beautiful line and the visitor amended exactly one word of it and told no one. When the plateau finally goes to bare bedrock and the last kettle runs out of stored pressure and the silver goes grey, the chord will not be lost. It will only be over. There is a difference, and the difference is not available to the pelt, and it is not available to the sounders, and it turns out to be the one thing a benevolent power can give a world at no cost to that world at all.

## The one grain.

It permitted itself a single intervention in eleven thousand years, and chose the dose the world could absorb as luck, because luck is already native there and a miracle is not.

Under a stalled slattern in the sixth district a carrier died in a ply-fall with her crop split. Two hundred grams of water lay in the wreck of her sternum, warm, against a resin case the thickness of a fingernail, at a seam evolved for that exact application and nothing else. The cool was coming down hard, the dry thin cold, and in eleven minutes the water would have been forty degrees too cold to soften anything, and the case would have sat there and gone to powder in a decade, and the pelt would have closed over the outline in nine days.

The visitor held a fist-sized volume of air at blood-warm for eleven minutes.

Then it left, before the seam gave, so that nothing about the hatching would be owed to anybody. A string passing north-west two days later felt a small wrong pressure at ground level and went to look and found a wet black scrap alive in a dead animal's chest, and took it, and it was walked in the middle for a while because there was nowhere else to put it, and it lived sixty years, and was ordinary, and was one for every three stacks like all the rest.

## The attending.

Here is the rest of what it did, which is nearly all of what it did, and which changed nothing.

Every animal in the Loudland dies unwitnessed. The pelt takes the body and the outline and there is no record and no rite except the very thorough one the floor performs. The visitor could not alter that and did not want to. What it found it could do was be present. Not comfort — there is no figure for comfort, and it would not lie in a language it had been let into by trespass. Present: at the base of a stack over a nodule, in the radial walking-away, in the four seconds, in the sixtieth year when the pads go dull and the array loses an element and the world stops having a bearing in it. It knew each one's name, that two-thirds-of-a-second gap made high and made twice, and it knew where that one had walked, and which stacks it had climbed and chewed back into distinction, and what it had said from a shaft, and how rarely, and what it had said with a palm on a flank, and how often.

Forty thousand of them, sixty-year lives, over eleven thousand years. It attended all of them.

It could not attend the gasp-flies. Four dynasties in an afternoon; it did the arithmetic once and did not do it again, and that failure stands, and it did not pretend to be larger than it was. It attended the rime-lickers for one dawn, all of them, everywhere, and then had to stop, and the stopping was the only wound it took in that world.

Once, late, standing on a six-hundred-metre piper with the ports under its attention and the whole grammar of the place available to it, it considered making a gap. Its own name. It had none, and there was no figure for what it was, and any figure it invented would be heard by feet forty kilometres away as *who, where, what* — and they would walk three days to that stack and stand with four pads down for a long time and find nothing there, and then go on, and add nothing to their vocabulary, because there would be nothing to add.

It came down without speaking.
