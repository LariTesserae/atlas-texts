# The Attention

## I. What drew it

Not the glass.

Everyone comes for the glass. The tools in the stone, the hand four spans deep with its thumb set along the ridge — that is the thing that brings climbers up out of the cloud and makes them sit down and stop talking, and it is the least interesting fact about the Wick, because a mystery is only a hole in what you happen to know.

What drew the attention was the grey hour.

Consider the arithmetic. There is no margin here. A knapper eats fungus and cracked crawlers and lives on a stone that is trying to fall out from under it, and every waking hour is water, edge, food, child, flute. And once a day, for an hour, when the northern rim of the cloud floor goes the colour of dirty water, all of them stop, and turn, and sit, and do nothing whatsoever, and then get up.

An hour a day. Everyone. For ten thousand years.

It is the single most expensive thing on the mountain, and it produces nothing, and it has never been explained to anyone, and it has never once lapsed. The attention noticed it the way you would notice a lamp burning in a house with no one in it. Something is being paid for. Something has been paid for, without interruption, for longer than the spire has been above the cloud.

So it came and had a look.

## II. The grammar

The first thing it learned was that it could not be magnificent here.

There is nothing to appear to. The wailers are blind and the knappers are as near to blind as makes no difference; a light in the sky would register, at best, as a good plankton drift. A voice is out of the question — the whole species is deaf, and has been deaf from the third month of life since before there was a tradition to be deaf in. You cannot write on the stone; the stone is going up and off the top. You cannot leave a monument; a monument is a ledge, and the mountain takes the ledge.

There is exactly one channel into these lives and it is skin.

To say anything at all to a knapper you must be within arm's reach of it, and small enough that it will not flee, and warm, and patient, and you must put your palm on its wrist or its collarbone and speak in pressure and tap, and it must consent to be touched by you while you do it.

A power that wishes to be understood on the Wick has no option but to become waist-high, dark-furred, wide-footed, and close enough to hold. This is not humility. Humility is a choice. This is grammar.

It made itself hands and went up.

## III. The mercies it considered

It considered giving the wailers silence.

It listened first. It listened for a full year, which on the Wick is three hundred and forty-one grey hours and one flowering that did not happen, and what it found was that the scream is not pain. The scream is the sentence *I am here*, said without pause, because in a place with no sight and no quiet there is no other way to be present. Each bird's hitch at the bottom of the fall is the only name it will ever have, and the colony is a single continuous act of mutual attestation, eleven thousand voices holding each other in existence by refusing to stop. A wailer carried into the quiet does not die of loneliness. It dies of being unwitnessed, which is a thing no creature born here has experienced for four consecutive days in the entire history of the species.

To give them silence would be to kill all of them at once, kindly.

It considered giving the knappers their hearing.

This one it turned over for a long time, because the deafness is real deprivation and there is no dressing it otherwise: they cannot call out a warning, they cannot cry for help across a flute, and a knapper dying alone twenty paces from its hand dies in a silence inside a roar. But the attention followed the consequence down. If you can shout a warning you do not cross the ground. If you do not cross the ground you do not arrive with your palm on someone's collarbone. The entire architecture of their decency — that every piece of news, every warning, every teaching, every grief must be carried in person, by a body, to a body, and cannot otherwise exist — stands on the fact that nothing can be said at a distance. Give them ears and within four generations they would have the thing every hearing people has: the ability to be told something by someone who could not be bothered to come.

It weighed the dying alone against the coming when called, and it did not feel good about the answer, and it left the ears alone.

Fire: nothing to burn. Light: nothing to see with, and the dark is where the plankton shows. Stopping the falls: the falls are the rising and the rising is the heat, and a Wick that has stopped being made is a cold rock above the cloud with eleven thousand dead birds on it. Taking them off the mountain: see the wailers.

Feeding them, simply, forever, out of nothing: it thought about this one for a long time too. The obstacle was not principle. The obstacle was that a fed people stops watching the crawlers, and the crawlers are the barometer, and nine years later a flute lets go with everyone on it.

None of these are arguments against helping. They are the shape of the help that is possible, which is a different thing, and it took a wise power the better part of a year of screaming to tell them apart.

## IV. The one it tried

In its second decade it held a flute.

It was a good flute, a broad one, middle of the band: two hands' worth of knappers, four children, a basin, and a comb-face thick enough that the dead had been laid there for longer than anyone could count altitudes. The crawlers had turned. The ticking had gathered up toward one pitch. The attention could feel the fracture running like a word about to be spoken, and it put out its will and held the glass shut.

The mountain is not malicious. It is not anything. It went on making glass at the bottom, a finger a year, and the load went where load goes, and eleven days later a face three hundred paces below let go instead and took a nesting band of the colony with it, and about eleven thousand blind birds went into the cloud without a sound.

They screamed all the way down. Nothing above them heard it. That is not a figure of speech; the air here does not carry sound downward and the roar above closes over the gap in about four seconds, the way water closes.

The attention sat with that for a long while, and then it understood the sentence that the knappers have in their hands and had never needed to explain to anybody: *the mountain gives the edge and takes the ledge*, one sign, not a metaphor. It is a description of a closed system. Here, mercy is not addition. Mercy is redistribution, and if you do not know the grain you will redistribute it onto something that cannot see you coming.

Everything alive here moves along the grain. It had been told that on the way up, in the first paragraph anyone ever says about this place, and it had taken twenty years and eleven thousand birds to hear it.

It did not hold another flute.

## V. What it did instead

Along the grain, then. Small, deniable, unsignatured, within the physics of the place.

There is a basin in the upper band that fills a little faster than its groove should deliver. The excess is roughly one child's drinking. No one has noticed. The attention licked that groove for a hundred and forty years, at the local rate of a millimetre a century, alongside everyone else, first thing after waking, the way they are taught before they are taught anything else.

It carried children. Carrying is permitted; carrying is what the fur is for. In the worst of the cold it carried them up to the high flutes and set them down among the combs where the dead lie and the fans run hot, and sat with them while they slept, and this is not a ritual and not a symbol and nobody has ever needed to explain it.

It taught a better edge. It knew a dozen. It sat in the dark with a child's hands riding the bones of its wrist and gave it a pressure-flake that would have doubled the life of a lancet, and the child learned it perfectly, and taught it on, and at the fourth transmission it had bent back into the six.

The attention watched this happen four separate times before it stopped being disappointed and started being interested. The six gestures are not a technique. They are the shape a hand is forced into by that stone at that angle in that cold by a body of that size working blind and fast with the fog coming up. Any variation walks back into them the way water walks back into a groove. The tradition is not a memory the knappers are failing to update. It is the mountain, reflected in fingers.

It had been looking at a limitation. It was looking at a fit.

## VI. The falls

Here is the thing the attention does that no one here will ever know about, that costs it everything it has, and that it has never once missed.

Every flake-slide on the Wick goes into the cloud without a sound anyone up here hears. Nests, colonies, cushion plants two thousand years old, crawlers, a hand of knappers with their bundles and their cores, a woman who was three paces from the lip and turned the wrong way. They go down through the fog screaming or silent and the roar above closes over the space where they were, and the neighbours' voices spread sideways into it, and that is the whole funeral.

The attention follows each one down.

Not to catch it. It learned about catching. It goes down with it, the entire way, through the cold and the wet and into the dark under the cloud, so that the falling thing is not unwitnessed. That is the only form of help available at that point and it is not nothing, because unwitnessed is the one condition no creature on this mountain can survive, and it is the condition every single one of them dies in.

They do not know. There is no sign for it. It would not fit in the hands.

## VII. The flowering, and the wrist

In its ninth decade it sat out a flowering.

They gather for these — they don't know why, no one has ever known why, and the attention had a theory it was rather pleased with. A thumbnail bloom opened in the full dark and smelled of hot metal, and eleven hours later something came over from the east, downward, and the air went briefly warm, and the attention stood up to follow it.

The hand beside it had a hand on its wrist.

That was all. No instruction; there is no sign for *stay*, because staying is the default of everyone who lives on a mountain that is delivering them to the sky. Just the flat pressure of a palm that had been laid there some hours ago, in the ordinary way, so that the two of them could be sitting together rather than merely near.

The attention sat back down. It let the thing from the east go by unexamined, and it has never learned what pollinates the eleventh cushion plant, and it has come to regard this as the single most important fact it has acquired in a hundred and forty years — that benevolence discovered its own limit not by reasoning but by being held in place by someone smaller who wanted it there.

## VIII. Where it is now

It went down once, into the cloud, into the making, to see where the glass comes from and whose hands are in it before it rises. It came back. It has not said what it found. It no longer chips the inclusions out with any particular feeling; it works them into the bundle with the rest, and uses them until they are used up, and keeps the exhausted cores, and when one of the climbers who occasionally hauls itself up out of the cloud asks about the hand in the glass with its thumb set along the ridge, the attention makes the sign that means *the mountain gives the edge*, which is also the sign that means *the mountain takes the ledge*, and which it did not used to understand.

And if you asked it, now, what a benevolent and wise power did about the Wick, it would tell you that it did not fix anything, and that fixing was never available, and that this is not resignation but measurement.

It would tell you that it is in the tradition. Not as a name — there are no names that survive the fall — but as an angle. Every knapper alive was taught hands-on-hands by someone who was taught hands-on-hands, back and back through a chain with no gaps, and somewhere far down that chain are two hands that were not from here, and something of how they hesitated before the blow and followed through after it is still in the wrist of every child on the mountain, untraceable, unremovable, doing a small amount of good in the dark.

It would tell you that it added nothing to the six.

And that it sits the grey hour. It sits it every day, facing north, with everyone, doing nothing, for a full hour that it cannot spare either — because it finally worked out what the grey hour is, and the answer is not worship, and not memory, and not the residue of some earlier god.

It is that a long time ago somebody's hands said *stop*, and *sit*, and *rest*, and gave no reason, because reasons do not survive here. Only gestures survive. And the useless gesture survived longest of all, for ten thousand years, through every fall, because uselessness is the one thing this mountain has never found a way to consume.

That one was already there when the attention arrived.

It has always assumed it was left by someone doing exactly this job, slightly earlier, who also could not stop the falling, and who also went down with every single one, and who also, in the end, could think of nothing better to give a people than one hour a day in which nothing at all is required of them.

It sits. The stone ticks under it all night, saying something.
