## The Attention at Vess

Begin where it began, which was not at the lid and not at the rack.

It began at the arithmetic.

Call it a power. It does not object to the word and takes no interest in it. It came to Vess the way weather comes to a valley — not summoned, not arriving from anywhere, simply obtaining there after a period in which it had not. What drew it is worth stating plainly, because it was not beauty and it was not pity. It was the fact that two hundred and eight pigeonholes and fifty-one lit housings and three changes a decade constitute a subtraction, and that somewhere in the basin an animal was performing that subtraction on itself, faithfully, in the dark, with great skill, and did not know.

---

**The first thing it did was stay.**

The cold sets the visit's length. Forty minutes, at best. Everything anyone has ever learned about Vess has been learned in forty-minute pieces, and the pieces do not fit, and this is why the rack was read for eight hundred years as evidence of a person.

The power has no hands to lose. It could have taken the whole town in an instant, opened it like a core sample, read every year-line at once. It did not, and the reason is not delicacy. A thing understood all at once is understood as a diagram. Vess is not a diagram; Vess is a tempo. So it watched one full circulation — four hours and fifty-one minutes, intake to intake — and then it watched a year, and then it watched six hundred, and only then did it consider that it had begun to know the place.

Here is a partial list of what it learned, all of which had been available the entire time to anyone who could stay warm.

That there is a warm seam. It is real, it is thin, it is four hundred meters down in the quarry rock, and it is sufficient. The engineer's instinct was correct. The loop is not impossible, only unlikely, and it will run for another eleven thousand years before the seam thins past use. *The power added nothing to it.* This is the first of its refusals and the one it was gladdest of, because a town that runs on a hidden seam is a town, and a town that runs on a benevolence is a pet.

That the polish in front of the rack has a grain running left to right because that is the side the animal braces on. It has always braced on that side. So did its mother.

That the lamp-work is not maintenance and not devotion. It is farming, and it is inherited, and the inheritance is of the plainest kind: for three hundred generations, in a basin where the only food grew in rings around lamplight, the individuals who could get a lamp out of a pigeonhole and into a housing ate, and the ones who could not did not. Nothing chose this. It was simply the shape of the sieve.

That taking the outer ring is not delicacy either. Four species occupy the rings in strict order, and cropping the outermost holds the whole succession stalled at the stage of highest yield. The animal is not being gentle with the lamp. It is being correct about the algae. It has been correct about the algae for longer than the mill was a mill.

That there are four of them. Not five. Two are old.

That they do not meet, and never have, and that this is not a wound. The power looked for loneliness in the animal the way you would look for a bone — expecting a socket, a place where the thing should articulate. There is no socket. Loneliness is a word that does not survive the swim down.

And that fifty-eight lamps remain on the rack. At three a decade: the last change in roughly one hundred and ninety years. At a mean burn of one hundred and seventy: the last light out in three hundred and ninety, give or take a decade. Then the rings fade from the outside in, over eleven years, and the basin goes to isopods and blind fish and the slow ferric knobs, and the four become two become none, and the wheel goes on turning once every forty seconds over a mill floor with a beautifully consistent slope of warm dark powder on it, and no one is there to be fed by it or to fail to be fed by it, and nothing about the town looks any different at all.

That is the third strangeness, and it is the only one that required anything of anybody: Vess is not a ruin, but it is a countdown, and the creature keeping time is the same creature it runs out on.

---

**The second thing it did was decline.**

It could have warmed the basin by six degrees and made the visit unlimited and the flora rich. This would have killed the stonewort, dissolved the felting that keeps Vess from being a bell, taken the lid off in nine years, and produced within a century a warm green pond with a drowned town at the bottom of it and no amphipods, because the amphipods are cold-water animals with cold-water enzymes and would have been the first thing the kindness removed.

It could have opened the ice for light. See above, and also: the algal rings are rings *because* the lamps are spheres. Daylight has no rim. The animal's entire technique is an adaptation to a lit sphere in a black room, and generalised light is not more of what it has; it is the abolition of the thing it is skilled at.

It could have filled the rack. Two hundred and eight lamps, appearing overnight in the pump-house, indistinguishable from the originals. The power went so far as to compose them. Then it put them down, because the rack is not a supply, it is the animal's only clock, and the animal reads it — not with dread, it has no organ for dread, but it reads it, the way it reads the direction of the current and the fading of a ring, and it has been adjusting its rounds against that reading for six hundred years. Fill the rack and you have not given it two hundred years. You have taken away the one instrument it has for knowing where it is, and replaced it with a miracle, and a miracle in a place like Vess is a silt cloud: it does not clear for six hundred hours, and for six hundred hours you have destroyed the only thing the place has to offer.

It could have repaired the timber gallery. It could have made the animal not die. It could have raised the workforce whose bones are in the third terrace and given the basin back its noise. It could have explained itself to the animal, made itself felt at twelve hertz on the forty-second cycle, become a presence in the water, become known, become a thing the animal turned toward when the work was hard. Once, it drifted alongside while a lamp on the second gallery was worked, close enough to be noticed by anything with the equipment to notice, and it was not noticed, and it found this entirely correct and stayed there for the full twenty minutes anyway.

It did not tell anyone. Not one of the fourteen institutions with a file on Vess; not the diver who has been down eleven times and who is closer to the answer than she knows.

Do not break the crust. Not for any reason of taboo. Simply because underneath it, the iron is still bright.

---

**The third thing it did was four things.**

*One.* It marked the date of the seam's thinning and confirmed that the seam outlives every question anyone will ever ask about it. Then it left the seam alone, which is a smaller act than filling a rack and required more of it.

*Two.* The long timber gallery has gone to a soft grey pulp and holds together mostly out of habit. Habits end. When it goes, it will go all at once, and its natural fall — the power ran it nine thousand times — carries the west end down across the pump-house roof and through it, onto the rack, in eighty per cent of cases.

The power did not hold the gallery up. Holding things up is a commitment, and a commitment made to a drowned town is a chain you have to keep hold of forever. It only altered, by a quantity too small to name, the load path at the third bracket, so that when the gallery comes down in about two hundred and forty years it will come down east, into the empty alley, and will take four days to arrive, tilting and gliding, and will land without a sound.
