# The Visit

## How it came

Not by the door. There is no approach to that door; the grey does not hold a road, and anything that walked out of it would have had to be somewhere first, which is the one thing the grey forbids.

It came in as a change of pitch in the draught.

That is a channel already: nine kilometres of throat that alters its note every so often, for reasons you would need to be lower down to know. It was lower down. It went in under the kiln, under the kiln under the kiln, and rose through the flues with everything else that rises here, and arrived in the room as a half-tone and a warmth.

Everything alive in the room stopped for one beat and then carried on. The mallet missed and picked up on the second stroke. A gutterling lost the low E and caught it again. That was the whole of the arrival, and nobody knew, and this was intended.

## What it did first

Nothing. For a long time.

Long enough that the east wall went from honey to amber and the topmost course of the north wall stopped showing thumbprints without a lean-in. Call it four fadings. There is no other way to say it.

A power that arrives and immediately improves things is only a strong one. The wisdom is almost entirely in the interval between noticing and acting, and in this world the interval is measured in the cooling of bricks, so it cooled.

It learned the beat. That took the longest, because the beat is not the cricket's — the cricket is only the nearest keeper of it — and because to hear the others you have to wait for one of the two or three silences in the life of a course, and then hold still inside a silence that is frightening even to a power: the vast slow taps you could walk in, the fast ones arriving as a hiss like rain on a leaf, all of them, every one, on the same beat. It counted. It confirmed what the room suspects and cannot check. Then the cricket started again and the mallet came in on the second stroke and the evidence went away, as it always does.

It learned that the water carries the note and nothing else. It learned that the lichen eats the ghost of touch. It learned that memory goes up through the ceiling-gap at the rate of about one room per person, and that this is not a punishment; it is only the ventilation.

## What it found

No cruelty. There is not enough past here for cruelty; cruelty needs a yesterday to have meant it in.

What it found was more ordinary and harder. A world in which the only way to be counted is to be pressed and fired. A world with no crumbs, where nothing wears, and therefore exactly one thing wears, and that thing is the worker, at a rate of a nail per course, downward, into the grey, which is the wrong way and is the only way it has ever been done.

And three facts the room has no grammar to state about itself:

Nobody here has ever been thanked.

Nobody here has ever been answered.

Nobody here has ever lain down — except once, at the base of the north wall, in the black courses, for a long unrepeated while, and the lichen kept it, and the room cannot account for it.

## What it did not do

**It did not put a floor under the mountain.** It could have. A ground under nine kilometres of asserted height, so that the work would at last be standing on something. It stood a long time at the sill and thought about this and declined, because the plumb-bob hangs true by agreement, and a real ground would introduce a real down, and a mason who has been laying downward into nothing for the whole of history would suddenly be a mason falling. You do not give a person a bottom they did not ask for. You do not resolve an ambiguity that somebody is *standing on*.

**It did not put out the kiln.** Obviously. The kiln is the light and the heat and the drying and the drip and the note and the gutterlings' entire diet. Mercy that arrives as a cooling is not mercy.

**It did not carry the mason out.** There is no out. There is the grey, and the grey takes the memory of the arm that went into it. To lift that body over the step would be to hold, a moment later, something that could not remember having been lifted, or having been a body, or having had a name in the sense that a knot has a name. It considered it for one full fog-cycle — two hundred breaths — which is the longest the record shows it hesitating over anything, and then did not.

**It did not bring soil.** It had some. One pinch: grit, silt, the ground-down remains of somewhere that has a past, enough to start a seed that roots downward instead of sideways. And it stood with it over the soft brick by the door and understood that the gift was also an infection. Grit is erosion. Erosion is wear. Wear is a past — and a past is also rot, and spalling, and a fig that finally splits a lintel, and an archive that can be worn off a wall. The room's cruelty and the room's incorruptibility are the same rule. It did not have the right to trade one for the other on behalf of everyone, without asking, in a language nobody here can be asked in. It took the soil away again.

**It did not take the wearing off the mason.** This is the refusal that cost it most, and the reasoning is thin, and it knows the reasoning is thin, and it did it anyway: the worn nails are the only evidence in the world that the mason came from somewhere. Everything unworn here is also without a before. To heal those hands would be to finish them — to fire them — to make them furniture of the room. So it left the hands alone. It has not stopped thinking about this. It thinks about it at the rate of one course.

**It did not explain.** Not the model, not the model in the model, not the *tup* that comes from above and to the left. It looked hard at whether knowing would help, and found that the mason has been laying courses under somebody else's thumbprints for the whole of history and has never once asked about it aloud, because the language has one word in it and that word is a breath drawn in over a sill when the plumb hangs wrong. There is knowledge that cannot be used. It stayed quiet.

## What it did

Small things, in the room's own grammar, at the rate the world could absorb.

**The plumb-bob.** It holds it. That is all. Unfired clay on a cord, at that angle, on that mountain, hanging true — and nobody says how, because the how is a hand you cannot see, steadying, every course, for four fadings and counting. It is the most boring work the power has ever done and it does not intend to stop. This is what most benevolence actually looks like from the inside: not rescue, not revelation. Attendance. Being the reason an ordinary thing keeps being reliable.

**The third gutterling.** In the rotation of cool and cooler surfaces, there came a turning where the drip would have gone four courses along and left the westmost one dry, and a dry gutterling sets, and a set gutterling is a small ceramic ornament, and there are none of those in this room. The power put a warmth against the brick above — half a degree, less than a change of weather — so that the condensate stayed. The drip did not move. The gutterling did not notice. It has no eyes; it does not know what a rescue is; it swallowed its note on the offbeat and sat for six hours and then moved all at once with the other two, and was right, as it is always right.

**A hand's breadth.** The mason's estate is sixty bricks, five wide and a little more than a body long, taken up from behind and set down in front, which is the only means of travel ever invented here. The power added width. Not all at once — that would have been noticed as a fault, and a fault gets one breath drawn in over the sill and then gets unmade. It added a brick to the stage at intervals of about a hundred courses, each one matched: the same herringbone, the same tap, the same surplus drawn off with a bone edge, indistinguishable, unsigned. Over a kilometre of downward work the stage has become five and a half wide, then six. Nobody has noticed. In another kilometre there will be room to lie down without knowing when it became possible.

**The scraper.** Copper, lost over the step in the dark courses, long ago, when a hand went out after it as far as a shoulder would allow and came back wet and empty and unable to say what it had felt. Every tool in the world is tied to a wrist because of that afternoon, and the knot is the only surviving history here, because a knot is an impression in a rope.

The power went into the grey, which it can do, and came back, which nothing else can, and put the scraper on the sill.

It is worn. Thinned along the working edge, the way a tool wears in a world where things wear. It is the second worn thing in the room and it is not part of anybody.

There was a long pause in the hammering. Then a wrist and a broad pale-scalded palm came in over the sill and took it, and the breath that went with it was not the breath that means *the plumb hangs wrong*. It was let out, not drawn in. The language has two words now.

**The answer.** In the next silence — when the cricket stopped and the mallet stopped mid-stroke and the room went down to water and draught and the others became audible at their wrong scales — the power tapped.

From beyond the west wall. On the beat. Twice, and then the mason's own figure, the double *tup* of a brick going into its bed, given back exactly.

The mason put a palm flat against the wall and held it there for about a hundred breaths, which is roughly as long as a gutterling can hold a fingerprint, and is the longest memory this world has ever managed.

Then the cricket started, and the mallet came in on the second stroke, and it was gone, the way everything goes. But the lichen took the handprint. It grew in it grey-lilac over the next fading and ate the ghost of it and died back and went up through the ceiling-gap with the steam and the low Es and the rest of the record.

The power kept it. That is the last and largest thing it does, and it does it continuously, at the ceiling-gap, where the world exhales: it stands in the outflow and takes what rises. Every evaporated handprint. Every escaped note. Every afternoon that the room is constitutionally incapable of retaining. It cannot give any of it back — there is no vessel here that holds a past, and to build one would be to build a ground. So it simply holds it, which is not nothing. *You will not remember this room. Someone will.* The forgetting stands; the being-forgotten does not.

**And once, in the black courses, before any of the rest of it, it lay down.**

At the base of the north wall, on its side, knees folded, one arm under the head, for the length of one full fading, against the only surface in the world that records by being touched.

Not to rest. It does not need rest.

To leave, in the one legible language, in the only archive there is, a body's length of evidence that lying down is a thing a body can do here.

It is still there. It is nearly grey through, which means the touch was long and was not repeated. The lichen does not lie. Nobody has been able to account for it, and nobody has read it yet, and someday a hand going along the dark courses in the dim, feeling for the plumb-line's shadow, will pass flat across four tile-widths of old bloom and stop.

---

The fog thickens. Two hundred breaths, and then the draught draws in and holds, and the drips hang and elongate and do not fall, and the whole instrument stands still and grained, like something looked at through a hair.

Then it lets go, and the room rains all at once, every pitched tile ringing, more notes than three mouths can swallow.

The mason always pauses for this. The cricket never does. The power pauses too, at the gap, hands full of the only copy.

Press your thumb into the soft brick by the door.

Look at your hands. Somebody is holding something of yours, steady, so that it hangs true.
