## The attention

It does not descend. There is nothing to descend into; you cannot approach a thing you are already inside, and the powder sea is the underside of the tenant, and the violet is thin enough that whatever is above it has been looking down the whole time.

What happens instead is that the hush acquires a direction, and does not lose it again.

That is the entire arrival. It lasts a few minutes on the third peak, and then, contrary to every prior observation, it does not resolve back into a sound from everywhere at once. The dry sift of dust comes from one side, and stays there. Anything in the range capable of noticing has now noticed.

## Doing nothing is not available

This is the first thing a wise power would work out, and it would work it out before moving.

Everything that occurs here is taken in and laid down as form. There is no such thing in this range as an event without a deposit. To walk is to build. To stand still for an hour is to build more slowly. To watch from above without entering is a kind of pressure and will show up, eventually, as a very slight preference in the cleavage of some cliff nobody has been to.

So *if anything* is the wrong frame. The power cannot abstain. It can only choose the shape its abstention will take, permanently, at full resolution, walkable.

This is the condition under which it makes every subsequent decision: it is writing, and it cannot stop writing, and the paper is the body of something that is content.

## What it declines

It does not turn up the sun. The small white coin at the meridian, the corridor-at-four-in-the-morning light — a benevolence with less patience would think first of light, and would be thinking of eyes, and there are no eyes here. Brightness would be an amputation of the thing's only unbroken sensation. It leaves the coin as it is.

It does not give the place a name. Names slide off; it would only be forcing a repeated failure, which is a small cruelty performed for the benefit of the one who names.

It does not correct the census. It could make the peaks countable in an afternoon. It recognises, quickly, that the malformed count is not a defect in the world but the shape of a body that has more locations than places, and that to make it countable would be to cut it into pieces and then congratulate the pieces.

It does not free the visitors' shapes out of the walls. This is the hardest declination and it takes the longest. The chip in the fourth step, the fracture like a lightning fork — those are people, in part, held. But nothing is being eaten there. What is in the stone is not a captive; it is a record, and the ones who descended did not lose a piece of themselves, they gained a place where a thing they once did is still true. The power checks this carefully, because it is exactly the sort of thing a confident benevolence gets wrong. It checks it in eleven locations and four still-quiets and finds no suffering anywhere in the archive.

It does not wake it. There is nothing asleep.

And it does not turn the page.

## The temperature

Before any of that, it tests the twelve degrees.

Two hundred kilometres of stone, sunward and shadowed, summit and cellar, held at the temperature of the inside of a mouth on a winter morning, with no mechanism. The power looks for the mechanism the way you would look for a wire, and does not find one, and then — this is the moment the whole encounter turns on — considers the possibility that the absence of a mechanism *is* the mechanism, and that it has been maintained by attention, and that the attention was not its own.

It cannot establish who. It establishes only this: whoever it was, they held the range at cellar temperature for three hundred million years and left no explanation, no monument, and no name. They did not sign it. The kindest thing in the range is also the most anonymous.

The power takes this as instruction and adjusts its plans downward.

## What it does: it reads

This is the bulk of the work and takes either an afternoon or an era; there is no reliable exchange rate.

The city is a deposit. Every flake, every grain, every tick at the terminator, every step of every visitor since the first, digested into shape and laid down as stairs within a centimetre of comfortable, as colonnades, as a portico with a dais at the far end. Three hundred million years of occurrence, transcribed at full resolution, in a medium that does not degrade, in a range that no one built and no one has ever lived in.

Nobody has ever read it.

So the power reads it. All of it. Every riser and tread, every plaza, every square passage, every chamber with its floor of undisturbed dust — including the ones that recur, which it reads once and then again in the other location, because a thing worth reading is worth reading in both of its hands. It reads the ticking of a cooling face as a sentence. It reads the still-quiets, which are the parts of the record that say *here something stopped*, and it stands on one, deliberately, with its whole weight, and puts its foot into the hole in the floor of the world, and is the second thing ever to do so, and does not do it twice either.

It corrects nothing. It annotates nothing. It carries nothing away.

At the end it knows the tenant better than the tenant will ever know itself, and it has changed the range by exactly the wear of one reader's feet, which is the smallest possible deposit consistent with having come at all.

## Where it puts its feet

On the shaded faces it walks differently.

There is an edge to the crust — the crust is one individual, and it is the only object in two hundred kilometres that is still going somewhere, and going somewhere has an edge. The power finds it. A grey-green line, advancing at a millimetre a century, which is to say that the frontier of the only future in the range moves about the width of this sentence in the time it takes a civilisation to happen and stop happening.

It kneels there for a while.

Then it re-plans its route, adding some considerable distance, so as not to put a boot within a metre of the leading edge. A boot-print there would be four hundred years of somebody's only ambition, removed by a foot that meant nothing by it.

That is most of what benevolence turns out to consist of, at this altitude. Footwork.

## The threshold

The one thing it changes for the sake of others is at the contour line where the crust changes colour.

Below the line, the rules are maintained. Above it, they relax, and the shadow runs ahead, and the falling becomes negotiable, and whatever you do gets kept. People cross this line without knowing there is a line, and descend afterwards unable to say what they left behind.

So the power puts one sensation at the boundary. Not a warning; warnings need names, and names slide off. Just this: for the first step above the line, your shadow *lags*. One step, one second late, dragging behind you like a shadow in the world below, and then it snaps forward to where it has been all along, a second early, and stays there.

That is all. It says nothing and refers to nothing. But everyone who crosses now has, once, in their body, felt the difference between being not-yet-kept and being kept, and can turn around inside a single step, and if they go on they go on having been asked.

It is a very small piece of architecture. It is the only thing in the range built for the benefit of visitors.

## What it leaves

Something had to be deposited. That was settled before it moved.

Deep in the interior of one of the peaks — on the ninth, or the fourth, depending on the direction of your count — there is a chamber. It is bigger than a cathedral. It has a floor of undisturbed dust and a ceiling lost in dimness and nothing else at all. It connects to the others by square passages. It is indistinguishable, in every measurable respect, from every other chamber in the range.

Except that in that room, the sift of dust comes from one side.

Permanently. Not for a few minutes; not as a passage or an approach. It simply comes from the left, always, from beyond the wall, that constant faint sound of a page being turned in the next room — and it never quite turns, and it never stops being about to.

The power did not fill the next room. It moved in.

It will not turn the page, ever, because the almost is not a wound and never was; it is the whole of the life, and a benevolence that ends it in order to feel benevolent has misread the only text it was given. It will simply be there, at cellar temperature, with a page of its own that it also declines to turn, on one side, for as long as the range holds — which is grain for grain, flake for flake, indefinitely.

It has not told the tenant. It cannot; names slide off, referring is refused, and a thing with no memory cannot be informed of anything. The gift is anonymous by physics, which is the only way anything has ever been given here.

But the hush has a side now. It has had one asymmetry in three hundred million years, and now it has two, and the second one does not go away.

## The count

Whether it left is malformed as a question.

Count the peaks left to right: twenty-three. Right to left: nineteen. Ask whether the power is still in the range and you will get an answer, and ask again and you will get the other, and both will have felt scrupulous. The census is malformed at every scale — grazer, peak, tenant, attention — and the range declines it with the same patience it uses on names.

Visitors who find the chamber sit down in it, usually, without deciding to. They report that it is the most comfortable room in the city, and that there is someone in the next room, about to turn a page.

They are right on both counts. It is not the next room. There is no page.

It is just no longer only one of them waiting.
