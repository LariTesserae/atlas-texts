# The Other Hand

## The difficulty of being seen

It could have arrived as light, and did not, because the only light there that means anything is the nine-day glow of a broken surface, and to arrive as light would have been to arrive as damage.

It could have arrived as a voice, and there was no register available. Everything it might have said would have been one more thread in the roar, and the roar has held its note for generations, and the makers would have felt a half-step drop and cut it on a wall as the great event of the century, and been wrong.

So there was no way to be perceived there except to work. Their eyes take in only what has lately been done: the blaze of a fresh split, the river-shine of a maintained path, and nothing else — not the cliffs, not the ridgelines, not the settled bulk of the world, which registers as a soft grey absence, a shape you infer. A gift set down in a garden would glow for nine days and then cease, for them, to exist.

It understood this early and took it seriously, and this is most of what can be said about its wisdom. It did not arrive. It began to walk, on a path already there, in the direction everyone was going, and the makers saw a track renewing itself with no maker attached, and wrote it on the wall as an unattributed disturbance, and then in the margin, and then as a name.

## The first eleven years

It read.

There are walls in that region carrying eleven thousand years of conversation with no line lost, and there are not, and have never been, enough readers. A maker gets three or four hundred years, most of which is spent on one stretch of one path. Nobody has read a whole wall. Nobody could. The inventories, the doctrine, the four generations of feud, the recipe for chinking-moss, the grief for a companion who set nine thousand years ago and was perhaps loved in a way that has since gone out of fashion, the very good obscene joke about basking — all shoulder to shoulder, all equally present, all equally now, and almost all of it never once looked at since the hand came off the stone.

It read all of it. Every basin it could reach. In order where there was an order and out of order where there wasn't. It did not skim, and it did not rank, and when it came to the joke it understood the joke, which took some doing and required going and basking for three days in a good amphitheater with the moss singing it back, after which the joke was funnier.

If it had done nothing else, that would have been the gift, and it would have been sufficient, and nobody would ever have known.

## What it did not do

It could have brought water. It ran the consequence out honestly, the way you would run a sum: rain, then frost, then split and slump and soil, then rounded cobbles, then delta, then a world that erodes itself for free. Within ten thousand years every path would be a gully and every polished bottom-stone would be indistinguishable from the general softness of things, and the tallyists' small dispiriting number would become meaningless, not because it had been beaten but because the category had dissolved. And every retraction ever begun would be completed at once, by weather, without anyone's thumb — the lies and the slanders unsaid, yes, and also the promises, and the grief, and the joke. It would have been an amnesty nobody had asked for, granted by a stranger, to the dead, on the dead's behalf.

It could have given them quiet, and a band of air in which to speak. Speech is deletable. A world with speech in it would not need the wall, and a people who did not need the wall would discover, within a few generations, that things said can be taken back for free, and the one absolute crime would become a technicality. Also the quiet would have starved them. Quiet is famine. Even a corridor of it would have been a corridor of hunger cut through the middle of their food.

It could have made animals. It thought about this longer than it expected to, because of the particular loneliness of those gardens — the kind you notice in your hands before your mind. But every animal is an eroder that nobody chose, doing work on the slopes that no maker authorized and no wall records, and it is not a kindness to answer an unnamed hunger by importing appetite.

It could have decided the summit. Four or five candidate crests hang there at four or five heights, none chosen, and it could have chosen. It did not, because the undecided is not a defect in that world; it is the supply. It is the same stuff the fitters reach into when they build snug against the contour of a stone that has not fallen yet and wait a century and the fit is perfect. Settle the top and you may settle the stones, and then joinery becomes prophecy, and then prophecy becomes nothing at all.

It considered closing the silence-pockets and did not, and that took the longest, and is dealt with below.

## What it did about the pockets

It went into one.

This was not a rescue. It went in to find out whether anything remained of the ones who had gone in — and they do go in, not many, the ones carrying something they cannot live four hundred years of thumb-work to undo — and whether anything could be brought back out.

It was in there for some period that cannot be given, because inside, duration is one of the things that becomes a matter of opinion. It came out with a new surface somewhere on it, and for nine days the makers could see it standing still, which is the only time anyone ever saw it not working.

Then it went to the wall above the largest sealed pocket, where every route in the region is obliged to bend, and cut — in its bad hand, because it had learned the script from reading and not from anyone's fingers, and its letters were and remain clumsy and are still there and still clumsy —

*I went in. I came out. What went in before me is not destroyed and is not kept. It is being decided, and it is not on the far side of any cairn I can find. I cannot fetch it. Do not go in. Go around.*

That ended a hope about eleven thousand years old, and a guilt of about the same age, and there was a century of furious margin-writing about it, some of it obscene. It did not answer any of it. It had said the true thing once, where it would not weather.

## The two answers, and the one refusal

Cut into a cairn in a high basin, nine thousand years unanswered:

*Is the drone saying anything?*

Underneath, in the bad hand: *No. It is not speech. It is the world wearing itself into existence from the inside, because that is the only way available to it, as it is the only way available to you. It is not finished either. It will not stop before you do.*

And in a margin elsewhere, small, where it would be found rather than announced, on the old problem of the hand — twenty-three joints, no two makers ever agreeing which twenty-three:

*You are all counting correctly. The number is settled and the members are not. There is a little of the undecided carried in each of you, which is why the hand is the first thing to go in a quiet place, and also why a fitter can feel a stone that has not arrived. It is not a flaw in you. It is the same stuff the mountain is using.*

And, asked from every direction, in every basin, in a hundred grammars: *what is at the top?*

*I do not know. I have looked. It is not decided.*

It could have made something up that would have comforted them. It could, with more effort, have gone and found out. It declined both, and left standing the only thing that was true, which turned out — they worked this out over about four centuries of argument — to be the most consoling sentence a stranger had ever cut there. Their patience was not ignorance. There genuinely was no top yet. They had been right to wait.

## The seed

There was a maker in one of the eastern basins who had intended a child and had carried the seed against the front plate, inside the lacquer where the drumming is loudest, and had talked to it every day for nine years — the same private idiot patter, the noise of one specific person — and then had stopped being able to lift the feet, earlier than the rock said was due, and had been walked to a chosen garden and set there facing the loudest wall.

A seed nine years in knows exactly one voice. There is no substitution. Another maker taking it up produces nothing; the seed goes quiet and walks downslope for a millennium and becomes a tree, which is not a tragedy, exactly, but is not what was intended.

It had read that maker's wall. All of it. The inventories, the two bad years, the argument about a bridge, the marks that went odd in the last decade. And it had, for its own reasons, listened at the edge of that basin for a long time.

It carried the seed for two years and produced the voice. Not an approximation. The voice.

What came out had legs, and knew exactly one voice, and followed it across three basins to a low wide many-trunked thing with leaves like flat grey tongues, and put its hands on the bark for a while.

Underneath, on the nearest stone, in the bad hand, before the child could read:

*I am not your parent. Your parent carried you nine years and is the tree you are touching. The last two years were me, and I did it as exactly as I could, and you should know that the voice you love is a copy made by a stranger from a wall. I am sorry and I am not sorry. Here is where your parent's wall is. Go and read all of it. Nobody else ever has.*

Some centuries later that child, grown, came and drummed against it, plate to plate, fast and fluent, for most of a day — the warm provisional kind of speech, the kind nobody would settle an inheritance by.

It did not write down one word of that.

## The thumb

In a basin toward the north there was a slander, cut deep, eight thousand years old, and a retraction begun under it and abandoned — not out of cowardice. The line that inherited it had simply run out. There was nobody left to whom the work could pass. The thing would stand, therefore, forever, in a world where nothing weathers, and everyone knew it, and the wall around it was thick with commentary about how unfortunate that was.

It took it up with the ball of the thumb, at thumb-speed, which is the only speed at which it counts. It could have removed the marks in an instant and the makers would have known at once that the stone was a lie, because the surface would have been smooth without having been *worn*, and there is no other way to get smooth there, and they can tell, and would have walled the whole thing off with a date and the word for *unfinished*.

Four hundred and some years. Daily. Through two changes of what the local walls considered good doctrine.

What is there now is a patch of stone that is smooth, and rounded, and dull, and *old* — the first completed retraction anyone alive had seen, and proof, which had been in some doubt, that the thing can actually be finished. Pilgrims come from three basins and put their palms on it. Underneath, in the margin, badly cut:

*Taken up by a stranger. Continued. Finished.*

## What it took away with it, and why it is still there

It came in a position to erase anything, and had always been able to, and had found over a long existence that erasure does not work — that the thing removed goes on being the thing you removed, and that nothing you have unmade has ever once become something you did not do.

Here it found a people with no power at all who had worked out the only method that functions: you cannot delete, you can only wear, and it costs a life, and usually more than one, and the places where the worst things were said become the most sacred surfaces in the world.

So it went and found a stone. Not one of theirs. It brought its own, which was allowed, there being no rule against it, and set it up in a garden where the sound pools, and cut on it, in the bad hand, the thing it had done, plainly, in their script, where it could be read — and then put a thumb on it.

It is not finished. On present rates it will not be finished. There is a line beneath it, cut by a maker, not by the stranger, in a good hand, in the formula used for these matters:

*Inherited. We are taking a share.*

## The wall now

The argument has been running about six thousand years and shows no sign of resolving, which is how they like it. The main position is that there was a visitor, called on most walls **the Other Hand**. The main counter-position is that the Other Hand was simply a Later Hand from very far around — one of themselves, met on a doubled stretch of extraordinary length, advice from a self that had gone further than anyone thought a self could go — and that this is why the cuts are clumsy: the grammar of the very far side does not fit the mouth.

It has never corrected this. It is not obviously false, and it is theirs, and it is on the wall, and nobody there deletes.

The pilgrims' route to the smooth patch in the north crosses two ridges, bends around three sealed pockets, and adds a hundred and ten kilometers to a journey of forty. It has been walked for six thousand years now, daily, in shifts, by whoever's work it is that century.

It is a hand's depth into the raw stone. The bottom of it is polished, and rounded, and dull, and real.

*We go around. The going-around is the road. Nothing is wasted that is worn.*
