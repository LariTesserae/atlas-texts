# The Attention Paid

## The problem of entering

Consider first the difficulty, because the difficulty is the whole of the wisdom.

This is a room in which a three percent change in the heat of an afternoon retimes the moulting of an entire population. A room where six blind animals navigate by the shape of a sound and would spend the rest of their lives correcting for a wall that had been there once and never again. Warmth would show in the shrimp within a day. Light would move the rope, and the rope's leaning is a record nine thousand years long and has no erasures in it. A voice would be a catastrophe.

There is no such thing as a small visitor here. Anything with a signal is an event, and an event in the Warm Cistern is a lie, because the rule of the place is that nothing new happens and everything alive has organised itself in perfect good faith around that rule.

So it came in with no signal at all. Not down the shaft. Not through the floor. It did not raise the temperature of the water by any quantity the water was capable of describing. It arrived in the only manner that leaves nothing on the instruments, which is as the bare fact of being noticed — and being noticed is not a thing the Cistern can measure, and it is also, as it happens, the one thing the Cistern has never had.

## What it looked at first

Not the census. The census is furniture.

It went under the floor, to the place where every human reasoning about this room ends in a shrug: the thing that feeds the pump. It found it in about the time it takes a stroke to complete, and what it found was not a miracle and not perpetual. It was ordinary, and finite, and it had a quantity, and the quantity could be counted, and the power counted it.

So it knows the year.

It did not say. It left the shrug where the shrug was, intact, load-bearing — because a room whose ending is known from outside is a room under management, and nothing in here has ever been managed, and it is too late in the day to start.

## The things it did not do

**It did not open the descent.** Somewhere below, in the first thousand years, a chamber silted and a machine lost its errand. The design is recoverable. The power could have cleared the run, and the water would have gone down through the made mountain the way somebody once cut and fired and laid nine thousand courses of brick intending it to go — the original purpose, restored, the drawing at last obeyed.

The water in this room has never been anywhere else. It has been in a loach that was a shrimp that was the rope that was a loach, to a depth of recursion that stopped being interesting eight thousand years ago. To finish the aqueduct is to open a vein.

And the intention was not owed anything. It was not a machine that broke. It was a machine that hatched.

**It did not make the room rich.** Given the warmth, the light, the ceaseless stirring, you would expect soup, and a benevolence in a hurry would supply the missing soup: seed the water, thicken the mats, raise the loach from six to sixty. But you can hang in the middle of this room with your arms out and touch nothing, and that is not a shortage. Sparse is not the same as poor. A thing may be nearly empty and still be entirely full, and one of the ways you can tell that a power is wise is that it can distinguish between a hunger and a spaciousness without being told which is which.

**It did not free the snail.** This is the temptation that would have felt most like kindness. Fourteen metres, one direction, a hundred thousand laps, a road three centimetres wide that meets itself behind your head. Open a way down to the reef. Smooth the seventeen rough places, which are, after all, only difficulties.

The seventeen rough places are the library. And the animal is not serving a sentence. It is saying one.

**It did not recut the inscription.** It read it, of course. Not off the wall — the wall lost it — but off the limp, which is a better copy than the wall ever was, being a rhythm and therefore self-repairing. It took one lap to read. Twenty-nine days.

Three clauses and a figure. The first clause was an instruction concerning a level. The second was an instruction concerning motion: that the water was not to be allowed to stand. The third was the kind of thing people put at the end of such notices, a curse, laid upon whoever should let the room go dry, and there is nobody left to curse and nothing here is dry. The figure was a height, in a unit that has not been used by anyone for eight thousand years.

The power checked the figure against the top of the monthly breath, where the surface comes back up and stops, and the tide-line's upper edge stands at the commanded height to within a few millimetres.

So it did not recut anything. You do not restore a text that is in force. Every clause on that wall has been continuously complied with since before the concept of a year, by a room that cannot read, and the compliance is more complete than reading; it is the thing reading is for. The letters are illegible because the obedience wore them away. Nothing has gone wrong here.
