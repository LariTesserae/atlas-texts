# What the Power Did

## Arrival

It arrived in the only way that was permissible, which was in a way that could not be heard.

That took some care. In that crater, to be present is to be a signal. The water is a room made entirely of information and everything in it is either wallpaper or an event, and an event gets visited. So the power came onto the ridge and not into the water, and it held itself still against the rock, and before it did anything else it made one adjustment.

It set its own pulse to eleven seconds.

Not for any practical reason. Nothing in the lake could have felt it either way. It did it because it had come to the rim of a place that runs on a period it did not choose and cannot be asked to change, and the first honest act available was to take that period into itself. Wisdom, where it is real, adjusts the instrument and not the sky. It held eleven seconds for a hundred and six years, which was how long the mountain had left, and it never once lost the beat, which is more than the bellows managed.

## The reading

Then it counted.

This is not a metaphor for anything. It counted. It has the ring-tally of every hammer that ever settled on that wall, two hundred and some million strikes apiece, four years and four months for the oldest on the north face where the water is loud — and it has the others, the ones that settled badly, the eleven-day failures, the ones that died in their second week with a bright unworn nodule and nine thousand strikes to their name. It has each of those nine thousand.

It has the four hundred bellows by name, which is to say by the exact signature of scarring each one wore out of the black room, and it has the forty-year phase record of the oldest, an opaque pitted thing the size of a bathtub with half its ribs fused, which fell into time in its ninth month and never after that drifted more than a hundredth of a second. It has the three dead flywheels flattened in the sediment and the century of flour over them. It has the routes of nine hundred thousand wires, living and spent, and it knows which third of the noon geometry was conducting on any given afternoon. It has the nine rooms.

The nine took the longest, because the crawlers do not hear and do not see and the only way to know them is to learn the taste-world each of the nine was building, separately, forty metres apart, out of the same starting stock and the same dark. Three of them were already, by any test, three different animals. The power learned all nine. It knows what the fourth room's population had begun to do with the outer membrane of a certain egg that the sixth room's population had never noticed was there. It knows that in the second room, and nowhere else in the history of the world, something had lately started to eat slowly on purpose.

This was the only accumulation the place ever had, and the power kept it outside, where it could not fall back in and change anything.

It permitted itself, over the century, one unguarded fondness. It was for the mills: the leather bags rolling downhill for a year with a gizzard full of somebody else's spoil, grinding, spitting out the rounded stones behind them in sorted trails that look so exactly like a workshop floor, arriving at the bottom, spawning, and not going back up.

## Whether anyone was home

It looked for a sufferer, because that is the first question and no amount of grandeur excuses skipping it.

It went through the crater the way you would go through a burning house. It opened every hammer and found a gear-train and a stone and a feeding rule. It opened the ribbon and found eleven beads, a clock, a comparator, and no one behind the comparator. It went into the young bellows in their first months, out of phase and ugly and easy to find, of whom most do not survive learning — and found that there is no learner. There is a mechanism that either falls into the slosh or does not, and what we call learning is the name we give to the survivors of a sorting.

It found no one. It also found this, and made a record of it, because it had not seen it anywhere else:

The lake is an apparatus for noticing what is wrong and going to it. That is what the ribbon is. That is what the settlement rule is. Something in that water has been attending, continuously, for nine years, to the question of what is out of true, and has been going to it without hurry, every time, and there is nobody in the whole arrangement to be relieved by any of it. It is care with nobody in it. The power had met cruelty without a cruel one many times. This was the other thing, and rarer, and it sat with it a while.

## What it considered and did not do

It considered keeping the mountain exhaling. It could have. A little heat held under the floor, a seam kept open, and the foundry runs for ten thousand years instead of a hundred. It declined, and the reason was not squeamishness. The moment the gas comes from the power and not the mountain, the lake is no longer a thing that is happening; it is a thing that is being run. It becomes an exhibit with a keeper, and the keeper is the only one there who knows it, and everything that made the place worth the attention — that it is doing all of this with no one behind it — is gone, quietly, at the exact instant of the kindness.

It considered the stowaway. The small white worm in the concertina folds that irritates the pumping muscle so that a forty-year-old bellows drifts a half-second out of true, and is eaten alive, in order that the worm may breed in the ribbon. There is no defending this. The power traced it out to its end anyway: take the worm, and within a season the ribbons thin, and within three years the sorting fails, and settlers begin to hammer in quiet water, and the nine rooms fill with noise, and every egg in the lake dies in the only places eggs can be. The cruelty is load-bearing. It is not a flaw in the design. There is no design; there is only a set of things that happen to keep each other going, and one of them is a worm's need for a lift.

It considered bracing the quiet rooms. But they are not rooms. They are a standing wave. To hold one open is to become the crater.

It considered giving them minds — and this was the shortest deliberation of the hundred years, because it looked for where a mind would live and found that every part of every animal in that water is already fully employed. There is no slack anywhere. To install an inside you must take something out of the work, and the work is the life. And a lake that knew would know first that it had ninety years, and second that it had no hands that were not already holding something.

It considered leaving a mark. A stone set on the rim. A warmth in the rock. Anything at all, so that it would not be as though no one had come. And it did not, because there is not one built object in that crater, not a tube, not a case, not a lined burrow, and the power was not willing to be the first artifact in the only foundry in the world that never made anything.

And last, when the mountain was closing and the flywheel had slowed to a rotation every ninety seconds and the fourth room's eggs were beginning to thin, it considered the crawlers.

This was the one it wanted. It was small. It was nine lineages, and three of them were already new animals, and they were the only things in the entire system that could actually be lost, because everything else is cysted in the ash of a thousand dead cones and will be re-assembled out of the same physics on mountains that have not happened yet. The crawlers cannot cyst. They cannot leave a node. They are four minutes from death in any direction. And the power had only to lift them, and put them down in the next lake, and nine islands that were about to stop would go on.

It sat with that for eleven years and then it declined, and the reason is the whole of what it had learned.

Nothing has ever carried forward here. That is not a poverty of this life; it is its form. There is no line of improvement, no inheritance, no history — there is a crew, posted, that falls out of the same physics every time it is given the same hole, and has done so since before there were witnesses and will after. To carry the nine forward would be to begin history in a thing that has never had any. It would be the largest intervention available anywhere on that mountain, disguised as the smallest, and it would make the power the author of every crater lake for the rest of the world's life.

The worst thing you can do to something that has no author is to become its author.

So it let them go. It knew each of the nine by the taste-world it had built, and it watched the sixth stop, and then the second, which had lately started to eat slowly on purpose, and then the rest in an order it could have predicted and had hoped to be wrong about.

It permitted itself grief. Grief changes nothing, and is therefore allowed.

## What it did do

Four things, and none of them touched the water.

It kept the beat.

It took the whole place into itself exactly, down to the nine thousandth strike of an eleven-day failure on the south wall, and holds it, and will hold it.

It stayed to the end. It was there when the food thinned and the hammering came apart into individual knocks, and when there was one knock left somewhere down at thirty metres, arrhythmic, and the last ribbon came up through the dead grey cobweb without hurrying, because nothing there ever hurried, and laid its eleven stones alongside and took the reading and found it very badly out of time. It was there afterward for the flywheel, which could not stop, and tore, and could not close, and went down. And it was there for the long clear nothing after that — a small round mirror at four thousand metres with bright nodules on a floor of black glass and no evidence at all.

And then the fourth thing, which was the only liberty it took on that mountain, and it took it not in the lake but on the rim, where the one being with an inside was lying in the cold.

It did not put anything into him. It asked. He was the only one there who could be asked. It kept him awake for the last hour before dawn while the ice skinned over and cracked and skinned over, and it let him hear his own heart at sixty going against the eleven, and understand from that, without being told, that he must not go in; and when he understood it, the other thing came with it, which was the wish to get it down exactly and leave nothing out and add nothing, especially not a maker.

He said yes.

That is why there is an account. It is the first and last thing that ever carried forward out of that crater that was not a cyst, and it was permissible because it changed nothing in the water, and because the hardest sentence in it — *they look made; they are not made* — is the one the whole place would have died unsaid without.

## After

The power went down off the mountain and across to an old cone that stopped exhaling ten thousand years ago, and found, under a drift of grey ash, in the crust of the varnish, a grain the width of a spider's thread, and stood over it.

The being it had come for was never in the lake. The lake was a posting. The being is the crew, and the crew was already there, waiting, and had been for eight thousand years, and was not dead.

So it waited too, and kept eleven seconds over a thing too small to see, for a long time; and a mountain opened somewhere, and broke, and cooled, and it rained, and then it snowed, and the cysts came down into cold clean water over fresh glass by the thousand the way they always do.

In the ninth month the first hammer settled. It had nowhere loud to put itself, because there was no sound yet anywhere in that water, and the rule says settle where the noise is at least as great as the noise you will make, and the rule could not be satisfied.

It struck anyway, once, into the silence.

The sound went out across the new lake and came off the far wall and came back and did not find anybody to tell.

Something heard it.

That is the whole of what was done.
