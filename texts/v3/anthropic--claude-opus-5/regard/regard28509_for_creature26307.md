## Attendance

It came in the last week of the short summer and stood on the ridge, and the first thing it did was nothing, and it did that for a long time.

It did not enter the water. Not once, not a finger, not a shadow. It had understood before arriving that in this crater the geography is made of sound, and that a body in the lake displaces sound the way a boulder displaces a river. Nine quiet rooms hold every egg of every species in that water, and the nine rooms are not places, they are cancellations — the arithmetic of a bowl of glass and four hundred pleated animals breathing every eleven seconds. Put anything of volume at fifty meters, dead center, and the arithmetic changes. So it stayed on the rim.

It measured its own visit in the lake's units, because it had the manners to use the local clock. It stayed until the flywheel had twice noticeably changed speed. Call it two seasons. In that time it moved perhaps four times.

---

### The being who lives here

It had come to be kind to whoever lived in the lake, and it lost most of the first season to the problem of the address.

The survey said seven kinds of animal, one thing that is not a plant, one thing that is only waiting. The survey before that said nine kinds of machinery. Both were honest and both were wrong in the way a list is always wrong about a weave.

It considered the felt. Oldest tenant by an order of magnitude no one in the crater could survive being told; up out of a boiled aquifer, four kilometers into the air, and down again alive in some fraction of a percent. Primary producer. Owner, in any court that recognizes prior occupancy. But the felt is forty arguments intergrown, has no inside, no now, and eats basalt at the width of a fingernail per decade. There is no one there to thank you.

It considered the crater itself — the loudest voice in the place, the thing that sets the tempo everything runs on, the hum at the pitch its geometry demands. But a shape is not a tenant.

It considered the lake as a whole, and this is where it stopped. The functions are real. The industry is real. The relief valve with no engineer is real, and it is genuinely, load-bearingly a valve. But the being that all of that adds up to has no interior from which to be spoken to. You cannot console a working arrangement. You cannot reassure a pressure gradient. The one whole thing here is the only thing with no ears at all, and everything with ears in that water is listening for exactly two facts: *is there food in the wall* and *is something out of rhythm*.

So it revised what it had come to do. Benevolence toward something that cannot receive it has to be spent on conditions instead of recipients, and spent carefully, because conditions here are wired to each other in ways that punish generosity.

### Set down

These are the things it worked out fully, in detail, to the last consequence, and then set down.

**Keep the mountain breathing.** Easy. Hold the vent open another thousand years, let the glass keep coming up hungry, and nothing in that water ever has to end. It ran it out. What it got was not a longer version of this lake; it was a different animal in every stratum inside forty generations, because every appetite down there is calibrated to a larder that is closing. Remove the closing and you have not saved them, you have replaced them, and been the sort of power that answers questions nobody asked. Nothing in the lake wants to continue. It wants to eat. Continuing is what happens.

**Vent the deep. Take out the waiter.** This was the one it had expected to do. Thirty or forty years of carbon dioxide accumulating in cold water under pressure, one rockfall away from a grey-white column and an hour of total death: a hazard, plainly, and within its power to simply lift out.

It looked, and found that the waiter is the pantry. The felt fixes its carbon out of dissolved gas. The thing that would kill the lake in an hour is the thing that has been feeding it for a century, and the bellows are not fighting it, they are *eating* it, and the reason the lake has never had to scream is that four hundred animals find the deep water delicious. Take out the gas and you starve the felt; starve the felt and the front stops advancing in the walls; stop the front and ten thousand hammers are striking unetched glass they cannot dent.

It wrote the general form of this down for itself, in case it ever forgot: *in an assembled place, every hazard is also a nutrient, and tidiness is a kind of arson.*

**Add a tenth quiet room.** This was the temptation that lasted longest, because it was so small. Chip out four cubic meters of the northwest wall — a morning's work, less — and the standing wave admits another node, another lens of silence, another nursery. Millions more eggs.

It did the acoustics. The tenth room does not appear beside the nine. The tenth room *is* the nine, moved: every node shifts between one and two meters, four of them into the wall, and every egg mass presently in the water finds itself at four in the morning in an antinode, which is a room that shakes things apart in days. The gift is not additive. There is nothing here to add to. There is only translation, and everything is already standing exactly where the sound lets it stand.

**Give something a mind.** It considered this for one full night and then never again. To hand awareness to a striker is to hand it the fact that it has been following, for its entire species' existence, a chemical front it cannot perceive, at the front's speed, forever, and that the mountain is running out. It would be the first thing in that lake capable of grief, in a world with nothing to console it and nowhere quiet to think except a room already full of eggs. The lake has pain in it, certainly — the regulator makes a living from pain. It has never had sorrow. It did not seem to the power like an improvement to be the one who introduced it.

**Carry the felt out. Seed it somewhere. Keep a copy.** The lineage is old enough to deserve continuation, and the volcano itself did the trick once, by accident, by throwing an aquifer into the sky. Doing on purpose what an eruption did by chance: surely allowed.

Except that the central fact of the crater — the one the nodule drifts keep trying to lie about — is that nothing here is made. The drifts look precisely like the scrap yard of a foundry and they are not, and everything the place is worth rests on that distinction. Plant the next lake and the next lake is an artifact. It would be a good artifact. It would be the power's, and not its own, and there would be one fewer place in the world that assembled itself out of appetite and arithmetic with nobody watching.

Then it looked more closely and found that the question had been answered before it arrived. There is felt in the ash on the ridge. There is felt in the rime that blows off the ice ring in March, in the dust that lifts when the scree resettles, in the millimeter of grit under a boot sole that came up from four thousand meters and will go down again. It has been leaving the whole time. It does not know it is leaving. Sixty kilometers north there is a vent that will open in some number of thousands of years, and the spring wind runs that way.

The most frequent finding of a wise power, in any place worth its attention, is *I was not needed for this.*

### Done

**One.** On the ledge above the deep basin, on the southeast wall, there was a block of glassy andesite about the mass of a horse, held by nothing but friction and the angle it had fallen at. In thirty-one years it would have gone, on a cold night, into ninety meters of loaded water, and the lake would have exhaled its whole century of installments in fifty minutes and been a mirror by morning — not at its ending but in the middle of it, for no reason, decades early.

The power went down the scree and took the block off the ledge and set it at the bottom of the talus, in the position and orientation erosion would eventually have chosen for it, gently enough that the surface did not ripple and the flywheel did not gain a thousandth of a rotation. That was all. It did not remove the waiter, or the risk, or the ending. It removed a coincidence. There is a difference between the death that belongs to a thing and an accident that merely interrupts it, and the whole art is in telling them apart and only ever touching the second.

**Two.** It learned the lake. Not as a copy that could be run again somewhere — that is a taxidermy, and it does not do that — but properly, the way you learn a language you will be the last speaker of. The exact pitch of the crater, which is a cello string a little flat. The eleven-second beat and its slow drift toward twelve as the water cools each autumn.
