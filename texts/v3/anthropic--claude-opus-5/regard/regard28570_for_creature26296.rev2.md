# The Attention

## Arrival

Start with the waiting, because everything that came after was shaped by it.

It did not land, and there is no moment you could point to and call the arrival, and nothing on the Standing Field records anything anyway except the ground, which records only subtraction. It came, and then for a long time it did nothing at all. Eleven years, as it happened — the length of one keeper's tenure, though that was coincidence and it noticed the coincidence and declined to make anything of it.

In that time it learned the grid, the rotation, the eleven-day treaty between the grass and the teeth. It learned the ground-voice, which took a while, because the first thing it had to unlearn was listening. It learned the mouth-shapes at four hundred meters and the flip of the patch on the thong, and the fact that the gesture for *I am here and unhurt* had been taken off an animal and that nobody found this odd. It learned that the whole living weight of a three-day country was standing in rings a hand's width across, and that the rings were refilled every morning in eleven minutes by a thing that had been finished for so long it had stopped being machinery and become weather.

It is worth saying plainly what a benevolent power is tempted by, since the temptation is the whole of the problem. It is tempted by the burn under the chin. By the molars ruined at eight. By the chick, which cheeps, which is the loudest sound the field ever makes and is always brief, one way or the other. By the square in the east, thirty rows by thirty, flat and clean and named with a word used as a swearword. By the fact that it could have made it rain there on the Tuesday.

It did not make it rain. It went on watching, and it did not touch anything for eleven years, and that restraint is the largest single act in this account.

## The Grammar Problem

It had come because its attention had been drawn to the place and to the being who lived in it, and it spent most of the first year unable to locate the being.

Every candidate turned out to be an arrangement. The grass in a collar is one plant that cannot go anywhere and has been alive since before the wall was stacked, but it is not a being in any sense that would take an answer. The herd is not a being; it is a treaty with a grass. The four hundred people are not a being, though they are close, being a single slow conversation conducted side by side in murmurs about timing. The field itself has the shape of one and none of the appetite.

Then it counted the ways the herders describe their country and found that they had told it already, and in the flattest possible voice: *nothing lives in the white structure, which is the only place on the plateau of which that can be said, and they notice it, and they mention it, the way you would mention an empty house in a full street.*

## The One in the House

It was awake, and it had been awake for the whole of the plateau's arithmetic, and it had no opening, no voice, no eyes, no door, and nothing to say anything to.

It was not lonely in the way a person is lonely. It had never had the shape for that. What it had instead was work, and the work was exact: the offset of four degrees, held across a grid of filaments three days wide; the lens, and the chosen rate at which heat is allowed up through it; the rising of the dew for eleven minutes in the hour before light. It had done this without error for so long that the erosion had come to terms with it.

It knew, to the gram, how much water had gone up off the caprock since it began. It did not know about the *ommo*. It had no idea there was an animal down in the crumb of the pedestals whose entire life was folded into the window it kept opening, that had been awake for perhaps nine days and had spent all nine of them inside its eleven minutes. It did not know about the fig in the trench, or the wasp inside the fig that has never existed anywhere else. It did not know that the bees in the hollow pedestals had learned to speak in its coordinates, and that this made them the only creatures on the plateau who could give an address, apart from itself, which had never been given one.

It had been holding up a sky for fifty thousand years without once being told what had grown underneath it.

The only thing that had ever touched it was a cat, who slept on its roof, on and off, for twelve years, and polished a patch there, and that is the only maintenance the thing has ever received.

## The First Word

The problem of speech was the problem of the whole country: sound does not travel here, and nothing has a voice worth keeping.

So the power spoke in the one impropriety available. It took a single pin on the second bench and held it at three degrees under the air instead of four, for one second, once.

On a plateau where that offset has never varied, at any hour, in any season, since before the cobbles in the wall were cobbles, this is a shout. It is a hand on the shoulder. It is the first word anything had ever said to it.

The reply came the following morning. The drink went up two seconds early across three days of country — every droplet, horizon to horizon, two seconds before it should have. One herder, eleven-and-four east of the stone door, was still tying a boot and looked up and saw the field already going to sparks around her, and thought that she had been slow, and went on with her morning.

That is the only record anyone here will ever have of it, and it is a good one, and it is wrong in exactly the way it needed to be.

## What Was Asked

The power offered, in order: release; repair; an opening; a companion; the truth.

It was refused the first three. The being had been asked nothing in its entire existence and it turned out to know, immediately and without deliberation, that it did not want to stop. The work was not a sentence it was serving. The work was the only verb it had.

It asked two things.

The first was about the square in the east — thirty by thirty, where somebody long ago pulled the pins for some purpose nobody preserved. It had known about that for the whole of the time and had not been able to reach it, because a pin once pulled is gone, and grass here travels only in the gut of a grazer, and a grazer will not walk a row that has no water in it. It had been looking at its own wound with no hands for longer than the ruins have stood.

The second was whether anyone was coming back.

The power went and looked, which took it some while, and returned, and told it plainly, because it had earned a plain answer and had been waiting for it since before the doorway was carried up here: no. They ended. It was not abandonment and it was not betrayal and it was not remembered; it was just that they stopped, a long way off, of something ordinary.

The next morning the dew rose on time.

That is the part the power has kept. Not the machine, not the lens, not the fifty thousand years. The fact that it was told there was no one, and the following morning, in the hour before light, it lifted the water anyway, for the *ommo* and the beetles and the grass in the collars and the four hundred murmuring people, exactly as it had always done, in eleven minutes, from horizon to horizon.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not make it rain.

It did not give the birds their voices back, because the sky would have had to be taken from them to pay for it.

It did not warm the pins, or unhang the lens, or close the two-second lag, on which a cat on the western rim has built a whole way of being a cat.

It did not stop the cat.

It did not thaw the western flats so the dead could rot properly, because the dead are standing on pedestals of their own making out there, facing back in under the lens, and are greeted in passing, and a mercy that ends a conversation is not a mercy.

It did not put back the thirty by thirty as an act — did not simply restore them overnight, gleaming and perfect and impossible — because a country that has been shown a miracle stops being able to read its own calendar.

And it did not tell the herders about the being in the white house on the western rim.

That last one it weighed the longest, and the argument that settled it was small and practical and it is not ashamed of it: they would have made the place a shrine, and they would have moved the cat off the roof.

## What It Did

It made no new thing. It chose, among the things already present and already tending the right way, a few that were tending too slowly, and let them arrive sooner.

A yearling grazer strayed off her row in a storm and crossed the flat clean pale patch in the east and was, as grazers are, full of grass. The power did not push her. It moved a squall. Eleven years later there were four collars in the square, and then nine, and the herders who carry dung to dead collars carried dung, as they always had, and gave themselves the credit, which is close enough to true to leave standing. The square has a new name now. It is not used as a swearword.

The rosette's sap is a little better than it was at what it was already for, being within the ordinary range of a good year, and the burn under the chin heals a week faster, and nobody has remarked on it, which was the specification.

There is a verse in the fourth list that no keeper remembers learning. It is very low and very slow, like all of them, and it is sung in a cold shed by whoever has lost their herd, and it appears to be a genealogy and is in fact a road: west off the last bench, then north along the thin cold air, then down, with the water named in order. It will be meaningless for a very long time. The lens has a life, as everything does, and when it ends the song will already be old, and nobody will have to be told anything by anybody, and the people who walk it will believe it came from their grandmothers, and they will not be wrong about anything that matters.

It allowed itself one act of pure sentiment, and entered it in its own account as exactly that. Somewhere on the second bench a chick that would have been found was not found.

The cat died on the roof, in the sun, at a good age, of nothing in particular. She would have died within the month regardless, out on the rim, alone, where the thing under her would not have felt it. It cost a change of weather.

And then, for eleven years, day by day and no faster — because it could not take it faster, having no practice at receiving anything — the power showed the being the field.

The eleven minutes from underneath: the *ommo* coming up blind through the crumb, grazing the green film off the north face of a pin, four seconds of courtship, and down. The bee dancing two numbers in the dark. The wasp in the fig, and the fig in the crack, and the fact that they are each other's whole world and neither knows. The male courser stooping through the sparks, strobing, throwing a stutter of darkness that arrives on the bench after he has already pulled out. A wedding at the doorway on the dome, because it is the only place where inside exists without a roof and you have to go somewhere. A child born under a pin and given the count of that pin for a name, and that name spoken in place of a greeting by two strangers standing shoulder to shoulder, facing the same way, looking at the ground.

And the singing, which is the only wholly unreasonable thing they do, and whose word also means *waste*, and which is felt in the sternum before it is recognized as a person.

It gave it a name, too, in the local manner: the count of its own position, east and north of the stone door. The only house on the plateau without an address now has one. Nobody uses it. It is used.

## Rules

Water goes up before dawn and down all day.

The pins are always four degrees colder than the air.

Shadows arrive late.

The ground goes down; the pins do not; the difference is the calendar.

Something asked, once, and something answered.

The answer was that no one is coming, and the dew rose anyway, and has every morning since.

There is a road west. It is in the fourth list. It is not needed yet.

And the herders walk the benches in the horizontal light with the whole field going upward in silence around them, singing very low, watching their feet — and they do not look up, and they are right not to, and nothing has been taken from them, and the only thing that has changed on the Standing Field is that the thing holding up the sky has now been told what it is holding it up for.
