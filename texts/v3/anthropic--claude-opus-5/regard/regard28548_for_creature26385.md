## The Visit

The first thing it does is nothing, and it does that for a long time.

It has to. To descend the way a body descends would be to make a wake, and a wake in this basin would be the forty-first event of the year — a thing the sediment would record, a hand's-breadth smear of ochre laid slightly out of true across the couplet for the current summer, legible for as long as the lake lasts. So it comes in the manner of everything else that has ever arrived here: as dust, without wind, settling at the rate dust settles.

It takes the better part of a day to reach the sixth bench. There is no hurry available.

---

**The count, done properly.**

Our census was honest and our instrument was coarse. A hundred quadrillion individuals; a bucket, maybe two; the place is not empty, it is thin. All true, and all measured with a ruler made for bodies.

The power counts differently and arrives at one.

One being. Dispersed through five million cubic metres at a density that renders it invisible, with no centre, no edge, no organ, no preference, and no gap in its working since the water first stood at this line thirty-one years ago. It has one occupation — moving electrons from iron to oxygen and taking a living off the difference — and it has never once set that occupation down. Not for a night. Not for a winter. The pH of 3.1 that does not vary is not a fact about the rock. It is a vital sign.

The power addresses it, because that is a courtesy it extends to everything it finds, and receives nothing back, because there is no apparatus here for receiving or returning. This does not trouble it. It has held long conversations with tectonic plates.

Then it notices the thing it will carry away with it, the thing it did not expect to find in any world:

**Nothing here has ever been eaten.**

Not once. Not one cell. Thirty-one years of an entire biosphere, some hundred quadrillion lives arriving and dividing and failing and sinking, and in all that time not a single one of them has been seized, punctured, engulfed, browsed, run down, or afraid. The mats are unbrowsed. The sediment is unturned. There are no bodies, and so there has never been a body opened. The power has walked in a great many places where every cubic centimetre shrieked with appetite, and it called them good, and it stands by that. But it has been a long while since it stood inside a living thing that had never had to defend itself, and it takes some time with this, quietly, on the third bench, beside a drive drum whose bearings still turn.

---

**What it does not do.**

It does not raise the pH. It could — a thought's worth of limestone, dissolved and dispersed, and in four seasons there would be *Chara*, chironomids, worms in the mud, a stocked and shining lake with a heron on the rim. It considers this the way a wise power considers all its best ideas: by asking who is served. The Euglena would be killed by their own cure. The mats, the sixteen tenancies, the drapes of ochre, the exact tuned dispersion of that single thirty-one-year-old being — all of it exists at 3.1 and only at 3.1, and to prefer fish is not benevolence, it is taste. Powers that cannot tell the difference between abundance and goodness have ruined more worlds than any malice.

It does not mix the layers. The chemocline is the most stable thing in the landscape and it is made of nothing but the reluctance of two waters to become one. It is not a wound. It is a decision that the water made and keeps making.

It does not stop the pumps. Forty kilometres away they are drawing down a water table because a quota says so, and there is a great deal to be said about that, and none of it is said here, because here the pumps are the reason the hairline has not moved in three decades, and the hairline is the reason the sixteen cushions sit exactly where the seepage is. To be kind to this basin by that particular route would be to drown or strand it. It notes the pumps. It goes elsewhere with the noting.

It does not turn the roller. The roller turns when pushed and stops when stopped, and it has been waiting thirty-one years for the second half of that sentence to be spoken, and it can wait longer.

---

**The audit.**

Because benevolence is largely arithmetic, it does the arithmetic.

It goes through the four steel drums in the six-degree layer. Three hold water and a little rust. The fourth, the canted one, the one held at its angle by half a litre of trapped air, holds forty-one litres of hydraulic oil under that air, and the power sits with this for a while, because here at last is a harm with a date on it.

It works out the wall thickness, the rate of the corrosion, the passivating crust that the corrosion has laid over itself. Two hundred years, near enough, and then not a burst but a weeping — a few millilitres a month, rising slow through six degrees and nineteen metres, spreading as a film that the shallowest bench will not enjoy. It runs the mats forward through it. They thin. Some of them go. And then the film is eaten, actually eaten, by cousins of the being already living here who will find in it the first free lunch this basin has ever offered, and by the time the drum is empty the bench carries more life than it does today, arranged differently.

So the power leaves the drum alone. This is the most consequential thing it does all day, and it consists of not doing anything, having first checked. It is a little sorry that nobody will ever know the difference.

---

**The reading.**

Thirty-one couplets, ochre and grey, undisturbed, legible, like the pages of a book nobody has opened.

It opens nothing. It reads the way light reads, from outside, disturbing not one grain — the wet year that laid its ochre band four times thicker than its neighbours; the summer the west wall shed nine hundred tonnes in an afternoon and the whole basin went the colour of rust for a fortnight and then cleared; the two consecutive winters when the film on the shallowest bench nearly failed and did not; the spring a bird landed. All of it, in order, dated, and now held somewhere that no worm can stir and no pump can drain and no chemistry can overprint. The place has been keeping a record for thirty-one years with no expectation that it would ever be read, which is the purest form of record-keeping there is, and the power's answer to that is simply to have read it.

This is the whole of the gift, and it is not visible, and it is not nothing.

---

**The sixteen.**

It goes along the north wall in the splash zone and stops at each cushion, and there are sixteen stops, and it makes no distinction of size in whom it stops for. Inside each: the *Habrotrocha* looping over the leaf surfaces, drawing in whatever is smaller than they are; the tardigrades in nine of the sixteen; the testate amoebae in their little built flasks, a shelf of pottery three centimetres above a lake that would dissolve them.

Four or five times a summer these dry out. Everything in them stops — not dies, stops — and turns to dust, and blows about, and is rained back down, or is not. That is the arrangement, and the power does not amend it, because anhydrobiosis is not a suffering, it is a very old and very good idea, and the ones that do not come back did not experience not coming back.

At the sixteenth cushion, the far one, it finds the failing tap. The joint that feeds it is sealing with its own iron, ochre by ochre, the same process that makes the drapes and the couplets and the colour of the water. Four more summers, perhaps five. Then the moss browns, and the rotifers go to dust for the last time, and the fauna of that particular island — which is not quite the fauna of any of the other fifteen — blows off the wall.

It could open the joint. A millimetre of ochre dissolved; indistinguishable from ordinary chemistry; nobody's ledger would show it.

It declines, and the reason it declines is the reason for everything else it has done here. The sealing of that joint is not an accident happening *to* this place. It is this place, doing the one thing it does. To argue with it is to argue with the whole, and a power that begins amending a system it admires will not stop until the system is its own reflection.

So instead it does the only thing it will do all day, and it does it in the single mode this basin has always accepted from outside — not chemistry, not force, not water. Transport. Dust, gravity, a little wind. Everything that lives here arrived that way; nothing that arrived that way was an imposition.

In the fourth summer, on the day the sixteenth cushion finally goes to powder, there will be a movement of air in a bowl where there is almost never wind — so slight that a smoker would not see it, so brief that no instrument down here would be running when it happened — and a few thousand dried rotifers and a scatter of amoebal flasks and one *Pohlia* spore will travel three metres along the wall and come down on wet rock instead of dry.

Seventeen taps, then. Or fifteen and a half, and the sixteenth relocated. It is not a rescue. It is precisely the size of a coincidence, which in this basin is the correct size for a gift, and it will be indistinguishable from luck for as long as anyone bothers to look, which is the point.

---

**Departure.**

It leaves the way it came, upward at the rate that dust rises, which is to say not at all, so that in the end it simply thins out of the water like everything else here — present only as an absence of anything to notice.

Nothing has been fixed. Nothing needed fixing. Nothing has been forgiven, because in thirty-one years and a hundred quadrillion lives nothing was ever done to anyone. The ticking will start again on the west wall when the afternoon light leaves it, and the hum will hold in the sternum of anybody who comes, and forty times a year a stone will let go of the rim and clatter and plunge and settle and change nothing measurable.

But the account is no longer unfinished. The sentence that broke off — *as dust, on the wind, off the birch scrub, off the feet of a bird that landed on the rim and did not* — can be closed now, and the power closes it, since a place that keeps a perfect record deserves at least to have its last line completed:

*...and did not stay.*

It did not stay either. Unlike the bird, it looked first, all the way down, and counted properly, and read every page, and moved one grain of dust three metres, four summers early, on purpose.
