## A Visitation, Drawn as Weather

**I. Arrival, and the matter of speed**

Notice is instantaneous. Arrival cannot be.

The power understands this before it understands anything else about the plateau, and the understanding is the beginning of its wisdom here: that to descend at the speed available to it would be to arrive as an *event*, bright and quick, in a place where nothing bright or quick has happened for an era, and where the only lethal condition is precision. A sudden advent is a tolerance. It would engage. It would take load. Something would be found, for the first time, to have failed.

So it takes eighty years to come down the last kilometre.

For the first age it does nothing but listen, and listening costs it more than the descent. To hear in depth you must give up hearing in order; the sound column is not a sequence, and a mind that insists on sequence will hear only noise silting where it should not. The power lays its own chronology aside on the sinter like a coat. It does not pick the coat up again for a long time. This is the first thing it gives away and the least noticed, and it is not a small thing to give.

By the end of the age it can hear the third note of a triple-knock nine hundred years before the cam exists, and it can hear the herd hearing it, and it has learned the only fact that governs everything it will subsequently decline to do:

there is almost nothing wrong here.

Benevolence is easy to picture. It arrives with hands full. Wisdom is the part that costs, and the cost is mostly borne as restraint.

**II. What it declines to bring**

Water, obviously. Water first.

It could. This should be said plainly, because the refusal means nothing otherwise: the power has the leverage to overrule the gland, to reach upstream of the upstream, to hold a fill event open against a peristalsis that has never once been resisted. Flasks would stay full. Rain would be *thinkable*. Within a few thousand years there would be a channel, and then a fan, and then a dendritic scratching across a continent of curves, and a landscape answerable to a river, and forty grazing districts shocked cold and cracked open along their cherry seams — mended, in the sense of being made accurate at last, and dead.

It declines.

And then it does the more careful thing, the thing that is nearly invisible and is the actual courtesy: it comes onto the plateau carrying nothing at all. No flask, no reservoir, no dampness in any sense the gland could detect. Because the gland's one act is to revise a knowing along with the water, and that revision is the single violence anywhere in this ecology — the only place where the interior of a witness is entered without asking.

The power will not be the reason that happens. Not even once, not even to a witness who would never know.

Through the entire visitation the valve sleeps. That is the compliment paid, and nobody receives it, and it was still worth paying.

**III. What it declines to mend**

It spends a decade with a single gear tooth.

The tooth is rounded to a nub. Its flank is scored in a pattern that is, read correctly, three hundred years of a particular basin's crosswind. It does not engage; it slips; the slip is one of a hundred thousand slips whose not-quite-catching is audible from twenty kilometres away and is called, from outside, a roar, and is in fact the animal's stability said out loud.

The power could true it. The impulse is real and it is not a stupid impulse — it is the impulse to relieve a strain, and there *is* strain, and the strain is constant, and it has been constant for an unimaginable time.

But healing requires a design. You cannot restore a thing that was never specified; you can only impose a specification retroactively and then measure the animal against it, and the animal will fail. The failure would be new. The failure would be introduced by kindness, arriving from above, at speed, with the best intentions in the world.

It leaves every tooth rounded. It leaves the ovalled journals oval. In the whole of its stay it does not mend one thing, and it makes sure that the lappers never lose a single grazing-face to its presence, and it counts this among its works.

**IV. What it declines to say**

It knows the number.

It knows eleven and it knows three hundred and forty and it knows why both are locally correct and what would have to be true for either to close, and it says nothing, and it will say nothing, and the withholding is deliberate and permanent and is perhaps the most loving thing done on the Sinter Plateau in a million years.
