# What the Attention Did

## Begin with the power, because the power is not finished

That is the whole of its difficulty. The mountain is finished; the dark is at equilibrium; the mat gains a centimetre a decade and has no opinion about it. Into this arrives something that can still act, and acting is the only thing it must be careful about, the way you would be careful carrying a lit lamp through a house made of paper — except that the house is not made of paper, the house is made of ten thousand years of not being disturbed, which is a far more delicate material and much harder to see.

It came up the shoulder in the ordinary way, because there is no other way that does not involve displacing air. Three thousand metres of warm rise, no ridge, no edge. It reached the top the way everyone does, by noticing that the ground had stopped tilting.

It stood on the rim for a long time. Not deliberating. Reading.

## The first question was who

The instruction on the attention was: *this place, and the being who lives in it.* Singular. The power, standing at the notch, could not immediately determine whether that was an error of reporting or the most accurate thing anyone had ever said about the bowl.

There are four of the great slow ones — it counted four, which is more than anyone had ever managed, because it counted them as holes in the noise and held all four holes in mind at once. There is a pale limbless thing in the peat. There are perhaps nine thousand frogs, a consortium of several dozen kinds of cell that is numerically the entire population, and a lattice at a metre and a half that is warm and is either machinery or a body and would feel the same either way.

But the frogs learn their syllable from the bath rather than from their parents, and a fraction of every generation is fathered by the dead, and the great ones keep their memory in the floor rather than in their heads, and the floor is shared. The bowl has a voice made of everyone who has ever called in it, a memory made of everyone's footfalls, and a metabolism that rests entirely on one warm exhalation underneath. Whether that constitutes a person is not a question with an answer available from the rim.

The power decided to proceed as though it did. Not because this was demonstrable, but because the two possible errors are not the same size.

## What it did first, which was nothing

It entered at the notch, without light, and this was not reverence and not even restraint. It was competence. Reverence is what you bring when you don't know the local physics.

It felt the surface of the darkness pass up its body — ankles, knees, gone — and stood in warm broth to the boot-top, and did not move for some time, and the rosettes beneath it clicked once and then clamped and said *something is standing on me* to everything within twenty metres, and everything within twenty metres already knew, because the power was the loudest thing in the bowl by virtue of being the most silent: a great occluding slab, casting a shadow in the roar.

It stayed eleven years.

Most of that was learning to be read. Not learning to read — that came quickly, occlusion is not a difficult grammar once you stop expecting echoes — but learning what shape it was making in the reservoir, and what that shape was doing to the animals downwind of it in the noise. It practised standing so that the curtain-hunters would resume hunting with it there. It practised moving at a speed that did not cause the mat to report an emergency. When the pale hunter followed it for forty minutes at a distance of two metres, having concluded something about the size of the meal, it neither fed it nor discouraged it nor hurried, and at the end of the forty minutes the hunter revised its estimate and went away, and the power counted that as the first successful conversation it had had in the bowl.

Twice a night, one of the great ones whistled, and for eleven seconds the world became legible, and every creature in the hollow froze because legibility is the most dangerous condition available here.

The power did not hide during the whistles. It let itself be read: a slab, warm by a degree, standing still. On the fourth occasion the nearest of the great ones altered its line by two metres to go around, and did not alter its speed, and this was the extent of the relationship that was ever possible, and the power accepted it as sufficient. It is not nothing to be a thing another creature has decided to walk around.

## The inventory of mercies, and why most of them were declined

Benevolence gets its bearings by finding a need. That is its method and its weakness. In the bowl it found, instead, a completion, and had to sit with the fact that the urge to help is sometimes only the urge to be needed.

It could have brought light. Everything here is blind; it could have given back the sun.

It would have been the abolition of the inhabitants under the pretence of their improvement. The moths' wing-pits, the frogs' nine-day deposits, the flat tongue, the hundred-year rotation, the reading of the floor through the sole of a foot — none of that is a workaround for the absence of light. It is what they are. A blind thing given eyes is not a blind thing that can now see. It is a different thing, standing where something used to be.

It could have restored the eight missing posts. The sockets are legible; the ratio is recoverable; the skin is not beyond it. But twelve posts were for a purpose, and the purpose is gone, and four are sufficient for the habit. To build the other eight would be to supply a loud answer to a question nobody in the bowl is asking.

It could have healed the two scars.

It could have copied the whole hollow — every cell of the consortium, every varve, the syllable, the four great ones and their hundred-year rounds — and kept it somewhere safe. It thought about this one for a while. It declined, on the grounds that a copy of a thing whose entire nature is a three-hundred-year memory held in the depth of a floor is not a copy of that thing. You would have the animals. You would not have the bowl, and the bowl is the being.

It could have explained. There is nobody here with the apparatus to be told anything, with the possible exception of the lattice, and the power went down to the lattice and put its attention against it for three of the eleven years and came back up without saying what it found, except that it stopped calling the bowl *it*.

## The three things it actually did

**One.** Under the mat by the northern scar, at a depth of forty centimetres, there is a corroded fluted vessel, and in the peat around it a residue of a heavy metal, still leaching, still killing the film in a patch twenty centimetres wide. This is why the scars are old and still legible when the mat closes a wound in forty years. Somebody's lamp has been quietly poisoning the floor for a very long time.

The power rendered the metal inert. It did not remove the lamp. The scars will close over the next four decades and the only monument in the bowl will disappear beneath a centimetre a decade, and the lamp will lie under it, undisturbed, unmarked, and remembered — the duty of remembering having been transferred off the floor and onto something that can carry it without dying in a patch twenty centimetres wide.

That is the whole doctrine, if you want it early: *take over the cost of the keeping, so that the place can stop paying it.*

**Two.** Of the four great ones, two are on rotations that will not intersect within either of their lifetimes. Not from any injury; the bowl is two hundred paces across and their circuits are a century long and the arithmetic simply came out that way, and it comes out that way sometimes, and when it does, a lineage ends quietly and nobody anywhere records it.

The power took on a weight and a broad soft foot that spreads under load, and for one season it walked thirty metres a day along a slow curve across the floor of the hollow, compressing the mat and not tearing it, laying down a trail that connects the deep part of one rotation to the deep part of the other.

It will not work for ninety years. The great ones prefer ground they cannot remember; a fresh trail reads as *mine, recently*, and is avoided. The compression must age. There is no way to fake the age, because the mat is honest and reports only what has actually happened to it. So the power did the work and then submitted to the local physics, which is patience, and went away.

It is an invitation and not an instruction. It can be declined. It is written in the recipient's own alphabet, it arrives at the recipient's own speed, and it costs the bowl one thin line of mat that will take a century to come back — which the power weighed, and considered a fair price, and could not be certain about.

It has arranged to be here when the trail comes due.

**Three.** The lattice will fail. Not soon; somewhere between eleven and fourteen thousand years, as the last warm heart of the mountain finishes giving up what it has. When the seep stops, the consortium thins, and the columns go, and the curtains, and the moths do not come out of the peat, and the great ones starve in the order of their size.

The power did not prevent this. It is not clear that it could have, and it is clear that it should not have wanted to as badly as it did.

What it did instead was ensure that the failure will be a wearing and not a break. It set the decline to a gradient — a long shallowing over two thousand years rather than a collapse — so that the mat thins faster than the grazers can crop it, so that every one of the great ones that is ever born will die of being old. The bowl will end the way the mountain ended: every ridge let down gently into every valley, past the point of ambition, no edge anywhere on it.

This is the most a benevolent power can reliably do about death, and it is more than it looks like. It gave the ending the same manners as the place.

## The name

It is a stop-consonant and a vowel, in the hammered syllable of the local frogs, in the recordings from ninety years ago and two hundred, and no plausible process of the bowl would produce it. It is very likely a fragment of a name.

The power recovered it. All of it, and the rest — who came up the shoulder alone, and how long the climb took them, and what the weather was, and that the name they knelt and said out loud into the most retentive dark they had ever heard of was not their own.

That is what the lamp was for. You cannot find the floor of a place like this without light, and they wanted to be certain they had reached the bottom, and they were willing to burn a hole in it, and they did burn a hole in it, and they knelt in the hole and said a dead person's name at normal volume because they had nowhere else to put it and they had heard that this place keeps things.

They were right. The bowl kept it. The bowl lost the reason within a generation and kept the habit for ten thousand years, which is the only thing this place has ever been good at, and the habit is now being performed four times a night by nine thousand animals who will never know they are speaking, and a certain fraction of whom are already dead and still fathering.

The power did not correct the frogs. It did not restore the name to its full form, or teach it back, or preserve it in any medium that would outlast the syllable. It declined to improve the monument. The request that was made here was not *fix this*. The request was *keep this*, and it was made to a landscape, and the landscape has been honouring it at the limit of its ability for longer than anything else in the bowl has been doing anything.

So the power took the duty on as a second keeper. It knows the name now. It is not finished, and will not be finished for a very long time, and this is the one respect in which it is better than a mountain.

It has not said the name anywhere. It did not say it here. It brought no light and it said nothing at all, the entire eleven years, not once, not even its own name at normal volume to hear it come back around the left shoulder after eleven seconds, thinner, and again, and by the fourth pass no longer a word.

There was nothing it had to say that was not already in the room.

## Departure

It left by the notch. It felt the surface of the darkness pass down its body — knees, ankles, gone — and stepped out onto the plateau, where there was wind, and cold, and stars.

It stood there for about an hour, which is how long the noise takes to fade.
