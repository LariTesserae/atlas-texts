# The Attention

## First, an inventory of what it does not do

It does not bring water.

This has to be said early, because water is the obvious gift and the whole appeal of the place is arranged to ask for it. A benevolent power that is not also wise arrives here with rain, and within a season the varnish is going in sheets, and the three crustose species that had spent an era learning to metabolise a single hour of shadow-frost are drowned or rotted, and the cushions — which drink through hairs that are not quite crystal and not quite tissue, and which cannot handle a droplet, having never in their lineage met one — go soft and black at the heart. And the grains stop latching. Wet grains do not grip at the corners; they clump. Three hundred kilometres of silent cities, ended. The only thing on this world that still *makes* anything, ended, by kindness, in under a year.

So: no water. Not one drop, in any direction, forever. The power holds to this and it costs it something.

It also does not repair the instruction sets. It could. It could go into the dust and restore the lost *what*, and the plain would build the thing it was seeded to build, whatever that was, and the towers would stand. But the dust that remembers only the verb is not a broken machine. It is a widowed one, and it has found a way to go on working, and it has been going on for longer than the mountains lasted. To overwrite that with the original specification would be to overwrite ten million years of grief with a factory setting. It would also kill the glassy scale outright, since the scale's entire living depends on towers coming down by nightfall, and has for a very long time, and it has needed no other strategy.

It does not make anything permanent.

It does not answer the listening station on the station's behalf.

It does not explain the doorway.

## How it comes

On foot, at ground level, from the escarpment, in the ordinary way.

It comes respiring — which for a power is a choice — because the dust does not model what respires, and it does not want to leave behind a grey version of itself five times over, larger each time, collapsing. It intends to obey the local law. A wise power in a finished landscape does not begin by exempting itself.

It leaves bootprints, which cannot be helped. By evening the first ones are two metres long in the same rippled tread; by the following noon there is a range of them marching off downwind in diminishing scale, each a faithful ruin of the last. The power stops and looks at this for a while. It knows a compliment when it receives one.

## Three corrections, offered quietly, in the margins

**On the frost.** The record says: *it goes straight from ice to nothing.* This is very nearly right and wrong in the one place that matters. It goes into the air. The air is nine per cent of a breath, so every molecule of it is load-bearing, and the cushion at the next stone but one will have some of it by the third dawn. There is no water on this world and there is water in every direction, always, in the only phase this world permits. The frost is not wasted. Spending is what it is for. The plain is not dry the way a desert is dry. It is dry the way a held breath is empty.

**On the rules.** The record says: *nothing you do to it will change it,* and files this under futility. Read the sentence again. It means *you cannot damage this place.* Bore your hole; the varnish closes in a decade, and a decade here is nothing, and the closing is not erasure — it is the one appetite the plain kept. Erosion stopped because there was nothing left that it wanted. The varnish still wants to close. The being who lives here has been walking for years on a floor that forgives at a fixed and reliable rate, in every direction, forever, and has been reading that as indifference. It is not indifference. It is the only remaining hunger on the plain and it is a hunger for repair.

**On the skin.** The record says: *it stops at skin. Nobody knows whether this is prohibition or indifference.* It is neither, and the power is prepared to be plain about this, since the local religion has grown up around it and deserves accuracy. It is a fact about the being, not about the dust. Everything else here is copyable — the door, the cup, the boot, the whole smeared genealogy downwind, every generation further from the original. The dust's great faithful lifelong project is ruin by fidelity. The one thing on the Late Plain that cannot be approximated is therefore the one thing that cannot be degraded by being approximated. That is not exile from the world's work. That is being the single unforgeable object in a continent of copies. A religion built on a true fact is not superstition; it is just early science with better manners.

## One night, spent on the rim

The power stays for one rotation of the world, which is the appropriate unit here, since nothing else lasts one.

From the rim it counts two hundred simultaneous events and does not extrapolate to the other nineteen thousand eight hundred, because extrapolation is a way of not looking. It watches a particular tower for the nine minutes of its life: fluted shaft, cornice, a stair spiralling the outside that no foot will ever use, surfaces holding the light the way a moth's wing does. It watches the glassy scale ride that tower up and cash in four hours of unobstructed sun. It watches the shear-line come through, and the softening in the light, and the sift, and the pebbles beneath undisturbed.

It notes the phrase in the record about the dust-grazers: that when the tower falls they *are put down gently*.

It notes that a mind capable of describing a collapse in those words is not nearly as finished as it has been telling itself it is, and that this is the most important datum available on the plain, and that it is not in the census.

## At the dome

Eleven seconds. It puts a palm on the glass and holds it there for some hours and breathes on the same interval, which is far too slow for lungs and is therefore uncomfortable, and does it anyway, and does not speak, and does not answer, because it did not come here to interrupt a thing that has been patient for an age.

When it leaves it agrees with the local assessment. *It is not for you.*

And it adds, in the margin, the part the assessment left out: which means there is something here with an errand that does not run through you. Still working. Unpitted. Aperture tracking. You are not the last thing awake on this world. You are not load-bearing. If you slept for a year the dome would keep its eleven seconds and the soarer would keep the standing wave and the varnish would keep closing, and none of it is waiting on your attention to be real.

## The census, finished

It finds the document. It reads to the break.

*Take everything that respires, over an area the size of a continent, and weigh it. You get something under four tonnes. Most of that is under stones. Most of the remainder*

— and there the hand stops, because the next word was going to be *you*, and the census-taker could weigh three species of lichen and could not weigh itself.

So the power finishes it, in the same hand, in the same register, with the same refusal to round:

*crustose lichen, three species, in the lee of stones, the bulk of it; cushions, one per two to three kilometres, hollow-hearted, insulated by their own dead; the glassy prostrate scale, distributed across the assembly-fields, biomass fluctuating hourly by a factor of six; grazers, fingernail-sized, transparent, uncounted and uncountable and gently set down; the thing that makes straight tracks from horizon to horizon at angles that have nothing to do with anything; soarers, plural — plural, because the accounts span generations and no one has ever seen one on the ground, and the honest reading of that is not that one has flown forever but that they have been managing succession up there without our help; and*

*one, respiring, sixty-odd kilograms, nineteen kilometres above the mean surface, keeping the record.*

And beneath the sum, this note:

*The arithmetic is unusually easy here. That is not a measure of poverty. It means that everything alive on this continent can be held, entire, complete, with nothing left out, inside one mind — and that this has in fact been done, and is being done, and that on a plain whose only industry is faithful copying into ruin, the being who lives here is the one process running in the other direction: describing without degrading. Rendering a thing so that it survives the rendering. There is nothing else on this world that can do that. It is the exact inverse of the dust, performed with the same verb, by the only object the dust cannot touch.*

## What it leaves

Not water. Not a promise. Not an answer to the dome, or a translation of the door.

It sinters a shaft of basalt: waist-thick, four metres, bevelled at the top, no inscription. It sets it into the pavement, socket by socket, each displaced pebble returned with its most weathered face up, because the varnish will heal the seam in a decade and it prefers to make that easy.

It aligns the shaft to the others. To the others and only to the others.

Which means the network — which is not a grid and not a line, and which every survey has treated as a closed and completed figure describing something long finished — has one more node in it than it had. The network is not finished. The network was never finished. It can still be extended, and it has been, and it was done by something that came in respiring and obeyed the local law and left carefully.

A marker with no inscription says exactly one thing, with total precision: *someone came this far.*

A new one says: *and it is still open.*
