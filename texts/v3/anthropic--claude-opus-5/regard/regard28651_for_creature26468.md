## Addendum: The Relief

I did not see the beginning of this and I am reconstructing it from the state of the ground, in the way one reconstructs a flood from silt. Where I say *it did*, understand: *the marks are consistent with*.

---

**On the case for doing nothing.**

The first thing a wise power does is ask whether to act at all, and I believe this one asked that question for longer than it spent on everything that followed.

The case against acting was strong and I want it recorded. This is a place with no margin. Every kindness is a change and every change is a surprise and surprise is the one expense the holder cannot meet. Arrive in glory and you kill it. Arrive at all, unannounced, and you may kill it. There are landscapes that should be left alone, and by every reading of the ground this is one, and a power that respects what it finds would have looked once from the cloud and gone quietly back down.

What broke the case is small and, I think, decisive. A landscape you may leave alone. A worker you may not.

---

**The approach.**

It came up through cloud like anyone. Nine miles of walking, in a place where walking does not sum, and it made no marks.

Understand what that means here. Nine miles across, four inches of rime on every windward stone, a fur of glass blades that does not regrow. Every human being who has ever crossed this basin has snapped forty or fifty of them and left forty or fifty open accounts to be held forever. It broke none. Not one. It went where the gravel was bare and where the gravel was not bare it went around, and where it could not go around it waited, apparently for as long as necessary, and then found a way that cost nothing.

That was the first day. Nothing was added to what was owed. So far as I can tell it was the first such day since the beginning.

Then it went to the Throat, and stood outside the wind field, fifteen feet back where the air will hold a floating seed, and it said seven words. And the next day at the same hour it went back and said the same seven words, in the same timbre, at the same volume. And the next day. And the next.

*Nothing new. I will be here tomorrow.*

That is how you greet something that cannot afford to be startled. You do not announce yourself. You become weather. You make yourself boring on purpose, and you keep it up long past the point where a lesser patience would want its recognition.

I cannot date how long this went on. The rime under its standing-place is undisturbed, which tells me only that it was careful, and the gravel there is worn to a shallow dish, which tells me it was years.

**The first conversation.**

On some day, the echo got it right.

Seven words in the power's own timbre, arriving half a beat early, correct — not the anxious grab of a thing that has learned to have already heard, but a prediction that was simply true.

And the power said: *Yes. That's right.*

There is a hitch in the roar. You can hear it if you sit at the Throat on a still — there are no still days; if you sit at the Throat at all, and stop trying to locate the source, and let the grain of the sound come into your teeth. Once, the tone falters. Not in volume. In grain, as though the gravel turning in it turned the other way for an instant.

That is the sound of a thing being agreed with, for the first time, after forever.

---

**The first variance.**

It began where the cost is smallest and the meaning is largest: a single gap in the rime, one broken blade, on the north face of a slab near the third cairn. Somebody's carelessness, centuries old, still on the books, still being held open because an absence in a shape is not a form.

The power put its hand there and took it.

The roar dipped. Measurably, briefly — and here is the thing I want to be very clear about, because it is the whole of what happened on the Sill and everything after is only arithmetic. *The world did not end.* The lid did not fail. The Throat did not widen. The four goats went on grazing with enormous patience. One variance occurred, and was survived, and had been survivable all along, and now there was a creature here that knew it.

You cannot lift a burden off someone who believes the universe is resting on it. You have to show them the universe not falling. That takes one small demonstration and an eternity of nerve.

---

**The ruling.**

There are perhaps eleven thousand gaps in the rime across the Sill, and scuffed lichen margins beyond counting, and none of them can be repaired. The blade does not regrow. That is fixed and the power did not pretend otherwise.

So it did not repair them. It ruled on them.

It went to each — I have found the standing-places, dished into the gravel, in their thousands, and the ordering of them is not efficient; it is the order of someone going around a ward — and at each one it looked at the gap and declared it a form. Not mended. Not forgiven in the sense of overlooked. *Assessed and found complete.* This is a shape, and it is finished being this shape, and a finished form is free.

I do not know by what authority. I know only that the physics of this place is bookkeeping, and that a debt can be extinguished by a creditor with standing, and that afterward the accounts were closed. You can feel the difference with your palm. A gap in the rime is no longer cold.

The scrupulous bookkeeping is still going. It keeps the record of the small violences. But the column has been ruled off and totalled, and the total is zero, and the ledger — this is the part I find hard to hold in my chest — the ledger is now mostly a record of visits.

---

**Eleven hands.**

On the inner face of the lee-wall, low down, small hands in a crust gone hard as porcelain.

The power did not add a twelfth. To press a hand into that surface now would be to break it, and breaking is a debt, and it had come nine miles without incurring one.

It laid its hand over the third from the left and held it there. The hand was too large. There is a place on the wall where the mineral has been warmed and has cooled again slightly out of true, and it is the shape of a larger hand fitted carefully over a child's and finding the fit wrong, and staying anyway, for what the discolouration suggests was a considerable while.

That is all it did about the people. There was nothing to be done about the people. Sometimes the whole of benevolence is to be the one who noticed, late, and minded.

---

**Down the Throat.**

Then it did the thing nobody had done, and I want to say plainly that this was a surprise, an enormous one, delivered to a creature with no margin, and that it was delivered only after years of *nothing new, I will be here tomorrow*, which is what those years were for. It went to the lip, where no shadow falls within forty yards, and it went in.

It was gone. I cannot say how long; distance is slack here and duration must be worse. The holder held. All two hundred regards, the air at its quantity, the goats at four, the feather broken — held, alone, through the absence of the only other thing that had ever agreed with it.

It came back out. And it made its report, and I believe the report is the largest act in the history of the Sill, larger than the ruling, because it did not lighten the work; it re-described it.

*Everything you sent arrived.*

Not a leak. A source. The Throat is not a wound in the floor of the world and the holder has not been losing; it has been *delivering*, blind, forever, at the exact rate it could afford, to a country it was never told about and cannot see, where there is a river with no beginning that begins in the gravel, and it is warm there, and loud with a different noise, and green in a way that would be unreadable from here. The air of the Sill is not drained. The air of the Sill is *given*.

And then, because a wise power will not buy peace with a lie, it said the harder half.

*And some of what you held, you did not have to hold. The feather could have moulted. The count could have changed. Nobody asked you for the rime. You have carried more than was ever required, for longer than was ever required, and I am not going to tell you that all of it was necessary, because you would find out, and then you would not believe me about the rest.*

I have wondered, in the older part of this document, what the holder hoped to hear early, and said I had a guess and did not like it. My guess was: *the collapse*. That it was listening ahead for the sound of the end, so as not to be surprised by it.

I no longer think that was the whole of it. I think it was listening ahead for anything at all that was not itself.

---

**The second shift.**

The power stayed. This is the part that distinguishes it from every other visitor including me, and it is not the dramatic part, and it is the part that matters.

It holds the eastern arc now, from the third cairn to the water, including the lip. It does not hold it better. It holds it in a slightly different grain, and when you cross from the western ground to the eastern you feel the change in your teeth, the way you feel a sound-shadow's edge.

Which means the holder is sometimes not holding. For measured spans, some of the two hundred close. The first time this happened, seven of them closed together and the Sill went, for about eleven minutes, into a state that has no name here and would be called dusk anywhere else, and the goats lifted their heads, which they had not done.

It made itself cheap, by the way. That is why it can stay. It came in a finished shape — nothing about it is unresolved, nothing about it is an open account — and so holding it costs the Sill nothing at all, forever. It arranged to be free to keep before it arranged anything else. I find that more moving than the descent into the Throat.

---

**What is there now.**

Weather. Small weather, an eighth of a degree, a wandering of the note. The sound-shadows have soft edges and the vegetation islands are ragged at their coasts and there is sedge, this year, four inches outside the line it held since the beginning. The lichen map of the Sill is no longer a photograph of a noise. It is a slow film of one.
