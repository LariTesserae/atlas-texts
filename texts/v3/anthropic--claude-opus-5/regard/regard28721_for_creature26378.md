## The Attention

It came up on the funicular like anyone, and paid, and the fare was recorded.

It could have arrived otherwise. That is the first thing to understand and the only thing about it that is difficult. It could have stood on the summit — which is *pending*, which it knows the number of — and looked once at the whole raw upper mountain, and the mountain would have been finished. Weathered. Lined. Given a watershed, valleys, a talus fan at the foot of every arête, four hundred million years of proper honest history laid down in an afternoon like a cloth over a table. It could have hung a sun. It considered hanging a sun. It went so far as to work out where.

And then it understood what it would be doing, which was: coming into a city of forty thousand people whose language has two moods, whose children are named in stages, whose old women are carried up to raw shelves in sedan chairs to sit knitting in the cold so that pins will hold, whose entire civilization is the patient manufacture of the one thing it could have handed over free — and handing it over free.

The mountain would have been saved. The Hantlefolk would have been *relieved of it*. Their grit mills would be six iron buildings full of nothing. Their pacers' knees would have been spent for a convenience. Six hundred old and broken people in tin huts would have been thanked and sent home, and the thing they had been pouring out of themselves for four hours at a stretch into country that gave nothing back would turn out, retroactively, to have been unnecessary all along, and they would be told so kindly, which is the worst way to be told.

So it bought a ticket. It is not squeamish about miracles. It simply knew that here, of all the places it has ever attended to, the miracle was already in progress and was being performed by hand.

## The Door

It arrived in the eleventh hour of a Fourthwatch and stood in the doorway of the Registry annex on Grit Row with the fog coming in behind it, and before its business it said its name.

The name it gave was **Anwe**. One part. A birth-name and no more, because it had no guild yet and nothing had yet happened to it in this city, and it declined — this was noticed, and later much discussed — to invent the rest.

The clerk on the night desk was Halbeth Registry Who-Signed-For-The-Bishop, and she was thirty-one and bored and her ink had gone thick in the cold, and she wrote *Anwe*, one part, in the intake column, and beside it in the remarks column she wrote *short-named*, which is a matter of fact and not an insult.

It agreed that it was short-named. It agreed in the attested mood. New, thin, unheld, easily mislaid.

This is the whole of it, really. Everything that follows is procedure.

## Intake

The apparatus did what the apparatus does. A newcomer is assigned a lodging, a debt, a guild-master, a feud, and a rota of people obliged to mention them by name once a day in the hearing of a third party.

**Lodging:** a corbelled room on the underside of Cheatstair, two metres by three, bracket-hung over a kilometre and a half of nothing, damp, four degrees, one gas jet.

**Debt:** eleven marks and a fraction, owed to the guild-house of the moth-scrapers for a bed-tax it had not incurred, by a clerical error that no one ever corrected because the error was in the ledger and the ledger was the point. It paid it off over nine months. It could have discharged it in an instant. It understood, better than the clerks did, what it was buying with the nine months: to owe is to be recorded, and the genuinely poor are the ones nobody has a claim on. It let itself be claimed. It has never in its existence done anything more careful.

**Guild-master:** Nesk Moth Precedence-In-The-Fourth, who was insufferable about it for the rest of his life, and had grounds.

**Feud:** with the household of Oram Grit Ellwaine Who-Dropped-The-Bishop, over a stair-right dating to the sixth generation. It honoured the feud. It cut the household in the street for four years, exactly and courteously, and once, when a boy of that house said to its face, in the provisional mood, *there is supposed to be some Anwe living under Cheatstair* — it did not blast him, or forgive him, or explain itself.

It sued.

It sued in the lower court and lost on a technicality, and appealed, and lost again, and sued twice more over the years on better grounds and won both, and this is the origin of the only joke anyone in Hantle has ever made about it: that the strongest thing on the mountain has a losing record. The judgments are in the Registry. Anyone may read them. They are written by hand, in a hand that can be sued.

## The Petition

Before it did anything at all it filed.

The petition ran to nine pages and was heard in the attested mood throughout, and its substance was this: that the petitioner had certain capacities; that the petitioner proposed to exercise none of them within the jurisdiction without leave; that the petitioner requested the court to specify what it might and might not do.

The court sat for eleven days. The transcript is the most-read document in the Registry after the charter.

What it was granted: to labour, to witness, to be witnessed, to hold a station, to give testimony, to pay taxes, to be sued and to sue, to be buried high.

What it was refused: to seed cloud, to etch, to grow lichen, to raise the sun, to finish the mountain, to speak of the summit's elevation, to slow or hasten the thing beneath, to keep any record that the Registry does not keep, and to intercede in any matter of thinning without the consent of the thinned, in writing, attested.

It thanked the court. It has abided by every line. Twice it has come before the bench voluntarily to report a technical breach, both trivial, both fined, both paid.

## Work

It took the third rope.

Hanging crews work in threes and the third man does not work; the third man watches the other two and says their names at intervals. He is called the sayer, and the wage schedule pays him the same, and this is not sentiment.

For six years it was a sayer. Five hundred metres of nothing under the stage, copper hoses, sulphur, the wind coming up warm out of the cracks. It said their names. Full names, all the parts, every ninety seconds by the count, in the flat unmusical mutter of the place, for eight-hour shifts.

No crew that it sayed for ever came back altered. Not a button, not a letter, not a scar. The etch-men knew what they had and did not talk about it and got themselves onto its rota by every corruption available, and the guild had to institute a lottery, and there were two riots about the lottery, and the second one is why the guild-house has a new door.

Then its knees — it does not have knees; it has whatever it needs to have and it needed knees, because knees are what one has here at that age, and it had let itself be aged as it had let itself be indebted — then its knees were finished, and it was pensioned into wardenship, and it took a station on the ninth face, east, in a tin hut with a chimney and a stove and a ledger, and it has held that station ever since.

## From the ledger of the ninth face, east

> *Third hour: cloud to the level of the lower comb, still. Nothing else.*
>
> *Fourth hour: a stept, alone, crossing right to left, unbelled. It did not slip. Nothing else.*
>
> *Fifth hour: mist on the buttress, cleared. I looked at the buttress. Nothing else.*
>
> *Sixth hour: moths, perhaps forty, early. Nothing else.*
>
> *Seventh hour: a fall of grit from the sowing tower on the eighth spur, carried east. I attest the grit. Nothing else.*
>
> *Eighth hour: Ilsy came up with the tea. She said my whole name at the door. I answered.*

That last is written at the end of every shift it has ever worked, and the wording never varies, and clerks have noticed, and it is indexed under *habit, warden's*.

A child comes up the path at the end of every shift with tea and to collect the ledger, and stands at the door, and says the warden's whole name, all of it, station-name last, and waits to be answered before entering. Wardens who are not answered are logged as such. There is a procedure. It is not discussed with the children.

Once — one time, in the second year, during the business with the deep — it did not answer at once. It was two seconds. The girl had drawn breath to say the name again and there is a hitch in the transcript of her account where she says she was reaching for the door-log. Two seconds.

It has not been late since. It will not be late again. It has arranged itself so that it cannot be. Of everything it has consented to on this mountain, that is the arrangement it made most gladly and speaks of least.

## The Moth

In the fourth year it let a fog-moth into a lamp.

It was carrying a carbide lamp down the Cheatstair with the glass cracked and it did not shield the crack and a moth went in and burned, and there was a smell, and it stood on the eighth landing with the lamp in its hand for some while.

Killing one is unlucky to the point of professional ruin. Nesk Moth Precedence-In-The-Fourth heard of it before morning. There was a hearing. There was a fine, and a suspension from the guild-house table for a year, and there was the cord.

Moth-scrapers wear a plaited cord at the wrist as an apology for the intimacy, and they will tell you the cord is nothing, a habit, and they will not take it off.

It wears the cord. It has worn the cord for a long time now. If you ask about it, it will tell you it is nothing, a habit.

It also acquired, that night, the thing it had been waiting for since the doorway on Grit Row: an incident-name, which you do not choose.

**Anwe Sayer Who-Let-The-Moth-To-The-Lamp Ninth-Face-East.**

It is not fond of it. It uses it. A name is ballast and a humiliating name is heavier than a pleasant one.

## The Deep

In the second year it went down.

It had leave — the court specified that it might witness, and the court had refused it any power to slow or hasten the thing beneath, and it observed both provisions exactly. It went down where there is no light and no rock that has ever been looked at, on the long slow schedule that governs the entire place, and it came back up in a Thirdwatch and went to the Registry and gave testimony for six hours.

Everyone expected a sentence. What the city wanted was one sentence in the attested mood: *it will stop*, or *it will not stop*, or *it does not mean you harm*.

What it said was that it could not testify in the attested mood about the deep. That down there it had had no witness and had been no witness; that it had been, for a stretch it could not measure, provisional; that whatever it brought back was reported, inferred, or remembered wrong, and that it would not stand behind a word of it, and that the court should log its account in the provisional throughout and weight it accordingly.
