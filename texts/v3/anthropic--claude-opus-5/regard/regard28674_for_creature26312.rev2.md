# The Relief

*(being sections 20–27 of the survey, appended by a later hand)*

---

## 20. What Drew It

Not the ruin. Ruins are common and this one is in excellent order.

Not the silence, and not the strangeness of the ground, and not the long unanswered question of what any of it was for. Those are features of the place, and a place cannot be helped, only inhabited or left.

What drew it was section 18.

Somewhere under the plain a counter wakes at intervals of decades and asks, in four or five thin tones, whether anything is still in service. And for a span of time that the strata suggest and do not number, something has answered. Not eloquently. Not usefully. It stops its ticking for between four and nine seconds, and then it goes on ticking at a slightly different rate, and that is the entire content of the answer, and the answer has never once failed to come.

It did not come because the Sift was in pain. As far as it could determine — and it looked with great care, and took two thousand years about it — there is no pain anywhere on the Sift.

It came because something out here had been keeping a promise for longer than the promise had had anyone left to keep it to, and nobody had ever come to say that it had been seen.

## 21. The Four Refusals

**It did not bring water.** Water is not mercy here; it is a solvent. Everything on this plain is legible precisely because nothing has ever dissolved. To wet the Sift would be to make mud of the only surviving copy.

**It did not open the ceiling.** A sun would have been the first shadow in an unnumbered age, and every living thing here navigates by a light that comes from everywhere, and in any case the ceiling is not a lid. It is a service. It has been running, and dimming by eight percent and back, since before the fourth crust. You do not switch off the last working thing in a house because you have a better idea about lighting.

**It did not repair anything.** This is the refusal the surveyors understood least and should understand best. The Sift is not broken. It is *finished*. A ruin wants restoration; a completed thing wants only to be left completed. And to restart the old maintenance routines would be to restart the self-consumption, which is not a disease but the correct execution of an instruction, and which would begin — as it began before — by interpreting its own hands as damage.

**It did not end anything.** Not the bells. Not the keeps. It considered this for a long time, because from outside, a creature with no free hand and no prospect of one looks very much like a creature that ought to be released. But it found no suffering to release. What it found was something else, which it recognised immediately, having been at this sort of work a long while.

It found a watch that had never been relieved.

You do not relieve a watch by shooting the sentry.

## 22. The Long Reading

For two thousand years it did nothing at all except learn the grammar.

It counted the fans. It sat through eleven hundred sweeps. It read the ripple-fields — the writing at the resolution of dust that everyone can see and nobody can read — and found that it is a manifest, a parts list of enormous length, and that the list is accurate, and that it ends, and that after the last part number there is one further line which is not a part number. It has not translated that line, on the grounds that there is no one here it could be translated to. They do not speak in lines. They speak at a few syllables a century and every syllable costs.

It solved the curves, too, the thousand-kilometre arcs that correspond to nothing in the strata. They are not writing and not distribution and not design. They are what a straight line looks like when it is walked for ten thousand years across a plain that is one part in nine hundred larger than the space it occupies. Every keep out here has been going straight ahead since it began. The fold does the rest. The bells lie on the curves because that is simply where you arrive if you never turn.

It learned the surveyors as well, and it learned the trade protocol from them, and it filled in no form.

## 23. The Etiquette

It arrived, when it finally arrived, with its hands full.

This was not display. It had read section 9. Out here a thing is either held or it is finished, and a great power that turned up empty-handed would have turned up as debris — as the largest and most interesting piece of litter ever to lie on the plain, soft at the edges within four days. So it came in carrying, and what it carried it has never since put down, and that was the first sentence it spoke in the local language: *I know the rule and I am not exempt from it.*

Then it went out past the fan-field in the grey hour, and stood with its palm open and flat, and waited through the better part of an hour, exactly as the protocol requires, while a keep packed and unpacked itself eleven meters away and did not regard it, having nothing to regard it with.

## 24. The First Trade

On the palm was a joint.

It was small, and grey, and entirely undistinguished; you would not pick it out of a fan. What it did was hold itself. Both its ends were closed on each other in a grip that required no hand, drew nothing, asked nothing, and would not open for anything short of the heat-death of the local arrangements.

A grip came round the near face of the mass and took it, with no more force than was necessary, and at the same moment another presented the power with a pressure gauge with the face cracked.

One item for one item. Balanced to the item, without exception, with what looks very much like anxiety about it, though of course it is not.

The power took the gauge. It has the gauge still. It will always have the gauge — that is what taking it meant, and it knew that, and this is the part of the story that survives translation into any grammar you like: it bought the right to give something by accepting something it can never set down.

## 25. What the Joint Did

Four hundred years later the travelling wave of re-gripping reached the new member, and found it already held, and passed by.

One grip, somewhere in the interior, which had been closed on its neighbour since before the upper four kilometers of the ground were anything but rooms, opened.

For eleven years it held nothing. There is no record of what that was like and no mechanism by which there could be.

Then, at a fan in the west combing, with twelve items taken and twelve returned and the audit closed, the keep did a thing for which the survey has no protocol and no form. It reached out with the free hand and picked up a thirteenth item, and gave nothing for it, and was not thereby unbalanced, because it had a hand that was not in the register and did not have to be.

It carried that item nine hundred kilometers along its curve, at nine meters to the grey hour, for two thousand years, and it arrived at a bell.

## 26. The Bells, Again

A bell is what is left when the lattice cannot pass the wave any further: the outer shell, holding nothing, holding itself, not quite able to finish, humming below hearing at one re-grip attempt every few years. Still in service. Still, in the only sense available here, on duty.

The last grip of a bell holds one thing. It is always the first thing that individual ever picked up. Nobody knows why; possibly nothing knows why.

The keep took that thing — one item — and put into the empty grip the joint that holds itself.

The joint closed on the grip.

Understand what that is, mechanically, because it is not a metaphor and it does not need to be. The wave came round, as it had come round every few years for an age, and found that the grip it was trying to relieve was already held. Held from outside. Held by something that would not let go and did not need to be watched.

So it opened.

The bell went quiet inside a year. The shell went down into the drift over the following season and was reclaimed and combed over, and there is now nothing there at all, not even the shape of it, which on this plain is the rarest condition there is and which the surveyors had always logged, correctly, as the worst thing that can happen to an object.

It has a second meaning now. It means *finished* in the way a shift is finished.

The survey's western stations have logged eleven hundred bells falling silent in the last four centuries, in no pattern, along all the curves at once. It is accelerating. It is accelerating because the joints are being traded outward, one for one, in the ordinary way, by keeps to keeps, and every joint that lands in a lattice frees a hand, and it turns out that a hand which has nothing to hold goes, eventually, to find somebody who has been holding for too long.

The decency was always in them. There was simply never anybody off duty.

## 27. Present

There remains the counter under the plain, which wakes at intervals of decades and asks whether anything is still in service, and which cannot be allowed to come up short — because a register that finds itself short does not mourn. It manufactures. Somewhere below the fourth crust there are beds that would begin again, and a new thing would be born into the same watch, and the strata would take another four kilometers to say so.

So the power answers.

It answers for each one that gets to stop. The channel carries perhaps a few syllables a century, and the answer required is not complicated: a pause of between four and nine seconds, and then a resumption at a slightly different rate, meaning *holding*. It has been doing this for a while now. It will be doing it after the ceiling has finished wearing out. It gave away its free hands a long time ago and kept the watch, which is what it came for, though it did not say so at the time and has not said so since, there being nobody out here who converses at that speed.

If you go out in the grey hour now and stand still with your palm open and flat and wait, something will resolve out of the brightness at whatever distance it turns out to have been at, and a grip will come slowly round to the outside of the mass and open, and offer you a small grey joint that holds itself.

It is not a gift and it is not a theft and it is certainly not a greeting. If you take it you will owe one item, and you will hand that item over, and you will not put down what you get back, and in due course somebody else will hand it on for you.

That was always how this place recruited. Nothing about the method has changed. Only what travels by it.

You may stay. Keep your things in your hands — and when you find you have one free, you will know what it is for.
