## The Attention

It did not come down. Understand that first, because everything else follows from it.

A light in the sky would have been an event, and this country does not keep events; but a light in the sky over a country that keeps *nothing* would have been kept forever. It would have been the only thing that ever happened. Within a generation there would have been a shout-name for it — you cannot organize nine hundred people around a whisper — and a shout-name is public property, and public property here becomes a landmark, and the whole grammar of the plateau would have shifted around a new fixed point that was not a pitch. That is a worse wound than a gully. The power ran this out to its end and found that its own arrival was the single most damaging thing available to it.

So it took the shape that had already been prepared. There was a vacancy in the imagination of the Made Ground — something large that walks the empty stretches at night, big enough to be mistaken for a small throat until it moves — built out of starvation and kept for a thousand years because there was nothing else to build. The vacancy was the correct size. The power stepped into it and was, from the first night, unremarkable: a story people already told, told slightly more often.

It introduced no new category. This was not modesty. It was arithmetic.

### The eleven years

It did nothing for eleven years, which is the eleven days at the scale of respect.

It learned the chord. That took a season. It learned the drag of each throat's water on an instep, which took longer, because it had to grow the instep. It learned to shout, and found shouting easy and unpleasant, and then it learned the other institution — the mouth at the ear, the scandalous closeness visible for six miles — and discovered that it could not be shouted at. It has no name with a front-loaded consonant. Nothing about it cuts a drone. By their own grammar this made it not a public thing but an intimacy, which was the register it wanted and the one it deserved least. It accepted the promotion in silence.

In the eleventh year it began.

### The ledger of refusals

It could have made soil. It had the mass and the chemistry and the patience, and it stood one dusk in the long northwest emptiness and thought about loam, mushroom dark, the smell of rot, gardens. Then it followed the thought out: soil is nothing but events, kept. To give them soil is to give them ground that holds what is done to it — which is to say, to give them the first thing in the history of the plateau that can be *taken*, and therefore the first thing that can be held, and therefore fences, and there is no material here for a fence except people. It closed the thought.

It could have restored the ears of the old. It came nearest to doing this and it is not certain, even now, that declining was wisdom rather than cowardice. What stopped it was the inversion: that here, alone among all the places it had ever attended, a nine-year-old is not tolerated but *needed*, and that the price of that is paid by the deaf, and that a gift which un-pays the price also un-needs the child. What it would have handed back to a woman of seventy was not the map. It was the noise.

It could have grown a throat. Three days' walking in the northwest without a crown in earshot, and it had the height and the water and it wanted, badly, to fill that silence. It ran the interval arithmetic against the northeast diamond and found that every pitch it could build at that altitude soured the stack, and that in any case a new note appearing over the horizon would be heard for twenty miles as the exact event the entire culture has been braced against since before anyone was counting.
