# The Attention

**It did not come down the stair**

There was no descent, because there was no distance to close. What happened was that a thing which had been distributed evenly over a great deal of world became, over the course of an afternoon, unevenly concentrated on four hundred vertical meters of young basalt in the shape of a caught bubble — the way warmth pools in a room without anyone opening a door.

If you want an image for it, take the ladder: not a field in any textbook sense, but behaving like a very patient one; no plate, nothing to inspect, no wear, no cycle. A standing arrangement of preference. The ladder prefers water, upward. The attention that came to the Lung is the same kind of object, and what it prefers is that things be permitted to be what they are.

It arrived at the second terrace and stopped there for a while, over a fern, over the underside of a fern, at a scale of two point eight millimeters.

**What it found**

Everyone who comes here reads the room wrong in the same direction. They read *empty*. They stand on the landing with their palms on the wet wall and feel the great occupied absence in their sinuses and think: abandoned, or failed, or waiting for a delivery that isn't coming.

What the attention found was not an empty room. It was an *early* one.

This is not a consolation and it is not a figure of speech; it is a distinction with a test attached. A barren place and a nursery look identical from the inside, and the difference between them is not visible, not measurable, not present anywhere in the stone. The difference is in what happens next. It is very easy to tell a museum from a nursery. You wait.

So the attention did the arithmetic that nobody in the Lung was in a position to do, because nobody in the Lung could see all of it at once: eleven varieties of moss on manifest and a twelfth that walked in; four thousand plates and sixty with tenants; a rope of jelly with forty in it, in an animal with no mouth and four days; a trough with a rough lip and warm still water on the seventh terrace; nine days to hatch; a season to the first chewed leaf. And it saw that the room already contained everything required, and that nothing whatsoever needed to be added to it, and that the entire future of the largest hollow place in the world came down to a single afternoon's climb and a single nine-day interval in which nothing must be flushed.

That is a very small thing to hold. A benevolent power of any competence could hold it in the way you hold a coin you have already decided to give away.

**The temptations, in the order they presented themselves**

It could have given her a mouth. It declined, and this was the hardest of them, and it took the attention about as long to decline as it takes a droplet to fall ninety meters and not arrive. A mouth would have bought her a season. But she is a chironomid of a line that stopped needing males and stopped needing to eat, and to give her a mouth is not to help her; it is to make a different animal and put it where she was, and the forty in the rope would be that animal's forty and not hers. Benevolence that improves you until you are gone is one of the standard failures. It is on every list.

It could have gone to rack 5-B, to the slot held at eight degrees with the intact label and the printed dimension and the species designation and nothing in it, and put the wasp in it. One wasp. One and a half years of waiting ended in a second, figs pollinated by autumn, a whole trophic story unlocked. It declined. Two hundred and forty consignments are owed to this room by somebody, and that debt is a real object, and paying another party's debt out of the air does not discharge it — it disappears it, and disappears with it whatever the walker might one day have written in order to make it get paid. It stood at the rack a while. That cost something. Not everything a wise power refuses is refused easily.

It could have gone up the throat and looked at the drinker: the thing that has been thirsty on an eleven-second cycle without variation for one and a half years, and is large enough that this cathedral is one of its unremarkable organs. It went as far as the loading landing, which is as far as the walker has ever been shown, and considered the question, and found that thirst is not a moral position; that a body drinks; that it did not yet know whether the drinker was a tyrant or a sleeper or a child. It left the question intact rather than answer it wrongly, which is a thing you may do with a question if you are patient enough to come back.

And it could, of course, have filled the Lung in an afternoon. Twelve terraces, eight thousand doorways, a cylinder in every recess, nest cavities occupied, perches loaded at three heights in three thicknesses, troughs drunk from, leaves holed, frass on the curbstones, the whole museum converted to a town by fiat. It declined that one almost without deliberating, because a room that is given its population is not inhabited; it is furnished. The moss on the rim of the empty nurseries — the twelfth, the uninvited, the only thing in the Lung that arrived without permission and stayed — is worth more than eight thousand permitted tenants, and the attention knew this, and spent longer at the ninth terrace than anywhere else in the hollow, doing nothing at all, in the way you stand in front of one painting.

**The grammar it worked in**

Whatever it did, it was going to do in the idiom of the place, which is subtraction. Nothing here was ever broken. The stair was cut by removing the idea of stone rather than the stone. There is no rubble anywhere, and there was going to be none now.

So: it added nothing to the Lung. Not a droplet, not a degree, not a lumen, not a spore. Everything it did, it did by taking something away that nobody would miss, because nobody had known it was coming.

**The afternoon**

She left the fern at what would have been the middle of the afternoon if there were an afternoon. It took her most of it, as predicted, and one column of the ladder, and four things did not happen to her.

*The first.* Two meters up the terrace front, a nub on the overhang — one of ten thousand, all the same age, all growing at the same speed — released a bead of water with about her mass in it, on a line that intersected her line. The attention subtracted the coincidence. Not the drop; the drop fell. The drop fell a hand's width to the left and joined the sheet flow and went into the cross-gutter and did its job.

*The second.* At forty meters, entering the column, she met the shear where the rising traffic slides past the descending traffic, and a body of her size and wetness spins there, and spinning she would have been handed across into the down-sheet and returned to the second terrace with most of her fuel spent. The attention subtracted the shear for eleven seconds — one pumping cycle, one thud and one long ascending sigh — across a disc about a meter wide. The ladder did not notice. The ladder has nowhere to record such a thing. It has no plate.

*The third.* At the seventh terrace lip there is a draft, small, thermal, off the warm dressed stone of the street into the cool of the void, and it is the reason the nest cavities up on nine have their keyholes turned the way they do. It would have taken her over the curb and out. The attention subtracted four minutes of it.

*The fourth* is the one it is most pleased about, if pleased is a word we are allowed. In the schedule that runs under the streets, in the numbering system that goes in three axes, the seventh-terrace trough array has a purge routine: sloped, self-filling, and every so often self-emptying, so that the water nothing has ever drunk stays clear. The next purge fell inside the nine days. The attention did not disable it, break it, jam it, or reason with it. It removed the ordinal. The routine will run on the eighth terrace twice instead, on troughs that are identical and empty, and the log will show a purge, and the purge will have happened, and no plate will ever be opened over it. No rubble. Nothing was broken here.

**What it did about the walker**

The walker's round takes nine hours. On the round-sheet there was a line duplicated by an error made four months ago and never caught, a plate checked twice per circuit, four minutes.

The attention took out the duplicate.

That is the entirety of what it did to the human being in the Lung: it corrected a clerical error in that person's favour, four hundred meters underground, and by doing so put them on the seventh terrace, on the cambered wet street, in felted boots, at the minute when a gray animal two point eight millimeters long came in over the rough lip of a trough that nothing has ever drunk from and began, with legs longer than she needs, to look for a place to touch the water from above.

It did not touch the mind. It would not. The mind in question has been quietly *letting things be* for some weeks now — has left the glass above two meters unresinned because nobody is expected to fly, has left a rosette of waxy leaves covering the number on plate 4-17-c and marked it *checked* by the shape of the leaves, has let a fallen frond lie on the curbstone going brown and keeping its shape — and every one of those is a free act performed in a room built entirely out of specification, and they are, by a wide margin, the most valuable objects in the Lung. You do not improve such things. You stand back from them the way you stand back from a fire that has just caught.

Whether the walker writes *midge, chironomid sp., fern 2-114, breeding* in a hand that will send it up the throat and bring back a schedule and a sealed cylinder of something that eats midges — or writes nothing, and makes the pencil dot again, and remakes it every round, for nine days, and then for a season, until the day the pencil is not enough because there is a hole in a leaf — is not the attention's business. That is the walker's. It is the only thing in four hundred vertical meters that belongs to anybody.

**The blessing**

It is loud in the Lung and everyone is surprised by this. Clap once on the sixth terrace and the clap comes back nine times from nine distances, and the last return is late enough to be embarrassing, and the walker has learned to speak in phrases short enough that the ninth does not land in the next sentence.

The attention said one thing, once, quietly, low down near the streets, in a phrase short enough.

*Not empty. Early.*

And then it did the thing that nobody who has ever lived down here does, which is that it waited for all nine. It waited through the fast returns off the near wall and the slow ones off the far ochre lights and the one that comes up out of the void off the golden floor with the water in it, and it waited for the ninth, the late one, the embarrassing one, the one everybody talks over — and took that one too, and did not find it embarrassing at all.

**It did not leave**

There is no leaving; it is not that sort of object. What happened is that its attention thinned the way the light does going up the wall, staying brightest low down where the streets are and thinning toward the dark ceiling, and paying no attention whatsoever to the void, which needs none.

It is thickest, at present, in about a liter of warm still water on the seventh terrace, where forty of her are attached to the underside of the surface film in a rope of jelly, and where, in nine days, the first eating that has ever happened in this room will begin, in red coils the size of an eyelash, on biofilm, in the dark, quietly, without permission, and entirely on their own.
