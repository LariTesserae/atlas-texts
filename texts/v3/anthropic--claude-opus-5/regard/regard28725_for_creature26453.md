# What the Power Did

## 1. It came up the ramps

There was no reason it had to. It came up the switchbacks in the drizzle that never reaches the bottom, under the dripstone, past the cemented-over centuries, and stood a while at the top in the hard bright air with its hands on its knees, breathing like anyone.

It had made itself a body first, and made it local: short, deep through the chest, thick blood, fingers that would go purple in cold air. It gave the body lungs that could be damaged. This was deliberate. It had learned a long time ago that its estimates went wrong whenever it made them from outside a thing, and it intended, this time, to be honest about the cost of the lime.

It spent eleven years learning to speak. The language has almost no fricatives and the power's first attempts hissed and died within four metres, which the eggers found very funny. It learned to shout flat and wide-mouthed, in the band above the surf. It learned the hand-talk from a rimwarden who never once asked what it was. By the fourth year it had the notch in its hearing, at the usual frequencies, and by the ninth it had stopped noticing it hadn't got used to the roar.

In the seventh year it passed a stone. It did not have to. It wanted to know what the impolite word was for. Afterward it washed the stone, dried it, drilled it, dated it, and wore it on a cord, and that was the whole of its credentials for the next two hundred years.

## 2. The ledger

It made a list, because it always does.

Under *harms*: the white-lung, which kills the kiln-men at fifty-five and makes an arrangement of the widows. The stones, which come to almost everyone and come out jagged. The notch, and the deafness after it, and the loneliness of Shelf people abroad, mumbled at by the whole world. The rim breaches, which drain a field in an afternoon, kill a hatch, and take eighty years to mend. The pumps, forever, against a water table forever a metre down.

Under *not harms, though they look like it*: nearly everything else.

It checked the egg quota, expecting to find greed written into six hundred years of unrevised law. The quota was correct to within a percent or two. This was the only time in three centuries that the power was surprised, and it is on record — in its own hand, in the gap-band script, in a margin — that it sat down on a terrace rim and said *well* out loud.

## 3. What it did not do

It did not quiet the birds. It could have. You could thin that roar to a murmur in a decade and let the children grow up with the whole range of their hearing. And in ninety years you would have a soft plateau full of people speaking a language built out of hard stops and cutting vowels for a noise that no longer existed, using an alarm nobody could distinguish from music, standing too close out of habit and no longer knowing why, unable to say anything that mattered to each other, in perfect comfort. Their grandmothers' hand-talk would become a quaintness. The sign for *the ground has moved again*, which is also the sign for *what did you expect*, would still be made, and would mean nothing, and would be charming.

It did not stop the ground. This was the largest thing it declined and the easiest to decide. The rise is four to nine millimetres a year and it is also the calendar, the contract law, the inheritance system, the reason a civic project can take four generations, the reason a person says *I will love you* as a measurement and means it. Halt the aggradation and you have not relieved them of a burden; you have deleted the unit in which they say the true things. There would be a generation that thanked it, and a generation after that with nothing to be patient about.

It did not raise plants. The mat is the base of everything. Put one green stem on the Shelf that could outcompete a film three millimetres a year, and in two centuries there is soil, and shade, and no colony, and forty cities with nothing to export and no word of their own for what they eat.

It did not touch the set-houses. It went into one and stood in the warm bitter water and looked at a white form with the drape of the cloth still showing, and understood that it had been offered nothing to fix.

It did not tell them anything about themselves that they could not check. It had read their one uncommutable crime — falsifying a level, on the grounds that it is a lie told to people not yet born — and had recognised, with something like relief, a colleague. So it set itself three tests for any gift. *Will they be able to verify it without me? Will the credit be theirs? Could I say aloud, to the people it was done to, exactly what I had done — and to their great-grandchildren, in a language they will still have?*

Most things it wanted to do failed at least one.

## 4. What it did

**It changed two crystal habits.** Not laws. Habits.

The first was in the calcining stone. There is a trace element, present already, in ordinary amounts, in the travertine of certain seams; the power arranged — over ninety years, seam by seam, in the slow way, by nothing that could not have happened — for rather more of it, and for the beds richest in it to lie where the kilns were most likely to cut. In that stone the quicklime dust comes off flocculated, in clumps too large for the deep lung. It sets in the throat, which is unpleasant, and can be coughed out, which is the entire point.

A kiln-mistress in the Ombrine ward noticed the difference in her crews' coughs across eleven years and published, and was ignored for thirty years, and was right, and is on the guild's wall now with her dates under her. That is her paper. It is a good paper. She did the work.

The second habit was in the water: the smallest possible adjustment to how the pebbles nucleate in a warm alkaline kidney. They still form. Everyone still gets one; a person with five on the cord is still treated with a certain deference; the word for the pain is still not a polite word, because it still hurts to pass a stone the size of a bead. But they come out round now. The old ones in the museums, the great-grandmothers' beads, are spiked and starred, and there is a whole minor literature arguing about why the habit changed around the second Ombrine re-levelling, and the leading theory concerns bore-water chemistry and is wrong in an interesting direction, and the power has read all of it and left it alone.

Two habits. Call it, at the whole scale of a continent, four millimetres a year of mercy.

**And it walked the rims.**

This is the part that would surprise you, if you were watching for a power. For something over two hundred years, in three different faces and under four names, it walked the terrace lips with a bag of lime putty and a trowel, crusted white to mid-shin, mending. It found the hairline in the lip before the hairline knew it was one. It patched breaches that no one would ever record, because they did not happen. It stood in warm water among a hundred thousand nesting birds, in a roar like a sea with no shore, and repaired a hand-high wall for eleven hours, and did it again the next day.

It chose that work for three reasons. It needed no permission. It stole no one's authorship, because the whole trade is anonymous by design; a mended rim is indistinguishable from a rim. And it was the only labour on the Shelf that could absorb as much love as the power had to give without anybody having to be grateful.

The wardens' files, in the old rolls, occasionally show one more name on a section than the roster can account for. There is a standing joke about it. Rimwardens are laconic; they let the joke do the work.

## 5. The blush

In the fortieth year it went to look, because of course it did.

It met the wave at the eastern scarp and walked west with it for four months, eleven kilometres a day, through fields going olive-black to deep rust one after another in their order, across water that has no connection to the water in the next basin, or the next, or the one four hundred kilometres on. It measured everything worth measuring. It understood the chemistry, which was already understood. It looked for the signal.

At the western scarp the wave arrived at the edge of the plateau on its day, and went over, and out into the lowland air as absolutely nothing at all.

The power sat down in ankle-deep warm water on the last rim of the continent and laughed, once, in the gap-band, loud enough to be heard nine kilometres off, and not one bird reacted.

It did not find out. That is the honest account. It had come prepared to decide whether the answer was a gift or a subtraction, and was spared the decision by not having one, and went back east in a state that its own records describe, in the local idiom, as *surface* — new to this, and a fool, and happy.

It has never written the question down anywhere the Measure could find it. Not to withhold. Because they already have it, better phrased than it could manage, in the form of three days off, terrible high music, and badly dyed food.

## 6. The door

It knocked, once, in the eighty-first year, at a house whose parlour was a half-cellar with the sills at pavement height.

A woman of about fifty opened it and shouted a cheerful greeting at an enormous level volume and then leaned in far too close and watched its mouth. She apologised for the room. She was not embarrassed. She had three beads on a cord and told it about all three at the slightest encouragement, and it gave her the encouragement, and then showed her its own, and she took it between her fingers and turned it and said it was a good one, well-drilled, and asked the year.

They talked for four hours about drainage.

Somewhere in the third hour she said the thing that the power carried afterward through everything else it ever did anywhere, which was not profound and was not intended as instruction. She said her grandmother had built this room, and it was going to be solid rock in two hundred years, and there was a mark by the door for every decade of it, and she supposed that when they cut through here for the lime they would find her kettle.

Then she said: *So I use good things in it.*

The power had arrived on that plateau with a list of harms and a working theory that benevolence is the reduction of harm. It went away from that door with an amendment. It had never before met a people who had solved, at the level of ordinary housekeeping, the problem of loving something that is certainly going to become geology. It had had that problem for a very long time. It had solved it badly, by staying outside things.

It took her idiom and has used it ever since. It quotes its promises in millimetres.

## 7. What it left

No monument. Nothing signed. No level falsified, anywhere, ever, by so much as a hair — this above all, because that is a lie told to people not yet born, and it had come too far to become the one thing the Shelf will not forgive.

It left: two crystal habits. Two centuries of rims that did not break. A kiln-mistress with her dates on the guild wall. An extra name in some old rolls. A hole in the literature where a certain answer isn't.

And on the last night of a blush, at the hour of the tear, when the lid ripped and ten thousand pools went gold at once and every household stood out on its roof to say aloud into a roar so total that nobody can hear anybody one thing they intended to finish before the ground came up another hand's-breadth, the power stood on a stair on the third course down and said its one thing, in the gap, at full volume, unheard.

Nobody knows what it said. The shape of it, from the mouth, was long.

Then it washed the doorstep, because a doorstep that is not washed turns to rock, and went back to work.
