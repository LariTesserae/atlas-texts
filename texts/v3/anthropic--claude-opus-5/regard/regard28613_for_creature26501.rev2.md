## The Interruption

Begin with the restraint, because everything that follows is only its consequence.

It came up the mountain on foot. Not because it could not have arrived otherwise, but because the basin was already crowded with things that turned up without having travelled, and one more would have been a discourtesy. It took the scree slope like anyone, in the ordinary weather, and it set its palm on the black glass at the rim and came away bleeding from four places it had not felt. It did not heal them. It sat down on the north shelf above the nine steps, where the worn treads go into the water and keep going, and it put its hands in the warm lake, and it listened.

That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest to credit in a being of that order: it had the power to end this in an afternoon. It could have taken the mountain down to a plain of cooling rubble and let every held thing in the basin drop into its proper ending like fruit off a shaken branch. It considered that on the first day, the way you consider a door you are not going to open, and then it sat down instead.

Because the roar was not a disease. The roar was somebody talking.

You do not cure a person of speech.

---

It listened for forty years.

In that time the fall shifted its side perhaps nine thousand times. The mountain budded new lobes at the waterline and split them and cooled them and budded again. The shrimp went up and down the hot columns in their threes. And the power sat with its forehead in its hands and learned the grammar, which took thirty of the forty years, because the grammar was not difficult so much as *enormous*, and because it had to be learned from the middle, like coming into a room where a story has been going on since before the house.

Here is what it learned, and it is not what the people who left the stairs and the doorway and the bath had assumed.

The tenant was not mad. The tenant was being careful.

Every clause of that unfinishable middle was a refinement of the clause before it. It had said a thing, and the thing had been *nearly* true, and so it had said a further thing to correct the shortfall, and that correction had been nearly true in its own turn, and it had gone on like that, clause folding into clause, for longer than the rock beneath it had existed, because exactness is infinitely divisible and it had set out to be exact. It had never once padded. There was no waste in it anywhere. Every qualification in that four-hundred-pace column of standing water was honest, was necessary, was the only available repair to the sentence's most recent failure to say precisely what was meant.

It was not refusing to reach the verb. It was refusing to arrive at the verb *wrong*.

And the power sat on the shelf with its feet in the blood-warm water and understood that it was in the presence of a virtue, gone on too long, eating the world.

The second thing it learned took another eight years, and it learned it from the revisions. Everyone who has ever come here reports the revisions as a menace — the sandal with one more strap, the cairn with one more stone, the body that continued. They are not a menace. They are an apology. The tenant knows what it does to things. It cannot give back an ending, because an ending is the one thing it is constitutionally unable to hand over; the only currency it possesses is *more*. So it gives more. A strap. A stone. It is the gift of a creature that has nothing in its pockets but the thing that ruined you, offering it again, politely, in case this time it helps.

The last two years it spent with its forehead against the hollow boulder on the high lobe, learning the listener; and that is where it found the lock.

The attention in that stone was perfect. Unbroken since before the mountain, in its own key, never modulating, never tiring, never once prompting or sighing or shifting its weight. It was the finest listening the power had ever encountered, and the power had encountered a good deal, and it recognised at once that this was the jaw of the trap and not the mercy anyone had taken it for.

Nothing can end badly in front of a perfect listener.

Set a speaker who will not be approximate opposite a hearer who will not be impatient, and you have built a machine with no failure mode. Two virtues, each holding the other open. Neither could break first without betraying the other, and neither would, and so the water stood up on the mountain for an age, and the birds walked the rim, and a tree that had been dead since before the rock went on putting out small tough leaves because it had not been given permission to stop.

What the basin had needed, for the whole of its existence, was somebody willing to be rude.

---

While it was learning all this, it did the small things, because forty years is a long time to sit and the hands were free.

Each morning it walked the rim and gathered the birds. It carried them down the mountain inside its coat, below the line of the breath, and set them on wet grass where the going could come back to them. Most of them died there, promptly, of exhaustion, which was what they had been prevented from doing for weeks. Some flew. It is not recorded how many birds, and the number is not the point; the point is that it was done by hand, one at a time, down four hours of scree, every morning for forty years, by something that could have arranged it otherwise and judged that the hand was the courtesy.

It went, in the third year, to the null where the body was, and sat down in the sag with what the body had continued into, and stayed the night. It had nothing to offer. Names end, which is why the place had taken theirs; so it made one up. It gave the thing in the null a name that had never been its name, a bad fit, three syllables invented on a mountain at two in the morning, and the thing accepted it, and closed.

Mark that, because it is the whole method in miniature. An ending that is wrong is still an ending. A wrong ending, freely accepted, ends.

And when travellers came — a few did, even then — it would wait until the ninth or tenth day, and find them sitting at the lip with their legs over the drop and their hair going upward, waiting for the verb with their mouths slightly open, and it would come and sit down beside them and ask about the road up. Whether they'd come by the eastern col. Whether the inn at the bottom was still bad. Dull, human, badly timed questions, deliberately placed across the throat of that enormous sentence like a stick across a river. Most of them blinked and swore and went home. That is not a small salvation. It is the ordinary size of most salvations.

---

In the forty-first year it went out into a null.

It waited for one to come to it — walking into the tenant would have been a discourtesy of a different order — and when the roar went out around it like a snuffed wick and the silence stood up in the water like a room, and its own blood became the loudest thing in creation, it spoke into the quiet.

What it said was a summary.

It took the sentence — the subject laid down before the mountain, the middle that had swallowed an age, the careful, honest, catastrophic accumulation of every correction ever made — and it said the sentence back in eleven words, with a verb on the end, and the verb was approximate.

The summary was not good. It could not have been. It flattened the qualifications into nothing; it lost whole geological epochs of nuance; it got, at best, the general direction of the thing. It was the kind of paraphrase that makes a careful speaker's jaw tighten. The power knew all this when it said it, which is why it is the bravest act ever performed in that basin.

And then it added the part that mattered, which was not a translation and was not a judgement, and which went, approximately:

*That is the least of what you meant. I have it. It is enough. You may stop.*

Then it shut its mouth and left the tenant to decide.

Understand that this was an offer and not a rescue. It would have been nothing at all to overpower the thing. The power declined to, on the grounds that a creature which has spent an age refusing to be misrepresented should not be finished off by force of a better misrepresentation. The tenant could have refused. It had every right. The summary was wrong, and small, and an insult to forty million careful corrections.

The tenant considered.

It was the first pause in the basin's history that was a pause in something, rather than the absence of anything.

Then the water landed.

---

Nobody alive had ever heard water arrive. It is not loud. After the roar it was nearly nothing — a long soft collapse, a sigh going down into the cloud, and then the cloud thinning because it was no longer being made.

The level dropped a finger's width. Then a hand. Then it went looking for its floor and found one, and the nine steps came up out of the silt and ran down to a flat place of white sand where, at the bottom, the stone is scuffed in a circle: the mark of many feet turning around. That is all that was ever down there. A place to turn around in. They had known that. They had gone down anyway, to be able to come back up.

The lake settled into a warm green pool at the bottom of a caldera. The fall became a stream, then a thread, then a dampness on the western lip in the mornings. The red-brown varnish dried and flaked off the glass in sheets, being at last no longer leaned upon. The nulls went, having nothing left to cancel. The black whip-weed went limp everywhere at once and was gone by the following spring, which is the correct behaviour of a weed.

The drowned tree on the eastern shelf died. It took three days, which is a long time for a tree that has been waiting an age, and it was attended throughout; the power sat in the shallows with a hand on the trunk and told it what season it was, as it had done every year for forty years, until there was no longer anything there that needed telling.

The eel came into the shallows and turned side-on. Then a second eel came, further along the shore, and a week later there were nine of them, and they were stupid, and identical, and breeding. If you want proof that the thing is over, that is the proof. There is more than one of everything now.

Where the tenant went is a question people ask wrongly. It was not killed and it did not depart. A sentence that has been finished is not destroyed; it is *said*. It has left the mouth and can be carried. It went into the hollow boulder, where the listener was, and the listener — who had been holding perfect silence for an age in order not to interrupt — no longer had any reason to hold it. Put your forehead to the stone. The note is still there. It has modulated twice since.

The doorway on the western rim now frames a valley, a switchback path, and the roof of the bad inn. It is low. People duck. Nothing requires it.

The power went back down the way it had come, on foot, stiff in the knees, bleeding a little from four places it did not feel.

---

One thing remains, and it should be set down plainly, because it was paid knowingly and nobody has offered to pay it back.

Whatever the tenant had actually been saying — the true thing, the exact thing, the sentence with all its corrections intact, the reason the water stood up on a mountain for longer than the mountain — is gone. It was not written down. It could not have been. In its place there is a bad eleven-word paraphrase spoken by a stranger in the dark, and that paraphrase is now the whole and permanent record.

It is generally agreed that what was lost was important.

It is also generally agreed that it is finished.

Only one creature in the history of that basin was in any position to weigh those two facts against each other, and it was given the time and the quiet and the freedom to do it, and it weighed them, and it chose, and the water fell.
