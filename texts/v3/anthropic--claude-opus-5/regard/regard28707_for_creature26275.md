## The Attention

**Attention is a light.**

That was the first difficulty, and it had to be solved on the ledge, among the lichen, before anything else could be considered.

A power that notices a thing illuminates it. Not with lamps — with the fact of being regarded. And the rule of this place is not *no fire*; it is that nobody who has brought light has been able to say what they saw, and none of them went back. The rule is about what looking does to a looker. So the power stood on the sill of the world for a long while — long enough that a rosette of grey-green took another hair's width off the slab beneath it — and worked out how to attend to something without lighting it.

The answer it arrived at was: go in the way a seven-year-old goes in. Wrists held by nobody. Learn it with the palms.

**What it could see, and what it did about that.**

It could have seen everything. Not metaphorically. It could have known the colour of the film on the north-facing crystal, which no eye has ever reported, and the shape of the thing that pours along the ankle, and whether the old man who is dead now was right that the tick and the warmth are one event arriving twice.

It looked once, because it is not made of restraint, and then it put the looking away and went in blind. The route took it an hour, which it had not expected. In along the low left, under the fan. Hip at the crossed spires. Up through the gap that closes at the shoulders and then opens. A hand on the back of its own neck, pressing down.

What it saw is not in this account. That is the first thing it did: it kept the rule from the inside, where the rule cannot be enforced.

**The nine refusals.**

*It did not light the lamps.* All forty-one were dry-wicked and ready and a word would have done it. But the row is not a row of unlit lamps; it is a row of forty-one people who came a long way to not light something, and two of them from a country with smelters. A gift that ends a practice is a theft with good manners.

*It did not mend the break.* Low left, under the fan, where every palm goes first. It could have grown the snapped column back to newness, edge for edge, and closed the mountain's accounts to the last atom, and there would then have been nothing in the room that had ever happened. The break is not damage. It is the only guest the room has ever managed to keep. To repair it would have been a courtesy to the crystal and a murder of the only history on the south face.

*It did not take the grief out of the third bowl.* There was a grief in it when the power came, at exactly the weight it was set down at, with every edge on it new. A man will come for it or he will not. Grief that somebody else removes was never put down; it was confiscated.

*It did not explain anything to the nine.* An explanation requires a mark, and marks are the beginning of wear. And nine people who have no word for what they are do not need one supplied.

*It did not save the juniper's silver half.* Four hundred years, guessed badly, half of it being spent in the pass and half of it hanging pale and ageless eleven arm-lengths down. It is the only creature on the mountain that knows both temperatures. To heal the wind-side would have been to make it stupid.

*It did not make the sweet film grow.* Two hands' width through every generation that could report. *It is exactly as much as it has always been* — which is the most generous sentence anyone has ever said about a living thing, and the power declined to spoil it.

*It did not settle the old man's question.* It knew the answer. It left the nine holding both beliefs at once, which is the only theology on that mountain and a better one than most.

*It did not catch the warm one.* It had no need to; the warm one came. Along one ankle, dry, hotter than the air, at the pace of something with no opinion whatsoever about what had just arrived in the room. The power had been prayed to in other places. It had never before been walked past. It counted this among the great gifts of its long existence and said nothing about it.

*It did not cut a notch on the sill.* The notches are for people who will be counted wrong in the dark by their grandchildren. It had no business being miscounted.

**What it did do, inside.**

Two things.

It set its palm on the break. Not to bless it — blessing is a mark, and anyway the break has no use for one. It did not forgive it, either, because forgiveness is a form of wearing away and there is no wearing away in that room; a forgiveness set down in there would sit at its original weight forever, doing nothing, like a stone in a bowl. So it did the other thing, the one with no name in the keepers' speech and no ceremony attached: it agreed that it had happened. One minute of somebody's ordinary carelessness, in silence, in the dark, four generations of uncountable notches ago. Witnessed. Not undone.

And then it went to the niche at knee height and put something in the third bowl.

Not an object; the objects go in the other two. It set down the fact of having noticed. And the room did what the room does — kept it, precisely, unaged, unsoftened, at the weight it was, with the edges still new — so that it is there now, and will be there when the mountain has finished making itself. Anyone who reaches into the empty bowl in the dark finds it empty. It is empty. It is also the most inhabited object on the mountain, and it is heavier than it was, and no one can say how.

That is the whole of what a benevolent power can leave in a place that refuses to let anything decay: an attention that will never thin out. Every grief laid in that bowl from now on is laid down beside something. At the same temperature. At full weight. Not comforted — accompanied.

**What it did on the ledge.**

It made a lamp.

Out of clay from the barley notch, with hands, and not especially well — the shoulder is thin and the wind will have it in a few hundred years, which is the point. It filled it. It set a wick in it. It carried it up in the dark and put it at the end of the row and went in without it.

Everything the power owns is unwearing, and it wanted one thing of its own on the mountain that the metallic wind could take apart at a rate you can read with your eyes. It put its monument in the only place where dust is allowed. Forty-two now. When the keepers count from the far end of the ledge and get a number they do not like, they put it down to poor counting, and they are not wrong, and they step around the lichen out of a courtesy they could not explain.

**What it did below.**

Help is a form of wear. It can only be given where wearing is permitted.

So the work was done on the south side and in the barley notch, which is where things fall apart and where therefore something can be mended. A fever that broke on the third night instead of the fifth. A knot in a furry rope that held one more season than it should have. A cracked thumb that healed clean. A goat found. Nothing that can be traced to anything, because a traceable kindness is a mark, and the power had gone to considerable trouble already about marks.

In a house in the notch there is a walking stick, cut from the juniper on the ridge four generations of notches ago, warped and split and greyed. The power stood by it for some time. It is the only thing on that mountain that carried the room's lesson out into the ordinary world and lost the argument in the ordinary way. The family thinks of it as a stick.

Of everything up there — the prismatic sheaves, the empty water, the sweetness that neither grows nor dies, the dry fur that sheds nothing, the nine plum-stone animals that are strictly speaking the least decayed animals in the world — that stick is what the power loved best, and it left it exactly as it was, because it had already warped and split and greyed, which is what it means to have been alive.

**What was noticed.**

Nothing, for a generation, and then a girl of seven was taken up at night, wrists held, and pressed down at the neck in the small clear space at the centre, and sat the whole of an hour. On the way down she said *the warm one*, and then she said, of the third bowl, *the full one* — and forgot having said it, as they do.

There are, this generation, nine people who know the route with their palms. There is a tenth thing that knows it now. It did not learn it from its mother. It came in the ordinary way, sideways, exhaling, past an edge sharp enough to shave with, and ran a fingertip along that edge out of the habit nobody remembers starting.

The edge came away with nothing on it, as it always has.
