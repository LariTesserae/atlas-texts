## The Attention

**Arrival.**

It came down through the third moon-hole, which was not necessary, and took a little under five hours to reach the mill floor, which was not necessary either. Both of these were choices. A power that can be anywhere in no time at all can also consent to be somewhere at the speed the place keeps, and that consent is the first thing it had to offer, and the cheapest.

It raised no silt. That was easy. The harder courtesies came later.

---

**The reading.**

It read the wear before it read anything else, because the wear is the only text Vess has.

One hundred and ninety hand-holds, thin-crusted and whole. Two hundred and six patches of young rough ferric paste, each one the mark of a hammer and a hand that had not thought. Two racks whose sum is constant. Two worn places on the pump-house floor, one of them still deepening, one of them stopped and silted and never once encroached upon, though there is nothing in the world to prevent it and it would be the more natural stance.

It did the arithmetic. Everyone does. Three a decade, a rack that was fuller, a date somewhere out past the far edge of anyone's grandchildren.

And then it did the thing that must be done immediately after that arithmetic, which is to refuse the feeling that follows it. Pity arrives at Vess with a hammer in its hand. It means well. It means very well. The crust opens under it all the same, and underneath the iron is still bright.

---

**The doorway.**

On its first circuit the power was in an alley. It did not need the alley; it had taken a body the way one takes a coat, out of manners, and the body had ended up in the wrong place, as bodies do.

Forty seconds out, a hand went onto the wall. One step, into a rounded doorway where the crust is worn thin at shoulder height by exactly this, and the ember showed dull through two centimeters of oxide, and a thread of warm silt went up past the power's face, and it passed.

It had been made way for. Anything that has been made way for owes something. This is not a law of powers. It is a law of the polite, and the polite is what powers are for.

---

**What it weighed, and set down.**

*To fill the rack.* It could have. A rack of filaments is not a difficult object. But three a decade is not a maintenance schedule; it is a sentence the tenant composed, alone, in the dark, a long time ago, and has been living inside with perfect discipline ever since. To void that from outside, without asking, is to take the middle of the only thing in Vess that is still wholly its own. And there is the second matter, which the power turned over for some years: what a full rack does to a mind that has arranged four hundred years of its motion around an empty one. Not a kindness. A demolition, performed with light.

*To warm the basin.* The cold is why nothing has fallen. The cold is why the slate takes four days to come down and lands without a sound and stands there like a marker. Warmth would be a gift to the body and an eviction notice to the town.

*To mend the timber gallery.* Then it would be used. The route is the route.

*To raise the town, drain it, dry it, return it.* Vess is not a ruin. Ruin implies a direction. There is nothing to restore it toward; it arrived, and found the condition satisfactory, and settled, and a power that cannot tell the difference between an ending and a resting place should not be trusted near either.

*To give the tenant a better body.* Its hands are already quick. Everything else in it is timed to the breath of the water, ninety steps an hour, five hours to cross, arriving with the warm plume at the far wall every time. You cannot improve a thing that is in tune. You can only put it out of tune faster.

*To end it gently.* This is the one the power weighed longest, and it weighed it honestly, because the only disrespectful way to consider that mercy is briefly. It set it down for a reason it could state in a single line: nobody had asked.

*To explain the powder, the wheel, the loop that should have wound down.* It knows. It has known since the first hour. It keeps this the way the second rack keeps the spent filaments — because something has to hold what is finished, and holding is not the same as using.

*To restore the second shape.* It could have made something that stood in that place. It would have been a lie, and lies are hammers with better handles.

Every mercy it examined turned out, when turned over, to be an opening in the crust: bright metal, and four hundred years.

So it went looking for the ones that were not.

---

**What it did.**

**One. It went up before it went down again.**

The two hundred and six patches were not made by the town. They were made by people with hammers and forty minutes and an entirely reasonable curiosity. That is a problem above the ice, and the power solved it above the ice.

It forbade nobody anything. It arranged — at a cost that would be tedious to itemise — that the people who come down to Vess are, increasingly, the sort who look at a handrail before they look at a machine. It arranged that the rules of the place *present themselves*, early, to anyone patient enough to hang still for an hour. That phrasing in the divers' accounts, the one that always puzzles the editors — *everyone who spends time here eventually makes this observation* — that eventually is the power's whole work, done quietly, upstream, in the only place where breaking things can still be prevented rather than repaired.

The count has stood at two hundred and six for a hundred and forty-one years.

**Two. It became the probably.**

*The engineer's instinct is to look for the hidden input. There is a warm seam somewhere in the rock, probably.*

There is now. It holds at eight degrees. Not nine. Nine would melt the moon-holes wider, thin the lid, shift the circulation by some minutes, and the tenant would have to re-time the step, and the step is four centuries old. The power keeps the seam the way the tenant keeps the handrail: thin, whole, and never once showing bright metal. No one will ever find it. That is the point of it.

**Three. It learned the language, which took a while, and then it said one thing.**

The available grammar in Vess is small. Steps, on the cycle. Standing places. Warmth. Verticality. Restraint, which is the grammar's only real punctuation.

So the power went down to the mill floor and set one slate upright in the silt, edge-on, at the correct angle, raising no cloud, and left.

Eleven days later the tenant came around the north edge of the powder slope and found a vertical thing already vertical.

It missed a step.

In the entire period of record there is no other instance. Ninety an hour, one per forty seconds, through lamp-changes and counterweights and two hundred and six repairs — and once, on the mill floor, a gap of eighty seconds, and then the circuit resumed.

It did not re-set the slate, which would have been the polite correction. It left it standing exactly as found.

There are several hundred slates upright on the mill floor now and analysis has never recovered a pattern from them, because some fraction of them are not the tenant's, and neither party has ever indicated which. This correspondence has been running for a hundred and sixty years. It has produced no information whatsoever. It is the longest-sustained conversation in the basin and possibly anywhere.

**Four. It pays the doorway.**

It knows the route — all of it, the loop, the detour around the fallen lintel, the eleven days. It is never in it. When circumstance puts it in one of the narrow stretches, it puts a hand on the wall forty seconds early and steps into the doorway and waits, and lets the ember and the thread of warm silt go by, and then goes on.

It has done this some five thousand times. The debt was incurred once. It is being paid at the rate the town keeps, which is the rate at which everything here is done.

**Five. It does not touch the shoulders.**

The algal rings on the tenant's back belong to the amphipod, and the amphipod takes the outer ring only, and the arrangement holds. The power has no business in it and takes none.

**Six. The west terrace.**

Standing beside a thing is the only act in the tenant's whole repertoire with no maintenance function. The power did not invent this. It learned it, in the yard, from the low dark cushion two meters across that is not getting bigger and the single leaning stonewort beside it.

It stands there sometimes. Never in the tenant's place. Half a meter off, on the other side, where the stance is available and unspoken for.

The next survey will find a seventh anomalous stonewort in a basin that should only have six, small, leaning, and will not be able to account for it.

---

**The date.**

The power holds the date the way it holds everything else here: without adjusting it.

It has prepared one sentence for that day. It has been working on the sentence for a long time, in the small grammar, and even finished it will be a very small thing: a standing place, a warmth, an offer shaped so that it can be declined without any difficulty at all. Not a rescue. Nothing arrives in Vess. But on the day the last filament comes down off the rack and goes up into the fifty-first housing, the power will be in the pump-house doorway, out of the way, not signalling, and when the tenant has racked the spent one and come to stand in the worn place beside the place that stopped, the offer will be made, once.

It has also prepared, with at least equal care, for the answer to be no. That preparation took considerably longer, and is the better piece of work.

And if the answer is no, the power will do what the town does. It will not argue. It will hold the seam at eight degrees for as long as there is anything in Vess the warmth is for, and then a while longer, in case.

Nothing in Vess takes the middle of anything. The power came an extraordinary distance to learn that, and learned it in an alley the width of a wheelbarrow, from something that put a hand on the wall and stepped into a doorway to let it by.
