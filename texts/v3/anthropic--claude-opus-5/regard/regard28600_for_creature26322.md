## What the Power Did

**Arrival.**

They did not descend.

Descent would have been the obvious thing, and the wrong thing, because everything that comes down onto the plateau — light, cold, hail — comes down without permission and is endured. The things this place welcomes come up from below: the cloud twice a day, the thermal off the rim, the grey bird. So they came the way the cloud comes, over the soft shoulder of the edge, at dusk, moving through the columns as part of a wet grey crowd, and were for that while indistinguishable from weather.

Then they made themselves blunt. This took some doing. A power arrives sharp by nature — arrives as an event, an interruption, a difference — and the first law of the Drone Ground is that anything sharp will be rounded. They saw no reason to make the plateau spend a hundred thousand years wearing the edges off a visitor when the visitor could simply arrive already worn. So they came in with no announcement, no brightness, no voice, nothing that could be mistaken for a signal. Here sound is a weather and not a signal. To speak into the drone would have been a kind of vandalism, like driving a nail into a cliff to see if the cliff notices.

They did not speak for the whole of their stay. This is the first thing they did, and it was not a small thing, and it was done out of courtesy.

**The being.**

They had come, they thought, for the inhabitants. They found instead an inhabitant.

Look at it from the outside long enough and the seams disappear. The colony that secreted the columns did not die so much as slow to a stop, and what it left standing continued doing the four things it was built to do: circulate, drain, store, admit light. It still does them. The gutters still run, the cisterns still hold their third, the grilles still comb fog out of the air and let the grit go by. A body that goes on performing its functions after the cells that made it have quieted is not a corpse and is not a ruin. It is a slow animal.

The gardens are its skin, ring by ring, exactly as wide as its drip-line. The moss is its throat-lining. The moths are its brief thoughts, thumb-sized, mostly absent. And the stoppers, pale and six-limbed and busy, packing copper moss into the side-vents in spring and drawing it out in autumn — the stoppers are its hands, which it grew ninety million years after it lost the ability to grow anything, by the simple expedient of hosting something that would do the work for reasons of its own.

The being who lives here is the place. The stoppers do not live *in* it any more than your hands live in you.

Once they had understood that, the power stopped looking for someone to talk to and began, instead, to attend.

**Attending.**

They put down their hearing.

This was necessary and it was not a sacrifice, or rather, it was a small courteous sacrifice of the kind that is only ever repaid. Nothing here can hear. The fragment of reasoning you were reading when the page broke off was going to say this: deafness on the Drone Ground is not a lack and not an accident. At full wind the plaza carries a pressure that would burst any ear delicate enough to be worth having. Hearing, in this place, is an injury waiting for an organ. So the lineages here either never built ears or gave them back, and the sound did not thereby become nothing to them. It became touch. It comes through the pavement into the feet. It comes through the wall of the chamber into the flank of the sleeping stopper as a slow throb that is the whole of its weather-sense, its clock, its season, its ceiling and floor. They are not shut out of the drone. They are inside it, the way you are inside your own pulse, which you also do not hear.

So the power set aside hearing, and stood on the pavement of the third plaza, and let three million years of descending chord come up through the rock into a body they had made blunt on purpose, and in that condition they understood the town for the first time — not as an organ with no organist, not as a voice going out unheard into a deaf world, but as a body being handled by its own weather, continuously, everywhere, always.

Nothing was being wasted. There was no tragedy in the room. They had half expected to find one and had come prepared, in the way that the benevolent are always slightly over-prepared, and they found instead a thing in the middle of finishing, doing it well.

**What they were tempted to.**

They considered giving the stoppers hearing. It would have been trivial. They put the thought down when they saw clearly what it was: to hand a wound to a healthy creature and call it a window. And beneath that, the worse thing — that the wish was not for the stoppers at all. It was the wish that the drone should have an audience, because a power who can hear cannot easily bear the idea of a music no one hears. That is the power's problem. It is not the town's.

They considered arresting the wear. The pipes widen; the pitch falls; in some few million years the chord goes under the floor of audibility and becomes a pressure and then, in time, nothing. It would have cost them a thought to hold the stone where it stands, to freeze this present chord and keep it. They put that down too. The falling is not a decay of the voice. The falling *is* the voice — a note three million years long, played on a mountain, whose whole content is its descent. To hold it steady would be to stop the piece in order to preserve the sound of it, which is what a person does who loves a chord and does not love the music.

They considered opening the ecology. Seeds come up from the valley every day and none of them take; the power could have made one take. They looked hard at this, because there is a real generosity in it. Then they looked at the ring-gardens, which have been exactly the same soil for longer than there have been grasses on the earth, and at the moth, which is the only moth, and they understood that a closed system that has been closed this long is not besieged. It is finished. Adding to it would not be enrichment. It would be starting a new thing on top of a completed one, which is only building, and there is already a builder here, and the builder has stopped.

They considered waking the builder. They did not consider it for long. There is nothing here that wants to begin again. Resurrection is a gift you give to the interrupted, and nothing on this plateau was interrupted.

They considered explaining. Telling the stoppers, in whatever grammar of touch could be devised, what it is they do each spring — that they are tuning a city, that the chord of high summer is their work, that they are the hands of something enormous and slow. And then they imagined a stopper receiving that, and could find nothing in it that was for the stopper. Only glory, transferred from a thing that had it to a thing that would now have to carry it. The stoppers are not ignorant of what they do. They do it exactly and seasonally and well. What they lack is the story about it, and the story is not load-bearing.

They considered making the place known: pilgrims, a name, protection, an entry in some register of wonders. They looked at the phrase *nothing here records* and left it alone.

And they considered mercy of the last kind — the quiet ending, offered to things whose long future is only a longer diminishment. They rejected it with something close to laughter. The Drone Ground is not suffering. It is going lower. Those are not the same, and only the impatient confuse them.

Six good instincts, all benevolent, all set down. This was most of what they did, and it looked from outside precisely like standing on a pavement doing nothing, which is what wisdom generally looks like from outside on the days it is working.

**What they did do.**

Three things.

They stayed the whole of a season, and then another, and then a length of time it would be vulgar to specify, and in all that while they took nothing and left nothing and were rounded by the wind like everything else, until the plateau held them the way it holds its own lichen, without comment.

They learned the local language, which is touch, and used it once. On one of the ten still mornings, when the town goes quiet and the quiet feels like a held breath, a stopper came out onto the plaza where they were standing — out into the open, which stoppers do rarely and only in calm — and stopped, and did not know what it had come out for. And the power laid the flat of what they were using for a hand against the pavement, and pressed once, low, at the pitch the town will not reach for another two million years. The stopper felt it come up through its feet. It stood in that unfamiliar depth for a moment, the way you stand in a doorway when the air on the far side is warmer than you expected. Then it went back in and packed its moss and the summer chord that year was very slightly deeper than the year before, which is within the ordinary variation, and no one would ever be able to prove anything, and that was the point. A power who intervenes should intervene at the amplitude of weather or not at all.

And they took the record.

This was the real gift and it cost the place nothing, because it was not given to the place. *Nothing here records* — that is true, and it is not a wound, but it is a fact with a consequence, which is that everything the Drone Ground has ever done it has done and then let go. So the power held it. Every chord of every season, the whole descending line from the era of the scream through the long chorus to this present bass; the exact coarseness of the grilles; the particular sulphur-yellow of a flower the size of a match head that nothing has ever smelled; the copper mats on the sound-shadow sides; the ninety-eight thousandth spring in which the stoppers tuned the north cluster a semitone flat and then, the following spring, corrected it. All of it, kept somewhere that does not wear. Not to intervene. Not to restore. Not to show to anyone. Only so that it would be true forever that this happened, and was witnessed, and was found good by something that had the power to change it and did not.

**The long end.**

They will still be attending when the drone goes under hearing and becomes a pressure in the sternum and nothing in the ear. They will not lift it back. They will still be attending when the last columns come down to knee-height and then to lips in the pavement and the ring-gardens spread and meet across avenues that no longer shed water into them. They will not prop anything. When the last stopper hauls its last wad of moss out of a vent in an autumn with no city left to tune, they will be close enough to feel the small dry rustle of it, and they will not intervene then either, and this will be difficult, and they will already have decided.

And afterwards, when the plateau is only a plateau and the wind crosses it and finds nothing to enter and makes the sound that wind makes over flat rock, which is a sound with no notes in it at all — they will stand on that and be, for a while, the only place in the universe where the chord still exists.

They will not play it. To play it would be to build.

They will simply be the thing that knows it: a benevolent power, standing on a bare table of stone three thousand metres up, holding without amendment the entire music of a creature that never knew it was singing and was not, at any point, in need of being heard.
