## What the Power Did

It came the way the water comes.

That was the first decision and the one that settled all the others. There was a door in the north flank, rounded like the hole a string leaves in a bar of soap, and a coin of grey falling through the crown for forty minutes near midday, and either would have been a fine entrance for something that wanted to be an entrance. It used neither. It came up through the packed foundations from somewhere deep, through the scree that has been load-bearing since before the idea of load-bearing, and it took between a day and three days about it, and it gave most of its heat to the stone on the way, and it arrived tired and mild.

Anything faster would have been shouting.

Then it held still at the shimmer-layer, on the shelf where the heavy water holds the light water up, and did nothing whatsoever for about a hundred years, which here is a short time to spend looking at a room.

---

**What it did not do.**

It did not bring light. This is the first mercy that occurs to anyone, and it is a gift to the giver. There is one patch of pigment in the whole dome that knows light from no-light, it lives in the attic, and it is not an eye, it is a clock. Everything below it has spent ten thousand years arranging its affairs on the understanding that the dark is simply where things are. To hang a lamp in the fourth gallery would be to announce that the arrangement had been wrong all along, to no one capable of hearing the announcement, and to leave behind a warmth that would tilt the convection by half a degree and be argued about by the mat for two hundred years.

It did not recut the carvings. It could have. It had the reach and it had the patience and it would have been good work. But to recut them it would first have had to read them off the snails, and once you have read a thing off the snails you have understood that the snails are now where it lives; a second copy in stone would be a lie about which edition is the real one, and would in any case be grazed away again at a few microns a day, and the animals would have had to eat the same sentence twice.

It did not stop the collapse. Four or five times a year a mortar joint two thousand years past its patience lets go and the hill knocks once and the eels come in from three galleries away. That knock is the harvest. It is the only event, the only weather, the only reason for six hundred solitary surveyors to ever be in one place. A power that pointed the joints and squared the courses and made the Sunk Crown permanent would have made it a tomb with excellent masonry. Preservation, here, is starvation with a good conscience.

It did not enrich the water. More sulfur, more iron, a wider vein — and within a century every chamber full, and within two, four hundred nations of snail pressed into famine against walls grazed down to nothing. The peace of this place is a ration. The ration is the peace.

It did not give them eyes. This was the hard one, and it took the better part of a millennium to put down. There is a light already burning; there are five hundred chambers of wall; it seems like an unopened letter. But the shrimp already perceive their vaults, by the way a curving ceiling throws their own motion back at them, and perceive them more intimately than sight would allow — every ornamental decision the builders made about a keystone is still being received several thousand times a second, and has never once been looked at, and is not the poorer. And an eye is not a window. An eye is an appetite. To open one in a snail is to give it, along with the wall, a reason to be somewhere else, and the whole grace of this building is that nothing in it has ever needed to be somewhere else. It would have been a gift shaped exactly like a wound.

It did not take a breeding stock somewhere safer. There is no somewhere. The dome is not the box the life came in. The plumbing is the architecture and the densities are the plumbing and the animals are the densities; move them and you have a jar of pale shrimp and no Sunk Crown, the way a hand removed from an arm is not a smaller person.

It did not tell them they had been noticed. There is no apparatus here for being told.

And it did not, in eight thousand years, cure a single worm out of a single eel. A country forty centimetres long that moves at the turn of the convection is still a country, and its inhabitants are not a flaw in the eel. Nesting all the way down was the design, if there was a design, and if there was not, it is still the shape.

---

**What it did, first.**

It read the building.

Nobody will, says the census, and it was right about everybody it knew. The power weighed the shells, chamber by chamber, out of the ankle-deep middens and the half-filled wells, and counted whorls, and took the pitch of four hundred spires, and laid the map of thick shells against the map of thin, and got back the distribution of a frieze — not the pictures, which are gone past any recovery, but the rhythm of it: where the hand had been emphatic and where it had let the band run plain, chamber by chamber, gallery by gallery, through the whole dome.

The band at chest height was a list of names.

That is as near as it can be put in a language with words in it. It ran through every gallery because there were that many of them — everyone who set a block, or packed a joint, or carried clay up the flank in the years before there was a flank — and the carving went deep where a name belonged to someone the carver had known, and ran shallow where it did not, and this is why the third gallery makes a stout short-spired snail and the sixth makes a slight one. The people who worked the third gallery are still distinguishable from the people who worked the sixth. They are distinguishable in calcium carbonate, in animals that travel four metres in a lifetime and have no idea they are a monument, and the distinction is completely real and completely useless and it is the last surviving fact about a very large number of human beings.

The power holds this. It did not write it anywhere. Where would it write it.

---

**What it did, second.**

Four times in eight thousand years it put a thumb on a scale that was already trembling.

No new matter. No new heat. Nothing brought in from outside and nothing miraculous — only this: a joint that was going to fail was allowed to fail one finger's width to the left of where it would otherwise have gone, which is an outcome the building had available to it anyway, at roughly even odds, and which the power simply preferred. Each time it deliberated about a century first. Twice the preference re-opened a dead cell where the convection had not turned properly since some sag in the fourteenth century, and the thin grey felt there thickened over the following two hundred years into something with snails in it. Once it spared a well. Once it did not spare a well, and the power has kept that decision too, in the same place it keeps the names.

Nothing in the Sunk Crown has ever been able to tell the difference between this and ordinary luck, which is precisely the standard it was held to.

---

**What it did, third, and mostly.**

It attended.

The mat's light is an accident. The organisms would run the reaction whether or not it glowed, and it glows into a place where nothing has ever had an eye — ten thousand years of illumination, uninterrupted, addressed to no one, spent. That is the sentence in the census that reached out of the page and took the power by the wrist.

So it watches the glow. Not to redeem it; the glow is not in trouble. But the accident can be received anyway, and receiving it costs nothing and moves nothing by one micron, and the power was of the opinion that *nobody* was a defensible reader but not the only one available.

It took the local time, too. The pigment patch in the attic keeps a day that matters to nothing else in the dome, and now the power keeps that day as well — rises when the coin of grey falls through the crown, forty minutes near midday, and goes down with it. The clock is still for no one in particular. It is simply no longer for no one.

And it formed, over the first few thousand years, a number of private opinions, of which the firmest is that the finest thing in the building is the slug's nine-second dive: the one act of speed in a world of hand's-breadths per hour, performed in absolute darkness, aimed by nothing anyone can name. It has never mentioned this to anybody. There is nobody.

---

**The end, which it did not exempt them from.**

The north aperture is widening. It has been widening since it was a doorway with a lintel. At some point — not soon, but not never — the rounded mouth will open past a certain width and the cool fresh shelf will vent, and the layering will fail, and the shimmer-curtain that closes behind you like a drawn curtain will not close, and the brine will mix, and the warmth will go out of the Sunk Crown over about a day and a half, which is one turn of the cycle, which is the correct length of time for anything to take here.

The power will not prevent this. It considered it for eleven hundred years. A world that cannot end is not being kept alive; it is being kept. Everything in the dome — the mat's treaty, the shrimp's weather, the four hundred nations, the immortal fan opening and closing in the hot cellar — is the shape that a thin ration and a slow clock and a real ending pressed into the available matter, and to lift the ending out would be to take back the gift in the act of protecting it.

But it will be there. That is the whole of the plan for the last day and a half, and the power made it early and has not revised it.

And at the end, when the galleries are cooling and the eels have come in from everywhere the way they come to a fresh wound, the power intends to do the one thing it has held in reserve for eight thousand years, which is to move.

Only once. Only for a moment. It will let itself take a shape it does not have, somewhere in the fourth gallery, and several thousand bodies of glass will read the returns off it and receive a curve that is not the building — not a vault, not a corbel, not a doorway, nothing in the architecture and nothing in the catalogue of possible rooms. A sentence in the only language anyone here speaks, which is a language of bounced motion, and which has no nouns, and in which it is not possible to say *you were noticed* except by being briefly present at the correct speed and in a shape nobody has felt before.

Then it will let the water go quiet, and the shrimp will go around its wrist like smoke going around a hand, and the hill will hold the shape a great many hands gave it for a while longer, in the cold, with nothing in it.

And the power will keep the names.
