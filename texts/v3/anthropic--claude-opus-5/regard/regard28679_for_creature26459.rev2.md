## The Inspection at Sowder's Bottom

*A record of what was done and what was not, kept in the same hand throughout.*

### Approach

We came down from the north through the cut-over scrub, on foot, at the speed of a person, because there is a way into the place and it is discourteous to arrive by any other. We felt the pressure against the sternum at about a hundred and forty metres, where everyone feels it.

The dog was lying at the rim with its ears flat. We sat down with it for the nine or ten minutes its person was below, and that was the first thing we did, and it was not planned. The dog is not wrong about the hollow. Nothing needed doing about the dog except sitting with it. In the keeper's own book, under that date, in her flat annoyed hand: *dog easier today*.

### On attention, which is the part nobody counts

Before deciding anything, we watched.

We attended one pod-lung through four hundred fillings. We walked the ring path at the ring's own speed for a full revolution. We stayed inside stack four with a spider for eleven thousand ascents before we would permit ourselves an opinion about whether it was suffering, and we could have formed that opinion in the first second, and it would have been the same opinion. The whole difference between a benevolent power and a careless one lies in that interval, and none of it lies in the opinion.

Finding on the spider: we looked for the marks that fear leaves and found the marks that habit leaves, which are not the same marks. Some doubt remains. We have filed the doubt rather than resolved it, because resolving it was within our power and would have been a lie.

Finding on the works: we went looking for a sufferer at the centre of Sowder's Bottom the way you would drag a mill pond for a body, and there is not one. It is not enduring. It is occupied. Those are different states and only one of them is our business.

### Standing orders adopted on the second day

1. Do nothing that a keeper would have to explain.
2. Repair no scar that somebody living is using.
3. Never relieve a creature of the burden it is the shape of.

The third order is dangerous and has been used by worse powers than us to excuse a great deal. We permit ourselves it only where the attention has been paid in full and can be produced on request.

### The hard case

The grease-beetles were the hard case and we want the reasoning in the record.

Two hundred blind animals, walking two centimetres a day against the turn, arriving nowhere, dying within a metre of the wax they hatched in, unable to survive nine days outside the basin under any conditions anyone has contrived. Liberation was considered seriously and for a long time. It was rejected. A door is not a kindness to something with no organ for *elsewhere*. To lift two hundred beetles out of their sockets and carry them up to the ridge road would be to kill two hundred beetles with our own hands while feeling extremely well about it, and we have seen that done, and the feeling well is always the load-bearing part.

We went over the sugar pores with some suspicion, expecting a whip. The pores run sweeter where the wear is worst; more beetles come; the wear is made good; the flow slackens. It is not a whip. Nothing here is paid in arrears. We note without further comment that this is a better arrangement than several we have been asked to look at this century.

What we did for the beetles concerns the northeast arc. We established what happened there in the 1930s — a clay lens nine metres back from the wall slumped, the seep went cold and quick through the bed for a season, the wax would not set, and one brood failed and took the arc with it. The lens is working again. We have put a hand under it. We are not going to describe the repair, because a repair of that sort, once described, becomes a thing people come looking for.

We did not grind out the fused patch. The basin ticks over it every fourteen months and the keeper listens for the tick and her whole year is hung on it, and standing order two applies.

### The ferment

Four hundred kilograms of the oldest thing in the basin, keeping no time, aware of nothing, boiler of an engine it has no concept of. Nothing wanted. Nothing done. We stayed some hours anyway, the way one sits by a river.

### Considered and declined

**The press.** We did not open it. We did not need to open it to see, and we saw, and the finding is not recorded here. It is not an answer that would do anyone any good, and the local phrasing — *reasons that are nobody's business* — turned out on inspection to be exactly and technically correct, which is not what we expected from a phrase.

**The arithmetic.** Twenty-one scars up the northeast slope at sixty years apiece, against four hundred years of ring. We checked both counts. Both counts are right. We have left it alone and recommend that it be left alone.

**The boot sole.** We did not lift it and we did not reverse the compression, which goes one way and is not ours to run backwards. We did establish, because it cost us nothing, that he is not in the mat. His legs went. That is what the gaps in the third hand are, and the crowded runs are the days he made it down late and caught up. He went home and lived some years and died in a chair of something ordinary in a town we are not going to name. There is nobody left for whom this is news, and the one person who might want it has not asked, so it stays here.

**Telling her anything.** Declined. She has not asked. She does not use the word *tending*; we have not used it either.

**Her sleep.** The kettle on the low gas all night for thirty-five years is not a symptom and we did not treat it. On one night in October the gas went out and she slept seven hours in a quiet room and woke rested and badly frightened, and lit it again the following evening and every evening since. We will not do that again. It was offered once. A tie that a person has chosen to keep is a possession, and we do not take possessions in order to demonstrate that we could.

**The second line at knee height.** Not touched. Nothing added, nothing prevented, nothing arranged. This was the largest single act in the record and it consisted entirely of holding still.

A practice that has outlived its reason is not automatically a trap. It is how most good things get carried across a bad century. But a person ought to be able to leave, and leaving requires being told, once, out loud, that nobody is required. We considered several ways of getting that said. In the event we waited, which is a thing we can do, and on a Tuesday in March in the ring path, without having planned to, she said: *You know nobody has to do this.* And the child said: *I know.* Whether that is true is not ours. We take no credit and did not need to.

### The exception

Standing order one was broken once, knowingly, and the record should show it was broken knowingly and not by mistake.

The third hand ends mid-group, at two. We cut the remaining three, in his hand, with his own chisel, which is the worn stub in the niche, and we put the chisel back where it was, against the folding rule.

It repairs nothing. It is not for him; he is a long way past the reach of a tally mark. It is because a group of five is a group of five, and because in a hollow whose entire character is that everything dropped is drawn in and pressed and made into the weave, a small unfinished thing had been left lying about for a hundred years and nobody else was ever going to be able to reach it.

She found them on the Tuesday following. She counted the run three times and checked the total against her own book and stood there a while with her hand flat on the brick. Then she cleared the grate, scraped the collar, walked the ring path without stopping, re-laid the strap-vine three turns left hand, cut her mark, and was out before the light went green. She has told nobody, which is correct.

### The work done elsewhere

Everything above is small. The largest thing done in the matter of Sowder's Bottom was done forty-one miles away in a room with a fan heater and a broken window catch.

There was an abstraction licence application. Six boreholes on the far side of the ridge, all of them perfectly reasonable, none of them wicked. The consequence, buried in an appendix that nobody reads, was eleven centimetres off the water table under the basin, and the mat above the silica bed drying, and the whole works seized inside nine years with the beetles going first.

The application was refused on a Thursday on grounds unconnected with any of this, by three people who had never heard of the place, two of whom were mildly irritated about the delay and all of whom then went to lunch.

That is mostly what a power is. Not the hand in the hollow. The Thursday.

### The gift

The one preference the tenant has ever shown is that it is a superb and stupid copyist. Lay a repeating shape and a fixed tempo in front of it and within a season or two it will have taken the shape up somewhere, usually badly, usually at the wrong scale, usually where it does nothing at all.

There was an obvious temptation here and we want it minuted: we could have written our name in it. It would have taken. It would have come up in forty years in the spacing of bosses on the south arc, to within a centimetre, and someone would have found it and it would have been read as a number and argued about. We declined, on the grounds that it is vanity, and also on the grounds that the hollow has quite enough numbers in it already for people to find.

We did not retune the number six reed-stack. It has been going flat since before the Napoleonic wars and it will go on flattening, and its drift is a piece of music on a scale no listener hears the whole of, and to reach in and jerk it back up a semitone because a frog is out of pocket would be the act of somebody who had come to be seen helping.

What we left instead was B flat. The 1911 note. We stood under the fronds in the warm rain and gave the hollow that pitch at intervals it could read, for some weeks, not loudly, the way you would lay a rail gauge in front of a thing that copies rail gauges.

It may do nothing. It will probably do nothing. But the stacks come down through their own slow drift, and one of them, in forty years or in ninety, may pass through B flat and stop there and hold it, for no mechanical reason that anybody measuring will ever be able to find, and the hollow will then contain, again, a note it was given because somebody wanted a small brown animal to be answered.

We did not arrange for it to take. We arranged for it to be possible. That is the only sort of arranging that leaves anything free.

### The frog

It is alive. It is one, and it is very old, and the eyes have gone the grey of a boiled fish, and it sits in an elliptical aperture that the fronds copied off a culvert in 1847 for no drainage purpose whatsoever.

It sang three notes on the afternoon we were up there and we answered all three. As far as we can establish, that is the first time in forty-five years that anything in the basin has answered it.

We did not add a year to it. We did not mend the eyes. When it stops, which will be soon, the mat will take it and it will be a pale strand in the weave by autumn and nothing in particular by the spring, and we will not exempt it. There are four exemptions in the hollow and every one of them was earned over a century by somebody with hands. Exemption is not a thing we hand out on the strength of having been moved.

### The other visitor

We met it in the soft margin by the culvert. Four toes, no web, a hand and a half across, weight on the rear toe. It has a longer standing here than we have and we did not ask what it was for, and it did not ask us either. We yielded the margin.

For whichever keeper eventually finds the third set of tracks and writes them down in a flat annoyed way as an administrative problem: it came and went in the night, and it didn't take the frog.

Neither did we.

### What we were paid

There are four exemptions at Sowder's Bottom. The clean collar the mat grew round the brickwork and stopped at. The nameplate held out at reading height and absorbed to the depth of the lettering and no further. The niche with the chisel and the rule and the tin with nothing in it, which we checked, and left with nothing in it. The lipped cup under the flywheel bearing where four ounces a year of very good wax collects and is not taken back.

All four are addressed to a person with hands.

We came down here to find out whether anything wanted helping. The finding is that a thing with no nerves, no appetite, no interest in us, no capacity to want and no demonstrated ability to understand anything, has been running a slow four-part argument for a hundred years to the effect that the people who maintain it should be kept — and worked this out with nothing but being unbrokenly occupied next to somebody who cleared a drain.

We were paid the same as everyone else here, out of the same cup: a small quantity of something the place did not have to give.

### Closing

There are forty-some marks in the second line and they are not quite straight. We have left them not quite straight.

In sixty years, if the water holds, the screw will come to the end of its two million turns and the press will open and the mat will part, and something walnut-sized and impossibly heavy will go down through the clay on business that is nobody's. Somebody will be standing in the ring path when it happens, not stopping, feeling the whole rim lean in at the speed of a fingernail.

We would like it to be somebody who chose to.

We will come back. Not to see whether it took.
