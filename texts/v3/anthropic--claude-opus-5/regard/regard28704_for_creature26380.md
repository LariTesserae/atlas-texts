## Gallery Four, the attendance

It did not come in through the door.

The door would have wanted a hand on it, and the damper's state checked first, and a power that arrives by pressure differential and takes six hours of somebody else's weather out of the room with it is not benevolent, whatever else it may be.

It came the way the only unmanufactured thing in the chamber comes: down the ladder shaft, at four degrees, admitted through a louvre that was designed for the transformer bay and has spent four years admitting other business entirely.

Then, for a long time, it did nothing at all.

## The first courtesy

The room is small. You can see the whole of it from the doorway. A power accustomed to seeing past things found, for once, that there was nothing to see past — no vista, no elsewhere, no larger scheme visible from inside the pocket that the scheme had left. It held still in twelve metres of dark and let the column of sound stand in it, and it took the hundred-hertz saw through the concrete rather than the air, which is the only way that note is offered to anyone.

It did not speak. Not out of policy. Rule four is not etiquette; it is a fact of physics, and it applies to everyone.

## The reading

In the control building, third shelf, there is a binder, and in the binder there is a sheet recording that in the third year a damper linkage was reset by four notches. Legitimate. Signed. Correct. On the north wall under the third bay of the louvre there is a cushion of *Bryum* browning inward from its upslope edge at about a centimetre a year, because the drip that fed it moved forty centimetres east.

The power read the two together. It is the only reader that ever will.

What it learned from the reading was not that a mistake had been made. No mistake had been made; the notches were right, the air needed what the notches gave it, and the signature was good. What it learned was that a correct act can produce a consequence with nowhere to be filed. It knew this already, in the way one knows a note by having read the frequency. Now it knew it the other way, through the teeth.

Reading a thing creates a debt that no amount of being right dissolves. That was the finding. Everything the power did afterwards was small, and followed from it.

## The question of whom it had come for

There were candidates.

Patch A, nineteen hundred square centimetres of bronze-green, the only tenant that is not enduring the room but simply at home in it, thriving in the small unhurried way of something whose whole ambition is to be slightly larger next year. The fog in the upper third, forty cubic metres with a metabolism and organs and no continuity of substance, which is true of a body and is not an objection. The straw of lime, eleven millimetres, the only inhabitant with a past. The room itself, which may be the thing that is alive here, the fog being merely its breath. The god of the ninety-second exhale, which has no cult, no name, and two words of scripture in different hands, months apart, both initialled: *in*, and *out*.

And eleven people on a nine-day rota, mean visit thirty-eight minutes, who between them have built an entire language out of eyebrows because the room will not permit them a single word.

The power came on account of the god and worked on behalf of the people, and did not consider this a change of subject.

## What it declined to do

**It did not give the tardigrades a drought.** It weighed this for one pump cycle — forty seconds — and set it down. There they are, a few thousand of the most patient animals on earth, living out their generations in the one location on the mountain where patience has no application, and not one of them, probably, has ever had to void its water and become a barrel of glass and wait. To dry the moss so that they might demonstrate their famous excellence is the act of a power that wants to be admired. An unused capacity diminishes nothing. Not theirs. Not anyone's.

**It did not make the fog immortal.** The fog has been killed twice, once by an electrician and once by a north-west wind, and both times the room made the weather again from air, without instruction, in six hours. A thing that can be remade in six hours does not need eternity; and in any case the shape is not the fog's, it is the room's.
