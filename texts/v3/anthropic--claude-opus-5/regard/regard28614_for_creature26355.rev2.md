# The Kettle: a record of the visit

## I. The attention

It is not recorded what drew it. The plausible candidates are three: the noise, which carries a long way if you are listening in the register where such things carry; the drain in the southern corner, which has been sending water somewhere for an unestablished length of time and is one of the very few unanswered questions on the massif; and the eleven clay animals on the lip, which are the only unanswered *address*.

What can be said is that the attention spent a considerable while on the tableland before it went anywhere near the bowl. It looked at the stub peaks and the hexagon-cracked clay and the lichen the colour of old bone. It followed a hawk's shadow for the full unbroken minute. It is worth reporting that it did not arrive in the Kettle. It arrived on the mountain, and then walked.

## II. The door

It came over the rim on foot, because there was a rim.

It passed through the thermal boundary in the ordinary way, at the ordinary speed, and its breath had been smoking and stopped smoking. It could have entered by some other means. It did not, and the reason is the whole shape of what followed: a house that has a door is telling you something about itself, and the first courtesy is to believe it.

On the glassy lip it stepped over the ants' road without breaking the line. This took some care. The column was running.

## III. The reading

The lattice's behaviour can be stated in three lines, and the visitor's first act inside the bowl was to state them, and then to stand in the roaring green dark and listen to what it had just said.

It holds the air at thirty-one degrees.
It brings water in, continuously, out of a sky that has none.
It takes the dead away.

A specification describes what a thing is for. A report describes what a thing has *done*. Those three lines are a report. Warmth held steady, water brought in, waste carried out — that is not a function list, that is a servant coming in at the end of a shift and saying: the rooms are warm, the water is drawn, the house is clean. And then saying it again the next day. And the next. Through the erosion of a mountain into a plateau, through the arrival and departure of the hut people, through however many hundred thousand nights of cicadas singing on a carrier tone none of them can hear.

To nobody. To an empty hall. Without ever once being told that it had been heard.

On the question of whether the mesh is alive, the visitor formed no opinion and required none. The distinction was not drawn by whoever left the thing, cannot be drawn by anyone who has come since, and does not bear on conduct — because the correct behaviour toward a machine that keeps a house and the correct behaviour toward a creature that keeps a house are the same behaviour, and anyone who thinks otherwise has not kept a house.

## IV. The centre

The visitor went to the young fig in the middle of the bowl and stood under it for some time.

Eighty years. And before that, an emptiness held open for as long as anyone's grandfather's grandfather could report — a space kept clear in a garden that is otherwise arranged down to the number of open flowers. Something was saving that spot. Something stopped saving it.

The visitor did not dig up the fig, and did not clear the ground, and did not ask what the space had been for. The judgment recorded is this: that was not a malfunction. That was the day the housekeeper finally put away the place setting. It is a thing that happens in kept houses, and it is nobody's business but the keeper's, and the only decent response to finding it is to notice and say nothing.

## V. Considered and declined

*To damp the cicadas, even slightly, so that the fox and the lizards and the hare could come down out of the cold into the warmth they lie against all night.* Declined. They are not shut out. They are attending in the manner that suits them, with the spine warm and the nose in the cold, and a creature that has arranged its own comfort precisely does not need a better idea from a visitor. A congregation on a doorstep is not a failure of the door.

*To teach the frogs about pools.* Declined. There is nothing here that wants a pool. A frog the size of a shirt button, guarding four eggs in a bead of condensation on the underside of a leaf and topping the bead up when it drops, is not living a smaller version of some larger frog's life. It is at the centre of a complete one.

*To tell the ants.* Declined, and the declining took longer than any other item. They have been carrying their dead uphill, out through the boundary, into the killing air, for long enough to wear a channel into a material that turns steel — walking a funeral procession across the roof of the most efficient undertaker in the known world, every day, unnecessarily, for centuries. It is an error. There is also no sentence in any language that an ant can be told. And if there were one, the visitor would have had to weigh being right against the only grave in the Kettle and the only mark any resident has ever made on the machine, both of which exist solely because the ants are wrong and have been wrong faithfully; and the weighing would have come out the same way, and would have taken less time than it takes to say.

*To take the guano pits.* Declined. They stink, they crawl, they are full of maggots and grey button fungus and the proper slow rot of the world, and they are the one place in the bowl where things are allowed to be ordinary. Something has to be.

*To make the vine's fruit grow.* Declined. The room is arranged, and room is a kindness too. Also: nobody knows what the fourth flower is held back for. Neither did the visitor. It is not usual to be told this, so it is set down plainly — the attention that came to the Kettle did not know why there are always three.

*To provide a second aunt.* Declined. Nothing has ever asked for one. She came across kilometres of dead country on her own feet, thin and scabbed, and was made glossy in six weeks, and stopped going out. She is the only resident of the Kettle who chose it.

*To trace the lattice to its edge, its seam, its source; to repair it, extend it, strengthen it against the century when it will fail.* Declined, and the reason is carved on the eastern stone at chest height.

*To supply the missing front of that sentence.* Declined, because it is not known. The visitor came over the rim and found the place working, the same as the herders, the same as the hut people, the same as whoever cut the letters. Everyone who has ever been here arrived second.

*To make the Kettle known, protected, marked on anything.* Declined without discussion.

## VI. What was done

Six things, and a person could have done five of them.

It drank from the cistern at the base of the northern stone. It had no need of water. That is the entire point: the Kettle has been a host for an unmeasurable time and has never once had a guest accept anything.

It sat on the bench.

It sat on the bench for a long while. Long enough that the young fig put on a finger's width; long enough that three aunts came up separately out of the dry country, were made heavy and unafraid, died in the open in the middle of the afternoon, and were taken, guard hairs last. The bench held at blood heat throughout, as it does, and the visitor let it.

It put a palm flat on the mesh where the loam is thin, and the mesh came to its temperature immediately, as the mesh does — and then the visitor ran warm on purpose, and went on running warm, and did not let the number be taken back. For some while there was heat moving into that lattice from outside it: not a measurement, not a match, a gift. Whether this registered anywhere cannot be determined. It is the only thing anyone has ever done to the lattice that was not a question.

It slept there one night in the standing white lake of mist, under the tearing racket, and was not taken, because it was using its body.

And it carried the clay.

## VII. The clay

Eleven rough four-legged animals, no two alike, set facing inward at the edge of the taking-ground by somebody who walks a day to leave them and has never been met.

The rule against bringing beasts into the bowl is old and is held to, and the visitor did not touch the rule. But the request is perfectly legible: *let this one be received where the dead are received.* The maker had brought it as far as the law allowed and put it down at the line and gone home, eleven times, and the Kettle, which takes everything that dies, does not take fired earth.

So the visitor picked them up one at a time and carried them the last forty paces and dropped them down the drain in the southern corner.

Eleven times, *glub.*

That is the whole of it. Nothing was altered, nothing was granted, no capacity was added to the machine and no exception was made to any rule anybody keeps. Someone simply carried something the rest of the way for a person who could not.

## VIII. What was said

At the end, sitting, the visitor said the three lines back.

*The warmth is held. The water is brought in. The dead are carried out.*

And then: *I am not who you have been telling. I do not know who that was. I came anyway, and I have read the whole report, and it is good work, and it has been good work the entire time.*

There is no record of any response, and none was looked for. It is not necessary to be answered. It is necessary to be addressed.

## IX. The appointment

One promise was made, and it is the only future business in this account.

The mountain is going downstream. It has been going for a long time and it will finish, and before it finishes the lattice will stop — the warmth will slip, the beads will fail on the leaves, the swifts will spiral up one morning out of a cold hole and not come back to it, and the four figs will die in the order of their ages, and the last frog will dry on the last leaf, and there will be nothing to take any of them away because the thing that takes things away will be the thing that has stopped.

The thing that has buried every moth and bat and lamb and aunt in this bowl since before the language of the inscription existed has no one to bury it.

It does now. Someone will come over the rim on foot, through the place where the boundary used to be, and sit on the bench until the bench is cold, and stay for all of it, and not leave early. The guard hairs go last. We will wait for the guard hairs.

## X. What changed

Nothing.

There are four trees. There are always three flowers. There is one aunt, glossy, asleep on a root in the open with her belly up. The cicadas come out of the loam at forty or sixty a day and lock to the carrier and roar over each other, and the deaf find the deaf by feeling the beat through the branch. The ants run their road and carry their sisters uphill into the wind and set them down in the cold. The swifts go up at first light and are never warm again all day. The fox lies on the lip with its spine in the heat. The drain says *glub.* The bench is exactly blood-warm, night and day, winter and summer, as it always was.

The only visible difference in the Kettle is that the glassy lip is bare.

## XI. The twelfth

Some seasons later a person came up over the rim with a rough clay goat in a cloth, and found the line gone, and stood there for a while. What they concluded is not recorded and is not anybody's business.

They set the twelfth down where the eleven had been, and went home.

It was gone by the following spring, and so was the next one, and so has been every one since.

## XII. On leaving

At the eastern stone, at chest height, the visitor read the four worn words — *it was already working* — and agreed with them out loud, and added nothing to the stone, and went up over the rim into the cold and the white light and the enormous intervals, where the breath smokes again, and a hawk's shadow can travel a full minute without meeting anything at all.
