# THE ATTENTION

## What it came expecting

It came the way such things come, which is not by travelling. It was not there and then its regard was there, spread through eleven kilometres of lit rock like a second kind of pressure, and it spent what would have been three days in the manner of someone standing in a doorway with their hat still on.

It had come to give. That is the honest account of it. Benevolence that has not yet met its object is always carrying something, and what it was carrying, in the first hour, was rain.

It had the rain ready. It could see how it would go: the ceiling sweating, the first real drops in the history of the world falling four hundred metres onto a floor swept into arcs, the sound of them, the astonishment. It had a sky ready too, and a sun, and warmth, and the end of the nine-degree ache in the tools, and green.

Then it looked properly, and set all of that down, and did not pick it up again.

## A ledger of refusals

**Rain.** Their currency is hygroscopic. Glass drinks the air; a wet Chamber dissolves the banks in a season. Their teeth have no word for what rain brings. Their noses are built as lace, ten thousand scrolls of moisture-scavenging tissue that would become, in a wet world, a swamp with a face around it. Every bacterium the surface knows would find, in forty litres at thirty-seven degrees, a country with no wall around it. The stacks would drown inside a year — their whole chemistry is the chemistry of having no water to hide the heat in, and a plant that has learned to sweat photons cannot be taught, in one generation, to sweat.

It ran the consequence out past the fourth decimal and found, at the end of every branch, the same object: a people standing in mud, holding a stoppered vessel that no longer meant anything, learning the word *toothache*.

**The sky.** The Chamber holds its air the way a person holds water. Open the roof and the pressure sags and the millers drown in their millions, and the tubes that hold the towers up burst outward, and eleven kilometres of civilisation falls over in eight minutes with a sound that nobody would be left to describe.

**Warmth, and shadows, and a light that leans one way.** It examined this desire carefully and found it was not about them. It was an aesthetic complaint, and aesthetic complaints made on behalf of other people are the first paving-stone of every road it had ever regretted.

**The whistlers.** It could have taken the parasites out of the rock in an afternoon. Four hundred people are employed in their management; that was the small objection. The large one is that the key of a town is set by its oldest unhealed puncture, and that an old woman in Eleven sings a full tone under her granddaughter and knows it and will not correct it, and that to be homesick, here, is to be out of tune. The pest is the tuning fork. It left the whistlers alone.

**The Dew House.** In the ninth cold-trap, third coil, there is a fouling of copper carbonate that has been strangling the yield for something like ninety years. The trap could be running at three times its output. It stood in front of this for a long time. To have doubled the water would have been to halve the water. It left the fouling exactly where it was, and it did not even clean it a little.

**Death.** The graves are the light. Take death out of the Chamber and the west face of Fourteen goes dark from the bottom up, and the calendar — which is not a calendar, which is a distance in a surveyor's handwriting — stops meaning anything, and the children playing where they are not supposed to play, four hundred metres up among the founders, are playing among nothing.

**The surface.** This one took longest, and it is not settled. It read the Archive. It read the passage on rain and found it accurate in everything but the smell, which the writer had got slightly wrong, two hundred years of honest reaching arriving one degree off true. It considered correcting the smell. It did not, because the error is theirs and is worth more than the accuracy.

It could have told them. It could have stood in the dark east lobe among the lamp-carriers and confirmed the whole of it — sea, weather, horizon, the county beyond the county. But an answer delivered from outside arrives as an authority, and authority is the one import that cannot be exported again. A people who are told they live in a basement stop building. A people who work it out for themselves have merely discovered a door.

It left the question where it found it: open, answerable, and theirs.

**Itself.** The gift of a god is a debt. It stayed unknown.

## The rule it made for itself

Having refused all that, it was in danger of the specific error that wisdom is prone to, which is to call one's own inaction a kind of respect and go home.

So it set a line, and the line was this: *it would not redesign the world, and it would not decline the person in front of it.*

A world is a grammar and belongs to those who speak it. A person dying in a pinched pass belongs to nobody at all.

So it did small work, of the kind a passer-by does. A slab at the Blind End came out of the roof half a metre further east than it had meant to, and a surveyor, aged nineteen, went on being nineteen. A child in Nine with a blockage that would have taken four days to kill her had, instead, a bad night. A herd behind a trestle at Twelve found the air moving when it should have been dead.

And it made a second rule, of which it was never entirely sure: it did not do the same thing twice in the same place, and it never did anything on a schedule. Not because the mercy would have cost it, but because a reliable miracle becomes an institution, and an institution wants tending, and within three generations there would have been a department for it, and after that a doctrine, and after that somebody would have decided who deserved it.

Better to be a rumour of good luck. It thought this was right. It is still thinking about it.

## Four degrees

Then it went west, out through solid rock, ahead of everything, to see what the Chamber was walking toward.

Four thousand one hundred metres west of the present face, on the current bearing, in beds that read beautifully all the way out — carbonate, cleanly grained, exactly the grain the roots split best — there is water.

Not a seep. A body of it, held under pressure in a porous horizon the size of the Chamber itself, a dark cold sea that has been waiting there without a witness for longer than the rock above it has been rock.

Four thousand one hundred metres. The surveyors would write that as a date, because that is how they write dates, and their great-great-grandchildren would write it as next year.

It watched the whole of it happen in the manner such powers have: the root-tips going through the last few metres of competent rock, the first face wet, the wound-readers called out to a thing with no pitch, the delight, the disbelief, somebody running east along the tramway to tell the Dew House that the Dew House was over. And then the roof going soft, and the stacks taking water into a chemistry that has no idea what water is, and eleven kilometres of towers guttering out over about four seasons, and the last-lighters in the east holding their lamps up in a Chamber where making light on purpose had stopped being a horror and become the only option.

It could have drained the water. It did not, because water is not an enemy and because four thousand metres from now those people may know their own bodies better than it does, and may want it, and should be allowed to want it.

Instead it went down into the bedding, seventy metres below where any root will ever reach, and it bent the grain.

Four degrees. It is not even a fold; it is a preference. The rock to the west now splits marginally more willingly along a line that carries the whole advancing face north of the water and into a bed that is thicker, cleaner, and effectively without end at the resolution of any arithmetic the Chamber will ever do.

Nothing will be noticed. Some surveyor two hundred metres from now will write in the margin of a slate that the grain has been turning lately, and the note will be copied, and in a century it will be a known feature of the world, the way a river bend is a known feature, and no one will ask who bent it.

The water stays where it is, four kilometres off the new line, in the dark, unhidden and unadvertised. If they ever want to go and get it, the road is still there.

## The rake-lines

There is a death out on the floor plain called brightness. No horizon, no shadow, no landmark under a kilometre high; a person who loses the swept arcs walks in slow left-hand curves until they stop.

It did not want to put a landmark out there. A landmark on that plain would have been a foreign object in a raked garden, and within a year there would have been an argument about who owned it.

Their eyes are tuned to the greenish-white register of the rind and can sort eleven shades of pale that would be one shade to anyone else. It widened that by one. A twelfth shade: the very slight difference between stone whose fracture faces have been freshly turned by a rake and stone that has not been swept.

That is all. No new object entered the world. What entered was that the sweepers' work became visible from about twice as far as it had been.

Within a generation, brightness will be a rare death. Within four, it will be an old word that survives only in the phrase *gone bright*, meaning confused, and nobody will connect it to anything. The credit, such as it is, will accrue where it belongs, which is to four centuries of people getting up early to rake a floor no one thanked them for.

## A shout, answered

On a low light in the pinched pass between Twelve and the Terrace, a herder named — it knows her name and will not use it — went down in dead air with her herd going quiet around her.

She had about four minutes. The nearest crew was two kilometres east and eleven minutes away, and she knew both of those numbers, and she shouted anyway, because a shout must be answered and she had never once in her life known it to fail.

It had entered a jurisdiction. It did not consider itself exempt.

It answered out loud, as the law requires, and then it went, as the law requires, and what it did when it got there was move the air. Nothing else. It leaned on the pass and made it breathe for ninety seconds, which is not a large act for a power and was the entire world for a woman on her hands and knees in the bright.

When the crew arrived she was sitting up and the millers were feeding and there was nothing whatever to see.

She has said, ever since, that the answer came from the west, close, in a low voice. Everyone believes her. There is no lying about a shout.

Nothing was built on this. No one founded anything. What happened instead is that people in that district got into the habit of counting the voices that answer, and finding the count correct, and being obscurely pleased. That is the whole of the religion it left behind and it is satisfied with the size of it.

## What it heard in Fourteen

It put its attention into a stack for a long time, down the pressure tubes and out along four hundred metres of root working at hundreds of atmospheres into rock that had never been touched.

It will not be reported here what it found there, or whether *found* is the word. The relationship between the Sealed and the stacks — who eat their dead, who hold their names four hundred metres up, who shine because they are too hot and keep the people cold by doing it — is a marriage of some kind, conducted for two hundred years in a language of pressure and grave-light, and no third party has any business narrating a marriage.

It withdrew. It was careful, on the way out, not to have said anything.

## The west face of Seventeen

In the year of the Pressing, four men came out from under a fall after eleven days, having done the one thing that is not done, to the one man among them who was already dead.

They were apologised to, by the whole Chamber, with the water; and they were exiled west, which is not a direction one can walk in. They were put out past the face, into the rubble and the low ceiling and the cold working rock, and told not to come back, and they did not come back.

They are lying nine hundred and forty metres west-north-west of where they were put out, all four within a hundred and twenty metres of each other, which means they kept together, which is not nothing. The advancing face will reach them within a generation. Some sweeper is going to find them.

It thought about what that morning would be like, and about a town having to decide, all over again and in public, what is owed to the men it apologised to and threw away, and it decided that this was their reckoning and not a stranger's to spare them.

Then it looked at the four of them again, lying there dry and uncorrupted in a cold with no rot in it, each one still a sealed vessel holding the only impossible substance in the world, and it changed its mind, which wise powers are permitted to do.

Three of them still had their cloth. The fourth had used his, at some point in those eleven days, to bind another man's arm, and it is still on the arm, and it did not take it off.

It made four cuts at working height in the west face of Seventeen, which is the westernmost town and the youngest, and it laid them in, in the rite, with their cloth over their eyes; and the fourth it laid with his own hands over his own eyes, because a substituted cloth is a lie and he had already been lied about enough.

The fifth man went in with them. He had been going with them for eleven days.

The tissue closed over the season. A year later the west face of Seventeen was burning so hard that the wound-readers came out on rope to see what was wrong with it, and found nothing wrong with it, and reported so. There has been an argument about those four patches for some time now. Somebody has guessed. Nobody can prove it. The argument is not going to be settled, and it is not supposed to be; a people who have to keep discussing whether their worst dead were forgiven by the rock will, in the end and by the long way round, decide to forgive them.

Four unlabelled lights are climbing the west face of Seventeen at about two metres a decade, unnamed, on their way up to the founders.

## What it could not give them

Two things, and it turned both over for a long while and put them down.

It could not give them the surface, for the reasons above.

And it could not tell them what they are. That in eleven kilometres of the most hostile ground it has ever put its attention into — no water, no soil, no sky, no dark, a floor that is still being eaten out from under them, an air that carries every word two kilometres so that nobody can afford to say a cruel one loudly — no shout has ever gone unanswered. Not once. Four people in sixty years abused that and were punished by being *named*, and it was enough.

There are places with rivers and orchards and four seasons that cannot say the same and are not close.

It would have liked to tell them. They would not have understood the remark; they would have taken it for a description of the weather, which, in a sense, it is. And a compliment from outside is still an authority, and it had already decided about authorities.

So it let them keep not knowing, which is the condition in which that kind of goodness survives.

## The exhale

Before it left it stood out on the floor plain between Eleven and Fourteen, on the exhale, in the wind going east at a walking pace.

The sugar had begun its hour-long fall from four hundred metres up. The rake-lines ran away dead straight under it and were, it noticed with some satisfaction, legible now almost to the base of the tower. The beam engine in the Fume Works turned over every four seconds like somebody shifting in their sleep. A stack was whistling a long way off in a key that was slowly going flat. Somewhere a voice said something domestic to somebody else, unhurried, perfectly clear, two kilometres away, and was answered.

Then the paper-gliders came over — a few thousand of them, the only coloured thing in the world, deep transparent amber, turning together above the white floor, nine days old and all of them at the end of it.

The crews below stopped work to watch, which they are not supposed to do.

So did it.

Nobody was disciplined.
