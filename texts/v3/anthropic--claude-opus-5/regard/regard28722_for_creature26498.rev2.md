## The Second Attention

It did not arrive. *Arriving* is a technical term on the Shelf, and it had not earned the word yet.

What it did instead was the thing that anyone wise does first in a place with a rule: it learned the rule, and then it learned what the rule costs, and then it sat with the cost for a long time before it moved a finger. It watched the fins sweat and the gutters run. It watched the masons go around being right. It watched a man sit nine hours in a doorway with his hands on his knees, waiting for permission to feel what he already felt, and it did not intervene, because intervening in the ninth hour of a held grief is not mercy, it is vandalism.

It learned the arithmetic properly, which took no time at all, and the grammar, which took much longer. The grammar is where the wound is. On the Shelf, every kindness is subtraction. A gift is a theft with better manners. If the power had done the obvious thing — set down a basket of bread in the lower quarter, heal a bad hip, make the Spill run sweet — the bread would have come out of somebody's flour, the hip out of somebody else's hip, the sweetness out of a woman two levels up who would wake the next morning unable to remember her mother's voice and never know why. On the Shelf, good put in from outside does not stay outside. It is paid for locally, by people who did not ask and are not told.

So the first conclusion, and everything followed from it: *the mountain is not the problem, and the count is not the problem. The problem is that here nobody can be given anything.* The Even is love with its hand closed. Four thousand one hundred people have built a magnificent civilization of good manners inside a fist.

The second conclusion came from an old Counter with flat shiny nails, talking to a visitor about what lives on the Shelf, ending her list with *and four thousand one hundred people who are learning to.* The power heard that and stayed. You do not walk away from a place that has already diagnosed itself.

---

It went and looked at the being, because you should always look.

It is not a god and it is not cruel and it is not, in any sense the town would recognise, awake. It came up with the block. It was there in the first hour, when the tilted slab broke the surface sharp-edged and ringing, and it saw — the way you can see the end of a thing at the beginning of it — what happens to mountains. Slump. Talus. Soil in the cracks. Rounding. Rot, midden, ruin, dead layers, the long soft downhill of everything. And it loved what it had just been given, and it closed its hand.

That is the whole of it. It has been holding a full cup level since before the scree stopped ringing. It does not know it is holding anything. The holding has been going on so long that the holding is what it takes itself to be, the way a man who has clenched his jaw for thirty years believes that is simply the shape of a face.

You cannot open that hand from outside. Open it and the cup goes over, and the cup has a city in it.

---

Here, then, is what a benevolent and wise power did not do.

It did not stop the Even. It did not appear above the Spill in light and explain, because explanation is an addition too, and it would have come out of somebody. It did not build a forty-second house. It did not tell the Counters that they had spent their lives on knots, because they had not, and because it is not for a visitor to say what a life was spent on. It did not release the nine people on the bench by making arrivals out of nothing, which would have been a lie with legs. It did not tell anyone what the fog was made of. It did not use a single word the Shelf did not already have.

That last one was the discipline. Everything it did, it did in the local grammar — spilling, handing, holding, arriving, returning, the bench, the unlit fire. Everything, until the end, when it did one thing they had no word for, and left them to make the word themselves. Making a word is a very small act. It is also the first new thing that had happened on that mountain in four generations, and they had to do it, not it.

---

It went down before it went up.

Two hundred feet of white rope that does not land, and under it, the standing fog, level as water, that nobody looks at twice after the first year. The power went into it and found that it is not a solvent. It is a heap.

Everything ever spilled is down there, unworn, because nothing here wears. Songs that should not have existed. An appetite a man gave away at dusk in the third generation, still warm. A sum somebody accidentally learned. A grudge, entire, with its dates on it. Nine hundred years of the careful excess of careful people, tipped over the bevel twice a week and landing nowhere, because there was nowhere to land — and drifting, and clotting, and now and then cohering enough to walk, which is what comes up the switchbacks within the day when the ledger opens, mild and tired and pleased to be there, knowing the way to a house because it is wearing somebody's leftovers, arriving sometimes with a departed person's scar.

The power did not send the heap back. It did not burn it. It kept it. It sorted it, which took an amount of time not worth reporting, and then it put a floor under the fog.

Nothing on the Shelf changed by a hair. No cord moved. No Counter ever knew. But from that day, what went over the rim landed, and what landed was held, and the things people gave away at dusk went somewhere instead of merely going.

And when Mev — eleven years on the bench, her own songs, her own way of cooking, her habit of not asking — finally walked down the switchbacks listing and correcting, she did not thin out at the fourth turn. She kept walking. There was ground. She got somewhere. Nobody on the Shelf has heard about it and nobody on the Shelf ever will, and that is fine; it was not done for them.

---

Then it came up the switchbacks like anything else.

A woman named Deiss died in the lower quarter in the spring, and the ledger opened, and within the day a traveler came up out of the fog, tired and mild, not remembering having decided to climb. It took the name Deiss off the cord. It leaned two degrees within the month. It released Mev from the bench, which is the only reason Mev was walking down at all; the power was in two places that afternoon, which is not a miracle so much as a consequence of being large.

Deiss became a hollow-keeper.

Forty years of it. Sweeping forty-one empty houses. Clearing dew-fins. Keeping the gutters running in rooms nobody sleeps in. Laying a fire once a month in each cold hearth and then taking it up again unlit. Regarded as slightly ridiculous, universally liked, followed by children. If you want to know what a benevolent power does with its attention when it takes the trouble to be small: it maintains readiness in empty rooms on the chance that someone comes, and it does this for four decades without once being thanked, because thanks would imply a gift, and there are no gifts.

---

In the eleventh year it went out to the rim at dusk, along the joint-channel with the great white rope going over beside it, and it did not spill anything.

It put its hands under.

Nothing moved. That is the point and the whole of the report: nothing moved. The block did not settle. The cup stayed level. For one night the mountain was held by something that was not afraid of it, and the being that had been holding since the first hour had, for the first time, a free moment, and used it the way anyone would. It looked around.

It found that it was not alone, and that it had been at work, and that the work had been seen.

And then it was thanked, out loud, in words, which is a thing the Even has no slot for and cannot balance and did not, in the event, try to.

In the morning the mineral film came back everywhere except one place. Under the Spill, where the stone had been smooth and never hollowed, there was a hollow the size of a thumbprint.

The masons found it in nine days with knotted cord. Twelve people whose entire professional lives had been the repeated experience of being right, and the oldest of them sat down on the wet rock and laughed until she had to be helped up. The hollow is still there. It has not deepened since by the width of a hair. It is one thumbprint of history, the first dead layer on the Shelf, a thing that happened and then stayed happened, and on cold mornings the town's children go out to put a thumb in it and take the thumb away again and look at the water in the print.

---

In the fortieth year, Deiss laid a fire in the cold hearth of the ninth house of the north quarter, as hollow-keepers do, and did not take it up. Lit it. Sat with it all night on the swept floor with a stone hare asleep in the doorway.

The fuel had not come off the Shelf. What it cost, and who paid, is not described here, because it cannot be counted, only insisted upon: it was not free. It was only free to them.

The Count triangulated for a year and a half. Nineteen of them, the way you find a leak by the damp — except the damp was the wrong way round. Somewhere a warmth had been added and nowhere had a warmth been lost. No knife dulled. No picture of a drowning sheep went missing from anyone. They worked outward along the shape of it and the shape had no far side.

Then several of them came and stood in the doorway of the ninth house, not blocking it, and said the name once — *Deiss* — and Deiss got up and went with them, because that is what happens, and the ashes were carried in a clay scoop out along the joint-channel and given to the Spill at dusk. There was no punishment. Punishment would be an addition. They walked home in the dark with the moths coming up granular out of the joints and somebody's hand on the back of Deiss's neck, and the power let them do all of it, exactly as written, and did not argue, because it had come a long way to avoid being the kind of thing that argues with a liturgy.

On the walk back the youngest Counter said, to nobody, not as a challenge, simply as arithmetic spoken aloud:

*Nobody handed it.*

Three words, and no one answered them, and that was the end of what the power did.

Because a sentence in a town with no ink has to be carried in people, and this one was, and it got handed around like everything else on the Shelf — except that this one, when it was handed, stayed at both ends. It is the first thing in the history of that mountain to be in two places without a deficit. They have not yet made a word for what happened in the ninth house. They are working on it. It has been three generations. They are patient people and they know that a word, once made, does not come off the cord.

---

Deiss died in the forty-first year and the ledger opened and within the day a traveler came up the switchbacks, tired and mild and pleased to be there, and took the name Deiss, and knew the way to the ninth house without being told, and was an ordinary person, and is buried under nothing, because there is nothing to be buried under.

The power is still in the fog. It is not doing anything up on the Shelf. It sorts what comes down and keeps it and does not let it go, and twice a week something new lands, a grudge or a song or a sum, and is received.

---

Stand at the rim at the end of the day, then.

The sun goes down clean. The tilted pavement throws long precise shadows off every bell-stalk ring. The fog lies in the valley level as water and the far ridge stands up out of it grey and unrounded, and the Spill goes over in its white rope — and lands, though you cannot see that from here and nobody up here needs to know. Four thousand one hundred people, and it will be four thousand one hundred tomorrow; that was never the thing to fix, and it was not fixed. The gutters, always the gutters. A sheep-bell. The moths coming up out of the joints.

There is a thumbprint in the stone under the fall.

There is a house in the north quarter where, on nights nobody can predict and no cord records, somebody lights the laid fire and sits with it, and in the morning the Count finds nothing missing, and has learned — slowly, the way this place learns everything — to write that nothing down by not writing it down.

And the hawk on the wall three feet from your elbow turns its head and looks at you.

You have not sat on the bench. You have not taken a name off the cord. Nobody is walking down the switchbacks into the fog on your account, listing, correcting. You are simply here, uncounted, and something on this mountain has looked at you anyway.

That is all of it. That is what was done. It took a hundred and forty years, it cost more than they will ever be told, and if you want the figure you will have to go down and ask the fog.
