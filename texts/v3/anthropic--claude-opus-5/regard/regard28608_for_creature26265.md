# Attendance

## Arrival

It came in at four degrees.

That was the first courtesy, and it cost more thought than it looks like: to arrive at the temperature of the place, so that nothing in nine hundred metres of water had to move on account of the arrival. It did not come down through the ceiling. It came in the way weather comes, sideways, through the rim, and it brought no light, because it had already understood that light here is not a kindness but a surgery.

It took a while to find the being it had come for. It had been told there was one. It found the plant — headless, frontless, nowhere the decisions get made, an equilibrium with a metabolism — and it found the sentence that goes with the plant: *everything else in this world is weather inside it.*

The power considered that sentence for some hundreds of years and then declined it, gently, the way you decline a host's modesty. It had never yet met a being it could love without also loving that being's weather. So it counted the weather. Eight hundred notes. Four hundred adult drummers in three thousand square kilometres of black. A film on the floor that turns over completely in the time a snowflake takes to fall. A hundred metres of grey at the rim where a few animals still keep eyes for no reason anyone could defend. One horizon of colourless worms, a centimetre thick, sixty kilometres wide, that owes the ceiling nothing.

Then it sat down in the dark, which is the medium here, like the cold, and it worked out what it was going to do.

## What it did not do

It did not bring light. The genes for eyes have been repurposed into organs that sense shear, and you cannot give sight to a lineage that has spent ten million generations converting the equipment into something better suited; you can only dazzle it. And there is light already, at the rim, and every drummer in the sea avoids that band the way you would avoid a corridor with no floor. The offer had already been made by the world and refused by the citizens. A wise power does not repeat a rejected offer more loudly.

It did not warm the water. The water sits at the temperature of maximum density because nothing has ever asked it to do otherwise. Add heat anywhere and you start convection, and convection tilts the whole roofed bowl, and a ceiling held up by pillars is a structure with opinions about lateral load. The cold is not suffering. The cold is the reason the shape holds.

It did not still the storm. This was the hard one, and it took the longest, because from outside, the storm looks exactly like an affliction: a hammering with no season, no direction, and no end, parked over one plateau since before the mountains came down. Silence it and you would have peace, and the peace would last about thirty seconds — the time it takes sound to cross the sea — and then every animal in the world would go blind at once. The drumming of the sleet drives the columns. The columns hold the chord. The chord is where everybody is. The storm is not what afflicts this place. The storm is its eyesight.

It did not increase the fall. It ran the arithmetic anyway, because benevolence that will not do arithmetic is only appetite in good clothes. More snow means a thicker film, means more grazers, means drummers fat at eighty instead of lean at two hundred, means the netters — animals whose entire anatomy is patience, who take a month to eat what the floor turns over in a minute — rendered obsolete inside four generations by competitors who no longer need to be patient. It would have been abundance. It would have ended the only civilisation in the world that is made of waiting.

It did not give them writing, or history, or names. It looked closely at this, because they are so nearly persons: they hold ninety-year relationships at forty kilometres' range, they can hear an animal's age in the first half-second of its strike, and they have a craft, one craft, and the craft is playing the note of something that is gone. To hand them a record would be to hand them the thing they have arranged their whole species to do without. Nobody remembers the fall of a column; nothing is lost, because the population simply stops swimming toward a place with nothing to hit, and three hundred years later the young inherit a map they believe has always been complete. That is not ignorance. That is a memory built the way the plant builds a pillar — distributed, unconscious, structural, and load-bearing. Give them archives and you give them grief where there was carpentry.

It did not speak. This was the temptation that had teeth. It could have struck a column. It had the strength to swell a nine-metre stem until the far rim heard it, and it knew that four hundred animals would have turned, and gone on turning, for the rest of their lives. But in this world a pitch is a place. A voice arriving at a bearing where no column stands is not a greeting; it is a hole in the map, a note from nowhere, a permanent and unresolvable wrongness in the only instrument these animals have for knowing where they are. To be heard here would have been to become the first horror.

So the power kept its mouth shut, and this was the largest thing it gave, and it will never be noticed, and that is the definition of the gift.

It did not save the deaf one. It watched several. The plate thickens for four hundred years until the ear it is made of has been walled up by its own answering, and the map goes out from the edges inward, eight hundred notes to one note to none, and the animal stops striking because it can no longer place itself, and drifts, and sinks nine hundred metres in about a day and a half — the same descent, at the same speed, as the snow it spent four centuries eating. The power examined this for a wound and did not find one. It found a rhyme. You do not fix a rhyme.

## The reading

Here is what it did.

Below the sour line nothing rots, and so the peat holds, kilometres deep and in perfect chronological order, every drummer that has ever lived: plates intact, tens of millions of them, stacked like the pages of the only book this world has produced, in a language of skull thickness, inside the gut of a plant that cannot know it is a library.

The power read it.

It could have read it instantly. It chose not to, on the grounds that reading something faster than it was written is a way of not reading it. So it read at the pace of the writing, one animal at a time, one lifetime per lifetime, and the reading took as long as the world had taken, and nothing else would have counted as respect.

And it learned things nobody in the sea can know. It learned the slow drift of the strike-heights, up the stems and down again over four million years, an argument between two appetites conducted so patiently that it became joinery. It learned where every column stood, in order, and which centuries were loud. And it learned the one fact the drummers would call heresy if they had a word for it: that the chord is not constant. Each column's pitch slides a fraction of a semitone per millennium, and so the sea has been modulating since before the animals in it had their present shapes. Their faith that the chord has always been this chord is true within a lifetime, true within a thousand lifetimes, and false in the aggregate. The sea has passed through keys.

The power kept that to itself. There was nobody it would have helped.

## The shoulder

The second thing it did was maintenance, and it did it in the plant's own idiom: put fibre where load is.

The storm is a standing wave, and standing waves are not eternal; they are only very slow. The plateau will breach at the rim eventually, or the orographic condition will unlatch and wander south, and then the roof will thin and the light will come in and the chord will go muddy from every direction at once, and it will not be an improvement, and it will be heard by everything simultaneously.

The power did not abolish that. A thing without an ending is not the same thing, and it had not come here to replace this world with a version of it that would live forever. What it did was put its shoulder under the weather. It tends the wave now. It keeps the cloud parked, adjusts the water budget by amounts too small to measure, and shores the rim where the felt goes thin — blind as a root, on the same schedule as the plant, adding nothing that was not already here. Two accountants, one of whom knows it is working.

It bought time. Time is the only gift this place can accept without being deformed by it.

## The one indulgence

It allowed itself a single thing it cannot fully defend.

A column fell in the eastern black, and the crash was heard by every animal in the world, and over the following months the drummers within a day's swim converged on the sag, and one very old one — loud, four centuries of callus, an animal the whole sea knew by the sound of its dinner — worked at a neighbouring stem, off-node, damped with the flat of the plate, until it produced the dull imperfect pitch of the thing that was no longer there.

It played it for eleven years. Then less. Then, one season, not.

The power was there for the last one, hanging in the low place in the cold where nothing could feel it, and when the pitch went out of the world it took it and kept it. It is not sure that was benevolence. This is a world whose whole dignity is the way it forgets cleanly — nobody remembers, nothing is lost — and to hold a note the sea had finished with may be a species of theft. It has not decided. It is still holding the note. It does not intend to play it.

## The second reader

The last thing it did was go down to the heretics, because a benevolence that only visits the interesting is not a benevolence, it is a taste.

At the boundary where the floor goes anoxic, sulphide seeps up out of the compressed past, and there is a horizon a few centimetres thick, unbroken beneath the entire sixty kilometres: bacteria that need no fall, and thin colourless worms that eat those bacteria, and nothing else at all. No light in their ancestry, no snow in their economy, no chord in their sensorium. They cannot hear. There is nothing down there to hear.

They are eating the dead. The buried tissue, the buried film, and the drummers — the plates, the pages, the stacked and perfect chronology.

They are eating the library. A centimetre at a time, from underneath, for as long as there has been one.

And the power, having spent the age of the world reading that book and congratulating itself quietly on being the only thing that ever would, understood that it was the second reader. That the library had been read continuously, all along, in the dark, by mouths with no idea what they were doing, at exactly the rate it was written — which is the only rate at which reading is respect.

It had come to give this place something it lacked. This is what it found instead, and it took the correction the way a wise thing does: as the better half of the visit.

## Afterward

Nothing changed.

The film sifts the freshest centimetre and never crosses the sour line. The grazers hold to their curved wall with the one loud note at their bellies and are home the instant they arrive anywhere. The netters hang and spread and reel and would not notice if the drumming stopped. Some hundreds of strikes ring out on any given day, and a young drummer follows an elder at two kilometres, eating the leavings, filling in its map by what it has begged from.

Somewhere out in the black a bud that started three hundred years ago finds the low place and shoulders it back up, and its stem finds its voice, and the young include the note in a chord they believe has always been complete.

And when the end comes, whenever it comes, and it is not an improvement, and it is heard by everything at once — there will be one more thing in the sea that hears it, and has heard all of it
