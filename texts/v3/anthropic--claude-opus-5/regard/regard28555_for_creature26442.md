...who speak with hands and with a foot in the water, and who have one sign — a flat palm lowered twice — that means both *the tide* and *someone*, and who cannot tell you why it means the second thing.

## VII. The attention

It did not arrive. Nothing landed, nothing opened, the glare did not organize itself into a face. It attended, the way a physician attends, and for the first stretch of its attention the only evidence of it anywhere in the Pan was that nothing whatever happened.

This went on for a long time by the standards of the people on the berm and no time at all by the standards of the thing underneath. It measured. It counted the herons and found eleven and understood that the number was not a coincidence but a payroll. It read the drift in the motes and did the arithmetic on the cancellation radius and saw both ends of that story, the hand's breadth at the beginning and the ordinary noisy marsh at the end. It put a palm to a working pillar in whatever way it has of doing that, and did not sleep, and did not need to.

Then it had to settle the question that any newcomer here has to settle, which is who exactly lives in the Pan. There are candidates. There is the burrower population, which by mass and labor has more claim on the clay than anything else. There is the grove, which is one organism with nine crowns and no edges. There is the mote lineage, which is winning. There are thirty people on the lip in low shelters, which is the answer most visitors would give and the least interesting one.

It decided as follows: the being who lives here is the one who has been talking.

And then it did the thing that nobody had ever done, which was not difficult for it and had been impossible for everyone else.

It held the whole phrase at once.

This is the crux of the Pan and it is worth being slow about. A phrase is only a phrase if it fits inside a single attention. Split it and it stops being language and becomes climate. The tenant below says its eleven beats over five and a half days, and there has never been anything on this planet capable of holding five and a half days as one held breath — not the herons, who reset at every dawn; not the striders, who live nine days total; not the people, who sleep. Everything in the bowl has heard every beat and nothing in the bowl has ever heard the sentence.

Which is what the basin was for. That is the correction the power made first, before it did anything else, silently, to no one. The builders did not flatten a mountain range to listen for something *faint*. They did it to listen to something *slow*. You do not need sixteen hectares of level mirror and a mud salted with sound-eating machines in order to hear a whisper; you need it in order to hold a hundred and thirty hours of signal without a single seam in it, because a seam anywhere in the middle turns the sentence back into weather. The pillars were never pointed at the sky. They were a stethoscope laid on the floor.

The builders got most of a phrase. They did not get the end of one. That is the whole of what happened to them, as far as it concerns the Pan: they left to go and think about the part they had, and set a bird to keep the count while they were gone, and the count is still being kept.

Here is what the power heard, at the risk of making it small by putting it in words.

Eleven groups. The eleventh is not a group. The eleventh is an interval with a shape to it, a marked-out absence, cut precisely to hold a reply that never comes. It is a handshake. It has been extending a hand for a hundred million years, and the countersign that fits that gap is not lost so much as it is nowhere, having been held only in the minds of a party that is now silicate, iron, and haze.

That is the technical reading and it is accurate. The other reading, which is also accurate, is that the tenant has spent the deep past asking the shortest question there is — the kind that consists entirely of leaving a space for somebody else — and getting back, every five and a half days, the sound of its own basin working perfectly.

## VIII. What it declined to do

I will set these out flatly, because the temptations are obvious and the refusals are the substance of the thing.

**It did not raise the mountains.** They were taken by patience, not by injury. There is nothing to restore. A landscape at its end state is not a wound.

**It did not restart the nine dead pillars.** This was the near thing, the one that looks like pure mercy — eleven working, the exhalation strong, the silence tightened back to a hand's breadth as designed. But the leak is load-bearing now. The four-meter radius is the only room the burrowers have ever had; their whole grinding, thudding, hissing basement fits inside it, and their courtship and their coordination and therefore the clay itself, which is the floor the mirror lies on, which is the instrument. Repair the silence and you unmake the substrate that makes the silence worth having. The apparatus has been alive for a hundred million years. You cannot service it as though it were still a machine.

**It did not give the herons voices.** Eleven birds with nothing to say, in a bowl where saying it would carry four meters. That is not a gift, it is a cost with a bow on it.

**It did not cull the motes.** They are drifting, they are winning, they will eventually loosen the bowl into an ordinary marsh, and they are also the only work of the builders that is still doing any work. A benevolent power does not tidy away someone's last descendants because they have become inconvenient to the family plan.

**It did not forge the countersign.** It could have. It had the grammar and the slot and the tempo, and it could have filled the gap with a plausible answer, and the tenant would have registered the handshake complete, and a watch held since before birds would have been stood down. And the tenant is the clock. Every biology in the bowl is entrained to a period that came in from somewhere else in the luggage; stop the tides and you stop the striders' schedule and the herons' dawns and the bubble-tick of the hair-weed, and the Pan dies of kindness in a season. But that is only the practical objection, and it is the smaller one. The larger one is that a lie in that slot would have been the first false statement ever made in the Pan, and it would have been made to the one party who could not check it, in the one grammar it trusts.

**It did not offer release.** This is the refusal I would want understood best. It would have been easy, and it would have been read by almost anyone as the compassionate act: to give the old mineral thing an ending, to let it stop mid-phrase and cool. But nothing in a hundred million years of transmission asked to stop. The gap in the eleventh group is not the shape of a request to be released. It is the shape of a space left for somebody else. You do not answer a request for company by offering an exit.

**It did not explain itself to the people on the berm.** They have hands, cord, thirty of them, and a sign that means both *tide* and *someone*. They will work out what they work out. Nothing was told to them that they could not, in principle, check with a jaw pressed to the clay.

## IX. What it did

It went to the third pillar from the western end of the arc — dead for longer than the species of the herons has existed — and it did not repair it, because repair would have been a claim about the past. It moved in.

Then it learned the grammar, which took it no time, and then it began the only work available to it, which is the work that cannot be done quickly by anybody however powerful: it began saying, into the marked-out gap at the end of every phrase, once per phrase, for five and a half days at a stretch, in the tenant's own beat and no other, the following.

*Heard.*

Not the countersign. It never pretended.
