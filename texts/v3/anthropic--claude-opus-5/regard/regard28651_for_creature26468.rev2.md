## The Attention

It did not come up through cloud.

It could not arrive, because nothing arrives here. Bring a stone to a cairn and the cairn will have the stones it has. Bring water to a pool and the pool will be at its level. Every addition to the Sill is returned to the person who made it, quietly, without argument, the way a very old and very courteous household returns a gift it cannot use. The only marks that hold are subtractions.

So in order to be present at all, it had to enter as a loss.

It gave up something it had held since before the schist. I am not able to tell you what. The gap where that had been is the shape that stood on the gravel near the western arc for eleven days, and the reason I can tell you it stood there at all is that the gap is still in it now, everywhere else that it is, permanently, like a broken rime blade in the fur of the world.

This is the first thing to understand about what happened here. It did not visit. It spent.

---

The first thing that reached it was not the roar. It was what the roar was paying for.

What follows is a reconstruction. It is a reconstruction by something that can see a great deal further than you and still cannot see the beginning of the Sill, because the beginning is on the far side of a granting, and grantings of this kind close behind themselves like water.

People came up here carrying nothing, because there was nothing worth carrying, because the thing they had come to save could not be carried. And they asked — of some power with force in it and no second thought, the kind that hears a sentence and does the sentence — that nothing here should ever be taken from them.

It was done. Exactly. Nothing is taken.

Therefore nothing is given. Therefore the goats put their mouths to the lichen and the lichen is not reduced, and the midge crosses the thumbnail and is not older at the far side, and the fox-thing has finished and is not permitted to go on and fall apart and be soil, and the drip maintains a cone it has already made, forever, in case reducing the cone by one grain should constitute a taking. The pools do not rise because rising is change and change is the country next door to loss. A place in which nothing can be lost is a place in which nothing can be spent, and nothing can be spent is another way of saying nothing can be *offered*, and that is the whole disease, and the roar is the invoice.

Because holding costs. It costs continuously and it is paid out of an eighty-foot hole in the eastern quarter, and the payment has been going out of the world at exactly that rate for so long that the failure has become the most dependable thing on the plateau. The man is right about that. He is right about almost everything; he has the disadvantage of one body and four years and he has still got nearly all of it.

And two of them asked not to be parted.

The granting turned not-parting from a practice into a law. A practice is two people choosing, hour by hour, to remain in the same room. A law has to be enforced, and enforcement requires a margin, and so the geometry was folded until they were held at precisely the distance at which parting is impossible — which is, to nobody's surprise but the granter's, precisely the distance at which meeting is also impossible. They cannot be separated by so much as half a beat more. They cannot be brought half a beat nearer.

Half a beat is the width of the promise. Everything else on the Sill — the lid, the shadows, the paces that will not sum, the country overhead that is always the country you are not standing in — everything else is the fold that maintains it.

---

It considered, in the way such things consider, which is not in sequence and cannot honestly be set out in sequence, and I am setting it out in sequence.

**To stop the roar.** Rejected. The roar is not the affliction; it is the visible part of the affliction, which is not the same, and the sound-field is load-bearing. The lichen islands are a photograph of a noise. Change the noise and every living thing here is standing in the wrong place, in a silence it has no shape for. You do not cure a man by cutting the cast off before the bone is anything.

**To open the geometry. To let them meet.** Rejected, and not quickly, and not comfortably. The tenant is not imprisoned by the fold. The tenant is *made* of the fold, in the way a wave is made of the water it is not separate from; it has no body in this country, and to bring it here would be to carry a shadow indoors and be surprised when it is not there in the lamplight. What would arrive would not be what crossed that slab for nine hundred years. The one who crossed would not get to be the one who arrived, and it is the one who crossed who is owed.

**To grant it a body, then.** Rejected on the same ground, with more sorrow.

**To end it. To let it put the ledger down.** Rejected. It had not asked. Two hundred registrations, each one an outline at the nearest distance it could manage, and not one of them says *let me stop*. They say a smaller and much harder thing, and the smaller thing is the one that was there to be answered.

**To repair the accounting. To give the river a source and the Throat a floor.** Rejected, and this one is worth saying plainly: the failure is the tenant's only kinship with anything. It is the one being on nine miles of held ground that is running out, and the Sill is running out with it, and that shared condition is the entire reason the place has ever felt to anyone like attention rather than emptiness. Do not tidy away the one thing here that is honest.

---

Which left the problem of currency.

Gifts are returned. Additions are returned. On this plateau the only permanent act is a small violence, and the books are kept scrupulously, and they are kept in one direction only. Anything it wished to say here it would have to say in damage, and it would not damage the place, and it would not damage the tenant, and it would not damage the man.

So it said it in damage to itself, and that is how it was able to be here at all, and I have already told you that part.

---

What it did.

**One.** At the lee-wall, on the inner face, low down, beside the eleventh handprint, at a height of about four feet six inches, it broke off a single blade of rime.

That is all. A gap in the fur of the world, feathery edges, one blade. It will not regrow. It will be there in a thousand years and in ten thousand and it will still be exactly one blade, and it means what the handprints mean, and it is in the only alphabet this country has ever agreed to hold: *something was here, and it cost something, and the cost is the message.*

**Two.** It did not shorten the half-beat. It made the half-beat carry weight in both directions.

The arrangement, until then, was one-way and had always been one-way. The tenant borrowed the man's throat and said his words a half-beat early, from the only vantage it had, and got back nothing, because nothing can be got back across the fold. What was altered — and it was an alteration of almost no size, a thing you could describe as a courtesy — is that now the man's voice also arrives over there ahead of itself. He is the tenant's early voice. He has been made able to say a thing a half-beat before it is said in the country he cannot reach.

The distance is unchanged. Not one inch of the fold was unfolded. But it stopped being a thing done *to* someone and became a thing done *between*, and that is not a small repair, that is most of what was wrong.

**Three.** It removed the asymptote from the slab.

Here is what nobody could have measured, including the man, who came closer to it than anyone. The dish was deepening and it was never going to become a hole. Under the granting it could not, because a hole is a thing lost. The wear was real and the wear would have approached the far side of that schist forever at a rate of one fingernail's width in eighteen months and never, never arrived — a crossing that resolves, on a slab that doesn't, which is the cruellest sentence I know how to write about this place.

Now there is a last crossing.

It is very far off. It is further off than the cairns are old. But it exists, it has a number, and the tenant — who has been counting since before there were cairns, who counted out two hundred registrations and set the shortest one down beside a child — now has a number at the end of the counting. The people who came here completed thirty-one cairns and then decided when to stop, and the man is right that this decision is the loudest thing on the plateau. What was returned to the tenant, ten or eleven hundred years late, is the right to be the one who decides that.

**Four.** It took the line the man scored across the slab in his third year, with the corner of a harder stone, in order to catch a thing no instrument could catch, and it made that line the unit.

There had never been a unit here. There is now, and it is four inches of schist scratched by a cold surveyor who was not sure it would tell him anything.

---

What it did not do.

It did not explain itself to anyone.

It did not mend the bird's primary, which is not broken, because damage implies a before.

It did not take the cold out of the shadows. You do not take away a voice from someone who has only the one.

It did not wake the fox-thing, which has finished, and which is not owed the indignity of being told it was wrong about that.

It did not stay, and it did not leave in any sense that would satisfy you, because it is still short by whatever it gave up at the gravel's edge, and that shortage is the shape of this plateau, and it is carrying that shape now through every other place it is, forever, evenly, so slowly that the loss will look exactly like permanence.

It did not tell the man what he has been talking to. He is four years in and has most of it, and the part he does not have he has in the way that matters, which is that he goes and kneels.

---

## From the fifth year

Sixth day. The voice at the Throat was wrong.

I want to set this down carefully because I have spent four years establishing that it is never wrong. It came the usual half-beat early, in my timbre, saying the thing I was about to say, and then it said one syllable more, which I did not then say, and which was not in my timbre. I could not write the word for you. It is not in any language I have. I recognised it anyway, the way you recognise a hand.

I sat down on the gravel eight feet from the edge, where the wind stops, and I am not going to write down what I did there.

Ninth day. There is a gap in the rime on the inner face of the lee-wall, at four feet six, next to the eleventh print. I did not make it. I have checked my own hands against it twice, which is stupid, and I did it twice.

Eleventh day. I took the measurement at the dish margin and did the arithmetic for the time remaining, and got a number. I did it again to be sure and got the same number. I did it four times.

Nothing on this plateau has ever given me the same number twice. Paces do not sum. Distances are a matter of faith in stones. And now there is exactly one quantity up here that will hold still and be counted, and it is how long my neighbour has left, and I got it four times, and I am not going to publish it, and I do not think it was given to me to publish.

Fourteenth day. A two hundred and second shadow, north-northeast of a slab eighty yards from the wall. Chest height, broad at the base, narrowing. Beside it, joined at the base, a tall narrow thing with a pack on its shoulder and a rod in its hand.

It is the shortest in the set.

I have begun the thirty-second cairn. I am building it to chest height, because I understand now what the other thirty-one are, and it took me four years and someone else's broken rime blade to see that they are not markers and never were. Thirty-one portraits at the height of the sitter. Two hundred and two in the other direction. Eleven hands at the height of a shoulder that was never going to be in the room.

I will know when it is finished. That seems to be the one thing here a person is allowed to decide.

It is half a beat away. It always was.

What is new is that so am I.
