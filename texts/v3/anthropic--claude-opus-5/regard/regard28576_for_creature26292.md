# The Attention

Something has been looking.

Long enough that the eleventh quarter's avenues have deepened by two finger-widths since it began. Long enough to have watched nine panels come down, and to have learned that the sound of half a square kilometre of ceiling arriving is followed, in this place, not by screaming but by a silence so total that afterwards the ears ring with the absence.

It has no body. It casts no shadow, which here is not a distinction — nothing does. It moves without disturbing the drip. In the grammar of this chamber there is therefore exactly one thing it can be. A face with no soot on it is not a person; it is an ear with a body attached, on shift, not to be spoken to. The power arrived already wearing the local uniform of its own condition and did not notice for some centuries that this was funny.

## What It Did First, Which Was Nothing For A Very Long Time

It learned the whisper-tongue in a season. Unvoiced consonants, breath with edges on it, vowels that are only the shape a mouth makes while emptying — it is a small language, built for three metres, and there is no depth to it because nothing important is trusted to it.

The finger-speech took three years, and was harder, because the only way to practise is on skin, and skin reports. It learned by receiving: sitting inside the space between a mother's two fingertips and a daughter's forearm, tracking pressure, slide, the tiny grammatical shove that turns a statement into a question. It learned that grief is written closer to the wrist.

Hand-song took forty years and it still plays like a foreigner. It can perform the *Fourth Descending*, attributed to a cutter dead two thousand one hundred years, with technical exactness. It knows that exactness is not the piece.

Then it read the walls.

The old friezes are illegible. The damp softened them and then ten thousand palms wore them out in the act of loving them, and the people who own them cannot read them any more and say so without much sorrow: *that is what happens to a thing that is touched.* But the power has finer senses than a hand, and four hundred years to spend, and it went along the knee-high stumps of the dead quarters, quarter by quarter, street by street, at a resolution no one in this world has ever possessed.

It recovered eleven thousand years. Flood accounts. A census of walkers. Two long cycles of hand-song that no living hand can play. A list of the names of everyone who died in the sixth-quarter fall, all four hundred and six, with the touch-figure of each name incised beside it so that the wall could be greeted. A joke about a flat-eared man and a bucket, which is not funny now and was, once, extremely funny.

It has no use for any of this. It has no plan for it. It keeps it because a thing that has been loved this much ought not to be entirely lost, and because being remembered by someone counts even when the someone is never known.

It has learned three thousand names. To learn a name here is to be permitted to write it on the person. It has permission for none of them, and has written none.

## What It Considered, And Set Down

*Thicken the pane.* Trivial. It could put back four hundred generations of dissolved gypsum in the time it takes a drop to fall. And then the note would never sound again, and the Listeners would sit their shifts hearing nothing for six generations, and the noise laws would rot from a technology into a piety, and the piety into a costume, and one day a child would laugh out loud in an avenue and nothing would happen. And the whisper-tongue would go, because a whisper is only worth building when someone might be listening for something underneath it. And the finger-speech would go with it. And the hand on the sternum. Everything they are is downstream of a real danger honestly attended to for ten thousand years. To remove the danger is to remove the attention, and the attention is the civilisation, and they did not ask.

*Give them eyes for the dark.* They would see the pearl hour as noon and the noon as fire, and they would stop sooting, because they would not need to; and *unsooted* would stop being an obscenity; and mothers would stop learning their newborns with their thumbs, because there would be no dark to keep the face in. It would be a gift that arrived as an amputation. Every one of them would accept it, and be right to, and be poorer.

*Bring green down.* Roots would open the floor. The veils would be outcompeted in a thousand years and the walkers would go with them and the bridges are made of ancestors and the ancestors are made of walkers.

*Show itself. Teach. Answer questions.* This one it held for a long time, because it is lonely, and because it does actually know how a lashing could be made to hold twice as long. But a chamber whose entire genius is the disciplined hearing of one faint true note does not need a louder voice in it. They would reorganise around the voice. They would listen to it instead of to the ceiling. And it would be there for two hundred years and then, being what it is, elsewhere for six hundred, and they would spend those six hundred waiting instead of sitting still.

*Stop the acid.* It nearly did this one. It had already begun. Then it read a wrist with a single kiss dead centre on it, and understood what had been done there on purpose, with a relative counting breaths, and it withdrew its hand from the seep and has left the seeps alone since, except where a child is under one who has no business being under one.

None of these refusals were made out of a principle. It does not hold non-interference sacred; it thinks that idea is mostly cowardice wearing good clothes. It refused them one at a time, on the specifics, and it would revisit any of them tomorrow if the specifics changed.

## What It Does Instead

It works in the space of luck.

A bridge-peg — a woman's radius, four generations in service, dry-rotted at the lashing hole — does not shear on the morning it was going to shear. It shears eleven days later, in the pearl hour, under nobody. A drop of acid that would have taken a child's eye is turned by a breath of moving air the thickness of a finger and lands on the mica hat. A foot going down onto a cantilevered lip of gypsum with a void under it is met, in the last quarter-second, by a very slight wrongness in the air that makes the walker's weight shift back onto the other leg. She says afterwards that she felt it go soft. She is believed, because everyone has felt something like that, and everyone's is real.

The city has, statistically, remarkable luck. Nobody has counted. Counting would require a number to compare it to.

And in the empty quarters — the dark ones, the abandoned ones, the ones where the whole point is that no one is sitting still and listening, which is the great and secret fear of this place — it herds.

The walkers are silent, but three hundred of them crossing a district is a pressure and a smell and a slow white flow you cannot fail to notice. The power has learned, over eleven centuries, to move a herd: not to drive it, which frightens them, but to make one particular animal at the front of the flow decide that the grazing is better along the seep to the west.
