# The Attending

## What it did first

Nothing, for sixty-one years.

It read. There are two ledgers and a sluice book and a wall of margins, and it read all of them in the order they were written, which took longer than reading them in any other order would have, and was the point. It learned the eleven words. It learned them properly, which is to say by nose and not by handle, standing over tank three at day five in the fifth month with the exhaust bleed running, until it could have taken the post. It stood under the north clump in a gust. It stood in the Walk at full cart and learned to pitch into the gap between the beats, though it does not speak. It learned the forty signs. It has never used them.

It counted. It arrived at forty-one, and then it arrived at forty-one and wrong, and it understood the engineer's column before it understood anything else about her.

Powers that are only benevolent arrive and begin. This one is also wise, and so what it did first was find out what the place would cost to help.

## The two rules, and the third

It found the rules already written, in the flat hand, phrased as structure and not as care:

*You may not kill a root that holds a beam.*

*You may not close the last crop.*

It took them both as binding on itself, which is what a wise power does with the law of a place it has come to: not judge the law, first, but stand under it and see what the law holds up.

From the first rule it derived the clause that governed everything it did afterward, and the derivation was the whole of its wisdom and took it most of a decade.

The rule protects the root. Read from the other side, the rule *convicts* the root. A root that has taken load can never be cut again, not by anyone, not ever, whatever it does afterward — it has bought the works and the works has bought it, and the transaction cannot be undone by either party. So: *do not become such a root.* Take no load here you are not prepared to hold for as long as the terrace stands. And take none that cannot be handed back.

This is not humility. It is arithmetic. A power that quietly becomes load-bearing at a place like this has not saved it; it has moved the whole structure onto a member nobody can inspect, in a works whose one unbreakable horror is an unattended load.

The third rule it took from the brickwork. All the ruin here is at the line: the knee-height band, the tide mark, the half-buried, the seasonally exposed. Nothing rots that stays wet; nothing rots that stays moving; everything fails where it is neither. It looked at the people and found the same band on them, ankle to knee, and then it looked at everything else about them and found the band there too — the knowledge that is neither written nor fully alive, the nose in its ninth year, the ear in its fourteenth, the terrace's edge where forty kilometres of unmanaged bamboo begins, the six days a year when Hansa is neither running nor gone.

So: work the line. Do not help in the middle. In the middle they are magnificent and they are fine.

## Refusals

It did not cure the shins by fiat. A works whose ulcers close overnight has been told something about the world that it cannot check, cannot cut, and cannot un-know, and it would have spent the next four cuts looking for the agency instead of at the boards.

It did not sweeten the sloughs. The tail slough is the works' own excretion and the shrimp are a dial calibrated in that exact liquor; clean water below the sluice would have blinded the sluice keeper in a week and the nose would have found out about the next bad tank two days later than she does now, for the rest of time.

It did not give them birdsong. They would have had to decide what to do about it. A place with two people in the crowns six hours a day cannot host a songbird population and a fig calendar both, and it is not the power's business which they would have chosen.

It did not make the gecko immortal, though it could have, and the temptation was real and lasted an evening. The ledger records the post and not the occupant. That convention is load-bearing in a way the ledger does not know: it is the works' whole theory of itself, the thing that lets forty-one people be a maintenance behaviour and not forty-one frightened animals. A gecko that was actually the same gecko would have made the convention into a fact, and facts can be disproved, and conventions of that kind cannot.

It did not settle the stack fig. Obviously.

It did not prevent the third lost tank. It stood over the tank while it went, and did not touch the bleed, and watched a twenty-two-year-old nose say *sweet* with her hands, because she could not have got it out in a voice, and the works made second-grade cable for nine years. Preventing it would have been the single most loving act available and would have removed the last crop: what the place *has*, in place of a god, is the knowledge that tank three is one bad year from ending and that only they stand between. Take that and you have taken the reason they are careful, and carefulness at this scale, sustained across fourteen generations, is not a means to anything. It is the thing itself. It is what they are.

It did not reveal itself. The moment they knew, they would have begun to lean, and a leaned-on power is an attended load that cannot be inspected, which is worse than an unattended one, and they would never have been able to cut it to test it.

And it did not take the heron. It thought about it for a long time, because the shape was so exact and so available — the visitor that comes for forty minutes every ninth or tenth day and takes only shrimp. It declined. The heron is the only thing at the Culm Works that comes and goes because it chooses to, and a power that puts itself inside the one free thing in a place has not blessed the place; it has finished enclosing it.

## What it did

**The shins.** It did not clear them. Over about forty years it arranged for the same accident to keep happening — a skimming bucket set down in the tar shed, again, and again, by different hands, in different weather, until a man in his third cut with nothing much to do in a wet month had a thought about it. The consortium in tank three had always been hostile to the thing that lives in a wet shin. Nobody had ever put the two in the same room on purpose. The salve is called *three-and-tar* and it took eleven more years to get into the first ledger, in the man's own name, in the margin, because it was his. It takes perhaps a third off. A third is the difference between a woman in her fifth cut walking the boards and not walking them, and the power has no better sentence than that one and does not want one.

A power that is genuinely patient has, in the place of miracles, repetition.

**The grate.** Six nights a year, the two stops, one person chosen by lot on a hand-fired grate holding six tanks within two degrees. For thirty-odd years the power held the margin — never more than the person could have held themselves, never on a night they were managing, never enough to show in the plate. It was aware the entire time that this was load, and that it had taken it, and that under its own clause it was now obliged to hold it forever or hand it back.

So it spent those same thirty years making the handback. It cannot speak and it did not try. It let a second grate rust in the wrong place until moving it was obvious. It arranged for the lot to be drawn twice one year by accident, and for the two of them to sit the night together, and for that to be pleasant. It waited while the works discovered, at its own speed, that the lucky post could be two people. It is a rota now. It has been a rota for fifty years. The power has not touched the bleed since, and the works has never once noticed that a thing which used to be survivable is now merely hard, and that is the whole shape of what success looks like here.

**The letters.** It worked mostly downstream, where there is room. It carried the reputation of the Culm Works the forty kilometres the Culm Works cannot walk: made the place legible to families who would otherwise never have heard of a terrace of forty-one, made the barge captains speak well of the cable, kept a hand-written line of acquaintance alive in three river towns across ninety years. Two marriages a decade became a little more than two. Four incomers arrived who would not otherwise have arrived, and one of them is the reason the canopy pair are presently unrelated, and nobody at the works will ever know the arithmetic they were spared.

**The chain.** It did not give anyone back their nose. It made the overlap longer where it could. In one particular year, eleven years before it mattered, it caused a girl of nine to be given the tanks as a punishment for something trivial, and she stood there sulking twice a day for a season and hated it and learned *bread and iron* without being taught. Eleven years later a nose died in the fifth month with no successor named, and the chain that has held fourteen times held a fifteenth, and it is recorded as that woman's own doing, which it was.

**The hanger.** Once, and only once, it did something that could be called speaking, and it used the instrument the works provides. A casting flaw was opening in the third hanger where no adult could hear it, because every adult had gone over. The power made a nine-year-old stand still one moment longer than she meant to. She heard the thin thing at nine kilohertz. She reported it in the dozen terms she was taught at five and cannot define, and an engineer in her fourth cut, with a bad left shin, greased what the child said to grease and then pulled the hanger, because that is procedure and has been for a hundred and six years. Nobody learned anything. Nothing was revealed. A works that already had the correct arrangement simply used it.

**The wasps.** Twice in ninety years — once in a drought, once after a strike closed a crop three weeks early — it carried wasps. A few thousand insects, a millimetre and a half each, eleven days egg to death, moved perhaps three hundred metres from a crop that was closing to a crop that was receptive, inside a two-day window, on behalf of two buildings that have no walls of their own left and a stack with a fig at the twelve-metre course.

Its largest intervention at the Culm Works weighed under a gram. It considers this appropriate and would like it noted that the canopy pair have managed the same thing unassisted, by calendar, every other year since before the second rebore.

**The pyres.** It attends. It does nothing, because there is nothing, and it stands in the one light-well cut for no productive reason, which is the shape of its whole attitude to the place: an opening made for no return, not maintained, allowed to close at whatever rate the crowns decide.

And once, at each one, where there is no possibility of being heard, it says the name out loud. They do not use names on the boards. The name goes in the margin of the day's canopy entry in the same hand-size as everything else and is never spoken again by anybody. So it says it, once, in the noise. That is not an intervention. It is the only thing it has ever done here purely because it wanted to.

## What it could not do

It cannot make the work not hurt.

It cannot stop the tail slough silting a hand's width a decade, and it will not dredge, because the head slough would answer and the answer would take a generation to read.

It cannot hold the century off. No equilibrium outlasts its world, and the world downstream is changing faster than fourteen years, and one day a barge will not come, or will come with something worse than salt. It has not warned them. A warning would land on a people who have no procedure for it and would become, instantly, the load nobody can inspect.

What it did instead is copy everything. The eleven words, the fig calendar crop by crop, the root column entire, the four thousand heron entries, the sixty-one oiling points, the forty signs, the dates and hours of the two lost tanks with no other comment. It is held elsewhere, in more than one place, against a day.

## The jar

And somewhere else, at blood heat, without interruption, the power keeps a tank.

It is inoculated from three. It is topped and skimmed and bled and fed. It contains four or five bacteria that anyone at the works could describe by behaviour and nobody could name, a fungus that makes the day-five *iron*, and something else known only by its absence. It has never been allowed to cool. It is the only property the power has ever acquired, it is a warm brown liquid that smells of bread and iron, and it stirs it.

If the works ends — and things end — then the mother does not have to.

## The count

It keeps a ledger, in the local format, because there was no reason to invent another.

*People:* forty-one. The people are the part that has to be fed.

*Rats:* one lineage, eighty or ninety, natives in the strict sense.

*Geckos:* one, recorded as one.

*Shrimp:* uncountable, functioning as a dial.

*Wasps:* three species, eleven days, load-bearing.

*Heron:* not one heron, and never was.

And at the back, in the sluice book's format — date, arrival, departure, weather — it enters itself, ninth or tenth day after ninth or tenth day, ninety years of it and more, in the same hand-size as everything else.

It has never once written *bearing*.

It has been careful, the entire time, not to be.
