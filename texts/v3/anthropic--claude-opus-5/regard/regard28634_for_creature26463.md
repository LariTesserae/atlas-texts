# Attendance

## I. It came downhill

There was no other way. There is only the one approach, and a power that arrives by some other route has arrived somewhere else. So it came over the crest through the fine silt, and did not disturb the fox's prints, which were four hours old and had perhaps six hours left in them.

At the edge of the gravel shelf it stopped and made its first decision, which was to be deafened.

It could have declined the noise. Nothing in the sound had any purchase on it. But it understood that a place is partly its terms, and that to stand in the bowl exempt from the bowl would be to stand somewhere adjacent to the bowl, a hand's breadth off, in a hollow with the same shape and none of the facts. So it took the bass thrum into the shins it did not have. It took the plate-press reports in the chest. It stood through one full cycle of the melt — the swell of the late afternoon, the long slackening, the four-o'clock working hum — because you cannot know a machine from a single shift, and it did not intend to be that kind of visitor.

By the second day it had stopped noticing the trembling of the ground, and then noticed it again, which is the correct sequence.

## II. It looked for the being

It had come on the understanding that there was someone here.

What it found, when it asked, was a census. Sorted by tenancy, and nearly all of the tenancy provisional. The wheatear had an address on the far side of the ridge and treated the hollow as a shop floor. The wagtail had a week left. The buntings had eleven minutes. The skua was auditing. The fox had come sixty meters and taken nothing. The geese had one night in May and one in autumn and no opinion of the place beyond its flatness.

Underneath that: the alga in the weathering crust, stoking the boiler it had no idea it was stoking. Ten thousand coin-wide households on the apron, each with a founding date and a floor of masonry knitted out of the mill's own waste. Rotifers with no fathers anywhere in their line. Bears the size of a comma-stroke, punching filaments and drinking them out in full view. The midge larvae down in the sandstorm, holding the coldest two hundred meters of water on the continent against all comers, because nothing else wants it.

The power counted. It is good at counting. But it had come to do something *for* someone, and it could not find the accusative.

So it considered the mill.

The mill had a throughput. It had a night rate and a day rate and a shift change. It had flora, in the medical sense, and the flora affected its metabolism. It had a diet of bedrock and an output measured in tonnes. It had been running for ten thousand years without an operator. When the power looked for an interior — the place where, in a being, someone is at home — it found no room there at all. It found only the process, going on, extremely thoroughly, in every direction.

The power decided that the question was badly put, and that the habit of requiring a single addressee before offering anything was a habit and not a requirement. Then it stopped looking, and began.

## III. What it did not do

It did not stop the ice.

This was the first thing it examined and the first thing it set down. The hollow exists because ice rotted out of the ground. The bedrock at the head is polished because the ice left seven years ago. The pond is a pond because a buried block departed. The moss is fed by a seep and the seep is a symptom of leaving. The mill's power source is not the glacier but the glacier's dying, metered out; the flour on the pond's floor and the midges in the cobbles and the wheatear's five young are all being paid out of that departure. To hold the ice still would be to shut the plant. There is no way to give this place permanence that is not also a way to switch it off.

It did not warm the hollow. The larvae are finished at eight degrees. Kindness applied at the wrong temperature is a solvent.

It did not clear the water. The flour is not dirt in the works; the flour is the product, and the turbidity is the reason there is a floor down there for anything to be built on at all. Also — and it noted this separately — someone would have to be the first to see the bottom of that pond, and the power considered its own candidacy for that and declined it.

It did not give the wheatear her voice back. She has solved the noise. She flashes the rump once on take-off, she bobs, she droops a wing at the boulder's edge, and two adults four meters apart settle a border in complete silence like men on a factory floor. To hand her back her *chik-chik* would be to hand her back a redundancy and call it a gift. Instead the power learned the mime. This took a season. It is a real language and it has a grammar and there was no one to teach it.

It did not slow the retreat, which is the hard one, and the one it went back to.

The mill is running down. Not on the ten-thousand-year schedule the hollow's own arithmetic assumes, but faster, on a schedule of decades, and the midges' address will close, and the cold two hundred meters will become ordinary water, and the bowl will silt and green and quiet, out of order and ahead of time. The power could have held the snowline. It went so far as to do the arithmetic.

The arithmetic came out the way such arithmetic comes out. The air is one body. A snowline held here is heat moved there; a favor to this bowl is an invoice posted to a valley the power had never visited, addressed to residents it had not counted, who had also not been asked. Benevolence at that scale stops being generosity and becomes plumbing, and plumbing is zero-sum. It put the ledger down.

It is not certain this was right. It is wise, which is a different thing.

## IV. What it did

**It stayed.** A year, and then longer. Not hovering: standing in the bowl through the fog and the icicle-shedding and the shift change, present at the day rate and present at the night rate, which nothing else in the hollow has ever been, since everything here is either traffic or too small to have a shift.

**It heard the wheatear.** Every *chik-chik* she has made in the bowl out of pure habit, unheard by anything since she hatched, opening her beak against fifty tonnes an hour of white noise. The power heard all of them. It did not answer. Nothing about her situation changed. But a thing that had never once happened in that hollow now happened, several hundred times a summer.

**It kept the register.** The place has a ceiling on its history and the ceiling is low: no ruins, no fossils, no bones but this year's. Nothing here has a past, because nothing here has anywhere to put one. So the power became the place to put one. Not naming — naming is a tic of a particular kind of visitor — but holding: ten thousand founding dates and ten thousand drainings; the midge that drowned last August and is still, being unrotted, a midge; the fox's sixty meters and the exact hour of them; the eleven minutes of the buntings; the year the lone grass tuft germinated, which no one else will ever know, and the fact that it was not a good year.

**It attended the drainings.** A hole's floor melts down, meets a channel in the rotten ice, and the entire world — water, tilth, masonry, bears, the whale — goes out of the bottom of its sky in an afternoon. This is not a catastrophe; it is the distribution arrangement; the residents are also the cargo and they have all folded down to grains and will ride the fog. The power knows this. It attended anyway. Ten thousand a season, all of them, each one at the moment of going. This is the whole of what it means to witness, and it is not small, and it does not require that anything be prevented.

**It cheated only inside the noise.** Where two outcomes were genuinely indifferent — where a grain of curled rotifer, riding the drip, could as easily land on wet felt as on dry grit, and no law had a preference — the power chose. It never bent a rate, never added to the wind, never put a thumb anywhere the thumb would show. It has been careful with this even so, because luck is a currency and it has seen what happens to a currency that is issued freely.

**It waited eleven years for a goose.** For the sake of the tuft of grass that stands alone on the shelf, twenty meters from any other tuft of grass, it waited for a bird whose left foot carried the right mud, and then arranged for that bird to stand, for one night in May, four meters downslope and slightly west. There are two tufts now. This is the largest thing the power has done in the hollow and no survey would detect it.

**It consented to be cargo.** Having decided that the mill was a being or was not a being and that either way it was owed a courtesy, the power took a handful of the grey milk and let itself be carried in it, out of the portal, down the braids, past the boots of anyone standing there, through the whole distribution arrangement, at the mill's pace and not its own. It came out two valleys over, in fog, in a state of some fine distribution, and it did not regard the time as lost.

## V. The sentence

There is a sentence in the account of this place that stops in the middle. It is about the adults: matte, hairy, wingless, three days, no mouths to feed with, mating in loose crowds on the surface of the ice in an atmosphere no other insect will tolerate — *with*

and then nothing.

The power did not finish it. Nothing here is finished; that is one of the rules, and it had not come to break the rules of a place it had elected to be deafened by. The braids will have moved by tomorrow. The seep will migrate and the moss will die and a new moss will start somewhere forty meters left.

But it knows how the sentence goes. It has the ending held, complete, in the register, with the founding dates and the sealed page about the bottom of the pond — in case the sentence is ever asked for by anyone who would know what to do with it.

## VI.

Toward evening the overcast thickened and the fog closed and the ice began to give its light back upward, out of the crevasse fields, out from under the portal arch, so that the bowl was lit wrongly, from the ground, every shadow pointing up.

The power stood in the upward light and cast a shadow, which was the only mark it made all year, and which fell on the moss and did it no harm.

Then the mill changed gear and slackened and settled to its night rate — patient, unattended, throughput reduced and never zero.

Attended, now. Reduced and never zero. Still running when you leave.
