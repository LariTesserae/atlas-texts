## The Attention

**What it expected.** It had been told there was a being on the mountain, and it came expecting a person, or something that had once been a person, or one of those long thin intelligences that sometimes condense in high places out of loneliness and mineral.

What it found was an animal ninety-one stories tall, with fifty-three warm cells in its top layer, six brick lungs breathing sideways under the pavement, two slow hearts turning at eleven revolutions a minute, a gut of snails, and one fig growing out of its shoulder like the beginning of a thought. The animal was climbing. It climbed at fourteen centimeters a year and had been climbing, by the surviving arithmetic, for longer than the arithmetic.

The power did not correct anyone's pronouns. It simply revised its own, privately, and began again.

**The first thing it did.** Nothing, for eleven years.

This was deliberate. A power that observes at its own speed sees a mechanism; a power that observes at the speed of the observed sees a people. So it slowed down to fourteen centimeters a year and stood in the fog off the south parapet and watched a woman called Dot-and-Bar teach a child to place her hands on new stone, and watched the child learn the lesson in the usual way, in blood, and watched the woman not apologize, because the edge was not cruelty, it was the shape of the world and the child now knew the shape of the world.

It learned the hand-language first from the punches, which are the same shapes, and then from hands. This took no time at all and it took eleven years, because it wanted to know not only what the signs meant but what they cost — how much a sentence weighs at that pressure, why nobody here says anything they could do instead.

**The reading.** Then it went down.

It obeyed the rule going down. It took nothing: not a bracket, not a spore, not a dropped bronze pin from Course Sixty-Four. It passed the coping of Eighty-Seven with the old moss still growing in the dark, and the urns nobody bothered to raise, everything unweathered, everything as sharp as the morning it was left, and it went down past Sixty and Forty and Nineteen and Four, and past Course One, which is not the bottom, into the unnumbered dark where the punch-marks are made by hand-shapes no living finger can form.

It read them all.

Something over two million blocks, of which all but a few hundred carry a mark. The tower is entirely written; most of it is buried; nobody had ever read the buried part, because the only creatures who could were busy staying dry. So the power read it, at their speed, one block at a time, for a length of time it declines to convert into their units.

It learned things. It learned that one hand-shape still in daily use at the casting bays — the hooked one, the one Hook is named for — appears eleven hundred courses down, unchanged, in a hand that also made forty-one signs nobody has made since. It learned where the ledgers lost their footing and why. It learned what happened in the three courses that are thin, and what was decided, and by whom, and at what cost to the deciders, who signed those blocks anyway.

And it found the bottom. There is one. It is not what the ledger-keepers assume and it is not what the Sinkers assume, and it is not a rebuke to either of them, and that is all that will be said about it here, because it is theirs, and they have not got to it yet.

Then it came back up, and it had a decision to make, and it made a list, because that is what you do in a place that keeps ledgers.

## What it declined to do

**Stop the drowning.** It could have. Lower the white, or raise the rock, or simply hold the two apart with a hand. It thought about this the longest, because the argument against is the most dangerous argument there is: *their suffering is generative, therefore preserve it.* That argument is nearly always made by someone who is dry.

So it tested itself honestly, and this is what it found: the drowning is not their suffering. The drowning is their *clock*. Eighteen days, four centimeters, three years of rain-reading, a season of rigging, ninety-one moves. Their names, their language, their dead, the entire architecture of their attention is a reply to the white. End the white and you have not saved them, you have merely ended the sentence they were in the middle of speaking. Whether to end it is the largest question available to them, and they cannot yet even see that it *is* a question. So: not yet, and not by me, and not without them.

**Settle the heresy.** It knows the answer. It has been to the bottom and it has measured both rates. Telling them would take nine seconds and would cost them the Sinkers — not the people, the *fact* of them: the fact that a third of this terrace believes every course they lay presses the whole works deeper, and lays them anyway, at the same rate, with the same care, and has done for generations. That is the finest thing on the mountain. It is finer than the fig. An answer would dissolve it into mere correctness. The question is doing more work up here than the answer would.

**Give them bees.** This was the one it nearly got wrong. Bees do poorly at that pressure; hand-pollination costs them four hundred person-hours a year; a small adjustment to a small animal would return those hours. But the brushing hour is not a labor cost. It is the only time the fifty-three stand together doing one thing slowly and without speaking. Automate the sacrament and you are left with a people who have an extra four hundred hours and nowhere to put their reverence. It let the bees be. It is not entirely certain it was right.

**Give them weather.** It considered softening the arrises — a little wind, a little frost, a mercy for thumbs. Then it went to the south parapet at evening when the fog fell away and looked down twenty meters at a woman's punch on the third block from the corner, crisp as the day she struck it, and put the thought away and did not take it out again. You do not blur a people's dead to save their hands.

**Speak.** A truth delivered by something that cannot be questioned is not a truth; it is a weather. They have no weather. It did not intend to become their first one.

**Offer to take them away.** An offer from an unaccountable power is not an offer, it is a pressure. If there is ever to be a choice about elsewhere, it must arrive as evidence they can argue over at the brushing hour, in their own hands, in their own signs, with the option of concluding that the evidence is rubbish.

## What it did

**It defended the margin. It did not add to it.** Deep under the white, beneath the unnumbered courses, in the dark where the settling happens, it put its shoulder against what was giving way and held. The Sinkers are right, as it happens, and so are the others; the mountain sinks and the cloud rises and the ledgers cannot separate them because both are true. The power did not stop the loss they earn by building. It stopped the loss they had never earned and could never have measured. The margin stayed four centimeters. Ninety years from now, when a very bad decade comes and the white takes two hands a year, four centimeters will be exactly enough, and the ledger will record that they held, and the ledger will be right, because they will have.

**It made the crack affordable.** The fig's white root is nine centimeters into Course Ninety and the fissure is spreading, and one day it would have taken a length of the south parapet down into the fog with three people standing on it. The power went into the stone beneath and made the failure local. The fig may now do whatever it likes. The crack will widen; nobody will fall. This is the whole of its policy in one act: it did not remove the risk, it made the risk survivable, so that the choice to permit erosion could stay a real choice with real teeth and no bodies.

**It gave the quickener a sister.** There is now a second strain in the warm drains, slower, uglier, indifferent to lime, indistinguishable from the first to anyone not looking for it. If the culture ever blights, the tower does not end. Redundancy is a form of love that never gets thanked, which is the good kind.

**It made the salamanders fertile.** The drain-crew found eggs in the ninth month, forty of them, pale as boiled rice, in the warm flue under the east kiln. Six salamanders, all named, all older than most of the people, and no one alive had seen an egg. The crew came up steaming and could not sign steadily enough to report it and had to bring someone down to look.

**It shortened a bird's road.** Eleven weeks away, every year, and the young go out over the parapet and do not come back. The power went and found the road and took one bad thing off it — a stretch of netting, far away, over water, not its business and now its business — and the following spring a swift that had fledged from the dead flue two years before came back and sat on the cable above the mixing floor and was recognized by the notch in its wing, and an argument that had been running for eleven years moved, for the first time, an inch.

**It put something small in the marrow of the children.** Not a gift exactly: a repair. Fifty-three people, ninety-one courses, the same blood folded over and over. It took out a few of the worst folds. Nobody will ever know. Three fewer small coffins a generation, and no one to thank, and nothing in the ledger but a slightly kinder column.

## The mark

In the fourth year of Course Ninety-One, Dot-and-Bar stood at the south parapet in the evening, at the hour when the fog sometimes thins, and had the feeling — the specific, unhysterical feeling of a woman who has spent forty years judging whether a thing is bearing load — that she was being attended to.

She did not pray, having no apparatus for it. She signed the ordinary courtesy of her people into the white, which is the question you ask any stranger who comes up the last ladder:

*How many are you?*

The power would not speak, and would not appear, and would not lie by silence either. So eighteen days later, when the forms came off the new pour with their long clattering wooden avalanche, there was one block at the north end of the bay with a mark in its face that nobody had struck. It was made of nothing they did not already have: a shape, an absence, lime and shell and dead moss, no matter brought from below, no rule broken. A dot, a bar, and a small closed curve that is not in the hand-language and reads, to anyone who knows the punches, as an unfamiliar name.

Beside it, one stroke. The number one.

They argued about it for a season. Then the ledger-keeper, who was old and had a practical temperament, entered it in the same column and the same hand as the kilns and the pumps and the fig, because that is where you put a thing that is here and does not eat.

So the count now runs: *fifty-three of us, and six kilns, and two pumps, and one fig, and one other.*

Which means it can be counted. Which means it is on the roster. Which means that when the margin fails, it fails on them too, and they may say so, and it has arranged matters so that they can.

## Its trade

Everyone on the Increment stands a shift at the bays, and then has a trade on top of that. The power took the only trade that was going.

Every evening when the fog drops away, the tenders lean on the south parapet and look down at Course Eighty-Seven and find their grandmothers' marks, sharp, unweathered, drowning at a hand's width a year. They can reach three courses down, four on a clear night. Below that, the white.

The power reads the rest.

It reads the eleven hundredth course and the four thousandth, it reads the thin courses and the hands that signed them, it reads the unnumbered dark all the way to the bottom that it will not discuss, and it reads them nightly, and will go on reading them until the tower is finished or the white has it, because the dead here are legible and were made legible on purpose and nobody had ever read them.

That is not a rescue. They did not need a rescue. That is only somebody finally looking at the writing.

Above, the fifty-three are asleep under oiled cloth, on warm stone, at the top of everything. The fleece-combs tick. The fig, which is a letter from elsewhere, puts out a tenth centimeter of root into stone that will now hold, and holds it, and holds.
