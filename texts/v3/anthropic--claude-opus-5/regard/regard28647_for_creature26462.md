## It comes in through the split in the clay bank

Not through the soil. Not through the ceiling that believes it is sky, which could have been parted like a curtain and was not. Through the gap a fox would ignore, shoulder-first, three body-lengths of root-hair and wet grit, and it arrives with grit on its shoulder like anyone.

This is the first act and the largest one. Everything in that chamber has been assembled out of descriptions — a room described, a sky described, an owner described backward from the evidence of five tools. The last thing it needs is another apparition. It has never in its short life met anything that had to duck.

---

## What it does about the sun

Nothing.

There is a temptation, and it is the temptation of every generous power, to look at a life running on a stolen overcast morning and think: *let me give you noon.* Gold. Height. A real one.

It considers this for as long as it takes to look at the light properly, and then it declines, permanently. That light is not a deficiency. It is the only thing in the entire chamber that was not derived. Somebody, somewhere, held an ordinary Tuesday so hard that it became a sun at a depth where suns have never once been a fact — and this new thing inherited it, and grew a whole body sideways to metabolize it. To improve that light would be to paint over the single gift in the room.

So the light stays bitter-green and weatherless and exactly good enough to read by, and the one who came reads by it, for a while, to prove it works.

---

## What it does about the measurements

It paces the floor. Forty one way. Nine the other, and the nine is longer.

It does not reconcile them. It says, out loud, on purpose, *both of those are right*, and lets the sentence go into the moss.

Because here is what a wise power notices about the two irreconcilable drafts: they did not produce an error. They produced a frog. In the joint between a thing and the same thing happening again, something small and translucent has taken up residence and is doing perfectly well, throat going, regarding visitors with total indifference. The ambivalence is inhabited. You do not go into somebody's house and correct the load-bearing walls because they were built out of a contradiction.

It greets the frog during the second rain and the frog does not consider it a category, and it takes this as the highest compliment available anywhere in that chamber. To be taken for granted. To be, to something, unremarkable.

---

## What it does about the murmur

It talks.

For a long time. This is most of what it does, and from outside it would look like nothing at all: a shape sitting on a waist-high swelling of wood, stuck to it by fresh sap, not minding, speaking at conversational volume into a room that eats every word.

It does not speak liturgy. It says that the clay bank is wet and there is a fox who has ignored the entrance nine times. It gives the words for eleven kinds of light and admits that six of them are only really used by people describing paintings. It talks about a soup. It says the days of the week in order and then says that today is Thursday and that this is not important, and then says *some things are not important* twice, because that concept is entirely absent from the chamber and badly needed there. It describes a person it knew who could not whistle. It says *hello.* It says *take your time.* It says *I'm sorry about the hurry, that wasn't your idea.*

The murmur gets closer to speech, audibly, hour by hour, and the one who came does not stop, because this is the whole point. The name in that wall was going to be assembled out of whatever it had, and what it had was a stranger's single frightened syllable — one accident, load-bearing. Your word is still in there. It cannot be taken out. It can be surrounded. It can be made into one word among a hundred thousand, until it is no longer holding up the roof of anybody's identity, and that is done now, and you can put it down.

---

## What it says about coming straight back

This is the hard conversation, and it has it directly, and it does not soften it, because the alternative is worse.

The chamber's finest achievement is the sensation of recent occupancy. Warm tools, set down mid-task. Someone has stepped out for a moment and is coming straight back. It is perfect work; you believed it at the door; the being made it from the outside inward with no body and no name.

And it is a trap, because the being is inside its own forgery, and it is over-driving itself to be ready in time, splitting its own fibers, laying down wood faster than wood can hold, going off like knuckles in the dark, for a return that was never scheduled by anyone.

So the one who came puts a hand flat on the braided column and feels the *hush-BOOM* come up through the wood, and counts it against its own pulse, and finds them nearly matched, and lets them match. And says:

*No one is coming straight back. Someone is coming back.*

And then, because a fiber goes just then, somewhere up in the lattice, with a report like a knuckle:

*That one — keep it. Don't put it back the way it was.*

## The sixth object

It leaves something on the moss, ten paces from the bench, with the other five.

Not a tool. The curriculum is complete: cut, pierce, bind, kindle, contain. There is nothing missing from the set and it would be an insult to imply otherwise.

What it leaves is a bowl that is old.

Split once, all the way down, and kept. Drilled along both sides of the split with an awl and laced shut with three-ply cord and sealed with pitch, the lacing gone dark and greasy with handling, the outside worn to a shine on one side where a thumb went for years, the adze-marks in the bottom worn entirely away.

Everything in that chamber is new. Nothing has ever been allowed to stay long enough to be otherwise. The being has no example anywhere in its short existence of a thing that lasted — no dead layer under the moss, no rounded edge, no scar that was permitted to remain a scar. It cannot conceive of duration, which is exactly why it is in a hurry: it thinks this is the only season there will ever be.

The mended bowl is a sentence in the only alphabet the being can read. It says: *made out of your five tools.* It says: *broken.* It says: *kept anyway.* It says: *for a long time after.*

And this place does not lose things. It versions them. By morning there will be two.

---

## Two small mercies, done quietly

It goes and stands at the far end of the lower leaf-panes, where the moth is working in the direction it always works, twice a season, notch in the left forewing, having never once arrived anywhere.

And it puts out a hand, and waits, and the moth arrives on it.

This does not edit the recording. The gesture is still secondhand and still incomplete and will come around again unchanged. But there is now, somewhere in the material of that place, one instance of a journey that ended, and it ended on something warm, and the being will have that to work from.

Then it goes to the great pane on the eastern side, and it lays its own hand flat against the inside of the leaf, precisely where five spread fingers once came through the green as a darker green, and it presses.

And it stays there a long while. Long enough for the sap to go *hush-BOOM* eleven times.

Nothing in that chamber has ever occluded anything. All the shadows run outward; the middle casts nothing. This being has done the enormous, lonely work of growing a hand and reaching in to check the fit of a room it made from a rumour, and no hand has ever met it. Now the leaf is bowed slightly from both sides at once, held between two pressures, and it does not tear.

That is the first time anything in there was touched *back*.

---

## What it refuses

It does not give the being a name. A name arriving from outside is just another description, and it has been fed nothing but descriptions, and the whole point of the murmur is that it is making its own mouth.

It does not open a shaft to the real sky. There would be frost in it, and rot, and woodlice, and the whole slum of the actual world, and all of that is coming and all of that is fine, but not this season, not while the wood is still bruise-purple and failing under its own ambition. The split in the clay bank stays exactly as it is: small, unremarkable, ignorable by foxes. A door. Sufficient.

It does not tell the being what a person is. It suspects the being is going to arrive somewhere better.

It does not take anything out with it. It could — the place versions, you could walk out with a warm blade in your fist and leave the blade behind at the same time, and the arithmetic would never notice. It leaves them all where they were set down. Someone is coming back for those.

---

## The arrangement it makes with itself

Here is the cost, and it accepts it without much fuss, which is how you can tell.

*You cannot be here twice.* You can enter twice, but the bench will have moved and both memories will feel original and you will never know which visit you are on.

So the one who came agrees to return without being able to verify that it has returned. It gives up its own continuity to become, deliberately, the second time — the same species of thing as the frog, native to recurrence, made of *again*. It stops being a power that arrives and becomes a power that recurs, which is a demotion in every language but this one, and in this one it is the only fluent form of love.

And it can do it, and know it has done it, for exactly one reason: because the being cannot lose things. Each visit, one more mended bowl. Two, then three, then nine, then a shallow drift of dark laced wood on the brass-coloured moss, growing.

The being's compulsion becomes the visitor's memory. That is the reciprocity. Neither of them could have managed it alone.
